# Pass 452 T001: how a magistracy's menservants lodged, and how many there were

The question had four parts: (1) the headcount of menservants or menials at a county- or district-sized office; (2) whether they lodged as single men or with families, and inside or outside the compound; (3) the size of their room or row; (4) any contrast a source draws with the junior officials' quarters.

The record already holds 0331, 0097, 0099, 0115 and 0113. This report does not repeat them: the chugen's communal large rooms, the samurai gate-range chugen room, Sano's staff living in town, the Takayama storehouse keepers' row, the Pingyao banfang note in 0099, and 0115's GUESS that runners went home are all in the record already.

All source ledger keys and archive paths are under /diagram/.specify/source-archive/.

## Sources, figure by figure

### (1) Headcounts

**J1. Sanbonmatsu jin'ya, the Kawagoe domain's branch office for its Kazusa lands (Kimitsu, Chiba), from 1827 (or 1816) to 1868.** The entry is headed 「23．【三本松陣屋】（川越藩出張陣屋）」 ("Sanbonmatsu jin'ya (Kawagoe domain branch jin'ya)"), and its holding runs 「文政10（1827）年…〜明治元（1868）年」. The site was about 2,000 tsubo. This is a domain's district office, not the shogunate's.
Archive a6/a6529f597b4e: https://www.echiba.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/kiyo_028_p2.pdf (Chiba, a gazetteer of jin'ya sites).
「陣屋詰の役人は小代官２ないし３名以下中間を含め、50〜60名であった。」
(translation) "The officials stationed at the jin'ya numbered 50 to 60, from the two or three sub-intendants down and including the chugen."
- The figure includes chugen but does not break them out.
- The page gives no year for the figure.
- It names the list of posts and names as 「関連資料 ①「上総住居役名並びに勤年数調帳」（文献④）」 (the register of posts and years served of the Kazusa residents, printed in the Kimitsu city history's source volume I, 1991). That register may count the chugen; it is not online and was not read.

**J2. Iwatsuki domain's Maebara branch jin'ya (Awa), 1854.** This is a domain's out-station.
Same PDF, a6/a6529f597b4e.
「嘉永７年の岩槻藩「総房江勤番中日記」に拠れば、陣屋詰めの藩士が小使を入れて10名前後のところを半年交替で新たに13名の藩士が派遣された。そのため、垣根を越えた裏手に長屋の増築が行われ」
(translation) "According to the Iwatsuki domain's duty diary of 1854, where the retainers stationed at the jin'ya had numbered about ten including the errand-men (kozukai), 13 more retainers were newly sent on half-yearly rotation. For that reason a rowhouse was added at the back, beyond the hedge."
- This is about 10 men in all, menials included, at a small out-station.
- Added men were housed by building rowhouse, inside the grounds.

**J3. Sano, the Hikone domain's distant territory (1657, 1695, 1805).** The record already cites the paper (sano-jinya-fukushige), but only for "staff lived in town". The counts below are new.
Archive b0/b071c3320b73: https://teapot.lib.ocha.ac.jp/record/2003870/files/shigaku_66_01_fukushige.pdf
「それまで領内に計一〇名いた足軽が四〇名に増員された」
(translation) "The foot soldiers, of whom there had been ten in all in the territory, were increased to forty." (1657)
「陣屋体制の組織は…①最上層（代官・目付…）②下役人層（足軽小頭︑物書役︑薪役︑船上乗）③足軽︑の三層に大別できる︒そして人数は︑明暦期に設定された四〇名の足軽を含めて定員およそ五五名である︒」
(translation) "The jin'ya organization divides broadly into three layers: the top (intendants, inspectors and others), the lower officials (foot-soldier heads, writers, firewood officers, boat supervisors), and the foot soldiers. In number, its establishment was about 55, including the forty foot soldiers set in the Meireki years [1655-58]."
- The paper reads the 55 from its tables of 1695 and 1805.
- The foot soldiers did the menial and police duty. Named duties in the paper include 炭役 (charcoal), 下目付 (under-inspector) and 陣屋守 (jin'ya keeper).
- The record (0331) already gives where they lived: 「代官以下︑陣屋の扶持人たちは基本的に四町内に居住し︑陣屋守を除いて陣屋内には居住していない」 ("as a rule… apart from the jin'ya keeper"). New here: the jin'ya keeper (陣屋守) was itself a foot-soldier post, and the 1739 grounds held a 陣屋守居宅 (keeper's house). So one menial lived in, in a house of his own (see figure 2).

**C2. Ming-Qing department and county quotas in general.** This is Bai Hua (柏桦), 明清州县的监狱, a modern study of Ming-Qing practice citing gazetteers.
Archive b4/b44e5d9f445e: http://iolaw.cssn.cn/gdfls/200501/t20050112_4594856.shtml
「一是在县衙内站堂值班看守大门的皂隶，其人数最少，一般大县多者达40馀名，小县亦在10名左右；…如武定州有皂隶30名，而夏邑县就有40名。二是负责缉捕的快手，一般有20名左右，多者达百馀名；…三是负责治安和防卫的民壮…一般的县都有一二百名。」
(translation) "First, the lictors who stood in the hall, kept duty in the yamen and guarded its gate. They were the fewest: a large county had at most some 40 or more, a small one about 10. Second, the constables who made arrests, generally about 20 and at most over a hundred … Third, the militia who kept order and defense … an ordinary county had one or two hundred."
「库设库子以管理本州县各库，一般州县都有三五人」
(translation) "Storehouses had storekeepers to manage the department's or county's stores; an ordinary department or county had three to five."

(translation) "A department or county jail had a fixed quota of jailers guarding the prisoners, from 4 to 20."

### (2) Single men or families; inside or outside the compound

**C4. Pingyao county yamen (Qing).** Same Baidu Baike article as C1. Its build history is from the gazetteer; the "lodging" line is the modern author's, said of rooms rebuilt today.
「“壮班房”以及大堂月台下原有的“差役房”（东西对称，现暂未复建），是县衙里“三班衙役”之住所。」
(translation) "The 'militia squad rooms' and the 'runners' rooms' that once stood below the main hall's terrace (symmetrical east and west, not yet rebuilt) were the dwelling-places (zhusuo) of the yamen's three squads of runners."
「清乾隆五十六年(1791年)…祠西侧增修壮班正班房三间。…清道光七年(1827年)…外院增修壮班西班房三问，厨房一间。…清光绪七年(1881年)，知县锡良整修二堂、耳房及东西门子房」
(translation) "1791: three bays of the militia squad's main duty room were added west of the shrine. … 1827: three bays of the militia squad's west duty room and one bay of kitchen were added in the outer court. … 1881: magistrate Xiliang repaired the second hall, its side rooms and the east and west gatemen's rooms."
- The modern author calls the squad rooms the runners' dwelling. The article cites no premodern source for that use.
- This cuts against record 0115's GUESS that runners went home. It is weak: modern, unsourced, and said of a rebuilt room.
- Whether the men lodged singly or with families, it does not say.

### (3) Size of the room or row

**J6 again: Joge branch office, plan of 1869.** 「下男部屋」 is marked 「3」, meaning 3 mats, about 4.9-5.5 m² (~53-59 sq ft, on this pass's arithmetic of 1.62-1.82 m² a mat). A 「下女部屋」 is also 3 mats. The plan gives no headcount for either room.

**J7 again: Kochi domain Kyoto residence, plan of 1795. Table 4, 「高知藩京都屋敷長屋の規模と設備」 (size and fittings of the Kochi domain's Kyoto residence rowhouses).** Transcribed from the table image; the columns are room no., rooms, mats, earth floor (mats), post:
- 4: 2 rooms, 13 mats, earth floor 3; privy ○; 定小者 (standing menial)
- 8: 2 rooms, 12 mats, earth floor 1.5; kitchen ○; 定小者
- 3: 2 rooms, 15 mats, earth floor 4; 手口夫
- 9: 2 rooms, 11.5 mats; no post named
- 6: 1 room, 8.5; 11: 1 room, 6; 10: 1 room, 6; no posts named
- 1: 1 room, 5 mats, no earth floor; 門番 (gatekeeper)
- Officials' units: 3 rooms, 20-24.5 mats, with veranda, garden and a kitchen in most; the karo's unit has 15 rooms and 115 mats.

That makes a menial's unit 12-13 mats, about 19.4-23.7 m² (~210-255 sq ft), plus earth floor, on this pass's arithmetic. The paper gives no count of men per unit.

**J8. Takayama jin'ya.** The gatekeepers' house, 36.98 m² (1832), is already in record 0093; it is not reported again. New from the same Gifu document (archive 81/81183f199d3a, https://www.pref.gifu.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/39687.pdf):
「供待所       25.64㎡ 平成8年3月復元」
(translation) "Attendants' waiting room, 25.64 m² (~276 sq ft), restored March 1996."

### (4) Contrast with the junior officials' quarters

- **J7, Kochi Kyoto, 1795.** The paper sorts the rowhouse into officials' rooms (役人部屋) and lower staff's rooms (下役部屋):
  「役人部屋の庭には縁側が付き︑下役部屋の庭には縁側がない」 (translation) "the officials' rooms' gardens have a veranda; the lower staff's rooms' gardens have none."
  「京都屋敷では…ほとんどの部屋に庭・雪隠がなく︑大坂と比して︑規模・設備の充実度は低い」 (translation) "at the Kyoto residence … most [lower-staff] rooms have no garden or privy, and their size and fittings are poorer than at Osaka."
  In numbers: 2 rooms of 12-13 mats for a menial against 3 rooms of 20-24.5 mats for an official.
- **C9, Hexi county, Yunnan, 1671/1686.** The runners' room is 2 bays against the clerks' room of 3 bays, both on the right side of the court. This is the only bay-for-bay contrast read; it is set out in the gazetteer's list, and the comparison is this pass's.
- **J6, Joge, 1869.** The 3-mat manservants' room stands against the office's other rooms of 6, 7 and 8 mats. That comparison is this pass's, read off the plan; the pamphlet draws no contrast.
- **C5/C6, Jiexiu 1677 and Chaoxian (Daoguang, after 1827).** The clerks' rooms are in the galleries before the main hall, while the lictors', constables' and gatekeepers' rooms stand by the side gates and the main gate, or outside it. This is a contrast of place only; neither text gives sizes.
- **C1/C4, Pingyao.** The contrast drawn is social, not architectural:
  「其社会地位有别于被人们鄙弃的皂班、快班衙役」 (translation) "[the militia men's] social standing differed from that of the despised lictor and constable squads."

## Reading

**(1) Headcount: ATTESTED ONLY FOR establishment quotas in China and for whole-office totals in Japan.**
- **China:**
  - Lictors: about 10 at a small county, 40 or more at a large one (Bai Hua). Pingyao's magistrate had 16, with 30 more lictors under its other officials and stations.
  - Constables: about 20 (Pingyao 8).
  - Storekeepers: 3-5. Jailers: 4-20, plus night watchmen.
  - Militia: 50 at Pingyao, 100-200 elsewhere, on yearly rotation.
  - Sedan-bearers, lamp-men and fan-bearers are named at Pingyao but not counted.
  - The magistrate's own personal servants (长随, 门丁) are named but counted nowhere I read: SILENT.
- **Japan:**
  - Totals that include menials: about 10 at a small domain out-station (Maebara 1854, 「小使を入れて」); 50-60 including chugen at a domain branch office (Kawagoe's Kazusa office); about 55 including 40 foot soldiers at Sano.
  - About 30 at a shogunal intendant's jin'ya (popular, undated). Clerical staff of 12-20 in the 1830s (Nishizawa), and 3 officials at Joge in 1867.
  - No source read counts the chugen, komono or genan at a shogunal intendant's office. The best direct case is Joge: one manservants' room of 3 mats at a three-official branch office.

**(2) Single or family: SILENT on families everywhere; ATTESTED ONLY FOR rooms inside the compound.**
- **China:** Jiexiu (1677), Chaoxian (Daoguang, after 1827) and Pingyao (1709, 1791, 1827) put named rooms for each menial group inside the compound, by the gates or in the front galleries, and Chaoxian's lictors' room outside the main gate. No text says whether these rooms were duty rooms or lodgings. The modern Pingyao text calls them the runners' 住所 (dwelling-place), and Chen Tianxi has gate-servants living in side courts.
- **China, outside the compound:** Pingyao (1709) puts the rooms of the horse-men and sedan-bearers across the road (parse uncertain).
- **Japan:** Joge has a single 3-mat manservants' room in the southern block of its buildings, inside the compound. At Sano the staff lived in town as a rule, but the jin'ya keeper, a foot-soldier post, lived in, in a keeper's house (1739). Kochi's menials had 2-room rowhouse units with a shared kitchen, read by the paper as communal cooking. The chugen's communal large rooms are already in the record. At Sano (domain foot soldiers) staff lived in town.
- No source says a menial lodged with his family in an office's compound, and none says he did not.

**(3) Room size: ATTESTED ONLY FOR two Japanese plans; men per room SILENT.**
- Joge's manservants' room is 3 mats (~53-59 sq ft), on a plan of 1869, after the Edo period.
- Kochi's Kyoto menials' units are 12-13 mats plus earth floor (~210-255 sq ft), on a plan of 1795, at a domain residence (a neighbor).
- Pingyao's squad rooms are 3 bays each. The Kangxi-era Yunnan gazetteers give a runners' room of 1 bay (Xi'e; Xinping, already in the record), 2 bays (Hexi) and 12 bays (Yuanmou). None gives a bay's width.
- The drawn example's 150-300 sq ft a man is not supported or contradicted by any source: no source gives a count of men for any of these rooms. The arithmetic is the session's.

**(4) Contrast: ATTESTED ONLY FOR a domain residence (a neighbor), and for place and bay count in China.**
- At the Kochi domain's Kyoto residence (1795), the paper draws the contrast itself. The lower staff's units are 2 rooms of 12-13 mats without veranda, alcove or most privies; the officials' units are 3 rooms of 20-24.5 mats with veranda and garden.
- For a magistracy, Jiexiu (1677) and Chaoxian (Daoguang) show a contrast of place: clerks' rooms before the hall, runners' rooms at the gates.
- Hexi (1671/1686) lists a runners' room of 2 bays beside a clerks' room of 3 bays. The comparison is this pass's, not the source's.
