# Pass 452 T003: how long a junior officials' rowhouse or hut ran

The question: is a rowhouse under about 60 ft (18 m, 10 ken) attested, which would lower the pop-up's 80 ft floor?
Ledger: QUESTION=none. Clone: /diagram/.clones/diagram-research. Archive key: t003-rowlen.

## Where the record's 80-145 ft comes from

The range in l7r/diagram/interactive/assets/modals/sheet/retainers--quarters.md and in record 0097 rests on three buildings:
- the floor is Kaibara jin'ya's gate range, 13.5 x 2 ken, about 81 x 12 ft (kaibara-jinya-tamba);
- Omori intendant's office's gate ranges, 33 m (about 108 ft), sit between;
- the ceiling is Shibata's foot soldiers' row, 43.6 x 7.3 m, about 143 x 24 ft (shibata-ashigaru-nagaya).

Both of the lower two are GATE ranges, not dwelling rows. Shibata is the only dwelling row of the three. Sayama, Obata and Sakai give dwelling sizes in the record, not building lengths.

## 1. Sources: the length and depth of a staff rowhouse or hut

### 1a. Sayama domain jin'ya: dwellings 4-6 ken along the row, four or perhaps TWO to a row (domain plans, undated on the page; the author's reading)
URL: https://note.com/kuematu0404/n/n7cd277b4e3f3 (archive 2d/2deb775751d7, key sayama-jinya-uematsu). Uematsu Kiyoshi reads the Sayama domain's jin'ya plans. This is a drawn plan with dimensions, not a surviving building.

> 「中・下級藩士には、席次に応じた規模の長屋が供給されました。１軒あたり100坪の敷地に建てられた長屋は、４軒長屋であったとされますが、共同井戸が２軒単位であることから２軒長屋の可能性もあります。」

(translation) "The middle and lower retainers were supplied rowhouses sized by rank. The rowhouses, built on plots of 100 tsubo a dwelling, are said to have been four-dwelling rowhouses, but since the shared wells were one to every two dwellings, they may have been two-dwelling rowhouses."

> 「長屋の平面（図５～８）をみると、梁行が2.5間で共通し、図５～７までは、「中ノ間」10畳、「奥ノ間」６畳、「納戸」４畳と、主要な生活空間については席次による差異はありません。」 ... 「一方、桁行は、図５は６間、図６は5.5間、図７は５間、図８は４間となり、桁行の長さによって席次による差異が設けられていました」

(translation) "Looking at the rowhouses' plans (figs. 5-8), the beam span is 2.5 ken in all, and figs. 5-7 show no difference by rank in the main living spaces: a middle room of 10 mats, an inner room of 6 mats and a closet room of 4 mats." ... "The ridge length, on the other hand, is 6 ken in fig. 5, 5.5 ken in fig. 6, 5 ken in fig. 7 and 4 ken in fig. 8, so the difference by rank was made in the length along the ridge."

Figs. 5-8 are titled 中小性席長屋, 中小性並長屋, 供組長屋 and 徒士長屋.

Are the 4-6 ken those of a dwelling or of a building? Of a DWELLING. Three things show it:
- the figures give each plan one household's rooms (10 + 6 + 4 mats);
- 2.5 x 5 ken is 12.5 tsubo, which is those 20 mats plus a small earth floor;
- the text speaks of 100 tsubo "１軒あたり" (a dwelling).

The record reads it the same way. On this project's arithmetic:
- a four-dwelling row runs 16-24 ken, about 96-144 ft;
- a TWO-dwelling row, the author's alternative, runs 8 ken (about 48 x 15 ft) for the foot samurai (kachi) to 12 ken (about 72 ft) for the pages.

### 1b. Sakai town magistracy: the rows on the plans of 1711-1729, measured off the author's scaled redrawings (this project's measurement)
URL: https://zokei.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/192/files/08_19takaya%20%E8%8B%A5%E7%8B%AD%E9%87%8E%E6%B5%85%E9%87%8E%E5%B1%8B%E8%B3%87%E6%96%99%E3%81%AB%E3%81%BF%E3%82%8B%E5%A0%BA%E5%A5%89%E8%A1%8C%E6%89%80%E3%81%AE%E5%AE%B6%E4%B8%AD%E9%95%B7%E5%B1%8B.pdf (key sakai-bugyosho-kachu-nagaya-takaya). The archive holds only a partial copy.

We read it from an earlier pass's local copy: /tmp/l7r-pass/sakai-pdf/s.txt, r.txt, and the page render h6-06.png. `make source-pages` could not read the PDF ("no text layer"). This is a drawn plan with dimensions: the author's 起こし図 (redrawing to scale) of the plan 「堺御役屋敷絵図（手鑑弐冊之内）」, dated 享保13 (1728/29).

Note 10 says what a measurable figure is. That fig. 5 is such an 起こし図 is INFERRED from the scale bar it carries; the text says only 「堺奉行所の構成を図5に示す」.
> 「絵図に記された寸法を反映し作図した図を「起こし図」として、寸法などを反映しないトレース図と区別している。このため、起こし図にはスケールを加筆している。」

(translation) "A figure drawn to reflect the dimensions written on the plan is called an 'okoshi-zu', as distinct from a tracing, which does not reflect dimensions. A scale has therefore been added to the okoshi-zu."

On the doshin rows:
> 「同心は長屋に配置されており、同資料にみる限りは、一人あたりの規模として梁間二間桁行三間であることや、桁が連続する長屋の形態が共通し」

(translation) "The doshin were placed in rowhouses. As far as these materials show, they have in common a size per man of 2 ken across the beams and 3 ken along the ridge, and the form of a rowhouse with a continuous ridge."

On row A:
> 「家中長屋Aは、東西方向に一列に並ぶ長屋として描かれている。」

(translation) "Retainers' rowhouse A is drawn as a rowhouse standing in a single line east-west."

On row B, a block of TWO dwellings:
> 「絵図Aの家中長屋Bは、「家中長屋」と表記された1、「家中屋敷」と表記された2のふたつの区画から構成されるとみられる。北側の区画2、家中屋敷は床を伴う座敷と独立した縁のある庭を備えていると判断できる。」 ... 「絵図Cにおいても、区画はふたつと考えられる。」

(translation) "Retainers' rowhouse B in plan A appears to consist of two compartments: 1, labeled 'retainers' rowhouse', and 2, labeled 'retainers' residence'. The northern compartment 2, the retainers' residence, can be judged to have had a formal room with an alcove and a garden with a veranda of its own." ... "In plan C, too, the compartments are thought to be two."

On row C, small compartments:
> 「家中長屋Cは、絵図Aのみに描かれている。絵図Aのなかでも、家中長屋Aや家中長屋Bと比較して区画の規模が小さく、比較した長屋のうちでは簡素な平面構成でもあり、床を伴う部屋と考えられる表現もみられない。」

(translation) "Retainers' rowhouse C appears only in plan A. Within plan A its compartments are smaller than those of A and B. Its plan is the simplest of the rows compared, and nothing is drawn that might be a room with an alcove."

How the figure was measured: fig. 5 (the whole magistracy, 起こし図 of plan A) on page 6 was rendered at about 300 dpi. Its 10-ken scale bar measures 132 px, so 1 ken is about 13.2 px. The figures below are the PROJECT's measurements, not the paper's.

The scale was checked against the stated sizes:
- the doshin rows measure 2.05 ken deep, against the stated 2 ken;
- their partitions repeat every about 3.5 ken, against the stated 3 ken along the ridge.

So take every length as +/- about 15%. The rows:
- **Doshin rows (two):** about 15 and 16 uniform dwellings each. At the text's stated 桁行三間 a dwelling, that is 45-48 ken, about 270-290 ft, and this figure does not depend on the scale. The cross-check off the scale bar gives about 52 and 55 ken by 2 ken.
- **Retainers' row A:** about 55 ken long, a single line of many compartments.
- **Retainers' row C:** about 17 ken (about 100 ft; about 15 ken at the doshin-pitch scale) by about 2 ken, in six compartments of unequal width.
- **Retainers' row B:** a block of two compartments, one labeled 家中長屋 and one 家中屋敷, about 11.5 x 7.5 ken on the scale bar (about 70 x 45 ft). The bounding box includes wall strokes, so the building itself is somewhat smaller. At the doshin-pitch scale it is about 10 x 6.6 ken (60 x 40 ft). It is squat, not long and narrow, and it housed retainers of HIGHER standing: one compartment has an alcove room and a garden of its own.

Wuding prefecture's own hall had 「左右列書吏房各七間」 (translation: "clerks' rooms ranged left and right, 7 bays each"), and Hequ subprefecture's likewise. So a clerks' or runners' range ran from 1 to 12 bays, commonly 3 to 8 a side. 書吏房 and 班房 are workrooms and waiting rooms, and Wujiang puts the lodging (周廬) elsewhere. At an UNSOURCED bay of about 3 m (the record's only Chinese bay widths are shopfronts of 2.5-3 m in 0135 and a gate of about 3.2 m in 0147, neither a yamen range), the project's arithmetic gives:
- 3 bays: about 9-10 m (about 30 ft);
- 7 bays: about 21 m (about 70 ft);
- 12 bays: about 36 m (about 120 ft) if in one range.

## 4. A rule by rank

**ATTESTED for one domain, Sayama.** The dwelling's length along the row was set by rank, all at 2.5 ken deep: 6 ken for the pages (中小性席), 5.5 for those ranked with the pages (中小性並), 5 for the escort company (供組), 4 for the foot samurai (徒士) (1a). The record already has this.

Two other things work like a rule, though neither is a rule stated in writing:
- Sakai gives every doshin the same 2 x 3 ken (1b).
- In Kaga the plot was the measure: 「平足軽で50坪、小頭で70坪」 (50 tsubo for a plain foot soldier, 70 for a squad head). This appears only in a search snippet of the Kaga pages, not in the page read; the page read gives 165 or 230 m².

No Chinese rule by rank for clerks' quarters was found.

What IS attested short is the detached single-household house in a foot soldiers' company quarter, not a row and not at an office:

The nearest cases at an office or jin'ya, neither of them a measured short row:
- **Sakai town magistracy, plans of 1711-1729:** a two-compartment retainers' block, row B, about 70 x 45 ft on the scale bar (at least 60 ft at any reading). This is the project's measurement off the author's scaled redrawing. It is squat, about 1.5:1, and it housed retainers of higher standing.
- **Sayama jin'ya:** the author's POSSIBILITY that the rows held two dwellings, not the four "said". On that reading a foot samurai's row would be 8 x 2.5 ken, about 48 x 15 ft. It is an inference from shared wells, not a measured building.

Every measured staff row is long:
- Sakai's doshin rows: 15-16 dwellings at 3 ken each, 45-48 ken, about 270-290 ft;
- Matsusaka's castle-guard rows: 90.9 m and 83.6 m;
- Shibata, the dwelling row in the record: 143 ft;
- the guard and dwelling rows of 15-17.5 ken (about 90-105 ft): Tarumizu, Yamauchi, and Sakai's row C.

So rows that EXCEED the pop-up's 145 ft are attested twice (Sakai, Matsusaka). The pop-up's 80 ft floor rests on a gate range (Kaibara), and no dwelling row shorter than it was found. Whether the ceiling should move is the record's ruling.

The county plan's 2:1 block of four 12 ft dwellings is matched by no source: the Sayama possibility is two dwellings of 4 ken at 15 ft deep, and Sakai's block B is two large dwellings.

For China, the gazetteers count clerks' and runners' rooms in bays (班房 1-12; 書吏房 3-8 a side), but no bay width is given, so there is no length. Those rooms are offices and waiting rooms. The lodgings (吏舍, 周廬, 公廨) are placed apart from them (Neixiang, Wujiang, Pingyao) and counted only at Weizhou (吏舍 12 bays) and, if 公廨 are lodgings, at Lingqiu (20) and Guangling (7).
