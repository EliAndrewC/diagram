# Pass 449 T428: the shape of dry strips beside a supply canal, and what a strip did where its canal bent

Question 0006, block "A dry plot beside a canal stands square to it". Read-only pass; nothing in the clone was edited. The Japanese quotes below were checked against the rendered page images, not the PDF text layers.

## Sources that give the thing asked

### 0. The block's own source: 水道いしずえ, 「武蔵野の新田集落」

https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/02/03.html. Archived as `ishizue-musashino`. Modern text describing Edo-period villages.

> この台地上に成立した新田集落は、道路にそって列状に並ぶ屋敷と、その背後に細長い短冊状の畑地と林地を備えた独特の形態をもつ。

"The new-field villages formed on this plateau have a distinctive form: houses in a row along a road, and behind them long, narrow strip-shaped fields and woodland." (translation)

> 台地南部は街道にそって掘られた用水路の方向に、北部は水源が井戸のため比較的自由な方向に、列村が形成されている。

"In the south of the plateau the row villages formed in the direction of the canals dug along the highways; in the north, because the water came from wells, in a comparatively free direction." (translation)

- So the strips are tied to the road. The canal runs along the road. The strips' own angle to the canal is not given.

### 2. Yamamori Yoshirō (山森芳郎), 「武蔵野台地における列状集落について」

Journal of Architecture, Planning and Environmental Engineering (Transactions of AIJ) no. 436, June 1992. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/436/0/436_KJ00004077200/_pdf. Archived as `santome-shinden-allotment`, at /diagram/.specify/source-archive/2f/2f662ec56095/. Evidence: Edo-period Musashino in general, and Santome's layout of 1694-96. Measures: household strips, 40 x 375 ken at Santome.

p. 97, the general Musashino principle:

> 1）耕地を農家各戸へほぼ均等に配分する 2）農家屋敷と配分された耕地を直結し，短冊状の地割とする 3）農家屋敷を直線状にのびる集落道の片側または両側に沿わせる　したがって短冊状の地割は集落道に直交する。

"(1) the farmland is shared out nearly equally among the farm households; (2) each farmhouse lot is joined directly to its allotted land, as a strip; (3) the farmhouses are set along one or both sides of a settlement road running in a straight line. So the strip allotment stands at right angles to the settlement road." (translation)

p. 99, Santome:

> まず上富中央の多福寺の南側に，南南東方向に平行する3本―中富との境界，上富中央，そして北永井との境界―の集落道を通す。道路の幅員はいずれも6間（約10.92m），通称「六間道」である。既存の村落境に沿っては，屈曲する。ついで多福寺側から各戸へ中央集落道の両側に区画割りをすすめる。…多福寺から南下するに従って，また中富側より北永井側で，区画のひずみが大きくなる。

"First, three parallel settlement roads were run south-south-east from south of Tafukuji: on the boundary with Nakatome, through the middle of Kamitome, and on the boundary with Kitanagai. Each was 6 ken (about 10.92 m) wide, the 'six-ken road'. Where a road follows an existing village boundary, it bends. Then, starting from the Tafukuji side, the plots were divided out to each household along both sides of the central road. ... Going south from Tafukuji, and on the Kitanagai side compared with the Nakatome side, the distortion of the plots grows." (translation)

> 集落道の片側だけでは奥行を確保できなくなると，各戸の持ち分はその両側に分かれ，ときには連続した区画が確保できずに飛地も生ずる。このような計画原理の「やわらかな」適用にもかかわらず，区画相互の境界は直線的で，鋭敏である。

"Where one side of the road could not give the full depth, a household's share was split across both sides, and sometimes, when no continuous plot could be had, detached parcels arose. Despite this 'soft' application of the plan, the boundaries between plots are straight and sharp." (translation)

p. 103, the comparison table 別表, entry 6, 郷原 (Shiojiri, Nagano; settled 1614-19):

> （区画は不規則）（例）14間（25.2m）

"(the plots are irregular) (example) 14 ken (25.2 m)" (translation)

On the same table, entry 2, 鳥栖新田 (1591-1602), has a frontage entered as "(不規則) (狭) 12間 (広) 25間": "(irregular) (narrow) 12 ken, (wide) 25 ken" (translation).

Santome had no canal. The Miyoshi town report (https://www.town.saitama-miyoshi.lg.jp/town/chosa/kenkyu/documents/documentssantome_houkoku.pdf, modern, on the evidence from 1694):

> 開拓当初は三富新田へ用水を引く計画があったが実現に至らず、代わりに11ヵ所の共同の深井戸を掘って水を得た。

"At the start a canal into Santome was planned but never built; instead eleven shared deep wells were dug." (translation)

## Reading

### Point 1: ATTESTED ONLY FOR household strips squared to a straight road, in Edo Musashino dry-field villages of 1661-1729

**The nearest case.** At Shinbori in Nishibori village (source 1, p. 56), strips stand at right angles to a road that runs parallel to the Nobidome canal. The modern schematic Fig. 2-33 draws them as rectangles lying side by side along the canal.

**What every source squares the strips to.**
- Every source squares them to the settlement road (sources 0-3), and the plan principle calls for a road "running in a straight line" (source 2, p. 97).
- The unit is a whole household holding of house lot, fields and woods, 7.5-45 ken wide and 300-1,200 ken deep. It is not a small dry plot.
- At Nobidome village itself the highway runs at right angles to the canal (source 1, p. 54). Strips square to the highway therefore run along the canal there, not across it.

**What the canal did.**
- The Nobidome canal was dug for drinking water, and no paddy lay along it in Niiza.
- One page says it also served irrigation without saying for which fields (Saitama).
- Where the same canal was used for irrigation, no strips formed (source 1, p. 14). That removes the Musashino strips as a ready analogue for a hem watered from its canal. It is not itself evidence of the plot shape there.

**Whether Santome matters.** It does. Santome had no canal at all, only a road and wells. So it attests the strip form and its distortions, but it says nothing about a canal.

**On shape.**
- The Musashino strips were rectangles with straight, sharp boundaries (source 2, p. 99). Departures were known:
  - a strip widening toward its back, 12 to 14 ken, "because of the land" (source 3);
  - irregular plots in two early strip villages (source 2, 別表).
- In China, slanted (trapezoidal), basket-shaped and triangular fields were standard cases in the land arithmetic (source 4). The registers held plots of "every different shape" (source 5). Planned ancient grids were rectangles inside canals and roads (source 6).

**No premodern source in either country gives the shape of a single dry plot beside an irrigation canal.**

### Point 2: For a canal bend, SILENT. The nearest evidence is ATTESTED ONLY FOR roads and boundaries

**Santome.** The paper places two statements side by side:
- a road "bends" where it follows an existing village boundary;
- plot distortion grows on the Kitanagai side, where one such boundary road runs.

It does not say the bend causes the distortion; that link is my inference. Where a road could not give the full depth, shares split across both sides or became detached parcels. Plot boundaries stayed straight (source 2).

**Shimo-Moro'oka.** Where the bounding highways converge, the strips grow shallow, and the lost area is made up with irregular land elsewhere (source 3).

**What no source says.**
- No source says strips tapered into wedges, overlapped or followed a curve. None says what lay in a leftover wedge.
- My reading of Santome's Fig. 2, a modern base map, finds straight strip lines ending obliquely against curving boundaries and no curved strip. That is a reading of a figure, not a statement of the source.
- Fei's paddy strips are perpendicular to a stream margin, with nothing said about curves.
- No source anywhere shows strips at a canal bend.

### What the block should say on this evidence

1. **The frame.** Keep it a RECONSTRUCTION, with its grounds restated: Edo Musashino household strips were squared to a straight road, in some villages a road beside the canal (Shinbori, 1661). That canal was dug for drinking water.
2. **The limits.** Add two facts:
   - where the same canal was used for irrigation, no strips formed;
   - at Nobidome village the strips ran along the canal, not across it.
3. **"Rectangle".** It can cite straight-edged strips (Yamamori 1992). Name the known departures: a strip widening 12 to 14 ken toward its back, and early strip villages with irregular plots.
4. **The bend.** How the drawn plots meet a canal bend (abutting along a curving line, the outer edge ragged) should be labeled a GUESS or a drawing CONVENTION. The only analogues are a bending boundary road beside growing plot distortion, with split or detached shares (Santome), and converging roads made good with irregular land elsewhere (Shimo-Moro'oka). Neither shows wedge-shaped or curved strips.
