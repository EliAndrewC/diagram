I ran the pass read-only: archive first, then the web. Every page I read is on the ledger as `pending`, `nothing-found` or `unreadable`.

**Answer to the main question.** 0018's sentence holds for the *kind* of channel. The ponds lay among boat creeks (河涌, 浜, 浍), not field ditches. But no premodern source gives the width of a channel between ponds. The 1.2–2.5 ft width the engine uses is not supported for a dike-pond block by anything I found. A channel on every pond edge is not attested either: the one premodern plan of a pond block (Qu Dajun, 1678) draws its lines as dikes, not water.

**Answer to the second question.** SILENT on a ring canal at the inside foot of a dike. The sources put the bounding channels outside the dike, on a planned grid.

## 1. Sources

### Channels and boats

**A. Qu Dajun, 廣東新語 (1678).** Archived: f1/f11c59d2494f (zh.wikisource 卷22). The 棋枰 passage is quoted from the record's 0018 originals page.
- 「九江之地如棋枰，周回三十餘裏，其黑脈者堤也，方方罫池塘也。」 = "Jiujiang's land is like a chessboard, some thirty li around; its black lines are dikes, its squares are ponds." (translation)
- 「基下為池以畜魚」 = "Below the dikes are ponds for raising fish." (translation)
- 「魚苗入塘，養至下年春，乃以船載往兩粵州縣，兌換穀米」 = "The fry go into the ponds, are raised until the next spring, then carried by boat to the counties of Guangdong and Guangxi and traded for grain." (translation)
- **Reading:** ATTESTED ONLY FOR boat transport of fish out of the township. NOT ATTESTED for a channel between ponds: the lines between the ponds are dikes.

**B. Jia Dan 郏亶, 吳門水利書 (c.1070, Suzhou).** In 吳郡志 卷19: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/吳郡志/卷19 (saved at /tmp/l7r-pass/wujunzhi19).
- 「民…各從其行舟、安舟之便，決其堤以為涇。今昆山諸浦之間，有半里、或一里、二里而為小涇，命之為某家涇，某家浜者」 = "For the convenience of moving and mooring their boats, people cut the dikes to make small creeks; now between the Kunshan 浦, every half li, one li or two li there is a small creek, named 'so-and-so family's creek' or 'so-and-so family's 浜'." (translation)
- **Reading:** ATTESTED ONLY FOR rice-polder country by Lake Tai. Household creeks were cut for boats. No width is given.

**C. Shen Kuo 沈括, 萬春圩圖記 (1061–62, Wanchun polder, Wuhu).** Archived: 6b/6b0d3db9d121 (https://wuhurec.com/archives/153/).
- 「方頃而溝之，四溝澮之為一區。一家之澮，可以舫舟矣。隅落部伍，直曲相望，皆應法度。」 = "Each square qing is ditched; four ditches feed a larger channel (澮), making one district. A single household's 澮 can float a boat. Corners and ranks, straight and bent stretches in sight of one another, all follow the rule." (translation)
- **Reading:** ATTESTED ONLY FOR the interior of a planned rice polder. It has two grades: 溝 (field ditch) and 澮 (boatable).

**D. 沈氏農書 (c.1640, Huzhou).** Archived: 29/29913b34ce12 and 60/602505e0a8d4.
- The month-by-month work list repeats 「罱泥」, 「罱田泥」 and 「載壅」, and has 「修船」 and 「車池潭」. Its gloss: 「車池潭：冬天抽干鱼池潭里的水，捉鱼挑泥」 = "pump dry the fish pond in winter, catch the fish, carry out the mud." (translation)
- A Southern Song poem quoted in 35/35803d7e3d84: 「竹罾兩兩夾河泥，近郊溝渠此最肥，載得滿船歸插種」 = "Pairs of bamboo scoops grab the river mud; the ditches near the town have the richest; a full boatload carried home for planting." (translation)
- **Reading:** ATTESTED for Lake Tai. Mud was dredged and fertilizer carried by boat, and even 溝渠 carried mud boats.

**E. F. H. King, Farmers of Forty Centuries (observed 1909).** Archived: 84/842e8bba9073 (gutenberg 5350).
- Kashing–Hangchow: "there entered from the west 134 and there left on the coast side 190 canals. The average width of these canals, measured along the water line, we estimated at 22 and 19 feet respectively."
- South of Kashing: "The boat anchored in front of the mulberry orchard…", and "The stacking of soil along the banks was probably in preparation for its removal by boat to some of the mulberry orchards."
- Pearl delta: "From the gates long canals, six to eight feet wide, led back sometimes eighty or a hundred rods. Across these and at right angles, head channels were cut…"
- **Reading:**
  - 22/19 ft is the width of side canals off a trunk route, not of channels between ponds.
  - 6–8 ft is for diked rice and cane fields in the delta, not ponds.
  - Boats carried mud to mulberry: ATTESTED.

**F. Chen Caixia et al., 资源科学 2021 (Foshan, four villages).** Archived: 0d/0d65a734f991 (https://www.resci.cn/CN/lexeme/showArticleByLexeme.do?articleID=49240).
- 「以往农户往来"耕塘"和塘鱼的销售,均通过小船运输」 = "Formerly, farmers' trips to work the ponds and the sale of pond fish were all by small boat." (translation)
- 「经整治,每口鱼塘均可实现与毛支涌相接」 = "After consolidation every pond can connect to a branch creek." (translation)
- 「基塘南北一侧为约7 m宽的机耕路,一侧为约6~8 m宽的排灌毛支涌」 = "on one side a ~7 m farm road, on the other a ~6–8 m irrigation-drainage branch creek." (translation)
- **Dating:** the boats come from informant memory of pre-reform practice (post-1912). The 6–8 m is a post-1998 consolidation design: listed, but it does not count toward the verdict.
- **Reading:** ATTESTED ONLY FOR modern recollection that ponds were worked by boat. My inference, not the paper's statement: before consolidation, not every pond touched a creek.

**G. 简村 landings (Qing, Guangxu reign).** People's Daily 2021, archived c9/c9501f2d5aa0 (http://ent.people.com.cn/n1/2021/0216/c1012-32029812.html).
- 「西樵山简村蚕桑业兴盛，不到两公里河段修建了58处埠头。"民乐有个窦，家家纺织声"，"一船蚕丝出，一船白银归"」 = "Jiancun's silk trade flourished; 58 landings were built along less than 2 km of creek… 'a boat of silk goes out, a boat of silver comes back'." (translation)
- The same page: 「村民通过堤围、河涌、窦闸灌排」 = "villagers irrigated and drained through dikes, creeks and sluices." (translation)
- **Reading:** ATTESTED for the Sangyuan polder. Its creeks were navigable and lined with landings.

**H. Tian Mengxiao, Landscape Architecture Frontiers 2019.** Archive gm-copies "Seeing from Above…".
- 「在20世纪50年代末，许多基塘区的土地与水塘已经高度破碎化，河涌越发弯曲」 = "By the late 1950s… the creeks grew ever more winding." (translation)
- 「基围则是马赛克间宽阔的"镶边"」 = "the dikes were the broad 'borders' between the mosaic [ponds]." (translation)
- **Reading:** 1950s evidence that the dike-pond creeks were winding 河涌. The broad borders between ponds were dikes, not water.

**I. Huzhou (2019 Xinhua, archived a7/a7d2809a750b).**
- 「五里七里一纵浦(溇港),七里十里为一横塘」 and 「具有排涝、灌溉、通航等综合效益」 = "a lengthwise 浦 every 5–7 li, a crosswise 塘 every 7–10 li… with drainage, irrigation and navigation benefits." (translation)
- **Reading:** spacing of district waterways, not inter-pond channels.

**L. 广蚕桑说 (Qing), quoted in 智汇三农.** Archived: fb/fb250ee997ac.

- **Reading:** ditch-type evidence, but for Jiangnan mulberry land, not dike-ponds.

### The ring canal question

**Jia Dan (as in B):**
- 「於江之南北，為縱浦以通於江。又於浦之東西，為橫塘以分其勢而棋布之，有圩田之象焉。其塘浦，闊者三十餘丈，狹者不下二十餘丈…蓋欲取土以為堤岸」 = "North and south of the river lengthwise 浦 were cut to the river, east and west of them crosswise 塘, laid out like a chessboard, in the form of polders. The 塘浦 were 30-odd zhang at the widest, not under 20-odd at the narrowest… meaning to take their earth for the dike." (translation)
- 「使水行於外，田成於內」 = "so that the water ran outside and the fields lay inside." (translation)
- The old Kunshan 浦 traces: 「闊者二十餘丈，狹者十餘丈」 = 20-odd zhang wide at most, 10-odd at least (translation).
- By my conversion (Song zhang about 3.1 m), 30-odd zhang is roughly 90 m or more.
- **Reading:** ATTESTED ONLY FOR the bounding channels, outside the dike, on a straight planned grid. This describes what "the ancients" built, as seen about 1070.

**Shen Kuo (as in C):** interior 溝/澮 grid 「直曲相望」. His dike was 「博六丈，崇丈有二尺」 (six zhang broad, twelve chi high), 「夾堤之脊列植以桑」 (mulberry planted along both sides of its crest), 「為水門五」 (five water gates). He names no canal along the dike's foot.

**Fei Hsiao-tung, Peasant Life in China (1936, after 1912).** Archived: 67/675c43301bf7.
- "The land is divided up by streams into tiny pieces which are called yu. Each yu is surrounded by water."
- "A big trench has therefore to be constructed in the lowest part of the whole dish-shaped yu."
- "Along the margin of each yu, ten to thirty metres of land is left for plantation of mulberry trees."
- **Reading:** the drain is at the centre and the bounding water is natural stream. There is no ring at the inside foot.

**King, Pearl delta (as in E):** gate canals with head channels "at right angles", fields "in long straight lands".

## 2. Verdicts

**Main question: ATTESTED ONLY FOR "creeks that boats used, among which the ponds lay".** This is shown for:
- the Pearl delta: Qu Dajun, the Jiancun landings, and Chen's informants;
- Lake Tai and Jiangnan: Jia Dan's household 浜 (c.1070), Shen Kuo's boatable household 澮 (1060s), 沈氏農書's mud dredging and boat hauling (c.1640), and King (1909).

So 0018's "waterways the ponds lie among, not field ditches" stands.

What is NOT ATTESTED is a channel along every pond edge. Qu Dajun's chessboard has dikes for lines, and Tian's broad borders are dikes. That points toward ponds sharing banks, with creeks threading blocks of ponds. Read that way, 0019's "lattice of ditches" between ponds is the weaker sentence, because its lattice lines are drawn as water where the 1678 source has dike.

**Width: SILENT for a premodern channel between ponds.** The figures I found, each with what it measures:

| Figure | What it measures | Date |
|---|---|---|
| 6–8 ft | King's gate canals in diked delta cane and rice fields | 1909 |
| 22 / 19 ft | Average side canals off the Kashing–Hangchow route | 1909 |
| 6–8 m | Designed 毛支涌 | after 1998; does not count |
| 20–30+ zhang | 塘浦 bounding channels | c.1070 |

None supports 1.2–2.5 ft. The narrowest delta figure is 6–8 ft. For whether each pond had its own sluice or culvert, SILENT before modern times. The record's existing choice (sluices only in the polder's outer dike) is not contradicted.

**Second question: SILENT** on the form of a ring canal at the inside foot of the dike. No source names one:
- Jia Dan puts the bounding channels outside the dike, cut straight and chessboard-fashion.
- Fei (1936) puts the drain at the centre.
- Shen Kuo's interior is a ruled grid of 溝/澮, 「直曲相望」. The phrase admits both straight and bent stretches "by rule", so it supports neither "waver gently" nor "square corners" decisively.
- Only the 1950s dike-pond 河涌 are described as winding (Tian), and those are creeks, not a ring.

The engine's straight trunks and 0019's "drawing convention" are both unattested. The evidence leans to straight, planned bounding channels outside the dike.
