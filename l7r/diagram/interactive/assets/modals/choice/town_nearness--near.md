About: A settlement near its market town lay within that ring of rich manure. A market town sat in the middle of the countryside it served: in China the villages that traded at one town lay within
a walk of a few miles of it, the longest about 3 miles, and farmers came in on its market days to buy, sell and barter.
A second crop on the paddy, barley in the winter after the rice, needed twice the manure of one crop, and in medieval
times enough could be had only near densely settled districts. Night soil was heavy, and carrying it far cost too much:
around Kanazawa in the Edo period the fields within about 2.5 miles of the town got urine and abundant manure, those
further out cheaper fertilizers, and remote places bushes and grasses as well. Only a paddy that drained well could carry
the barley at all.

Guesses:
- Whether this settlement's market town is near or far: no record of the settlement gives the distance, so it is rolled
  with even odds.
- How a town's nearness and a settlement's drainage weigh against each other: both set the odds of a winter crop, but no
  source says how much each counts; a far town halves the odds the drainage gives, short of the medieval "none" for
  remote places, since bought fertilizer spread the second crop further in the Edo period.
