About: Where a settlement's households washed together, they shared a footing at the water's edge, on a channel
or a stream that passed the houses; what the footing was built of is not recorded. At Gujo Hachiman, whose canals were cut through the town early in the Edo period, the
shared washing places on the town channels are still used for washing clothes and vegetables, each channel with its
cleaning rota. In Fuji, in Saga, the washing places on the settlement's channels had rules for their use. At Harie, on Lake Biwa, where most houses had their own spring water, some of the spring-fed washing places outside the houses were open to neighbors too, and were valued as places to gather and talk.

How many households shared one, and how wide one was, are not recorded.

Guesses:
- That half of settlements wash at shared places like these and half at a step by each house: no account gives a share between them.
- One for every four households on a bank: no account counts a village's washing places.
- A footing about 4 ft wide: no account gives one's width.

Depiction: The map sets shared washing places on the bank of a stream or ditch that passes the houses,
one for every few households on that bank, each drawn as a small footing of steps down the bank. The steps are the
map's own: footings built for washing are known, but not their form or what they were built of.
