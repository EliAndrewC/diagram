About: A plank bridge (itabashi) was a bridge made of boards, a deck laid across small water. Plank bridges were centuries
old in China and Japan: a poem of China's Tang dynasty opens on someone crossing a plank bridge over a spring stream
beside a mountain farmer's house, and the Japanese word is first recorded in a poem of about 1310. What crossed a paddy
ditch, a plank, a single log or earth over logs, is not recorded.

A visitor crossed on a deck of boards laid from bank to bank.

Over Japan's rivers, until the Edo period, plank decks were kept to an important few, and earthen bridges over logs were
by far the commoner. How long or wide a village's plank crossing ran, and how often one stood along a ditch, are not
recorded.

Guesses:
- That a settlement's ditch crossings are all planked, at even odds with a single log and earth over logs: no count of
  the forms over a farm ditch was found, and the one proportion found, for river bridges, would make the planked deck far
  rarer than earth over logs.

Depiction: Every ditch crossing on the map takes this form, drawn as a timber deck with its seams running across it, so
the boards read as laid from bank to bank. Each is drawn narrow and about 8 ft long,
so it reads as a single-file crossing and not a road bridge.
