About: Where a channel ran past the houses, each house could wash at its own step on the water. At Harie, on Lake Biwa, most houses had their own spring water, and at each
house's kabata, its washing place on that water, the soil was washed off the vegetables, and in the Jiangnan water towns of China nearly every
riverside house had a small landing at its door, where the clothes and the vegetables were washed and the rice rinsed, and small boats could moor; washing, buying food and cooking could all be done at the riverside.

At Harie the kabata served for drinking and cooking as well as washing, and some outside the houses were open to others than the household and were places to gather and talk.

In Suzhou a house that backed onto a canal had a landing at its back with stone steps down to the water, used for boats, for drawing water and for washing. How a Japanese house's washing place was built, and how big one was, is not recorded.
