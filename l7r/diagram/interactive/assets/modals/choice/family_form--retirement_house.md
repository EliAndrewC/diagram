About: Moving out was the main form of retirement, and one form of it was the old couple alone leaving the farmhouse for a retirement
house (inkyoya) in a separate building. The custom was strong from the Pacific coast through the Inland Sea, above all in
Kyushu and Shikoku, and thin in the northeast and along the Sea of Japan, where the generations stayed under one roof. The two
households often kept separate meals and purses - one family living as two.

Most retirement houses stood inside the family's own house plot, with an entrance of their own: a second roof in
the homestead; what one was built of is not recorded. In one windswept village of Shiga, the retirement houses and storehouses stood to the west
and southwest of the house, sheltering the work yard before its door. A retirement house was small: in one village east of Edo, counted some years after the Edo period, its
eight retirement houses ran from 3 to 15 tsubo (about 110 to 540 sq ft), one of 9 tsubo, 3 ken square (about 18 ft square), being a
middling one; a samurai house's retirement house of 1820, in the Edo period, in Matsushiro, is about 25 by 15 ft (7.7 by 4.6 m).

Where the custom was kept thoroughly, every house had one. In that village, Kakimochi, 8 of its 16
households kept a retirement house. Across a whole region the share is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That between three and seven in ten of a settlement's farmsteads keep one: no regional share was found, and a household held a retired couple for only part of its life.
- Its size, about 18 by 15 ft, a room or two and an earth-floored entry: the record gives a range of sizes but no one plan, so the map draws one size inside that range, a little under the middling 9 tsubo.
- Where in the yard it stands, and how far from the farmhouse: no source gives a rule for the side beyond one windswept village's bearings, and none gives the distance.
- That a settlement keeps retirement houses or one roof at even odds: the sources say which form was commoner in which region, but none gives the share of households keeping each across a region.

Depiction: The map draws the retirement house as a small house in the same homestead as its farmhouse. It is a second roof of the same family, so it is
not counted as a household.
