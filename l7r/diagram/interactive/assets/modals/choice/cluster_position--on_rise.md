About: On low, wet ground a village took whatever rise the land offered. On a river's lowland the old villages stood on the
natural levee, a slight rise that drained well above the wet backswamp behind it, which they farmed as paddy. On the
flood-prone plain where the three Kiso rivers meet, farmers made their villages and fields from early on on the natural levees
and other slightly raised ground, and raised their own house plots on fill. In the delta at those rivers' mouth, where natural levees were few, the houses
crowded instead onto the highest dike inside the village's ring dike.

A visitor saw the houses standing a little above the paddy on their rise, the paddy spread out below them on the lower, wetter ground.

How high such a rise stood, how often a village sat on one, and how large it was, are not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a village may sit on a rise off a high corner of its fields, and how often each seat comes up: which ground was driest
  depended on the place, and no count of village seats was found.

Depiction: The map seats the cluster on the higher, drier ground beside its paddy, standing for a dry rise, and off any
drawn marsh; the rise itself is not drawn as raised ground.
