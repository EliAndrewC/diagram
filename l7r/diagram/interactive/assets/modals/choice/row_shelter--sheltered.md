About: In a row village with farms on both sides of its road, the farms on the lee side of the road could lean on the
grove row across it for their windbreak.

At Ogawa on the Musashino plateau, a farm south of its road grew its bamboo all along its south side and sold
it to basket makers: the row of farms with groves across the two roads to the north already broke the wind, so no bamboo was
needed on that side as a windbreak, its owners said. The farm still kept a grove north of its house, but a thin one, mostly
zelkova. At Minami-cho, on the old Yanagikubo new fields, another farm south of
its road had its bamboo on the south, likely because of the row of grove farms across the road.

Few farms seem to have done so. How deep the bamboo stood, and how far past the yard, is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a row village takes this form or every farm's own grove at even odds: both are attested, but the one survey that
  counts found only two farms of all it visited with their bamboo on the south side of the homestead, where this row's farms keep it, so even odds may draw this form
  more often than it stood.
- That a sheltered farm's windward grove is one tree deep: the account calls Ogawa's "rather thin" and gives no depth, though the same survey found another farm's grove on its north road a single line of white oak and zelkova.
- That a street sheltering its lee row runs within about 67 degrees of across the wind: no account gives an angle.
- The bamboo band's depth, 16 ft, and that it stands just past the yard's and beds' sun ground: no account gives either.

Depiction: The map draws the farms on the street's lee side with a thin grove, one tree deep, on their windward sides, and
each with its bamboo in a band
along its whole south side, true south whatever the map's wind, just past its yard's and beds' sun ground, where the
bamboo would shade neither; where that ground is taken the band is moved further south, and a farm with no room left
keeps none, as does every farm on a map whose farmsteads keep no bamboo (the title card says which). The farms on the street's windward side keep their groves. The band's bamboo is drawn as paired culm marks in jade over a light wash, because one culm is too small to show at the map's scale; the real stand was a single-species grove of timber bamboo some 33 to 66 ft tall.
