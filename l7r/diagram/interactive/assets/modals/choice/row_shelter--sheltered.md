About: On a row village with farms on both sides of its road, the farms on the lee side of the road could lean on the
row across it. At Ogawa on the Musashino plateau, a farm south of its road grew its bamboo all along its south side and sold
it to basket makers: the row of farms with groves across the road to the north already broke the wind, so no bamboo was
needed on that side as a windbreak, its owners said. At Minami-cho, on the old Yanagikubo new fields, another farm south of
its road had its bamboo on the south, which the survey ties to the row of grove farms across the road.

Guesses:
- That a row village takes this form or every farm's own grove at even odds: both are attested, and neither is counted
  against the other.
- That a sheltered farm keeps no grove of its own: the accounts say no bamboo was needed on the road's side, and this
  project reads that as no windbreak of the farm's own.
- That a street sheltering its lee row runs within about 67 degrees of across the wind: no account gives an angle.
- The bamboo's depth, a strip's 16 ft, and how far past the yard it stands: no account gives either.

Depiction: The map draws the farms on the street's lee side with no grove of their own, each with its bamboo in a band
along its whole south side, true south whatever the map's wind, just past its yard's and beds' sun ground, where the
bamboo would shade neither; where that ground is taken the band is moved further south, and a farm with no room left
keeps none. The farms on the street's windward side keep their groves.
