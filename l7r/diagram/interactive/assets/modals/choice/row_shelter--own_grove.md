About: In a row village with farms on both sides of its road, each farm could keep its own grove on its windward sides,
whichever side of the road it stood.

At Ogawa on the Musashino plateau, a farm settled in the Edo period had
its road on the north, and still kept its grove north of its house, mostly zelkova and rather thin. At Santome, a planned row on the same plateau, the homesteads were planted round with bamboo, oak and zelkova, the zelkova raised as building timber and cut only for the house's own need. In the Edo period,
a homestead's grove could cover roughly 6,000 to 28,000 sq ft; how big a row farm's grove was is not
recorded.

Guesses:
- That a row village gives every farm its own grove, or leaves its leeward row sheltered by the grove row across its road, at even odds: both are
  attested, but the one survey that counts found only two farms of all it visited with their bamboo on the south side of the homestead, as the farm sheltered by a grove row across its road kept it,
  so even odds may draw the sheltered row more often than it stood.
- The grove's size, its windward stand about one and a half house depths deep and any other planted side one tree deep:
  no record of a row farm's grove gives one, so a row farm's grove is drawn as every farm's grove is.

Depiction: The map draws every farm of the row with its own grove on its windward sides, on both sides of the road.
