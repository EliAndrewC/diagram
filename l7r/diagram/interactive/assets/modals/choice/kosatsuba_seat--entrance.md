About: A notice board (kosatsuba) was the wooden frame on which the shogunate and the domains posted standing law - the ban on
Christians, the rules on coin, fire and packhorse charges - so that everyone would know it. The shogunate ordered boards set at
the entrances and centers of towns and villages, and every village of the Edo period, more than 60,000 of them, had a site
for one. A village's way in was one of the places its board stood, with its center and the ground before a village official's gate,
and the post town of Ogose kept its board in its lower town, at its southern entrance.

The boards measured at two post towns were frames about 10 ft high and 16 to 18 ft along the road; the one rebuilt at Kanagawa
stands on a stone base inside a fence, under a roof, its notices hung high above the passers-by, even in two tiers. A village's board could be built the same way, but none was
measured. Every site known was on the road itself - a verge, a gate's front, the foot of a
bridge - and none seems to have stood set back on open ground.

How many villages kept their board at the way in, rather than at the center or before an official's gate, is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a village's board was the size of a post town's: no village board was measured.
- How often a settlement's board stands at its way in, its center or an official's frontage: no source counts them.

Depiction: Only a settlement with a way in from outside takes this seat. The map seats the board last, once everything else
stands, near where the way out leaves the houses, where every household passes on its way out, a few feet off the way's edge
and turned broadside to it. It is drawn as a roofed frame on a stone footing inside a fence. On a village or city map a frame the size of those measured at two post towns, about 16 ft by 6 ft, would be too small to see, so the map draws a larger marker in its place, footing and all, and its label says what it is. A board may stand under a tree, and its label is drawn above every crown, so it shows where the board is even when a crown hides it.
