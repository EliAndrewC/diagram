About: A farming household of the later Edo period averaged about five, across two or three generations, and the ordinary household had become a stem
family: the parents living with one married heir and the heir's children. In this form the generations stayed together
under the farmhouse's one roof when the old couple handed the farm to their heir, sharing one house, one table and one purse.
That was the common way along the Sea of Japan coast and in the northeast, where the large family held together; elsewhere
the retired couple often moved out to a house of their own in the yard.

On the ground nothing marked it: one farmhouse stood for each household, its old couple living under the same roof as their heir's
family.

How common the one-roof form was, against the retirement house, is not recorded across a whole region; in one village counted in
1885, 8 of its 16 households kept a retirement house.

Guesses:
- That a settlement keeps its old couples under one roof or in retirement houses at even odds: no source weighs the two forms against each other.

Depiction: The map draws one farmhouse for each household, and no retirement house in its yard.
