About: A farm's grove on all four sides ringed the house completely. On the Izumo plain, before 1868, the pine groves went the
whole way round the house; the pines had been planted in the first place to strengthen an earth bank built round the house
against floods. As flood damage lessened and building changed after 1868, the ring gave way to a hook covering only the north
and west. Early in the Edo period, an order in the Takada domain had farmers plant cedar around the homestead, but camellia and bamboo grass on
its south side, so the cedars need not have closed a ring.

In Okinawa, in villages thought to have been laid out in the 1700s, a line of fukugi, an evergreen, surrounds each house, its large trees thickest on the north against the monsoon and next on the east against the typhoons.

So a ringed farm stood behind a wall of trees on every side - on the Izumo plain, pines on the bank round the house. Where its
way in broke the ring, and how wide, is not recorded.

How many farms elsewhere kept a full ring is not recorded; in the Edo period, three households' woods covered about 6,000, 10,700
and 28,000 sq ft.

Guesses:
- That a settlement's groves take all four sides twice in ten, rising to four times in ten on flood-prone ground: no source
  counts farms by the shape of their grove, and the rise follows the Izumo ring's stated cause.
- That the sides away from the wind are a band of lesser trees one tree deep, carrying the Tonami pattern round the ring: no
  source gives the depth of a ring's lee sides, and on the Izumo plain the pines went the whole way round; but an order of 1625 in the Takada domain set camellia and bamboo grass on the south, and in Okinawa, where a line of trees surrounds each house, fewer large trees are recorded on the south than on the north and east.
- How deep the windward stand runs, about one and a half house depths: no source gives a grove's depth, so it is reckoned
  from tree counts and crown sizes.
- That the ring is broken once, at the middle of its front, for a way in about 36 ft wide: a farm must be reached, but no
  source says where in a ring its opening stood or how wide it was.

Depiction: Every farm in the settlement takes the same shape of grove, since the shape went by region. The map draws each tree
at its real size, but only its top layer: no tree is drawn under another's crown, though neighbors' crowns overlap at their edges so the stand reads as closed, where a real grove had lesser growth beneath its tall trees. It draws the windward sides as a deep stand and the others as a thinner band one tree deep; no tree stands in the
yard's or the kitchen garden's sun, so a thin band beside the house closes only the house's own side and the drawn ring stands open beside the yard and the bed, where a real ring went the whole way round; the ring is opened at its front for the way in. Where the farms keep bamboo (the title card says whether), it shows as a patch of paired culm marks on the house side of each windward stand: one culm is too small to see at this scale, so the mark, taken from modern map legends, stands for bamboo that grew among the grove's lesser plants. About one plant in twelve of a windward stand is bamboo too, and the same mark shows it only between the crowns and along the stand's edge: the map draws the grove from above, so bamboo under a crown is hidden, as it filled the low part of a real grove.
