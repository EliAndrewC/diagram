About: A clustered village's shape followed its ground, its water and its roads. In a river's lowland the old villages stood
on the natural levee, the slight, dry rise beside the stream, with the paddy on the low ground behind, and some strung
their houses out in a line along it; in the hills of southwest Zhejiang, in China, villages stretched along their rivers and
roads. A lane along the water runs beside the stream, with the houses along it.

The lane was a strip of bare earth, trodden hard and muddy in rain, made for feet, carrying poles and the odd barrow or
packhorse rather than carts; heavy loads went by water where they could. How wide a village lane ran is not recorded; the
one old figure for a field road is about 3 ft.

What shapes a village's lanes took, and how common each was, is not recorded.

- That a village's lanes took one of a few shapes - a spine, a T, a Y, a crossroads or a lane along the water: no description of the shapes a village's lanes took was found.
