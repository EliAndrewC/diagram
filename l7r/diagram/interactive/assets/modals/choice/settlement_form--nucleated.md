About: Most farming villages in Japan were clustered, the shūson: the farmhouses gathered in one limited area and the
fields lay around them. A village stood on dry ground beside its paddy. On a river's lowland the old villages stood on
the natural levee, a slight rise that drained well above the wet ground behind it; a lump of houses grew at the foot of a
hill or plateau, on a river terrace, or where two highways crossed.

What a visitor saw differed by region. Around the old capital and the Inland Sea, villages were dense, farmhouses behind
walls standing side by side; in the Kanto and the Tohoku they were loose, each house on a wide lot with its own grove, the
houses set well apart. Two clustered villages could look nothing alike, because each took its shape from its own site, its
density and its roads.

The clustered village was the general form in Japan, the scattered village a regional one, but no count or share of either
is recorded for any period. A clustered village's houses always stood closer together than a scattered village's, though by
no fixed measure, and how far apart they stood before 1868 is not recorded.

Guesses:
- How often a settlement is clustered: half the settlements on the maps, against three in ten scattered and two in ten in a
  row. No share of the forms was found for any period; the clustered village was the general form, so the true share was
  higher, and the odds are kept flatter so the rarer forms appear at all.

Depiction: The map gathers the houses together on the dry ground at the edge of the fields, each house reached by a
lane, and shelters the houses behind a belt of trees along their windward edge rather than a grove at each farm, a belt that runs on past the edge of the map where it meets it;
clumps of trees, the dooryard copse, stand in the gaps between the farms. The houses are drawn as a compact cluster,
whatever its shape, never strung out in a wide, hollow arc that leaves houses stranded from the fields; a village is held to a least packing, which a hamlet is not; how densely
a real village was built is not recorded. The title card says where this cluster sits and what
shape it takes.
