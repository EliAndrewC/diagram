About: Many row villages followed a line the land itself gave. Japanese geography names the linear village, strung along a
river's natural levee or the spring line at a hill's foot, and a row could follow springs and waterways too. On a river
plain the old settlements stood first on the levee, a slight rise that drained well above the wet backswamp behind it; on
land reclaimed from the sea the farmhouses lined the dike, and round a drained lake they ran along the old shore. On the
Shuso plain of Iyo, villages lined up in an arc at the foot of an alluvial fan, where the water that sank into it rose again
as springs. In the hills of southwest Zhejiang, in China, villages tended to grow along their rivers in the same way.

Such a row bent with its ground: one pictured on a levee runs in a curve beside a river's old channel. The levee carried the
village's dry fields as well as its houses, the paddy lying on the low ground behind; on reclaimed ground a farm's land lay
near the house and fairly compact.

How far apart a levee or dike row's houses stood, how long such a row ran, and whether this line or a road laid first was the
commoner are not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a row follows the dry edge of its ground or a street laid first at even odds, away from flood-prone ground: both are
  attested, and neither is counted against the other.
- That a farm on the dry edge holds its land one lot deep beside the house, and keeps its grove on its windward sides as a
  planned row's farms did: no levee or dike row's lots or groves were measured.

Depiction: The map runs the row along the field's dry margin, which stands for a levee or a fan's foot, since the map knows
a field's margin and not a levee's line; the street curves with it, kept a little off the field so that it is not on a bund yet the field still reads as the row's own. On low ground behind dikes, or where the houses stand on
a dike, every row takes this form, standing for the dike. The farms stand lot against lot along it, and where they stand on
both sides, each farm across the street from the paddy has a compact holding of dry field drawn behind it.
