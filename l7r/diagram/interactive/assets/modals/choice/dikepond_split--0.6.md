About: In this village a parcel was about six parts water to four of planted bank. A dike-pond village dug each parcel into a
fish pond and heaped the spoil into the bank round it, where mulberry grew.

How a parcel was split between water and bank may have differed by region: six parts water to four of dike, six parts dike to four
of pond, and seven of water to three of dike all seem to have been used.

Guesses:
- That a village takes each of the three splits at even odds: no account gives a share between them, and every account
  found that writes the split as a ratio is modern.
- That one split holds for the whole village: the accounts give the split for dike-ponds in general, never parcel by
  parcel.

Depiction: The map sets every pond in the village in from its parcel's edge by the same distance, so that a parcel of the
village's middling size is six parts water to four of planted bank, a smaller parcel a little drier and a larger one a little
wetter; the planted bank fills the ring between the pond and the canal.
