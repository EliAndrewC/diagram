About: A dike-pond village dug each parcel into a fish pond and heaped the spoil into the bank round it, where mulberry grew.
How the parcel was split between water and bank is written down both ways round - six parts water to four of dike, and six
parts dike to four of pond - and as seven of water to three of dike besides; every one of these figures comes from modern
writing. This village keeps seven parts of each parcel water to three of planted bank.

Guesses:
- That a village takes each of the three splits at even odds: no account gives a share between them.
- That one split holds for the whole village, each parcel's pond set in from its edge by the same distance: parcels of
  different sizes run a little drier or wetter than the split.

Depiction: The map sets each pond in from its parcel's edge so that a parcel of the village's middling size is seven parts of each parcel water to three of planted bank; the
planted bank fills the ring between the pond and the canal.
