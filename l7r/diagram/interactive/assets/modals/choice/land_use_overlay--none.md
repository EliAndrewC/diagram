About: A village on rice land in China or Japan could give part of its ground to a crop grown for sale - fish ponds, lotus or
tea - or keep all of it in rice. Each cash crop needed ground of its own: ponds and lotus the low, wet hollows, tea the dry
hillside above the rice. Lotus districts were found in some places, not everywhere, and most of the time even a cash crop
took only part of a village while the rest stayed in rice.

In a village without one every paddy stood in rice. A polder village of this kind could still
grow mulberry, not in its paddies but along the raised margin of the polder that served as its dike, as in one village by
Lake Tai in the 1930s, where rice remained the main crop.

How many villages grew no cash crop on their rice land is not recorded.

Guesses:
- How often a settlement grows no cash crop rather than ponds, lotus or tea: no source counts villages with and without one,
  so the choices come up evenly where the ground allows them.
