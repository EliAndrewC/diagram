About: Two stone pillars set on the border line, one north and one south of the parley room, so that both sides
at a parley can read the line on the ground. They mark the border; they do not ward it, as the Fox Clan's vermilion threshold stones ward the Imperial road.

Earth-mound boundary markers were common under the Tokugawa, like the hundred and more that Morioka and Sendai
raised along their border after agreeing it in 1642, large and far apart at first and smaller and closer together later.
What a boundary pillar of the period looked like is not recorded.

Guesses:
- A pillar's size, a shaft of about 1 to 1.5 ft, up to about 3 ft with its plinth: no source gives one, so it is an estimate.

Depiction: The plan draws the border as a dashed line with no width, as the period's provincial maps drew their district
boundaries as lines, and each pillar as a small stone-gray block standing on the line itself. The earth mounds that marked a real
border are left out on purpose: a mound is a structure the rest of the plan would have to stand clear of, and the magistracy
stands its east wall on the line.
