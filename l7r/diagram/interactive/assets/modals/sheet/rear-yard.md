About: The rear yard was the open ground behind a samurai house, between the house and the household's storehouses, apart from the house's
formal garden. The formal garden took the sunny south side of the grounds, before the
reception rooms, and a storehouse could stand behind the house, beyond the yard, as one did at the Higuchi house at
Matsushiro, though at another, at Kakunodate, one stood just inside the gate.

What the yard's surface was like, and what work was done on it, are not recorded. The storehouses beyond it were small,
about 12 by 18 ft being the common size, and a middle-rank house at Matsushiro kept two.

How large the yard was is not recorded. It lay within the house's walled grounds, which at a district magistrate's house
at Matsushiro in the Edo period ran to about a thousand tsubo (about 0.8 acre), round a house of about 1,740 sq ft, with about half the grounds in vegetable field.

Guesses:
- The yard's size on the map: how large the yard was is not recorded, so the plan keeps it no wider than its use.

Depiction: The plan leaves the ground between the house and its storehouses open and names it, drawn as the court's own
ground. It is never drawn as a wide empty band behind the house: like every open stretch of the compound, it is kept
no wider than its use.
