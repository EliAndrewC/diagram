About: A small altar to the kami of the wood, kept in Ubame's Inari shrine beside Inari's own altar. It is public - anyone may
pray at it - but a private hand keeps it up, and no one from the Ministry of Rites stands behind it. It is kept small and tidy.
A second kami could share a shrine's hall or be given a small shrine of its own on the same ground; Ubame's shares the hall.

An altar below the rank of a shrine, a hokora, had no torii, or only a very small one. The nearest kin on record, a household's
shrine to the god of its own ground, was most often a small shrine of stone or of wood; one measured stone shrine is about 16 in
(40 cm) wide, deep and high.

Depiction: The plan draws the altar as a small square inside the shrine hall, about 5 ft across, larger than life so that it can
be seen, in plain wood color rather than a shrine's vermilion, beside Inari's torii mark. Whether the wood's kami shares
Inari's hall or keeps a shrine of its own is picked at random for the plan, and it came out sharing the hall.

Name: wood-kami altar
