About: A small altar to the kami of the wood, kept in Ubame's Inari shrine. It is public - anyone may
pray at it - but a private hand keeps it up, and no one from the Ministry of Rites stands behind it. It is kept small and tidy.
A second kami could share a shrine's hall or be given a small shrine of its own on the same ground.

What this altar looks like is not recorded. An altar below the rank of a shrine, a hokora, had no torii, or only a very small one. The nearest kin the record offers, a household's
shrine to the god of its own ground, was most often a small shrine of stone or of wood; one measured stone shrine is about 16 in
(40 cm) wide, deep and high.

Guesses:
- That the wood's kami shares Inari's hall rather than keeping a shrine of its own: which it did is not recorded, so the plan
  picked one at random.

Depiction: The plan draws the altar as a small square inside the shrine hall, about 5 ft across, larger than life so that it can
be seen, in plain wood color rather than a shrine's vermilion, beside Inari's torii mark. It is drawn sharing
Inari's hall, which is a guess.
