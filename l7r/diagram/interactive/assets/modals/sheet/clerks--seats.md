About: The clerks' seats were the places in the hall above the hearing court where the clerks sat during a hearing, not on the court floor below, where officers crouched at either side and the commoner parties knelt on straw mats.

At
Edo's South Town Magistracy in the early 1800s, the court was a stepped hall whose top level, a tatami room, was divided
into three: the magistrate sat in the innermost room, the examining officer and the clerk in the middle one, and a
witnessing officer in the room nearest the court. Some magistracies set an upper level apart for the examiners and the
scribes, below the magistrate's own room; others did not.

The middle room was matted with tatami like the magistrate's; a board veranda ran along the whole front of the matted level, beyond the witnessing officer's room.
At Edo one clerk sat in the middle room, on its tatami; how large a clerk's place was is not recorded.

Guesses:
- The size of the seats the map draws, about 27 by 7 ft each: no account gives how much room a clerk's place took.

Depiction: Our maps seat the clerks to either side of the magistrate, level with the dais, rather than in the middle room, with the examining officer,
in front of the magistrate's, as at Edo - a simplification.
