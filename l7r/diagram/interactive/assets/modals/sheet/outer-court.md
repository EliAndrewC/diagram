About: The outer court was the public half of a magistrate's compound, the ground just inside the main gate. Whoever
came on business - villagers bringing suits and petitions, the accused brought in to be judged - crossed it to the office hall's hearing courts. In
China a county office was laid out so by regulation, the office in front and the residence behind an inner gate; in
Japan the household lived inside the compound too, though at the one intendant's office that survives, at Takayama, the
residence stood beside the office rather than behind it.

It was open ground: at Joge a small office's entrance faced the open ground inside its main gate, and a Chinese
governor-general's office planted its front court with dozens of stout junipers. At Edo's South Town Magistracy a stone path about 6 ft wide ran straight from the main gate to the entrance, with small black gravel spread over the ground beside it; how such ground was surfaced elsewhere is not recorded.

On it stood the office hall with its roofed
hearing court, and around a compound's courts its storehouses and service buildings. A Chinese county office ran its
courts one after another; at Neixiang a visitor going north from the main gate crossed two rectangular courts in turn.

How large the outer court itself was is not recorded. At Takayama, the one intendant's office that survives, most of the compound lay open: the floors of all its buildings came to about three-tenths of its site without the plaza in front of the office, and the
plaza itself was about an eighth of the whole designated site, some 15,000 of about 120,000 sq ft. The smallest Japanese office
known, a branch post of three officials at Joge, stood on a site of about 230 by 90 ft.

Guesses:
- That buildings cover up to about 42% of a compound's ground: only Takayama's site and buildings are measured, at about
  30% in floor area, and no other office's site and buildings were found measured.
- That the forecourt and the hearing court together take about 12 to 18% of the site: no measure of them was found.
- That the ground is bare earth: the ground before an office's entrance was found surfaced only at Edo's South Town Magistracy, a stone path with gravel beside it, and nowhere was it found recorded as left bare earth.

Depiction: The plan draws the outer court as the compound's bare earth, labeled with its name and, in smaller type, "forecourt". It is
always drawn in front of the inner court, with a gate between them; for a Japanese office that is a simplification, since
at Takayama the residence stood beside the office. The plan keeps its buildings a little further apart than a real jin'ya (an intendant's office compound),
so that each reads as its own labeled footprint; like a real one, it draws the office hall as one building, its offices and the magistrate's dais under one roof with the roofed hearing court against its front, and leaves the rest of the ground open.

Name: outer court
Covers: the outer court's ground and its labels, the forecourt among them
