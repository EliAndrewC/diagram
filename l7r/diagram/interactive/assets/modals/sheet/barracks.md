About: The barracks was the rowhouse (nagaya) where an office's junior staff lodged. A rural intendant's office kept the
huts and rowhouses of its junior officials on its own grounds, and Takayama's rowhouse for its storehouse keepers
survives, restored; who else of such an office's staff lived inside is not recorded, though at Omori a mid-ranking official lived in a house of their own in the town. Edo's great town
magistracy housed its constables apart, in a district of their own, and the lower warriors who came to Edo with their
lord lived in rowhouses inside his residence.

A rowhouse was a long, narrow building of one story, divided along its length into small dwellings, each entered by a
door of its own: an earth-floored entry for leaving shoes, then a raised plank floor. A country jin'ya's rows could be thatched, as Sayama's were, and so was a foot soldiers' row built in 1842,
and a gate rowhouse tiled; one row has earth walls as it stands today, and what an office's staff row was walled and framed with is not recorded. A standard dwelling was about 9 by 12 ft. No bed or bunk is known in one before modern times.
The servants below the foot soldiers lived instead in large common rooms.

The rowhouses measured ran about 80 to 145 ft long and 12 to 24 ft deep: that row of 1842 held eight households, and a
foot soldier's dwelling in another was about 12 by 21 ft. Takayama's has a floor of about 3,700 sq ft. At Sakai's town magistracy each constable had a dwelling of their own, about 12 by 18 ft, while three
retainers on duty in Edo shared one dwelling of their domain's rowhouse; how many unmarried men usually shared one is not
recorded.
