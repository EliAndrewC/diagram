About: The gatehouse was the guardroom where the gatekeepers kept the main gate and watched who came and went; even a
branch office of three officials had its gatekeepers' room by the main gate. At a samurai residence the gatekeepers lived
there too: at Matsue the gate range housed the gatekeepers and the household's menservants.

The gatekeepers sat in one of two places. One was a room of the gate range (nagaya-mon), the long building the gate
passes through, under the range's one unbroken roof: at the Kashiwara domain's seat at Kaibara the guardroom is a room of
eight tatami mats (about 140 sq ft) with an earth floor beside it, and the Matsue gate range had lookout windows onto the street. The other
was a gatehouse of its own: at Takayama the gate and the gatekeepers' house stand apart, each
a gabled building of one story. What their walls were made of is not recorded.

Gate ranges ran long: Kaibara's is about 81 by 12 ft, with rooms on both sides of the gate, and the one at
the intendant's office at Omori about 108 by 13 ft. A guardroom of its
own measured at the temple Kita-in is a small gabled building of about 18 by 12 ft, and Takayama's gatekeepers' house
has a floor of about 400 sq ft. Kaibara's range and the Kita-in guardroom were about 12 ft deep, but a large gate range could be deeper and taller: one in Edo from the 1860s is about 72 by 15 ft, two stories, with a guardroom on its face. How many gatekeepers kept a gate is not recorded.

Guesses:
- The length of a gatehouse of its own, about 40 ft: no account gives the length of a magistracy's gatehouse, so it is set
  about a fifth longer than Takayama's gatekeepers' house, whose floor of about 400 sq ft at the 12 ft depth of the one
  gatehouse of its own that was measured gives about 33 ft.

Depiction: The plan draws the gatekeepers in one of the two places, a gatehouse beside the main gate or a guardroom in
the gate range; each plan takes one. A gatehouse of its own is drawn about 40 by 12 ft, and a gate range about 12 ft deep.
