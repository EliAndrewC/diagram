About: The day office was the room of the office hall where the office's own officials did its daily business.
At Takayama, the one intendant's office that survives, the officials sent by the shogunate worked in
such a room, with the room of the officials hired mostly from the district partitioned off beside it; documents for the
shogunate were drawn up in a room kept for writing and bound in the room beside it. The working rooms were most
likely one block with the hall where cases were heard.

Takayama's office hall was a single-story timber building roofed with wooden shingles, its floor about 8,100 sq ft for
a whole province; a county magistrate's hall was most likely smaller. An intendant governed with about ten retainers and
a few local hires; what the working rooms looked like inside, how large they were and how many worked in each is not
recorded. The day office was a place of work, not of lodging: an intendant's staff lived in huts and rowhouses
elsewhere in the compound.

Guesses:
- That the day office stood in the back row behind the dais band, in the same hall: no account places such a room behind
  the court, and which room stood directly behind the magistrate's seat at an intendant's office is not recorded (at Edo's
  North Town Magistracy, on a plan of 1841, it was a room whose name marks it as the examiners' working room, though no account states its use); it is reckoned from what a working office had to hold.
- The day office's size on the plan: no account gives how large such a room was, so it is drawn to share the hall's
  back row with the clerks' room, the official study and the other working rooms.

Depiction: The plan draws the day office as a room of the office hall's back row, behind the dais band, between the clerks' room and the
official study, its floor drawn in the hall's own color.
