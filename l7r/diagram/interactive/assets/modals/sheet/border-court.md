About: The border court is a receiving court on a magistracy that stands on a clan border. In Rokugan it is where a
delegation of rank from across the border steps in through a door of its own and is received with ceremony before it is
shown in. It is kept apart from the hearing court, where the accused kneel, because those who come here are guests.

It rests on two real things. In the Edo period a domain kept guard posts where its roads crossed into a neighbor's land,
to watch the people and goods passing and sometimes to tax them; at Nuruyu, one of the few whose buildings survive, the
road ran in through the gate and past the inspection hall that faced it, so the ground between was where everyone
crossing passed. And at a house of standing a guest of rank did not step from the road into a room: there was the gate, then open ground a
palanquin could cross, then the step of the formal entrance. No real border post is known to have kept a court for
receiving a neighboring domain's officials; that use of it is the setting's own.

Guesses:
- That the court is a garden set with a stone lantern rather than open ground: no account puts a garden between the
  gate and a guest's formal entrance. The two drawings found of the ground inside a gate both show it open: Joge's,
  and a garden-book plate Morse reproduced in 1886, whose stone path runs through a second gateway to the entrance and
  which he dates only loosely, to Sakai "nearly two hundred years ago".
- What the court's ground is: no source read says the ground before a formal entrance was swept, raked, graveled,
  sanded or left bare earth before modern times, so the plan gives it no surface of its own.

Depiction: The plan draws the border court as a garden of its own, stippled and set with a stone lantern, inside the
guests' door and apart from the hearing court.
