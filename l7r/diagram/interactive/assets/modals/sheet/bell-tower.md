About: A bell tower, the shoro, was where a temple's great bell hung; in several Japanese towns the town's time bell, struck about every two hours, day and night, hung at a temple. Most temples were small ones of only a main hall and
a bell tower; only the large temples where many monks lived had the full plan of seven halls. In Rokugan the village's shrine
is kept by its country monk, much as a shrine with a Buddhist temple attached was kept in Japan before 1868, but whether a village
shrine kept by a monk had a bell is not recorded.

One early Muromachi bell tower, Ryosen-ji's in Nara, is a single bay square under a hip-and-gable roof, its lower story
boarded in and flared outward like a skirt. What an ordinary temple's bell tower was built of, and whether its bell story
stood open, is not recorded. Over the centuries the bell tower lost its fixed place in a temple's plan, and
some temples joined it to the gate.

A bell tower ran from a single bay square upward: one village temple's, in the years after 1868, was 1 ken (about 6 ft)
square, and the drum tower of a Beijing temple, built in the same form as its bell tower, is about 23 ft square. How big a
village temple's was before 1868 is not recorded.
