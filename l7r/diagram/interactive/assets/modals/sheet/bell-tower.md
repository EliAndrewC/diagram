About: A bell tower, the shoro, was where a temple's great bell hung. Most temples were small ones of only a main hall and
a bell tower; only the large temples where many monks lived had the full plan of seven halls. In Rokugan the village's shrine
is kept by its country monk, much as a shrine with a Buddhist temple attached was kept in Japan before 1868, but whether such
a shrine had a bell is not recorded.

One early Muromachi bell tower, Ryosen-ji's in Nara, is a single bay square under a hip-and-gable roof, its lower story
boarded in and flared outward like a skirt. Over the centuries the bell tower lost its fixed place in a temple's plan, and
some temples joined it to the gate.

A bell tower ran from a single bay square upward: one district's register of its temples lists one 1 ken (about 6 ft)
square, and the drum tower of a Beijing temple, built in the same form as its bell tower, is about 23 ft square. How big a
village temple's was is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a country shrine has a bell tower only where its plan calls for one, none by default: no account says whether a
  village shrine kept by a monk had its bell.
