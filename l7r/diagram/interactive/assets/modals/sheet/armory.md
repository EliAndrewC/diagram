About: The armory was where a magistracy kept its arms. A Chinese office counted it among the stores it could not do without, and kept one even in a county with no standing
barracks: Pingyao's county office in China had none under either of its last two dynasties, yet it kept
an armory, which its war office guarded. When that office was rebuilt,
its great hall was raised at five bays, the west end bay the armory and the east end bay the store for money and grain.
In Japan the gate range of a small domain's seat at Kaibara held a store of arms, on the same side as a horse-viewing room, across the gate
from the guardroom.

An armory stood in one of two places: in the office hall itself, or in the range of rooms the main gate passes through.
Kaibara's was a room of eight mats, about 12 ft square; how large Pingyao's bay was is not recorded, nor what an armory was built of, how many arms it held or how they were racked.

Guesses:
- Its size on the plan, 14 by 20 ft: no account measures Pingyao's bays, and the one store of arms measured, Kaibara's eight-mat room of about 12 ft square, stood in a gate range rather than an office hall.

Depiction: The plan draws the armory as a room at the west end of the office hall's back row, beside the clerks' room,
its floor drawn in the hall's own color.
