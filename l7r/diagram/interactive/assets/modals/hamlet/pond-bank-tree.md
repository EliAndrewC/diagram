About: A Song-dynasty Chinese farming manual tells a farmer who digs a pond to plant its bank sparsely with mulberry and
cudrania, so that cattle can be tied there in the shade; the cattle tread the bank firm, and the trees grow well on the
pond's water. The same pond's bank could also be left bare, as most ponds' banks are kept today, mown and burned so that no
tree weakens them.

[pond_bank=planted] This hamlet's tail pond has the planted bank: a few mulberry and cudrania trees stand on it, beyond the
reeds at the shore.

[pond_bank=bare] This hamlet's tail pond has the bare bank.

How many trees a planted bank carried, and how far apart, is not recorded.

Guesses:
- Which of the two banks a pond has, at even odds: no account gives a share between them.
- [pond_bank=planted] One tree about every 30 ft of bank: the manual says only "sparsely".
- [pond_bank=planted] A crown about 12 ft across: no account gives the trees' size on a bank.
- [pond_bank=planted] Mulberry and cudrania in turn round the bank: the manual names both, not their order.

Depiction: The map draws each tree as a round green crown on the bank beyond the reeds, the cudrania a shade darker than the
mulberry. A tree is left out wherever its crown would fall on a roof, a wellhead or the sun of a yard or a bed, or its trunk on
a lane, the crop or the water, so a bank crowded by the farms carries fewer trees, or none.
