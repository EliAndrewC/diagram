About: A village on a river plain cut its grass on the river's floodplain. Beyond its fields a village kept common grass
ground, the magusaba, "fodder ground", for its animals' fodder, for thatch and for the green manure its paddies needed. In
hill country that ground was the grass hill, the kusayama; on the Kanto plain a river's floodplain served instead, and
villages whose own fodder ground ran short as new fields were opened came to depend on the floodplain of the Arakawa river.

Depiction: Where the map shows a river flat and no hills, it draws the open ground past the fields as meadow: grass with the
odd brush dot, repeated across the ground as one small block with no outline, the way a map's legend shows a kind of ground,
and no pines, since the scraggly pines belong to a grass hill's scrub. Like the hill's scrub it runs as one band round the
settlement and off the edge of the map, the real ground being far larger than the map.
