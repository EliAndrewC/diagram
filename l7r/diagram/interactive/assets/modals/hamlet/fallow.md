About: A resting paddy (kataarashi) was a rice plot left out of crop for a year while the plots around it were worked. In
the late Heian and early medieval periods, where water ran short or soil was thin, a village chose anew each year
which plots to work and which to rest, to rest the soil and to fit the planting to the water. Resting ground was not
abandoned ground. In Rokugan a paddy once made can be cropped for centuries without tiring its soil, so a plot
rests, if at all, for want of water, and resting paddy is the rarer case.

A resting plot lay in among the worked ones, not in a block of its own. Nothing marked it off; while it rested it
was common grazing for the village's cattle and horses. What grew on it while it rested is not recorded. Like its
neighbors it was one paddy plot, in the 1600s about a tenth of an acre to under half an acre.

In the late Heian period only about three-fifths of the paddy was worked in an average year. In 1102 one estate in
Tanba province worked about 110 of its 220 acres of paddy; about 64 acres rested that year. From the Kamakura period
on, better water works and fields in the advanced farming regions ended the need, and the nucleated villages worked
the same ground every year. By the early modern period, when land surveys taxed paddy by area, resting had ended.
