About: A washing place was a footing at the water's edge where a household washed its clothes and its vegetables. In Japan
villages and towns built them on a channel, a stream or a spring-fed tank, shared by the neighbors on the water or set at a
single house; in China's canal towns the washing place was the house's own landing at its door, in Suzhou with stone steps down to the water.

[washing_form=shared] This settlement's are shared: a footing at the edge of the stream or the ditch that passes
its houses, used by the households near it. At Gujo Hachiman such a shared washing place was called a kawado, and the town's channels
still have theirs in use for washing, each channel with its cleaning rota.

[washing_form=each_house] This settlement's are each a house's own: a footing at the water by
the house. At Harie, on Lake Biwa, most houses had their own spring water and their own kabata, the place for all the household's water work, where the soil was washed off the vegetables, and in
the Jiangnan water towns nearly every riverside house had a small landing at its door.

What Japan's footings were built of, how many a village kept, and how wide one was, are not recorded.

Guesses:
- Which of the two forms a settlement takes, at even odds: no account gives a share between them.
- [washing_form=shared] One for every four households on a bank: no account counts a village's washing places.
- A footing of steps about 4 ft wide and 6 ft down the bank: no account gives one's form or size.
- That the water passes the houses within about 120 ft: no account says how far a household walked to wash.

Depiction: The map draws a washing place as a small pale footing at the water's edge, its steps marked across it so it reads as a footing; what Japan's footings were built of, and whether they were stepped, is not recorded, though Suzhou's house landings had stone steps down to the water. The footing is
turned to the bank on the households' side; it is set where nothing else stands, so a footing whose spot a lane, a bridge
or a building takes is moved along the bank. A footing too small to read at the map's full view still shows when the map is
zoomed in.
