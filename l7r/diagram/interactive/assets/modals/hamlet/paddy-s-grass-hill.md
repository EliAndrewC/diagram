About: A small grass hill was sometimes set aside for one paddy's sole use, its green manure cut there and nowhere else; it
was called a tazuke kusayama. A village's paddies needed far more grass than their own bunds gave: the grass and young shoots
for green manure were cut in early spring and plowed straight into the fields, and a farm record of 1854 shows about two
horse-loads put on every tan of paddy. Most of that grass came from the village's common grass ground, the grass hills past
the fields; a grass hill kept for one paddy was the exception, held apart from the commons. How large such a hill was is not
recorded.

Guesses:
- That about one hamlet in three keeps one: the record says only that it was done sometimes.
- That it is about the size of one paddy plot: no record gives its size.

Depiction: The map draws the paddy's grass hill as a patch of open grass, with the odd brush dot and no pine, standing on the
slope just above one paddy at the top of the field, where the scrub of the common grass hills keeps out of it, so it reads as
a cut patch on the scrubby slope. Like the commons it is one small block repeated across its ground, with no outline.
