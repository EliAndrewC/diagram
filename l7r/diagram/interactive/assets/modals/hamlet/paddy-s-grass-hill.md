About: A small grass hill was sometimes set aside for one paddy's sole use, its grass cut for that paddy alone; it
was called a tazuke kusayama.

A village's paddies needed far more grass than their own bunds gave: the grass and young shoots
for green manure were cut in early spring and plowed straight into the fields, and on one farm in the late Edo period about two
horse-loads went on every tan (about a quarter acre) of paddy. Most of that grass came from the village's common grass ground, the grass hills past
the fields; a grass hill kept for one paddy was the exception, held apart from the commons.

Grass hills were cleared of their trees and kept for their grass, though their cover ran from open grass to a thin pine wood about 10 to 13 ft tall. This one's area, and where it lay against its paddy, are not
recorded.

Guesses:
- That about one hamlet in three keeps one: the record says only that it was done sometimes.
- That it is about the size of one paddy plot: no record gives its size.

Depiction: The map draws the paddy's grass hill as a patch of open grass, with the odd brush dot and no pine, standing on the
slope just above one paddy at the top of the field. Like the commons it is one small block repeated across its ground, with no outline.
