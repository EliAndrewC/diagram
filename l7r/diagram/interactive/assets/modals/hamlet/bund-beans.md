About: Bund beans (azemame) were soybeans grown along the tops of the paddy bunds. A household sowed them in a row on the
bund after the rice was transplanted and harvested them at the same time as the rice, so the narrow ridge that held the
water in also bore a crop. They were grown to eat - in Masaki, in Ehime, made into tofu at home or boiled - not to feed the
paddy's soil: the paddy took its green manure from a vetch sown in it over the winter. Beans were often grown on the bunds, beside the grass that was cut there
for manure or fodder.

A soybean plant is an erect bush, branching from a central stem, knee to waist high, about 2 to 4 ft (50 to 125 cm),
with broad, pointed, medium-green leaflets. In Asuka they stood in a row along the
bund.

How many plants a bund carried, or how far one spread, is not recorded. Bund beans were once grown across Japan; land
consolidation, weedkillers and damage by animals took most of them in modern times, and the village of Asuka still grows
them.

Guesses:
- How long a row of beans ran: no record of how long a stretch of bund was sown was found, and the one village account found points to longer rows, so the map's runs of two to four
  beads are a guess.
- How many bunds carried beans: no record of the share of bunds sown was found, and in that village every household grew them, so the map's beans on about three bunds in eight are a guess, and probably too few.

Depiction: The map draws the beans as round beads about 3 ft across, in a single row on some of the bunds, with at least
two beads on any stretch that carries them, since a single bead does not read as a row. The beads are a deep pine green,
darker than the real plant's medium green, so they show against the rice; the bead marks where a plant stands, not how far
it spread. The beads stand about 8 ft apart so that each reads on its own; in one village of terraced paddies the beans went into holes about 1 ft (30 cm) apart, two or three to a hole, so a drawn row holds far fewer beads than a bund held plants.

Name: bund beans
Covers: the bead run along the bunds (`bund_beans`)
