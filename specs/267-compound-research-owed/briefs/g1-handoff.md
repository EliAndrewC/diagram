R01 KNOB - the veranda runs along the garden face, and at the scale of a provincial residence either stops there or wraps two or three faces (late Edo); a four-sided ring is the great mansion's; the irikawa is one ken by the dictionary, and Katsura shows half a ken to one ken changing face to face; no general nure-en width was found (absence note) - `engawa` on O H U: the garden face always, one or two more faces rolled from the seed, 3-6 ft wide (the spec paragraph in 240 says so, and labels the garden-face-wider choice this project's reading).
R02 KNOB - one block under one roof is the ordinary form (almost no corridors below the daimyo's scale; the Kuchiba house); halls in echelon joined by watari-roka are the great house's, and at Katsura the form of a house grown hall by hall over half a century - `residence`, `residence corridor` on O H U: a corridor-joined echelon must read as accretion (fits Hayakawa's lineage-held accretion); an ordinary posting drawn in echelon has no attested ground.
R03 KNOB - two orders attested: the palace order (omote reception in front, the lord's working room and bedroom behind it, the family's oku beyond) and the small house's (entrance rooms, the living rooms, the formal zashiki deepest in); in both the reception sits at one END - `reception room`, `lord's quarters`, `family quarters`, `inner rooms` on O H U: Hayakawa's reception BETWEEN the master's and the family's rooms is in neither form and no source supports it; the master's rooms adjoin the family's in the palace order.
R04 ACCURATE - the lord's suite beyond the study is, in the sources, a room for working by day and a bedroom (the naka-oku, a modern term; Edo drawings count it in the omote), and the daimyo mansion's goza-no-ma stands on the omote side; the Matsue master's living room is set apart from the zashiki - `lord's quarters`: working room plus bedroom, behind the reception, before the family.
R05 KNOB - the butsuma beside the zashiki's tokonoma, or a room of about six mats behind the zashiki or the oku-no-ma (farmhouses, Nipponica; the Nomura house beyond its moved-in audience room); a butsuma among the family's private rooms was not found - `ancestral alcove`, `inner rooms` on H U: the alcove belongs at the formal end, by or behind the zashiki, not among the private rooms.
R53 SILENT (with a labeled GUESS) - a Japanese house keeps the tablets of its successive heads along its line of inheritance; nothing readable says tablets of predecessors IN OFFICE were kept at an office or its residence (absence note, 2026-09-27) - `ancestral alcove` on U: the alcove follows the house, not the post; past holders stand in it only if they were heads of the incumbent's own house, which is canon for the GM.
R06 ACCURATE (a separate private garden SILENT) - at Takayama one garden is seen from both the great hall and the intendant's living room, with a tea room and stepping stones from each; a separate small garden for the private rooms was found nowhere (absence note) - `garden` on O: the private rooms look onto the far part of the one garden rather than a garden of their own.
