## Items

- R26 KNOB - a Japanese post's main gate is one of two attested forms: a one-bay gate (yakuimon) of 1.8-2.6 m, about 6-8.5 ft, or a nagaya-mon opening in two ken, about 12 ft; a yamen's gate is a roofed three-bay building with six leaves, its width unread (absence); the Jōge post's gate (main gate, small gate, gatekeepers' room) has no width - `main gate`: O H U P roll one of the two forms per plan (6-8.5 ft or ~12 ft); the 9-12 ft samurai-gate anchor is within the span (accurate); a yamen gatehouse drawn as three bays is accurate, its 18-24 ft a GUESS.
- R27 KNOB - two wall forms are attested: the lighter clay-laid or plastered wall at about 30-60 cm (a surviving neribei 0.6 m at the base), about 1-2 ft, and the rammed-earth tsuijibei at about 1 m, about 3.3 ft; no magistrate's post's own wall was measured (absence) - `compound wall`: the town/city tiers' 1.5-2 ft is the lighter form's measured range (accurate; religion-and-death 200 corrected here); buildings 070's 3 ft is the tsuijibei form, drawn by the GM's ruling as a convention. T17's correction to buildings 070: its "the ordinary neribei / dobei was ~1 shaku" and "the county compound's own thickness is unsourced" should point at buildings 490 and say the lighter wall is measured at up to 0.6 m (about 2 ft) at its base, so the 3 ft it draws is the heavier of two real forms, not a thickening of the only one.
- R28 ACCURATE - arrival was staged: the genkan was where a palanquin was set down against the step so the rider went straight in, the main genkan was kept for the head of the house and honored guests, and on the Jōge drawing the entrance faces the ground inside the main gate; a garden (rather than a court) between gate and entrance is not read (absence) - `genkan`, `residence`: the staged gate-court-genkan approach is accurate and now cited in buildings 120; `garden`: a guest garden standing where the court would be (Hayakawa's guests' river door into the guest garden) is a GUESS.
- R29 ACCURATE (correcting buildings 030) - the Jōge post, an outpost with three officials in 1867, had an Inari shrine and a Tenjin shrine inside its walls on the 1869 drawing reproduced by the city of Fuchū; the 2008-2010 excavation confirmed the walls, steps, ditches and foundation stones but not the shrines - `compound shrine`: a modest shrine, Inari by default, is accurate for a post of any size; the record now calls the source a drawing, not an excavation plan.
