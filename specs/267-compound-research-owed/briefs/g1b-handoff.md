R07 KNOB - two approaches are attested: a genkan with a shikidai, which is the senior house's form (at Takayama the genkan is on the office, and the visitors' route runs through the office to the residence), or no genkan at all, where a middle gate in a wall leads along a walled garden path (roji) to the zashiki's veranda and the everyday door is the kitchen entrance (the Koseki house at Shiroishi, middle rank); a forecourt fenced from the garden and a pond on the gate-to-entrance line were not found (absence note) - `genkan`, `garden`, `garden pond` on O H U: roll the two forms where rank allows either; put the genkan on the office block where office and residence share a compound; a pond on the nakamon-genkan axis and a fenced forecourt are labeled GUESS.
R08 ACCURATE - the guest privy belongs to the formal side of the house, at the rear of the guest parlor (Kotobank) and, at the Sayama council hall, two upper privies with board verandas beside the shikidai genkan and zashiki; mid-rank houses got bath and privy as add-ons; buildings/310 points at buildings/220 for the upper/lower privy split - `latrine` on H: a guest privy at the rear of the zashiki along a board veranda and the family privy apart toward the living rooms, neither a yard building.
R09 ACCURATE (a detached bath SILENT) - a yudono was the upper-rank shoin house's (middle and lower samurai used a tub or the public bath); c. 1600 the warrior family's bath was a one-person tub in a corner of the doma; the Takayama residence had a bath with its well and kitchen, a hatamoto's 1843 plan names its yudono, and Sayama's mid-rank houses had the bath added on; a bath as a building of its own, and any bath size, were not found (absence note) - `bath` on O H U P: a room of the residence or a small addition to it, not a 12-18 ft pavilion; the service-side placement is labeled this project's reading, the size a GUESS; a middle-rank or lower house may have none.
R10 ACCURATE (a detached guest house SILENT) - the Takayama residence had a room for receiving guests, and a middle-rank house's guest space (shikidai genkan, announcing room, zashiki) sat in its one main house; travelling lords and officials lodged at a honjin, whose jodan-no-ma suite at Yakage is listed apart from the family's main house (whether it is a separate building is not said); a detached guest house at a samurai house was not found (absence note) - `guest quarters` on O H U P: the zashiki and the rooms beside it, inside the residence; a detached guest house is a GUESS (Hayakawa's guest house among them; see Left open).
R11 KNOB - canon: a county magistrate's samurai include the magistrate's karo (Rank 4); history gives a mansion of their own near the lord or in the castle at a daimyo's scale (the Saigo of Aizu: 2,400 tsubo, 38 rooms), or, at a shogunal intendancy, the staff living inside the compound in small houses (koya) or long-houses (nagaya); the head of the staff was the motojime, but where the motojime lived is not said; a chief retainer's house inside the lord's own compound was not found (absence note) - `karo's house` on O H U P: a small house or a long-house bay inside the compound, the intendancy's form; a small house OF ITS OWN (rather than a long-house bay) is labeled GUESS.
R12 SILENT (with a labeled GUESS) - the amado ran on a one-groove sill along the veranda, was stowed in a tobukuro by day and shut at night against weather and for security (seventeenth-century form); that a household kept the amado of unused rooms shut by day was not found (absence note) - `shuttered wing` on U: the shutters are real, but a wing shuttered in daylight because it stands unused is a GUESS.
