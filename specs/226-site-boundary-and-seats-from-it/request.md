# Feature 226 - the GM's request, verbatim (2026-09-12)

The GM, on the homesteads stage (1.0-2.3 s per hamlet), after the session's per-house profile (the last section
of `.claude/skills/diagram/dev/performance.md`):

> Why not draw a section of cords which literally separate the area in which we are placing our homesteads from
> literally everything? then that would prevent you from ever having to check whether a farmhouse is overlapping
> with any of those other features that you mentioned, like dry crops and water features and so forth. wouldn't
> that be just so much easier? I mean, why not just draw an outline or a separating line, which is to say a set
> of line segments. And then you just have a few, which is to say a relatively small number of line segments
> that you were checking. Wouldn't that be much easier and also much more computationally efficient?

The session agreed, described the one wrinkle (the stream and the feeder cross the homestead ground, so they are
a few corridor segments beside the boundary), said maps would change, and asked whether to build it. The GM:

> Okay. Also, for what it's worth, the fact that we are proposing one hundred and fifty seven seats for sixteen
> houses makes me think that our placement algorithm is inefficient. Like, if we're doing essentially ten wrong
> guesses for every correct guess or, I suppose, something approaching that ratio, then it seems like it means
> that the way that we are guessing and checking is wrong and inefficient.

Earlier in the same conversation, on what may change: *"I don't care about byte identity ... I am completely okay
with the layout of these things changing somewhat, like farmhouses being placed in a different pattern as long as
the actual invariants are maintained. like, things not overlapping and there being enough sun on people's gardens
and threshing fields, that kind of thing."*
