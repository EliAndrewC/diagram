## Sources that bear

**1. Head mound, marker stone and rite — Tonai Sennin-zuka, Sannohe (Aomori), 1784–85.** Modern prefectural heritage page: https://www.pref.aomori.lg.jp/soshiki/kyoiku/e-bunka/kensiseki_17.html
> 首一つを24文で買い集め、千首に達したので施主となり大供養をし参詣人からも60貫文の寄付があった。翌5年これらの浄財で千人塚四つを築造し供養碑を建立した。この塚は「千人首」とも呼ばれ、同7年まで3か年にわたり寒夜30日の百万遍供養をした。

(translation) "He bought up the heads at 24 mon each; when they reached a thousand he became the sponsor and held a great memorial rite, and worshippers gave 60 kanmon. The next year [1785], with these offerings, four thousand-man mounds were built and a memorial stone set up. The mounds are also called 'thousand heads'; until 1787, for three years, a 30-night winter recitation of the nembutsu a million times was held."

The page also says: 「墳丘4基は盛土、供養塔・台座は石造」 (translation) "The four mounds are heaped earth; the memorial tower and its base are stone." The stone's inscription is dated 天明五巳 (1785).

**2. Four green bamboo with a rope, and the ground rite — Kotobank 地鎮祭.** https://kotobank.jp/word/地鎮祭
- Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, citing a Kōyasan document of 1544 (pre-Edo; I read the dictionary's citation, not the document):
  > 「次御供所造立之事地鎮祭二貫五百文」(出典：高野山文書‐（天文一三年）（1544）奥院興隆作事雑事入目日記)

  (translation) "Next, the building of the offerings hall: ground-pacifying rite, 2 kan 500 mon."
- Sekai Daihyakka on the setup and its date:
  > 現在の一般的な方式は，工事場の一部を掃き清めて祭場を設け，四隅に青竹を立てて注連縄（しめなわ）を張り…

  > 江戸時代の大工技術書を調べてみても，地鎮祭が建築儀礼のなかで重要な位置を占め…るのは，江戸時代後半以降と考えられる。

  (translation) "The *present* usual form sweeps part of the site clean as a ritual ground, sets green bamboo at the four corners and strings a shimenawa…" and "Judging from Edo carpenters' manuals, the ground rite took an important place among building rites only from the latter Edo period."

## Reading, item by item

- **Head mound** — **ATTESTED ONLY FOR its funding** (Sannohe, 1784–85). The page gives 24 mon a head and 60 kanmon in offerings. Four earth mounds and a stone stele were built from that money, followed by three years of nembutsu rites. It gives no builder's cost, no price for the stone and no fee for the rite.
  - My arithmetic: 1,000 heads × 24 mon = 24 kanmon.
  - Against the record's Osaka builder's helper wage of 236 mon in 1785 (0275; same year, far away), 60 kanmon is about 254 helper-days.
- **Four green bamboo with a rope** — **NOT ATTESTED for Edo.** The encyclopedia calls the four-corner bamboo and rope the *present* form, and says the ground rite gained its place only in late Edo.
- **The ground rite's fee** — **ATTESTED ONLY FOR 1544**: 2 kan 500 mon at Kōyasan, pre-Edo, through a dictionary citation.
- **Unveilings** — **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** two narrower cases:
  - An unveiling at the temple that owns the image (居開帳): only as a per-head viewing fee, 100 mon at Tsu in 1790 and 1834. The 1790 diarist paid 130 mon for a night's inn in the same entry.
  - Zenkōji's great travelling unveilings in Edo (出開帳), whole takings against whole costs:

    | Year | Takings | Costs | Result |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | 1692 | 10,210 ryō | 914 ryō | about 9,296 ryō net |
    | 1803 | — | temporary hall alone, 93 ryō | takings fell short of costs |
    | 1820 | 3,062 ryō | 3,819 ryō | about 757 ryō loss |

  - My reading: at 60 monme to the ryō and Edo's 5–5.5 monme carpenter (0275, 1830s–50s, about 40 years later), 93 ryō is roughly 1,000–1,100 carpenter-days.
  - No takings for a small village temple's unveiling were found.
- **Shroud and coffin (Qing)** — **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a poor Fuzhou widow's 15 dollars set aside for her coffin "and in part" other burial costs. That is not a coffin price. My arithmetic: 50–75 days of a carpenter or mason, or about 2.5–3.75 months of a hired man boarding himself.
