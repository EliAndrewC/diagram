## Sources that bear

**1. Murata Michihito (村田路人), 「宝永元年大和川付替手伝普請について」, 待兼山論叢 史学篇 20 (1986), pp. 21–44.**
- URL: https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/47993/mrh_020_021.pdf
- `make source-pages` could not read the PDF (NOT-CHECKED), so I downloaded it, extracted the text with pdftotext, and checked these passages against the page images.
- The evidence describes 1704, Ōta village (太田村), Shiki district, Kawachi. Table 2 is built from the village's 「河州志紀郡太田村川違御普請ニ付人足諸色入用帳」 (宝永元年11月).

Table 2 rows, p. 28. The figure after each row is silver in monme, as the table gives it.
- Row 28 (4/24–4/28): 「川御奉行様御越ニ付家内庭廻リ掃除人足四拾人日用銀」 **60**
  - (translation) "Day wages of 40 laborers who swept the house and garden grounds for the river magistrates' visit." 60 monme for 40 man-days works out to 1.5 monme each.
- Row 32 (5/22): 「土岐伊予守様・松野河内守様新川御見分ニ御通リニ付道筋掃除人足七人之日用代、壱人ニ付壱匁五分ツヽ、但知行方ニ割符」 **10.5**
  - (translation) "For Lords Toki and Matsuno passing to inspect the new river: day wages of 7 laborers who swept the road, 1 monme 5 bu each, shared with the fief side."
- Row 36 (6/8): 「道筋掃除幷内夫人足共十七人之日用代」 **30.6**
  - (translation) "Day wages for 17 people in all, road sweepers and house porters together." This works out to about 1.8 monme each.

p. 36, the estimate wage and a farm comparison:
- 「一日銭弐百文ッヽ、三度支度為致やとひニ茂無人」
  - (translation) "even hiring at 200 mon a day with three meals, no one came." These were wheat-harvest hands, and the Yamato River works drew them away.
- 「「大和川新川大見積」、「川違新川普請大見積り」では人足賃銀をいずれも一人一日銀一匁五分としていた」
  - (translation) "both preliminary estimates set the laborer's wage at 1 monme 5 bu of silver per man per day."

p. 30, earthwork by the tsubo:
- 「見積りの基準となる一坪あたりの人足賃銀も、幕府担当分と三大名担当分は五匁九分八厘としているのに、本多氏担当分のみ五匁となっている」
  - (translation) "the laborers' wage per tsubo used as the basis of the estimate was 5 monme 9 bu 8 rin for the shogunate's and the three daimyo's sections, but only 5 monme for the Honda section."
- The tsubo here is earth dug or embanked (堀坪 / 築堤坪).
- Dividing 5.98 by the 1.5 wage gives about 4 man-days per tsubo. That is my arithmetic, not the paper's.

The paper gives no craftsman's wage.

**2. Li Dou (李斗), 《揚州畫舫錄》 卷十七 「工段營造錄」.**
- URL: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%8F%9A%E5%B7%9E%E7%95%AB%E8%88%AB%E9%8C%84/17
- Compiled in 1795. The norms are largely those of the 1734 Board of Works building code, as used in Beijing practice. The book gives no day wage.
- 「及刨夫壯夫，工用有制。若柵木牆、竹籬、柳藥欄、刨溝子，每四丈用壯夫一名。」
  - (translation) "The labor of diggers and strong laborers has fixed norms: for a palisade wall, a bamboo hedge, a willow or herb railing, or trenching, one strong laborer per 4 zhang."
- 「𡧗石片板、魚鱗石、虎皮石、冰紋石、墁石子、石望板、盆景樹、池山，皆以丈見方尺。」
  - (translation) "Flagstones, fish-scale stone, tiger-skin stone, ice-crack stone, laid pebbles, stone panels, potted trees and pond rockeries are all reckoned by the square zhang." This gives the unit of costing only, not a labor figure.
- 「京師以車載論，城內每一車給價二錢，出城十里內，加給一錢；十里外每里加給二分。如人夫抬運，照人數給工。」
  - (translation) "In the capital, trees are reckoned by the cartload: 2 qian a cart within the city, 1 qian more within 10 li outside it, 2 fen more per li beyond. If carried by porters, pay by the number of men."
  - Caution: the second clause is my repair of a garbled line. The fetched text reads 「十里外海裏加給二分」; I read 海裏 as 每里.
- 「扛抬上住，每丈壯夫二名，此石橋做法也。」
  - (translation) "Lifting into place, two strong laborers per zhang: this is the stone-bridge method."

**3. Miyazaki Katsumi (宮崎勝美), 2000, on the Hagi domain's Edo mansions.**
- Already archived under the key miyazaki-2000-hagi-yashiki.
- Evidence date: the An'ei rebuilding, ending 1773 (安永2年).
- 「国元の土１０坪（１坪はここでは６尺５寸立方）を…桜田上屋敷まで届けるのに必要なのは銀４貫294匁、荒木田土は土屋権三郎の見積もりで銀537匁5分、渋谷土は渋谷土屋忠七の見積もりで銀825匁」
  - (translation) "To deliver 10 tsubo (here 6.5 shaku cubed) of home-province earth to the Sakurada mansion took 4 kan 294 monme of silver. Arakida earth, on the earth-dealer Gonzaburō's estimate, cost 537.5 monme; Shibuya earth, on Chūshichi's estimate, 825 monme."
- This is earth for storehouse walls, not gravel.

## Reading, item by item

- **Swept earth path (S): ATTESTED ONLY FOR** sweeping a road or house grounds for an official's visit, Kawachi 1704, from source 1.
  - Crews of 7, 12, 17 and 40 laborer-days per occasion.
  - Paid 1.5 monme per man-day. The project estimates also used 1.5 monme a day, and a wheat-harvest hand got 200 mon plus three meals.
  - No craftsman's wage is given. This is upkeep of a path, not building one.
- **Clearing around a great tree, and leveling: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** river digging and embankment (1704), from source 1.
  - 5.98 monme of wages per tsubo of earth, against a 1.5 monme day wage.
  - Bought earth was 54–83 monme per cubic tsubo delivered in Edo, 1773 (source 3).
- **Sakaki hedge: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** Qing bamboo hedges and palisades: one laborer per 4 zhang (about 12.8 m), with no wage given (source 2). Japan is SILENT.
- **White-gravel path and ring of river stones:** Qing only. Pebble paving was costed by the square zhang (source 2), but no labor figure was found. Japan is SILENT.
- **Standing stone and rock seat: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** Qing stone bridges, two laborers per zhang for lifting (source 2). Nothing at the scale of a shrine stone.
- **Himorogi (S): SILENT.**
  - Archive terms: 神籬, ひもろぎ, himorogi.
