# Pass, group 2 (small carpentry and furnishings), 2026-10-21

Most items are still SILENT. Two new sources bear on the question: a 1989 paper on the Nakai family of carvers in Tanba and Tango, and Morisada's entry on Edo shop signboards. A Qing stele of 1902 serves as the comparison. Nothing was edited or committed.

## Sources that bear on the items

**1. Nakai carvers paper (1989) — carvers' day rates beside carpenters' and sawyers' day rates, same region.** Hayashi (or whoever wrote it), "丹波柏原の彫物師中井氏とその営業形態", 日本建築学会計画系論文報告集 396, pp. 63-65. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/396/0/396_KJ00004074727/_pdf/-char/ja . I did not check the author's name. `make source-pages` returned NOT-CHECKED (it could not read the PDF), so I downloaded it with curl and read pp. 63-65 from rendered page images. It is a modern article, but it quotes and summarizes period documents; the dates below are the dates of those documents.
- p.63, Nakai's standard rate, 1810s-1820s: 「こうした事例から，中井氏の作料は3匁で，別に飯料が6分というのが基準の作料であったとみられる。」 (translation) "From these cases, the Nakai's standard fee seems to have been 3 monme, with a separate 6 bu for meals." The cases behind it are Enkoji (文化15年, 1818: 66 工, 237匁6分 including meals, so 3 monme 6 bu a day) and Hanakura village (49 工 of carving for 147匁).
- p.63, the guardian dogs, 1814-15: 「「上林西屋村八幡宮」では狛犬も彫っているが，その作料は1人工3匁であった。また「すゝめ板」も3匁であったが，これら以外の彫物では1人工当り3匁3分とされている」 (translation) "At the Kamibayashi Nishiya village Hachiman shrine they also carved komainu; the fee was 3 monme per man-day. The 'sparrow boards' were also 3 monme, but the other carvings were reckoned at 3 monme 3 bu per man-day." The work ran 「文化11年から12年夏にかけて」 (translation) "from Bunka 11 into the summer of Bunka 12", i.e. 1814-15 (p.62). The paper does not say what the komainu were made of. I read them as wooden because a woodcarver made them. No count of days is given.
- p.63, carpenters nearby: 「文政2年から5年にかけての永富家住宅…では1人工2匁であった。また文化12年（1815）における柏原八幡三重塔の作事では2匁5分であり，同5年（1808）における鴨神社（氷上郡吉見）拝殿再建の作事では，作料1匁5分と別に飯料として白米1升が給されている。」 (translation) "At the Nagatomi house (1819-22) it was 2 monme per man-day. At the Kaibara Hachiman three-storey pagoda (1815) it was 2 monme 5 bu. At the rebuilding of the Kamo shrine's worship hall (1808) the fee was 1 monme 5 bu, with 1 sho of polished rice given separately for meals."
- p.65, note 20, the primary ruling in the 『宮津日記』, Miyazu 1777: 「丁酉（安永6年）二月，大工・屋根屋・木挽等作料ノ儀色々願候処，一匁八分ニ相極ル」 (translation) "2nd month of 1777: after various petitions about the fees of carpenters, roofers, sawyers and the like, they were fixed at 1 monme 8 bu." The paper adds (p.63) that more than 20 carpenters on a 1783 Miyazu house were each paid 1 monme 8 bu per man-day.
- p.64, Fukuchiyama (from 福知山市史 資料編一; the passage gives no date): 「また福知山においては大工，木挽の作料は1匁5分で，別に飯料として（「扶持方」と称している）1升4合が給された事例が多い」 (translation) "In Fukuchiyama the fee of carpenters and sawyers was often 1 monme 5 bu, with 1 sho 4 go [of rice] given separately for meals (called fuchikata)." It concludes: 「彫物を専業とする中井氏の作料は，大工の作料に比べて高かったといえそうである」 (translation) "the Nakai, specialist carvers, seem to have been paid more than carpenters."
- p.65, note 16, a Kyoto carver's per-piece prices (長谷川与助, a written order to the Sakai carpenter 五兵衛, dated only 戌ノ十二月七日, year not given): 「彫手間壱枚ニ付 代銀五拾七匁宛」, 「代銀卅弐匁宛」, 「代銀四拾匁宛」, the large frog-leg strut 「代銀百卅七匁」, 「御拝象鼻 壱枚ニ付 代銀百六拾匁」, 「同 獏鼻 壱枚ニ付 代銀弐百廿五匁」 (translation) "carving labour per piece, 57 / 32 / 40 / 137 monme of silver; elephant-nose bracket end 160 monme; baku-nose 225 monme." These are pieces for a Hongwanji hall, not plaques.
- p.62 and note 15: the 4th-generation Nakai repaired the 随身 (attendant guardian) statues of 粟賀神社 in 宝暦5年 (1755). This shows the practice; no price is given.

**2. Morisada, Edo standing signboards (1837-53).** 類聚近世風俗志 上 (1908), 第四編 生業上, koma 81-82: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/82 . I read it from a headless-browser screenshot of the NDL viewer; the archived capture is a 4 KB shell. Koma 82, right page, top:
> 「建看板は溝外の路上に係るを以て必ず官に請撿使を得て裁レ之也京坂は路狹きが故に不レ製レ之江戸諸所に有レ之本町四丁目殊に多く彫物等甚精美の物あり看板及柱の價と官に許ひ撿使を給ふ等の諸費共少き物金七八十兩多きは百四五十兩也」

(translation) "Because a standing signboard stands on the road outside the ditch, one must apply to the authorities, receive an inspector, and only then put it up. Kyoto-Osaka do not make them, the streets being narrow. Edo has them in many places, especially Honchō 4-chōme, with very fine carving. With the price of the board and posts and the costs of applying to the authorities and of the inspector, a small one is 70-80 ryō of gold and a large one 140-150 ryō."

- Two glyphs are uncertain. 溝 (ditch) fits the 「溝」 printed in the koma 81 figure. 許ひ is as printed.
- Koma 81 shows the roofed sign (屋根看板) and adds 「此所近世は種々槻の彫物に善美を盡し諸人の眼を驚す物多し」 (translation) "lately these have many kinds of zelkova carving, as fine as can be, and many astonish the eye."

**3. Qing comparison: 至聖殿重修碑記, Midu, Yunnan, 1902.** https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/至聖殿重修碑記 . Lines from the stele's own accounts:
- 「一出裝修四面聖牌神龕窗櫺，工料錢貳百捌拾千文」 (translation) "fitting out the four spirit tablets, the shrine niche and the window lattices, labour and materials, 280,000 wen"
- 「一出買香爐碑心刻碑共用錢捌千陸百伍拾文」 (translation) "buying the incense burner and the stele core, and carving the stele, 8,650 wen in all"
- 「一出油刷工料錢壹百零伍千文」 (translation) "oiling and painting, labour and materials, 105,000 wen"
- 「一出貼聖牌金銀箔，共用錢陸千四百文」 (translation) "gold and silver leaf for the tablets, 6,400 wen"
- 「一出買墩盤共用錢貳拾柒千文」 (translation) "plinth stones, 27,000 wen"
- The stele's own conversion: 「籌銀肆拾捌兩合錢陸拾貳千肆百文」 (translation) "48 taels of silver, equal to 62,400 wen", i.e. 1,300 wen a tael.

## Reading, item by item

- **Flat stone as offering table: SILENT.**
- **Bamboo tube: SILENT.** The 竹筒 hits in Morisada 下 (11 koma) are unread.
- **Plaque carver's fee: ATTESTED ONLY FOR other carved pieces, not a plaque.**
  - Tango and Tanba carvers were paid 3 monme a day plus 6 bu for meals (1810s-20s), when local carpenters got 1.5-2.5 monme.
  - The Kyoto carver Hasegawa charged 32-225 monme of silver a piece for temple bracket carvings (year not given).
  - For a plaque, the reading is: carver-days × 3-3.6 monme. That arithmetic is mine, not the source's.
- **Origin-story board under a roof: ATTESTED ONLY FOR Edo luxury shop signboards, 1837-53.** These are roofed, carved, standing signs at 70-150 ryō including posts and official fees. It is an upper bound for carved-board work, not a village shrine board. Painting the board: SILENT.
- **Felling and sawing: ATTESTED.** Sawyers' day rate: 1 monme 8 bu fixed in Miyazu in 1777, the same as carpenters and roofers, and about 1 monme 5 bu plus 1 sho 4 go of rice in Fukuchiyama (undated). Sawyers per amount of timber sawn: SILENT. The Morisada searches for 木挽 found only the place-name 木挽町; I did not open 地方凡例録.
- **Wooden guardians: ATTESTED ONLY FOR the day rate.** Komainu carved for 3 monme a carver-day, 1814-15; the days and total are not given. The Nakai repaired attendant guardian statues in 1755, with no price.
- **Roofs: nothing new.** The Kasuga shrine re-roofing of 1749 is already in the record (`kawakami-1992-shrine-headman`): 187 carpenter-and-sawyer days at about 1.67 monme, and 24 Edo roofer-days at 3 monme.
- **Qing comparison: ATTESTED ONLY FOR a county Confucian hall in Yunnan, 1902.** No wage is given. 聖牌 are spirit tablets, not plaques, and the 280,000-wen line lumps tablets, niche and lattice together. The 8,650-wen line is the only one near a name-stone.
- **Still SILENT:** stone offering table, refitting an old table, paper wand, wooden offering chest, stone-set offering box, lattice door, name-stone, painted wooden torii, low fence, bamboo spout.
