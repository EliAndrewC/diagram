## Q1. Buddhist temple precinct plan - buildings, sizes, seven-hall plan, Chinese axial plan

### Seven-hall plan (七堂伽藍) - definitions
- ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/伽藍 (fetched): old sense - "金堂、塔、講堂、鐘楼、経蔵、僧坊、食堂の七つがあるのが伽藍、としており、これを七堂伽藍と呼ぶ。" (Old usage: a temple having these seven - golden hall, pagoda, lecture hall, bell tower, sutra repository, monks' quarters, refectory - is called the seven-hall garan.)
  Zen version: "仏殿（金堂）、三門（山門）、僧堂、庫院（くいん）もしくは庫裏（くり）、法堂（はっとう）、浴室、東司（とうす）（または西浄〈せいちん〉）から構成される" (Buddha hall, main gate, monks' hall, kitchen/administration hall, dharma hall, bathhouse, and toilet.)
  No ken/meter sizes given for Japanese examples; the only dimension in the article is for a Pala-era Indian monastery platform (109 x 96 m) - not applicable here.
- mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R2-01260.html (Japan Tourism Agency, English, fetched): "Sanmon (Main Gate), Butsuden (Buddha Hall), Hattō (Dharma Hall), Sōdō (Monk's Hall), Daikuin (Kitchen and Administrative Building), Yokushitsu (Bathhouse), and Tōsu (Toilet)." "The buildings of the shichidō garan are all interconnected by a network of covered corridors and stairs." No sizes given.
- New Jodo-shu dictionary (jodoshuzensho.jp - listed, not fetched this session; queue for follow-up) and Kotobank both corroborate the same two lists (old/general vs. Zen).

### A real Edo-period urban-temple main hall (concrete size anchor)
- kunishitei.bunka.go.jp/heritage/detail/102/3244 (national cultural-property DB, fetched): Jōroku-ji main hall (丈六寺本堂,元方丈), Tokushima. "桁行18.7m、梁間11.8m、一重、入母屋造、本瓦葺" (ridge-direction span 18.7 m, cross span 11.8 m, single story, irimoya roof, tile-roofed), built 寛永6 (1629), early Edo. This is a genuinely useful anchor: ~10 x 6.5 ken hondo for a middling temple.

This remains OPEN - flagged as absence, not a guess.
- seikomuseum / gakken (time-bell background, fetched via search only): temple bells were rung for Buddhist rites ("寺の鐘(梵鐘)は...仏事や勤行のために...最初一日に3回鐘を撞いていました"), separate in origin from castle/town time-bells ("城の鐘"), which later spread from Edo to other castle towns ("地方では京都・大坂・長崎などの主な城下町にも時報制度が広がっていき"). This supports treating the temple bell tower as a normal fixture of an operating temple, independent of any civic time-bell system, but does not give a prevalence percentage.

## Q4. Chinese state cult in county/prefectural seats; Japanese domain-school analogue

### Placement pattern - Ming gazetteer data (primary source, fetched verbatim)
zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/欽定古今圖書集成/方輿彙編/職方典/第0232卷 (Gujin Tushu Jicheng, 職方典, fetched):
- Jiaxiang county (嘉祥县): 社稷坛 "在县西七里小青山下" (7 li west of the county, below Xiaoqing Mountain); 风云雷雨山川坛 "在县南三里澹台山下" (3 li south); 邑厉坛 "在县北城外萌山之下" (outside the north wall); 城隍庙 "在嘉祥县东萌山之阳" (east of the county, south side of Meng Mountain). No 文庙 entry on this page.
- Juye county (钜野县): 社稷坛 "在县西门外" (outside the west gate); 风云雷雨山川坛 "在县南门外" (outside the south gate); 邑厉坛 "在县城北" (north of the county wall); 城隍庙 "在县治西北" (northwest of the county yamen, i.e. inside the wall).
- Yuncheng county (郓城县): 社稷坛 "在城西门外"; 风云雷雨山川坛 "在县南门外"; 邑厉坛 "在城北门外"; 城隍庙 "在县治西" (west of the yamen).
- Pattern across all three: the altars of soil-and-grain and of wind/cloud/thunder/rain/mountains/rivers are OUTSIDE the wall (west and south respectively, matching the west=soil/grain, south=natural forces convention); the plague altar is outside the north; the city god temple is INSIDE the wall, near the yamen. This is consistent with the earlier WebSearch summary of a Ming example (社稷坛 northwest-outside, 风云雷雨山川坛 south-outside, 城隍庙 inside near 文庙) - same institutional logic, different exact county.

### City god temple rank system (Hongwu statute)
- From WebSearch summaries only (not yet independently fetched verbatim from a primary Da Ming Huidian page - flagged): Hongwu 2 (1369) ranked city gods in four tiers by seat level - capital (王, king), and then fu/zhou/xian at descending ranks (府 威灵公, 州 灵佑侯, 县 显佑伯); temples required plain wooden spirit tablets, not anthropomorphic statues, "only calling them the city god spirit of a certain fu or xian." This supports "every fu/zhou/xian seat had a statutorily-defined city god temple," but I have not yet fetched the Da Ming Huidian / Ming History passage itself verbatim - treat the rank numbers as provisionally sourced pending a direct-page refetch.
- Measured examples (mix of fetched and search-only):
  - 黎城城隍庙 (Licheng county, Shanxi; zh.wikipedia, FETCHED verbatim): "东西36.23米、南北52.65米，占地面积1892平方米" (36.23 m E-W x 52.65 m N-S, 1,892 sq m); axis "由南至北依次遗有山门、正殿" (gate then main hall, south to north); national-level protected cultural property.
  - 宁波府城隍庙 (Ningbo prefectural city god temple; zh.wikipedia, FETCHED verbatim): "占地面积约4700平方米，建筑面积约4200平方米...中轴线自南向北由照壁、大门、仪门、戏台、大殿、后台组成" (~4,700 sq m site, ~4,200 sq m built; axis: spirit wall, main gate, ceremonial gate, opera stage, main hall, rear stage) - notably a PREFECTURAL (府) example is larger than the COUNTY (县) Licheng example, consistent with rank.
  - Pingyao (county) and Dengfeng (county-level city) city god temples: search-summary only (baike.baidu.com pages 403-blocked on fetch) - Pingyao ~7,302 sq m total; Dengfeng ~8,200 sq m. NOT independently verbatim-confirmed; flagged below as blocked sources.

### Confucian temple/school (文庙/学宫) - scale and layout
- Shanghai Confucian Temple (zh.wikipedia/上海文庙, FETCHED verbatim): current site "占地17.3亩" (17.3 mu, ≈1.15 ha) since an 1855 relocation to "县城西门内右营署的明代海防道署旧址" (inside the west gate of the county wall, on the old Ming coastal-defense office site) - i.e. INSIDE the wall, near a gate, similar institutional logic to city god temples. 大成殿 "约260平方米" with "面阔三间" (~260 sq m, 3 bays wide - smaller than the provincial-capital examples below). 魁星阁 "高约20米" (~20 m).
- Shaozhou prefectural school (韶州府学宫; zh.wikipedia, FETCHED verbatim): 大成殿 "殿内面积374平方米，殿高16.4米" (interior 374 sq m, 16.4 m tall), "面阔五间，进深三间" (5 bays wide x 3 bays deep), with a "月台" (moon terrace) in front - a PREFECTURAL example, larger than Shanghai's county-tier hall, again consistent with rank scaling.
- Additional county-level 大成殿 dimensions surfaced by WebSearch (summaries only, not independently fetched verbatim - flag for confirmation): Zhengding county 文庙 (正定县文庙) "面阔五间，进深三间，单檐歇山顶"; Cangzhou 文庙 "东西18米，南北9米"; Ruzhou 文庙 "面阔五间...进深10米". All show the same 5-bay-wide convention regardless of exact rank, with depth/height scaling by rank.
- Count and typology (search-summary only, zhihu.com/p/587315996, page ITSELF returned 403 on direct fetch both times - flagged below): "明代全国就有府、州、县三级文庙约1560所" (~1,560 prefecture/subprefecture/county Confucian temples nationwide under the Ming) and the layout typology "左庙右学、左学右庙、前庙后学" (temple-left/school-right, school-left/temple-right, temple-front/school-back). This 1,560-count figure is USEFUL but comes only from a WebSearch synthesis of a page I could not open directly - needs independent verification before being treated as a citable number (it reads as plausible and is a commonly repeated figure in Chinese heritage literature, but "commonly repeated" is not "verified on a page I fetched").

### Japan: domain schools (藩校) and Confucian shrines (聖堂) - county-level? Provincial academies?
- ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/湯島聖堂 (FETCHED verbatim): Yushima Seidō (shogunate-level, Edo/Tokyo) - "後に幕府直轄の学問所となった" (later became a direct shogunal school); "大成殿の建物も水戸の孔子廟にならい創建時の2.5倍規模の黒塗りの建物に改められた" (the Taiseiden was rebuilt at 2.5x its original scale, black-lacquered, modeled on the Mito domain's Confucian shrine) - confirms domain (藩) Confucian shrines (e.g. Mito) existed and were themselves used as a design model for the shogunal one, but the fetched page gives no dimensions for Mito's or any other domain shrine, and no statement about county-level (as opposed to domain/han-level) Confucian shrines in Japan. This matches what the setting needs to know: the Japanese analogue is at the HAN (domain) level via 藩校, not at a county level - Japan had no county-tier state-cult architecture equivalent to China's 文庙/城隍庙/社稷坛 lattice; this is a structural difference worth recording explicitly rather than forcing a parallel.
- Search-summary only (not fetched verbatim - flag): "孔子廟の基本的形式は仰高門・入徳門を設け、中央正面に大成殿をおくもので、大成殿は正面5間、奥行6間で、内部正面に孔子像、左右に四配十二哲像などが祀られました" (generic Confucian-shrine form: Gyōkōmon and Nyūtokumon gates, Taiseiden centered, 5 bays wide x 6 deep, Confucius image inside with the Four Correlates/Twelve Philosophers) and "湯島聖堂：大成殿は間口20メートル、奥行14.2メートル、高さ14.6メートル" (Yushima Taiseiden: 20 m wide x 14.2 m deep x 14.6 m tall) and "多久聖廟（佐賀県・多久藩）：宝永5年（1708）...現存する聖廟としては足利学校、閑谷学校に次ぐ古い建物" (Taku Confucian shrine, Saga/Taku domain, built 1708, third-oldest extant). These numbers did not come from a page I fetched directly (they came from the WebSearch tool's own synthesis) - I could not re-locate and re-fetch the specific source page in the time available; flagged for confirmation before use in the record, though they are internally consistent with the fetched Yushima Seidō page's "2.5x scale-up" statement (a larger Taiseiden than an ordinary domain one).
- No page found addressing prefectural-city-scale 书院-equivalent Confucian ACADEMIES in Japan at a scale between han-level 藩校 and shogunal Yushima Seidō; this looks like a genuine structural gap (Japan's system was han-organized, not prefecture-organized, so there is no "provincial academy" tier to find) rather than a missed source.
