# Feature 272 reader T - temple/shrine research (parts A, B, C)

## A. The city temple complex

### 1. Zojo-ji (Jodo-shu head temple, Edo/Tokyo, Tokugawa family temple)

Precinct area: 250,000 tsubo at the Edo-period peak; 16,000 tsubo today.

> "寺所有の領地（寺領）は一万余石。二十五万坪の境内には、坊中寺院四十八、学寮百数十軒が立ち並び、「寺格百万石」とうたわれています。"
> (translated: "The temple's own domain [land stipend] was over 10,000 koku. Within the 250,000-tsubo precinct stood forty-eight affiliated sub-temples and one hundred-odd dormitories/seminaries, earning it the epithet 'a temple ranked at a million koku.'")
> - https://www.zojoji.or.jp/info/history.html

> "現在、焼失をまぬがれた三解脱門や黒門など古くからの建造物をはじめ、大殿、安国殿、圓光大師堂、光摂殿、鐘楼、経蔵、慈雲閣等の堂宇が、一万六千坪の境内に立ち並んでいます。"
> (translated: "Today, old structures that escaped fire - the Sangedatsumon gate and Kuromon gate - along with the Daiden [main hall], Ankokuden, Enkodaishi-do, Koshoden, bell tower, sutra repository, and Jiun-kaku stand within a 16,000-tsubo precinct.")
> - https://www.zojoji.or.jp/info/history.html

Monk population (scale context): "檀林とは僧侶養成のための修行および学問所で、当時の増上寺には、常時三千人もの修行僧がいたといわれています。" (translated: "A danrin was a training and study institution for producing monks; Zojo-ji reportedly had as many as 3,000 monks in residence at all times.") - same page.

Main-hall dimensions: not stated for the Edo-period hall (rebuilt 1974); no ken/meter figure found.

Layout (distances given in ken, unconverted):
> "大門から三門...約108間" / "三門から大殿...距離にして約48間"
> ("from the Daimon [outer gate] to the Sanmon is about 108 ken; from the Sanmon to the Daiden [main hall] is about 48 ken")
> - https://www.zojoji.or.jp/keidai/

> "経蔵：九間半（約17m）四面、土蔵造"
> ("the sutra repository is nine-and-a-half ken square on each side, earthen-walled storehouse construction")
> - https://www.zojoji.or.jp/keidai/

Layout elements present: outer gate (daimon) -> sanmon (Sangedatsumon) -> main hall (daiden), plus sutra repository (kyozo), bell tower, and at the Edo peak 48 sub-temples (tatchu) and 100+ dormitories/seminaries (gakuryo).

### 2. Kan'ei-ji (Tendai head temple, Ueno, Edo, Tokugawa mausoleum temple)

Precinct area: 305,000+ tsubo at the late-Edo peak (roughly double present-day Ueno Park).

> "江戸時代後期、最盛期の寛永寺は寺域30万5千余坪、寺領11,790石を有し、子院は36か院に及んだ（現存するのは19か院）。現在の上野公園のほぼ全域が寺の旧境内であり、最盛期には、さらにその2倍の面積の寺地を有していた。"
> (translated: "In the late Edo period, at its peak Kan'ei-ji held a temple precinct of over 305,000 tsubo and a temple domain of 11,790 koku, with sub-temples reaching 36 in number [19 survive today]. Almost the entire area of present-day Ueno Park was the temple's former precinct, and at its peak it held land twice that area again.")
> - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寛永寺

Main hall (Konpon Chudo):
> "根本中堂...重層入母屋造、間口45.5メートル、奥行42メートル、高さ32メートルという壮大な規模の仏堂であった。"
> (translated: "The Konpon Chudo [completed 1698] was a two-story hip-and-gable-roofed Buddhist hall of grand scale: 45.5 m wide, 42 m deep, and 32 m high.")
> - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寛永寺

Layout: main hall encircled by a rectangular cloister with a karamon front gate; a twin-hall pair (Hokke-do/Jogyo-do) linked by a roofed corridor; abbot's residence (honbo, on the present Tokyo National Museum site); up to 36 sub-temples. Quotes and URL as above.

### 3. Chion-in (Jodo-shu head temple, Kyoto)

No tsubo/m2 figure found, but the site is explicitly organized in three graded terraces:

> "知恩院の境内は、三門や塔頭寺院のある下段、御影堂（本堂）など中心伽藍のある中段、勢至堂、法然廟などのある上段の3つに分かれている。"
> (translated: "Chion-in's precinct is divided into three: a lower terrace holding the sanmon gate and sub-temples [tatchu], a middle terrace holding the central complex including the Miedo [main hall], and an upper terrace holding the Seishi-do and Honen's mausoleum.")
> - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/知恩院

Main hall (Miedo/Daiden): "間口44.8メートル、奥行34.5メートル...桁行（正面）11間、梁間（奥行）9間" (44.8 m wide, 34.5 m deep; 11 ken across the front, 9 ken deep). Sanmon gate: "五間三戸二階二重門...高さ24メートルの門で...現存する日本の寺院の三門（山門）の中で最大" (five-bay, three-portal, two-story double-roofed gate, 24 m tall, the largest extant sanmon in Japan). Same URL.

### 4. Nishi Honganji (Jodo Shinshu Hongan-ji-ha head temple, Kyoto)

Precinct area not found on official site or Japanese Wikipedia (caveat: building dimensions only are given).

Main halls: Goeido "南北62メートル、東西48メートル、高さ29メートル" (62 m N-S, 48 m E-W, 29 m high); Amidado "桁行45.2メートル、梁間42メートル" (45.2 x 42 m, rebuilt 1760). - https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/西本願寺

Layout (site-diagram key, translated): "A: Somon [main outer gate], B: Goeido gate, C: Amidado gate, D: Goeido, E: Amidado, F: Shoin, G: bell tower, H: sutra repository, I: Hyakka-en garden, J: Tekisui-en garden, K: drum tower, L: Hiunkaku pavilion," perimeter enclosed by an earthen wall, with the Horikawa canal as a natural moat on the east. Goeido and Amidado stand side by side at the precinct's center, Goeido the larger. Same URL; corroborated https://bunka.nii.ac.jp/heritages/detail/175062 and https://www.hongwanji.kyoto/see/keidai.html.

### 5. Suzhou Confucian Temple / Prefecture School (蘇州文廟, Ming/Qing rebuilds)

Precinct area: present 17,800 m2, stated as one-sixth of its peak extent.
> "At present, the temple only occupies 17,800 square metres, which is one sixth of the area when it was in the prime."
> - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou_Confucian_Temple

Historical scale: "According to the record of County Wu, it had 213 rooms during the Southern Song dynasty (in 1241). In its day, it had classrooms, dormitories, exam rooms and canteens, besides the hall and temple." Same URL.

Main hall (Dacheng Hall, rebuilt 1474 Ming, most other buildings rebuilt 1864 Qing): "Dacheng Hall...is 7 rooms wide, 13 purlins deep...supported by 50 Nanmu columns." Gate: "Lingxing Men, built in 1373, Ming dynasty, is a huge limestone memorial archway with six columns, three doors and four door leaves." Same URL.

Layout: paired eastern temple precinct (Lingxing Men archway -> Ji Men gate -> Dacheng Hall [main hall] -> Chongsheng Memorial Temple, ancestral shrine) and western school precinct (Pan pond, Qixing pond, Minglun Hall [lecture hall]). Same URL.

### 6. Yichun Confucian Temple (宜春文廟, Jiangxi, prefectural-level)

Main hall: "22.5-metre (74 ft) wide, 16.6-metre (54 ft) deep and 13.5-metre (44 ft) high...double-eave gable and hip roofs covered with yellow glazed tiles." Historical (mostly non-extant) layout: "Gate, Pond (泮池), Hall of Minglun (明伦堂), Pavilion of Zunjing (尊经阁), Palace of Wenchang (文昌宫), and Pavilion of Kuixing (魁星阁)." - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichun_Confucian_Temple. No precinct area given.

## B. The temple neighborhood (teramachi) and the small urban shrine

Primary source: Kanazawa City's official preservation plan for the Teramachidai (寺町台) traditional-building-group preservation district, fetched as PDF via https://digilib.city.kanazawa.ishikawa.jp/preview/pdf/FXHmogAAA (index page https://digilib.city.kanazawa.ishikawa.jp/doc/89/).

### District scale and composition
> "保存地区は、南北約1,220メートル、東西約1,000メートル、面積約22.0ヘクタールの範囲で...保存地区内には、52寺、２社及び約320の町家等の建築物が存在している。"
> (translated: "The preservation district runs about 1,220 m north-south and 1,000 m east-west, an area of about 22.0 ha...within the district there are 52 temples, 2 shrines, and about 320 townhouse-type buildings.")

### Small temple plot: two street types, two packing regimes
Old Noda road (wide, straight, the Maeda clan's mausoleum approach):
> "旧野田道は...広幅員で直線的な街路となっており、通りに沿って大規模な境内地を持つ寺社が整然と連続する。多くの寺院は境内地の前面通り沿いに土塀や門を配し、土塀越しに本堂、庫裏、鐘楼などを垣間見ることができる。"
> (translated: "...a wide, straight street, along which temples and shrines with large-scale precincts stand in orderly, continuous succession. Most temples place an earthen wall and a gate along the street frontage of their precinct, and the main hall, kuri [priest's residence], and bell tower can be glimpsed over the wall.")

Old Tsurugi road (narrower, curving, former monzen-chi lot pattern):
> "旧鶴来道は...通り沿いには、かつての寺社門前地としての地割が残り、現在も町家の建物が並ぶ。従って...通りからは寺社が直接見えにくく、土塀も少ない...通りから下がった位置に門が配され参道の奥に本堂、庫裏、鐘楼などが見られ"
> (translated: "...along the street the old lot pattern of temple-gate-town [monzen-chi] remains, and townhouse buildings still line it today. So...the temples are not directly visible from the street, and there are few earthen walls...the gate is set back from the street, and beyond the approach [sando] the main hall, kuri, and bell tower can be seen.")

Contrast with townhouse lots (implying temple/shrine lots are NOT narrow-and-deep):
> "寺社以外の敷地形状は、間口が狭く奥行きが深い。これらの宅地は、通りを挟んで両側に向かい合って町を形成している。"
> (translated: "The plot shape of non-temple/shrine land is narrow-frontage, deep. These house lots face each other across the street on both sides, forming the town.")

Hall scale: "寄棟造は全体の約２割弱を占めるが...規模はいずれも間口８間以上と大きく、町並みの重要な構成要素となっている。" (Hip-roofed halls are a little under 20% of the total; their scale is in every case 8+ ken of frontage [about 14.5+ m], an important part of the townscape.)

Graveyard behind the hall, screened by garden:
> "座敷から見て庭の地形が下がっていくものにおいては、石積みや擁壁により建物の建つ地盤と同じレベルに平坦な土地を広げた上に更に築山を置き、背後の墓地や隣地と一線を画す作庭となっている。"
> (translated: "Where the garden's ground level drops away as seen from the reception room, stone retaining walls level a flat area to match the building's grade, with an artificial hill built on it, so the garden draws a clear line against the graveyard and neighboring lot behind it.")

Ownership origin and sect clustering:
> "寺地については、拝領地として与えられた寺地がほとんどであるが...寺社の配置の特徴としては、宗派ごとのまとまりが見られることが挙げられる。旧野田道沿いには、日蓮宗、法華宗が多く、旧鶴来道沿いには曹洞宗、臨済宗が多く配されている。"
> (translated: "Most temple lots were domain-granted lots [haizo-chi]...a feature of the arrangement is clustering by sect: Nichiren and Hokke sects are common along the old Noda road, Soto and Rinzai along the old Tsurugi road.")

Building ledger example (structures on one plot, repeated pattern): "10 A5 本堂 １棟...(妙立寺) / 11 A5-2 庫裏 / 12 A5-3 山門 / 13 A5-4 浄行堂 / 14 A5-5 経蔵" - Myoryu-ji: main hall + kuri + gate + a subsidiary hall + sutra repository, one address.

### Hirosaki teramachi - planned, sect-sorted temple streets
> "2代藩主信枚が慶長15年(1610)に弘前城の築城を開始するとともに、城下防衛や領民掌握のため寺社を移転させ、ここに集めました。禅林街には、津軽家の曹洞宗の菩提寺長勝寺を中心に曹洞宗寺院が立ち並び"
> (translated: "The second domain lord, Nobuhira, began building Hirosaki Castle in 1610 and at the same time relocated temples and shrines here for castle-town defense and control of the domain's subjects. Zenrin-gai is lined with Soto-sect temples centered on Choso-ji, the Tsugaru family's own Soto funerary temple.")
> - https://www.city.hirosaki.aomori.jp/jouhou/keikaku/files/rekimati4shou.pdf

> "慶安2年(1649)に元々の寺町が火災で焼失し、新たに溜池の南側の高台に曹洞宗以外の宗派の寺院街が町割りされ、「新寺構」（国指定史跡）と呼ばれています。"
> (translated: "In 1649 the original temple town burned down, and a new temple street for sects other than Soto was laid out [machiwari] on high ground south of the reservoir; it is called 'Shinji-gamae', a nationally designated historic site.")

Hirosaki Shin-Teramachi town-plotting: "慶安２年（１６４９）の寺町（元寺町）の大火により焼失した寺院の移転のため、翌３年に町割りされたもの...延宝６年（１６７８）の弘前町方屋敷割りでは、寺院の他に４５軒の町家と武家屋敷一軒がある。" (translated: "Plotted the following year [1650] for the relocation of temples burned in the 1649 fire...in the 1678 lot survey, besides the temples there are 45 townhouses and one samurai residence.") - https://matinami.o.oo7.jp/hokkaidou-tohoku/hirosaki-sinteramati.html (no individual lot dimensions given).

### Small urban shrine

Kanazawa Teramachidai's own two shrines - the clearest illustration of how small:
> "神社建築については、保存地区には２社が存在する。諏訪神社は明治末期の再建で、拝殿、幣殿、本殿を接続する複合型社殿である。泉野菅原神社は玉泉寺天満宮と称され、かつて玉泉寺内にあった。明治初年神仏分離令によって分離独立するが、明治４年の火災により翌年玉泉寺の境内西側を社地とした。"
> (translated: "Two shrines exist in the preservation district. Suwa Shrine was rebuilt in the late Meiji era, a composite hall connecting haiden [worship hall], heiden [offering hall] and honden [sanctuary]. Izuno-Sugawara Shrine, called Gyokusenji Tenmangu, formerly stood inside Gyokusen-ji temple. It separated and became independent after the Shinto-Buddhist separation edict of early Meiji, but after an 1871 fire the following year it took the west side of Gyokusen-ji temple's own precinct as its shrine ground [社地].")

Building ledger: "35 C18 社殿 １棟...（諏訪神社）/ 36 C18-2 社務所 １棟...（諏訪神社）/ 37 C18-3 稲荷社 １棟...（諏訪神社）" - Suwa Shrine: one main hall, one shrine office, one subsidiary Inari shrine on a single small lot, no separately described large grounds.

Informal modern comparandum for "how small": https://ansin-kouji.com/tochi/oinarisan.html - "庭のスペースを一坪ほど使用して建てられていることが多く" (household Inari shrines are often built using about one tsubo [3.3 m2] of garden space) and commonly have a torii installed. (Modern/informal, not a historical register, flagged as such.)

Closest historical-register comparandum (rural, not castle-town - flagged as an order-of-magnitude floor only): a 1915 Kanzaki District shrine register quoted at http://www5b.biglobe.ne.jp/~kusidagu/siryou/chinju.html gives a Tenjin-sha explicitly sited within a machi address at "面積 35平方メートル86" (35.86 m2 / about 10.85 tsubo) - one of the smallest hard shrine-precinct figures found in this whole pass, though it is a hamlet-register shrine, not an intramural castle-town ward shrine specifically. Nine other unranked (mukakusha/zassha) hamlet shrines in the same register: 312, 50, 61, 662, 148, 79, 85, 622, 180 tsubo (median 148, mean 244.3, n=9) - order-of-magnitude context only.

**Absence note:** no Edo-period register giving a castle-town ward's own tutelary (chinju) or street-corner Inari's precinct area, specifically inside a jokamachi's built-up cho, was found.
