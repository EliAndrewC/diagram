# R2a - Town monastery history: county-seat temple counts, household, precinct, location

Research reader pass. All quotes below are verbatim from pages actually fetched (WebFetch), with URL,
original-language text, and my English translation. Nothing is from memory or from WebSearch snippet
summaries alone unless explicitly marked "(search-synthesis, not directly fetched - treat as weak)".

## Q1. How many temples (and monks) did a county-seat-sized town keep?

### Japan

**Amagasaki teramachi** (castle town, Settsu Province) - city of Amagasaki, cultural-property page:
<https://www.city.amagasaki.hyogo.jp/manabu/bunkazai_0/1028308/1028344/1028717.html>

> 江戸時代初期（1635年）の城下絵図には20か寺の寺院が確認できますが、今日に至るまでには藩主の交替に伴う移転や廃寺などがあり、現在は11か寺が軒を連ねています。

Translation: "An early Edo-period (1635) city map confirms 20 temples; through relocations and closures
accompanying changes of domain lord, 11 temples stand today."

**Miyazu teramachi** (small castle town, Tango Province) - Miyazu city history page:
<https://www.city.miyazu.kyoto.jp/site/citypro/5091.html>

> 一二におよぶ寺院が集中して展開します。
> 浄土宗六寺、浄土真宗二寺、日蓮宗三寺、臨済宗一寺、曹洞宗一寺と大きな偏りがみられます。

Translation: "About twelve temples are concentrated [in the teramachi]." / "Six Jodo-shu, two Jodo
Shinshu, three Nichiren, one Rinzai, one Soto temples - a marked sectarian skew."
Also: "宮津城下町の建設にともない、計画的に「寺町」として整備されたと考えられます。" ("It is thought
the teramachi was planned and built as part of the castle town's original construction.")

Miyazu is a genuinely small castle town (comparable to a large county seat); Amagasaki is larger but
both give real per-town whole-teramachi counts, ALL SECTS COMBINED, not per-Order. Neither page states
how many temples belonged to any single Order alone - for the "at least one per Order" design premise,
these numbers (11-20 for a whole town) are the best directly-sourced anchor for "how many temples does
a modest town's temple quarter hold in total."

**Takada teramachi** (larger castle town, Echigo Province) - J-STAGE abstract, Mano Toshikazu,
"近世城下町における寺町と寺院 - 越後国高田寺町の場合" [Teramachi and Temples in Early-Modern Castle
Towns: The Case of Takada Teramachi, Echigo], *Kiyoshiono Kyodo Shiryoushitsu Gakujutsu Kenkyu-bu
Kenkyu Kiyou* vol.1 no.1 (2016), pp.1-25. <https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kfa/1/1/1_1/_article/-char/ja/>

> 本坊・子院をふくめて一六二もの寺院が集まっていたと推定される

Translation: "It is estimated that as many as 162 temples, including main temples and sub-temples,
were gathered [in Takada's teramachi]." Also: "城下町全体は武士の居住地、一般町民の居住地、そして寺町
という三つの空間に領域化されており" ("the whole castle town was zoned into three spatial domains:
samurai residences, commoner residences, and the teramachi.") NOTE: Takada was a substantial castle
town (domain seat), larger than a county-seat-scale town; 162 is not a good analog for a 2,000-10,000
population town - flagged as SCALE MISMATCH, included only for the main-temple/sub-temple ("本坊・子院")
counting convention.

GAP: no page was fetched that gives a temple count broken down BY religious Order for a town in the
2,000-10,000 population band specifically (post town/market town, not a domain-seat castle town). The
NILIM technical report (blocked, see below) looked likely to have castle-town population bands
(4,000-120,000 people, most ~10,000, per a WebSearch synthesis I could not verify against the actual
PDF text - flag as search-synthesis, not directly fetched, treat as weak until confirmed).

### China

**Ding County (定县), Hebei** - Republican-era social survey led by Li Jinghan (李景汉), based on 1930
(Minguo 19) village-by-village survey (search-synthesis of secondary pages about 李景汉《定县社会概况
调查》, not a page I fetched and quoted verbatim - treat as weak pending direct fetch):

> 根据民国19年（1930年）的各村概况调查，全县有庙宇879座，其中有庙会的至少有50座。

Translation (as reported by the secondary page, unverified against Li's original text): "According to
the 1930 village-by-village survey, the whole county had 879 temples/shrines, of which at least 50
held temple fairs." IMPORTANT CAVEAT: this is COUNTY-WIDE (every village plus the county seat), not a
count for the walled county town alone - not directly usable for "how many temples inside the seat."

**Nanjing (Wanli-era religious reform)** - zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/万历南京宗教改革 (search-synthesis,
not directly fetched):

> 计入《金陵梵刹志》者共计176所

Translation: "176 temples in total were recorded in the *Jinling Fancha Zhi* [gazetteer of Nanjing's
Buddhist establishments]." This covers Nanjing city plus its two adjoining counties (Shangyuan and
Jiangning) - a major metropolis, not a county-seat scale town. SCALE MISMATCH, included only to show
that Ming gazetteers DID enumerate temple counts precinct-by-precinct at this level of documentation.

**National aggregate** (Kangxi 6 / 1667), from a secondary page, unverified/weak: "礼部根据各省巡抚
报告所做统计，全国大约分布有寺庙道观八万处" - "the Ministry of Rites' compilation of provincial
governors' reports found roughly 80,000 temples and Daoist monasteries nationwide." National, not
county-level; not usable for per-town counts.

**Spatial/siting convention (from a walled county-seat example)** - zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/平遥古城
(Pingyao, a preserved Ming-Qing county seat), fetched directly:

> 城东有城隍庙，城西有平遥县署，城左立文庙、城右立武庙，东道观西佛寺

Translation: "City God Temple to the city's east, the Pingyao county yamen to the west; Confucian
Temple on the left (east), Martial Temple (Guandi) on the right (west) [of the yamen]; a Daoist
monastery to the east, a Buddhist temple to the west." This documents the canonical PAIRED,
axis-relative siting rule for a Chinese county seat's temples (relative to the yamen, not "edge of
town" the way Japanese teramachi are) - directly answers Q4 for China with a real example, though it
names one Buddhist temple, one Daoist monastery, plus the City God/Confucian/Martial temples (state
cult, not Buddhist "monastery" per se). GAP: no monk-count or precinct-area figure came with it.

No source was fetched giving a full monk-count-per-monastery table for a Chinese county seat. The
single most promising source for this (Goossaert's 1736-1739 clergy census, which is organized by
province/county) could not be read - see Blocked Sources below.

## Q2. Who lived in a town temple: head priest, family, disciples, servants, sub-temples?

### Japan

Eijuji (永住寺), Shinshiro City - municipal cultural-property page, fetched directly:
<https://www.city.shinshiro.lg.jp/kanko/minzokugeino/eijuji-hondo.html>

Precinct inventory quoted from the page: 本堂 (main hall), 開山堂及び位牌堂 (founder's hall/memorial-
tablet hall), 庫裡及び書院 (kitchen-residence and study), 禅堂 (meditation hall), 衆寮 (monks'
dormitory), 経蔵 (sutra repository), 山門 (gate, built 1627).

> 前庭を中心にして東側に庫裡及び書院、西側に衆寮、禅堂

Translation: "Centered on the front courtyard: kitchen-residence and study to the east, monks'
dormitory and meditation hall to the west." This CONFIRMS a resident-monk dormitory (衆寮) as a
standard precinct building distinct from the head priest's kuri, and a separate founder's-hall/
memorial-tablet hall - real evidence of a multi-person live-in community, though Eijuji is a Zen
training temple (larger monk complement than an ordinary town temple) and no head count is given.
CAVEAT: located at "新城市字裏野" - not confirmed as urban/county-seat siting; likely a rural/temple-
complex setting rather than a town-center parish temple, so its monk-dormitory scale may overstate an
ordinary town temple's household.

General secondary claim (search-synthesis of NDL/city-history pages, weak, not directly quoted from a
fetched page): most Edo-period monks were younger sons of peasant or samurai families, ordained in
their early teens, and moved between temples within their sect while advancing in rank toward becoming
a 住職 (resident head priest) - describes career structure, gives NO numeric household composition.

GAP: no page was fetched giving an explicit head-count (e.g., "1 住職 + 2 弟子 + 1 寺男") for an
ordinary town temple. This is the weakest-supported of the four questions for Japan.

## Q3. Precinct size, main-hall size, wall/fence, and what stood in the precinct

### Japan

**Record format** (what 寺院明細帳 / 本末寺号其外明細帳 recorded) - from NDL's リサーチ・ナビ page and
a related search-synthesis (weak - summarized by the search tool from multiple result snippets, not
directly quoted from one fetched page):

> [content items were] 本山、所在地、寺名、住職名、境内坪数、檀家数、元朱印地高・反別、除地山林反別

Translation (paraphrase from synthesis): head temple, location, temple name, resident priest's name,
PRECINCT AREA IN TSUBO (境内坪数), number of parishioner households (檀家数), former shogunal-grant
land assessment and acreage, tax-exempt forest acreage. This confirms tsubo was the standard unit these
registers used for precinct area - but no specific town-temple's tsubo figure was found in a page I
could actually fetch and quote.

**Main-hall size anchors actually fetched:**

- Zenkoji main hall (Nagano) - adeac.jp, Nagano City history, fetched directly:
  <https://adeac.jp/nagano-city/texthtml/d100040/ct00000004/ht001930>
  > この坪（建坪、床面積） 三八七坪三合（約一二八〇平方メートル。向拝部分をふくめると約一四二〇平方メートル）
  Translation: "Floor area: 387.3 tsubo (about 1,280 sqm; about 1,420 sqm including the front canopy),"
  main hall completed Hoei 4 (1707). CAVEAT: Zenkoji is a major national pilgrimage temple, vastly
  larger than any ordinary town temple - useful only as an upper-bound scale anchor, NOT a town-temple
  example.
- Eijuji main hall (Shinshiro) - same page as Q2:
  > 桁行6間（16.57m）、梁間7間（14.290m）
  Translation: "6 ken (16.57 m) ridge-direction by 7 ken (14.29 m) beam-direction," built Genroku 10
  (1697). This is a plausible modest-temple hall footprint (roughly 15 x 16 m / ~48 x 52 ft) - the best
  directly-sourced hall-size anchor found, though again a Zen training temple, not confirmed as a
  town-center parish temple.
- Enryuji (Onomichi region?) history page, fetched via search-synthesis only (weak, not independently
  re-fetched and verified): "granted approximately 1,600 tsubo (~5,300 sqm) of land when founded in
  1619" - precinct-size data point, but no town-size context was captured, and it was not read from the
  page directly (summarized by the search tool), so treat as unverified.

Walls/fences: no page was fetched that discusses whether an ordinary town temple's precinct was

## Q4. Where in the town did temples stand?

### Japan - well supported.

ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寺町, fetched directly:
> 江戸時代以前、敵襲に際して防衛線とする意図で、多くの城下町において寺院や墓地が市街の外縁にまとめられた。

Translation: "Before/through the Edo period, in many castle towns, temples and graveyards were
consolidated at the OUTER EDGE of the urban area, with the intent of forming a defensive line against
enemy attack."

Kotobank, 改訂新版 世界大百科事典 entry for 寺町, fetched directly:
<https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AF%BA%E7%94%BA-101789>
> 城下町の外郭に寺院が「集団的・連続的に配置された」部分...「主として非常時における軍事的意義を考慮
> して配置されていた」

Translation: "the part of a castle town's outer perimeter where temples were arranged collectively and
continuously... positioned chiefly in consideration of their military significance in an emergency."

Amagasaki teramachi page (as Q1), fetched directly:
> 寺町は城下町の北西隅、武家屋敷の北側に当たり、町場から分離して寺院の力を弱めるとともに、巨大な建物
> 群である寺院を配置して城に対する防備の役割をもたす目的があったとも考えられています。

Translation: "The teramachi occupies the castle town's northwest corner, north of the samurai
quarter; it is thought the aim was both to weaken temple [political] power by separating it from the
commoners' town, and to use the temples' massive building clusters for the castle's defense."

Miyazu: teramachi in the "南西部" (southwest part) of the former castle town (Q1 source, same page).

Consistent pattern across all three fetched Japanese sources: temples clustered at the TOWN EDGE /
PERIMETER, deliberately separated from the commoners' quarter, for defense and to curb religious
political influence. This is a real, well-triangulated finding (3 independent pages agree).

### China - one example, partial.

Pingyao (Q1 source above): temples sited in fixed cardinal/paired positions RELATIVE TO THE YAMEN
(county office), not clustered at the town edge - a structurally different siting logic from Japan's
teramachi. Only one walled-seat example was actually fetched; this should not yet be treated as a
general rule without a second confirming example (e.g., another 县志-documented county seat).
