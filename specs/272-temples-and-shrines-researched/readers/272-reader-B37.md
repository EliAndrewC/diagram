## Q1. Monk counts per city temple

### Sensoji (Asakusa), Edo - large city temple, reorganization structure
Source: ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/浅草寺 (fetched via WebFetch)
> "商店は東側に54店、西側に35店を数える" - not Q1, see Q2.

Search-engine paraphrase (from soka.repo.nii.ac.jp, 近世後期における浅草寺別当代の就任過程,
Soka University repository PDF - could NOT be fetched verbatim, see blocked list) states, per the
WebSearch summarizer:
> PARAPHRASE, NOT VERBATIM: "The abbot... categorized the monks within the temple into 'shuto'
> (学僧/scholar monks) and 'terasu' (寺僧/prayer monks). He established 12 shuto temples and 21
> terasu temples (later increased to 22), reorganizing over 100 sub-temples within the compound
> into about 60."
URL: https://soka.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/35917/files/sokahogaku28_1_2.pdf (page itself blocked - PDF
binary, see blocked list; treat this number as UNVERIFIED pending a direct read)

This is the closest hit for a documented large-city-temple clergy structure: Sensoji did not have
one "monk count" but a federation of ~60 sub-temples (each presumably housing its own small
resident clergy), split administratively into scholar-monk and ritual-monk lineages. No aggregate
head-count of individual monks was found on any fetchable page.

### Nakamise - Edo-period shop types and origin, verbatim
Same page (ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/仲見世通り):
> "江戸時代前期に浅草寺の境内や参道の清掃の賦役が課せられていた地元住民に対して、特権として参道
> 等での営業の特権が与えられたのが起源"
> (English: "The origin was that, in the early Edo period, local residents who were obligated to
> clean the temple precinct and approach road were granted, as a privilege, the right to trade
> along the approach road etc.")
>
> "元禄から享保年間（一説には貞享2年（1685年）頃）にかけて形成された日本で最も古い商店街の一つ
> とされる"
> (English: "Formed between the Genroku and Kyoho eras [one theory dates it to around 1685],
> considered one of Japan's oldest shopping streets")
>
> "伝法院から仁王門寄りの店を役店（やくだな）と呼び、20件の水茶屋が並び、雷門寄りは平店（ひらみせ）
> と呼び、玩具、菓子、みやげ品などを売っており"
> (English: "The shops nearer Nioumon from Denboin were called 'yakudana' [service stalls], where
> twenty water-tea stalls [mizu-jaya] stood in a row; those nearer Kaminarimon were called
> 'hiramise' [flat shops], selling toys, sweets, souvenir goods, etc.")
URL: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BB%B2%E8%A6%8B%E4%B8%96%E9%80%9A%E3%82%8A

## Q3. Graveyards: parish-only or shared ground; size against precinct

No public page gave a quantified ratio of graveyard area to temple precinct area for a city temple.
General/definitional findings only:

Source: ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寺町 (fetched, verbatim)
> "寺町（てらまち）とは、日本の都市の中で寺院が集中して配置された地域のこと。および、それに由来
> する日本各地の町名。"
> (English: "Teramachi is an area within a Japanese city where temples are densely concentrated,
> and place-names throughout Japan derived from this.")
>
> "江戸時代以前、敵襲に際して防衛線とする意図で、多くの城下町において寺院や墓地が市街の外縁に
> まとめられた。"
> (English: "Before the Edo period, with the intent of forming a defensive line against enemy
> attack, temples and graveyards were consolidated on the outer edge of the city in many castle
> towns.")
URL: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%AF%BA%E7%94%BA

This establishes graveyards as bundled with temples at the city margin (defensive/zoning logic),
consistent with the danka (檀家/parishioner) system, but the page names no danka-vs-shared-ground
split and gives no size ratio. Direct searches on "檀家 墓地 敷地 坪" returned only modern
cemetery-service pricing pages, not Edo-period precinct data - none usable.
