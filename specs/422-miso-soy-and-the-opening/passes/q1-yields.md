I found modern recipe-level yields for miso. For soy sauce, no source I read states how many liters a kg of soybeans makes; the closest is a press yield from Noda in about 1900. Every ratio below marked "arithmetic" is my own calculation, using 1 sho = 1.804 L, 1 koku = 180.4 L, and the record's 129 kg of soybeans to a koku.

## (a) Miso per kg of dry soybeans

**A1. Kawaguchi city recipe sheet (modern home miso).** https://www.city.kawaguchi.lg.jp/material/files/group/183/misorecipe.pdf. The fetch tool could not read the PDF, so I read it with curl and pdftotext.
- 「大豆 ・・・ 約1kg ※この分量で、約3kgの味噌ができるので / こうじ ・・・ 約1kg それが入る容器(ﾀｯﾊﾟｰ・かめ等)を準備 / 塩 ・・・ 約500g」
- (translation) "Soybeans about 1 kg. Since this amount makes about 3 kg of miso, have a container that holds it ready. Koji about 1 kg. Salt about 500 g."
- The beans are dry: step ① soaks them overnight. The koji is weighed as finished koji, not as rice.
- Proportions: beans 1 : koji 1 : salt 0.5 by weight. Ageing is 「約１年」 (about a year).
- Arithmetic: 3 ÷ 1 = **3.0 kg of miso per kg of dry soybeans.**

**A2. Amagasaki city recipe sheet (modern, koji-heavy).** https://www.city.amagasaki.hyogo.jp/_res/projects/default_project/_page_/001/021/517/misodukuri.pdf, read with curl.
- 「【材料】できあがり約 2.5kg 分 大豆（乾燥） ５００ｇ 米こうじ １０００ｇ 粗塩 ３００ｇ（12～13％）」
- (translation) "Ingredients, for about 2.5 kg finished: dry soybeans 500 g, rice koji 1000 g, coarse salt 300 g (12-13%)."
- Its salt formula gives a factor for cooked beans: 「塩ｇ ÷（大豆(乾)g × 2.3 倍 + 麹ｇ + 塩ｇ ） = 約 12 %」 (translation) "salt ÷ (dry soybeans × 2.3 + koji + salt) = about 12%." In other words, cooked beans weigh 2.3 times the dry beans.
- Proportions: 1 : 2 : 0.6, so twice as much koji as beans. That is a sweet, koji-rich miso.
- Arithmetic: 2.5 ÷ 0.5 = **5.0 kg per kg of dry soybeans**, at this koji ratio only.

**A3. Marukome (miso maker), "Make miso by hand".** https://www.marukome.co.jp/miso/handmade/
- 「甘口味噌米こうじ300g 大豆250g 食塩120g 大豆の煮汁110ml 辛口味噌米こうじ300g 大豆430g 食塩155g 大豆の煮汁10ml ※上記の材料で約1.3kgの味噌を作れます。」
- (translation) "Sweet miso: rice koji 300 g, soybeans 250 g, salt 120 g, bean broth 110 ml. Salty miso: rice koji 300 g, soybeans 430 g, salt 155 g, broth 10 ml. These amounts make about 1.3 kg of miso."
- The beans are dry: they are soaked overnight in 900 ml or more of water.
- Arithmetic:
  - Salty miso: 1.3 ÷ 0.43 = **3.0 kg per kg of dry beans**, with koji at 0.7 times the beans.
  - Sweet miso: 1.3 ÷ 0.25 = 5.2 kg per kg.
- The page gives the same "about 1.3 kg" for both recipes, so treat it as rough.
- The page lists みそ健康づくり委員会 publications among its references.

**A4. みそ健康づくり委員会 (the miso industry's committee), 『みそ知り博士のQ&A 50』.** https://miso.or.jp/misoonline/wp-content/uploads/2012/07/misoqa1.pdf, read with curl. It gives proportions only, no yield.
- 「こうじ歩合＝ 米（麦）/大豆 × 10 … こうじ歩合10とは、大豆と米（麦）の使用量が同じであることを指す」
- (translation) "Koji ratio = rice (or barley) ÷ soybeans × 10. A koji ratio of 10 means the same amount of soybeans and rice (or barley) is used."
- Its Table 3 gives the ordinary salty red rice miso (仙台, 秋田, 津軽 and other regional misos) a koji ratio of 「6〜10（7）」 and salt 「11〜13（12.5）」, aged 「3〜12カ月」. (translation) "Koji ratio 6-10, typically 7; salt 11-13%, typically 12.5%; 3-12 months."

**A5. Niigata prefecture, Itoigawa farm-extension office (2023).** https://www.pref.niigata.lg.jp/sec/itoigawa_norin/nougyo-misoresipi.html
- 「麹歩合10割の場合塩分 13％大豆 15.0kg 麹 16.5kg （原材料米15kg）塩 7.8kg 種水 2リッター」
- (translation) "Koji ratio 10 (equal), salt 13%: soybeans 15.0 kg, koji 16.5 kg (from 15 kg of rice), salt 7.8 kg, seed water 2 L."
- It states no finished weight.

**A6. Kyoto prefecture, Tango farm-extension office.** https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/tango/no-tango-nokai/documents/42.pdf, read with curl. This recipe is measured by volume, like the Edo-period recipes.
- 「大豆……１升（１．３㎏）米こうじ……米１升分（１．６５㎏）食塩……６６０ｇ（仕上がり塩分濃度１１％）」
- (translation) "Soybeans 1 sho (1.3 kg); rice koji made from 1 sho of rice (1.65 kg); salt 660 g (finished salt 11%)."
- It states no finished weight.
- Arithmetic:
  - 1.3 kg a sho is 130 kg a koku, which agrees with the record's 129 kg.
  - Borrowing Amagasaki's 2.3 factor (a different source): 1.3 × 2.3 + 1.65 + 0.66 = 5.3 kg, or **about 4.1 kg per kg of dry beans**. That is a cross-source estimate, not a stated yield.

**Verdict (a): ATTESTED ONLY FOR modern home recipes from city and prefecture guides and one maker.** They state about **3 kg of miso per kg of dry soybeans** when koji is about equal to the beans or less (Kawaguchi at 1 : 1 : 0.5; Marukome's salty miso at 0.7 : 1). Koji-rich recipes state about 5 kg per kg.
- The "大豆1kg 麹1kg 塩0.45kg → 約4kg" figure the question mentions was **not found stated** anywhere I read. Amagasaki's own formula applied to Kawaguchi's amounts gives 2.3 + 1 + 0.5 = 3.8 kg (arithmetic), but Kawaguchi itself states 3 kg. Both are shown; I did not reconcile them.
- The industry committee and the official sources give koji ratios but no yield.

## (b) Soy sauce per kg of soybeans

**B1. Oguri Tomoyuki, 「醤油製造技術の系統化調査」, 国立科学博物館 技術の系統化調査報告 Vol.10 (2008).** https://sts.kahaku.go.jp/diversity/document/system/pdf/040.pdf
- The live URL now serves an HTML index page; the ledger had it as unreadable. I read the Wayback capture: https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/https://sts.kahaku.go.jp/diversity/document/system/pdf/040.pdf. The session should cite and archive it under one of the two URLs.
- Page 142 describes the 1900 主税局 (tax bureau) survey of Noda. The proportions are in a box that only the page image shows:
  - 「9水仕込：大豆15石、小麦15石、食塩13.5石 水27石（大豆、小麦元石に対し9分）」 (translation) "9-water setting: soybeans 15 koku, wheat 15 koku, salt 13.5 koku, water 27 koku (0.9 of the soybean-plus-wheat raw koku)."
  - 「10水仕込：大豆15石、小麦15石、食塩14.4石 水30石（大豆、小麦元石に対し10分）」 (translation) "10-water setting: the same, with salt 14.4 and water 30 koku."
  - 「一般的に10〜11.5水を通常としていたが、当時諸味に税が課されていたので…減水して仕込んでいたと思われる。」 (translation) "10-11.5 water was normal, but because moromi (the fermenting mash) was taxed, brewers seem to have set it with less water."
- The press yield, same page: 「従来は、諸味に対し生揚醤油の歩合は65〜68％であったが、螺旋機器を使うと70％以上となった。」 (translation) "Formerly, raw pressed soy sauce was 65-68% of the moromi; with screw presses it became over 70%." The page does not say whether this is by volume or by weight.
- How far this reaches back, page 139: 「明治初期頃までの醤油の製造法は、江戸時代後期の様相と大きな変化はなかったと思われる」 (translation) "Soy sauce making up to the early Meiji years seems not to have changed much from the late Edo period."
  - Dating: this is **1900 Noda practice**. The author assumes it continues from the Edo period; it is not premodern evidence itself.
- Arithmetic, mine, every step:
  - Fig. 3.7 lists tub capacity against "仕込石数", e.g. 「68〜70」 against 「36〜38」 and 「20〜21」 against 「10〜11」. I read 仕込石数 as raw-material koku, which is my interpretation. That makes capacity about 1.84-2.0 times the raw koku.
  - The text says tubs are filled to 「通常桶容量の9割以下」 (usually 90% or less), so moromi is at most about 1.66-1.8 times the raw koku.
  - Times 0.65-0.68 gives about 1.08-1.22 koku of raw sauce per raw koku.
  - One koku of soybeans goes with one of wheat, so that is about 2.16-2.45 koku of sauce per koku of soybeans: 390-442 L for 129 kg.
  - **About 3.0-3.4 L of raw soy sauce per kg of soybeans (with equal wheat). This is a derived upper bound and is not stated anywhere.**

**B2. The same report, Table 2.3 (after Iino Ryōichi's 「醤油の歩史3」 in Kikkoman's FOOD CULTURE), page 135.** It puts the Edo recipes and a modern one on one basis. The note under the table: 「原料配合の%は容量による」 (translation) "ingredient percentages are by volume." Percentages are soybeans / grain / salt / water:

| Source | Soybeans | Grain | Salt | Water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 多聞院日記, 1550 (唐味噌) | 17〜18 | 17〜18 | 17〜18 | 47〜48 |
| 料理物語, 1643 | 19.6 | 25.5 | 15.7 | 39.2 |
| 日本歳時記, 1687 | 19.2 | 19.2 | 19.2 | 42.3 |
| 本朝食鑑, 1695 | 約22 | 約22 (barley) | 約22 | 約34 |
| 和漢三才図会, 1712 | 18.2 | 18.2 | 18.2 | 45.5 |
| 萬金産業袋, 1732 | 20.8 | 20.8 (wheat) | 16.7 | 41.7 |
| 新撰包丁梯, 1803 | 18.2 | 18.2 | 18.2 | 45.5 |
| 広益国産考, 1844 | 約20 | 約20 (wheat) | 約20 | 約40 |
| **参考 丸大豆醤油, 1990〜** (modern whole-soybean brewing) | **約20** | **約19 (wheat)** | **約20** | **約41** |

- The report's own reading: 「原料は大豆と麦であり、その配合割合はほぼ等量」 (translation) "the raw materials are soybeans and grain, in nearly equal amounts."
- Its flow chart, Fig. 2.4, gives the general Edo ratio: 大豆 1.0 : 小麦 1.0〜1.3 : 食塩 0.8〜1.0 (0.8〜1.4水).
- **So the modern volumetric recipe is the same as the Edo ones**, and so is the 1900 Noda setting: by my arithmetic, 15 : 15 : 14.4 is 1 : 1 : 0.96.
- It also quotes the 1550 diary: 「唐味噌今日入了、大豆一斗三升、小麦一斗三升、塩一斗三升、水三斗三升入了、水ハ惣シテ一色ノ升数ノ三倍也」 (translation) "Set the Chinese-style miso today: soybeans 1 to 3 sho, wheat 1 to 3 sho, salt 1 to 3 sho, water 3 to 3 sho; the water is in all three times the measure of one ingredient."

**B3. A schoolchild's prize essay, soysauce.or.jp (weak; 2023 contest file).** https://www.soysauce.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/6268ba1045710196651a2962c5e9f36d.pdf
- 「ハチマルさんは小麦、六百キログラム、大豆、六百キログラム、塩、四百五十キログラムを三千リットル入るおけに入れているときいた。」
- (translation) "I heard that Hachimaru-san puts 600 kg of wheat, 600 kg of soybeans and 450 kg of salt into a tub that holds 3,000 liters."
- Arithmetic: a 3,000 L tub for 600 kg of soybeans, filled at most 90% and pressed at 65-70%, gives at most about 2.9-3.2 L per kg. That is consistent with B1. But this is tub capacity reported by a child, not a stated yield.

**Not a volume yield:** the report's 「原料利用率：70%以上」 (the 1950 standard) and 「窒素利用率」 measure how much of the raw materials' nitrogen ends up in the sauce. They are not liters per kg, and they should not be used as a yield.

**Verdict (b): ATTESTED ONLY FOR the press yield (raw sauce = 65-68% of moromi, Noda c. 1900, before screw presses) and for the proportions.** The proportions are equal volumes of soybeans, wheat and salt, with water at 0.9-1.0 of the soybeans plus wheat, and the modern volumetric recipe is identical. **Liters of soy sauce per kg of soybeans is NOT STATED by any source I read.** The derived figure of about 3.0-3.4 L of raw sauce per kg of soybeans depends on my reading of 仕込石数 and on the 90% fill.

- None of them states a yield. No Edo miso recipe with stated proportions is in the record; the only miso-like one is that hishio.
