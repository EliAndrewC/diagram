I found no figure for how much miso or soy sauce an ordinary Kyoto or Osaka adult used in the mid-1700s. What exists is soybeans per person going into miso or soy sauce, from rural manuals and domain estimates. No Edo-period recipe I read says how much miso or soy sauce a measure of beans made, so kg of miso and liters of soy sauce cannot be derived from premodern evidence. Bassino & Ma name no source for their 52 kg: it is the amount that makes their basket reach its calorie and protein target.

Unit used throughout (my arithmetic): 1 koku = 10 to = 100 sho = 180.4 L. Bassino & Ma's own conversion, 129 kg of soybeans a koku (from the official long-term statistics), turns bean volumes into kg.

## Sources that bear on it

**A. Bassino & Ma 2005, where the 52 kg comes from.** Archived: `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/7c/7c5d60ee3cc3/20261018T232430Z/text.txt` (works-cited `bassino-ma-2005`).
- 「Table 1 reconstructs two consumption baskets A and B based on available consumption data for staple food in the early 18th, late 19th (Kito, 1986, 1989; Umemura, Takamatsu, & Itoh, 1983), and the first surveys for non-staple consumption of the early 20th century (Toyo Keizai, 1980).」
- 「…construct consumption baskets at both ends that would satisfy an annual per capita requirement of 1,940 cal and 80 g of protein plus fixed quantities of linen and lamp oil.」
- Note to Table 1: 「Soybeans are used as a proxy for various processed products: soybean paste (miso), soy sauce (shoyu), tofu, and fermented soybeans (natto). Thus, our annual amount is higher than the actual consumption reported for the 1920s in surveys by the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (quoted in Toyo Keizai (1980), volume 2, p. 590)… The average per capita consumption for 1921–1925 was 18 kg beans (of which 12.9 kg soybeans)…」
- Table 2, soybean row: 「Soybeans (kg) 558 279 49 24」 (calories and grams of protein a day, baskets A and B).
- Arithmetic (mine): 52 kg × 3,920 cal/kg ÷ 365 = 558 cal a day, and 52 × 343 g/kg ÷ 365 = 49 g of protein a day. That is exactly the Table 2 row.
- Observation: in the same Table 1, Allen's European and Indian baskets each hold "52" liters of beans. The paper does not say whether the Japanese 52 is linked to that.
- **Verdict: NOT ATTESTED as a consumption figure.** No source is named for the 52 kg; it fills the 1,940-calorie, 80-gram-protein target. The paper's own comparison is 12.9 kg of soybeans in 1921-25, which is early-20th-century evidence, not premodern. The note does not say whether that 12.9 kg includes beans already made into miso and soy sauce.

**B. 『家訓全書』 (1760, Katakura village, Saku district, Shinano), as cited in Shigematsu 2022.** Archived: `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/fb/fb75c0555ba8/20261003T020501Z/page-cache-text.txt` (https://www.oceanochemistry.org/publications/TRIOC/PDF/trioc_2022_35_50.pdf). The text is laid out in two columns that interleave, so I rebuilt the sentence from the right-hand column:
- 「1760 年（宝暦 10）に信濃国佐久郡片倉村の依田惣藏が書いた『家訓全書』によると，一人当りの食糧の年間消費量は玄米 1.8 石，大麦 1 石，味噌用大豆 0.13 石である．」
- (translation) "According to the Kakun zensho written in 1760 by Yoda Sōzō of Katakura village, Saku district, Shinano, a person's yearly food is 1.8 koku of brown rice, 1 koku of barley and 0.13 koku of soybeans for miso."
- Arithmetic (mine): 0.13 koku × 129 kg = **16.8 kg of soybeans for miso a person a year**. "Per person" is a household average that includes children. It is not per adult, and it is not miso by weight.
- **Verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a Shinano farm household's planning figure in 1760, read second-hand through Shigematsu; the original is in 日本農書全集 vol. 24, which I did not read.

**C. Hirosaki domain estimate, 1816, same Shigematsu page.**
- 「弘前藩では，1816 年（文化 13）に農家 17.8 万人が米 33.04 万石を消費していると見積もっている．…それ以外に，総人口 20 万人分として味噌大豆 1.4 万石，糀米 0.7 万石，酒造 2 万石，餅米 0.167 万石，糊米 0.1 万石，酢・醤油・菓子 0.6 万石がある．」
- (translation) "Hirosaki domain in 1816 estimated that its 178,000 farmers ate 330,400 koku of rice… besides this, for a total population of 200,000, 14,000 koku of miso soybeans, 7,000 koku of koji rice, 20,000 for sake brewing, 1,670 of glutinous rice, 1,000 of paste rice, and 6,000 for vinegar, soy sauce and sweets."
- Arithmetic (mine): 14,000 ÷ 200,000 = 0.07 koku = **about 9.0 kg of miso soybeans a person**, children included.
- The 6,000 koku for vinegar, soy sauce and sweets sits in a list of rice uses, so it is probably rice, not soybeans. I did not use it.
- **Verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a northern domain's official estimate in 1816.

**D. 『廣益國産考』 (Ōkura Nagatsune, 1844), a household's soy sauce, as quoted by eonet.** http://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-4.html
- 「五人口に暮らせる家にては、酒樽の古きを三つ調ふべし。一樽に大豆六升大麦のつきたるを六升づつはつくられるもの也。三樽にては豆壱斗八升麦壱斗八升也。」
- (translation) "A household of five mouths should get three old sake barrels. Each barrel takes 6 sho of soybeans and 6 sho of polished barley; three barrels make 1 to 8 sho of beans and 1 to 8 sho of barley."
- 「何國にても家毎に造るは六月土用中に仕込むなり。」 (translation) "Everywhere, households that make it set it in during the midsummer doyō of the sixth month."
- 「是を樽に仕込むには、豆一斗八升ならば一升鹽のわりに壱斗八升鹽を入れ、水は三升のわりに五斗四升入るべし。」 (translation) "To set it in the barrels: for 1 to 8 sho of beans, put in salt at 1 sho per sho, 1 to 8 sho; and water at 3 sho per sho, 5 to 4 sho."
- The bracketed gloss 「四斗樽[豆六升麦六升仕込むたる也]」 makes each barrel a 4-to cask. The text also tells the reader to boil the pressed sauce again 「翌三月」, the following third month.
- Arithmetic (mine): 18 sho ÷ 5 = 3.6 sho = 0.036 koku × 129 = **about 4.6 kg of soybeans for soy sauce a person a year**, children included.
- "A year's supply" is implied by the once-a-year setting-in, not stated. The text gives inputs only; how much sauce comes out is not stated.
- **Verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a rural home-brewing prescription in 1844.

**E. Edo-period soy sauce recipes (same eonet page): proportions only, no yields.**
- 『本朝食鑑』 1697, in the site's Japanese translation: 「大豆一斗を水漬けしてから十分煮る。精白した大麦一斗を炒って挽ひいて粉にする。塩一斗と水一斗五から六升を混合したものと麹を一緒にして大桶の中に取る。」 (translation) "Soak 1 to of soybeans and boil well; roast and grind 1 to of polished barley; put 1 to of salt and 1 to 5-6 sho of water with the koji into a large tub."
- 『和漢三才図会』 1712, Japanese rendering: 「大豆一斗を水煮し、精麦一斗を妙りて、之れを粗磨し…別に塩一斗水二斗五升を用いて煎沸し…凡そ市鄽の醤油、皆な小麦を用ゆるなり」 (translation) "Boil 1 to of soybeans, roast and coarsely grind 1 to of polished grain… separately boil 1 to of salt with 2 to 5 sho of water… the soy sauce sold in shops is all made with wheat."
- Proportion: equal volumes of soybeans, grain and salt in every case.
- **Verdict: ATTESTED** for the proportions. **SILENT** on how much sauce a measure of beans made.

**F. The nearest miso-type proportion: 醤 / ひしお in 『和漢三才図会』 1712 (same eonet page).**
- 「醤倭名比之保、豆醢也、今造法、大豆一斗、炒麤磨去皮、精麦一斗、一夜浸水、豆麦混合、盦麹別用、醢二升六合水一斗…俗謂比之保未醤」
- (translation) "Jiang, in Japanese hishio, a bean paste. Present method: 1 to of soybeans, roasted, coarsely ground and skinned; 1 to of polished grain soaked overnight; beans and grain mixed and made into koji; separately 2 sho 6 gō of salt and 1 to of water… commonly called hishio-miso."
- This is hishio (未醤), not miso by name, and it gives no yield.
- **Verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** hishio's proportions.

**G. Edo soy sauce arrivals, 1821 (secondary sources only).**
- https://s-shoyu.com/knowledge/0602/: 「1821年の醤油問屋の上申書によると125万樽のうち下り醤油はわずか2万樽にまで減少しています。」 (translation) "According to the soy sauce wholesalers' petition of 1821, Kansai soy sauce had fallen to only 20,000 of 1,250,000 barrels."
- http://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-17.html: 「文政四年(1821)の統計によると、江戸に入る醤油の数量は年間125万樽で、その約9割が下総国(千葉県)の銚子や野田から運ばれてくる「地廻り醤油(濃口醤油)」であった。」 (translation) "By the statistics of 1821, 1,250,000 barrels of soy sauce entered Edo a year, about 90% Kantō dark soy sauce from Chōshi and Noda in Shimōsa."
- https://wheatbaku.exblog.jp/21689775/ (a blog, read through WebFetch): 「文政４年（1821）の江戸への醤油の入荷量は、全体で１２５万樽（１樽７．５升）」 (translation) "In 1821 soy sauce arriving in Edo totalled 1.25 million barrels (1 barrel = 7.5 sho)."
- The eonet reference-4 page has a different barrel: 「野田の（亀甲萬）ハ上品にして八升六合入を一樽と定む」 (translation) "Noda's Kikkōman is the top grade, and a barrel is fixed at 8 sho 6 gō."
- Arithmetic (mine):
  - At 7.5 sho a barrel: 1,250,000 × 7.5 × 1.804 L ≈ 16.9 million L.
  - At 8.6 sho a barrel: ≈ 19.4 million L.
  - The only Edo population in the record is "one million" for the early 1700s (`edo-josui`, in 0141), not 1821. Over an assumed 1.0-1.2 million people, that gives **about 14-19 L a person a year**.
- This counts all arrivals, including restaurants, shops and goods shipped on. It is an upper bound on household use, not a laborer's figure.
- **Verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** total arrivals in Edo in 1821, from secondary sites that do not name the original document.

**H. Shinshu "to-miso" (a 20th-century folk memory, not premodern evidence).** https://matsu-haku.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/159.pdf, a Matsumoto museum exhibition leaflet. `make source-pages` could not read the PDF, so I read it through curl and pdftotext.
- 「毎年､家族1人について用意した大豆の量は5升(9リットル)から1斗(18リットル)であったとされ､ひと口に｢斗味噌｣といいました｡」
- (translation) "Each year the soybeans prepared per family member are said to have been 5 sho to 1 to; this was called 'to-miso'."
- It agrees with 『家訓全書』's 1.3 to, but it describes 20th-century farm practice. It is not evidence for the Edo period.

## Reading

**SILENT** for the question as asked: I found no miso or soy sauce consumption figure for a Kyoto or Osaka laborer, or for any Edo-period city household. The nearest figures are in soybeans, not product:
- Beans for miso: 0.13 koku a person a year on a Shinano farm in 1760 (about 16.8 kg, my arithmetic), and 0.07 koku in Hirosaki's 1816 estimate (about 9 kg).
- Beans for soy sauce: 3.6 sho a person in an 1844 farm manual's home brew (about 4.6 kg).

All three are rural averages that include children, while the basket is per adult. The recipes give the proportions (soybeans, grain and salt in equal volumes for soy sauce), but none gives a yield. So **kg of miso and liters of soy sauce per kg of beans are SILENT** in the premodern record I read. Turning beans into miso and soy sauce would need a modern ratio, and that would be a modern figure, not an Edo one. The share going to tofu and natto is **SILENT**. Putting the Shinano miso beans beside the 1844 soy sauce beans gives about 21 kg, which would leave about 31 kg of the 52 kg for tofu and natto. That combines two sources, and nothing attests the remainder. For Edo as a whole, the 1821 arrivals give at most about 14-19 L of soy sauce a person a year.
