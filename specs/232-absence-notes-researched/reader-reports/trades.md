## 1. `cities/capitals.html` fn-70 - Edo's pawnshop count

**Verdict: CITED.**

URL: <https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B3%AA%E5%B1%8B-73884> (Kotobank's entry for 質屋, which
reprints two published encyclopedias - Heibonsha's 世界大百科事典 and Shogakukan's
日本大百科全書(ニッポニカ). Not an AI-generated encyclopedia; each block is signed with its
publisher at the foot.)

Passage 1, verbatim (世界大百科事典, Heibonsha):

> 江戸時代の質屋は，三都（大坂，京都，江戸）を中心とする貨幣経済と技術文化の浸透が地方に展開するなかで，民衆生活を補完する短期動産金融業者である。18世紀江戸では2700軒，19世紀中ごろの大坂では2400軒の質屋が存在した。

English translation by this reader (the original is above as the checker's anchor): "The
pawnbroker of the Edo period was a short-term lender on movable property, supplementing the
lives of the common population as the money economy and the culture of technique, centered on
the three cities (Osaka, Kyoto, Edo), spread out into the provinces. In eighteenth-century Edo
there were 2,700 pawnshops, and in Osaka in the middle of the nineteenth century 2,400."

Passage 2, verbatim (日本大百科全書(ニッポニカ), Shogakukan) - the same count with a date and a
source shape, plus Kyoto and Osaka for comparison:

> 1723年（享保8）の江戸質屋仲間2731、1699年（元禄12）の京都質屋628、幕末の大坂質屋2420という数字からも、近世庶民金融に占める質屋の地位は想像できよう。

English translation by this reader: "From the figures of 2,731 in the Edo pawnbrokers' guild in
1723 (Kyoho 8), 628 Kyoto pawnshops in 1699 (Genroku 12), and 2,420 Osaka pawnshops at the end
of the shogunate, one can imagine the place the pawnshop held in early-modern popular finance."

How it supports the claim: the record's "Edo: 2,700+ pawnshops" is exactly these numbers -
2,700 in the eighteenth century, 2,731 counted in the guild in 1723. The per-capita ratio the
record draws from it ("one per ~400 residents") remains the record's own arithmetic against an
Edo population it takes from elsewhere; 2,731 shops against a million inhabitants is one per
about 370, so the stated ratio is of the right order but is a derivation, not a quotation.
The earlier pass looked at the Japanese encyclopedia article on 質屋 and found no count; the
count is one level down, in the published encyclopedias Kotobank reprints.

## 2. `cities/defenses.html` fn-17 - why the wall towers are rectangular and never round

**What is citable, piece one - the projection exists for flanking fire.**

English translation by this reader: "The mamian are platforms built one after another against the
outside of the wall; in war they make it convenient to strike the besieging enemy from two sides.
Sometimes a small wall is built round the three outer sides of a gate to strengthen the defense

**Piece two - the shapes and the spacing, measured, on the city the paragraph names.** The Chinese
Wikipedia article on 西安城墙, read through the MediaWiki API:

> 墙的外壁筑98座敌台，延伸出墙12米，宽20米，高与城齐，各自相距120米，恰在弓箭和火铳的射程范围内。敌台上筑敌楼，可驻士兵和储存物资。城四隅各有一座角楼，除西南隅沿袭了元代建筑为圆形外，其余三座均为方形。

English translation by this reader: "Ninety-eight enemy platforms are built on the outer face of
the wall, projecting 12 meters from it, 20 meters wide, level with the wall's height, and 120
meters apart from one another - just within the range of bow and handgun. On the platforms are
built enemy towers, which can quarter soldiers and store materiel. At each of the four corners of
the city is a corner tower; apart from the southwest corner, which follows the Yuan-dynasty
building and is round, the other three are all square."

That is the record's sentence in measured form: rectangular projections spaced at exactly one
bowshot so that the ground under the wall is covered from two sides, square corner towers, and
one round corner that is an inherited older piece. I am offering it with the project's own caveat
that this is an encyclopedia article - but the numbers are specific enough to be checked against
any survey of the Xi'an wall, and no better-provenanced page turned up in six queries.

**Piece three - what the round corner is, and the disagreement about its date.**
<https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260206A06Z5M00> (Tencent News, 2026, a popular-history piece,
fetched and read):

> 目前最权威、最受考古和历史学界认可的解释是：为了避开并利用隋唐长安城皇城的西南角遗址。

> 根据《明实录》的记载，明洪武三年（公元1370 年），大明王朝开始大规模地建造秦王府和西安明城墙。当年西安城墙西边和南边大部分沿用唐皇城旧址修筑，西南城角这个地方刚好有一个圆形基台，为了"省时省力"，工匠们就在原有形状的基础上改建而成，修建了这个圆形的城角。

English translation by this reader: "The explanation currently most authoritative and most
accepted by archaeology and history is: to avoid and reuse the southwest corner site of the
imperial city of Sui-Tang Chang'an." / "According to the Ming shilu, in the third year of Hongwu
(1370) the Ming began the large-scale building of the Prince of Qin's palace and the Xi'an Ming
wall. Most of the west and south sides of the Xi'an wall of that time were built along the old
site of the Tang imperial city, and at the southwest corner there happened to be a round base
platform, which the craftsmen rebuilt on its existing shape to save time and labor, making this
round city corner."

Note the disagreement a reader of the record should be told about: this page (and a 搜狐 repost of
a 城墙百问 column quoting 《西安城墙史话》, which also names Han Jian's late-Tang "new city" and a
1983 excavation) dates the round corner to the Tang imperial city; the Chinese Wikipedia article
dates it to the Yuan. Both agree it is an inherited older corner that a later rebuild went
around, which is the only thing the map rule needs - so the record can say "an older piece of
wall" and say plainly that the century is disputed, rather than dating it.

**Nothing at all was found on the Japanese half** - that yagura and tenshu are square or
rectangular timber on stone bases while the ishigaki batter is the curved element. The ja.wikipedia
櫓 article, read through the API, catalogues the word's senses (theater, sumo, fire tower, siege
tower) without giving a plan form. That half stays unsourced.

### Later addition: a scholarly page that CONTRADICTS the paragraph's central claim

A second reader found, and I then fetched and verified against the PDF's own text layer (not a
page image), a peer-reviewed architectural-history paper that says Chinese fortification DID build
round towers, deliberately, for a ballistic reason.

URL: <https://www.jgcm.ac.cn/jah/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.12329/20969368.2026.02011.pdf> -
刘炜・黄茜・徐腾「基于《武经总要》的北宋中期军事城池营建研究」,『建筑史学刊』2026年第2期, 122-132.

Verbatim (my own extraction; the paper sets two columns, so the sentence is reflowed here and each
fragment was checked separately):

> 城墙上每十步（约16米）设置一个马面，每个马面上都设有"敌棚"或"敌楼"（图3）。敌团的形制与马面相似，不同之处是敌团位于城角或瓮城转角并且呈弧形。"敌团"应是"圆形角楼"之意。角楼设置成弧形是为加大敌人攻击难度以及减少受攻击面积，这种形制在汉唐时的文献中未见记载，应是宋代的创制。

English translation by this reader: "A mamian is set every ten paces (about 16 m) along the wall,
and each mamian carries a 'dipeng' or a 'dilou' (Fig. 3). The form of the dituan resembles the
mamian's, the difference being that the dituan stands at a city corner or at the turn of a
barbican and is ARC-SHAPED. 'Dituan' should mean 'round corner tower'. The corner tower was made
arc-shaped in order to increase the difficulty of the enemy's attack and to reduce the area
exposed to attack; this form is not recorded in Han or Tang texts and should be a Song creation."

**What this does to the paragraph.** Three of its sentences do not survive as written:

- "A round tower is a European medieval signature" - no. The Song made round CORNER towers on purpose.
- The stated European/Chinese inversion - Europe shaping towers against the sapper, China for flanking volume - is not what this paper gives as the Chinese reason for the round form: it gives increasing the attacker's difficulty and REDUCING THE EXPOSED SURFACE, which is the European rationale, arrived at independently.
- "a round tower on a map says 'an older piece of wall', never simply 'a tower'" - the opposite of this paper's reading, in which the round corner is a Song INNOVATION, absent from Han and Tang.

What survives, and is now better sourced than before: the projections along the curtain (mamian)
are rectilinear and spaced at bowshot so the wall base is covered from two sides. The same paper
gathers the primary spacing records (『武经总要』, 『梦溪笔谈』, 『东京梦华录』, 『守城录』), and the
classic statement of the flanking rationale is Shen Kuo's, which the second reader found quoted on
a design-media page (<https://www.archcollege.com/45284.html>) rather than in a primary text
(ctext.org refuses fetchers): 「若马面长，则可反射城下攻者，兼密则矢石相及，敌人至城下者，则四面矢石临之。」
("If the mamian are long, one can shoot back at those attacking below the wall; and if they are
close-set, arrows and stones reach one another, so that an enemy who comes up to the wall has
arrows and stones bearing on him from all four sides.") That quotation is second-hand and should
be re-sourced to an edition of 《梦溪笔谈》 before it is used.

So the verdict on this note is upgraded from STILL ABSENT to **CONTRADICTED**: the record should
keep the rectangular mamian and the flanking logic, drop the European-signature framing, and
either drop or heavily qualify "a round tower says an older piece of wall" - which remains true
only of Xi'an's southwest corner specifically, whose own date is disputed (Tang imperial city
versus Yuan) as recorded above.

> 乾隆八年(1743年)，湖南巡抚蒋薄指示各州县组织救火义役，专门负责扑救火灾。这些救火义役都要登记造册，上报官府，由官府每年发给一定的银两。

English translation by this reader: "In 1743 the governor of Hunan, Jiang Bo, instructed every
prefecture and county to organize volunteer fire companies specifically responsible for
putting out fires. These companies were all to be registered on rolls and reported to the
authorities, which issued them a fixed sum of silver each year."

URL 2: <https://www.rujiazg.com/article/11577> - 吴钩, 「大宋消防队」 (an essay by a published
writer on Song society, quoting the Song documentary record directly; a serious secondary
source but not peer-reviewed, so it is offered second):

> 那么其他城市是不是也设立了专职的公共消防机构呢？当然。根据文献记载透露出来的信息，建康、会稽、泉州、静江等州府，均设有专业的潜火队、潜火衙兵，如《宋会要辑稿》载，南宋隆兴年间，建康府修建了一千间官屋，拨给马军司、"亲随衙兵及潜火官兵、吏舍、库局等使用"。到南宋后期，基本上所有州县城市及重要市镇都应该设置了专职的防火机构。只是地方消防队的规模与设施自然不及作为京城的汴梁与临安。

English translation by this reader: "So did other cities also establish full-time public
firefighting bodies? Of course. From what the documentary record discloses, Jiankang, Kuaiji,

Jiankang prefecture built a thousand official rooms, allotted to the cavalry command and 'the
retinue yamen soldiers and the fire-duty officers and men, clerks' quarters, stores and
offices'. By the late Southern Song, essentially all prefectural and county cities and
important market towns should have had a full-time fire-prevention body. It is only that the
scale and equipment of a local fire company naturally fell short of Bianliang's and Lin'an's

Note the honest limit, which the record should carry if it uses this: the last sentence is the
author's inference ("应该" - should have), while the named prefectures rest on the Song huiyao
jigao. The strong, documented part is that fire squads existed in prefectures other than the
capital, and that Ming and Qing county towns organized night patrols and volunteer fire
companies. What NO page I read attests for an ordinary market town is the thing the paragraph
is actually about - the brick lookout TOWER. So the record can stop saying it "cannot show
either way" about a fire WATCH, while keeping the tower itself as capital-attested only.

<https://www.zjstm.org/edu/science-magazine-detail.htm?magazineId=7155> - 浙江省科技馆

it is a code figure rather than one observation.

## 4. `cities/fabric.html` fn-36 - "each Edo machi block-group kept its own tower"

**Verdict: CONTRADICTED** on the tower (it was one tower to every TEN machi), and CITED on the
principle the sentence is there to carry (the watch was parceled out by neighborhood, and the

URL: <https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%81%AB%E3%81%AE%E8%A6%8B%E6%AB%93-120594> (Kotobank's 火の見櫓
