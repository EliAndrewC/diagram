### `cities/defenses.html` fn-17 (searched 2026-09-12)

*Under the heading:* Why are the wall towers rectangular and never round?

**The paragraph:** A round tower is a European medieval signature: a curved front resists a sapper's pick and, later, deflects a cannon shot. Chinese and Japanese fortification is rectilinear from the ground up, and the reason is an inversion of the European one that is worth keeping straight. Europe shaped its towers against the ATTACKER's siege engineering; China and Japan shaped them for the DEFENDER's volume of flanking fire, and a rectangular tower's flat faces let more archers line up and shoot straight down the wall base, leaving less dead ground beneath it than a curved face does. The same problem, the opposite answer. The genuinely round bastions that survive are archaic fragments a later rebuild went around - the roundel at the southwest corner of Xi'an's circuit is the example - so a round tower on a map says "an older piece of wall", never simply "a tower". Japanese yagura and tenshu are likewise square or rectangular timber on stone bases; the one thing Japan curves is the batter of the ishigaki WALL, not the towers standing on it. (This rests on general reading; no source is cited.) [HERE]

### `fields.html` fn-36 (searched 2026-09-06)

*Under the heading:* Tract sizes - no settlement-class cap

**The paragraph:** The town edge has NO tier of its own. A market town / county seat is the CENTER of a farmed hinterland (Skinner: the standard marketing community is ~18 villages over ~300-500 km2), [HERE] and cultivation historically pressed against the built edge - Chinese county seats with farmland to the walls and farmers walking out from town; Japanese post towns strung along highways through continuous paddy. Tract size is set by water, terrain, and mouths fed - never by settlement class. A mixed-use edge (urban core + hay/grazing + farms, the Hoshizora premise) legitimately carries anything from a hamlet-grade fan (~8-20 ac) where irrigable ground is short, through village-grade tracts (~50-90 ac), up to open farmland bounded only by the frame; the attested LOW end is terrain-limited (the upland Kiso post towns), and even there the limit is the terrain, not the town-ness. Small is legal where the map shows the terrain reason (hay country, forest, slope); tiny-with-full-waterworks is not attested anywhere.
