### `archetypes.html` fn-92 (searched 2026-09-12)

*Under the heading:* Why is a hand-piled bund never straight - and never square at the corners?

**The paragraph:** A farmer piling a rectangular basin out of puddled mud intends four straight sides and four right angles and gets neither, for two separate physical reasons worth drawing separately (this rests on general reading; no source is cited). [HERE] Corners round. A right angle in soft earth is the thinnest, least supported point of the whole ridge - it slumps under its own weight and under every rain - and it is the one spot on the bund that every foot, hoof and carrying pole cuts across instead of walking out to the point; the spring re-plastering restores the ridge, not the geometry, so a corner converges on a walked-and-slumped curve a few feet across. Edges wander. A run is paced and eyeballed between two corners, and re-cut a little differently every time a holding is split, sold or re-plastered, so a straight hundred-foot bund bows by a foot or two: straight in intent and to the eye at a distance, never to the ruler. The argument covers every earthwork the farmers piled, the dug ponds and the planted banks included, not only the ones holding fish.
