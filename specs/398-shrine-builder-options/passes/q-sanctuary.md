# Pass - sanctuary alternatives, halls and finishes (#7 Q1) (research-pass, 2026-10-19; its reply verbatim)

Five of the eight things you asked about came back SILENT. I found no Edo price for kasuga- or shinmei-zukuri sanctuaries, stone hokora, timber of any species, vermilion or bengara paint, or black lacquer. The new evidence is a carving contract for a small sanctuary (Tanashi, 1858) and line items from the Shibasaki Hachiman diary (1838–42). The diary entries quoted below come from the archived Kawakami 435 PDF, read off the page images rather than the garbled text layer.

## Reading, item by item

- **Kasuga-zukuri and shinmei-zukuri sanctuaries: SILENT.** Every small-sanctuary cost in the record is nagare-zukuri. Kawakami 420 gives the Nogami Kasuga shrine's sanctuary as nagare-zukuri too (「流造の本殿は…慶安元年（1648）に建立」), so its figures are not a kasuga-zukuri price.
- **Stone hokora: SILENT.** The nearest stonemason prices are the 1841 Shibasaki ones: a pair of stone lanterns for 4 ryō 3 bu, and 2 kan mon for laying the plinth stones. They are related stonework, not a hokora.
- **Cover-shed (覆屋): ATTESTED ONLY FOR a repair bundled with others,** covered by 0255 (Mitake). Tanashi says only that a wooden 覆殿 stood before 1972, with no cost.
- **Worship hall: covered by 0255** for the 11.5 ryō. **ATTESTED** additionally for 1841 Shibasaki fittings: a 1-ryō deposit on the fittings as a set, and 2 bu 2 shu as the final payment for twelve doors. The full fittings price is not given. **幣殿 (offering hall): SILENT.**
- **Timber: NOT priced.** The only entries are that cedar and pine at Hachiōji in 1841 were "out of stock and very dear", and that 1 ryō was sent to buy scaffold cedar. That is money carried, not a price paid. Ten woodcutters' wages and food came to "2 bu and some coppers"; the entry gives no number of days and leaves the copper amount unstated, so no day rate can be drawn from it. Hinoki against keyaki against sugi, per piece or per koku: SILENT.
- **Thatch: ATTESTED ONLY FOR quantity and labor.** At Shibasaki in 1840–41, 36 horse-loads of thatch grass went to the shrine, unpriced. Helpers on the roofs of both sanctuary and worship hall were paid 2 ryō 3 bu in wages and keep. The thatch thickness was reckoned at 1 shaku 2 sun (about 36 cm).
- **Shingle: ATTESTED ONLY FOR one board purchase.** At Shibasaki in 1840 the roofer got 3 bu extra and a 2-shu gift, and eight bundles of roof boards cost 2 bu 1 shu. The paper does not name the sanctuary's roof material or the bundle size. No per-tsubo price was found, nor any 木賊葺 price.
- **Copper roof: covered by 0256.**
- **Vermilion, bengara and black lacquer: SILENT.**
- **Carving: ATTESTED.** At Tanashi in 1858, 147 carved pieces (wall panels, beam-end noses, tie beams) were contracted for 135 ryō, on a one-bay sanctuary of 4.3-shaku bay. That is the closest size match in the record to the small sanctuary you asked about. The page itself calls the price exceptionally low. At 60 monme to the ryō, 135 ryō is 8,100 monme. Against 0256's Edo carpenter wage, that is about 1,080 days at the 1853–60 rate of 7.5 monme, or 1,600–2,000 days at the 1854 rates of 4 monme (or 3.75 monme) plus 1.2 monme for meals. Openwork transoms (欄間) were not priced separately anywhere: SILENT.
- **Wages:** none of these sources gives a new wage. The nearest for Shibasaki 1838–42 is 0255's Kumehara carpenter of 1830, 2 monme a day plus 1 monme allowance. The nearest unskilled wage is 0250's Kyoto rate of 1802–04, about 0.92 monme.

## Leads not followed
- 吉野禎次家文書 [G.30]「宮葺替諸入用覚帳」: the 1749 roofing ledger itself, which would give itemized materials.
- 小野武雄『江戸物価事典』 and 小柳津信郎『近世賃金物価史史料』, named on the NDL research guide (https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/rnavi/humanities/post_101127); these are the likely places for timber, lacquer and pigment prices.
- 「尉殿権現彫物積帳」 and 「普請請負一札」 (Tanashi, 1858), printed in the Tanashi city library's 『田無神社（2）』: the carving and building estimates in full.
- Kawakami 435 note 29: competing 1839 bids for the Fusai-ji bell tower at Shibasaki, 17 ryō 2 bu against 14 ryō plus 56 monme. It is a small-structure price, outside this question.
