# Pass - stone work (#7 Q2) (research-pass, 2026-10-19; its reply verbatim)

Only one of the eight items has an attested price: a pair of stone lanterns at 4 ryo 3 bu in 1841. The same diary gives a few smaller stonework figures. Neither the archive nor the web turned up any other price, and I found no named stonemason's day wage (石工 手間) as a rate.

## Sources

**1. Kawakami AIJ No. 435 (1992), archived copy** `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/1f/1fb356a8d7ca/20261019T050338Z/served.pdf`, p. 94, note 36 (I read it from the page image, not the OCR). The source is the diary 『公私日記』 of Suzuki Heikurō, headman of Shibasaki village (柴崎村), Tama district, Musashi (now Tachikawa). The work is the rebuilding of the village's Hachiman shrine, 1837–1841.

- **Lanterns: the year and place you asked to have checked.** The entry comes after 「同12年3月4日」, so it is Tenpō 12 (1841), 5th month, 2nd day:
  「5月2日「日野宝泉寺案内ニ而相州厚木町石工忠五郎来り，八幡宮石灯籠壱対八王子新町受取候代金四両三分ニ而，為受負手附金弐分也相渡，其序玄関敷石誂之事，但三方かまち斗金弐分弐朱之積也」」
  (translation) "Guided by Hōsen-ji of Hino, the mason Chūgorō of Atsugi town in Sagami came. For a pair of stone lanterns for the Hachiman shrine, to be received at Hachiōji Shinmachi, the price is 4 ryo 3 bu. A deposit of 2 bu was paid to bind the contract. On the same occasion paving stones for the entrance were ordered, but only the three-sided curb, estimated at 2 bu 2 shu."
  - 四両三分 is a single figure. It only looks split in the OCR text.
  - The 2-bu deposit belongs to the lantern contract. The paving is a separate order.
- **Foundation-stone work (亀腹, the stone plinth under the shrine).** Same note:
  - 天保11年8月3日「八幡宮亀波石積書三内隼八継ニ而返事有之に付，巴屋より相頼金弐両弐分弐朱ニ而相渡候積書状相頼」
    (translation) "A reply came on the estimate for the Hachiman shrine's plinth stones. A letter was sent asking the Tomoeya to pay 2 ryo 2 bu 2 shu."
    The note does not say whether this is the whole price of the stone or a partial payment.
  - 同12年3月4日「伊奈村（五日市町）石工八幡宮亀腹取合昼時着土台石取付，手重ニ付増人数四人分金壱分也相増候対談也」
    (translation) "The masons of Ina village arrived at noon to fit the plinth and set the foundation stones. Because the work was heavy, it was agreed to add the pay of four extra men, 1 bu."
  - 「八幡宮亀腹石十積賃銭弐貫文，同所立替之分今日相渡」
    (translation) "Today paid what the Tomoeya had advanced: 2 kanmon in carriage/stacking fees for the plinth stones."
- **Wages in the same paper.** Note 14 gives carpenters and sawyers at 9, 10 or 15 men per 1 bu (about 1.67 monme a day at most), and an Edo roofer at 「1人工あたり銀3匁」 (3 monme a man-day). These come from the Kasuga shrine ledger 「宮葺替諸入用覚帳」 in a Shimo-Moroka village document collection, not from Shibasaki in 1841. They appear to be the same Tama 1749 carpenter wage already in record 0257, though I did not confirm the date from this paper. The Shibasaki diary itself pays carpenters and laborers in lump sums only, not day rates.

## Verdicts

- **Stone torii:** covered by 0257 (Hatogamine, 3,200 ryo, 1708). Nothing new for a small torii.
- **Pair of stone lanterns: ATTESTED.** 4 ryo 3 bu, delivered, Shibasaki (Tama), 1841, by an Atsugi mason.
  - In 1841 money, the only same-place, same-year day rate is the extra-men payment of 1 bu for four men, which is 1 shu (about 3.75 monme) each. At that rate the pair cost about **76 man-days**.
  - The note does not say whether those four were masons or helpers, or that they were paid for one day each.
  - At the 1749 Tama carpenter wage of 1–1.67 monme it would be 171–285 carpenter-days, but that wage is 90 years earlier.
- **One tall lantern:** SILENT.
- **Mason-day (石工 手間): ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the 1841 Shibasaki entry of 1 bu for four extra men on the foundation-stone work, with the caveat above. Otherwise SILENT. I rejected kakeisi.com's "石切 3 monme a day including meals": it is undated and secondhand, citing a modern popular book (『江戸の家計簿』).
- **Stone stairway:** **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a shrine's stone plinth, not a stair: 2 ryo 2 bu 2 shu paid toward the plinth stones (1840), 2 kanmon carriage and stacking, and 1 bu for extra hands (1841). For the entrance curb stones, 2 bu 2 shu estimated.
- **Komainu or foxes, water basin, stele, five-ring pagoda, stone coffer: SILENT.**
  - **Archive:** searched one term at a time for 石灯籠, 石燈籠, 狛犬, 五輪塔, 墓石, 石段, 手水鉢, 石鳥居, 石碑, 石棺, 石櫃, 石工 and 石屋 (258 texts). I scanned every text for these words within 80 characters of a money term (両, 匁, 文). The only priced hit was Kawakami 1992; the rest were 両墓制 ("double-grave system") or 両側 ("both sides").
  - **Web queries:** 狛犬 奉納 代金 両 石工; 石工 手間賃 一日 匁; 石灯籠 一対 代金 両 奉納; 墓石 値段 江戸時代 両; 石鳥居 建立 入用 金 両 寄進帳; 手水鉢 奉納 金 両; 常夜灯 建立 費用; 狛犬 代金 尾道/岡崎; 関根達人 墓石 値段 (no results); 石燈籠 代金 両 天保 日記; 文政年間漫録 石切.
  - **Pages read with no bearing:**
    - Iwasaki 2016 on Oura stone: abstract only, no text.
    - Sugimoto, "Osaka masons and stone merchants" (Osaka Museum of History bulletin no. 17): about the trade and where masons worked, no prices.
    - Azumino dōsojin survey: its 帯代 sums (3 to 1,000 ryo) are the ritual betrothal gift of the paired deities, a Matsumoto custom, not the carving's cost.

All outcomes are on the ledger; nothing is marked cited.

## Leads not followed
- The 1841 entry's source, 『公私日記』 (Tachikawa City Board of Education edition, vols. 1–23, 1972–83), may hold more stone payments and day rates around 天保11–12年.
- 『江戸物価事典』 (Ono Takeo), for 石灯籠 and 墓石 entries.
- Sekine Tatsuhito's gravestone studies, for prices and orders.
- The second lantern's place, 八王子新町, suggests a Hachiōji stone dealer; Hachiōji city history may have more.
