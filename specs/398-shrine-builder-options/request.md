**[diagram]** Interim findings from the four search passes. These are **unchecked**: they become a record feature with the usual checks, and the checked answer will supersede this. Japan first, all from the Edo period unless marked. Where #6's answer (0255-0258) already covers an item, I say so.

## 1. Sanctuary alternatives, halls and finishes

- **Kasuga- and shinmei-zukuri sanctuaries: SILENT.** Every small-sanctuary cost found is nagare-zukuri (0255).
- **Stone hokora: SILENT.**
- **Cover-shed:** covered by 0255 (the Mitake repair, bundled).
- **Worship hall:** covered by 0255 (11 ryo 2 bu). Also from Shibasaki in 1841: a 1-ryo deposit on the hall's fittings as a set, and 2 bu 2 shu as the final payment for twelve doors. The offering hall (heiden) is SILENT.
- **Hinoki, keyaki and sugi timber: SILENT** (no price per piece or per koku). Shibasaki in 1841 found cedar and pine "out of stock and very dear".
- **Thatch: ATTESTED ONLY FOR quantity and labor** (Shibasaki, 1840-41). 36 horse-loads of thatch grass went to the shrine, unpriced. Helpers on the roofs of sanctuary and worship hall were paid 2 ryo 3 bu in wages and keep, for thatch reckoned 1 shaku 2 sun thick.
- **Shingle: ATTESTED ONLY FOR one board purchase** (Shibasaki, 1840): eight bundles of roof boards for 2 bu 1 shu. The roofer was given 3 bu extra and a 2-shu gift. No price per tsubo.
- **Copper roof:** covered by 0256.
- **Vermilion, bengara, black lacquer: SILENT.**
- **Carving: ATTESTED.** Tanashi shrine, 1858: 147 carved pieces (wall panels, beam-end noses, tie beams) on a one-bay sanctuary with a 4.3-shaku bay, contracted for 135 ryo, which the shrine's page calls "exceptionally low". *Arithmetic:* about 1,080 Edo carpenter-days at the 1853-60 rate. Kawagoe Hikawa (1842-49, a large all-keyaki sanctuary): 1,369 ryo excluding the roof, an upper bound only.
- **Openwork transoms: SILENT.**

## 2. Stone work

- **Stone torii:** covered by 0257 (a great torii, 3,200 ryo, an upper bound).
- **Pair of stone lanterns: ATTESTED.** 4 ryo 3 bu, delivered, Shibasaki (Tama), 1841, by a mason from Atsugi.
- **One tall lantern: SILENT.**
- **Mason-day: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** 1 bu for four extra men on the 1841 plinth work. That is about 3.75 monme each, if one day each; the source names neither the trade nor the days. Otherwise SILENT.
- **Stairway: ATTESTED ONLY FOR a plinth** (1840-41): 2 ryo 2 bu 2 shu toward the plinth stones, 2 kanmon for laying them, and 2 bu 2 shu estimated for the entrance curb.
- **Komainu or foxes, water basin, stele, gorinto, stone coffer: SILENT.**

## 3. Metal and furnishings

- **Sacred mirror, waniguchi gong, hanging lanterns, reliquary, offering chest and lock, lacquered chest: SILENT.**
- **The nearest is a metal floor.** Raw copper sold to the Osaka copper office (1774) at about 139-157 monme per 100 kin; copper for the home market was "about twice" the China export price. *Arithmetic:* about 1.5-1.7 Kyoto laborer-days per kin of raw copper.
- A Takaoka shrine mirror of 1833 weighs 34 kg (unpriced; it needed an official's gift and merchant subscriptions).
- **China fallback: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** coarse copper ware sold by weight, 0.27-0.28 tael a jin (early Qianlong).

## 4. Rites and the dead

- **Night enshrinement:** the cost share is covered by 0255/0256 (the 1749 Kasuga repair's ridge-raising, enshrinement and sundries were 20.0%). The priests' fee is SILENT. A 1549 Izumo list gives goods only, no money.
- **Goryo pacification festival: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** one Hakata ward's Gion float duty, 1838: 7,300 monme of silver (about 122 ryo at 60 monme), 800 of it procession money. This is a ward's festival duty, not a priests' rite or a village rite.
- **Eitai kuyo, a portion to Koyasan, washing bones: SILENT.**
- **Calligrapher's plaque fee: SILENT in Japan. ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a Chinese price list: Zheng Banqiao, Yangzhou, 1759, six taels a large piece, one tael a scroll or couplet. *Arithmetic:* six taels is about 150 days of Jiangsu's unskilled construction wage (1769).

## Day wages beside them

No source gives a new wage. The nearest are:
- **Craftsman:** Kumehara 1830, carpenter 2 monme plus 1 for food (0255); Edo carpenter 1853-60, 7.5 monme (0256).
- **Unskilled:** Kyoto 1802-04, about 0.92 monme (0250).
