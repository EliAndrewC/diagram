**Q1, is each factor derived?** No.
- **The rungs (derivation.md:21-24, :40).** Nothing keys the sites heap by (rung of the memoized fifth-nearest, walk), and nothing memoizes the ten test within a well. Without one of those, walking the rungs in turn reruns the ten test (the 9 + n term) up to four times a site. Taking the first admitted in walk order alone breaks the widening reach. State the key or the memo, and the count holds.
- **The fallback (derivation.md:24).** The pseudocode says nothing about the case where no site is admitted. The first well always hits it: every household is unserved, so every site in reach serves all n > 10. Keeping the fallback seat costs at least one comparison a site, and that is not in the row.

**Q2, a cheaper standard algorithm?** Yes, for the dry-households row (derivation.md:43).
- The row is a pass over the households inside the loop over the wells. That is the S3 shape verdict-1 Q2 flagged, moved to its own row rather than removed.
- It is also redundant. Any household a new well brings within 760 ft lies in that well's 3 by 3 cells of 760 ft, which the service row already re-keys. Whether anyone is still dry is the max-heap's top against 760 ft.
- Tighter bound: 0 for the row, plus 1 comparison a well. The cover by squares of 760 x root 2 ft bounds an optimal cover, not a greedy worst-served sequence, but that question goes away once the row is 0.

**Q3, is any factor padded?** Yes, twice.
- **The service row (derivation.md:42)** works out as (9 cells + n) x (1 + 2 x log2 n, rounded up), so the 9 cells are charged the push cost. Its own reason gives 9 + n x (1 + 2 x log2 n, rounded up): 458, 2,116 and 9,672, not 638, 2,548 and 10,680.
- **The dry row** counts 2 + cover = 4 and 6 wells at 20 and 40. The contract's total is 2 and 4 (contract.md:6).

**Q4, is each figure read at its source's size and unit?** No.
- **The sites a house (derivation.md:39).** 4 x 184 / 25 gives 30, the envelope's own perimeter. But a site is the well's center, and a well "off" the homestead has its center 12.4 ft outside the envelope. That path is about 736 + 2 x pi x 12.4 = 814 ft, so 33 sites, or 34 if the envelope is grown as a square. This understates the work rather than padding it; a budget set at 30 is one the rebuild cannot meet.
- **Two figures with no source in the Bounds:** "a part's side under 64 ft" (derivation.md:37), and the width of the way's verge. The 4-cell clearance query is right only if the radius plus the verge is at most 32 ft.

**Growth (S6).** The exponent from 20 to 80 is about 2.06 (1,751,242 / 101,416). The stated reason, that the 3 by 3 cells of 760 ft hold every household until about 150, is the kind of reason S6 accepts. Past 150 only the dry row would grow faster than linear, and Q2 removes it.
