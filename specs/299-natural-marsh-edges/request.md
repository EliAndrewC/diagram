## The session's proposal the GM said yes to (context, not the GM's words)

There is one scrub tile; the variety the GM sees is the individually scattered pines over it and the woods and farmsteads
cutting into it. The straight edges and the right angle are the marsh's own outline (the wet-toe band is laid as straight
strips), which the old reed scatter hid by thinning out over its last 46 ft. Proposed:

1. A natural outline: the marsh's corners rounded (a curve about 40 ft across) and a slow irregular wave along its open edges
   (roughly 10-25 ft in and out over a few hundred feet), applied before the fields and ponds are cut out of it, so where it
   meets a paddy it still follows the paddy's bank.
2. A mixed fringe where scrub meets marsh: a band about 30 ft wide along that wavy edge with its own tile - sparse reeds with
   grass - so the change is gradual rather than a meeting line.
3. More variety in the marsh, the way the pines give it to the scrub: over the regular reed tile, a second sparse tile with a
   different, larger repeat (about 97 ft against 64) carrying a few denser reed clumps and small open-water patches; the scrub
   given the same second layer so both covers are treated alike.

The marsh's recorded outline changes with the curved edge; that outline is no-build ground and what the scrub avoids, so a few
nearby placements could shift slightly, within the existing rules.
