## Q3 - 0248, how much rice was a koku

Verdicts:
- Koku volume ~180 L (budgets.md ~180 L, ~48 US gal): ATTESTED - 180.39 L, from the 1669 order (new Kyōmasu; in use from
  the ~1620s). The older Kyōmasu gave ~174 L (~3.6% smaller; our arithmetic on 62,500 vs 64,827 cubic bu).
- Domain measures: ATTESTED that some large domains kept their own; their capacity SILENT.
- ~150 kg a koku of brown rice (budgets.md "canonical"): ATTESTED ONLY FOR modern official reckoning, by analogy - 150 kg
  (1946-50 basis, 400 monme a shō = ~0.83 kg/L), 146.3 kg (a 1949 ministry table), 827 g/L measured 2023 (= ~149 kg).
- kg per liter of uncooked brown rice: ~0.83 (modern, by analogy).
- ~136 kg polished: ATTESTED ONLY FOR the milled yield of one koku of brown rice (en.wiki: 0.91 koku milled; polished
  rice ~150 g a gō, i.e. a koku-volume of polished rice weighs about the same as brown, ~150 kg). Another page gives a
  milling loss nearer 12% (4 to -> ~3.5 to).
- ~187 kg paddy: NOT ATTESTED by any page read. The one clue (a Heian bale) makes paddy LIGHTER per volume than brown
  rice: ~0.55 kg/L, a koku-volume ~100 kg (labeled GUESS). How much paddy makes a koku of brown rice: SILENT.
- ~138 kg per koku: NOT ATTESTED for Japan; the only 138 kg read is a koku-volume of polished barley in Korea.
- 0.021 koku/gallon: ATTESTED as arithmetic (1/48, a US gallon of a 180 L koku).
- ~1.5 koku (3.33 shi) of grain a person a year: NOT ATTESTED by anything read. The convention was 1 koku a year (3 gō a
  day, 333 days); the Edo stipend ration was 5 gō of brown rice a day, ~1.8 koku a year (a merchant house code reported
  to say the same 1.8). Bunongshu (Lower Yangzi, before 1644): 1.5 sheng a day, women half - sheng size not given.
- l7r.md "about 40 gallons": ATTESTED ONLY FOR imperial gallons (180 L = ~40 imp gal = 48 US gal). In US gallons, 40
  gallons = 151.4 L, ~16% short of a koku.
- l7r.md "feeds a man for a year": ATTESTED as the convention (1 koku ~ one adult's year); the samurai ration was 1.8.
- 2017 spreadsheet's 151.5 kg: matches the ~150 kg brown-rice koku, though by a different route (40 gal read as kg).
- The asker's ~120 kg (40 US gal x 0.8): 40 US gal of brown rice at ~0.83 kg/L is ~126 kg; but a koku is 180 L, ~150 kg.
- Chinese shi: Song 10 dou; Tang dan 59.44 L; present-day shi 100 L and dan 50 kg (weight). Qing shi volume: SILENT.

Contradictions to flag for the GM:
- l7r.md's "40 gallons" (read as US gallons, 151 L) against the koku's 180 L and budgets.md's own ~180 L / ~48 US gal.
  The two canon files disagree unless l7r.md means imperial gallons.
- budgets.md's ~187 kg paddy per koku: the one clue points the other way (paddy lighter per volume, ~100 kg a koku-volume).
- budgets.md's ~1.5 koku a person a year: not found; history gives 1 koku (convention) or 1.8 koku brown (ration).
- l7r.md's 1 koku "feeds a man for a year" vs l7r.md's 2 koku/year "need": not a contradiction (the 2 covers more than rice).

Verdicts:

- ~2 kg of rice a day: ATTESTED ("about 2 kg ... on average in the Tokugawa era"); 1745-54 at 1.49 g/kg = 1.89 kg.
- Edo from 1818: ATTESTED, levels "broadly equal" to Kyoto.
- City vs country: ATTESTED ONLY FOR Kyoto against one village ~40 km off (Kami-Kawarabayashi, Osaka plain). City

  (city/country ~0.92). Skilled premium: village carpenters 2.6x, Osaka carpenters 4.7x the unskilled.
- 250 working days: NOT ATTESTED as a premodern count - a modern convention (Allen et al. assume it; Bassino and Ma
  mention it only for Allen 2001). No count from the time found (SILENT).
- Qing: Canton (VOC) mostly 0.08-0.1 tael/day, no food; Yangzi delta baseline 0.0456 (a regression estimate, ~half of
  Suzhou/Canton); farm day labor averaged 0.045 (likely above a year-round rate); official standard 0.04 (possibly a
  1600s figure); year-hired hand 10.4-13.4 taels (cash 2-5 + ~5 shi rice worth 8.4), 0.035-0.045/day over 360 days;
  0.024 tael ~ 1 kg rice in Canton, so baseline ~1.9 kg/day, Canton ~3.3-4.2 kg. All ATTESTED.

2017 working figures:
- 3.32 koku a year: ATTESTED ONLY FOR arithmetic on the 250-day convention. It equals exactly the 1745-54 Kyoto wage
  (2.82 / 1.49 x 250 = 473 kg) over the paper's 1.425 kg a shō (142.5 kg a koku); at the paper's other conversion, 150
  kg a koku, it is 3.15; at an even 2 kg/day and 150 kg it is 3.33. The paper's two conversions (150 kg/koku and 1.425
  kg/shō) disagree with each other.
- ~2 kg rice a day: ATTESTED. 250 days: a convention, NOT ATTESTED.
- 1.33 zeni a day: SILENT (a setting coin; nothing historical read prices the day in it).
- Farm labor 3.5 koku a year: SILENT - no Japanese farm worker's year in rice was found. The nearest is the Qing
  year-hired hand's ~5 shi of rice plus 2-5 taels.
- City/country ratio for the skill: ~1 from the 1750s (0.92 in 1802-4); higher in the city before, by an unstated amount.

## Q1 - 0251, what farmland sold for against what it yielded (session 1, continued)

Verdicts:
- Years-of-net-yield multiple for Japanese farmland: SILENT. Washizaki 2007 reports that Takeyasu (1966, pp. 171-178)
  and Uemura (1987, pp. 319-329) showed Kinai and Sanuki farmland priced at its capitalized sakutoku, with no rate in
  the passage; neither book was read.
- Years-of-net-yield multiple for Qing farmland bought outright: SILENT. ATTESTED ONLY FOR rent rights pledged or sold
  in Yongchun, coastal southern Fujian (Cao 2019, 36 cases, 1740s-1904): 6.5-37.2% a year, mean 12.6% = ~8 years of the
  rent (2.7-15 across the range; our arithmetic). Net of tax in one contract (tax passed with the rent); the rest not
  said. The study treats a rent pledge as a LOAN, so 12.6% is a village lending rate, "overturning" the belief in 20-30%.
  1746 case 17.7%; 1792 a "nominal" 7% (~14 years), resting on an assumed second rent.
- Pledge (= live sale) price against an outright sale: ATTESTED - about two-thirds at Shicang (Zhejiang); 60-80%
  "usually" (Pomeranz, citing McAleavy, Buoye, Macauley).
- Village credit: ATTESTED - Shicang land-pledge rates fell from the mid-Jiaqing to the late Guangxu reign, cash 30% ->
  20%, grain 20% -> 10% (Cao's earlier paper); Pomeranz takes 12% as "far below the rates usually quoted", 36% "often used".
  The pass's "village credit at 12%" was a misreading: 12% is Pomeranz's hypothetical rate.
- Songjiang price-and-rent pairs (閱世編): ATTESTED as figures; the multiples are OUR ARITHMETIC on gross rent, rent's
  form (husked or not) and the owner's tax not stated: early 1660s under 1 year of rent (tax and labor service); 1680 at
  2 taels a shi, ~1.4 years (Shanghai) and ~2.3-2.9 (Huating and Lou). All at moments of crisis. The pass's 6-9 years
  (1680 prices at 1678 rice) was dropped: it mixes years.
- Jiangnan price series (履園叢話): ATTESTED, prices only (mid-Ming 50-100 taels a mu; 1-2 at the Ming's fall; 2-3 early
  Shunzhi; 4-5 Kangxi; back under Yongzheng; 7-8, best 10+, ~1760s; 50+ fifty years on). The entry names no place.
- Japan's pledged land: ATTESTED - the 1643 sale ban; pledges as the general form; the lender holds the land and applies
  its yield to principal and interest, so the principal bears no interest (kotobank 質地); the lender pays the tax
  (rainō illegal from 1687; the leaseback 直小作, Nakabayashi 2020); forfeiture 1695 / 1722 ban / 1723 withdrawn / 1744.
- Kumehara pledges (岡安家文書): ATTESTED - 1700, ~1.15 cho mixed land, 70 ryo, 2 years (the pledger of Suka village; where
  the land lay is not stated); 1720, ~4.1 tan, 24 ryo, 2 years; 1726, ~1.07 tan lower paddy, 3 ryo, 3 years, forfeit on
  lapse; the lender pays the tax in each. ~6.1, 5.8, 2.8 ryo a tan (our arithmetic). Loan sums, not prices; no yield given.
- Wigmore (Meiji survey): ATTESTED - Uwonuma (Echigo) mortgage up to 10 years, sale forbidden; Suruga villages
  nominally no sale, a mortgage used.
- Edo town lot at 20x net (5%): ATTESTED ONLY FOR one valuation by the Edo town office of a central town lot let wholly
  to ground tenants, a valuation standing in for an out-of-date sale price. Town lots, not farmland.
- Irrigated against dry land: ATTESTED ONLY FOR the tax form - Kumehara 1698, upper paddy 0.48 koku of rice a tan (4 to
  8 sho, after a flood-loss deduction), middle dry field 90 mon in ei (1698 only; 85 mon in 1699-1702). No price for
  either. China: price "depended on fertility" (Qian Yong) and on the rent borne (Ye).
- Near-city against remote: SILENT in both countries. Songjiang's one premium (25-50%) is for the plot beside one's own.

- The setting's ~50-koku farm read against the landlord's 2.5 koku is 20 years of net, a 5% return: the same as the

  ("previous adventures", "enormous variation") beside the FREEHOLDER's farm, the exception, not the tenant plot; the
  20x pairing is the asker's.
- The asker's interest canon (15-20% cities, ~25% countryside) is NOT in the setting notes (`make canon` on "per cent|

  Beside history: Fujian's rent pledges averaged 12.6% (the study's point is that rural rates were lower than the 20-30%
  usually believed); Shicang's land pledges ran 30% -> 20% (cash) and 20% -> 10% (grain) over the 1800s; 36% is "often
