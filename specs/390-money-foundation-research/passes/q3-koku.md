## Reading
- **Volume.** The new Kyōmasu (from 1669, in use from ~1620s) koku = 180.39 L. The Taikō-era Kyōmasu (62,500 cubic bu)
  ≈ 173.9 L, 3.6% smaller (the pass's arithmetic). Domain measures persisted; only bale sizes are quoted (bakufu 3.5 to,
  Kaga 5 to).
- **Brown rice ≈ 150 kg.** The 1950 basis gives ~0.83 kg/L; MAFF 1949 146.3 kg; MAFF 2023 827 g/L ≈ 149 kg. Modern rice,
  by analogy.
- **Polished and paddy.** No page gives the weight of a koku-volume of either. Milling shrinks 4 to to ~3.5 to (~12%).
  The Nara bale implies paddy ≈ 0.55 kg/L, LIGHTER per volume than brown. The GM's ~136 kg polished and ~187 kg paddy
  read as brown-rice equivalents (milled yield of a koku of brown; paddy needed to make one), not as the weight of a
  koku-volume. The ~138 kg is attested only as Korean polished barley.
- **Consumption.** "1 koku a person a year" is a convention (3 gō a day; Yoshida 1910's assumption). The Edo ration was 5
  gō of brown rice a day, 1.8 koku a year; the Bunongshu hand 1.5 sheng a day. ~1.5 koku a year of grain is not
  attested by anything read.
- **China.** The Song-on shi is 10 dou; the Tang ≈ 59.44 L; modern 100-103 L; 担 a modern weight of 50 kg. No Qing jin
  figure for a shi of rice.
