   The data are Kyoto day laborers, 1741-1867 (Mitsui Bunko).

     a day's pay could buy him over three kilograms of rice in the 1880s and 1890s"
   - fn 6: "daily nominal wage multiplied by 250, assuming 250 working days and no other income"
   - fn 7: "In the Mitsui Bunko records, there is also a nominal wage series for Tokyo (Edo) for 1818-1871. Although with

   - Appendix: "1 silver monme = 3.11 grams of pure silver. 1 koku of rice = 150 kg … 1 sho rice = 1.425 kg". The two

   - The pass's arithmetic, 1745-54: a wage of 2.82 g silver a day, with white rice at 1.49 g/kg, buys ~1.9 kg of rice
     (~0.91 monme) a day. 250 days gives ~473 kg, which is 3.15 koku at 150 kg (3.3 at 142.5).

     substantially higher than that in Kami-Kawarabayashi, a village a little more than 40 km away from the city. However,
     during the 1750s and 60s Kyoto's market rate caught up to the village's (Saito 1978: 92). Thereafter, the wage rate
     for the city's unskilled workers was equilibrated with that for agricultural work. In the base period of 1802-4, for
     example, the wage rate for spring tasks performed by Kami-Kawarabayashi farm workers was 1.0 monme while the
     corresponding spring rate in Kyoto's casual labour market was on average 0.92 monme."
   - "the village carpenters' wage rate was 2.6 times higher than the spring rate for agricultural labourers while the
     urban carpenters earned 4.7 times more than the unskilled could"

## Reading
- **Japan.** Kyoto unskilled pay was ~0.9 monme a day, ~1.9-2 kg of rice; Edo was about the same from 1818.
- **City against country in Japan.** Equal from the 1750s-60s (village 1.0 against Kyoto 0.92 monme in 1802-4); higher
  in the city before that, by an unstated amount.
- **Qing.** Big-city wages were ~2x the delta baseline; farm day labor 0.045 tael; the baseline buys ~1.9 kg of rice.
  A delta farm hand hired by the year earned 10.4-13.4 taels.
- **Working days.** 250 is a modern convention. No Japanese annual servant wage was found.
