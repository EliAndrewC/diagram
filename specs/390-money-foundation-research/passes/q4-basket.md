## Table 1, quantities per person per year (published version)
Japan A: beans (excluding soybeans) 4 l; soybeans 52 kg; rice 114 kg; barley and wheat 10 kg; fish 3.5 kg; buckwheat
and others 16 kg; edible oil 1 l; linen 5 m; lamp oil 2.6 l.
Japan B: beans 4 l; soybeans 26; rice 30; barley and wheat 70; fish -; buckwheat and others 75; edible oil 1; linen 5;
lamp oil 2.6.
Verbatim: "Consumption basket A, with its relatively higher quantities of rice, soy-beans, and ﬁsh, aims to capture the
expenditure of the ''normal'' or average income group, while basket B, with a relatively higher amount of barley, but
lower quantities of rice and soybeans and zero ﬁsh, represents the subsist-ence consumption."
Date of the evidence: "available consumption data for staple food in the early 18th, late 19th (Kito, 1986, 1989;
Umemura, Takamatsu, & Itoh, 1983), and the ﬁrst surveys for non-staple consumption of the early 20th century (Toyo
Keizai, 1980)." Soybeans stand in for miso, shoyu, tofu and natto, so their quantity is "higher than the actual
consumption reported for the 1920s".

## Table 3, Kyoto 1745-1754 (grams of silver; share of basket cost)
Beans 0.99 (1.2); soybeans 1.49 (23.8); rice 1.49 (52.1); barley and wheat 0.76 (2.3); fish 2.18 (2.3); buckwheat and
others 0.61 (3.0); edible oil 5.66 (1.7); linen 5.83 (8.9); lamp oil 5.66 (4.5); total 326 (100.0); nominal wages 2.82;
real wages 0.009. Tokyo 1882-86: 1.01 / 0.97 / 1.5 / 0.77 / 2.19 / 0.62 / 5.62 / 3.07 / 1.79, total 277, wage 4.5, real
0.016. "we use consistently the Japan basket A."
Source note, verbatim: "Data for prices of rice and lamp oil in 1745 - 1754 are based on Mitsui Bunko retail prices in Kyoto
(Table 6). Prices of soybeans are available for Osaka in Mitsui Bunko from 1757 (Table 3). … we just assume that the
soybeans price in 1745 - 1754 to be equal to rice prices. … There are no retail prices for ﬁsh, barley and wheat, buckwheat
and other grains, and linen for 1745 - 1754. We derived them using their price ratio to rice in early Meiji (beans 0.63,
barley and wheat 0.51, buckwheat and other grains 0.41). For price of cotton linen, we used price in Osaka in 1840 - 1849 …
As lamp oil is rapeseed oil, we used the price of lamp oil as a proxy for edible oil. … assuming 3.11 g of pure silver
per monme." Note: 1.49 x 0.63 = 0.94, not 0.99.

## Against the 2017 snapshot
All nine A quantities and prices match; the products sum to exactly 325.816 g. "Adzuki" and "cotton" are the
snapshot's own labels for "Beans (excluding soybeans)" and "Linen (m)". Basket B at the Kyoto prices = 235.876 g.
Footnote 8 mislabels basket B as A in both versions ("Costs of basket A for 1750 and 1884 benchmark periods would be 236
and 200 g of silver respectively ...", and its "0.09" should be 0.009). No other variants; Japan A and B have no fuel or
soap.
