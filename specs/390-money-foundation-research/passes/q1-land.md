# Pass - Q1, land price against yield (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

I found no attested price in years of net yield for farmland in either country. I couldn't get to a range you could use as a firm figure without going past what the sources say. Here is what does exist:

- **China:** returns on pledged rent (典租) in Minnan.
- **China:** prices of Songjiang land quoted next to its gross rent per mu.
- **China:** the terms of dian and live sale.
- **Japan:** the terms of pledged land (質地) and three pledge sums from one Musashi village.
- **Japan:** an attested multiple of 20 times net income, but only for town lots in Edo, not farmland.

Every multiple marked "computed" below is my own arithmetic on figures from the same source.

## CHINA

**1. Cao Shuji, 典地与典租：清代闽南地区的土地市场与金融市场 (《清史研究》2019 no. 4).** Read at https://www.aisixiang.com/data/119372.html (a repost at iqh.ruc.edu.cn is the same text). The evidence is contracts from Yongchun, Fujian, dated 1704–1898, plus Shicang in Zhejiang.
- 「在石仓，土地的活卖价即典价大约是绝卖价的三分之二。」 (translation: "At Shicang, the live-sale price, i.e. the dian price, was about two-thirds of the outright-sale price.")
- 「承典者用价银9两，购买每年212斤租谷……年利率为17.7%。」 (translation: "The pledgee paid 9 taels of silver to buy 212 jin of rent grain a year … an annual rate of 17.7%." 1746.)
- 「在表1中，年利率超过20%的共有3例，最者年利率高达37.2%；4例接近20%，11例低于10%，其中有5例在6.5%-7.1%之间。合而计之，此36例算术平均年利率约为12.6%」 (translation: "In Table 1, three cases exceed 20% a year, the highest at 37.2%; four are near 20%; eleven are under 10%, five of them between 6.5% and 7.1%. Taken together, the arithmetic mean of these 36 cases is about 12.6%.")
- On example 8: 「"迎年贴纳粮银贰分"表明爱卿将税责之一部分亦随租典出」 (translation: "'Paying 2 fen of tax silver each year' shows that Aiqing pledged part of the tax liability along with the rent.") So the buyer's yield here is net of tax.
- On a 1792 outright sale of a rent right: 「孔藻投资的名义年利率为7%」 (translation: "the nominal annual rate on Kong Zao's investment was 7%").
- On Shicang from the Jiaqing reign to late Guangxu: 「"借钱还钱"的年利从30%降为20%，"以谷付息"的年利则从20%降至10%」 (translation: "annual interest on 'borrow cash, repay cash' fell from 30% to 20%, and on 'interest paid in grain' from 20% to 10%").

**2. Pomeranz, "Land Markets in Late Imperial and Republican China".** Archived copy of lse.ac.uk …/Conf6-KPomeranz.pdf, around p. 26.
- On Qing practice: "Various scholars have estimated that the payment made at the time of a 'living sale' was usually 60-80% of what the price for outright sale would have been (McAleavy 1958:406; Buoye 2000: 94; Macauley1998: 231)."
- "…an annual interest rate of 12%, which is far below the rates usually quoted for credit in the village … (36% per year is often used…)"
- "A Qing sub-statue of 1740 did provide for the middleman to negotiate a one-time supplemental payment…"
- Not premodern evidence: his North China terms ("half to two-thirds of the market price … redeem … within three years") come from Communist land-reform teams in the 1940s (Pepper 1978).

**3. 叶梦珠《閱世編》卷一「田產一」 and 「食貨」.** Read at https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/閱世編. Evidence is Songjiang, 1630s–1680.
- 「崇禎中，華、青美田，每畝價值十餘兩。上海田美者，每畝價值三、四、五兩」 (translation: "In the Chongzhen era, the best land in Huating and Qingpu was worth over ten taels a mu; Shanghai's best land three, four or five taels.")
- 「順治初，米價騰湧，人爭置產……華、青石五六斗田，每畝價值十五六兩；上海六七斗田，每畝價值三四兩不等。」 (translation: "In early Shunzhi, rice prices surged and people competed to buy land … Huating and Qingpu land with a rent of 1.5–1.6 shi was worth 15–16 taels a mu; Shanghai land with a rent of 6–7 dou, 3–4 taels.")
- 「厥後，米價漸平，賦役日重，田價立漸馴減。至康熙元、二、三年間，石米價至五六錢……中產不值一文，最美之業，每畝所值，不過三錢、五錢而已。」 (translation: "Afterwards rice prices levelled and taxes and labour service grew heavier daily, so land prices fell steadily. By Kangxi 1–3, rice was 5–6 qian a shi … middling land was worth nothing, and the finest land fetched no more than 3 to 5 qian a mu.")
- 「康熙十九年庚申春，因米價騰貴，田價驟長，如吾邑七斗起租之田，價至二兩一畝，甚至有田地方，各就近爭買者，價至二兩五錢以及三兩。華、婁石四五斗起租之田，價至七八兩一畝。……至次年辛酉，米價頓減，其風稍息。」 (translation: "In spring 1680, because rice was dear, land prices suddenly rose: in our county, land rented at 7 dou reached 2 taels a mu, and where neighbours competed for adjoining plots, 2.5 to 3 taels. Huating and Lou land rented at 1.4–1.5 shi reached 7–8 taels a mu … The next year rice fell abruptly and the fever subsided.")
- Rice prices from the 食貨 section: 「十九年夏，白米每石價銀二兩。」 (translation: "Summer 1680, polished rice 2 taels a shi.") Also 「至十七年，早新米每石價銀亦不過七錢三分。」 (translation: "By 1678, new early rice was no more than 0.73 taels a shi.")
- Computed multiples (price ÷ rent × same-source rice price). These are gross rent before the landlord's tax, the passage gives no tax figure, and it does not say whether the rent is in milled rice or unhusked grain (if grain, double every multiple):

| Date | Land | Price per mu | Rent per mu | Rice per shi | Years of gross rent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Early Shunzhi | Huating/Qingpu | 15–16 taels | 1.5–1.6 shi | 2–4 taels | about 2.5–5 |
| Kangxi 1–3 | Huating/Qingpu, best | 0.3–0.5 taels | 1.0–1.6 shi | 0.5–0.6 taels | under 1 (tax burden) |
| 1680 | Shanghai | 2 taels | 0.7 shi | 2 taels | about 1.4 |
| 1680 | Huating/Lou | 7–8 taels | 1.4–1.5 shi | 2 taels | about 2.5–2.9 |
| 1680 prices at 1678 rice | Huating/Lou | 7–8 taels | 1.4–1.5 shi | 0.73 taels | about 6–9 |

  Both spikes were driven by rice prices and reversed the next year, so none of these is a steady-state figure.

**4. 钱泳《履園叢話》卷一「田價」.** Read at https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/履園叢話/01. Evidence runs from the late Ming to about the 1810s, in Jiangnan.
- 「前明中葉，田價甚昂，每畝值五十餘兩至百兩，然亦視其田之肥瘠。……至本朝順治初，良田不過二三兩。康熙年間，長至四五兩不等。雍正間，仍復順治初價值。至乾隆初年，田價漸長。然余五六歲時，亦不過七八兩，上者十餘兩。今閱五十年，竟亦長至五十餘兩矣。」 (translation: "In the mid-Ming, land was very dear, 50 to 100 taels a mu, though it depended on the land's fertility … In early Shunzhi good land was no more than 2–3 taels; under Kangxi it rose to 4–5; under Yongzheng it returned to the early-Shunzhi level; from early Qianlong it gradually rose. When I was five or six it was still only 7–8 taels, the best over ten; now, fifty years on, it has reached over fifty taels.")
- This is a price series only. It gives no rent, so I did not turn it into a multiple.

**China verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** returns on pledged rent in Minnan, 1746–1898, plus price-and-rent pairs from Songjiang at moments of crisis.
- Pledged rent yielded 6.5–37% a year to the buyer, mean 12.6% over 36 cases. Example 8 shows tax passing with the rent, so for that case the yield is net of tax. I could not confirm this for the whole sample.
- Computed: 12.6% is about 8 years of the pledged rent. Scaled to outright sale with the Shicang two-thirds ratio, that is about 12 years. The 1792 outright sale at 7% is about 14 years. These describe pledged or sold rent rights in Minnan, not outright ownership of irrigated paddy.
- Dian and live sale paid about two-thirds (60–80%) of the outright price. Village credit ran at 12% or more, often 36%, so land paid far less than lending.
- Songjiang 1640s–1680 shows only 1–9 years of gross rent, and less than a year when tax and labour service peaked. That is evidence of how heavy the tax was, not a net figure.
- Irrigated vs dry: nothing beyond Qian Yong's 「視其田之肥瘠」 ("depends on the land's fertility") and the rent differences between counties.
- Near-city vs remote: SILENT. The 閱世編 premium of 25–50% is for buying the plot next to your own, not for nearness to a city.

## JAPAN

**5. 岡安家文書, 武州埼玉郡百間領久米原村.** Archived copy of town.miyashiro.lg.jp …/okayasukebunsyo.pdf. Evidence is 1700–1726.
- 1700 (元禄13): 「田畑藪屋敷合壱町壱反五畝九歩……不残質物ニ相渡、代金七拾両借り申所実正也、但シ年季之義……中弐年ニ相定申候、右之田地二御座候御年貢諸役入用共貴殿御勤可被成候」 (translation: "Fields, dry fields, thicket and house plot totalling 1 chō 1 tan 5 se 9 bu … all handed over in pledge, and 70 ryō truly borrowed; the term is fixed at two years; the annual tax and levies on this land you [the lender] shall pay.") The holding is mixed: 3.4 tan paddy, about 6.9 tan dry field, plus house plot, thicket and flood-damaged paddy.
- 1720 (享保5): 4 tan 1 se 6 bu, mostly lower dry field and middle paddy plus flood-damaged paddy: 「右之田畑しち物ニ相渡シ代金弐拾四両借用申所実正也……弐年季……御公儀様御年貢諸役入用ハ貴殿御勤可成候」 (translation: "These fields handed over in pledge, and 24 ryō truly borrowed … two-year term … the shogunate's tax and levies you shall pay.")
- 1726 (享保11): 「下田合壱反二拾壱歩……右之田しち物入置金子三両借用申所実正ニ御座候、年季之儀ハ午ノ暮より酉ノ暮迄中三年……御年貢諸役入用等ハ貴殿御勤可成候……若請留可申共流し申候而」 (translation: "Lower paddy totalling 1 tan 21 bu … pledged, and 3 ryō truly borrowed; term three years, end of 1726 to end of 1729 … tax and levies you shall pay … if not redeemed, it is forfeited.")
- The same village's 1701 tax assessment, in rice per tan: 「上田…反ニ四斗八升取」「中田…反ニ四斗取」「下田…反ニ三斗三升取」 (translation: "upper paddy 4 to 8 shō per tan; middle paddy 4 to; lower paddy 3 to 3 shō"). Dry fields paid in coin: 「上畑…反ニ九拾五文取」 (translation: "upper dry field 95 mon per tan").
- These sums are loan principal, not sale prices. The lender's interest was the land's whole net yield after he paid the tax (see 6).

**6. kotobank 「質地」 (世界大百科事典), https://kotobank.jp/word/質地-520863.**
- 「なお質地にもとづく貸借では，質取人が質地の利用権をもち，その収益を本利にあてるために債務元本そのものには利子がつかない。」 (translation: "In loans on pledged land, the lender holds the use of the land and applies its yield to principal and interest, so the debt principal itself bears no interest.")
- 「87年（貞享4）に質年季の規定を追加した……95年（元禄8）6月の質地取扱いに関する12ヵ条の覚……流地文言が銘記されている場合には質流れを認め」 (translation: "In 1687 rules on pledge terms were added … the 12-article memorandum of 1695 … allowed forfeiture where the deed stated a forfeiture clause.")
- 「22年4月に流地禁止令を発布……翌年8月には撤回された。そして44年（延享1）には……事実上の田畑永代売買禁止令の撤廃」 (translation: "The anti-forfeiture edict of 4/1722 … was withdrawn in 8/1723; and in 1744 came the de facto repeal of the 1643 ban on permanent sale of fields.")

**7. 中林真幸 「享保元文農地法の成立」 (『経済研究』71-2, 2020).** Full text at hit-u.repo.nii.ac.jp record 2048820. Evidence is shogunal law, 1720s–1740s.
- 「頼納とは，土地の所有者ないし質権者が年貢諸役を負担せずに耕作者に負担させている状態」 (translation: "Rainō is the state in which the owner or pledgee does not bear the tax and levies but makes the cultivator bear them.") This was illegal.
- 「直小作契約とは，質地契約の債務者が質地を債権者に預けた上で，債権者から借り受けて小作する態様の契約」 (translation: "A direct-tenancy contract is one in which the debtor hands the pledged land to the creditor and then leases it back as tenant.")
- From the English abstract: shogunal protection of property "provided farmers with loans at low interest rates with pledging their property". It gives no figure.

**8. Wigmore, *Materials for the Study of Private Law in Old Japan*.** Archived archive.org djvu text. These regional paragraphs come from the Meiji survey of customs (about 1877–80) describing Tokugawa practice.
- "Mortgages of land are permitted for a term not exceeding 10 years … but sales are absolutely forbidden" (Uwonuma, Echigo).
- "In villages there is nominally no sale of land; a mortgage is used and the register is changed" (Suruga).
- From the same book, a premodern domain edict of 1809 (Bunka 6, as quoted): "There has been and still is much land transferred by way of mortgage and private gift."

**9. 鷲崎俊太郎 「江戸の土地市場と不動産投資」 (『社会経済史学』73-2, 2007).** jstage sehs/73/2 PDF (OCR text). The 20× figure is for Edo town lots and is premodern.
- 「地[主]手取金の20倍が「当時町並値段」に相当していた。これは，手取金を5％の利率で資本還元した価格」 (translation: "20 times the owner's net receipts equalled the current ward price, i.e. the net capitalised at 5%.") The OCR prints the second character as 卞; it should read 主.
- On farmland: 「竹安繁治や植村正治が……作徳……を利子率で資本還元した現在割引価値が農地価格に等しかったことを実証してきた」 (translation: "Takeyasu Shigeji and Uemura Shōji have shown that farmland price equalled the net retained by the cultivator (sakutoku) capitalised at the interest rate.") This passage gives no rate.

**10. kotobank 「地租改正」, https://kotobank.jp/word/地租改正.** This is the Meiji reconstruction of 1873–76, not premodern market evidence.
- 「地価は、その土地1年の収益を各地慣行の利子で資本還元して求める……利子を自作地は7分以内、小作地は5分以内に制限」 (translation: "Land value was to be found by capitalising one year's yield at each locality's customary interest rate … the rate was capped at 7% for owner-farmed land and 5% for tenanted land.")
- Another entry on the same page: 「種肥代・利子率などの算定要素の数値は，なんら現実的根拠をもつものではなかった」 (translation: "the figures for seed and fertiliser cost and interest rate had no basis in reality").

**Japan verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the terms of pledged land and three pledge sums from one Musashi village (1700–1726), plus a 20× net (5%) multiple for Edo town lots.
- Pledges ran 2–3 years in these deeds; one region allowed up to 10 (Meiji survey).
- The lender paid the tax and levies and took the land's yield in place of interest. Forfeiture was allowed if the deed said so (1695), forbidden in 1722–23, and permanent transfer was in effect allowed from 1744.
- The one clean per-area figure is 3 ryō for about 1.07 tan of lower paddy in 1726. The deed gives no yield (石盛), so it can't be turned into years of yield.
- The years-of-net-yield multiple for farmland is SILENT. Takeyasu and Uemura are said to have shown that price equals capitalised net yield, but the passage I read gives no rate.
- Irrigated vs dry: only the tax difference (paddy paid in rice, dry field in coin).
- Near-city vs remote: SILENT.
