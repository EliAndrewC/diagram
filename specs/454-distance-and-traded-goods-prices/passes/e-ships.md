## JAPAN

### J1. 船の科学館『船の科学館資料ガイド10 菱垣廻船/樽廻船 弁才船』 (2010; modern secondary on Edo-period ships; cites 石井 『図説和船史話』, 『和船I』, 安達 『日本の船』, 柚木)

- URL: https://fields.canpan.info/report/download?id=3233
- Read via curl + pdftotext, because source-pages would not read the PDF. It is the 39-page guide CiNii lists as BB01748923.
- Every quote below was checked against the rendered page images (printed pp. 12, 30, 31, 33, 36).
- Period of the evidence: late Edo (Tenpo to bakumatsu) and the Edo period generally.

Crew:
- 「乗組員は千石積級で15人前後でした。入出港時など特別な場合を除いて、櫓を使用することが少なくなり比較的少人数で運航できたことが弁才船の発達理由の一つでした。」
- (translation) "The crew of a 1,000-koku-class ship was around 15. Except on special occasions such as entering and leaving port, oars came to be used little, and being able to sail with a relatively small crew was one reason the bezaisen developed."

Build cost, and its split between hull and fittings:
- 「建造費は千石積で1000両といわれていますが、幕末になると1500両程度まで高騰しました。そのうち、70％が船体、30%が碇、帆、綱などの諸道具の費用でした。」
- (translation) "The build cost is said to have been 1,000 ryo for a 1,000-koku ship, but by the end of the Edo period it had risen to about 1,500 ryo. Of this, 70% was the hull and 30% the cost of the fittings: anchors, sail, ropes and the like."

Working life and refits:
- 「弁才船の寿命は使い方や建造の善し悪しによって差がありましたが、25年や30年の長寿船もありました。一般に、新造から6 ～ 7年目に水漏れする部分に槇皮をうって釘締めを行い、11 ～ 12年目には腐った木材や釘鎹の取り替えを行なっていました。」
- (translation) "A bezaisen's life varied with use and with how well it was built, but there were long-lived ships of 25 or 30 years. Generally, in the 6th-7th year after building the leaking seams were caulked with maki bark and the nails tightened, and in the 11th-12th year rotten timber, nails and clamps were replaced."

Taru ships (Tenpo record):
- 「天保期（1830 ～ 1844）の記録によると、樽廻船は船の作り方が丈夫で道具類（艤装品）も質が良く、船員も優秀で、新造船を使用するのが一般で、新造後12 ～ 13年使用した船は地方に売却して塩廻船や一般の運賃積船に転用されたとしています（因みに一般の弁才船の寿命は、適当な補修は必要ですが、およそ20年程度とされていました）。」
- (translation) "A record of the Tenpo period says taru ships were strongly built, with good fittings and good crews, and were usually new-built. A ship used 12-13 years after building was sold to the provinces and turned to salt shipping or ordinary freight carrying. (An ordinary bezaisen's life, with suitable repairs, was reckoned at about 20 years.)"

Size of the late taru fleet:
- 「幕末の主な樽廻船問屋は大坂と西宮にあって、その隻数は約80隻でしたが、1600ないし1800石積といった大型船が主流で１隻に3000樽程度搭載できたと言われています。」
- (translation) "At the end of the Edo period the main taru-ship agents were in Osaka and Nishinomiya, with about 80 ships. Large ships of 1,600 to 1,800 koku were the mainstream, said to load about 3,000 casks each."

Burthen against hull weight (the guide's own conversion):
- 「「回船仕様覚書」という資料によると、大工間尺で算出した積石数と弁才船の空船の重さとの間には、積石数100石につき空船の重さ1600貫の関係があるので、1000石積であれば16000貫（60重量トン）となるとしています。」
- (translation) "A source called 'Kaisen shiyo oboegaki' says that between the burthen computed by the carpenters' measure and the weight of the empty ship there is a relation of 1,600 kan of empty hull per 100 koku of burthen. So a 1,000-koku ship weighs 16,000 kan (60 weight-tons)."
- This is the EMPTY HULL weight, not the cargo.
- The guide's own definition of the koku of burthen (printed p. 33): 「米1石相当の重量で、幕府による量制の統一が行われた寛文9年（1669）以降は40貫、即ち150kgに相当します。」
- (translation) "[the koku of burthen is] the weight of 1 koku of rice; after the shogunate unified the measures in 1669 it equals 40 kan, that is 150 kg."

The guide dates the printing of 『和漢船用集』 to 宝暦11年（1761） (printed p. 36). 安達 (J2) dates the composition of its book 4 to about 享保初年 (c. 1716). This report uses only those two dates.

Labour to build:
- 「大工、木挽きの必要人数はおよそ100石あたりで400人程度と言われています。従って、1000石の弁才船を平均15人で造るとすれば270日程度かかったことになります。」
- (translation) "The carpenters and sawyers needed are said to have been about 400 man-days per 100 koku. So building a 1,000-koku ship with 15 men on average would take about 270 days."

Reconstruction (modern; listed, not counted toward any verdict):
- 「平成12年（1999）に「千石積菱垣廻船二拾分一図」を基に菱垣廻船"浪華丸"が、平成17年（2005）に"みちのく丸"が復元建造され」
- (translation) "the higaki ship Naniwa-maru was reconstructed in 1999 from the 'twentieth-scale drawing of a 1,000-koku higaki ship' [dated c. Bunka, 1804-17, by 安達 in J2, not by this guide], and the Michinoku-maru in 2005."
- This is a modern reconstruction.

### J1b. 小嶋良一「復元された菱垣廻船「浪華丸」の意義について」 (日本船舶海洋工学会講演会論文集 22, 2016, 2016S-OS2-3)

- URL: https://zousen-shiryoukan.jasnaoe.or.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/column/2016S-OS2-3.pdf
- Read via curl + pdftotext.
- This is the paper on the 浪華丸 reconstruction. The reconstruction is modern and is listed only; the Edo-period statements below are separate from it.

Crew:
- 「弁才船は一隻で大量の荷物を運ぶことができ，たとえば 1000 石積クラスでは 14 人程度の船員で運行されていた．」
- (translation) "A bezaisen could carry a great deal of cargo in one ship; a 1,000-koku-class ship, for example, was worked by about 14 crew."

The pack horse as the land unit:
- 「幕府の駄賃荷物の運送規定によれば馬 1 頭の積載量は 40 貫 (150kg), 米に換算すれば 1 石」
- (translation) "By the shogunate's rules for pack-horse loads, one horse carried 40 kan (150 kg), that is 1 koku of rice."

Build time, 1844-45:
- 「銭屋五兵衛の日記「年々留」によると，弘化元年の 9 月に手斧始めを行った常豊丸は翌年 4 月に浜卸しが出来たとあり，7 ヶ月ほどで新造したことになっていて」
- (translation) "By Zeniya Gohei's diary, the Tsunetoyo-maru, begun in the 9th month of 1844, was launched in the 4th month of the next year: built new in about 7 months."
- By contrast, the 1998-99 reconstruction took 1 year and 3 months. That is modern and listed only.

### J2. 安達裕之, monograph on bezaisen models, chapter 五「宝暦期以後の弁才船」 (2012, Nippon Foundation-funded report)

- (translation) "By the crew numbers the shogunate set in the 6th month of 1735 for its castle-rice ships, that is the tax-rice carriers, a 1,000-koku ship carries 16 men."

和漢船用集 (c. 1716-1735) on the 千石船:

1787 (a special ship):

- (translation) "the 1,500-koku Sangoku-maru with 22 aboard, in service from 1787."
- A shogunal hybrid ship of Japanese, Western and Chinese design, not an ordinary coaster.

1853 Uraga petition, crew falling as ships grew:
- 嘉永六年（1853）三月, the Uraga elders' petition to the Uraga magistrate: 「往古は千弐百石積位ノ船ニ而水主拾六七人乗ニ御座候処、近年船乗共巧者ニ罷成、船斗大キク造、千六七百石積之船ニ而水主拾五六人乗位」
- (translation) "In former times a ship of about 1,200 koku carried 16-17 hands. In recent years the seamen have become skilled and only the ships are built bigger: a ship of 1,600-1,700 koku carries about 15-16 hands."

### J3. Japanese Wikipedia 「弁才船」 (secondary; footnotes to 石井 『和船II』 and to 栃木県図書館 answers citing 『図説和船史話』 and 『日本史大事典』)

- URL: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/弁才船 (also served at /wiki/千石船)
- Fetched by source-pages.
- Period of the evidence: 18th c. to 1844, and 1878.

Size, crew, and the koku as weight:
- 「18世紀中期の1000石積の弁才船は全長29メートル、幅7.5メートル、15人乗りで24反帆、積載重量約150トンであった」
- (translation) "A mid-18th-century 1,000-koku bezaisen was 29 m long and 7.5 m wide, carried 15 men, had a 24-cloth sail, and carried about 150 tons."
- 「当時、米1石は40貫とされ米以外の荷物を積む際もそれぞれの重さを換算して、廻船に積み込んだ」
- (translation) "At the time 1 koku of rice was reckoned at 40 kan (about 150 kg), and other cargo was loaded by converting its weight to that."

Size over time:
- 「弁才船は17世紀後期までは100石から500石積が主だったが、19世紀初期には菱垣廻船が1000石積、後期では樽廻船が1400石から1800石積を主力とした」
- (translation) "Until the late 17th c. bezaisen were mostly 100-500 koku. In the early 19th c. higaki ships were 1,000 koku, and later taru ships were mainly 1,400-1,800 koku."
- 「近世前期の弁才船の積石数は、110石から960石で、主力は250石前後」
- (translation) "Early-Edo bezaisen were 110-960 koku, mainly around 250."

Price, life, refit:
- 「1844年（天保15年）の船価（船体・道具込み）は1000石積で約1000両、500石積で約500両とし、菱垣廻船や松前渡航船は約2割増しとされていた。耐用年数は標準で20年程度だが、状態が良いものには30年程度の現役を可能としたものもある。なお、11・12から15・16年目に大規模な補修工事（中作事）を行う必要があった。」
- (translation) "The ship price of 1844 (hull and fittings) was about 1,000 ryo for 1,000 koku and about 500 ryo for 500 koku, with higaki ships and ships for Matsumae about 20% more. Standard working life was about 20 years, though some in good condition served about 30. A major repair (nakazukuri) was needed between the 11th-12th and the 15th-16th year."
- The paragraph has no footnote of its own.

Meiji estimate:
- 「1878年（明治11年）に同じ1000石積で業者が見積をした所、その船体価格は弁才船は2,900円、西洋船は4,600円」
- (translation) "In 1878 a builder's estimate for the same 1,000-koku size gave a hull price of 2,900 yen for a bezaisen and 4,600 yen for a Western ship."
- This is Meiji, outside the premodern window. Listed only.

Sail cloths per 1,000 koku:
- 「1000石積の反数を挙げると、前期（3尺幅）は21反、後期（2尺5寸）は25反」
- (translation) "For 1,000 koku the sail was 21 cloths in the early period (3-shaku cloths) and 25 in the later (2.5-shaku cloths)."

English Wikipedia "Bezaisen" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezaisen) is a translation of the same figures. It adds nothing.

- (translation) "In 1619 a man of Sakai hired a coaster of about 250 koku from Tondaura in Kii, loaded it with goods gathered here, and for the first time shipped them round to Edo."

### J5. 木部和昭「萩藩における領主廻米運賃について」 (Yamaguchi Univ., 第47巻第2号, 1999)

- Archived: 58/58cc6a890deb, key kibe-1999-hagi-kaimai-unchin.
- URL: https://petit.lib.yamaguchi-u.ac.jp/8245/files/164377
- Quotes below are read from the page image, p. 272 (750), Table 2.
- The figures date from about 1855-56. The paper notes that its 1840 (Tenpo 11) draft uses the same numbers.
- The source is 塩田家文書907 「諸廻船御改之御沙汰ニ付判断書」.
- The ships are the domain's model coasters for tax rice from Ogori.

330-koku ship:
- 「①大坂運送米運賃米受払之目安 330石積廻船（小郡積出し）→運賃16.896石 作法により廻船規模の2割引きで米264石を積載」
- 「3.08 5人乗り乗組員4名への給米」
- 「0.975 30日往来の5人分飯米」
- 「銀総計209.96匁（米にして2.93944石：米相場100匁＝1石4斗）」
- (translation) "Osaka run: a 330-koku ship; freight 16.896 koku of rice. By rule it loads 20% under its burthen, 264 koku. 3.08 koku: pay-rice to the 4 crew of a 5-man ship. 0.975 koku: food for 5 men for the 30-day round trip. [Rice market: 100 monme of silver = 1.4 koku.]"

150-koku ship:
- 「②萩御蔵納米運賃米受払之目安 150石積み廻船（小郡津出）→運賃8.04石 作法により廻船規模の２割引きで米120石を積載」
- 「1.19 2人の舸子（水夫）への給米」
- 「1.5 3人乗り、50日の食料」
- (translation) "Hagi run: a 150-koku ship; freight 8.04 koku. Loads 120 koku. 1.19 koku: pay-rice to the 2 sailors. 1.5 koku: food for a 3-man ship for 50 days."

So, per round trip (project arithmetic):
- A kako on the 330-koku Osaka run got 0.77 koku of rice for 30 days. That is about 55 monme of silver at the source's 100 monme = 1.4 koku.
- A sailor on the 150-koku Hagi run got 0.595 koku for 50 days.
- The skipper took only the remainder: 0.80 koku on the Osaka run and 0.81 koku on the Hagi run.

What the domain's terms imply about hiring a ship (from Kibe's text):
- Kibe quotes the 1764 law: 「定法積登不仕船之義ハ、銘々積石之運賃米之半方ニ当ル辻船頭より出米被仰付、右之出米并定運賃等を以他国御借船被仰付事」.
- He reads it: 「他国船を傭船しようとすれば自国船の1.5倍の運賃を必要とした」.
- (translation) "To charter another domain's ship cost 1.5 times the freight paid to the domain's own ships."
- That is, the domain's ships were held to about two-thirds of the market freight.

### J7. Kitamae crew pay by rank and private-cargo rights (popular and museum sources, undated "Edo period", mostly late Edo)

(a) 加賀市橋立 signboard, as transcribed by 千國写真館
- URL: http://senkuni.org/209kitamaebune/kitamae.html
- 「給与‥‥年間給料は船頭２～３両、役付１両２歩か３歩、若衆は１両か1両１歩､炊は二歩(半両)である｡その代りに船頭は積荷の10分の１の荷物をつみこむ特権(帆待ち)があり、水主は全体の１割程度の｢切出し｣を全員で分けた。」
- (translation) "Pay: yearly wages were 2-3 ryo for the skipper, 1 ryo 2 bu or 3 bu for officers, 1 ryo or 1 ryo 1 bu for a young hand, and 2 bu (half a ryo) for the cook. In return the skipper had the privilege (homachi) of loading goods of his own up to a tenth of the cargo, and the hands shared among them a 'kiridashi' of about a tenth of the whole."

(b) 倉敷 玉島 local-history page 「第９話 北前船と玉島」
- URL: http://www.kcv.ne.jp/~sspm571/rsanpo2/dai09wa/index.htm
- 「船頭３両…知工(ちく) ２両…表(おもて) ２両…親司(おやじ) ２両…若衆(わかいしゅう) １両…炊(かしき) ２歩…大体１隻に２０人前後の船乗りがいたようである。またそれぞれの給金は１年分のものであって」
- (translation) "Skipper 3 ryo; purser (chiku) 2 ryo; navigator (omote) 2 ryo; boatswain (oyaji) 2 ryo; young hand 1 ryo; cook 2 bu. There seem to have been around 20 seamen to a ship. Each of these wages was for a year."
- 「船頭と知工には「帆待(ほまち)」という特権を与え…一般の船乗りたちには、「切出(きりだし)」という特典があり、積荷の一定割合(積荷総量の３～９％程度)が船乗り全員に平等に分け与えられていた」
- (translation) "The skipper and purser had the homachi privilege [to trade their own goods]; ordinary seamen had the kiridashi, a fixed share of the cargo (about 3-9% of the total) divided equally among all hands."
- The page adds a late-Meiji reminiscence: kiridashi of 「普通は１航海で５０円ぐらい、炊はその半分ほど」 (translation) "usually about 50 yen a voyage, the cook about half". That is Meiji and is listed only.

(c) 北前船寄港地 site 「北前船とは」

## READING, figure by figure

### JAPAN

Burthen and the koku:
- **ATTESTED.** Bezaisen ran from 110-960 koku early in the Edo period (mainly about 250), to 1,000 koku for higaki ships about 1800, to 1,600-1,800 for bakumatsu taru ships (J1, J3).
- Kitamae ships were "really" 300-800 koku (J7e).
- Domain coasters were 150-330 koku in 1840-56 (J5).
- A koku of burthen = 1 koku of rice = 40 kan, about 150 kg (J3, J8). So a 1,000-koku ship carried about 150 t.
- Hull weight ran 1,600 kan per 100 koku, so a 1,000-koku hull weighed 60 t (J1).
- Hagi loaded only 80% of burthen (J5).
- Dimensions: a mid-18th-c. 1,000-koku ship was 29 m by 7.5 m (J3). An 850-koku ship had keel 47.8, beam 24.3 and depth 8 shaku (J6).

Crew:
- **ATTESTED.**
  - 1,000 koku: 16 by the shogunal rule of 1735 (J2); about 15 in the mid-18th c. (J1, J3); about 14 (J1b).
  - 1781: 12 aboard a 750-koku load, or 10 for 800 koku (J6).
  - 1853: 16-17 for 1,200 koku formerly, 15-16 for 1,600-1,700 koku "now" (J2).
  - Hagi, c. 1840-56: 5 men on 330 koku, 3 men on 150 koku (J5).
  - An older hagase hull of 500 koku carried 11 in 1772, 50% over a bezaisen (J6).
  - Kitamae ships: 11-13 (J7c), or "around 20" (J7b).

Crew pay:
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a dated, sized case, Hagi c. 1840-56. The Kitamae rank scales are undated popular and museum figures. Two kinds of evidence.
- (i) A dated primary account, Hagi c. 1840-56 (J5). Per 30-day Osaka round trip a kako got 0.77 koku of rice, about 55 monme at the source's own rice price. A sailor on the 50-day Hagi run got 0.6 koku. Meals were extra.
- (ii) Undated "Edo-period" Kitamae scales from museum and popular sources (J7), giving yearly fixed pay:

| rank | yearly fixed pay |
|---|---|
| skipper | 2-3 ryo |
| officers (chiku / omote / oyaji) | 1.5-2 ryo |
| young hand | 1-1.25 ryo |
| cook | 2 bu |

  - (J7c says the skipper's 2-3 ryo was per voyage.)
  - Kitamae private cargo: homachi of about a tenth of the owner's cargo for the skipper (and purser); kiridashi of 3-9%, 5-10% or "about a tenth" for the hands.
  - Higaki and taru skippers: 30-40 ryo a year, against a master carpenter's 25 (J7c).
- No dated primary wage book for a higaki, taru or Kitamae ship was read.
- Crew private cargo is attested in a dated primary by 1664 (Hirosaki, J7f).
- Beside the pay: the Edo carpenter earned 5-5.5 monme a day in the 1830s (record).

Build cost:
- **ATTESTED.**
  - Primary, with size and date: silver 53 kan 480 monme for a new 900-koku ship in 1789 (J4). That is about 59 monme a koku of burthen.
  - Secondary: about 1,000 ryo for 1,000 koku, and 500 ryo for 500 koku, in 1844, with higaki and Matsumae ships +20% (J3).
  - Bakumatsu: up to about 1,500 ryo, 70% hull and 30% fittings (J1).
  - Second-hand: 500 ryo for a 1,000-koku ship (J7c).
  - Labour: about 400 man-days per 100 koku (J1).

Working life:
- **ATTESTED** (secondary, from 石井 and the Tenpo record).
  - About 20 years, some 25-30 (J1, J3).
  - Caulking in year 6-7; a major refit (nakazukuri) at 11-12 or up to 15-16 (J1, J3).
  - Taru ships were sold off after 12-13 years into salt or freight work (J1, Tenpo-period record).

Charter / hire:
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the following:
  - The bare fact of hire: a 250-koku ship hired in 1619 (J4).
  - The relative cost of hiring another domain's ship: 1.5 times the Hagi domain's own freight, 1764 (J5).
- No time-charter (silver per month or per voyage, for the hull) was found. Hire was paid as freight by the koku, which is in record 0280 and the parallel pass.

### CHINA

Burthen:
- **ATTESTED.**
  - Shanghai sand junks, 1825: small 400-500 shi, middle 800-900, large 1,000-1,500, up to 2,000 (C2, C3).
  - Average 965 shi of grain per junk in 1826 (C3); "average 1,000" (C1).
  - 1885: 1,600-3,000 shi (C3).
  - Fujian bird-ships: 1,600-3,000 shi (C1, C2).
  - In tons, by eyewitnesses: Shanghai and Ningbo junks about 200 tons or less, 1831 (C6); 100-400 tons at Woosung, 1832 (C7); Siam-built junks 250-300 tons (C6).
  - China-Cochin-china junks of the 1790s, 40-150 tons (C10a).
  - Shanghai's sea junks of the Jiaqing-Daoguang years, about 125-175 tons on average (C10b, project arithmetic).
  - No source read converts a shi of burthen to weight or volume.

Crew:
- **ATTESTED.**
  - Ningbo/Zhejiang coasters: 16-22 aboard in drift records of 1727-1841, and "about twenty a ship", c. 1826 (C5).
  - A Suzhou sand junk of 1827: 16 (C1).
  - Fujian junks of 1897: 20-30 (C1).
  - Customs survey, late 19th c.: 16-24 for northern-trade types, 8-12 for southern bird-ships (C5).
  - A Siam-Chaozhou junk of 1831, 250 tons: about 50 (C6).
  - Ranks: owner or qimin (cargo master), helmsman (duogong), sailors (shuishou). On Gutzlaff's ship: captain-supercargo, pilot, helmsman, clerks, priest, head men, comrades.

Crew pay:
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the following:
  - Fujian/Amoy, 1897: 2-3 dollars a month for a sailor; the captain mostly out of net profit (C1).
  - The share system: Gutzlaff, 1831, "every one is a shareholder" with goods aboard, and only dry rice from the captain, yet "full wages" paid at Tianjin (C6). A crew-goods class appears on a bird-ship in 1684 (C8).
  - 1826 sea transport: 30% of the hold left to the ship's own goods (C2).
  - Ming 1597: Zhenjiang seamen 1.6 taels a month and boatmen 1.2-1.3, against 0.033-0.04 a day for craftsmen (C9).
- No sand-junk wage of the 1820s was found.

Build cost:
- **ATTESTED**, all c. 1824-26, Jiangnan.
  - 7,000-8,000 taels a sand junk (C1, 海運南漕議).
  - Large over 10,000 taels, middle several thousand (C1, 禀復魏元煜).
  - Zhejiang sea junk 5,000-6,000 taels, about the value of one cargo (C5, 謝占壬).
  - A 1920 Ningbo small coaster at no more than 1,500 yuan, outside the window (C5).

Working life:
- **SILENT for coasters.**
- Searched:
  - WebSearch 「沙船 使用年限 OR 壽命 OR 可用 年 海船 清代 修艌 拆造」: it found only 清史稿 卷135 on naval 沙唬船 being 拆造 (rebuilt) in the yards, with no period.
  - WebSearch "Chinese junk lasts years … cost of building junk taels": nothing on service life.
  - Grepped C1, C3 and C5 for 年限 / 壽命 / 耐用: nothing.
- The only term found is the ten-year rebuilding limit and 208 + 590 taels for canal tribute boats (C4), not sea junks.
- 齊彦槐 notes in 1825 that many junks had rotted for want of repair money (C2, C1 note 112). That gives no lifespan.

Charter / hire:
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the state's hire (封雇) of sand junks in 1825-26, all paid as freight per shi:
  - 0.7 tael a shi set by the intendant in 1825 (C2), against 0.05 tael a shi proposed in Qi's essay (C2). The two are fourteen times apart and are NOT reconciled here.
  - 70% of the freight paid on loading and 30% on delivery (C4).
  - 30% of the hold left to the ship (C2).
- No per-voyage or per-month hire of a junk as such was found.
