### J1. Kibe Kazuaki 木部和昭, 「萩藩における領主廻米運賃について」 (archived 58/58cc6a890deb, text layer of a repository PDF) - evidence 1693-1862, Hagi domain (Choshu), rice by sea to Osaka and to Hagi
NEW beyond the record (record has the percentages only):
(a) silver value of the freight per 100 koku of rice shipped to Osaka from Ogori district, 1838-1851 (author's conversion of the rice freight at each year's rice price):
「図2の小郡運賃に基づいて，天保9年以降の大坂廻米100石当たりの運賃米を，根運賃に基づいて銀に換算すれば，天保9年で940．8匁だったのが，翌10年には517．8匁に，翌々11年には489．6匁と，米価にともなって大幅に下落している。嘉永年間を見ても，元年573。4匁，2年752匁，3年983．9匁，4年519匁と」
(translation) "Converting into silver the freight rice per 100 koku of rice shipped to Osaka since Tenpo 9, from the Ogori freight [rate] of Fig. 2 and on the base freight, it was 940.8 monme in Tenpo 9 [1838], fell steeply with the rice price to 517.8 monme the next year and 489.6 monme the year after; in the Kaei years too, 573.4 monme in year 1 [1848], 752 in year 2, 983.9 in year 3, 519 in year 4 [1851]."
(b) market charter rate about 1.5 x the domain's fixed freight (1764 order):
「藩廻米に従事しなかった領内廻船から，運賃の半額相当を過料として徴収し，これに定運賃を加えて他国船を傭船するというものであるが，これはすなわち，他国船を傭船しようとすれば自国船の1．5倍の運賃を必要としたことを物語っている。他国船傭船に必要な運賃は，当時の一般廻船市場における運賃水準に近かったと考えられるから，萩藩の廻船はその三分の二程度の不利な運賃を藩に強要されていたということになる。」
(translation) "...a fine equal to half the freight was levied on domain ships that did not carry the domain's rice, and with it plus the fixed freight ships of other provinces were chartered; this tells that chartering another province's ship needed 1.5 times the freight of a domain ship. Since the freight needed to charter another province's ship is thought close to the level of the general shipping market then, the domain's ships were being forced to accept about two-thirds of it, an unfavourable rate."
(c) Table 2, 「表2，萩藩の運賃米の内訳（1855〜56年頃と推定）」 (journal p. 272), read from the served PDF page image (the text layer is corrupt):
「①大坂運送米運賃米受払之目安 330石積廻船（小郡積出し）→運賃16,896石 作法により廻船規模の2割引きで米264石を積載，運賃率6.4％」; expense lines 「大坂往来30日，湊3箇所での茶米（順遅により増減あり）」, 「5人乗り乗組員4名への給米 3.08」, 「30日往来の5人分飯米 0.975」; 「銀総計209.96匁（米にして2.93944石：米相場100匁＝1石4斗）」; 「差引 0.80006石・・・・・船頭手取」.
「②萩御蔵納米運賃米受払之目安 150石積み廻船（小郡津出）→運賃8.04石 作法により廻船規模の2割引きで米120石を積載，運賃率6.7％」; 「3人乗り，50日の食料 1.5」; 「差引 0.8139石・・・・船頭手取」.
(translation) "(1) Model receipts and payments of freight rice for rice shipped to Osaka: a 330-koku ship (loading at Ogori) -> freight 16.896 koku; by rule it loads 264 koku of rice, 20% under the ship's size; freight rate 6.4%." "Osaka round trip 30 days; tea-rice at 3 ports (more or less as the voyage goes fast or slow)"; "rice wages for 4 crew of a 5-man ship, 3.08"; "food rice for 5 men for the 30-day round trip, 0.975"; "silver total 209.96 monme (in rice 2.93944 koku, at the rice rate 100 monme = 1 koku 4 to)"; "balance 0.80006 koku ... the skipper's take." "(2) For rice to the Hagi storehouse: a 150-koku ship (shipping from Ogori) -> freight 8.04 koku; loads 120 koku, freight rate 6.7%"; "3 men, food for 50 days, 1.5"; "balance 0.8139 koku, the skipper's take."
Kibe's comment (p. 273): 「船頭の手元に残る純益は驚くほど少なく，表2の③にまとめたように，大坂で運賃額の4.7%，萩で10.1%の微々たるものであった。」 (translation) "the net left in the skipper's hands was astonishingly small: as summarised in Table 2 (3), a mere 4.7% of the freight for Osaka and 10.1% for Hagi."
Arithmetic (labeled): at the table's own rice rate (100 monme = 1.4 koku), the Osaka freight of 16.896 koku = about 1,207 monme for 264 koku, i.e. about 457 monme per 100 koku of rice, Ogori-Osaka, c. 1855; a round trip of 30 days.
(d) lateness discounts on Osaka freight, 1826: 「①年内積切，正月廿日までに大坂着船の船…歩引き無 ②正月中積切，二月廿日まで大坂着船…2．5％引 ③二月中積切，三月廿日まで大坂着船…5％引 ⑤三月朔日以降積切…10％引」 (translation) "(1) loaded within the year and arriving at Osaka by the 20th of the 1st month: no deduction; (2) loaded in the 1st month, arriving by the 20th of the 2nd: 2.5% off; (3) loaded in the 2nd month, arriving by the 20th of the 3rd: 5% off; loaded from the 1st of the 3rd month: 10% off" - i.e. the allowance from loading to arrival at Osaka was about 20 days to a month and a half.
Distance: Kibe gives none. Ogori (Yamaguchi) to Osaka by the Inland Sea is roughly 400-450 km (this pass's estimate, not a source; treat as arithmetic input only).
Arithmetic (labeled): 100 koku of rice ~ 15 t (record convention 150 kg/koku). 489.6-983.9 monme per 15 t ~ 3.3-6.6 monme per 100 kg for the whole voyage; over ~420 km that is ~0.8-1.6 monme per 100 kg per 100 km (this pass's arithmetic, distance estimated).

### C2. 松浦章 (Matsuura Akira), 『清代帆船沿海航運史の研究』 (Kansai University doctoral thesis, chapter PDFs) - text layers read with pdftotext

1862 charters, a Ninghai (Shandong) junk of 20 crew:
「同治元年（1862）７月に、牛庄の豊裕号という木材を扱う商人に登州府寧海州において僱船され材木1,350個を運搬して、僱船代金、銀400余両を受け取っている。またこの船は、つづいて、牛庄で江南の客商厳大生に僱船され、江南へ油20簍と豆630石を輸送するが、その代金は銀535両であった。先の場合は寧海州（現在の牟平縣）から牛庄へ、後者は牛庄から、おそらく上海へと、その航海距離は、３倍余の違いがあるが、僱銀は約1.3倍であり…運賃は航行距離と積荷量によって決められたものと考えられる。」
(translation) "In the 7th month of Tongzhi 1 (1862) it was chartered at Ninghai, Dengzhou, by a Niuzhuang timber merchant, the Fengyu house, carried 1,350 timbers, and received a charter fee of 400-odd taels of silver. Next it was chartered at Niuzhuang by a Jiangnan travelling merchant, Yan Dasheng, to carry 20 baskets of oil and 630 shi of beans to Jiangnan, for 535 taels. The first run was Ninghai (now Muping) to Niuzhuang, the second Niuzhuang to, probably, Shanghai; the distances differ by over three times, the charter money by about 1.3 times ... freight seems to have been set by distance and load."
Arithmetic (labeled): 535 taels / 630 shi of beans (oil ignored) = about 0.85 tael a shi, Niuzhuang-Shanghai, a whole-ship charter.

## Reading, route by route

**Distance:** no source read gives a distance in km, ri or li for any Japanese route. The Chinese distances are Matsuura's nautical miles: Jinjiang to Tianjin about 1,310, Donglong to Shanghai about 690.

**Osaka/Nishinomiya - Edo (higaki / taru kaisen). Freight: SILENT beyond the record.** No source read gives a freight per da, per taru or per 100 koku with a year; the record's 60 monme per 10 da (Kato 2007 citing Yunoki 1988, no year) stays the only figure. Voyage days: ATTESTED. Dated documents: the 1866 race winner took 9 days, another ship of the fleet 12, and the 1868 winner about 14-15, Nishinomiya to Shinkawa (Hakushika museum, from its own documents). Undated or unverified modern summaries: 3-4 days to Shinagawa (Nishinomiya city), and a 58-hour record of 1790 (ja.wikipedia citing Kwansei Gakuin, not read). Ordinary ships: 12 days on average Kamigata-Edo, 6 for the fast, 'it is said' (J10, modern booklet; a new-cotton race ship made Osaka-Uraga in 50 hours at an average 7 knots, i.e. about 350 nautical miles, this pass's arithmetic - the only distance handle for the run); an average of 30 days up to the Genroku era, falling to about 10 at the end of the period, with anything from 10 days to 2 months (ja.wikipedia, flagged unsourced); the record has 10-14 days (Kobe city archives). Early Meiji: sailing ships over 10 days, steamers about 5 (Hakushika). Searched: archive key sea-freight-454d (5 batches); queries 樽廻船 運賃 一駄 匁, 菱垣廻船 運賃 百石 日数, 新酒番船 最短 記録, 上村雅洋 樽廻船 運賃, 津川正幸 樽廻船. Unreadable: the Kobe city kn-3-6/4-6/5-5 PDFs (404) and core.ac.uk 228701504/228707644 (Tsugawa; 403).

**Inland Sea - Osaka (domain rice): ATTESTED, 1693-1862.** Hagi from Ogori: 6.4% of the rice as freight on a 330-koku ship, a 30-day round trip, about 457 monme of silver per 100 koku c. 1855 by this pass's arithmetic at the table's rice price; 489.6-983.9 monme per 100 koku in 1838-1851 (Kibe's conversion). The market charter rate was about 1.5 times the domain's fixed rate (1764). Kibe gives no distance.

**Sea of Japan - Osaka / Edo (western route, castle rice; Kitamaebune). ATTESTED ONLY FOR castle and exchange rice, 1835-1860.** Sakata to Osaka, 9 monme of silver net per koku in 1835 (Morioka exchange rice; 900 monme per 100 koku). Sakata to Noheji 7 to 7.25 ryo, and to Miyako 11.5 ryo, per 100 koku (1835). Voyage days, Sakata to Edo Shinagawa loaded: average 58, range 32-104 (15 runs, 1835-46); empty Osaka to Sakata 52 (29-173); Niigata to Edo 81 average (1860). Commercial Kitamaebune: NOT ATTESTED as a freight trade. The canpan booklet (J10, Q8) says they did not mainly earn freight but sold their own cargo, so there is no freight rate to find; round trips a year were not found (the Ishikari city sheet was read and gives no figures; the eonet series returned 404). No distance is given in any source.

**Eastern route / shogunal 御城米 rates by origin port (Kawamura Zuiken 1671-72): SILENT.** Pages read (Kotobank 東廻海運/番所, ja.wikipedia 西廻り航路/東廻り航路, Inoue 2007 法政史学 67) say only that Zuiken replaced high contract fees with "low freight"; none gives the rate.

**Comparison (Japan): ATTESTED, qualitative.** Overland rice carriage cost 'several times or more' than water (Kotobank, 世界大百科事典 廻米), and sea freight, rivers included, was 'several times cheaper' than land (マイペディア, J3b). The Aizu Nanzan land-and-river shipment to Edo took 3-4 weeks for 250-400 ryo on 1,000-2,500 koku (NTA museum, no year; J9). Set beside the Hagi sea rates of 5.9-6.9% for longer runs: an inland Musashi village paid boat freight to Edo of 4 bales per 100 in 1818 (4%). That it went by river, and the comparison itself, are this pass's inference, not a source's statement.

**China - north of the Yangzi (sand junks and the bean trade): ATTESTED ONLY FOR one whole-ship charter (a Shandong junk, not a sand junk) and the record's tribute rate.** Niuzhuang to (probably) Shanghai, 1862: 535 taels for 630 shi of beans plus 20 baskets of oil, about 0.85 tael a shi by arithmetic (Matsuura). In 1826 a thousand sand junks carried 1.5-1.6 million shi in two trips. Steamers in 1902: 0.388 tael a shi, then 0.338 (清史稿). No commercial 水腳 per shi, Shanghai to Tianjin, was found. 包世臣 海運南漕議 and 魏源 籌漕篇 could not be reached online.

**China - Fujian/Taiwan and Amoy: ATTESTED.** Official, 1787: 0.036 tael per shi per stage, so Amoy to Lu'ermen (12 stages) is 0.432 tael a shi by arithmetic. Market, Amoy Customs 1891: Amoy to Tainan about 30 cents (Mexican) a picul until 1890, then 30-55 cents a package, about 2 months a round voyage; Amoy to Quanzhou about 150 cash a picul; northbound junks about 4 months a voyage. From the north: grain 50 cents a bag, beans 20-25 cents a piece; sugar out 40-50 cents a bag. All far above steamer freight, the report says.

**Voyage days, China: ATTESTED.** Ningbo junks took 6-10 days to Tianjin and made three voyages a year (Customs 1882-91). The drift-record table (lunar dates): Jinjiang (Quanzhou) to Tianjin, about 1,310 nautical miles, in 15 days (1861); Chenghai to Tianjin 18 days (1785), 49 (1813), 55 (1836), 69 (1824); Guangdong to Tianjin 70 (1801); Taiwan to Tianjin 87 and 91 (1824); Amoy to Shanghai 19 (1749); Donglong to Shanghai, about 690 nautical miles, 11 (1830); Zhenjiang to Tianjin 38 (1784); Shanghai to Pizikou 20 (1808); Tianjin to Liaodong 10 (1816). The record has Shanghai to Tianjin in 'a little over ten days' (1826). The 1891 Amoy report gives about four months a voyage for northbound junks, a round trip with trading.

**Comparison as a source states it, China:** the 1891 Amoy report says junk freight was far higher than steamer freight. Matsuura says junk freight was set by distance and load: a run over 3 times as long paid about 1.3 times as much (1862). The record's Qiu Jun and Cheng remain the sea, river and land statements.

**Day wages beside them:** Kibe Table 2 (c. 1855) gives crew pay on the Ogori-Osaka run: 3.08 koku of rice for 4 crew for the 30-day round trip, plus 0.975 koku of food rice for 5 men. That is 0.77 koku a man a trip, about 55 monme at the table's own rate of 100 monme = 1.4 koku, or about 1.8 monme a day plus board (this pass's arithmetic). Otherwise the record's nearest are the Edo carpenter at 5-5.5 monme a day (1830s-50s) and Tianjin's 0.09 tael (1769).

## Conversions (this pass's arithmetic, labeled; 1 koku rice ~ 150 kg as in the record)
- Ogori-Osaka c. 1855: 457 monme / 15 t = ~3.0 monme per 100 kg for the voyage; 1838-51: 3.3-6.6 monme per 100 kg. Distance not in source (Inland Sea, roughly 400-450 km by this pass's estimate) -> ~0.7-1.6 monme per 100 kg per 100 km.
- Sakata-Osaka 1835: 9 monme per koku = 6 monme per 100 kg for the voyage (distance not given; the western route is well over 1,000 km).
- Sakata-Noheji 7 ryo / 100 koku at 60 monme a ryo (the rate at which the table's totals reconcile) = 420 monme / 15 t = ~2.8 monme per 100 kg; Sakata-Miyako 11.5 ryo = 690 monme = ~4.6 monme per 100 kg.
- Against an Edo carpenter's 5-5.5 monme a day: 100 kg of rice by sea Ogori-Osaka or Sakata-Noheji ~0.6 of a carpenter's day; Sakata-Osaka ~1.1 days (across places and decades).
- Amoy-Tainan 1787 official 0.432 tael/shi; Niuzhuang-Shanghai 1862 ~0.85 tael/shi (whole charter, includes oil).
