## (a) JAPAN: the wholesaler's commission (口銭) and a merchant house's margin

### A1. Yamakawa Nihonshi shōjiten (2016), entry 口銭, via Historist
URL: https://www.historist.jp/word_j_ko/entry/032946/ . A modern dictionary describing Edo-period practice; no year is given for the rates.
> 「荷受問屋は荷主から仲買への商品売買の仲介をする際，荷主への前貸金の利息とともに，荷主・仲買の双方から売捌価格に対して一定の割合で手数料を得る。この手数料が口銭である。口銭の割合は問屋仲間によって個別にきめられ，京都の糸絹問屋の場合は荷主と仲買の双方から売値の1％ずつ，あわせて2％の口銭，大坂の綿問屋では荷主から1.3％の口銭をとった。江戸では仕入問屋が多かったが，下り酒問屋は荷受問屋で，摂津国灘の荷主から3％の口銭を徴収した。」

(translation) "When a consignment wholesaler brokered a sale from the shipper to the middlemen, it took interest on its advances to the shipper and also a fee from both shipper and middleman, a fixed share of the selling price. This fee was kōsen. Each wholesalers' guild set its own rate. Kyoto's silk-thread wholesalers took 1% of the selling price from each side, 2% in all. Osaka's cotton wholesalers took 1.3% from the shipper. Edo's wholesalers were mostly buying wholesalers, but the down-sake wholesalers were consignment houses and took 3% from the Nada shippers."
(Records 0280 and 0283, read in full, do not hold this 3%. They hold the sake freight and the Edo price.)

### A2. Sakai Ryōsuke, 「近世大坂の生魚問屋・仲買の商内」 (J-STAGE, 1998)
URL: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsds1988/1998/11/1998_11_127/_pdf/-char/ja (pp. 129-131, checked against the page images).
Evidence: a petition of 1795 (Kansei 7) from the Zakoba fresh-fish wholesalers to the Osaka magistrate, with the author's reading of it. Later rates are dated 1816 and 1841.
> 「右商人方へ売上く高 銀百目之手前にて 内八拾弐匁壱分四厘 荷主方へ仕切ニ遣す 引残て拾七匁八分六厘問屋口銭ニ成る 此内より商人之向々ニより三歩引より九歩引迄之歩□引あり〔□ one character not read with certainty from the page image〕 此平均押しならし六匁三歩引 残て拾壱匁五分六厘正味口銭依之凡壱割壱歩五厘之口銭に当る 此内より網元仕入銀并ニ仕切先借其外掛損等之引残りを以て私共渡世仕来り候事」

(translation) "On sales to the merchants of 100 monme of silver: 82.14 monme is settled to the shipper. The remaining 17.86 monme becomes the wholesaler's commission. From it, rebates of 3 to 9 percent go to the buying merchants, averaging 6.3. What remains, 11.56 monme, is the net commission, about 11.5%. From this we take the advances to net-owners, the shippers' advance drawings, bad debts and the like, and we live on what is left."

> 「本銭賣物商人方へ売渡候節 … 引残て百弐拾弐文 正味口銭 但し商人方へ之歩引なし 依之此口銭凡壱割弐歩弐厘ニ相當」

(translation) "[For] 'hon-zeni' goods … 122 mon remains as net commission, with no rebate to the merchants. So this commission comes to about 12.2%."

Author: 「問屋口銭は建前として11.5%であるが，純利益としてはそれより少なくなる.」 (translation) "The wholesaler's commission is nominally 11.5%, but its net profit was less than that."
Author: 「1816(文化13)年11月には四津手網物の口銭は1割になった.」 (translation) "In the 11th month of 1816 the commission on yotsude-net fish became 10%."
Author: 「(参考)塩魚干魚商は1841(天保12)年の浜口銭は9歩であり, うち問屋口銭は4歩であった. あとの2歩は諸掛費と仲仕賃にあて, 3歩を仲買に歩戻しする.」 (translation) "For reference: for the salted- and dried-fish trade in 1841 the landing commission was 9%, of which the wholesaler's own was 4%. Of the rest, 2% went to sundry charges and porters, and 3% was rebated to the middlemen."
On advances, the author quotes 『大阪府誌』 (1903) and 『大阪魚市場調査』 (1923), both describing older practice:
> 「仕入金は利足を附せず着荷の際仕入金の内より留金と稱して五分一割を引去るの慣例」

(translation) "Advances carry no interest; at each arrival 5 to 10% is deducted against the advance as 'tomegane'."
> 「但し問屋によりては二分五厘, 乃至五分の利息を徴するものありて一定せず.」

(translation) "But some wholesalers charge interest of 2.5 to 5 [percent], and it is not uniform." (1923; the period of the rate is not stated.)

### A3. Japanese Wikipedia, 江戸口油問屋 (it cites 国史大辞典 and 日本歴史大事典)
URL: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B1%9F%E6%88%B8%E5%8F%A3%E6%B2%B9%E5%95%8F%E5%B1%8B . Evidence: Osaka, from 1617 to 1832.
> 「代銀100目分につき口銭8分を上乗せして販売する慣例であった。」

(translation) "It was the custom to sell adding a commission of 8 bu on every 100 monme of the price." 8 bu of silver on 100 monme = **0.8%** (arithmetic).

### A4. Shimomukai Norihiko, 「寛政年間における三井越後屋の木綿仕入状況とその特質」 (Mitsui Bunko ronsō 47)
Archived: 5a/5a93e9475270 (https://mitsui-bunko.or.jp/archives/pdf_data/mbr-47_shimomukai.pdf). Record 0249 already holds these figures (not on the do-not-report list). Evidence: the Mitsui Echigoya Edo Mukaidana, a cotton shop, in the Kansei (1789-1801), early Bunsei and late Tenpō periods, plus a clerk's note of 1798.
> 「木綿単価の高さは札掛率（利益率）の低さに直結する。… 勢州木綿の札掛率は寛政期に二・六％、文政期に三・九％、天保期に二・一％であり、大坂木綿の四・四％、四・二％、三・五％、尾三木綿の三・三％、五・八％、三・〇％に比べても低いのである。」

(translation) "A high unit cost of cotton meant a low fudakake rate (profit rate). … The fudakake rate on Ise cotton was 2.6% in the Kansei period, 3.9% in the Bunsei period and 2.1% in the Tenpō period. Even this is lower than Osaka cotton's 4.4%, 4.2% and 3.5%, and Owari-Mikawa cotton's 3.3%, 5.8% and 3.0%."
> 「すなわち、伊勢島木綿の札掛は四から五％（ツサ歩）である。…大和島木綿を仕入させて江戸に下したところ、向店の札掛は七％から八％（ヱチ歩）になった。また、伊勢晒島浴衣地の札掛は三％（サ歩）である。京本店から下す岐阜織に切替えたら札掛は一一％から一二％（イイセ）となった。」

(translation, the author reading the 1798 code-numbers) "That is, the fudakake on Ise striped cotton is 4 to 5%. … When Yamato striped cotton was bought and sent down instead, the shop's fudakake became 7 to 8%. The fudakake on Ise bleached-stripe yukata cloth is 3%. Switched to Gifu weave sent from the Kyoto main shop, it became 11 to 12%."
The paper glosses fudakake as profit rate (利益率) and, in its conclusion, as 売上益 (gross profit on sales). It does not say whether the rate is taken on cost or on price.
What kind of house: the paper sets the Edo Mukaidana beside 「一般的に、江戸の木綿問屋は勢州木綿を松坂の買次問屋から仕入れていた」 ((translation) "in general Edo's cotton wholesalers bought Ise cotton through buying agents at Matsusaka"). It does not name the shop a 仕入問屋 in so many words. It says Mitsui's Osaka main shop 「大坂木綿問屋東堀組に属し、大坂木綿問屋として木綿を仕入れ」 ((translation) "belonged to the Higashibori group of Osaka cotton wholesalers and bought cotton as an Osaka cotton wholesaler"). So A4 is a buying house's gross markup on its own purchases.
Buying agent's commission, same paper, 1795: 「京本店は寛政七年以降、青谷木綿の仕入にかかる口銭収入を西紙屋を介さず、角屋へ木綿仕入額の一％（一歩）を直接渡すようにした。」 ((translation) "From 1795 the Kyoto main shop paid the commission on Aoya cotton purchases directly to Kadoya, 1% of the purchase amount, no longer through Nishikamiya.") The source document: 「角屋方へイ歩口銭勘定相立遣し候」 ((translation) "we settled a commission of 1 bu [1%] to Kadoya").

## (b) JAPAN: the shipowner who traded on his own account (買積), Kitamaebune

### B1. Takahashi Yoshihiro, 「北前船の組織・情報・インセンティブ」 (Senshu monthly 698-699, 2021)
URL: http://www.senshu-u.ac.jp/~off1009/PDF/geppo2021/698%E3%83%BB699/smr698%E3%83%BB699_takahashi.pdf (pp. 21-23, notes on p. 26). Evidence: the Ukon house, Tenpō era; the Sakaya house, 1863. The 1863 figures are second-hand, from 牧野隆信『北前船とそのふる里』 (2018).
> 「運賃積は他の商人たちからの依頼で荷物を運ぶわけだから、収益は予め決められていた。これに対して買積は、利益率は高いが、商品の仕入れと販売を自らおこなうため、リスクも大きく」

(translation) "Freight carrying moved other merchants' goods on order, so its earnings were fixed in advance. Kaizumi had a higher profit rate but larger risk, since the owner bought and sold the goods himself."
> 「船頭は船の荷物の一割を自分の荷物として積み込むことができ、これは「帆待ち（ほまち）」と呼ばれた。また、その他の水主たちは船の荷物（船主の荷物）の一割程度を分け合うことができた。これが彼らの分け前で、「切り出し（きりだし）」と呼ばれた。」

(translation) "The captain could load a tenth of the ship's cargo as his own goods; this was called homachi. The other crew could share about a tenth of the ship's (owner's) cargo; this was their share, called kiridashi."
> 「天保時代、一航海あたりの利益は1000両と言われた（この時代、金1両＝銀60匁）」

(translation) "In the Tenpō era the profit of one voyage was said to be 1,000 ryo (1 ryo of gold = 60 monme of silver then)."
The author's own arithmetic, not a source figure: homachi 100 ryo, kiridashi 100 ryo. The 1835 Hachiman-maru's 11 crew had wages of 銀1068匁 in all. Officers' wages in the Tenpō era were 195 monme (captain), 130 (clerk, bosun and mate), 60-100 (hand) and 35-45 (cook).
Note 14:
> 「酒谷長兵衛所有「幸長丸」の1863年（文久3年）の場合、利益は、下り荷物105両、登り荷物932両、木綿9両だった。航海に要した費用が217両だったから、正味の利益はおよそ830両。牧野によれば、実際には、一航海からの利益は千両を一割から二割くらい下回っていたようである。」

(translation) "For Sakaya Chōbei's Kōchō-maru in 1863 (Bunkyū 3), the profit was 105 ryo on the northbound cargo, 932 ryo on the southbound cargo and 9 ryo on cotton. The voyage's expenses were 217 ryo, so the net profit was about 830 ryo. According to Makino, the real profit of a voyage seems to have fallen 10 to 20% short of 1,000 ryo."
This is the only in-window per-voyage account found. The cargo value is not given, so no percent on cargo can be made from it. The southbound (herring) leg made about 89% of the trading gain (932/1,046, arithmetic).

### B2. Nanpo Masaru, 「北前船主の館 右近家」を訪ねて (Fukui Prefectural University column)

(translation) "A Kitamaebune's profit for one voyage was the earnings on the northbound and southbound cargoes less the crew's wages, food and ship repairs. ### B4. Kawamura Akimasa (as A5): one full voyage account, Kantoku-maru, **1876 (Meiji 9), outside the window**
URL as A5, pp. 125-137. A hired captain's 差引帳 (voyage account) with its sale and purchase settlements.
> 「ここで対象とする観徳丸は年２回の運航を行っていたようである。」

(translation) "The Kantoku-maru seems to have made two runs a year." It sailed Niigata 25 July and reached Osaka 23 October.
> 「…100.8995円が「下り」の商いで得られた売買益であった。この売上利益率は2.96％であった。」

(translation) "100.8995 yen was the trading gain on the northbound business. This profit rate on sales was 2.96%." Rice bought at 3,310.9175 yen in Niigata sold for 3,411.8170 at Otaru.
> 「個々の取引相手および個々の商品で見ると３～10％の利益となった。だが、小樽での仕入れ商品には仕入の際の問屋口銭や積入賃など港湾関係の費用や税金が含まれるので、「登り荷」全体の収支計算では表中に見られるように「利分」は15.4201円にとどまった。売上に対する利益率は１％にも満たなかったことになる。遭難など航海中のリスクを考えるとかなり厳しいビジネスを行っていたことになる。」

(translation) "By buyer and by good the profit was 3 to 10%. But the Otaru purchases carried the wholesaler's commission, loading charges, port costs and taxes, so for the southbound cargo as a whole the 'profit' was only 15.4201 yen, less than 1% of sales. Given the risks of the voyage, such as wreck, this was a hard business."
Per good, from the paper's text just before this passage: herring roe 3%, herring meal 6.3%, split herring 10.0% of sales.
Profit and loss (table 2(I)): purchases 3,310.9175 + 2,140.2843; sales 3,411.8170 + 2,155.7044; voyage expenses 128.2976. The table's loss is 「損失 11.9780」; the text says 「11.9180円の赤字」 ((translation) "a deficit of 11.9180 yen"). The two disagree as printed.
Expenses by kind: ship parts and repair 37.4%, food 25.6%, port and dues 14.0%, crew pay 10.3%, gifts 9.9%, sundries 2.8%.
Arithmetic: the gross gain on both legs, 116.3196, is 2.1% of total sales of 5,567.52; after expenses the voyage made about -0.2%.
The same owner's Kōun-maru in 1876 (from 安宅's study): 「買仕切合計7,219円、売仕切合計7,942円…単純に売買差益を計算すると723円」 ((translation) "purchase settlements 7,219 yen, sale settlements 7,942 … a simple trading gain of 723 yen"). That is 9.1% of sales, gross (arithmetic).
Ship cost: 「観徳丸船代金」2,454円 in 1874; book value 2,801 in 1877, then 2,000 in 1878 and 1,000 in 1880.

## Reading and verdicts

**(a) Wholesaler commission: ATTESTED.** Consignment-wholesaler commissions as a percent of the selling price are attested in Edo-period Japan, by guild, from a dictionary (A1):
- Kyoto silk thread 1% from each side (2% in all);
- Osaka cotton 1.3% from the shipper;
- Edo down-sake 3% from the Nada shipper.

The Edo-bound Osaka oil wholesalers added 0.8% (A3, arithmetic). Osaka's fresh-fish wholesalers in 1795 kept a net 11.5-12.2% of the auction price, out of a gross 17.86%, after rebating about 6.3% to the buying middlemen, and then bore their advances and bad debts from it (A2). The fish commission became 10% in 1816. In 1841 the dried- and salted-fish trade charged 9%: the wholesaler 4%, costs and porters 2%, the middlemen's rebate 3%. So the consignment house's own cut ran from about 1% (silk, cotton, oil) to 3% (sake), 4% (dried fish) and 11-12% (fresh fish, a perishable auctioned daily).

**Buying wholesaler's markup: ATTESTED ONLY FOR one buying house.** Mitsui's Edo cotton shop marked up its own purchases by 2.1-5.8% (fudakake), 1789-1840s (A4). The paper does not call it a 仕入問屋, and no other 仕入問屋 markup was found. Searched: 仕入問屋 利益, 十組問屋 口銭, 荷受問屋 仕入問屋 違い 利益. Mitsui's buying agent at Aoya took 1% of the purchase as commission in 1795 (A4).

**Merchant house net margin: ATTESTED ONLY FOR gross margins on particular goods.** Mitsui's Edo cotton shop made 2.1-5.8% (fudakake) on cotton by source and period, 1789-1840s. A 1798 clerk names 3-12% on striped and yukata cloths (A4). No house's net profit on sales or on capital (Mitsui 大元方, Nakai, Konoike, Shirokiya) was read. The Nakai thesis read gives only lending rates.

**(b) Shipowner trading on his own account: ATTESTED** for the saying and for one in-window account.
- **The saying.** "A thousand ryo a voyage" is attested as a Tenpō and bakumatsu saying (B1, B3).
- **The 1863 account.** The only in-window account, Kōchō-maru in 1863, made 1,046 ryo of trading gains (105 northbound, 932 southbound, 9 cotton) less 217 ryo of costs, about 830 ryo net. Makino reckons real voyages fell 10-20% short of 1,000 ryo (B1).
- **Percent on cargo value: SILENT in the window.** No in-window source gives the cargo value. The only percents on sales are from 1876, outside the window (B4):
  - Kantoku-maru, northbound rice 2.96%, southbound herring under 1%, single lots 3-10%, a net loss after a 128-yen voyage cost;
  - its sister ship, 9.1% gross (arithmetic).
- **Return on ship cost.** Yunoki, via Kawamura, has a Tenpō ship of 1,000 ryo earning about its own cost each year, about 100% a year on the hull. This is second-hand.
- **Voyages a year: ATTESTED.** One round trip a year Osaka-Hokkaido (B5); twice a year Niigata-Hokkaido-Osaka in 1876 (B4).
- **Northbound against southbound legs: ATTESTED** that the herring leg dominated: 932 of 1,046 ryo in 1863; 1,169 of 1,538 in 1872.
- **Profit split: ATTESTED.** The captain's homachi was a tenth of the cargo and the crew's kiridashi about a tenth (B1).
- **Loss rate to wreck: SILENT.** Searched 北前船 海難 割合, 菱垣廻船 樽廻船 難船 割合. Leads: 上杉喜寿 1993 and 金指正三 1968 compile wrecks, but give no rate against sailings.
- **Higaki and Taru voyages a year: SILENT.** Searched 菱垣廻船 樽廻船 年間 往復 回数. The Kobe city pages that might hold it return 404.
- **Interest on cargo money: ATTESTED.** Nagegin ran 25-40% a voyage in 1626-1638, and 50% if back within the year, 80% if the next August (A8). This is consistent with the canon's "30-110%" only at its low end; nothing above 80% was found. The Edo legal rate was 15-18% a year (A7). Lianghuai salt merchants borrowed at 1.5% a month in c. 1830 (C5).

**(c) China: ATTESTED ONLY FOR single cargoes and one Canton margin.**
- **Single cargoes.** In 1831 a Siamese junk at Tianjin made over 100% on sapanwood and pepper, 50% on calicoes and little on sugar and tin. Wrecks every season made the profit "on the whole amount of shipping … comparatively small" (C1).
- **Canton tea.** Ball gives the Hong merchant's gross on tea as about 30% on cost, about 25% of the price (arithmetic), in the Company years (C2).
- **Voyages a year: ATTESTED.** Shanghai's big bean-trade houses made 3-4 round trips a year to Guandong (Tongzhi). Amoy coastal junks made several a year, and Nanyang junks one (C3, C4). A sand-junk cost 7,000-10,000+ taels (C3).
- **Brokerage (牙用) as a percent: SILENT for the Qing.** Searched 牙行 牙用 百分之, 清代 牙行 佣金 比例. The Shanghai freight brokers explicitly took no cut of the freight (Bao Shichen, C3). The only commission percents are Fei's 4% on silk and about 0.7% on rice (1936, outside the window) and Smith's 1% fair levy (1890s).
- **Return per voyage or per year on capital, and Huizhou or Shanxi profit rates: SILENT.** Searched 徽商 利润率 什一, 沙船 获利, 松浦章 利益; Ng Chin-keong's OAPEN book returned 403.

### One line per figure
- **Commission as % of sale, consignment wholesaler:** ATTESTED. 1-1.3% (Kyoto silk each side, Osaka cotton); 3% (Edo sake); 4% (Osaka dried fish 1841); 11.5-12.2% net (Osaka fresh fish 1795).
- **Buying wholesaler markup:** SILENT.
- **Merchant net margin % of selling price:** ATTESTED ONLY FOR gross per good. Mitsui cotton 2.1-5.8% (1789-1840s); Kantoku-maru 1876 legs 2.96% and under 1% (outside the window).
- **Return per voyage:** ATTESTED in ryo, not in %. About 830 ryo net (1863) against the saying of 1,000; no cargo value in the window.
- **Voyages a year:** ATTESTED. 1 (Kitamaebune), 2 (1876 Niigata ship), 3-4 (Shanghai bean junks), several or 1 (Amoy coastal or Nanyang).
- **Loss rate:** SILENT (only qualitative, C1).
- **Profit split:** ATTESTED. Homachi a tenth of the cargo to the captain, kiridashi about a tenth to the crew.
- **Interest on cargo money:** ATTESTED. Nagegin 25-40% a voyage, up to 50-80% (1626-1638); 15-18% a year legal (Edo); 1.5% a month (Lianghuai, c. 1830).
