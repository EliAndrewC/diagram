QUESTION=none. Clone /diagram/.clones/diagram-research. Archive searched with `make research-search` under KEY=p454b
(general), p454b-tea, p454b-sugar (two batches on p454b, one on each of the others; the counts said 39/40/58 more were

was cited by me: the session cites.

Conversion used throughout (this project's arithmetic, label it so): 1 tael = 37.3 g silver (kuping, record 0289); 1 picul =
100 catties = 133 1/3 lb = 60.48 kg; so **1 tael/picul = 0.617 g silver/kg**. Dollars to taels at Ball's Canton rating, 7 mace
2 candareens (0.72 tael) to the dollar. The Canton trade tael was somewhat heavier than kuping (about 37.7-37.8 g), so the g/kg
figures for Canton are about 1% low. Prices "per jin" are taken as per catty.

## 1. TEA

### 1a. Ball 1848, Appendix pp. 379-382: "Cost of Tea in the Bohea District" (NEW - record 0283 used only pp. 341-342, 353-354)
Archived: cb/cbfeea3b791d (https://archive.org/stream/accountofcultiva00ballrich/accountofcultiva00ballrich_djvu.txt).
Period of the evidence: the East India Company's last years at Canton; the contract terms are dated "the season 1824-6". What it
measures: (i) Wuyi-district selling prices by grade; (ii) the account of two chops (1,000 and 600 chests), first cost to
packed cost in the hills; (iii) the Hong merchants' contract with the tea-men, itemized from the hills to delivery at Canton.

"175 185 195" read as 17 5 / 18 5 / 19 5), and with my column reading where the OCR scatters figures. Those
are MY READINGS, not verbatim, so check the page images before citing. I read the columns as the sums allow: both columns of the contract table add to their stated totals
(11.91 + 1.31 + 2.40 + 3.92 + 0.36 + 0.60 = 20.50; 10.83 + 1.31 + 1.48 + 3.92 + 0.36 + 0.60 = 18.50). **Check against the page
images before citing** (as 0283 did for p. 353).

Prices in the hills, by grade (p. 379-380):

> Chinese class as Kong-fu or Congou, sell from 22 to 32 tales the pecul ; and the teas known to us as Congou, sell from 7 to 13
> tales the pecul. These are the prices after the usual allowances of trade have been deducted. On teas selling above 20 tales
> the pecul, an allowance is made from the seller to the buyer of 7 per cent. ; and below 20 tales, the allowance is 20 per cent.
> A further allowance is also made, on payment in cash, of 5 per cent. There is also a gain to the Canton merchant on the dollar,

> moreover, a gain of 10 per cent, in the weight of tea." ... "The first gatherings of these latter teas sell from 11 to 21

And p. 382:
> "Further, I have a statement of the cost of 12 chops of tea, varying in price from 10*2 to 14*4 tales, and furnishing an average
> of 12'1 tales. The cost of chests is stated at 1*1 tales, together making the cost of tea packed, 13*2 tales the pecul." ...
> "Congou tea, equal in quality to such as the East India Company formerly received as contract teas, cost on an average 12 tales

Figures (a)-(d), Congou, 1824-26:
- (a) hills, unpacked: 11.91 taels/picul (7.35 g silver/kg); delivered at Canton under contract: 20.50 taels/picul (12.65 g/kg).
  Second column 10.83 to 18.50 (6.68 to 11.41 g/kg). Same contract, same season, same grade.
- (b) ratio Canton contract / hills: **1.72** (col. 1), **1.71** (col. 2), this project's arithmetic. Ball himself gives no ratio
  here. On p. 354 (already in 0283) he gives 12 to 20.24 to 27 (the Company's price).
- (c) Wuyi to Canton: down the Min, porters over the passes to Hekou, then the Jiangxi rivers and the Meiling pass (already in
  0283). The p. 356 figure for the Foochow water route is 270 miles. The time for the inland route is in 0283.
- (d) what made up the 8.59-tael gap (col. 1): chests and packing 1.31; interest on the advances 2.40 (12-18% a year for 8-10
  months); transport and duties to Canton 3.92; interest on the transport money 0.36; tea-factor's profit 0.60. In words: carriage
  and duties 46% of the gap, interest 32%, packing 15%, factor 7% (this project's arithmetic).

### 1b. Ball 1848, pp. 355-357: what the same Congou should cost at Foochow against Canton (the table is not in 0283)
Same archive copy. Evidence: c. 1816 (the paper "originally printed at Macao, in 1816"), restated for after 1842.

> cultivation and manipulation - 12 0 0 | 12 0 0 / Cost of chests, canisters, and packing 1 3 1 | 1 3 1 / Expense of transit
> 3 9 2 | 0 4 3 / Government duties 2 0 0 | 2 0 0 / Profit to factors or merchants, charges of shipment, &c. 15 per cent, say
> 2 0 0 | 2 0 0 / 21 2 3 | 17 7 4 / Thus teas ought to be bought from 15 to 17 per cent, cheaper at Foo-chew-foo than at Canton."
> "...good Congou tea, such as used formerly to cost 27 tales the pecul at Canton, ought now to be purchased at the port of Foo-
> chew-foo at from 17 to 18 tales the pecul"
Ratio hills / Foochow cost: 17.74 / 12 = **1.48**; Canton cost: 21.23 / 12 = **1.77** (arithmetic). This is a cost estimate, not
an observed price. Note that the 3.92 here is "expense of transit" with duties put separately, while the 1824-6 table's 3.92 is
"transport and duties". Ball uses the one figure both ways.

### 1c. Fortune 1852, *A Journey to the Tea Countries of China*, ch. XV "Cost and expenses", pp. 267-270 (NEW to the record)
https://archive.org/download/ajourneytoteaco00fortgoog/ajourneytoteaco00fortgoog_djvu.txt (fetched, ledgered; the other copy
journeytoteacoun00fort is truncated - recorded unreadable). Evidence: 1846-1849, Wuyi black tea brought via Tsong-gan-hien
(Chong'an) to the Shanghai export market. It measures the farmer's selling price, an itemized cost to Shanghai, and the observed
Shanghai price for the same grade.
(OCR normalized: OCR "8c?." read "8d.", "ccilti- vators" read "cultivators", "Ghang-8ban" read "Chang-shan", "CmH." read
"Cash"; bracketed figures are my placement of the OCR's figure column. Verify on the page images.)
> "In the first place, let us examine the expenses upon what is called good common Congou. By this is meant such tea as was
> selling in England in December, 1848, at about 8d. per pound. This tea was sold in Shanghae at about 12 taels per picul in
> 1846, 11 taels in 1847, from 9 to 10 taels in 1848, and 11 taels in July, 1849. These prices included the export duty. I will
> suppose this tea to be brought from the town of Tsong-gan-hien by the route which I have already described. The expenses for
> coolie and boat hire upon it will be nearly as follows : — Tsong-gan-hien to Hokow (by land) [800] Hokow to Yuk-shan (by
> water) [150] Yuk-shan to Chang-shan (by land) [400] Chang-shan to Hang-chow-foo (by water) [200] Expenses for coolies at
> Hang-chow-foo [10] Hang-chow-foo to Shanghae (by water) [180] Total for carriage . . Cash 1740 per chest ... 1740 cash per
> chest would amount to 2718 cash per picul, which, converted into silver, would be about 1 dollar 80 cents, or 1.359 taels. To
> this sum must be added the cost of tea in the tea-country, the expenses of the wholesale dealers for inspection, charcoal, and
> labour in extra firing, the cost of the chest and packing, and custom-house and export duties. Such tea as that above referred
> to is sold by the cultivators and small farmers at about 80 cash a catty, which is equal to 4 taels per picul. The following
> table will show the total amount of these expenses : — Cost of tea at 80 cash per catty [4 taels per picul] / Do. of chest and
> packing [0.847] / Wholesale dealer's extra expenses [1] / Carriage, as above [1.359] / Hang-chow-foo custom-house [0.037] /
> Export duty at Shanghae [2.530] / [9.773]"
(The OCR puts the figures in a column after the labels. I placed them by order, and they sum to 9.773.)
> "Take for example the good and middling Ohows, and finest teas, which sold in Shanghae, December 1846, at from 20 to 28 taels,
> long price; in 1847 at 18 to 26 taels; in 1848 at 14 to 22 taels; and in July 1849 at 16 to 25 taels per picul. ... These fine
> teas are said to be sold by the small farmers to the dealers at, on an average, 160 cash a catty, a sum probably higher than
> that which is actually paid. But suppose 160 cash per catty is the original cost, the matter would stand thus : — Cost of tea at
> 160 cash per catty ... 8 taels per picul. Total charges, as before, less the cost of tea 5*773 [=] 13*773 In round numbers, the
> whole cost of bringing these fine teas to the port of Shanghae is 14 taels. The average price received from the English
> merchant during these four years appears, from the above prices, to have been about 22 taels, thus showing a clear profit of 8
> taels per picul."
> "Long price [l. p.] means that the export duty is included."
Figures:
- (a) common Congou: farm 4 taels/picul (2.47 g/kg); cost laid down and duty-paid at Shanghai 9.77 (6.03 g/kg); observed Shanghai
  price 9-12 (11 in 1847 and July 1849; 6.79 g/kg at 11). Fine teas: farm 8 (4.94 g/kg); cost at Shanghai 13.77; Shanghai price
  14-28, average about 22 (13.57 g/kg).
- (b) ratio Shanghai market price / farm price: **about 2.75** for both grades, ex-duty about 2.1 (11 - 2.53 = 8.47 / 4);
  cost / farm 2.44 and 1.72 (arithmetic; Fortune states only the absolute profit, 8 and 4 taels).
- (c) porters and boats: Chong'an to Hekou by land, Hekou to Yushan by water, Yushan to Changshan by land, Changshan to
  Hangzhou by water, then to Shanghai by water. Carriage per chest is itemized in cash.
- (d) chest and packing 0.847; dealer's firing and inspection 1.0; carriage 1.359; Hangzhou customs 0.037; Shanghai export duty
  2.530; the rest is profit ("a clear profit of 8 taels" on fine teas, an average of 4 taels in 1848). Fortune doubts the farm
  price for fine tea ("probably higher than that which is actually paid").

### Verdict, tea
**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the export roads, hills to port. Wuyi Congou cost 11.9 taels a picul in the hills and 20.5 delivered at
Canton under the Hong merchants' 1824-6 contract: ratio 1.72, itemized, from one source, one season and one grade. It cost 4
taels at the farm and sold at 11 at Shanghai in 1846-49: ratio about 2.75, itemized (Fortune). Packed at Fuzhou, Ball's estimate
was 1.48 times the hill cost. For Beijing, or any domestic consuming city, I found no hill and city price at the same date. All
I have are city retail prices: Shanghai c. 1680 (Songluo 0.2-0.3 tael/jin) and Peking 1821 (black tea 1 liang/kin). Neither is
matched to a producer price of the same grade and year.

## 2. RAW SILK

### 2a. Morse, *Chronicles of the East India Company Trading to China*, vol. V (1929, supplementary 1742-74)
https://archive.org/download/morse-1929-the-chronicles-of-the-east-india-company-volume-5/Morse%201929%20The_Chronicles_of_the_East_India_Company%20Volume%205_djvu.txt
(fetched, ledgered). These are port prices only, at two ports, Canton and Ningbo (Ningbo is about 200 km from Huzhou). Each
is the Company's purchase price per picul.
- Canton, 1756 (statement of April 28, 1756): "Contracted for: Raw Silk, 800 piculs ; . Tls. 154,000" (= 192.5 taels/picul,
  arithmetic). Also "250 piculs raw silk, at Tls. 192·5 @ picul, to be delivered in December (1756) advanced cash Tls. 160".
- Ningbo, season 1757 (the last Company voyage there):
  > "The tea was bought, Bohea generally for Tls. 10 a picul, Hyson Tls. 42, Twankee Tls. 22. The price paid for raw silk ranged
  > from Tls. 225 to Tls. 250; and at the close of the season as much as Tls. 260 to 270 was asked for further supplies, but not
  > paid."
  The officials' memorial as Morse quotes it: "the duties by being much lower here than at Canton, all the ships wou'd in a few
  years quit that port ... the Cargo's which they carry to Europe, being chiefly the produce of this part of the Country".
- Canton, later: 1763 "113 bales only, bought at Tls. 240 to 250 per picul"; 1765 "The silk was bought at Tls. 269 a picul";
  1766 "contracted at Tls. 265 a picul"; 1767 "Tls. 260 a picul"; 1768 "Tls. 265 a picul ... an additional quantity of 200
  piculs at Tls. 294"; 1771-74 "Tls. 272·50 per picul". There is also an undated early contract "350 piculs at Tls. 198 per picul".
- Reading: Ningbo in 1757 (225-250) was no cheaper than Canton in 1756 (192.5). The draw of Ningbo was lower duties, not
  lower silk prices. That is one year apart and both ports are consuming/export ends, so it is not the pair asked for.
  In g/kg: 192.5 to 118.8; 250 to 154.3; 272.5 to 168.1 (arithmetic).

### 2b. Beijing 1736 and 1821, consuming end only
- **《九卿議定物料價值》 1736** (d7/d7234ae5c7ec), 表3 丝及丝织品: 「生丝每斤0.9；青白丝每斤1.5；黄丝每斤1.49；生丝线每斤1.44」
  (translation) "raw silk 0.9 [tael] a jin; blue-white silk 1.5 a jin; yellow silk 1.49 a jin; raw silk thread 1.44 a jin". The
  Ming 1615 list for comparison: 「生丝每斤0.5//0.9」. Beijing raw silk at 0.9/jin = 90 taels/100 jin (55.5 g/kg), against Canton
  export silk at 192.5/picul in 1756. The grade differs (Canton bought export Huzhou/"Nanking" silk), so this is no ratio.
- **Timkowski, Peking 1821** (88/88311ca5eebd): "Silk: the kin, according to the Chinese weight, contains 12 liang of pure silk.
  Silk, shaded ... 5 a 7½ liang". This is THREAD, dyed or shaded, per kin, not raw silk.

### Verdict, raw silk
**SILENT** on a Huzhou (Nanxun, Qili) price set against a Canton, Beijing or Lu'an price at the same date. I read Canton and Ningbo
export prices for 1756-1774 (Morse V) and Beijing's official 1736 raw-silk price (0.9 tael/jin, grade unknown). Nothing came from the producing end. Searched: archive KEY=p454b-silk (`絲價;湖絲;七里;絲行;生絲;raw silk|picul;Tsatlee;Nanking silk`, two batches of 115 sources; the
only producer-side silk prices printed are Fei Xiaotong's 1930s Kaixiangong village, outside the window and not paired); web: "Tsatlee raw silk price Shanghai Canton difference cost of carriage overland per picul 1840s Huzhou", "湖絲 每百斤
價銀 廣州 乾隆 絲價 昂貴 洋船 湖州 產地", "乾隆二十四年 李兆鵬 絲斤 出洋 價值 昂貴", "Morse Chronicles 1755 Ningpo raw silk
price", and "Tsatlee silk Nanzing likin laid down Shanghai 1880". None of these returned a producer price. Search snippets
pointed to a secondary claim that 李煦's Kangxi memorials give Suzhou-Hangzhou silk at 纬丝 0.6+, 单经 0.7+, 线经 0.8+ tael/jin. It
was not read in the original, so it is a lead, not evidence. The same goes for the snippet that 顾炎武 put 湖丝 at 「百斤，价值百两」 at the end of the Ming.

### 3a-bis. 黃叔璥《臺海使槎錄》卷一〈赤嵌筆談・賦餉〉 (written 1722-24): Taiwan's sugar price and the freight to Suzhou
https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/臺海使槎錄/卷1 (fetched, ledgered). The producing end, with freight to the consuming city:
> 「三縣每歲所出蔗糖約六十餘萬簍，每簍一百七、八十觔；烏糖百觔價銀八、九錢，白糖百觔價銀一兩三、四錢。全臺仰望資生，四方奔趨圖息，莫此為甚。糖觔未出，客人先行定買；糖一入手，即便裝載。每簍到蘇，船價二錢有零。自定聯䑸之法，非動經數旬不能齊一。及至廈門，歸關盤查，一船所經兩次護送、八次掛驗，俱不無費。是以船難即行，腳價貴而糖價賤矣（余奏准半線分設彰化縣，尚在經理，故仍三縣之稱）。」
> (translation) "The three counties produce some 600,000-odd baskets of cane sugar a year, each basket 170 or 180 jin. Black
> sugar is worth 0.8-0.9 tael of silver per 100 jin, white sugar 1.3-1.4 taels per 100 jin. All Taiwan looks to it for a living,
> and people come from every quarter seeking profit from it; nothing is more so. Before the sugar is out, the traders have already
> contracted to buy it; as soon as it is in hand, it is loaded. Shipping each basket to Suzhou costs a little over 0.2 tael. Since
> the rule of sailing in convoys was fixed, it takes several tens of days to get the ships together; at Xiamen they go under the
> customs for inspection, and each ship passes two escorts and eight inspections, none without fees. So the ships can hardly
> sail, freight is dear and the sugar is cheap. (I have had Zhanghua county split off at Banxian with imperial approval, but it is
> still being set up, so I still say 'three counties'.)"
Date: just before Zhanghua county was set up, so 1723. Figures: (a) Taiwan white 1.3-1.4 taels/100 jin (0.80-0.86 g/kg), black
0.8-0.9 (0.49-0.56 g/kg). (c) by junk, Taiwan to Xiamen for inspection, then to Suzhou, with convoys taking "several tens of
days". Freight 0.2+ tael per basket of 170-180 jin = about 0.11-0.12 tael per 100 jin, about 8-9% of white sugar's price and
13-15% of black's (arithmetic). (d) freight, convoy delay, and inspection and escort fees ("兩次護送、八次掛驗，俱不無費").
**No Suzhou sugar price of 1723 was found.** The nearest consuming-end price is Shanghai's, about 40 years earlier (3b). Stitched,
and labeled as stitched: Shanghai white sugar 0.03-0.06 tael/jin (3-6 per 100 jin) in the 1680s, against Taiwan white 1.3-1.4
per 100 jin in 1723, is 2.2-4.6 times. That is NOT the same date (arithmetic).
