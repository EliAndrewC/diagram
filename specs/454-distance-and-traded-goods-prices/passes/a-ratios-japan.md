**Conversions.** 1 kin (斤) = 160 monme = 600 g, except where a source says otherwise; the 1842 Edo tea list uses a kin of 200 me = 750 g. 1 kan (貫) = 3.75 kg. Silver 1 monme = 3.75 g, so **a price in monme of silver per kan of goods is numerically equal to grams of silver per kg**. Gold is turned into silver at the legal 60 monme to the ryo; the market rate was about 63-65 in the 1830s. Every conversion is this pass's arithmetic and is labelled.

## 1. RAW SILK: Kyoto against the Fukushima (Shindatsu) and Joshu (Maebashi) districts

**Source:** 烏蘭其其格 (Wulan Qiqige), 『前近代日中における蚕糸絹織物業の社会経済的分析』, doctoral thesis, University of Hyogo, submitted 10 June 2014, pp. 42-43. https://u-hyogo.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/141/files/%E5%89%8D%E8%BF%91%E4%BB%A3%E6%97%A5%E4%B8%AD%E3%81%AB%E3%81%8A%E3%81%91%E3%82%8B%E8%9A%95%E7%B3%B8%E7%B5%B9%E7%B9%94%E7%89%A9%E6%A5%AD%E3%81%AE%E7%A4%BE%E4%BC%9A%E7%B5%8C%E6%B8%88%E7%9A%84%E5%88%86%E6%9E%90.pdf . Both tables are reprinted from 山崎隆三『近世物価史研究』(塙書房, 1983).
**Period:** 1818-1867.
**What it measures:** the raw-silk price per kan in ryo at Kyoto and at Maebashi, and a Kyoto index against the Shindatsu district.

Table 18, 「都市の生糸価格」, 1貫目ニ付 両, Kyoto | Maebashi (verbatim, years with both ends):
「天保１ 5.986 5.256 / 2 5.917 5.133 / 3 5.649 5.000 / 4 5.486 4.444 / 5 5.188 5.122 / 天保6 5.778 5.644 / 7 6.028 5.844 / 8 5.903 5.411 / 9 6.306 6.056 / 10 7.778 6.056 / 12 6.528 6.056 / 嘉永１ 7.222 6.444 / 2 7.333 6.889」
(translation) Raw silk per kan in ryo, Kyoto then Maebashi: 1830 5.986 / 5.256; 1831 5.917 / 5.133; 1832 5.649 / 5.000; 1833 5.486 / 4.444; 1834 5.188 / 5.122; 1835 5.778 / 5.644; 1836 6.028 / 5.844; 1837 5.903 / 5.411; 1838 6.306 / 6.056; 1839 7.778 / 6.056; 1841 6.528 / 6.056; 1848 7.222 / 6.444; 1849 7.333 / 6.889.
For 1818-29 Kyoto alone is given (5.313-6.069). For 1851-67 Maebashi alone is given (5.000 to 63.000).

The text:
「表 19 は、信達地方の代表値を 100 とした京都糸価格の指数を示したものである（信達地方と他の地方もほぼ同水準である）。これを見ると、天保元年までが 130 以上を示すのに対して、同 2 年以降は 120－125 となっている。また、地方産地価格との動きとほぼ対応しており、年ごとの変動幅もそれほど大きくないことが確認できる。」
(translation) Table 19 gives the Kyoto silk price as an index with the representative value of the Shindatsu district set at 100 (Shindatsu and the other districts were at nearly the same level). Up to Tenpo 1 it stands at 130 or more; from Tenpo 2 on it is 120-125. It moves broadly with the district prices, and the year-to-year swings are not large.

Table 19, 「信達糸価を基準とする京都糸価指数」, in full:
「文政１ 132 / 2 126 / 3 125 / 4 129 / 6 131 / 7 135 / 8 139 / 9 143 / 10 120 / 11 135 / 天保１ 135 / 2 120 / 3 123 / 4 120 / 5 125 / 6 120 / 7 120 / 8 121 / 9 126 / 10 128 / 12 98 / 嘉永１ 123 / 2 121」
(translation) The same values, for 1818-1849 (Bunsei 5 and 12, Tenpo 11 and 13-14, and the Kōka years blank).

On Maebashi:
「前橋は消費地としての都市というよりも、産地の中心市場というべきである。」
(translation) Maebashi should be called the central market of a producing district rather than a consuming city.
Footnote 103: 「前橋糸は輸出生糸の中でも優良糸で、且つ、交通の便宜その他の関係から、その糸価は他の糸価より高価であったと言われている。」
(translation) Maebashi silk is said to have been a superior thread and, because of easy transport and other reasons, dearer than other silks.

- (a) Example, 1836: Kyoto 6.028 ryo a kan against Maebashi 5.844, about **362 against 351 g of silver per kg** at 60 monme to the ryo (arithmetic). For 1830 it is 359 against 315 g/kg.
- (b) **Kyoto against Shindatsu, from the source's own index: 0.98-1.43**, in most years 1.20-1.43. The source summarises it as 1.30 or more up to 1830 and 1.20-1.25 from 1831, but its own table shows 1.20-1.29 in some earlier years and 0.98 in 1841. **Kyoto against Maebashi, computed from Table 18: 1.01-1.28**, most years 1.03-1.15.
- (c) No route or distance is given in the source. By this pass's estimate the distances are about 500 km from Maebashi and about 700 km from Fukushima, overland.
- (d) No breakdown of the gap is given.

The thesis also has Fukushima floss and thread prices for 1688-1750 (Table 14, after Yamazaki) with no Kyoto pair. They were not transcribed.

## 2. SUGAR: Satsuma's Amami and Ryukyu black sugar, the island purchase rate (paid in rice) against Osaka

**A. 『薩摩藩法令史料集』二 (島津家歴代制度 巻之十五～), Kagoshima Prefectural Reimeikan.** https://www.pref.kagoshima.jp/ab23/reimeikan/siroyu/documents/6756_20230221111232-1.pdf . This is the primary text for the purchase rates. All quotations below were checked against the page images, because the text layer of this vertical typesetting is scrambled.

Kikaijima, undated on the page (PDF p. 32, printed p. 5, 巻之十五):
「喜界嶋免本 右ノ内、一米二千百七十五石起 但、当冬御買入砂糖五十八万斤、一斤ニ付三合七勺五才ヅヽ、 一同百六十四石一斗一升五合 但、右入樽四千六百八十九挺、但、三升五合ヅヽ、 一同六百四十一石二斗五升 但、御買重砂糖十三万五千斤代、一斤ニ付四合七勺五才、 … 一同九十石 但、白砂糖五千斤代、一斤一升八合ヅヽ、」
(translation) Kikaijima, of its tax rice: 2,175 koku for this winter's purchased sugar, 580,000 kin, at 3 go 7 shaku 5 sai of rice a kin; 164.115 koku for its 4,689 casks at 3 sho 5 go each; 641.25 koku for 135,000 kin of extra-purchase sugar (御買重), at 4 go 7 shaku 5 sai a kin; … 90 koku for 5,000 kin of white sugar, at 1 sho 8 go a kin.
Check: 2,175 koku / 580,000 kin = 3.75 go a kin (arithmetic).

An Ox year, undated on the page (PDF p. 34, printed p. 7). The next item is dated 文化七年 (1810):
「一米三千六百石 丑冬御買入砂糖百二十万斤、代米一斤ニ付三合ヅヽ、 一同四百二十石 右入樽一万二千挺代、一斤ニ付三升五合ヅヽ、」
(translation) 3,600 koku of rice for 1,200,000 kin of sugar bought in the Ox-year winter, at 3 go a kin; 420 koku for its 12,000 casks. The page prints 一斤 for the casks, but 420 koku / 12,000 casks = 3.5 sho a cask.

Freight in kind for the islands (道之島運賃), 1745 (PDF p. 169, printed p. 142, 巻之十八). The page does not name the destination:
「一延享二年道之島運賃左之通被相定候、 一大島 … 黒砂糖百斤ニ付運賃十斤二合 外ニ、一斤此節下リ、 一喜界島 … 黒砂糖百斤ニ付運賃拾一斤半 一徳之島 … 黒砂糖百斤ニ付運賃十七斤半 内、三斤此節重ミ、」
(translation) Freight for the Amami islands set in 1745: Ōshima, for black sugar, 10 kin 2 go per 100 kin, with 1 kin more taken off this time; Kikaijima, 11½ kin per 100 kin; Tokunoshima, 17½ kin per 100 kin, of which 3 kin is this time's increase.

Ryukyu, Bunka 4 (1807) (PDF p. 168, printed p. 141):
「(行間朱書)文化四年卯八月、琉球運賃御勝手方ヨリ御糺ニ付左之通、 一館内蔵方届砂糖、百斤ニ付運賃砂糖廿三斤五合七勺、 運賃米一斗一升三合二勺、」
(translation) [Red interlinear note:] Bunka 4 (1807), 8th month, on the finance office's inquiry into Ryukyu freight: sugar delivered to the [Ryukyu-]kan's warehouse, freight 23 kin 5 go 7 shaku of sugar per 100 kin; freight rice, 1 to 1 sho 3 go 2 shaku.
The page does not say whether the rice line is an addition or the same freight stated in rice.

- (d) freight in kind took about **10-17.5% of the sugar** (Amami, 1745) and about **23.6%** (Ryukyu sugar to the domain warehouse, 1807).

**B. Kagoshima Prefecture, 黒糖 leaflet.** https://www.pref.kagoshima.jp/ba08/kokuto/documents/128908_20260617134239-1.pdf . Period 1830.
「文政13年（1830）の「砂糖惣買入に付品直段之覚」「大島年中往来」という史料によると、島民は大坂相場の約４倍～90倍の価格で諸品物を購入していました（松下1983）。たとえば、島民の食糧ともなり得る米１石を購入するためには、大坂相場の約4.2倍～6.4倍の砂糖が必要でした。」
(translation) According to the 1830 "Memorandum of goods prices under total sugar purchase" and "Ōshima nenjū ōrai", islanders bought goods at about 4 to 90 times the Osaka market price (Matsushita 1983). To buy 1 koku of rice, which could feed an islander, about 4.2 to 6.4 times as much sugar as at the Osaka rate was needed.
Also: 「天保元年（1830）から同３年に大坂に集積された砂糖の約51％は薩摩藩産」; 「奄美・琉球産を中心とする薩摩藩の砂糖は主に江戸・北国（北陸）で消費されていました（荒武2006）」.
(translation) About 51% of the sugar gathered at Osaka in 1830-32 was Satsuma's. It was consumed mainly in Edo and the Hokuriku.
- **This is the source-stated ratio: 4.2-6.4, in rice-exchange terms.** It equals a sugar-price ratio only if a koku of rice is valued alike at both ends, which the source does not say.

**C. 「薩摩藩の財政改革と調所広郷」, 久留米大学『経済社会研究』58(1・2), 2018.** https://kurume.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/455/files/keisya58_1-2_1-19.pdf . Period: the Tenpo era.
「米１升に対して黒砂糖5.07斤の交換比率が設定されていた。当時の米１升の平均価格は銀６分６厘３毛余、黒砂糖１斤の平均価格は銀１匁１分７厘５毛であったから奄美三島の人々は本土（薩摩藩）の6.2倍の米を売りつけられていたのである。」
(translation) An exchange rate of 5.07 kin of black sugar for 1 sho of rice was set. The average price of a sho of rice was then about 0.663 monme of silver, and of a kin of black sugar 1.175 monme, so the people of the three Amami islands were sold rice at 6.2 times the mainland (Satsuma) price.
Earlier rates in the same paper: 「宝暦12年（1762）には、黒糖１斤代米３合換え」 (1762, 3 go of rice a kin), and 「斉宣が藩主になると…黒糖１斤代米４合換えに改正された」 (under Narinobu, 4 go a kin).
Footnote 36: 「定式仕登せ砂糖700万斤のうちの百万斤の販売権利と１斤につき２厘の口銭」.
(translation) The right to sell 1 million of the 7 million kin of regularly shipped sugar, with a commission of 2 rin a kin. This is the 口銭 item of the gap.

Arithmetic on the paper's own figures: the island price is 0.663 / 5.07 = 0.131 monme a kin, about **0.82 g of silver per kg**, against 1.175 monme a kin, about **7.3 g/kg**. That is a ratio of **about 9.0, not the paper's 6.2**; the paper does not show how it reached 6.2.
The paper's 1.175 equals Kito's Osaka sales total divided by his arrivals at 60 monme to the ryo (D): 2,350,000 / 120,000,000 kin × 60 = 1.175. So it is probably an Osaka sale average (inference).

**D. 鬼頭宏「日本における甘味社会の成立―前近代の砂糖供給―」『上智経済論集』53, 2008.** https://fe.sophia.ac.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/53-03.pdf
「天保期の薩摩藩砂糖の大坂廻着高は、年1200万斤（7200トン）に達し、全国におけるシェアは51％と過半数を超えていた。天保元年～10年（1830～39）の売上高は235万両にもなっていた。ただし薩摩産の砂糖は、白砂糖製造技術が低かったために、多くが黒砂糖であったといわれている。」
(translation) Satsuma's sugar arrivals at Osaka in the Tenpo era reached 12 million kin (7,200 t) a year, 51% of the national total; sales for 1830-39 came to 2.35 million ryo. Most of it was black sugar.
- This gives an Osaka average of about 1.175 monme a kin, or 7.3 g of silver per kg (arithmetic, assuming 12 million kin in every year).

**E. 箕輪優「享保十三年『大島規模帳』に関する考察」 (成城大学).** https://seijo.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/3744/files/jpn-039-04.pdf . Note 15: 「当初の換糖比率は砂糖一斤に対して米三合六勺であった。」 (the initial rate of 1747 was 3 go 6 shaku of rice for 1 kin of sugar).
**F. Marcon (2015), ch. 13, in a Warwick reading pack.** https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/arts/history/ghcc/event/events/marcon_readings_-_all_files.pdf
「purchased at a price of 0.63 liters (0.35 shō) of rice for every kin (600 grams) of sugarcane. Anything over the quota was purchased for 0.72 liters of rice per kin of sugar-cane」 (after Matsui, *Satsuma hanshū Shimazu Shigehide*, 134-36; second half of the 18th century). "Sugarcane" is probably a slip for sugar, given the 3-4 go rates of A, C and E.
**G. 来間泰男「17～19世紀琉球の砂糖生産とその流通」 (沖縄国際大学).** https://okiu1972.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/3096/files/18801706_12_1_YasuoKurima.pdf . ## 3. IRON: works COST against the Osaka SALE ESTIMATE, Harima (not Izumo)

**A. 土佐雅彦・加納亜由子, translation and commentary of 「元文年中鉄山仕法書写」 (三日月藩文書, Shisō district, Harima), 『ひょうご歴史研究室紀要』9 (2024).** https://rekihaku.pref.hyogo.lg.jp/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/e84030e2d4bfdbd38e0253caf6f99756.pdf
Period: the Genbun era (1736-41). This is an operator's annual prospectus (積り) for a "six-blast" (六ッ吹) ironworks.
「内長延弐百九拾弐太壱人役正味三拾貫匁ツヽ代拾九貫(七ヵ)五百廿匁但鉄山ニ而出来口六拾匁ニ当ル 中割弐千弐百拾八太小割鍛冶役数三千百六十八人正味壱人ニ付弐拾弐貫四百目ニして代百六拾壱貫三百廿九匁壱分但出来口山ニ而七十弐匁七分四厘 右荷数〆弐千五百拾太但し壱太ニ付山大坂迄駄賃諸色七匁計り掛り申候 右之内大坂ニ而売口之積り 一長延弐百九拾弐太代拾九貫九百七十弐匁八分 一中割弐千弐百拾八太代百八拾六貫九十三匁 二口〆弐百六貫六拾五匁八分 内百七拾八貫八百四拾九匁壱分入用前ニ有分 拾七貫五百七拾匁山大坂迄賃︑諸入用 〆百九拾六貫四百拾九匁壱分 残而九貫六百四拾六匁七分」
(translation) Of this: 292 da of nagano-be (long bar), at 30 kan net per worker-day, cost 19 kan 520 monme [the editors suggest 17 kan], which **at the works comes to 60 monme a da**. 2,218 da of nakawari (split iron), from 3,168 smith-days at 22 kan 400 me net each, cost 161 kan 329.1 monme, which **at the works comes to 72.74 monme a da**. Total 2,510 da; **for each da the pack-horse hire and sundries from the works to Osaka come to about 7 monme**. **Estimated sale in Osaka**: nagano-be 292 da, 19 kan 972.8 monme; nakawari 2,218 da, 186 kan 093 monme; together 206 kan 065.8. Less the costs above, 178 kan 849.1, and the carriage works-to-Osaka and sundries, 17 kan 570: 196 kan 419.1. Remaining profit 9 kan 646.7 monme.

Per-da costing of nakawari:
「〆五拾壱匁三分三厘 此正味七歩七拾三匁三分余 外ニ七匁山大坂迄入用 〆八拾目三分ニ而大坂着 但山山崎迄太ちん四匁五分ノ所ニ而候」
(translation) Total 51.33 monme; at a 70% net yield, 73.3 monme and more; plus 7 monme of costs from the works to Osaka: **80.3 monme landed at Osaka**. This is where the pack-horse hire from the works to Yamasaki is 4.5 monme.

- (a) Nakawari: **works cost 72.74 monme a da against an Osaka sale estimate of 83.90** (186,093 / 2,218). Nagano-be: **60 against 68.40** (19,972.8 / 292).
- **The da here is 32 kan**, from the document's own figures: 3,168 × 22.4 kan / 2,218 da = 32.0, and 312 × 30 / 292 = 32.05. That is 120 kg, so the prices are about **2.27 against 2.62 g of silver per kg** (arithmetic).
- (b) Osaka/works = **1.15 and 1.14** (computed). The source gives no ratio, but states the margin: 9.6 kan of profit on 206 kan of sales, about 4.7%.
- (c) By pack horse from the works to Yamasaki (4.5 monme a da), then on to Osaka (7 monme a da in all). By this pass's estimate the distance is about 100 km.
- (d) Freight of 7 monme a da, about 9.6% of the works cost. Also a levy, 「御運上弐百枚」, and the operator's margin.
- The works figure is a production COST (出来口) in a prospectus, not a price at which iron changed hands at the works. The commentators give pig iron at the furnace as 31.6 monme a da (銑一駄当り三一匁六分).

## Reading, with verdicts

**RAW SILK: ATTESTED.** In 1818-49 Kyoto's raw-silk price stood at 0.98-1.43 times the Shindatsu (Fukushima) district's, mostly 1.20-1.43, by Yamazaki's index as reprinted by Wulan Qiqige (2014). Against Maebashi, the Joshu district's central market, Kyoto was 1.01-1.28 times as high (computed, mostly 1.03-1.15). For example, 1836: 6.03 against 5.84 ryo a kan, about 362 against 351 g of silver per kg. Route and gap are not given.

**SUGAR (Satsuma black sugar): ATTESTED ONLY FOR the rice-exchange terms.**
- The islanders were paid in rice: 3.6 go a kin (1747); 3 go (1762, and an Ox year before 1810); 3.75 go regular and 4.75 go extra purchase (Kikaijima, undated); 4 go (Narinobu); 5.07 kin a sho, about 1.97 go a kin (Tenpo).
- In 1830 the islanders needed **4.2-6.4 times the Osaka-rate quantity of sugar** to buy a koku of rice (source-stated, Kagoshima Prefecture after Matsushita 1983).
- Valuing the Tenpo rate at the "average" prices one paper gives (rice 0.663 monme a sho, sugar 1.175 monme a kin) makes about 0.82 against 7.3 g of silver per kg, **a ratio of about 9** (arithmetic). The paper itself says 6.2, unexplained.
- Gap items: freight in kind of about 10-17.5% (Amami, 1745) and 23.6% (Ryukyu, 1807); an Osaka commission of 2 rin a kin; the monopoly margin.
- **Sanuki/Awa white sugar: SILENT.**

**IRON: ATTESTED ONLY FOR Harima (Shisō), 1736-41, as works COST against an Osaka SALE ESTIMATE in one operator's prospectus.** Split iron 72.7 against 83.9 monme a da of 32 kan (1.15); long bar 60 against 68.4 (1.14). Carriage was 7 monme a da, by pack horse to Yamasaki and on. Izumo and Iwami are attested at the producer end only (pig iron about 38-40 monme a da, 1830-35), and the Tetsuza years are described without figures.

**TEA: SILENT** for any pair. Only one end each: the shogunate's administered Uji price (78, 52 and 26 monme a kin from 1642) and Edo sencha retail in 1842 (88-500 mon per 200-me kin).

**Neighbors: safflower ATTESTED ONLY FOR the shipper's cost (mostly landed cost, purchase plus freight; 1747 the bare purchase cost) against the Kyoto sale.** Murayama purchase or landed cost against Kyoto sale was 0.92-1.33 by year (1737-63), and a 1822 lot was asked at at least 12.4% over Kyoto-landed cost. Commission was 1-3%, plus a 15% discount in 1738. **Rice ATTESTED:** Osaka/Fukui about 1.1-1.25 in the 1780s-1830s.

Every ratio is premodern (1736-1849).
