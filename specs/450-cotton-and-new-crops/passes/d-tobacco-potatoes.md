## Sources and passages

### Tobacco

**1. China.** Laufer, [Tobacco and Its Use in Asia (1924)](https://archive.org/stream/tobaccoitsuseina18lauf/tobaccoitsuseina18lauf_djvu.txt), giving Ming and Qing authors in his English. The Chinese originals of Zhang Jiebin and the unnamed writer were not read.
- Zhang: "only recently, during the period Wan-li (1573-1620) of our Ming dynasty, it was cultivated in Fu-kien and Kwang- tung"; "Every one in the south-west, old and young without exception, is at present addicted to smoking by day and night."
- "An imperial edict issued in 1638 prohibited the use of tobacco"; "in the closing years of the period Tsung-cheng (1628-43) there was hardly a boy three feet tall who did not smoke tobacco".
- Later witnesses, listed with their dates:
  - Staunton, on the 1793 embassy north of Beijing (archive bc/bc6b5f14789d): "In the low grounds in this part of the country great quantities of tobacco are planted."
  - Doolittle, Fuzhou, 1860s (archive ce/ce3bd5278564): guests are "invited to smoke a whiff of tobacco".
- A conflicting source: [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/102960940.html) (archive b2/b2cf4c6fe286), a modern account of Wanli prices: 「沈榜还列举了其他若干烟草价格，从每斤3分白银的本土烟草，到每斤1钱白银的吕宋进口烟草。这恰好成了最早的中国烟草消费记录。」 "Shen Bang also lists several other tobacco prices, from domestic tobacco at 3 fen of silver per jin to imported Luzon tobacco at 1 qian per jin. This happens to be the earliest record of tobacco consumption in China." (translation) I could not check this against Shen Bang's own text.

**2. Japan.**
- Richard Cocks, 7 August 1615, in Laufer: "It is strange to see how these Japons, men, women, and children, are besotted in drinking that herb ; and, not ten years since it was in use first."
- Laufer: "tobacco-leaves were first traded to the country by the Portuguese (Namban) toward the close of the six- teenth century. About 1605 the first tobacco planta- tions were established at Nagasaki"; "in the latter part of the seventeenth century the cultivation was practised on an extensive scale."
- [ja.wikipedia タバコ](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/タバコ) (tertiary): 「慶長10年（1605年）には長崎の桜馬場で、初めてタバコの種が日本に植えられた」 "In Keichō 10 (1605) tobacco seed was first planted in Japan, at Sakurababa in Nagasaki." (translation) The same page gives Keichō 6 (1601) for seed presented at Hirado, and lists 国分 and 水府 as traditional growing areas without dates.
- Bans: [Nazology](https://nazology.kusuguru.co.jp/archives/130098/2), popular: 「1609年に徳川幕府は喫煙の禁制令を出し…その後1612年にはたばこを売買する事や栽培することも禁止されたのです。」 "In 1609 the shogunate banned smoking ... then in 1612 trading and growing tobacco were banned too." (translation)
- [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/prohibitions): "were all lifted in 1625. By 1640, tobacco accompanied the tea ceremony and was part of daily life."
- [Tabako to Shio Museum](https://tabashio.jp/en/collection/tobacco/japan/index.html): "firmly established as one of the most popular consumer luxuries throughout all social levels".

**3. Korea.** The plant is identified by name and by smoking use, and no other plant is in question.
- [Jibong yuseol, 1614](https://ko.wikisource.org/wiki/번역:지봉유설/19권): 「淡婆姑草名。亦號南靈草。近歲始出倭國。…今人多種之。」 "Dampago is the name of a plant, also called namnyeongcho. In recent years it first came from Japan. ... Many people now plant it." (translation)
- Jeong Kee-Taeg, [2015](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201507740043433.pdf), quoting originals (read from page images). He reckons arrival at 1611-12.
  - Sokjabrok, 1622: 「四五年前，厥種來自日本」; 「南方人取以植之致富者多」 "Four or five years ago its seed came from Japan"; "many southerners who plant it have grown rich." (translation)
  - Gyegok-jip, 1635: 「我國自二十年前始有之」; 「今則上自公卿下至輿臺牧豎。無不服之」 "Our country has had it for twenty years"; "now from high ministers down to bearers and herdboys, all use it." (translation)
  - Sillok, 1638: 「越海來，人有服之者，而不至於盛行」; 「辛酉、壬戌以來，無人不服」 "It came across the sea [1616-17]; some used it, but it was not widespread"; "since 1621-22 no one does not use it." (translation)
- Hamel, as Laufer summarizes him (Hamel's own text not read): "In Hamel's time smoking was general and indulged in by both sexes, even by four or five year old children."
- The route and date are disputed. The [National Archives page](https://theme.archives.go.kr/next/koreaOfRecord/domesticSmoke.do) gives the Imjin War as the usual view, and says the Sillok's first mention of tobacco is a 1623 fire at the Dongnae wakan.

**4. India** (all from Laufer, except the Habib-based module).
- Asad Beg, 1605: "In Bijapur I had found some tobacco. Never having seen the like in India".
- Terry, 1616-19: "They sow tobacco in abundance, but know not how to cure and make it strong".
- Laufer: "smoking became general in India, and English invoices of date 1619 list tobacco as being shipped from India to Red Sea ports."
- Jahangir, 1617: "I ordered that no one should practise the habit."
- [A course module drawing on Habib](https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/socp6/?p=36): "its cultivation had begun on the western coast soon after 1600; but by 1650, it was being cultivated in almost all parts of the Mughal empire".

### Sweet potato

**5. China.** Xu Guangqi's own contrast identifies the plant, separating it from the native 山薯 (a *Dioscorea* yam).
- [農政全書 卷27](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/農政全書/卷27): 「薯有二種：其一，名山薯，閩、廣故有之；其一，名番薯，則土人傳云：近年有人，在海外得此種。」; 「山薯植援附樹乃生；番薯蔓地生。山薯，形魁壘，番薯，形圓而長。其味，則番薯甚甘」 "There are two kinds of shu: one, called shan-shu, Fujian and Guangdong have long had; the other, called fan-shu, the locals say someone recently obtained overseas."; "Shan-shu grows climbing on trees; fan-shu spreads along the ground. Shan-shu is lumpy, fan-shu round and long. In taste, fan-shu is very sweet." (translation) The treatise on it is dated 1608 by [zh.wikipedia 徐光啟](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hant/徐光啟).
- [廣東新語 卷27](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/廣東新語/卷27), Qu Dajun, early Qing: 「番薯近自呂宋來，植最易」; 「稱薯飯，為穀米之佐」 "Fan-shu recently came from Luzon and is very easy to plant"; "called shu-rice, a supplement to grain." (translation; the second phrase covers the 薯 group, which includes 蕃薯.)
- Dates:
  - [zh.wikipedia 番薯](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/番薯): 「万历二十一年（1593年）福州长乐县华侨陈振龙将番薯从菲律宾带回中国」 "In Wanli 21 (1593) Chen Zhenlong of Changle, Fuzhou, brought the sweet potato back from the Philippines." (translation)
  - [Pwsannong](https://www.pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/560776.shtml), a modern article on Guangdong: 「东莞小捷，万历十年传人番薯」; 「番薯在清初已成为仅次于大米的杂粮作物」 "Xiaojie in Dongguan received the sweet potato in Wanli 10 [1582]"; "by the early Qing it had become the coarse grain second only to rice." (translation)
  - Ho Ping-ti, only through [a food-history summary](https://www.flavorandfortune.com/ffdataaccess/article.php?ID=843): "he documents them in 1563 when they were called the 'red tuber.'" Ho's identification could not be checked.
- Common use, from modern scholarship on Qing records:
  - [Pan Honggang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/22698.html), citing memorials of 1725 and 1728: 「乾隆以前，番薯主要产于广东和福建两地，并成为下层百姓日常食物」 "Before Qianlong, sweet potato was grown mainly in Guangdong and Fujian and had become the daily food of the lower classes." (translation)
  - [Li Xinsheng 2023](https://www.cssn.cn/skgz/bwyc/202308/t20230818_5679810.shtml): 「从18世纪中期到19世纪中期，是番薯在南方山区推广最快的阶段」; 「北方的番薯推广要更晚，至清末民国时期才有较大发展」 "From the mid-18th to mid-19th century it spread fastest in the southern mountains"; "in the north it came later, developing substantially only in the late Qing and Republic." (translation)
  - [Li Xinsheng, recent article](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/177018.html): 「清代大部分时间…玉米栽培面积游荡在3%以下，番薯徘徊在1%以下，马铃薯更是可以忽略不计」 "For most of the Qing ... maize stayed under 3% of cultivated area, sweet potato under 1%, and potato was negligible." (translation)
  - Doolittle, Fuzhou, 1860s (archive): "Rice, of which there are several varieties, wheat, and sweet potatoes, are the most common crops."

**6. Japan.** 甘藷, 唐芋, 琉球芋 and 蕃薯 all mean *Ipomoea* here. The page gives the binomial and the Luzon-Ryukyu provenance. It reports, second-hand, that Kaibara's 1708 *Yamato honzō* divided 蕃薯 from 甘薯.
- [ja.wikipedia サツマイモ](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/サツマイモ) (tertiary, flagged by its own editors as under-sourced):
  - 「17世紀初めに中国から琉球にもたらされ」 "brought from China to Ryukyu in the early 17th century" (translation)
  - On Cocks at Hirado, 1615: 「コックスはこれを「琉球芋」と記している」 "Cocks recorded it as 'Ryukyu imo.'" (translation)
  - The note on 1698: 「元禄11年（1698年）…種子島久基が…琉球国王尚貞より甘藷一篭を譲り受け」 "In Genroku 11 (1698) Tanegashima Hisamoto received a basket of sweet potatoes from the Ryukyu king." (translation)
  - On 1732: 「サツマイモを栽培していた伊予国大三島の周辺では餓死者が全く出ず」 "Around Ōmishima in Iyo, where sweet potatoes were grown, no one starved." (translation)
  - On 1735: 「1735年、蘭学者の青木昆陽が薩摩から江戸に種芋を取り寄せて」 "In 1735 Aoki Kon'yō had seed tubers sent from Satsuma to Edo." (translation)
  - On 1773: 「この時点で江戸では芋が庶民にも親しまれ」 "by this time potatoes were familiar to Edo's commoners." (translation)
- [MAFF](https://www.maff.go.jp/j/kids/agri/satuma/s01.html): 「1600年ごろ、中国から日本にやってきました」 "It came to Japan from China about 1600." (translation)
- [Saitama Prefecture](https://www.pref.saitama.lg.jp/a0108/908-20091216-188.html) (archive), on the Santome settlers: 「寛延4年（1751）サツマイモがもたらされ、文化年間（1804～17）にはその生産が拡大し」 "Sweet potatoes were brought in 1751, and production expanded in the Bunka era." (translation)
- The name 野國總管 is on the Wikipedia page without a date. The year 1605 appeared only in a search snippet.

**7. Korea.** 감저 (甘藷) = 고구마 = *Ipomoea batatas*, which [Encykorea 고구마](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0003335) states with the binomial.
- 「1763년 조엄(趙曮)이 일본에 통신사로 가던 중 대마도(對馬島)에 들러 그 종자를 얻어 동래와 제주도에서 시험삼아 심게 한 것이 처음이다.」 "The first was in 1763, when Jo Eom, on embassy to Japan, obtained seed at Tsushima and had it trial-planted at Dongnae and Jeju." (translation)
- 「그러나 고구마가 전국적으로 재배되게 된 것은 1900년대 이후이다.」 "But sweet potato came to be grown nationwide only after the 1900s." (translation) The same page reports the 1663 castaway account from the Sillok.
- Against the 1763 date, [Encykorea 서경창](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0027593): 「선조에서 광해군 때에 걸쳐 도입된 것으로 추정되며, 1633년(인조 11) 비변사에서 고구마 보급에 노력한」 "estimated to have been introduced between Seonjo and Gwanghaegun; in 1633 the Border Defense Council worked to spread it." (translation)
- [Encykorea 김장순](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0010324) gives 1765 for Kang Pil-li's successful crop. Its "영조 31" should be 영조 41.

**8. India.**
- [Hobson-Jobson, SWEET POTATO](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Hobson-Jobson/S), late 19th c.: "a very palatable vegetable, grown in most parts of India"; "natives commonly call it shakar-ḳand". ### White potato

**9. China.** Laufer's *American Plant Migration*, in [a Chinese translated excerpt](https://www.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1779527473169840.html); his English was unobtainable.
- 「史初一（John Struys）…1650年到了台湾，并在该岛的产品中提到了马铃薯」 "Struys ... came to Taiwan in 1650 and mentions potatoes among its products." (translation)
- 「马铃薯，菜依树生，掘取之，形有小大，略如铃子，色黑而圆，味苦甘。」 "Ma-ling-shu: a vegetable growing on trees; dug up, it varies in size, somewhat like a bell, black and round, bitter-sweet." (translation; Songxi gazetteer, 1700.) My caution: a plant that "grows on trees" with bitter black tubers fits *Solanum* badly. Laufer accepted it, so 1700 should not stand as a secure first record.
- On 1848 (吳其濬): the plant is called 阳芋, colloquially 山药蛋, grown in Guizhou and Yunnan, 「在山西被作为田间作物种植」 "in Shanxi grown as a field crop"; it is 「穷人的储藏食物」 "a stored food of the poor." (translation; 吳's own text was not read.) The botanical description and drawing make the identification.
- 「马铃薯从未深刻影响过中国的农业经济…不是国史，而仅仅是地方史」 "It never deeply affected China's farm economy ... not national history, only local history." (translation)
- Rockhill's *Land of the Lamas* (archive 69/69e0e5a49b28), late 19th c.: "In the northern part of the province [Shan-hsi] the people live on white potatoes and oats".
- Li Xinsheng's "negligible" (under entry 5) applies here too.

**10. Japan.** ジャガタラ芋 and 馬鈴薯 mean *Solanum*.
- [ja.wikipedia ジャガイモ](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ジャガイモ):
  - 「諸説あるが、1598年にオランダ人によって持ち込まれたとされる」 "Accounts differ, but it is said Dutch people brought it in 1598." (translation)
  - 「享和元年（1801年）には小野蘭山が甲斐国黒平村…においてジャガイモの栽培を記録している」 "In 1801 Ono Ranzan recorded potato growing at Kurobera in Kai." (translation)
  - 「本格的に導入されたのは明治維新後で、北海道の開拓に利用された。」 "It was introduced in earnest after the Meiji Restoration, for the development of Hokkaido." (translation)
  - The page also lists 1706 at Setana, 1748 in Hida, and Russian influence in the late 18th c.
- [A Chinese article](https://www.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1787267359019245.html) gives 「荷兰人于1601年把土豆运到日本长崎」 "the Dutch carried potatoes to Nagasaki in 1601." (translation)

**11. Korea.** 북저 / 북감저 / 토감저 = *Solanum tuberosum* ([Encykorea 감자](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0000815)). These names are kept apart from 감저 = *Ipomoea*, even though modern 감자 now means the white potato.
- 「오주연문장전산고(五洲衍文長箋散稿)』에는 1824년과 1825년 사이에 관북에서 처음 들어왔다고 되어 있다.」 "The Oju yeonmun says it first came in through Gwanbuk between 1824 and 1825." (translation)
- The same article gives 1832, a British ship and a missionary teaching cultivation, and 「수십 년 사이에 각처에 보급」 "spread everywhere within a few decades." (translation)
- [Encykorea 감자밥](https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0000820): 「1847년(헌종 13)에는 전라·경기·충청·강원지방에까지 보급되어…고구마를 능가할 정도였다」 "By 1847 it had reached Jeolla, Gyeonggi, Chungcheong and Gangwon, to the point of surpassing the sweet potato." (translation)
- 1890s travel account, Gutenberg 69300 (archive af/afb75ffbad3a): "the potato is more extensively grown".

**12. India.**
- [Fryer, 1673, in Hobson-Jobson](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Hobson-Jobson/B): "The Garden ... planted with Potatoes, Yawms, Berenjaws, both hot plants". This separates potato from yam, but not from sweet potato.
- [A horticulture lecture](https://eagriorg.github.io/eagri50/HORT281/lec22.html): "The first mention of potato in India occurs in 'Terry's account of a banquet at Ajmer given by Asaph Khan to Sir Thomas Rao in 1615. Potato was grown in many gardens of Surat and Karnataka in about 1675."
- Achaya (blog above) disputes both: "it is doubtful whether the potato mentioned ... was really the potato at all"; "the identity of the 'basket of potatoes' ... to Warren Hastings around AD 1780, is not in doubt".
- [Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/potato-history-india): "very few Indians grew or ate potatoes"; "the potato never displaced rice as India's staple crop."
- [PotatoPro](https://www.potatopro.com/node/136): "By the late 18th and early 19th centuries, potato had become an established vegetable crop in both the hills and plains of India."

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** tobacco in all four countries, the sweet potato in China, Japan and Korea, and the white potato in Japan, Korea and China, the last at province level only. India is split: the white potato is firm only from 1780, and the sweet potato is SILENT on first arrival, first cultivation and when it became common.

Tobacco spread fastest by far, within about 10 years in Japan and Korea and about 30-50 in China and India. The sweet potato took more than a century to become a poor people's food, and even then only regionally. The white potato stayed marginal almost everywhere before the 19th century, Korea being the exception at about 22 years.

Much of the dating is tertiary or modern (Wikipedia, popular articles, Encykorea, Laufer in translation). The strongest primary evidence is Korean tobacco, Cocks's 1615 diary entry for Japan, and Xu Guangqi on the sweet potato. Modern scholarship on premodern records counts toward these readings. Buck's 1920s farm survey and the Meiji gazetteer (archive 78/78ee895d1916), which lists 芋, 甘藷 and 馬鈴薯 as three separate crops, fall outside the window and do not count.
