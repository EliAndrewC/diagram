# Pass A: cotton in Japan (research-pass, 2026-11-08; leads until read on the page)

**Sources, by what they give** (Ishiyama's column layout was reassembled; ︑︒︵︶﹁﹂ are rendered 、。（）「」)

**(1) First record of the plant or cloth**
- Kotobank, 綿織物 (小笠原小枝, 世界大百科), https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%B6%BF%E7%B9%94%E7%89%A9-141907. Evidence: Nara, 799.
  「それ以前，古く奈良時代にも木綿が舶載されていたことは，正倉院に伝わる2，3の木綿の断片によって知られ，また平安時代初期の799年（延暦18）には崑崙人が三河に漂着して，その種子を伝えたと《日本後紀》に見える。しかしその種子はほどなく絶えたものらしく，平安期を通じて木綿の資料は残っていない。」 (translation) Cotton was already shipped in during the Nara period, as two or three fragments in the Shosoin show. In 799 a Kunlun man drifted ashore in Mikawa and passed on the seed, says the Nihon Koki. The seed seems to have died out soon, and no material on cotton survives through the Heian period.
- 日本大百科 / 精選版, Kotobank 木綿, https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9C%A8%E7%B6%BF-142758. The dictionary's first use of the word:
  「布座申分は、もんめん者衣服類也。布と同篇也」(大乗院寺社雑事記, 応仁二年 1468) (translation) The cloth guild says cotton is a kind of clothing, of a piece with [hemp] cloth.
- Kawakatsu Heita, Japan Spotlight, Nov/Dec 2005, read as Wayback copy https://web.archive.org/web/20231117042330/https://www.jef.or.jp/journal/pdf/special%20article_0511.pdf.
  "An authentic record compiled in 841 stated that cotton seeds were first brought into Japan by a Malay who came from Sumatra to Japan in AD 799. Another record compiled in 892 described the method of cotton cultivation and its geographical distribution in the country. This cotton ... had died out by the Kamakura period (1192-1333)."
- ja.wikipedia 木綿, https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/木綿: 「栽培が開始されたが、1年で途切れたという」 (translation) Cultivation began but is said to have broken off after a year.

The dates of the die-out conflict: "soon" and "after a year" against "by Kamakura". I did not read the 日本後紀 text.

**(2) Cloth arriving mainly as an import**
- Kotobank 木綿 (佐々木銀弥, 世界大百科):
  「鎌倉時代以降には日宋・日元貿易における重要輸入品とされ，15世紀室町時代には日朝貿易における朝鮮側の重要回賜品となった。16世紀の天文年間（1532-55）には日明密貿易による唐木綿の輸入が増え，朝鮮産木綿の輸入にとって代わった。」 (translation) From Kamakura on it was an important import in the Song and Yuan trade. In the 15th century it was an important Korean return gift in the Japan-Korea trade. In the Tenbun years, 1532-55, Tang cotton smuggled from Ming grew and replaced the Korean import.
- 綿織物 (小笠原): 「最も早くは朝鮮からもたらされたもので…世宗（在位1418-50）の時代には，日本との貿易品としても木綿が最も多くなり…成宗（在位1469-94）の時代まで続いている。」 (translation) It came earliest from Korea. Under Sejong cotton became the largest item in the trade with Japan, and this lasted to Seongjong's reign.
- Kawakatsu, Table 2, counts of pieces of Korean cotton cloth exported to Japan: 1418 1,539; 1423 2,640; 1475 27,208; 1476 37,421; 1486 500,000; 1488 100,000; 1525 85,000; 1542 60,000. The source text says the exports "soon reached 500,000 pieces annually"; the table shows 500,000 in one year only.
  "Korea prohibited commerce with Japan in 1523 ... The cotton cloth from China flooded into Japan in the 1570s, exceeding the amount of Korean products. The trade ... stopped in 1592 ... and did not resume until 1609."
- Tanimoto (archive key tanimoto-cotton-peasant-economy; the paper is marked not to be quoted, and the record cites it in 0312 and 0313):
  "It was around 1400 that the cotton goods began to prevail in Japanese society. The provision of the cotton goods in those days, however, was totally dependent on the import from Korea and China, and the consumption of them was limited to the upper class as well as the special use such as the battle dress for soldiers."

Korea to China timing differs: Tenbun (1532-55) against the 1570s.

**(3) First cultivation on any scale**
- Yajima Iwao 2017, summarising Nagahara 2004 (Nagahara read only through this), https://www.human.kobegakuin.ac.jp/yajisoffice/papers_presentation/20170320kogawamenka.pdf:
  「永原は、日本各地における綿作の開始時期に関する史料を整理し、15 世紀末～ 16 世紀初頭にかけて、寒冷な東北地方などを除いて、ほとんど全国的に綿作と綿業が広がったとしちる（2004、pp.323-324）。」 (translation) Nagahara sorted the records of when cotton growing began in each region, and holds that from the end of the 15th to the start of the 16th century cotton growing and the cotton trade spread almost nationwide except the cold Tohoku and the like.
  The same paper also gives 「16 世紀の戦国時代織豊期のうちに、東北地方を除く各地で」 (translation) within the 16th-century Sengoku and Shokuho period, everywhere except Tohoku.
- Sasaki (Kotobank 木綿): 「1494年（明応3）のころ，越後上杉家領内には〈ミわた〉が現れ」 (translation) Around 1494 "miwata" (raw cotton) appears in the Uesugi lands of Echigo.
- 三河木綿 (瀬田勝哉, 世界大百科), https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%89%E6%B2%B3%E6%9C%A8%E7%B6%BF-637305: 「700年後の1510年（永正7）には奈良で三河木綿の名が見えるのをはじめ，京都の貴族にも贈答品とされる」 (translation) In 1510 the name Mikawa cotton appears in Nara, and it was a gift among Kyoto nobles.
- 堀部徹哉, a local historian, 2024, https://newswitch.jp/p/40552: 「『永正年中記』の永正７年…の条には、税として「三川木綿」を徴収したことが記されており、これが文献上にみる最古の国産綿織物とされている。」 (translation) The Eisho nenchuki for Eisho 7 records "Mikawa cotton" collected as tax, held to be the oldest domestic cotton cloth in the documents.
- Conflict on 1510: Kawakatsu has it "sold in Nara from around 1510s"; 堀部 has it levied as tax. I did not read the original.
- Matsue museum booklet, https://www.city.matsue.lg.jp/material/files/group/32/history_mono_6.pdf: 「15世紀末には日本国内で生産が開始されました」 (translation) Domestic production began by the end of the 15th century.

**(4) A regular commercial crop**
- Sasaki: 「16世紀には三河木綿商人が伊勢と近江を結ぶ千草街道などを通って，木綿を畿内地方に搬送・売却していた。戦国末期には関東から太平洋・瀬戸内海沿岸地方一帯に栽培が普及し，とくに大和・摂津・河内・和泉・播磨などが特産地として著名となった。」 (translation) In the 16th century Mikawa cotton merchants carried cotton to the Kinai by the Chigusa road and sold it. By late Sengoku cultivation had spread from Kanto along the Pacific and Inland Sea coasts, with Yamato, Settsu, Kawachi, Izumi and Harima famous.
- Kawakatsu: "In the 1570s, Musashi ... had local markets for cotton textiles ... There is a report that cotton was grown in Yamato ... in 1591 ... Osaka is mentioned for the first time as a cotton region in a record of 1623 ... In 1658 another guild was formed to monopolize sales of raw cotton for export to the northern Japan." And: "The cotton products brought into Osaka in 1735 consisted of cotton cloth (74%), cotton yarn (16%) and ginned and raw cotton (10%)."
- Yajima/Nagahara: 「木綿作＝実綿→繰綿→綛糸→織布という綿業の工程の分化が、とくに摂津・河内・和泉で、遅くとも寛永頃（1620-29 年）までに成立」; 「三河・尾張・伊勢を除く東海と関東における綿の商品栽培が全国的な比重を低下させた。代わって、畿内とその周辺地域において綿作と綿織の主産地が集中」 (translation) The division of the stages of the cotton trade was established, especially in Settsu, Kawachi and Izumi, by the Kan'ei period at the latest. In the 17th century commercial growing in Tokai and Kanto, Mikawa, Owari and Ise excepted, lost weight, and the main centers concentrated in the Kinai and around it.
- 河内木綿, https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B2%B3%E5%86%85%E6%9C%A8%E7%B6%BF-468756:
  - 山川: 「綿布は大永・享禄年間(1521～32)に作られ始めたともいわれるが，本格的発展は元禄年間(1688～1704)とみられる。…1830年(天保元)頃年産300万反に達したという」 (translation) Cloth is said to have begun in 1521-32, but its real growth is placed in the Genroku period. By about 1830 output reached a reported 3 million tan a year.
  - マイペディア: 「天保期…には河内国の全耕地の46％余に及んだという試算がある」 (translation) There is an estimate that in Tenpo it reached over 46% of all Kawachi's cultivated land. This is a land share, and the sentence's subject is implied, not stated.
  - 《毛吹草》, 1638, names 久宝寺木綿.
- Later regions:
  - Hoki, Shimomukai (archived, 5a/5a93e9475270): 「綿作は明和・安永期以降、木綿織は天明期以降急速に普及していき…文政年間には約五〇万反から八〇万反」 (translation) Cotton growing from Meiwa-An'ei on (1764-81), weaving from Tenmei on, about 500-800 thousand tan in Bunsei.
  - Matsuyama, Ehime archive 0d/0defb294cc6e: exports of about 7,000 tan in 1804, 86,000 in 1808, about 100,000 in 1812.

**(5) Normal clothing of commoners**
- Yajima/Nagahara: 「1628（寛永 5）年の「百姓着物之事」という幕府の定書に、一般百姓の着物は麻・木綿と定められていることから、すでに木綿が庶民の日常的な衣料として入手しやすい存在となっていた（永原 2004、pp.301-304）。」 (translation) Since the 1628 shogunal order sets ordinary peasants' clothes as hemp and cotton, cotton had already become readily available as everyday commoner clothing.
- Kashimo booklet (Tokugawa Rinseishi, archive c4/c47e027b4ab9), on the same law, citing 徳川禁令考: 「百姓の衣服は木綿と麻のみ、ただし名主と百姓の女房は紬…まで着用しても構わない」 (translation) Peasants' clothes only cotton and hemp, though headmen and peasant wives could go as far as pongee.
- 慶安御触書 (ja.wikipedia, archive 16/1609f529296e): 「農民達は、麻と木綿のほかは着てはいけない。帯や裏地にも使ってはならない。」 (translation) Peasants may wear nothing but hemp and cotton, not even for sashes or linings. The page says clauses vary by text. The ledger shows earlier sessions on its authenticity, which I did not re-read.
- William Adams, Keicho (1596-1615), quoted in 綿織物: 「当国には木綿多きがゆえに金巾およびカンバイヤ織物の需要なし」 (translation) Because this country has much cotton there is no demand for calico or Cambay cloth.
- Kawakatsu, quoting Ono Koji: "because the Japanese had hemp for summer clothing, cottons were initially employed for winter clothing; they were very thick."
- 綿織物: 「江戸時代以降には完全に従来の麻を圧して，庶民の衣料として定着」 (translation) From the Edo period it wholly overcame hemp and settled as commoners' clothing.
- Against that, Matsue: 「江戸時代後期になり木綿が庶民衣料の主流となると」 (translation) When in the later Edo period cotton became the mainstream of commoner clothing.
- Nagashima 2017 (archive cf/cf56a25e8e58; record 0312), on the 清良記's Iyo farm wife: 「冬は正月の着物のための木綿を織り」 (translation) In winter she wove cotton for New Year clothes. Nagashima dates the text to late Sengoku.
- 慶長見聞集 1614, dictionary clipping only (Kotobank 木綿): 「諸人のはかまもめん也。今の時代はあさなり」 (translation) Everyone's hakama are cotton; the present age is hemp. It has no context, and I do not assert which way it runs.

**What it replaced, and how fast**
- Tanimoto: "changed the contents of peoples' clothing having been based on the ramie or hemp"; silk "limited to the ruling class or wealthy upper classes"; "According to the estimation by Keiji Nagahara, the labour input for producing the cloth from raw ramie per tan ... reached nearly ten times more than that of cotton cloth."
- Nagashima: 「それまで中世社会の人々の衣料の主力は苧麻であり」 (translation) Until then ramie was the mainstay of medieval people's clothing.
- Kotobank's 越中布 note (archive 1f/1f93415f5d98; record 0274): 「中世末に木綿が入ってくるまでは衣料は麻が普通であった」 (translation) Until cotton came at the end of the Middle Ages, hemp was the usual clothing.
- No source gives kudzu's share.

**Region**
- Ishiyama Akiko, 弘前藩領における木綿・古手類の移入および流通について, 弘前大学國史研究 144 (year not on the pages read), Wayback copy https://web.archive.org/web/20250918060503/https://hirosaki.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/6322/files/HirodaiKokushi_144_37.pdf:
  - 「寒冷地域ゆえ木綿が自給できない弘前藩領においては、木綿は他地域からの移入に依存せざるを得ず、一般民衆とくに農民の衣料としては麻が主体であった」 (translation) In Hirosaki, too cold to grow its own, cotton had to come from elsewhere, and common people's, especially peasants', clothing was mainly hemp.
  - 古川古松軒, 東遊雑記, 1788, as she quotes it: 「中より下の百姓は綿入れの衣は貯え着ること聞かず、麻布を刺子…として寒中は三枚もかさねて着てこと足りぬといえり。中以上の人は、大坂より廻る古着を買い求めて着るなり。」 (translation) Peasants of middling rank and below are not heard to own padded clothes. They quilt hemp cloth and wear as many as three layers in the cold. Those above middling buy old clothes shipped from Osaka and wear them.
  - 「当時の一般農民は年間を通して麻でできた丈の短いこぎんに麻の股引を着用するというのが基本的な服装であったが、寒い時期には、綿入や木綿の肌着などを着用」 (translation) The ordinary peasant's basic dress all year was a short hemp kogin and hemp leggings, with padded or cotton underclothes in the cold (domain diary cases, 1757-61).
  - Conclusion: 「十八世紀末頃には、下層農民を除くほとんどの一般領民が、少なくとも古手のかたちで木綿衣料を入手し得たと考えられる。」 (translation) By about the late 18th century nearly all ordinary people but the lowest peasants could get cotton clothing at least as old clothes.
- Tanimoto, Akita 1808-10 (also in 0313's notes): "there was no cotton cultivation, nor cotton weaving for market in this region"; "more than half of the population should have procured their clothing other than purchasing cotton cloth".
- Aomori prefecture, https://www.pref.aomori.lg.jp/soshiki/sangyo/chikikigyo/aomori_dento-kogei_koginzashi.html: 「江戸時代、津軽の農民は木綿の衣料を着ることが許されていなかった。」 (translation) In the Edo period Tsugaru peasants were not permitted to wear cotton. Ishiyama's edict series below contradicts this as a flat statement.
- 古着 elsewhere: Kashimo in Mino, a cold mountain village, 「綿花は気候が合わず十分な収穫を得ることができませんでした」 (translation) cotton did not suit the climate, so villagers bought cotton from merchants (1772 entry).
- Tanimoto: Nara-region ginned cotton distributed to "Kanto and Tohoku ... already in the 17th century."
- 長岡 nothing on the Ezo margin was found.

**Class**
- Early: Sasaki: 「当初は武家など支配階級の奢侈的衣料とされていたが，戦国時代には兵衣・陣幕・軍旗，さらには鉄砲の火縄用など軍需物資としての性格が強くなった」 (translation) At first a luxury of the warrior and ruling classes, in Sengoku it became war material: soldiers' dress, curtains, banners, matchlock cord.
- Ishiyama, Hirosaki diary, 1661: 「一、衣服之事、百石以上向後絹・紬・木綿可着之、百石より以下ハ可為木綿、此外堅停止之、惣而下々之者ハ半ゑり・帯等に至迄木綿之外可為無用事」 (translation) Clothing: those of 100 koku and above shall henceforth wear silk, pongee and cotton; those below 100 koku cotton, all else strictly forbidden; all lesser people, down to collars and sashes, nothing but cotton.
- Later samurai rules she gives: 1711, 300 koku and under with wives and children, cotton; 1807, all retainers ordered into cotton.
- Peasants in her series:
  - 1703: 「日常の農作業の際は麻布を着用することとするが、五節句など特別の場合は高価でない木綿の着用を許可」 (translation) Hemp for daily farm work; cheap cotton permitted on the five festivals.
  - 1724: cotton for the wealthy and old; others for underclothes, headgear and sashes only.
  - 1777: 「手織の木綿はよろしいが、麻布の着用が原則」 (translation) Homewoven cotton is allowed, but hemp is the rule.
  - 1790: 「男女とも麻布と木綿の両方を着用することが明確に規定され」 (translation) Hemp and cotton both clearly prescribed for men and women.
  - Townspeople: 「原則として木綿の衣服を着用」 (translation) In principle cotton.
- Morioka (西村綏子 via Ishiyama): 「高知の面々は妻女とも絹の着用が許され、中級の藩士は妻女ともに紬、下級藩士以下は布・木綿のみを許された。そして町人は木綿を着用」 (translation) Late period: high retainers silk, middling pongee, low retainers and below only hemp-type cloth and cotton; townspeople cotton.
- Matsue booklet names a striped-cotton trade: 「才賀町小倉（雑賀町の武家の内職であった縞織物）」 (translation) Saika-machi kokura, striped cloth woven as a side job by samurai households of Saika-machi.
- Popular page, undated, Edo (archive fb/fb6ae1687ad2): 「江戸庶民が着ていた木綿の着物のほとんどが「古着」であった」 (translation) Nearly all the cotton kimono Edo commoners wore were secondhand.

**Errors in the sources.** Kawakatsu dates 農業全書 to 1597 and 百姓伝記 to 1580-82 while saying they describe the late 17th century; Yajima dates 農業全書 1697. Yajima gives Kan'ei as 1624-29 and 1620-29, 綿圃要務 as 1697, and a table year as 「1763（天文元）」, none of which fit the calendar names.

**Reading.** **ATTESTED**, with the common-wear step **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the areas named below.
1. First record: fragments shipped in during the Nara period, and the text of a 799 seed landing in Mikawa that did not take.
2. Import stage: Kamakura through about the mid-1500s, from Song and Yuan, then mainly Korea in the 15th century, then Ming China from the mid-16th century.
3. First cultivation on any scale: the end of the 15th to start of the 16th century, with 1494 Echigo and 1510 Mikawa the earliest dated traces found.
4. Commercial crop: 16th century in Mikawa and Tokai, shifting to the Kinai by the 1620s-30s, with Kawachi's great growth placed in Genroku and the later spread to San'in and Setouchi after about 1760.
5. Commoners: the 1628 law and Adams point to the early 17th century for the warm regions, and the sources disagree on "early" or "late" Edo for it being the main fiber. Law permitting cotton shows it was obtainable, not who wore it.

In the north the answer is different and later. Tsugaru peasants wore hemp as the rule to 1790 and had cotton, as old clothes above all, from the late 18th century, while the poorest went without padding. Akita still shows hemp and secondhand cloth around 1810.

A cotton kimono was widespread among Kinai and Tokai townspeople and farmers in the 17th century, and among low-ranking samurai by ordinance by the 1660s in Tsugaru. Those rules are ceilings and do not show what men owned; I found nothing on low-ranking samurai before 1661 that I could read. Across the commoners of all Japan the sources point to the 18th century, with the north at its very end and into the 19th century.
