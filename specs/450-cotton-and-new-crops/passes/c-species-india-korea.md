## 1. Sources

**Species and pre-1492 presence**

- [Moulherat et al. 2002, as presented by harappa.com](https://www.harappa.com/content/first-evidence-cotton-neolithic-mehrgarh-pakistan). Mehrgarh, 6th millennium BCE; measures mineralized fibres in a copper bead.
  - "several threads preserved by mineralization in a copper bead from a Neolithic burial (6th millennium) at Mehrgarh"
  - "these methods cannot be used to identify the exact (modern) species"
  - "not possible to ascertain whether this cotton thread was derived from an already domesticated species"
- [Fuller et al. 2024, J. Arch. Sci. Reports 57](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10206456/1/Cotton%20investment%20agriculture%20%26%202%20routes%20thru%20Africa.pdf). Prehistoric to c. 18th century; a review of archaeobotanical finds.
  - "G. arboreum began to be cultivated during the Middle Holocene (7000–4000 years ago) in Pakistan and Northwest India"
  - "textile production and use of cotton had been established within the area of Baluchistan by 5000–4300 BCE, but whether this was wild cotton use or represents early cultivation is unknown. It is not until c.2500 BCE in the Harappan period that cotton, as seeds, begins to be found with any regularity ... and we can infer that cultivation was well-established"
  - "it can be seen as a key cash crop"
  - "By early historic times, in the late centuries BCE, cotton was widespread across India"
  - "annual varieties appear from the 13th century AD in China, based on written sources, and annual forms only became established in parts of India around the 18th century"
- [Zurndorfer, LSE GEHN paper](https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/GEHNConferences/conf8/PUNEZurndorfer.pdf). China, Han to 12th century, following Chao and Deng.
  - "(1) the African-Asian species (Gossypium herbaceum) which followed a northern route overland from Central Asia to China's Gansu and Shaanxi provinces ... (2) the South Asian species (Gossypium arboreum and Gossypiumhardense [sic]) which travelled a southern course overseas from India ... to Burma, Vietnam, and on to Hainan Island, Yunnan, Guangxi, and Guangdong"
  - "cotton cultivation was still restricted to Xinjiang in the mid-seventh century"
  - "into non-tropical zones of the lower Yangzi valley sometime by the twelfth century"
- [zh.wikipedia 棉花](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%A3%89%E8%8A%B1), citing 梁書.
  - 《梁书·高昌传》记载：其地有"草，实如茧，茧中丝如细纩，名为白叠子。" (translation: the Liangshu on Gaochang says a plant there has fruit like a cocoon, with silk like fine floss inside, called baidiezi.)

## 2. Reading

**ATTESTED** that cotton in Asia was not post-Columbian, and the requester's framing fails.

(a) Old World cotton is shown from the 6th-millennium BCE Mehrgarh fibres (genus only; wild or cultivated unknown), with Harappan cultivation by c. 2500 BCE. China had herbaceum in the northwest and arboreum in the south, reaching the Yangzi by the 12th century. Korea and Japan grew arboreum. Identifications changed over time: Fortune and EB 1911 use herbaceum where Hutchinson 1947 and later work put Chinese and Korean cotton under arboreum. Dates for American species are India 1788/1790s trials, with a real but local foothold at Dharwar from 1843 (New Orleans seed on 178,000 of 379,000 acres, about 47% by my arithmetic, against an all-India 2-3% to the mid-20th century per one modern essay); China from 1865 (no share found); Korea from 1904, 93% of the southern crop by 1918 and 70% nationally by 1936. For Japan I found American cotton only as an imported raw material; G. barbadense appears only among failed Indian trials.

(b) India: plant 6th millennium BCE; cultivation on any scale c. 2500 BCE; cash crop by the Mature Harappan period; exported by the 1st century CE. "Cloth arriving mainly as an import" is not a stage India had. **SILENT** on cotton as commoners' normal clothing, though the Periplus's "coarser sorts" sit nearby. Korea: Baekje fabric c. 6th century, local weaving unproven; late Goryeo imports and textual cotton; first documented cultivation 1364-67 at Jinju, spreading through the country within a decade per a 1398 obituary (retrospective, and the Han Kim article treats the Mun story's versions as varying); large state and export volumes by the 1480s; regional market specialization attested for late Joseon, no exact date for a "regular commercial crop". Commoners' cotton is attested by the 18th century and for all of the population by the 1890s.

(c) **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** cloth: Korea was a main source of cotton cloth to Japan from c. 1400 through the Sō-controlled trade, at up to 500,000+ bolts a year in the 1480s by one figure. That Korea carried the plant to Japan is not shown in what I read; the one source naming a seed route says Ming. The contract's premodern window names only Japan and China; India and Korea are left for the record's checks.
