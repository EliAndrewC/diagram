# Feature 289 - adjacent before diagonal - the GM's request

The GM, 2026-09-29, after asking why the notice boards' labels on the magistracy sheets stand catty-corner to the boards:

> Okay, so here's a question. The notice board labels on the magistracy buildings look pretty weird to me because they are catty corner to the notice boards themselves, rather than being directly next to them or right in front of them. Why are they positioned diagonally? That looks weird. I mean, they are placed unambiguously, so it is not confusing. But I am curious why the placement algorithm puts them there, because that looks strange to me. Especially considering that there is ample space, either directly in front of them or directly next to them. I do see some other things that are placed catty corner as well to the things they are labeling, while other things are placed directly next to the things that they are labeling in a manner that I would expect. I am not asking for a change to be made right now, and instead at the present time I just want to understand why the things that I am looking at were placed where they are.

The session's answer: the placer ranks a point feature's positions by the QGIS / Krygier-Wood default (upper right, upper left, lower right, lower left, right, left, above slightly right, below slightly left) and takes the first free one; the corners come first by that standard, and centered-below is not in it.

> Well, I'm not particularly inclined to contradict a textbook default, but if you're telling me that the textbook default was designed for a different type of map than the one that we are using on these diagrams, then I suppose the logical question to ask is whether there is a different standard that applies to things like architectural diagrams or building layout diagrams or things of that nature. [...] So before we go any further and make any other changes, I just want to talk a little bit more about whether we have even selected the correct standard for the kinds of labels that we are putting onto these maps, and whether we should change to a different standard if there is one. I do not want to implement a standard and then just have me myself decide to deviate from it because I think it is better to just follow the standard, but if we have picked the wrong standard, then fixing that might make sense. What do you think?

Three research readers reported (the findings are in research.md): the corner-first order is point-symbol machinery; large-scale maps put a name inside its outline (which the placer already does); architecture keys small objects to a legend; the published point orders disagree (Imhof as reported ranks right-beside ideal or second; Zoraster's 1990s models put directly-above first, and a Mapbox documentation example tries top, bottom, left, right); no source treats small drawn objects on large-scale plans as their own case.

> Yeah, so I think for a small objects, I would like to report a deviation. And say that we prefer being adjacent to an object to being diagonal to it. I think that directly above an object is best. And then directly below, then left then right. So basically what we are talking about is just keeping exactly the same system that we have now, but reordering things to match Zoroaster's 1990s oil well labeling models. and the order that they use. But other than that, we basically just do exactly what we're doing now. So like if we are able to put a label inside of a thing, then we do that in exactly the way that we are doing now. And then what I am saying only matters for smaller objects where that is not possible. And then the only change that we're making is that we're saying that the adjacency is in the order that I have described prior to the other possible placements, like the catty corner diagonal placements and whatever else it is that our existing algorithm is trying. So essentially I'm trying to change as little as possible and just documenting a deviation in the order in which we try things, and that's all. So please go ahead and implement that change start to finish and let me know when you're done and then I will take a look at how the maps look after what you have done has landed on main. Thanks.
