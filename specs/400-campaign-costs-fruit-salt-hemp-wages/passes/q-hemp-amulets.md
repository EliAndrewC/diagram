## Question 3: hemp cloth and amulet donations, against cotton and day wages

**(a) Japan, hemp cloth: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** one early-Edo lot of Etchū hemp cloth, priced by the roll. I found no price for Nara-zarashi, Echigo jōfu or Ōmi hemp cloth, no cotton price of the same place and time, and no wage.
**(a) China, hemp cloth: ATTESTED** for Beijing in 1736 with wages, and for Beijing in 1615 without a wage.
**(b) Japan, amulet donations: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** one Ise oshi's total takings over 2,162 households in 1813. That is an average, not a charge per amulet. I found no shrine or temple price notice.
**(b) China, talisman (符) fees: SILENT.**

On what the record already holds: hemp does appear beside cotton in the Beijing 1736 list, in the same table (Table 4). It does not appear in the NDL entry with the Ako prices, nor in Shimomukai's Edo 1791 table.

### Sources

**1. Gao Shouxian (高寿仙), on the Beijing official price list of 1736 and the Ming Board of Works list of 1615** (already cited in the record as gao-shouxian-jiuqing-wuliao). Archived copy: /diagram/.specify/source-archive/d7/d7234ae5c7ec/20261019T074210Z/text.txt (https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2023/02/10/u63e60647c73ae.pdf).

Beijing, 1736, prices in silver taels:
- Table 4, the hemp row: 「苎麻每斤0.1458；…川麻每斤0.17；线麻每斤0.06；苎麻线每斤0.5；麻布每匹0.216；苎布每丈0.05」
  - (translation) "ramie 0.1458 a jin; … Sichuan hemp 0.17 a jin; thread hemp 0.06 a jin; ramie thread 0.5 a jin; hemp cloth 0.216 a pi; ramie cloth 0.05 a zhang"
- Table 4, the cotton row: 「白粗布每丈0.09；…夏布（宽1.3尺）每匹0.45」
  - (translation) "coarse white [cotton] cloth 0.09 a zhang; … summer cloth [ramie] (1.3 chi wide) 0.45 a pi"
- Table 23, wages: 「木石砖瓦油画等匠 … 不论长、短给钱154文」 and 「壮夫 … 不论长、短给钱75文」
  - (translation) "carpenters, masons, brick and tile workers, painters and other craftsmen … 154 cash, long or short work alike"; "laborers … 75 cash, long or short work alike"
- The conversion to silver: 「154文折合白银0.1925两、75文折合白银0.09375两」
  - (translation) "154 cash comes to 0.1925 taels of silver, 75 cash to 0.09375 taels"
- A caveat on the pi: 「古代虽有1匹等于4丈的说法，但查看《须知》标明尺寸的丝织品，每匹长3丈6尺、3丈2尺不等，难以直接换算比对」
  - (translation) "though there was an old saying that 1 pi equals 4 zhang, the silks in the Xuzhi that state their size are 3 zhang 6 chi or 3 zhang 2 chi a pi, so they are hard to convert and compare directly"

Ming, 1615 (the Gongbu changku xuzhi), set against Qing 1736, in Table 22: 「麻布每匹0.2//0.216；苎布每丈0.1//0.05」 and 「粗白棉布//白粗布每丈 0.094 // 0.09；…腰机夏布//夏布每匹0.35//0.45」
- (translation) "hemp cloth 0.2 a pi (Ming) // 0.216 (Qing); ramie cloth 0.1 a zhang // 0.05" and "coarse white cotton cloth 0.094 a zhang // 0.09; … waist-loom summer cloth 0.35 a pi // 0.45"
- The Ming list's date: 「成书于明万历四十三年（1615）」 (translation) "completed in Wanli 43 (1615)".

The like-for-like comparison is by the zhang (neither cloth's width is given):
- **1736:** ramie cloth was 0.05 tael a zhang against 0.09 for coarse cotton, so ramie was cheaper. That is about half a laborer's day, or a quarter of a craftsman's day, for a zhang of ramie, and about one laborer's day for a zhang of coarse cotton.
- **1615:** the order was the other way, with ramie at 0.1 against cotton at 0.094.

The per-pi figures, subject to Gao's caveat that a pi varied in length:
- Hemp cloth at 0.216 a pi was about 2.3 laborer-days or 1.1 craftsman-days.
- Summer ramie at 0.45 a pi was about 4.8 laborer-days or 2.3 craftsman-days.

I read no wage for 1615.

**2. Maeda Toshinaga's letter to "Kyūbē", 29th of the 4th month, year not given** (Takaoka City Museum), Bunka Isan Online: https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/614440

The evidence dates from Toshinaga's lifetime, before his death in 1614. A companion letter in the same set, No. 10, is dated Keichō 16 (1611). Ledger: /tmp/l7r-pass/maeda/01-online.bunka.go.jp.txt

- The transcription: 「中田よりこし候かい／ぬの廿まき、この銀子／百八十めつかい候わたし／候へく候、せんと／はや小左衛門あけ候、／ぬのよりわるく候」
  - (translation) "For the 20 rolls of cloth bought and sent from Nakata, I am sending 180 me of silver; pay it over. They are worse than the cloth Kozaemon presented the other day."
- The museum's note on the cloth: 「布（越中布）越中で織られた麻布」 (translation) "nuno (Etchū cloth): hemp cloth woven in Etchū".

By my arithmetic this is about 9 monme a roll. The roll (巻) is not a tan and its length is unknown, and the lot was judged inferior. There is no cotton price beside it, and the record has no Japanese wage that early, since Bassino and Ma's series begins in 1741.

**3. Ehime Prefectural History (愛媛県史 学問・宗教), 1985, on "the spread of Ise belief"** ("Ehime no kioku"): https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/54/view/7280

The evidence describes 1813. Ledger: /tmp/l7r-pass/ehime-oshi/01-www.i-manabi.jp.txt

- How the oshi were paid: 「檀家に対して御師は毎年、伊勢暦などの土産とともに神札（御祓い）を配り、米や金子で応分の初穂料を得たわけである。」
  - (translation) "Each year the oshi distributed talismans (o-harai) to their parishioner households together with gifts such as the Ise calendar, and received hatsuho offerings proportionate [to each household], in rice or money."
- The 1813 figure: 「文化一〇年（一八一三）に大洲領内の檀家二一六二軒を廻った某御師の初穂は、「檀所神徳勘定之事」（『宇治山田市史』所収）によると四八両二分とあり、これより土産や諸入用の経費二九両三分を差し引くと純益は一八両三分であった。」
  - (translation) "The hatsuho of a certain oshi who went round 2,162 parishioner households in Ōzu domain in Bunka 10 (1813) was, according to the 'Account of the divine favor of the parish' (in the Uji-Yamada City History), 48 ryō 2 bu. Taking away 29 ryō 3 bu spent on gifts and other expenses leaves a net profit of 18 ryō 3 bu."

By my arithmetic the gross is about 0.0224 ryō a household. At the 60 monme to the ryō that the record already borrows (its ryo-rate reading, not sourced in this pass), that is about 1.35 monme of silver. Against wages:
- **Unskilled:** the Kyoto laborer's 1813 wage in Bassino and Ma's Table A.1 is 2.91 g of silver, about 0.94 monme at their 3.11 g to the monme. That makes the offering about 1.4 laborer-days. The record's 1802-1804 figure of 0.92 monme a day gives about 1.5.
- **Craftsman:** the record's Osaka carpenter of 1802-1804 earned 4.7 times the Kyoto laborer's pay, about 4.3 monme. That makes the offering about 0.3 craftsman-days.

This is a gross average over all 2,162 households, paid in rice or coin, and it paid for the calendar and other gifts as well as the talisman. It is not a price per amulet.

### Reading

On hemp in Edo Japan, the only sum is one early lot of Etchū hemp cloth at about 9 monme a roll of unknown length. No tan price for Nara-zarashi, Echigo jōfu or Ōmi hemp cloth turned up, and there is nothing to set beside the record's cotton prices of 1781-1791.

For China, the Beijing list of 1736 prices hemp and ramie beside cotton, with wages. Plain ramie cloth was cheaper by the zhang than coarse cotton in 1736, though not in 1615, and the hemp cloth priced by the pi cost about 2 laborer-days.

On amulets, the one Edo figure is the Ōzu oshi's total of 1813: roughly 1.4 Kyoto laborer-days a household a year, for the talisman and the gifts together, given "proportionate" to the household rather than charged at a fixed price.
