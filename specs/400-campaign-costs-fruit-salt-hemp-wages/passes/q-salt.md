# Pass - salt (#5 question 2) (research-pass, 2026-10-19; its reply verbatim)

**Verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** some of what was asked. I found salt prices and day wages for one Owari village in 1842 and for one inland Mino mountain village in 1763–74, plus field-price, official-price and markup figures for Qing China (Lianghuai, Changlu, Beijing, Sichuan). ## Japan

**1. Handa city museum, 半田市立博物館「古文書に見る江戸時代の食材と食生活」, slide 9.** https://www.city.handa.lg.jp/_res/projects/default_project/_page_/001/002/711/r1komonnjo.pdf
- The document is the 諸品直段書上帳, 知多郡乙川村, 天保13年寅4月差出 (乙川文書5-51). Place: 乙川村, Chita district, Owari. Date: 1842. The village is on the coast but is not a salt producer.
- `make source-pages` cannot read this PDF; the ledger has the failed attempt. I read the slide image from an earlier pass's local download and quote the museum's printed transcription, which I checked against the scan:
  - 「一塩　但九升入　買入直段両ニ四拾五俵半替　売直段壱升代拾八文」 — "Salt, in bales of 9 sho: bought at 45½ bales per ryō; sold at 18 mon per sho." (translation)
- That is 45.5 × 9 ≈ 410 sho per ryō wholesale against 18 mon per sho retail. I have not converted the wholesale price to mon, because no ryō-to-mon rate for Chita in 1842 was found.
- The document gives no wage.

**2. Tokugawa Institute for Forestry History, 徳川林政史研究所『人・物・お金にみる山村の暮らし』, booklet 9, pp. 18–22.** https://rinseishi.tokugawa.or.jp/pdf_file/booklet_09.pdf
- It transcribes the Hikoshichi family diary 「御山方御用幷諸事日記」 from Kashimo village (加子母村), an upland village in Mino. Period: 1763–1774.
- Prices per 100 mon, from the table:
  - 宝暦13年 (1763): 「100文につき6升」 (about 16.7 mon per sho)
  - 明和2年 (1765): 「4俵余　1分756文…100文につき5升」 (the bale unit, 20 mon per sho)
  - 明和9年 (1772): 5升6合
  - 安永2年 (1773): 「100文につき3升2合」 (about 31 mon per sho)
- The diary entry the booklet dates to 明和九年（一七七一）: 「四ツ比久田見塩売来ル、百文ニ付五升六合ニ長嶋ニ而武助相極メ…升数三斗壱升ニ而五百五拾壱文相渡し遣ス也、九ツ前比又塩売来り、五升七合、弐斗壱升にて三百六拾六文ニ買之也」 — "Around the fourth hour a salt seller from Kutami came; Busuke settled on 5 sho 6 gō per 100 mon… 3 to 1 sho, 551 mon paid; before noon another salt seller came, 5 sho 7 gō, bought 2 to 1 sho for 366 mon." (translation)
  - Two dating problems: 明和九年 is 1772, not 1771, and the table puts the 551 mon purchase at 明和9年 but the 366 mon one at 安永3年 1/12. The session should settle the year from the booklet before citing.
- Booklet on the 1773 rise: 「余り高直ニ相成候付、塩代少々為手伝、六百四拾八文善右ニ渡ス」 — "Since it had become far too dear, [the son] Zen'emon helped with part of the salt money, 648 mon." (translation)
- **Wage, same household:** 「日数廿七日相働候賃銭一日六拾文充之積壱分ト四百文」 — "for 27 days worked, a wage reckoned at 60 mon a day: 1 bu and 400 mon." (translation)
  - This is unskilled autumn farm labour, 安永3年 (1774), the same house and decade as the salt prices.
  - The booklet gives no craftsman's day rate.

**3. Tanigama Hironori 2014 (谷釜尋徳), Tōyō University; in the archive.** https://toyo.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/6852/files/sportskenkokagaku11_055-077.pdf
- The page is two-column OCR, so I quote only the clean fragment: 「文政期頃の大工の日収は飯料込みで銀5匁4分（540文）であったという」 — "around the Bunsei era [1818–30] a carpenter's daily income, including meal allowance, was 5 monme 4 bu of silver (540 mon)." (translation)
- Edo, craftsman. The paper's source is 『文政年間漫録』. It gives no salt price.

**4. saltstory.wordpress.com, a blog citing a published salt history.** https://saltstory.wordpress.com/2014/06/14/%E7%BE%8E%E5%91%B3%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E5%A1%A9%E3%81%AE%E7%B3%BB%E8%AD%9C/
- 「瀬戸内では塩一升の値段が５，６文、東海・江戸まで運ばれると約１０文、三陸海岸で約１５文、これが内陸部に入ると、２里(８km)で二倍、三里で三倍に値上がりし、米沢藩で１２０文、最高は一の関で一升300文の値がついたといわれます」 — "In the Inland Sea a sho of salt cost 5–6 mon, about 10 carried to the Tōkai and Edo, about 15 on the Sanriku coast; inland it doubled within 2 ri and tripled within 3; 120 mon in Yonezawa domain, and at most 300 mon a sho in Ichinoseki, it is said." (translation)
- It cites 廣山堯道『塩の日本史』. Period: "early modern", undated. This is secondary, and its own wording is hearsay (「といわれます」).

## China

**5. 清史稿 卷123, 食貨四 鹽法.** https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%B8%85%E5%8F%B2%E7%A8%BF/%E5%8D%B7123
- Tao Shu on Lianghuai, c. 1830–31 (道光10–11): 「鹽在場灶，每斤僅值錢一二文，若就而收稅，則價隨課長」 — "At the saltworks salt is worth only 1–2 cash a jin; if taxed there, the price rises with the tax." (translation)
- Huaibei ticket-salt rules, 1832: 「每鹽四百斤為一引，合銀六錢四分，加以諸雜費，為一兩八錢八分」 — "400 jin of salt make one yin, worth 0.64 tael; with the various fees, 1.88 tael." (translation)
  - The CASS paper on Tao Shu's reform ([jds.cssn.cn](http://jds.cssn.cn/xscg/ybwz/201605/t20160506_5253985.shtml)) adds that the field price 0.64 was 「場鹽照錢時價」, i.e. the field salt reckoned at the current cash price. It also says that before the reform 「每單引成本即至十有余兩」: the cost of one yin by the time it reached the selling district was over 10 taels.
- Changlu official price (長蘆), 1688 and 1728: 「蓋長蘆鹽價，自康熙二十七年定每斤銀一分四毫至一分二釐六毫不等」 — "the Changlu salt price was fixed from Kangxi 27 at 0.0104–0.0126 tael per jin." (translation)
  - The censor's memorial of Yongzheng 6 (1728): 「康熙時，銀一兩換制錢千四五百，每鹽一斤，錢十六文。今每兩合錢二千，而鹽價如故，亦有減至十三四文者」 — "Under Kangxi a tael exchanged for 1,400–1,500 cash and a jin of salt was 16 cash; now a tael is 2,000 cash, yet the salt price is unchanged, some even down to 13–14 cash." (translation)
- Jiaqing edict, 1800: 「今計食鹽者每日止一二文」 — "a person eating salt now spends only 1–2 cash a day." (translation)

**6. Gao Shouxian, 『故宮學刊』 2022, on the 《九卿議定物料價值》 of 1736, Beijing; in the archive.** https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2023/02/10/u63e60647c73ae.pdf
- Table 1: 「盐每斤0.012」 (in taels). This is an official procurement price from the Ministry of Works schedule, not a street price.
- Table 23, wages from the same compilation: 「木石砖瓦油画等匠…不论长、短给钱154文」; 「壮夫…不论长、短给钱75文」 — "craftsmen 154 cash a day; labourers 75 cash a day." (translation)
- The article's own exchange rate: 「每纹银一两，换大制钱八百文」 (1737), so salt ≈ 9.6 cash per jin.

**7. 閱世編, 食貨, Ye Mengzhu; Songjiang/Shanghai, a salt-producing coastal county; in the archive.** https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/閱世編
- 「順治八年春，價至紋銀每斤一錢…自是以後，大約每斤以紋銀一分內外為率。至康熙二十二年癸亥春…每斤紋銀三分二厘，皆從郡邑販來官鹽，私鹽絕響…二十七年戊辰，每斤不過六七厘」 — "Shunzhi 8 [1651] spring, up to 0.1 tael a jin… thereafter about 0.01 tael a jin. Kangxi 22 [1683] spring, 0.032 tael a jin, all official salt brought from the prefectural seats, private salt vanished… Kangxi 27 [1688], no more than 0.006–0.007 tael a jin." (translation)
- Local retail prices. The rows from Chongzhen are Ming; the Qing rows run from 1651. It gives no day wage.

**8. F. H. King, *Farmers of Forty Centuries*, chapter "About Tientsin"; describes 1909, still Qing; in the archive.** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5350/pg5350.txt

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** village prices in Japan and for the field-to-official markup in Qing China.

In Japan, retail salt cost about 16–20 mon per sho in an inland Mino village in the 1760s and jumped to about 31 mon in 1773. There an unskilled farm hand earned 60 mon a day in 1774, so a day's wage bought about 2–3½ sho. In an Owari village in 1842 salt cost 18 mon per sho retail and about 410 sho per ryō wholesale. The nearest craftsman's wage is the Edo carpenter at 540 mon a day in the 1820s, which is a different place.

In China, the field price was 1–2 cash per jin in Lianghuai around 1830, and Ho's 1–4 cash agrees. The official Changlu price was about 0.010–0.013 tael per jin from 1688, which the 1728 memorial puts at 16 cash, later 13–14. That is roughly an 8- to 16-fold markup at the official price. Retail upriver ran to 30–70 cash (Wu), and King's 1909 figure is 12–15 times the cost. In Beijing in 1736 salt at 0.012 tael (about 9.6 cash at 800 cash a tael) sits against wages of 154 cash for a craftsman and 75 cash for a labourer.
