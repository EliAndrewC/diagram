Every one of the 25 is a tertiary work (an encyclopedia article or a reproduced dictionary/encyclopedia entry), so "tertiary summary" is a limit common to all and is noted once per block rather than argued each time.

## NOT-APPLICABLE

**None.** The two modern-only works in the batch (`conservation-headland-enwiki`, `floating-dock-enwiki`) are used by the record explicitly as the named modern CONTRAST to a premodern form, not as evidence for one, and each write-up says so; on that use they are APPLICABLE-WITH-LIMITS and are listed below.

## Limits MISSING or OVERSTATED

### `panmen-zhwiki`
- Tertiary encyclopedia article (zh.wikipedia 盘门, collectively authored, undated revision) / describes Pan Gate at Suzhou - the surviving gate built 1351 under the Yuan, the wengcheng added 1356, repaired in early Ming, early Qing, late Qing and 1986 / scale: one gate of one great Jiangnan city / method: tertiary description of a standing monument.
- **We use it for:** the water gate's sluice and grating (river cities).
- **Verdict: APPLICABLE-WITH-LIMITS** - (a) tertiary, its own sources not followed; (b) one exceptional surviving gate, not a norm; (c) the described fabric - the two layers, the 24 m depth, the 水闸 sluices and 木栅 wooden gratings - is the monument after four restoration campaigns, the last in 1986; (d) it is the water gate of a walled city on a canal system, read across to our river face.
- **`What it is:` INACCURATE.** It calls Panmen "the paired land and water gate of the Ming wall"; the article dates the surviving gate to 1351 (Yuan Zhizheng 11) and the wengcheng to 1356, saying the layout keeps a late-Yuan/early-Ming appearance: 「现存的城门是元至正十一年（1351年）建，1356年张士诚又增建了瓮城。」
- **Limits MISSING:** the restoration history (so the depth and the grating are of the monument as it now stands) and the singularity of the case. Both are stated in the sibling entry `suzhou-panmen-gov`, which cites the same monument, so the omission is inconsistent within the registry rather than unavoidable.

### `katabatic-wind-enwiki`
- Tertiary encyclopedia article (en.wikipedia "Katabatic wind") / describes a general atmospheric mechanism with regional examples: Antarctica, Greenland, the Adriatic bora, the Ore Mountains, Santa Ana, williwaws, **and the oroshi of Japan** / scale: general / method: a summary of physical geography.
- **We use it for:** cold air draining downslope (vegetation).
- **Verdict: APPLICABLE-WITH-LIMITS** - tertiary; a modern meteorological account of a process that does not change with era, so the discount is small; no measurement of any slope wind at settlement scale.
- **`What it is:` ACCURATE.**
- **Limits OVERSTATED.** The write-up disclaims "a general article with no East Asian case", but the article names the Japanese oroshi as an example and carries exactly the small-scale nighttime form the record leans on: 「This cooler air layer then flows down in the valley, producing the downslope wind that is easily observed at night in mountainous areas」. The source is closer to the use than its own write-up admits.

### `fengshuilin-zhwiki`
- Tertiary encyclopedia article (zh.wikipedia 風水林) / describes village fengshui groves, in detail for **Hong Kong**, with passing reference to Shandong and Shaanxi / scale: a class of village grove, with a Hong Kong size range / method: tertiary summary.
- **We use it for:** the back grove as the village's screen (river cities/vegetation).
- **Verdict: APPLICABLE-WITH-LIMITS** - tertiary; the substance is Hong Kong's, a subtropical corner of south China, read across to the setting generally; geomantic siting is a Chinese practice with no Japanese counterpart named on the page.
- **`What it is:` ACCURATE** for kind and subject.
- **Limits MISSING (and one wrong).** The write-up says "a tertiary summary with no measurements"; the article gives an area range - 「單個面積約為600平方米至一公頃」 (about 600 sq m to one hectare) - and puts the groves generally below 100 m elevation. That matters here because this project's grove sizes rest on a separate Hong Kong survey (`fengshui grove sizes`, feature 196): a page that does carry an area range should not be described as carrying none, and its range should be compared with the survey's rather than waved off. Also missing: that the described region is Hong Kong specifically, not "south China" at large.

### `riparian-zone-enwiki`
- Tertiary encyclopedia article (en.wikipedia "Riparian zone") / general stream ecology with regional sections (Africa, North America, Asia, Australia, Central Europe); largely modern ecology, management and restoration / scale: general / method: summary of ecological literature.
- **We use it for:** the natural stream bank vegetated to the edge (vegetation).
- **Verdict: APPLICABLE-WITH-LIMITS** - tertiary; the framing is modern ecology and restoration practice, though the fact taken (a natural margin carries hydrophilic vegetation to the water: 「Plant habitats and communities along the river margins and banks are called riparian vegetation, characterized by hydrophilic plants」) is a natural process and era-neutral; no East Asian premodern case.
- **`What it is:` ACCURATE.**
- **Limits OVERSTATED (minor).** "It says nothing of maintained banks on an engineered channel" is not quite true: the article states 「Riparian zones may be natural or engineered for soil stabilization or restoration」. It says nothing about a bank kept CLEAR by maintenance, which is the real contrast the record wants; the disclaimer should be narrowed to that.

### `coppicing-enwiki`
- Tertiary encyclopedia article (en.wikipedia "Coppicing") / describes lowland temperate European coppice, chiefly southern Britain (Kent, Sussex), with 16th-17th century English and French references and one mention of Japanese daisugi / scale: a silvicultural practice / method: tertiary summary.
- **We use it for:** the floor of a worked coppice (vegetation).
- **Verdict: APPLICABLE-WITH-LIMITS** - tertiary; British/European practice standing in for the East Asian worked wood (defensible, since the satoyama cutting cycle is separately sourced at `satoyama-jawiki`); the light-and-floor relation transfers, the flora does not.
- **`What it is:` ACCURATE.**
- **Limits MISSING:** (a) the floor plants the article names are European - 「After cutting, the increased light allows existing woodland-floor vegetation such as bluebell, anemone and primrose to grow vigorously」 - so only the light-driven pattern transfers, never the species a map might draw; (b) on this reading the overstood-coppice sentence I could find says 「Overstood coppice is a habitat of relatively low biodiversity」, not that the floor is shaded and bare, so the "shaded, bare floor" half of the write-up goes beyond the sentence I could read and wants confirming at the footnote by `quote-check`.

The write-up says only that they are particular boats, not that they belong to different centuries.

Two cross-cutting observations for whoever writes these up: every key in this batch is a tertiary work, and nine of them are English Wikipedia articles on European or general subjects - which is defensible where the claim is a natural process or a drawing convention (`crown-shyness`, `riparian-zone`, `forest`, `cartographic-relief`) or an explicit modern contrast (`conservation-headland`, `floating-dock`, `pier`), but `water-gate-enwiki` is the one case where a closer, better-sourced East Asian source (`panmen-zhwiki` / `suzhou-panmen-gov`) already sits in this same registry and the write-up does not say so.
