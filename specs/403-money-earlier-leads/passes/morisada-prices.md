(追書) made up to the Keio era (1865-68). Every passage below was transcribed from the page image: a headless-browser screenshot of the

NDL full-text search (POST /api/fulltext/search). Old glyphs and the kaeriten (レ) are kept as printed. Each koma is entered on the

## (a) Inns, a night - ATTESTED (Kyoto, Osaka, Edo, highways; 1837-53, with Keio additions)
koma 79-80, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/79 and https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/80 (第四編 生業上, printed pp.119-121).
- KYOTO (the entry opens on koma 79):「旅籠屋 はたごやと訓す三都とも同名也旅人宿也 京師は三條通りに多し茶久と云者を第一とす江坂の豪富等を宿すを專とし人品不良の者は泊ず其他にも上宿多し 又先斗町邊にもあり專ら上宿也一宿大概銀三匁五分を普通とし客の望により銀五匁或は金二朱もあり稀也並に朝夕二食也中食は別也」 then 「又三條以下にも錢二百文或は二百五十文計の宿もあり 又洛中諸所に散在する者もあれども是は多く商人宿と云て每時京に來り得意の者にて遊參の人に非ず商用に來る者故に專ら中食ともに二百文或は二百五十文計也食の菜には麁を用ふ」
  (translation) "Hatagoya (inns): the name is the same in all three cities; they are travellers' lodgings. In Kyoto they are mostly on
  Sanjo street ... these are all first-class inns. One night is generally 3 monme 5 bu of silver as the usual rate. At the guest's
  wish it may be 5 monme of silver or 2 shu of gold, but that is rare. Both include two meals, morning and evening; the midday meal is
  extra." "There are also inns of about 200 or 250 mon in coin, at Sanjo and lesser [三條以下 - read either as below the Sanjo grade or below Sanjo street; I do not commit]. There are also inns scattered through the city, mostly called merchants' inns: their guests come to Kyoto regularly on business, not for pleasure, so it is generally 200 or 250 mon including the midday meal, with plain dishes." Note the basis: the 3.5-monme rate excludes the midday meal; the merchants' 200-250 mon includes it.
- HIGHWAYS, with the Keio addition:「安政萬延以降諸品頻りに高價なるにより諸國ともに宿錢を增す安政以前東海道上旅籠二百錢中山道は百四十八文今慶應に至り漸くに貴く一宿七八百錢となる三都ともに傚レ之」
  (translation) "Since Ansei and Man'en (1854-61) all goods have grown steadily dearer, so inn charges have risen in every province.
  Before Ansei a first-class inn on the Tokaido was 200 mon and on the Nakasendo 148 mon. Now, in Keio (1865-68), they have grown
  dearer by degrees, to 700-800 mon a night. The three cities have followed suit."
- OSAKA:「大坂も上宿普通三匁五分下品宿二百文計也」
  (translation) "In Osaka too a first-class inn is usually 3 monme 5 bu [silver], and a low-grade inn about 200 mon."
- OSAKA, beggars' lodging (ぐれ宿), described by Morisada as a kind of 木賃宿:「長町も三四丁目にも旅人宿あれども下品也又ぐれ宿も交れり夫より南涯九丁目迄は專らぐれ宿のみ也ぐれ宿は他國に云木賃宿の類にて又異也住居の背に一宇數戸の長屋を建て一宇表口六尺奥行九尺計也乞丐人を泊る也一夜錢三十六文也長屋には古疊をしき土鍋一口を付すのみ夜具及び他物を貸ず是を名付てぐれ宿と云 京師には大佛邊にある由也江戸には此ごとき宿無レ之」
  (translation) "In Nagamachi ... from the 3rd and 4th blocks south to the 9th block there are only gure-yado. A gure-yado is like
  what other provinces call a kichin-yado (firewood-fee inn), but different: behind the house stands a row-house of several units,
  each about 6 shaku wide and 9 shaku deep. It lodges beggars. One night is 36 mon in coin. The unit has old tatami and one earthen
  pot, and no bedding or anything else is lent. In Kyoto they are said to be near the Daibutsu; Edo has none." (The full-text OCR
  splits the number as 三【十六文】; the image reads 三十六文.)
- KICHIN (firewood-fee) lodging, Morisada glossing an old text (柳莕雜筆 as printed, i.e. 柳亭雜筆 of Ryutei Tanehiko, on the Keicho era, 1596-1615):「…驛舍に着て湯をわかし糒を喰て寢る迄なりし去ば湯の木の代四錢五厘を拂ひ往來せしと也云々是今世の木賃泊と同き也葢四五錢は永錢なれば十六七文或は二十文也」
  (translation) "'... they reached the post station, boiled water, ate dried rice and slept; so they paid 4 sen 5 rin for the
  firewood and went on.' This is the same as today's kichin lodging. The 4-5 sen are in Eiraku coin, so they make 16-17 or 20 mon."
  The evidence date is the Keicho era, with the conversion into current coin in Morisada's own day.
- EDO (koma 80, left page):「江戸の旅人宿は公事宿と號け官府に用ある者を泊る…此故に京坂の如く美制の宿無レ之旅泊は甚だ劣れり膳部等も麁にして二食を供し大略二百四十八文也貴價の宿賃は求めても應ぜず」
  (translation) "Edo's travellers' inns are called kujiyado (lawsuit inns) and lodge people with business at the government
  offices ... So there are no handsome inns as in Kyoto and Osaka, and the lodging is very poor. The meals are coarse; two meals are
  served, for about 248 mon. A dear lodging is not to be had even if one asks for it."
VERDICT: ATTESTED. Per night with two meals: Kyoto first-class inns 3.5 monme of silver (rarely 5 monme or 2 shu of gold; the midday meal extra), and lesser
Kyoto inns and merchants' inns 200-250 mon (the merchants' inns including the midday meal). Osaka first-class 3.5 monme, low-grade about 200 mon. Edo kujiyado about 248 mon. Before Ansei a first-class
Tokaido inn was 200 mon and a Nakasendo inn 148 mon, rising by Keio to 700-800 mon. An Osaka beggars' lodging was 36 mon, without
bedding.

## (b) Soba and udon, a bowl - ATTESTED (Kyoto-Osaka and Edo; with dated earlier rates)
koma 74-76, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/74 , /75 , /76 (第四編 生業上, 溫飩屋 蕎麥屋).
- Earlier rates, quoted by Morisada from older books (koma 74):「或書云寬文四年慳貪蕎麥切始て製レ之下賤の食とす價八文云々又一書に貞享中江戸旅籠町蒸蕎麥切一杯六文無レ掛直云々」
  (translation) "A book says: in Kanbun 4 (1664) kendon soba-kiri was first made, as food for the lowly, at a price of 8 mon. Another
  book: in Jokyo (1684-88), at Hatago-cho in Edo, steamed soba-kiri was 6 mon a bowl, with no overcharge."
- Nihachi and the Keio rises (koma 74):「又或書云二八蕎麥は寛文四年に始る云々即價十六錢を云也慶應に至り諸價頻りに騰揚す依レ之江戸蕎麥賈請レ官て價を增し二十錢とし又尋て二十四錢となる故に招牌の類の二八を除く江戸然り京阪も恐くは同じからん然も二十四錢になりしかど三八とは云ず」
  (translation) "Another book says nihachi soba began in Kanbun 4. 'Two-eight' means the price, 16 mon. In Keio all prices soared,
  so Edo's soba-sellers petitioned the authorities and raised the price to 20 mon, then soon to 24 mon. They therefore dropped
  'nihachi' from their signboards. So it is in Edo; Kyoto-Osaka is probably the same. Yet though it became 24 mon, nobody calls it
  'three-eight'."
- KYOTO-OSAKA shop-wall price list (koma 75, right page). Morisada says that shop walls display a paper written like this figure, and
  that Edo has them too. The list is printed in cursive. My reading of the item names:「覺 一うどん 代十六文 一そば 代十六文 一しっぽく 代廿四文 一あんぺい 代廿四文 一けいらん 代丗二文 一小田巻 代丗六文」
  (translation) "Notice: udon 16 mon; soba 16 mon; shippoku 24 mon; anpei 24 mon; keiran (egg) 32 mon; odamaki 36 mon." The prices
  are clear. The item names are my reading of the cursive. The note under the list glosses しっぽく, あんぺい and 鷄卵 ("鷄卵溫飩の卵とじ也"), which
  supports the reading けいらん. Beside it:「器 十六文のうどんそば共に平皿に盛る常の肴皿の麁なる物也」 (translation) "Vessel: the
  16-mon udon and soba are both served on a flat dish, a coarse common side-dish plate."
- EDO (koma 75, left page):「江戸慶應以來增價 蕎麥溫飩ともに二十四錢となり霰以下准レ之て價を增す」 (translation) "Edo, the rise from Keio on:
  soba and udon both became 24 mon, and arare and the dishes after it rose in proportion." Also:「從來二八後に二十四文の物を商ふを駄蕎麥と云」
  (translation) "Shops that sold the formerly 16-mon, later 24-mon article are called 'da-soba' (common soba)."
- EDO price board (koma 76, top of right page). Morisada anchors it on koma 75 L2, in the Edo section: 「壁の張紙及格子掛行燈は左の一二頁上圖の如し」 (translation) "The wall notices and lattice lanterns are as in the upper figures on the next page or two." It is cursive; my reading:「御膳大蒸籠 代四十八文 一そば 代拾六文 一あんかけうどん 代拾六文 一あられ 代二十四文 一天ぷら 代卅二文 一花まき 代二十四文 一しっぽく 代二十四文 一玉子とじ 代三十二文 一上酒一合 代四十文」
  (translation) "Large steamer [of soba] 48 mon; soba 16 mon; ankake udon 16 mon; arare 24 mon; tenpura 32 mon; hanamaki 24 mon;
  shippoku 24 mon; egg-drop 32 mon; good sake, one go, 40 mon."
- EDO 1860 and Keio (koma 76):「萬延元年蕎麥高價のことに係り江戸府內蕎麥店會合す其戸數三千七百六十三店 蓋夜商俗に云よたかそば屋は除レ之 慶應中再會の後官に請て十六文を二十文とす 他准レ之て價を增す」
  (translation) "In Man'en 1 (1860), over the high price of soba, the soba shops inside Edo held a meeting: 3,763 shops, not counting
  the night sellers called yotaka-soba. After a second meeting in Keio they petitioned the authorities and made 16 mon into 20 mon;
  the other items rose in proportion."
VERDICT: ATTESTED. A plain bowl of soba or udon was 16 mon (二八) in both Edo and Kyoto-Osaka through Morisada's writing years (1837-53).
In Edo it rose in Keio (1865-68) to 20 mon and then 24 mon; Morisada supposes Kyoto-Osaka followed. Topped bowls were 24-36 mon, and an
Edo large steamer of soba 48 mon. The earlier rates (8 mon in 1664, 6 mon a bowl in Edo in the Jokyo era) are quotations from older
books.

## (c) Sake, by the measure - ATTESTED (Edo and Osaka, c.1815-1850s; one 17th-century figure)
- koma 229, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991467/1/229 (第二十八編 食類, printed p.424):「慶安年中東叡山塔中吉祥院の古帳面中に酒壹升四拾文とあり 右圖の引札年號未レ詳酒四十二錢より百三拾二錢に至る後考を待つ 余文化七年の生れ也幼年の頃は大坂にて賣酒極上品一升價百六十四錢也同時江戸は一升價二百錢より二百四十八錢也天保比より三百五十八十錢或は四百錢となる」
  (translation) "In an old account book of the Kisshoin, a sub-temple of Toeizan [Kan'ei-ji], from the Keian era (1648-52), sake is
  entered at 40 mon a sho. The handbill pictured at right is undated; its sake runs from 42 to 132 mon [the measure is not stated in
  the text]. This awaits further study. I was born in Bunka 7 (1810). In my childhood the best sake sold in Osaka was 164 mon a sho;
  at the same time Edo's was 200 to 248 mon a sho. From about Tenpo (1830-44) it became 350-380 [printed 三百五十八十錢] or 400 mon."
  Morisada's 幼年 dates the 164-mon and 200-248-mon figures to about 1815-1825.
- koma 137, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/137 (第七編 貨幣, printed p.234). This is Morisada in his own voice; the passage follows
  「守貞唯歎レ之」:「後年諸物萬品昔より多少高價になりたり古の價はさしをき三十年前は予は知る所也其比は江戸にて上諸白酒一升二百五十文ばかり今は三百五十文より或は四百文也是を四斗樽一樽にて金二分の昔價とし」
  (translation) "In later years every kind of goods has become somewhat dearer than of old. Leaving aside the prices of long ago, I
  know those of thirty years back: then in Edo good morohaku sake was about 250 mon a sho; now it is 350 to 400 mon. Reckoning this as
  [an increase of] 2 bu of gold a four-to barrel ..." The glyph before 價 is unclear (the OCR reads 書). The next column, "樽の價金二分高價なれば一萬三百六十八兩高直也",
  shows that the sum is an increase. Dating (my inference, not stated on the page): Morisada wrote in 1837-53, so "thirty years ago" falls in about the 1810s-20s, the same years as the koma 229 childhood figures. It is not a third date.
- koma 76, the Edo soba-shop board under (b):「一上酒一合 代四十文」 (translation) "good sake, one go, 40 mon" - a cup of sake
  in an Edo soba shop, 1840s-50s.
VERDICT: ATTESTED. Good sake per sho: Osaka 164 mon and Edo 200-248 mon in about 1815-25. Edo about 250 mon "thirty years ago",
rising to 350-400 mon from about Tenpo. One go of good sake in an Edo soba shop cost 40 mon. In Keian (1648-52), 40 mon a sho (from a
temple account book that Morisada cites). A price per go for an izakaya is not given; the 居酒 hit on 991467 koma 211 (Osaka, 坐摩の前の傘の下) has no
price.

## (d) Sewing fee and used clothing - SILENT on prices (the trades are attested)
- koma 70, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/70 :「縫物師匠 京坂の童女十三四以下書筆を學ばせ其後は縫裁の師家に通はして習レ之しむ…蓋三都ともに雇錢を以て裁縫を業とする工あり是は仕立屋と云也江戸にも此工はあれども此師を業とする者なく專ら母姉の教を受る然も京坂より上手多し」
  (translation) "Sewing teachers: in Kyoto-Osaka girls under 13-14 learn writing and then attend a sewing teacher's house ... In all
  three cities there are craftsmen who sew for hire; they are called shitateya. Edo has these craftsmen too, but no one makes a living
  teaching sewing; girls learn from mother and sister, yet more of them are skilled than in Kyoto-Osaka." The passage gives no fee.
- Used clothing: the 古着/古手 trade appears on koma 65-67 (the Edo 古手問屋 guild, 十三戸五十兩 - a guild sum, not a garment price), koma 95
  (古手 buyers "てんかみくず") and koma 106 (竹馬古着屋, Kyoto-Osaka). None of these gives a garment price.
Searched (full text, both vols): 仕立, 仕立賃, 仕立代, 縫賃, 縫代, 針賃, 手間賃, 裁縫, 古着, 古手 - no price.

## (e) Hand mirror - SILENT
鏡 (52 hits), 鏡屋, 柄鏡, 鏡一. The hits are the 鏡磨き itinerant (koma 95: 「鏡磨き 平日も來れども寒中を專とす」, no fee), the 三徳/鏡囊 (a pouch), 鏡開

## (f) Paper and ink - SILENT
半紙 (13 hits), 紙屋, 一帖, 墨 (52), 筆墨, 墨屋. The hits are signboards, theatre bills printed on 半紙, ink as a dye, and a 筆墨料 as a moneylender's

## (g) Rent - ATTESTED ONLY FOR the mechanism and the Edo move-in fee (樽代); NO rent amount
koma 57, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/57 (第三編 人事, printed pp.74-75), Edo:
- 「店借 他の地に家宅も造り有レ之し地と家宅ともに借て住を店借と云月收を店賃と號け毎晦家主に收レ之」
  (translation) "Tanagari: one who rents both the ground and the house on another's land is a tanagari. The monthly payment is called
  tanachin and is paid to the yanushi at the end of each month."
- 「又江戸にて始て地借店借し或は他より移り住む時其居宅に應じ金二朱或一分多きは金三五兩又は十兩も家主へ呈す號けて樽代と云 酒料の意也與レ之こと粗其宅に定制あるが如し京坂は酒一二升の手形を與ふのみ」
  (translation) "In Edo, when one first rents ground or a house, or moves in from elsewhere, one presents to the yanushi, according to
  the house, 2 shu or 1 bu of gold, or as much as 3 to 5 or even 10 ryo. It is called tarudai, meaning 'sake money'. The sum is more
  or less fixed for each house. In Kyoto-Osaka one gives only a chit for one or two sho of sake."
- 「又五節毎宅居に應じ五七十文より五六百文或は二朱一分を家主に呈す號けて節句錢と云京坂に更に無レ之」
  (translation) "Also, at each of the five festivals one presents to the yanushi, according to the house, from 50-70 mon up to 500-600
  mon, or 2 shu or 1 bu; this is called sekku-sen. Kyoto-Osaka has nothing like it."
- 「右の樽代節句錢天保府命に禁レ之れども止ず」 (translation) "The tarudai and sekku-sen above were forbidden by a Tenpo order, but they did not stop."
VERDICT: ATTESTED ONLY FOR the payment system: Edo monthly tanachin, paid at the month's end to the yanushi. Fixed extra charges are
given: tarudai on moving in (2 shu or 1 bu, up to 3-10 ryo) and sekku-sen at the five festivals (50-70 mon to 500-600 mon, or 2 shu
or 1 bu). Morisada gives NO monthly rent figure for a shop or a back-alley house. Searched: 店賃, 家賃, 地代, 裏店, 裡店, 月々, 一ヶ月, 晦日. The
家賃 hit on koma 135 is about pledge deeds, not an amount.

## Leads not followed

  note on koma 229. I did not transcribe it; a reader of cursive could take it off the image.
- 991466 koma 73: Edo chazuke-meshi shops at 36, 48 or 72 mon a serving (三【十六文】 in the OCR; not read from the image). Koma 99:
  Kyoto-Osaka street kabayaki at 16 mon a skewer. Both are food prices outside (a)-(g).
- 991467 koma 118-127 (下, 沐浴 - bathhouses): bath fees (湯錢十二文, 1863 and after) and tips. Not asked.
- 991466 koma 95: the price paid for old umbrellas by the 古傘買 (「古傘大略四文八文十二文」, OCR, not checked on the image).
