## Confusable pairs
- "Irrigation canals and how they are laid out (yosuiro)" / "Field drains (akusuiro)": the channels that bring water to the paddies vs the ones that carry the spent water away.
- "Where the ditch leaves the brook: the intake and its weir (toshuko and seki)" / "Sharing water between villages (bunsui)": the head works that take water off the brook vs the division works that split a canal's flow among villages.
- "Marshes and wetlands (shitchi)" / fields "Wet paddies that never drain (shitsuden)": uncropped reed ground vs a cropped paddy too wet ever to dry.
- "Marshes and wetlands (shitchi)" / "Ground too wet to build on": the marsh as a feature vs the rule that keeps houses, graves and wells off any wet ground.
- "Reservoir ponds (tameike)" / archetypes' fish ponds (dike-pond, 140-172) and 0041 (the crescent pond before a village): a pond stored for irrigation vs one kept for fish, and a village's half-moon pond.
- "Flood defenses of river-plain villages: ring dikes and refuge mounds (waju and mizuka)" / archetypes/080 and 330 (a polder's dike): a ring dike built round a village against river floods vs the dike that encloses land reclaimed from water.
- "Moats: where their water comes from and where it goes (hori)" / T14's defensive marshland (180): open water round a wall vs an engineered belt of marsh and ponds against cavalry.
- "Where a field meets its ditch: the bank, the bund and the inlet (mizuguchi)" / ways "Plank bridges over farm ditches (itabashi)": the edge a farmer's water enters by vs the plank a walker crosses by.
