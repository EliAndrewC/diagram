- "A village's groves ... (fengshui lin)" / "Village fuel woods and their coppice (satoyama)": the protected siting grove, left uncut, vs the worked wood cut on a cycle for fuel.
- "A village's groves ... (fengshui lin)" / religion-and-death "Shrine woods and fences (chinju no mori, tamagaki)": the village's own siting grove vs the kami's wood around a precinct (a Chinese earth-god shrine may stand in the former).
- "Shelter belts on a village's windward side (bōfūrin)" / archetypes "Polder dikes: what they were made of, how big, and what grew on them": the windbreak that shelters the houses vs willows planted to hold a dike (archetypes/100 says the one does not replace the other).
- "Charcoal kilns in the hills (sumigama)" / urban-features "Charcoal yards" and "Pottery kilns (noborigama)": a kiln that makes charcoal in the wood vs a depot that stores and sells it vs a kiln that fires pots.
- "Grass hills and fodder meadows (kusayama, magusaba)" / towns "Hayfields and hay barns at a town's edge": a village's common grass ground for green manure and fodder vs a road town's hayfield for its horses.
- "Scrub and rough grass at the edges of fields and channels" / religion-and-death "Ground swept clear around shrines and graves": grass kept cut for manure along the fields vs ground swept bare for the kami and the dead.
- "Reed beds and the marsh's edge (yoshihara)" / water "Marshes and wetlands (shitchi)" and water "Reservoir ponds (tameike)": the plants and the cutting at a marsh's margin vs the marsh as ground reclaimed for rice vs the reeded shore of a pond.
