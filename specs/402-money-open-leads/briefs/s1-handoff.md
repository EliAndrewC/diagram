## 0273 What did salt cost?

- Edo, Ako salt: old SILENT; new ATTESTED ONLY FOR the dealers' (下り塩仲買) price to shops, 1791: 9.5 bales a ryo,
  9.2-9.3 down to 9 with few ships in; bale 3 to 5 sho filled, tagged spring and winter down to about 3 to 2 sho (read
  on the image). About 290-330 sho a ryo, and about 15-21 mon a sho at 0277's Edo rates of 1791 (project's arithmetic).
- Wage beside it: Edo 1791 SILENT; nearest unskilled the Kyoto casual laborer 0.92 monme a day, 1802-1804 (silver, 11
  years later, another city; about 5-7 sho of the salt, arithmetic across two cities); nearest craftsman the Edo roofers'
  3 monme of silver a day, 1749 (42 years earlier).
- Wholesalers' commission (koma 71): not used - too cursive to read on the image.
- Edo retail by the sho, 1791 to the 1850s: SILENT; Morisada searched in both volumes (salt seller and kinds of salt, no
  price); 東京市史稿 and 廣山堯道 named as print only.
- Intro extended to "the dealers of Edo itself".

## 0275 What did a skilled craftsman earn against a laborer?

- Edo pair: old SILENT; new ATTESTED (Morisada, 類聚近世風俗志 上 koma 88, pp. 136-137, no year within 1837-1853):
  carpenter, no fixed rate, ordinarily about 5 or 5.5 monme of silver a day (over 10 after a great fire); tobi laborer
  (the same as the Kyoto-Osaka helpers) 300 mon a day. Ratio about 1.75-2 at 0277's Osaka rates of 105-110 mon a monme
  (project's arithmetic, an Osaka rate set on Edo wages).
- Castle town pair: SILENT, unchanged (藩法集 岡山藩 in-library only named).
- Not changed, worth a look: the Osaka bullet still says no ratio is computed for Morisada's Osaka pair (different coins),
  while the Edo ratio now uses 0277's rate; and Morisada's own Osaka figures (fixed 4 monme 3 bu, 3 monme 1 bu fed;
  helpers 280 mon, self-fed) are on the same page if a later feature wants them.

## 0276 What did a kitchen knife cost?

- Wage beside Mimasaka 1665: old SILENT, nearest Kyoto 1741 laborer and Musashi 1749 carpenters; new: 1665 and
  Mimasaka/Okayama SILENT; nearest the Kyoto carpenter 1 monme 8 bu, wage and rice together, 1653 (Hirai note 9; "a day's"
  is the record's reading, said so); the 1656 bath account's carpenters about 1.7 and day laborers about 0.8 monme a
  man-day (project's arithmetic, the tables read cleanly); Osaka day laborers 0.8-1 monme 1620, 1 monme Osaka / 0.5
  Fushimi 1621 (Kitano bulletin 33, note 26), 1.2 and 0.8 monme a man 1628 (Kitano R7, Table 1). Coin: stated only as
  falling between the first mintings of Keicho (1601) and Genroku (1695) silver (jawiki 三貨制度's table).
- Knife in days: old about ten laborer-days at 1741 rates; new about 4.5 days of the 1653 carpenter and about 10 of the
  1656 laborer (arithmetic).
- Okayama wage: 『藩法集1 岡山藩』 (NDL pids 3026815, 3026816) is in-library only - an unread lead, named in the absence note.
- Sharpener: fee SILENT, unchanged; new ATTESTED that Morisada's itinerant polisher (上 koma 94, p. 149) sharpened
  kitchen knives, small knives and saws, not swords; ATTESTED ONLY FOR swords: Uwajima 1662, 6 bu a sun old blade, 8 new,
  the bu as the page gives it.
- Inui's two papers: not publicly readable (abstract only; NDL in-library), said so in the maker-price absence note;
  Kawashima's line stands.
- Edo 1851 wage bullet: kept "none dated 1851" and added a pointer to 0275's Morisada Edo laborer (300 mon, 1837-1853) -
  a judgment call, since the new figure touches it.
