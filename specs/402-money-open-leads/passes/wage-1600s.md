# Pass: day wage, labourer / craftsman, 17th-c western Honshu (feature 401 lead)

## Sources that bear

### 1. Hirai Toshiyuki 平井俊行, 「妙心寺の浴室について」, 建築史学 49 (2007) pp. 21-39 - KYOTO, CARPENTER, 1653 and 1656
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsahj/49/0/49_21/_pdf (read via pdftotext; text is vertical, so the lines below were put back in reading order from interleaved columns. The wording is the page's own.)
Evidence date: 承応2 (1653) 『大庫裏高揚銀之帳』 (Myoshinji kuri rebuilding, Jul-Nov 1653); 明暦2 (1656) 『風呂屋普請高帳』; 延宝7 (1679) 『記録』. All at Myoshinji, Kyoto.

Note (9):
> 表-3の庫裏の坪単価を浴室の坪数三十坪に当てはめると，約銀十四貫程で新築建物が完成することとなる。天保の修復では銀二十貫となり，承応時の予想新築工事費をはるかに上回る額が支出されている。しかし，承応時の大工手間賃は飯米作料ともで銀一匁八分，天保時は四匁一分となり，物価上昇率は二・二七倍となる。
(translation) "If we apply the kuri's cost per tsubo (Table 3) to the bath's 30 tsubo, a new building would have cost about 14 kan of silver. The Tenpo repair cost 20 kan, far more than the expected Joo-era cost of a new building. But the carpenter's day wage (手間賃) in the Joo era was 1 monme 8 bu of silver, rice allowance and wage together (飯米作料とも); in the Tenpo era it was 4 monme 1 bu, a price rise of 2.27 times."

Note on the Joo source:
> 大庫裏高揚銀之帳 承応二年七月～十一月 (『重要文化財妙心寺庫裏ほか五棟修理工事報告書』 平成十一年三月 京都府教育委員会 第七章資料第五節)
(translation) "The 大庫裏高揚銀之帳, Joo 2 (1653), 7th to 11th month" (printed in the 1999 Kyoto Prefecture repair report on the Myoshinji kuri and five other buildings, ch. 7, section 5).

The 1656 bath account:
> 大工の出面は明暦二年十月十六日から十一月三十日までの四十五日間で延べ七百七人工を数える。…日傭の出面は，平日傭・木遣・手木者の三工種に細分化されている。
(translation) "Carpenters' attendance runs for 45 days, from Meireki 2/10/16 to 11/30: 707 carpenter-days in all. ... The day-labourers' (日傭) attendance is split into three kinds: ordinary day-labourers, log-haulers (木遣) and lever-men (手木者)."
Table 2 (表-2, 明暦2年『風呂屋普請高帳』における工事費内訳書, in silver monme) is a rotated table, and its text layer comes out as a run of numbers. These sums check out exactly, which tells you which numbers belong together. The totals and per-day rates are MY ARITHMETIC, not the author's.
- Carpenters (大工), 作料 / 飯料 / 取分 / 合計 (wage / food money / share / total):
  - one month: 245.450 + 107.000 + 19.900 = 372.350
  - the other month: 581.100 + 246.500 + 46.300 = 873.900
  - grand total: 372.35 + 873.9 = 1,246.250, a figure that appears in the table.
- Day labourers (日傭), 平日傭 / 手木 / 木遣 / 合計 (ordinary labourers / lever-men / log-haulers / total):
  - one month: 44.330 + 7.350 + 6.300 = 57.980
  - the other month: 84.630 + 111.825 + 5.400 = 201.855
  - grand total: 259.835, which also appears.
- Table 3 gives the man-days per 30 tsubo as 「707.0 … 23.57」 for carpenters and 「328.5 … 10.95」 for the next trade. The author lists the trades in the order 「大工・日傭・屋根葺師・木引・左官」, so the 328.5 is most likely the day labourers.
- Averages:
  - Carpenters: 1,246.25 / 707 = about 1.76 monme per man-day all-in. Of that, 作料 alone is 826.55 / 707 = about 1.17 and 飯料 is 353.5 / 707 = 0.50. That matches Hirai's 1.8 for 1653.
  - Day labourers: 259.835 / 328.5 = about 0.79 monme per man-day.
- Caveats:
  - The carpenter count may include apprentices (the Tenpo table counts 「内子供173人」 inside 大工), so 1.76 is a crew average.
  - The match of the 日傭 row to 328.5 rests on the order of the rows, not on a printed label.

"Day wage" is my reading, not the page's words. Hirai writes 大工手間賃 … 銀一匁八分 with no 一日; the per-day reading rests on 手間賃 being reckoned per 人工 (man-day), which is how the paper's own tables count.

For 1679 the account gives only a man-day count, no rate:
> 大工手間七百拾工餘入ル也
(translation) "Over 710 carpenter-days were used."

### 2. Kitano Hiroshi 北野博司, 「元和７年に行われた徳川期大坂城石垣修築工事の組織と技術」, 東北芸術工科大学紀要 33 (2026) - OSAKA, UNSKILLED, 1620-21
Archived: /diagram/.specify/source-archive/ab/abef9e51ac16/20261019T050511Z/text.txt (http://archives.tuad.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/tuad-bulletin33_1_KITANO.pdf). Footnote 26, put back in reading order from the two-column layout:
> （26）中川家では大坂での日用銀を1日1匁と見積もっているが（史136）、元和6年の黒田家では山からの石出し等に一人1匁3分、修羅引きに1匁5分（注24文献）としており、割安となっている。中川家は第1期工事で採石に難儀し、日用を雇っていた。元和6年正月には石丁場での日用賃金が一日につき8分〜1匁（史88・89）であり、元和7年正月には大坂で1匁、伏見（石垣崩し）で5分と定めていた（史136）。2月21日、現地からの第一報では、現時点では栗石の値段や日雇の賃金（今日まで七分、八分）が安価である（史156）としている。
(translation) "The Nakagawa house estimated day-labour silver (日用銀) in Osaka at 1 monme a day (doc. 136). The Kuroda house in Genna 6 (1620) set 1 monme 3 bu per man for bringing stone down from the hills and 1 monme 5 bu for sledge-hauling (修羅引き), so the Nakagawa figure is low. ... In the 1st month of Genna 6 the day wage at the quarry was 8 bu to 1 monme a day (docs. 88-89). In the 1st month of Genna 7 (1621) they set 1 monme in Osaka and 5 bu at Fushimi (demolishing walls) (doc. 136). On 2/21 the first report from the site said that cobble prices and the hired labourers' wages (7 to 8 bu so far) were cheap (doc. 156)."
The evidence is daimyo house documents of 1620-21 (Nakagawa of Oka, Kuroda of Fukuoka).

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a carpenter in Kyoto in 1653-56 and unskilled labour in Osaka in 1620-28. A Kyoto labourer rate for 1656 is my arithmetic only. Nothing for Mimasaka/Okayama.

- **Carpenter, Kyoto, 1653.** Hirai (2007) gives Myoshinji's Joo-era carpenter day wage as 銀一匁八分, rice and wage together (飯米作料とも). That is 1.8 monme of silver a day.
- **Carpenter and labourer, Kyoto, 1656 (my arithmetic).** The 1656 bath account gives about 1.76 monme per carpenter-day all-in and about 0.79 monme per day-labourer-day; see the caveats under source 1. It comes from the temple's own building account of 承応2 (1653), as reported by a modern architectural historian. This is the closest figure to the 1665 Mimasaka smith's list in date and region, but it is a figure from a large Kyoto temple, not a provincial castle town.
- **Unskilled labour, Osaka, 1620-28.** Hired labour (日用) at the Osaka castle works was paid 0.5 to 1.5 monme a day: 1620 quarry 8分-1匁, Kuroda's hauling 1匁3分-1匁5分, 1621 Osaka 1匁 and Fushimi 5分, 7-8分 reported as cheap, and 1.2 and 0.8 monme per man in 1628. These are wages on a huge state project and come four decades before 1665.
- **Same coin.** Both sets of figures are in Keicho silver (慶長銀, minted from 1601 until the Genroku recoinage of 1695), so they can be set beside 1665 monme without a recoinage adjustment. Source: the archived ja.wikipedia 三貨制度 copy, /diagram/.specify/source-archive/c5/c58cdc0ca3f7/20261019T161332Z/text.txt (「慶長銀（1601年）」「元禄銀（1695年）」).
- **Upper bound.** The c.1729 Edo bakufu rate of 1匁5分 (source 4), which carpenters said was too low to hire at, suggests the 1.5-2 monme range held into the 1720s. It is outside the window and the OCR is broken.
- **Nothing for Mimasaka/Okayama.** I found no domain craftsmen's tariff (作料定) and no figure for 1660-1700 in Okayama or Tsuyama. Okayama's 『藩法集1 岡山藩』 has a chapter 「賃銀雑事」 (contents page only, in the search results), but I did not read it.
