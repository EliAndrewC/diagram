# Where this feature comes from

Feature 238 returned the caravan inn glyph to a single story on the GM's ruling, and its reading found
a second attested analogue pointing the other way. The GM was asked whether that made the story count a
knob. Their words, in order.

## The ruling that removed the second story (2026-09-12)

> The caravan in does not a deliberate deviation. So if the record draws it as two story, then that is
> simply a mistake. If our attested analog reads it as a single story.

## The ruling that makes it a knob (2026-09-13)

> Ah, I said the two-story form was simply a mistake because of the 1 source; I agree that if there
> are opposing sources then it's a toggle.

## What the record holds (feature 238, `research.md` R2, R2a, R2b)

Two attested analogues, opposite on the one question:

- the north-Chinese wagon inn (大车店): the buildings in its yard uniformly single-story, with a cart
  yard behind, a long stable with one feeding trough, a grooms' lean-to and a well - `towns.html`
  fn-22 and fn-25;
- the Japanese post-station inn (旅籠): the preserved Ō-hatago Kashibaya of Okabe-juku, c. 1836,
  "wooden, two stories, tile-roofed", its second floor the guest rooms - fn-26. Nothing read describes a
  cart yard or a long stable at a hatago; a dictionary usage example does put "a large stable" behind a
  travelers' inn (the umayado arrangement), which is the inn-in-front, stable-behind layout the town
  maps draw.

Constitution XII: two supportable answers become a knob with per-settlement variance, never a choice.
