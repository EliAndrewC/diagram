## Items

- B67 B68 C110 D135 ACCURATE (post town, hatago size) + SILENT (county town count, Chinese inn size) - towns/320: a
  Tokaido station held 55 inns on average (Nakasendo 27), from Fujisawa's 45 to Miya's 247, one house in five or six at
  Kusatsu; a hatago was graded by frontage, small at 3 ken or less (~18 ft), middle ~4 ken (~24 ft), large 5+ ken
  (~30 ft), most of them small; one measured inn was two-story, 5.5 ken (~33 ft) wide, 57.25 tsubo (~2,040 sq ft);
  no page gives a hatago a yard or stable; no count for a non-relay county town, no size for a Chinese inn (absence
  notes) - the kinds: the post-station inn form of `caravan_inn_form` should be a street-front house 18-33 ft wide
  (not a compound with a yard); the town's ONE inn stays a labeled calibration (towns/050 now says so); if a town
  should ever carry several inns, the record supports hatago-sized houses on the main street, but gives no count.
- B70 ACCURATE (footprint) + KNOB (siting) - towns/330: canon gives the waystation (15-20 staff, 25-30 busy, 8-10
  horses, main hall/barracks/stables/gates/courtyard walls, ~10 miles apart); the analogue is the ancient ekika, whose
  one excavated station was a rammed-earth-walled compound ~80 m (~260 ft) a side with 7+ tile-roofed buildings, 20
  horses on a great road; many stations were run by the district office or kept in the station master's house; the
  Chinese walled post town (Jimingyi, 2,330 m around, 300+ garrison) is recorded and set aside as too big - the maps:
  a waystation is a walled compound ~260 ft square on the Imperial road; siting knob = standalone on the road vs. at a
  county seat beside the magistrate's compound; which towns carry one is the canon's ~10-mile spacing.
- B71 B72 D138 C45 ACCURATE + KNOB + SILENT - towns/340: the toiyaba was an office (sometimes part of the headman's
  house), one or two a station, at the middle or the ends, staffed by headman, deputies, clerk and dispatchers; the
  numbers 36 horses (1601), then 100/100 Tokaido, 50/50 Nakasendo, 25/25 elsewhere, taken from the residents; at a
  great passage hundreds of sukego horses and thousands of men gathered at it; no page describes a stable or horse
  yard at the office (absence); Jimingyi kept its horses in one horse compound on its back street; a castle town kept a
  tenma-cho near the castle's main gate - the maps: a relay office is a street-front house, not a yard; where relay
  horses stand is a knob (dispersed in householders' stables - this record's READING of the Japanese station - vs one
  horse compound, Chinese); a provincial city that keeps a relay keeps it in a quarter near the lord's main gate.
  Whether the setting's towns/cities keep a relay office at all (beside the Imperial waystation) is for the GM: the
  canon names only the waystation.
- B73 B74 D139 C114 ACCURATE (stall) + SILENT (inn/relay stable size, yard ground) - towns/350: a farmhouse stable
  was inner (a corner of the earth floor) or outer (with privy and shed), barred entrance, plank walls, loft room
  above, about 3 m (~10 ft) square in the Kinki region, wider in the north; no page measures an inn's or a relay's
  stable, or describes a stable yard's ground (absence notes); a waystation stable of 80-100 ft of stalls is labeled a
  GUESS - the maps: nothing changes for the gate yard (urban-features/080's beaten earth stays "this page's reading",
  now pointed at 350); a stable glyph can be sized at ~10 ft per stall.
- B69 C111 D137 ACCURATE + SILENT - towns/360: honjin one or two a station on average (none at some, five or six at
  others), single-story in principle, with gate, wide entrance, raised audience room and garden; Kusatsu's surviving
  one (among the largest) sits on ~4,719 m2 (~50,800 sq ft) with ~1,547 m2 of floor; the waki-honjin smaller, same
  standard, open to ordinary guests; Jimingyi kept a three-courtyard official lodging court; canon: busy waystations
  occasionally host ceremonial Imperial travelers; SILENT on where a honjin stood in the street and on a county
  yamen's guest house - the maps: a lord's lodging, where drawn, is a gated one-story house, the grandest on the
  street, sized between a waki-honjin and Kusatsu's site; a busy waystation may keep a lodging court.
