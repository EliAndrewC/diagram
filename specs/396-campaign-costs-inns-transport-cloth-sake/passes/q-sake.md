# Pass - sake and drink (#3 Q4) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

**Verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR the separate pieces.** I found Edo retail prices by the sho, Edo tavern and soba-shop prices by the go, a day wage near each, and a Qing price per jin with a wage. No source gives a shop price and a tavern price for the same sake, place and years, so I can't give you a reliable Edo markup ratio. For Qing China there is a stated markup (buying wine costs twice as much as brewing it), but I found nothing on tavern prices by the pot or bowl.

## Sources

**1. Kazusa province, Nagata village, 1842 (Tenpō 13).** Archived as a new read: `/tmp/l7r-pass/oamishirasato/` — https://adeac.jp/oamishirasato-city/texthtml/d100010/mp100010-100010/ht012240. This is a modern town history quoting a village document (「教諭願書議定連印帳」). It is the only place where a sake price and an unskilled wage appear in the same document. These are prices the shogunate ordered after the 1842 price-cut decree, not market prices, and the village is rural, not Edo city.
> 幕府は、その調査結果を基にして各組合村に公定の価格で売買するよう命じた。… 表38 天保13年手間賃・物価表 … 百姓日雇男 1人女 1人銭132文銭100文 … 地酒 1升銭100文伊丹 1升銭180文

(translation) "The shogunate ordered each village association to buy and sell at official prices… Table 38, Tenpō 13 wages and prices: farm day-labourer, man 132 mon, woman 100 mon … local sake 100 mon per sho; Itami sake 180 mon per sho."

- Per go, local sake is 10 mon and Itami sake 18 mon.
- One go is 7.6% (local) or 13.6% (Itami) of a man's day wage.
- The 1.8× gap between the two is a difference of grade, not a shop-to-tavern markup.

**2. Edo, Bunsei era (1824), shop prices per sho.** https://jp.sake-times.com/knowledge/culture/sake_g_lost-100years_09 — a modern article quoting the shopping guide 『江戸買物獨案内』 (1824).
> 酒売場の案内として、酒の銘柄と一升分の価格が載っていました。「大國酒代三百三十二文」「布袋酒代三百文」「明乃鶴代二百六十文」… 三年酒や龍田川、しらぎく、満願寺などほとんどの清酒が三匁…さらに安い高砂は百八十文

(translation) "As a guide to sake sellers, it lists brands with the price per sho: Daikoku 332 mon, Hotei 300 mon, Akenotsuru 260 mon… most clear sake, such as Tatsutagawa, Shiragiku and Mangan-ji, 3 monme [of silver]… the cheaper Takasago 180 mon."

- That is about 18–33 mon per go for named brands of good sake.
- 3 monme of silver is roughly 315–330 mon. That conversion is mine, not the article's.

**3. Edo, Bunsei era, the same guide.** https://negotoya.sakura.ne.jp/gosyu47.html — an amateur sake-history compilation, here citing 『江戸の居酒屋』 (伊藤善資編).
> 九年古酒は最高級で、一升が銀十匁(八百文～千文)もしたことが『江戸買物独(ひとり)案内』に記されている。こうなると、江戸の庶民が高級酒として飲んだ｢瀧水(たきみず)｣は、一升で三百文だから安い酒に分類されてしまう。

(translation) "Nine-year aged sake, the very best, cost 10 monme of silver (800–1,000 mon) per sho according to the Edo Kaimono Hitori Annai; by that measure Takimizu, which common Edo people drank as a fine sake, at 300 mon per sho counts as cheap."

**4. Edo, Bunsei era (1818–29), tavern prices per go.** Archived as a new read: `/tmp/l7r-pass/eonet16d/` — https://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-16d.html. A modern hobby site; it names no original source, only "a record", so treat it as the weakest piece.
> 文政年間(1818~29年)の記録によれば、酒の価格は酒一合が上酒(下り酒)で二十文～二十四文、下酒(地廻り酒)で八文～十文、酒の肴(さかな)は、特別なものを除けば一品が八文均一といわれている。このように、酒二、三合に肴が二、三品食べると百文程度となった。

(translation) "According to a record of the Bunsei era, one go of sake cost 20–24 mon for good sake shipped from Kamigata, 8–10 mon for poor local sake; snacks were mostly a flat 8 mon each. Two or three go with two or three dishes came to about 100 mon."

The same page also quotes 伊藤麻耶: 「浅草の盛り場には寿司屋が現れ、茶店では28～32文で酒が販売された」 (translation) "in the Asakusa entertainment district… teahouses sold sake at 28–32 mon." It gives no measure or date for this.

**5. Edo, Tenpō to Kaei (1830s–1853), soba-shop price per go.** https://nichimen.or.jp/know/zatsugaku/30/ — the noodle trade association's transcription of the soba-shop menu illustrated in 『守貞謾稿』. I did not see the original.
> 御膳大蒸籠代四十八文そば代十六文…天ぷら代三十二文…上酒一合代四十文

(translation) "Large steamer of fine soba 48 mon; soba 16 mon … tempura soba 32 mon … good sake, one go, 40 mon."

**6. Edo, Bunsei to Ansei, day wages.** Archived copy: https://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-8.html. Same hobby site, no original source named.
> 人夫日当が約200文の相場であった時…文政時代(1818～31年)には大工の手間賃が銀で3～5匁、銭に換算すると約320～530文、そして、安政二年(1855)の時点では銀六匁(約630文)

(translation) "When a labourer's day rate was about 200 mon… in Bunsei a carpenter earned 3–5 monme of silver, about 320–530 mon; by Ansei 2 (1855), 6 monme (about 630 mon)."

**7. Edo, before the Meireki fire of 1657.** Archived copy: https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B1%9F%E6%88%B8%E3%81%AE%E7%81%AB%E4%BA%8B, footnote [46]. I did not trace the source it cites.
> 明暦の大火後には、酒1升が40文から1000文に…なったという記録が残されている

(translation) "After the Meireki fire, there is a record that sake rose from 40 mon to 1,000 mon per sho." This gives only an early-Edo starting price of about 4 mon per go.

**8. Huzhou (Zhejiang), late Ming to early Qing (about 1640s–1658).** Archived copy: http://agri-history.ihns.ac.cn/books/bns.htm — 『補農書』 (沈氏農書 and 張履祥's supplement). This is the only source that states a markup outright.
> （二十二）…故苏、湖、绍、金、人家无不酿酒者，沽酒比之自酿，相去一倍，犹为廉价也。

(translation) "So in Suzhou, Huzhou, Shaoxing and Jinhua every household brews its own; bought wine against home-brewed differs by a factor of two — and that is still a cheap price."

> 每糟百斤，烧酒二十斤…零买每斤二分，顿买也有一分六厘

(translation) "Per 100 jin of lees, 20 jin of distilled spirit… bought retail 2 fen [of silver] per jin; bought in bulk, still 1.6 fen." That is a 25% retail-over-bulk markup.

> 旧规："生活人供酒，斗米买三十杓，谓之长行酒。"水多味淡，徒为店家出息。

(translation) "Old rule: for the field hands, one dou of rice buys thirty ladles, called 'long-run wine.' Watery and thin — it only makes the shop a profit."

> 长年，每一名工银五两，吃米五石五斗…柴酒一两二钱，通计十三两。

(translation) "A year-hand: wage 5 taels, rice eaten 5.5 shi… firewood and wine 1.2 taels, 13 taels in all."

**9. Qing, Jiaqing to Daoguang (1796–1850).** Archived as a new read: `/tmp/l7r-pass/dushu/` — https://www.dushu.com/showbook/101155/1039460.html. A modern survey, 白寿彝 (ed.) 『中国通史』 vol. 10, describing Qing prices; north China, partly from the Kong family accounts at Qufu.
> 康熙时，烧酒一斤一百文，干酒为一百五十文，这是曲阜孔府虚报之价。道光时，有大量报导酒价的资料，一般每斤均在三十到六十文之间，最少者仅有十余文。

(translation) "In the Kangxi reign, spirit at 100 wen per jin and dry wine at 150 are prices padded in the Kong mansion's accounts. In Daoguang there is much material on wine prices; generally 30–60 wen per jin, the cheapest only a little over 10."

> 清代嘉庆道光年间的各种工价与清初比几乎没有什么两样。日工工价最少者为三十文，最多达二百文，但一般的为五十文至七十文

(translation) "Wages in Jiaqing–Daoguang hardly differ from early Qing. A day-labourer earned at least 30 wen and up to 200, but generally 50–70 wen."

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR the separate pieces, not the markup in Japan.**

- **Edo retail, 1820s:** good named brands cost 180–332 mon per sho, or 18–33 mon per go (item 2).
- **Edo tavern, 1820s:** 20–24 mon per go for good sake and 8–10 mon for local sake (item 4), against a labourer's rough 200 mon a day (item 6). On those figures one go of good sake was about a tenth of a day's pay.
- **Edo soba shop, 1840s–50s:** 40 mon per go for good sake (item 5), about a fifth of that same 200 mon.
- **Only same-document pairing:** the 1842 Kazusa list (item 1) — 10 mon per go for local sake and 18 for Itami, against a man's 132 mon. These are ordered ceilings in a rural village, not Edo market prices.
- **Edo markup:** the tavern range of 20–24 mon overlaps the shop range of 18–33 mon, and the two sources cover different grades. Item 4's own source is unnamed. So I can't compute a reliable ratio. Morisada's 40 mon in the 1840s–50s is roughly 1.2–2× the 1820s shop price, but that comparison spans decades and different kinds of seller.
- **Qing:** wine cost 30–60 wen per jin against a day-labourer's 50–70 wen in 1796–1850, so a jin cost roughly a day's wage. One jin (about 600 g) is more than three go (about 540 ml), so per volume this is much dearer than Edo; that conversion is mine. For a markup, the early-Qing Huzhou text says bought wine cost twice home-brewed, and spirit cost 25% more retail than in bulk. Tavern prices by the pot or bowl are SILENT.
