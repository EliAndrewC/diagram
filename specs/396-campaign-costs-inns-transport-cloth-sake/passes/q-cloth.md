# Pass - cloth and kimono (#3 Q3) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

I found per-tan cotton prices and the tan's length for Japan, and per-bolt cotton and per-length silk prices for Qing China, each with a same-period wage beside it. For Japan, plain silk has one weak data point and pongee, used kimono, sewing and silk bedding have none. Bassino & Ma's linen figure looks high next to the cotton prices recorded for Japan.

## Sources

**1. Length of a tan.** Kotobank 反物 (Nipponica, 角山幸洋 and Heibonsha 世界大百科, 山下悦子): https://kotobank.jp/word/反物. Modern encyclopedias describing Edo practice.
> 小幅織物とくに着尺地では、鯨尺九寸5分（約36センチメートル）、長さ二丈六尺または二丈八尺とし、成人和服用布量を単位としていた … 疋は絹織物に、段、端は麻織物に使われたが、反は疋物を半分にした単位として現れ … 「一疋」とは、二反をさす

(translation) For narrow cloth, especially kimono cloth, the width was 9.5 sun in kujira measure (about 36 cm) and the length 2 jō 6 shaku or 2 jō 8 shaku, the cloth needed for one adult's kimono. 疋 was used for silk and 段/端 for hemp; 反 appeared as half a 疋 and spread from there, so "one 疋" means two tan.

> 江戸時代に小袖1枚分に要する布地の幅，長さが定まり，これを1反とするようになった。1反の長さは時代によりたびたび改定された

(translation) In the Edo period the width and length of cloth needed for one kosode were fixed, and this became one tan. The length was revised many times over the period.

**2. Edo cotton prices, 1791, and Mitsui's order specification, 1797.** 下向井紀彦「寛政年間における三井越後屋の木綿仕入状況とその特質」, 三井文庫論叢 47: https://mitsui-bunko.or.jp/archives/pdf_data/mbr-47_shimomukai.pdf. `make source-pages` could not read the PDF, so I used curl and pdftotext. The PDF's columns are interleaved, so these are the extracted fragments, not continuous text.
> 勢州木綿は一反あたり一七匁余と一三匁七分余であり、向店で仕入れる尾州木綿は六匁から一二匁余、三州木綿も六匁から八匁程度である。その他、大坂の河内木綿一二匁や関東の真岡木綿の一三匁六分余など

(translation) Ise cotton was over 17 monme and over 13.7 monme a tan. Owari cotton bought by the Edo store was 6 to just over 12 monme, Mikawa cotton about 6 to 8 monme. Besides these, Osaka's Kawachi cotton was 12 monme and Kantō's Mooka cotton just over 13.6 monme.

These are Edo cotton wholesalers' selling prices, reported to the town magistrate in a 寛政3 (1791) price inquiry. The author warns: 「問屋仲間は幕府に対して売値段を低く記載して提出するであろうから、数字そのものの史料批判は必要」 (translation: the wholesalers' guild probably reported low selling prices to the shogunate, so the figures themselves need source criticism). The same paper says Hakushū cotton bought by Mitsui cost 「概ね一反あたり五匁前後」 (about 5 monme a tan).

Mitsui's 1797 order specification for undyed cotton, joined from lines 850 and 879 of the extract:
> 地巾九寸已上、惣尺二丈七尺より短キ者一切無用 … 幅は九寸以上、尺は二丈七尺より短い物は不可

(translation) Width at least 9 sun; nothing shorter than 2 jō 7 shaku in total length.

**3. Kansai cotton and silk prices, 1692–1790.** NDL Reference Collaboration case (大阪府立中之島図書館): https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?page=ref_view&id=1000187424. It summarises 小柳津信郎『近世賃金物価史史料』 pp. 229–234.
> 元禄5年羽二重半疋（1反） 45匁の地絹「世間胸算用」 / 享保元年（1716）白木綿1疋（2反）四ツ宝銀ニて売買之筋ハ14～5匁…高値ハ1疋40匁ぐらい迄上り申候 / 天明元年（1781） 1反上 7匁、（1反）中 5匁4～5分、（1反）下 4匁「赤穂市史」 / 寛政2年（1790） 1反上 9匁、（1反）中 7～8匁、（1反）下 6匁「赤穂市史」 / 寛政2年（1790） 1反 9匁より13匁「西宮市史」

(translation)
- 1692: habutae, half a 疋 (one tan), 45 monme, plain silk (from *Seken Munezan'yō*).
- 1716: white cotton was 14–15 monme a 疋 (two tan) in Yotsuhō silver, rising as high as about 40 monme a 疋.
- 1781 (Akō): one tan 7 monme for top grade, 5.4–5.5 for middle, 4 for low.
- 1790 (Akō): 9 top, 7–8 middle, 6 low.
- 1790 (Nishinomiya): 9 to 13 monme a tan.

The page also names the main series: 三井文庫『近世後期における主要物価の動態』, with 「河内木綿の一反ニ付銀匁」 for Osaka from 1757.

**4. Japanese unskilled wage and Bassino & Ma's linen.** Archived copy of Bassino & Ma, REH (`reh_japanese_wages_bassino_and_ma.pdf`).
- Table 3, Kyoto 1745–54: "Linen (m) … 5.83"; "Nominal wages (grams of silver) … 2.82".
- Table A.1, daily nominal wages in grams of silver: "1791 2.55".
- Conversion: "1 silver monme = 3.11 g of pure silver".
- Where the linen price comes from: "For price of cotton linen, we used price in Osaka in 1840–1849 (Miyamoto, 1963, pp. 208–210)".

**5. Edo craftsman's clothing budget, Bunsei era (1818–30).** eonet page quoting 『文政年間漫録』: https://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-8c.html. It is a secondary popular page.
> 稼ぎは、１日の手間賃が銀四匁二分で、それに飯米料(弁当代)一匁二分が加算される … 衣服代も百二十匁(約20万円)かかった

(translation) His day rate was 4 monme 2 fun of silver, plus 1 monme 2 fun for his meal rice. Clothing also cost 120 monme a year.

This is a carpenter's household of three, so it is a skilled wage. The same page lists 「木綿生地一反 600文」 (one tan of cotton cloth, 600 mon) with no source, so it is not evidence.

**6. Songjiang cotton cloth and pongee, 1640s–1690s.** Archived copy of 葉夢珠『閱世編』 (`ye-mengzhu-yueshibian`), 食貨五 and the clothing sections.
> 標布…每疋約值銀一錢五六分，最精不過一錢七八分至二錢而止…順治八年，價至每疋三錢三分。十一年十二月間，每疋價至四五錢，今大概以二錢為上下也 … 小布，闊不過尺餘，長不過十六尺 … 二十一年壬戌，中機布每疋價銀三錢上下。二十三年甲子…俱改木棉標布，每疋上上者價仍紋銀二錢上下，粗者一錢三、四、五分而已

(translation)
- Biaobu cloth was worth about 0.15–0.16 tael a bolt, the finest no more than 0.17–0.18 up to 0.2.
- Shunzhi 8 (1651) it reached 0.33 a bolt. In the 12th month of Shunzhi 11 (1654) it reached 0.4–0.5 a bolt. Now it is roughly 0.2.
- Xiaobu ("small cloth") is no more than a little over one chi wide and no more than 16 chi long.
- Kangxi 21 (1682): zhongji cloth about 0.3 tael a bolt.
- Kangxi 23 (1684): weavers all switched to cotton biaobu. The best is still about 0.2 tael of fine silver a bolt, coarse only 0.13–0.15.

> 山東繭綢…今最上者，價不過錢許一尺，甚而有三四分一尺者

(translation) Shandong pongee: now the best costs no more than about 0.1 tael a chi, and some is as little as 0.03–0.04 tael a chi.

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the price per tan of cotton, the tan's length, the silk-to-cotton ratio from one weak Japanese pairing plus Qing official prices, and Qing ready-made garments. Japanese plain silk rests on one data point taken from fiction; pongee, used kimono, sewing and silk bedding are **SILENT** for Japan.

- **Tan length.** A tan was 2 jō 6 shaku to 2 jō 8 shaku in kujira measure, about 9.85–10.6 m, by about 36 cm wide. Mitsui's 1797 minimum was 2 jō 7 shaku, about 10.2 m, by 9 sun.
- **Cotton, Japan.** Plain cotton ran about 4–9 monme a tan in Akō (1781–90) and 6–17 monme in Edo (1791 wholesalers' prices, probably understated).
  - Beside Bassino & Ma's 1791 wage of 2.55 g, about 0.82 monme a day, that is roughly 5–11 days' unskilled wage at Akō prices and 7–21 days at Edo prices.
  - Bassino & Ma's 5.83 g/m works out to about 59 g, about 19 monme, for a 10.2 m tan at their 3.11 g a monme. That is at or above the top of the 1791 Edo range and two to three times Akō's middle grade. It is their 1840s Osaka price applied to 1745–54, so it should be checked against the Kawachi-cotton series in 三井文庫『近世後期における主要物価の動態』 before the two numbers are set side by side.
- **Silk, Japan.** The only habutae price is 45 monme a tan in 1692, and it comes from Saikaku's fiction via a compiler. Next to white cotton at 7–7.5 monme a tan in 1716 (debased silver), it gives a ratio of about 6:1. It is suggestive, not firm.
- **Clothing budget, Japan.** In Bunsei Edo a skilled carpenter's family spent 120 monme a year on clothing, about 22 days of his 5.4-monme day.
- **Cotton, China.** In Songjiang (1640s–1680s) a bolt of cotton cost 0.13–0.2 tael in normal years and 0.33–0.5 tael in the 1650s spike. Beside Allen's 0.09 tael a day for Suzhou around 1700, a normal-year bolt is about 1.5–2.2 days' wage.
  - Pomeranz's 0.4–0.5 tael a bolt, used by Allen, is two to three times Ye Mengzhu's figure.
  - Ye gives no length for the standard biaobu, only that xiaobu was at most 16 chi.
- **Silk and garments, China.** In Beijing in 1736, plain silk cost 0.6 tael a zhang against 0.188 for blue biaobu and 0.09 for coarse white cloth, so silk to cotton is about 3:1 to 7:1 by length. Silk was also wider, 1.4 chi against 0.9.
  - A labourer's day was about 0.094 tael. On that wage a zhang of cotton cost about 2 days and a zhang of silk about 6.4 days.
  - A printed-cotton padded jacket cost about 5.8 days, a silk shirt about 23 days.
  - Songjiang pongee in the 1680s–90s was about 0.03–0.1 tael a chi, against Beijing plain silk at 0.06 a chi.
- **What I searched for the silent items.** Archive terms: 古着, 仕立, 紬, 布団 and 蒲団. Japanese queries: 古着 値段, 仕立賃/縫賃, 古手, 質物, 紬 一反 匁, 布団 値段. Pages read:
  - nippon.com Morisada article: the price it quotes is for used umbrellas.
  - tokaiedu used-clothing article and Kotobank and ja.wikipedia 古着: no prices.
  - ja.wikipedia 奢侈禁止令: only luxury price ceilings on kosode (silver 200–500 monme).
  - Tanimoto's LSE paper: no cloth prices.
  - Suzuki's J-STAGE paper on how silk and cotton were priced: a scanned PDF with no readable text.
  - Fujiwara's Hitotsubashi thesis: the host would not resolve.
  - A Japanese blog's "used cotton kimono 400–600 mon, new 1–2 ryō" and the "futon 30–100 ryō" claims are unsourced and not reported as evidence.
