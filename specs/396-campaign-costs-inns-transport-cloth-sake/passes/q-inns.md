# Pass - inns and stabling (#3 Q1) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

I found Edo prices for a night at both kinds of inn, but nothing on what feeding or stabling a horse cost, and no Qing inn price. Fuller hatago and kichin-yado figures come from a few towns and regulations, not a full series along the Tokaido. The best day-wage source I found gives the unskilled wage in grams of silver, not in mon. The only "about 200 mon a day" figures come from popular websites.

**Verdicts**
- Hatago with two meals: **ATTESTED**.
- Kichin-yado (floor and firewood only): **ATTESTED**.
- Horse for a night: **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the regulated kichin charge per horse. That charge was for firewood and hot water. Fodder or stabling for a night: **SILENT**.
- Qing inn price and fodder: **SILENT**.

## Sources

**1. Hatago, Tokaido, 1790 to the 1850s**
- https://www.lib.mie-u.ac.jp/r_and_d/gallery/gallery3/img/itiranhyou.pdf ("道中日記に見る津の記述", a table of travel-diary entries about Tsu, a castle town on the Ise road). The ledger tool couldn't read the PDF, so I read it through WebFetch's saved copy plus pdftotext.
  - 寛政2 (1790), Tsu, from 川瀬雅男『西国道中記』: 「ひやうたん屋ト申旅籠屋よし 是へ参候処 甚相込故 是より差図ニ而加美屋 百三十文宛」 — "the hatago called Hyōtan-ya was good, but it was very crowded, so on their direction [we went] to Kami-ya, 130 mon each" (translation).
  - 文政6 (1823), Tsu, from 「伊勢道中記」 (福島県歴史資料館, 庄司吉之助家文書): 「此所八日晩泊、木銭五十文」 — "stayed here the night of the 8th, kisen (firewood fee) 50 mon" (translation). This is the kichin-type price in a town.
- https://imidas.jp/jidaigeki/detail/L-57-131-08-04-G252.html (山本博文, 2009; it gives no date for the prices): 「東海道の旅籠の宿泊料は、1泊につき、上の部類で200文、下では120文、木賃宿なら50文以下で、中山道では、上の宿が150文ほど、東北地方なら木賃宿が16文というところもあった。」 — "On the Tokaido a hatago night cost 200 mon at the upper end and 120 mon at the lower; a kichin-yado 50 mon or less. On the Nakasendo the better inns were about 150 mon; in Tohoku some kichin-yado charged 16 mon" (translation).
- https://www.ktr.mlit.go.jp/yokohama/tokaido/02_tokaido/04_qa/index7/answer7.htm (government highway office page, mid-19th century, Ise pilgrimage by the Tokaido): 「一九世紀半ばの段階では旅籠代は一泊二〇〇文程度が相場だったようです。また、昼食代は七〇～八〇文」 — "by the mid-19th century the going rate for a hatago was about 200 mon a night; lunch 70-80 mon" (translation).
- https://coin-walk.site/J029.htm (a popular price table, about 1820). It lists 小野武雄『江戸物価事典』 and other books as sources. 「旅籠賃中等（１泊２食付き）２００文」 — "middle-grade hatago, one night with two meals, 200 mon" (translation).

**2. Kichin charge with the horse rate (regulated, 1611-1711)**
- Archived copy of https://kotobank.jp/word/木賃宿-51029:
  - 改訂新版 世界大百科事典 (丸山雍成): 「1611年（慶長16）の幕府法令は，木賃代を人は銭3文，馬は6文と規定するが，これは馬の使用湯量が多いためである。」 — "the shogunate's 1611 law set the kichin at 3 mon a person and 6 mon a horse, because a horse used more hot water" (translation).
  - 日本大百科全書 (山内まみ): 「1611年（慶長16）には人は銭3文、馬は6文と規定されたが、のちにその差は縮まった。また1665年（寛文5）には主人16文、従者6文。」 — "in 1611, 3 mon a person and 6 mon a horse, the gap later narrowed; in 1665, 16 mon for a master and 6 mon for a servant" (translation).
  - 山川 日本史小辞典: 「1658年(万治元)には薪代として銭6文であった。」 — "in 1658 it was 6 mon as firewood fee" (translation).
- https://adeac.jp/shinagawa-city/texthtml/d000010/mp000010/ht002820 (品川区史, the Shinagawa ward history):
  - On the 1611 Tokaido rules: 「木銭は鐚（びた）三文、馬は一疋に鐚六文」 — "kisen 3 mon in bita coin; a horse, 6 bita per head" (translation).
  - On 1663: 「房銭ハ其薪柴ト共ニ銭六文馬ハ銭八文」 — "room money including firewood, 6 mon; a horse, 8 mon" (translation). The author says this 房銭 still means a firewood-fee stay, not a hatago stay.
  - On 1686: 「木銭として主人の分は一二文、下人は六文」 — "as kisen, 12 mon for the master, 6 for a servant" (translation).
- http://bird.blue.coocan.jp/forarai/arai_syuku_kinou.html (a local-history site on Arai post town): 「正徳元年の(1711) 新居宿元賃銭（公定賃銭）…宿泊賃主人一人３５召仕一人１７馬一疋３５」 — "1711 official rates at Arai: lodging, master 35, servant 17, one horse 35 [mon]" (translation).

**3. Kichin-yado in Edo city (1764-1843)**
- Archived kotobank 木賃宿, 世界大百科事典 (南和男): 「1843年（天保14）の江戸では下谷山崎町などに木賃宿があり…そこでは木銭はなく，屋根代として1日1人24文，布団借用者は別に16～24文を支払った。」 — "in Edo in 1843 there were kichin-yado in Shitaya Yamazaki-chō and elsewhere… there was no kisen; a 'roof fee' of 24 mon per person per day, and those borrowing bedding paid another 16-24 mon" (translation).
- Archived copy of https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/木賃宿 (Meiwa era, 1764-72, citing 草間八十雄): 「宿泊者はみずから飯を炊き、薪代すなわち木賃として鐚3文を支払った。」 — "lodgers cooked their own rice and paid 3 bita mon as firewood fee, i.e. kichin" (translation).

**4. Day wage beside the prices**
- Archived Bassino & Ma, https://www.econhistdbm.com/uploads/8/1/9/7/81977286/reh_japanese_wages_bassino_and_ma.pdf (Kyoto unskilled day laborers, from the Mitsui records):
  - "nominal wages increased from 2.82 to 4.5 g of silver between 1750 and 1884"
  - "a day's work by an unskilled laborer could purchase about 2 kg of rice on average in the Tokugawa era"
  - These are in grams of silver, not mon. I did not convert them; turning them into mon needs a silver-to-copper exchange rate for each period.
- Archived https://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-8.html (a popular page that names no source; Edo, about 1804-30):
  - 「人夫日当が約200文の相場」 — "a laborer's day pay ran about 200 mon" (translation)
  - 「大工・左官・土方などが１日320~540文、野菜の棒手振りが１日100~200文程度」 — "carpenters, plasterers and navvies 320-540 mon a day; vegetable peddlers about 100-200 mon" (translation)
- coin-walk (above), carpenter's day pay: 文化・文政 「銀3～5匁（330～550文）」, 慶応 「銀5～6匁（350～420文）」. These are skilled-craftsman figures, not unskilled.
- Qing comparison, archived Allen et al., https://www.lse.ac.uk/asset-library/information/wp123.pdf (18th century):
  - "coastal Fujian province fetching the lowest 0.030 taels for unskilled labourers"
  - "daily earnings of 0.035 to 0.045 taels per day"
  - These are in silver taels; I did not convert them to copper cash.
- Qing inn structure only, archived http://jlrbszb.dajilin.com/pad/paper/c/202604/25/content_1049361.html (a modern newspaper recollection about carters' inns, no date and no figures): 「大车店只收宿费、饭费和草料费」 — "carters' inns charged only for lodging, meals and fodder" (translation).

## Reading

**ATTESTED.**
- **Hatago:** from 1790 to the mid-19th century a night with two meals on the Tokaido and Ise roads cost about 120-200 mon. The one primary diary entry is 130 mon at Tsu in 1790. The secondary sources give 200 mon for good inns and 120 for poor ones, and 150 on the Nakasendo.
- **Kichin-yado:** about 50 mon in the 19th century (Tsu, 1823: kisen 50 mon). Edo city in 1843 had no kisen; lodgers paid 24 mon for the "roof" plus 16-24 mon for bedding. Early in the period it was only 3-16 mon (1611-1686).
- **Against the day wage:** the only "about 200 mon" laborer figure is from an unsourced popular page. On that figure a good hatago night is about a full day's pay, and an urban kichin bed about a fifth to a quarter of it. Bassino & Ma's Kyoto unskilled wage is about 2.8 g of silver a day (1750), or about 2 kg of rice. I could not set that in mon without an exchange rate.

**Horse:** ATTESTED ONLY FOR the regulated per-horse kichin. It was 6 mon in 1611 (twice a person) and 8 mon in 1663 (person 6). At Arai in 1711 it was 35 mon, equal to the master's rate and twice the servant's. The encyclopedia says this charge was for hot water, not feed. I found no per-night price for hay or stabling.

**China: SILENT.** I searched:
- Archive terms: 客店, 店钱, 骡马店, 草料, 大车店. The 大车店 texts and the jiemian and visitbeijing pages have no figures.
- Chinese web queries on 清代 客店 店钱 文 and 草料, and English queries on 19th-century North China inns.
- Williamson, *Old Highways in China* (archive.org full text): no inn charge in it.
