# Pass - hiring transport (#3 Q2) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

I found the official stage rates and the return-trip rule for Edo Japan, but not the rest. There is no unskilled day wage in mon for any period. There is no daily price for a hired horse with its driver, nor for a 大八車 or 牛車. On the Qing side I found only one 1863 private mule hire, and no official 脚价.

## Sources that bear on it

**1. Tachibana Toshio (橘敏夫), 「寛文5年における江戸幕府の陸上交通政策」** — https://aichiu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1320/files/%E6%A9%98%E3%80%80%E6%95%8F%E5%A4%AB.pdf (period: 1658–1665; I checked the quotes against the page images on pp. 70 and 73)
- From the 浜松宿御役町由来記: 「一万治元戌年御条目之表，宿賃薪代壱人ニ付六文，馬ニハ十文ニ御定被下置候，同三子年東海道五拾六継御高札相立，駄賃銭壱里十文増，人足賃五文増御定被下置」 — "By the 1658 regulations, lodging-and-firewood was set at 6 mon a person and 10 mon a horse. In the same 3rd year, a 子 year [my reading: 万治3, 1660], notice boards went up on the 56 Tōkaidō stages, and the horse fare was raised 10 mon per ri and the porter fare 5 mon." (translation) This is a raise, not a tariff. The base it was added to is not in the passage.
- 「『浜松宿御役町由来記』による値上げ後の人馬賃銭は，舞坂宿まで本馬83文・軽尻54文，見付宿まで本馬151文・軽尻104文であった」 — "After the [1665] increase, the rates were 本馬 83 mon and 軽尻 54 mon to Maisaka, and 本馬 151 mon and 軽尻 104 mon to Mitsuke." (translation)
- Takamiya 1665 statement: 「一鳥居本江壱里半　四拾文　廿七文　弐拾文　内弐文ハ大堀川小板ノ増」 — "To Toriimoto, 1.5 ri: 40 mon, 27 mon, 20 mon; 2 mon of this is a surcharge for the small Ōhorigawa bridge." (translation) The page has no column heads. That the three figures are 本馬, 軽尻 and 人足 is my reading, from the ratio of roughly 1 : ⅔ : ½.
- 1665 Nakasendō order on porters versus horses: 「一五貫目迄ハから尻同前ニ駄賃銭取可申候，其より重キ荷物ハ本駄賃銭可為同前事，一夜ニ入，から尻馬ハ本駄賃銭同前ニ可取之事，一本銭ニ弐割増之駄賃銭取候義は…」 — "Up to 5 kan, charge the same fare as a 軽尻; heavier loads pay the full 本馬 fare. At night a 軽尻 horse is charged the full 本馬 fare. The fare may be the base rate plus 20%…" (translation)
- Side figure, which is lodging and not fare: 「木銭ニ而泊り銭之事…主人壱人拾六文…馬壱疋拾六文…下々壱人六文」 — "Lodging at firewood rate: master 16 mon, horse 16 mon, servant 6 mon [a night]." (translation)

**2. Yamamoto Mitsumasa (山本光正), 「東海道における人馬賃銭について」, 法政史学 21** — https://hosei.ecats-library.jp/da/repository/00010841/shigaku_21_yamamoto.pdf (period: 1690–1853; quotes checked against the page images on pp. 89, 91 and 100, because the text layer misreads 本馬 as 木馬)
- 「正徳元年（一七一一）に元賃銭が決定され…正徳の元賃銭は、元禄三年（一六九○）に決められた人馬賃銭が、宝永四年（一七○七）に三割増になると、この賃銭がそのまま継続されたもので」 — "The base rate was fixed in 1711. It is the 1690 rate, raised 30% in 1707 and then kept unchanged." (translation)
- 「御定賃銭による人馬を使いきってしまったときや、御定賃銭で人馬を使用することができなかった者は、相対賃銭で人馬を継立てたが、相対賃銭は物価の値上り等に比例して値上りしており、御定賃銭の二倍が相場であったようである」 — "When the official-rate men and horses were used up, or for those not entitled to the official rate, men and horses were hired at the negotiated rate. That rate rose with prices, and **it seems** to have run at about twice the official rate." (translation)
- Maisaka stage document, under the heading 「人馬賃銭五割増」 (弐割増 from 文政二年 for ten years, plus 三割増): 「浜松宿江道法弐里半拾町　一本馬壱疋　此賃銭百八拾六文　五割増…百弐拾五文　元賃銭」 — "To Hamamatsu, 2 ri 10 chō; one 本馬; fare 186 mon at the 50% surcharge … of which 125 mon is the base rate." (translation) Period: 1820s; the article's distribution table is for 文政8 (1825). 125 × 1.5 is 187.5; the document says 186.
- On the return trip, Shinagawa asking for a 50% surcharge in 1853: 「品川宿は江戸へ出向いた人馬の戻りの稼ぎがないこと」 — "Shinagawa's men and horses that go into Edo have no earnings on the way back." (translation)

**5. Archived 改訂 街道と宿場の豆知識** — https://akiyama.my.coocan.jp/kaiteikaidoumame.pdf (a modern summary)

**6. Wage (Japan), weak** — archived https://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-8.html. This is a modern popular page with no sources, describing the 1804–1830s.
- 「１日の稼ぎは、大工・左官・土方などが１日320~540文、野菜の棒手振りが１日100~200文程度であった」 — "Daily earnings: carpenters, plasterers, labourers 320–540 mon; vegetable hawkers about 100–200 mon." (translation) These are skilled trades and a peddler's earnings, not an unskilled wage.

**7. China: Alexander Michie, *The Siberian Overland Route* (London 1864)** — archived https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45167/45167-h/45167-h.htm (journey August 1863)
- "We thereupon engaged eight pack-mules at four taels[2] each, and three mule-litters at eight taels each, to convey us and our belongings to Chan-kia-kow, distant about 400 li, or a four days' journey." **8. China wage: Allen, Bassino, Ma et al., LSE WP 123/09** — archived https://www.lse.ac.uk/asset-library/information/wp123.pdf
- "the wage level for Beijing and Canton was set in 1700 … 0.0897 and 0.0835 taels respectively". The appendix (Gamble's rural Beijing fuel-store series) gives 1865 as 265 wen, 5,180 cash per tael, 0.026 taels a day. The paper calls this series "very low".

## Reading

**ATTESTED** for the Edo official stage rates. **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the 宿継 relay on the return question. **SILENT** on carts, day hire and the Japanese unskilled wage. **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** one private 1863 through-hire on the Qing side.

**Japan, per ri.** The per-ri figures below are my arithmetic: 1 ri = 36 chō, so 2 ri 10 chō = 2.78 ri.

| Period | Source | 本馬 | 軽尻 | 人足 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1665, Takamiya | Tachibana | ≈27 | ≈18 | ≈13 (columns my reading) |
| 1665, Hamamatsu–Maisaka | Tachibana | ≈30 | ≈19 | – |
| 1711 base, still in force in the 1820s | Yamamoto | ≈45 | – | – |
| 1820s, with 50% surcharge | Yamamoto | ≈67 | – | – |

All figures are mon per ri. Two caveats on the 1665 Hamamatsu row: I took the 2.78 ri distance from Yamamoto's 1820s document, and that document reads 舞坂→浜松 while Tachibana's figures are 浜松→舞坂.

- **Ratios.** 軽尻 ran about ⅔ of 本馬 and a porter about half. By the 1665 rule a porter's 5 kan paid the 軽尻 fare, and a 軽尻 at night paid the 本馬 fare.
- **Who paid what.** Holders of 朱印状 or 証文 rode free. Shogunate business paid the 御定賃銭. Everyone else paid 相対賃銭, which Yamamoto hedges as "seems" to have run about twice the official rate. For an ordinary 1820s hirer that is about 135 mon per ri for a 本馬.
- **The return trip.** Fares were paid stage by stage and one way only. The horse changed at every station, and the unpaid return leg was the station's loss, as in Shinagawa's 1853 complaint. Nothing I found covers through-hire (付け通し, 中馬) or hiring by the day.
- **Wage beside the fare.** I have no sourced unskilled day wage in mon for any period. The one figure is item 6, a modern unsourced page giving 320–540 mon for craftsmen in the 1820s. Against that, a 2.78-ri stage at the 1820s official rate (186 mon) is about half a craftsman's day, and at about twice that, the market rate is about a full day.
- **Carts.** The archived 大八車 entries give only the 1701 licence levy on carts (極印賃), not a hire rate.

**China.** Michie's hire works out to about 1 tael per mule per day, or 1 tael per 100 li; the return was folded into the engagement and is not stated. That is roughly 11 days of a Beijing unskilled wage at the 1700 level (0.09 tael), or about 38 days of the 1865 rural Beijing series (0.026 tael). The wage and the hire are from different years and places, and it is a foreigner's fare that he himself called "slightly exorbitant".

I found no official per-li 脚价 and no daily cart rate. The archived 清代北京旗人社会中的民人 text has bannermen hiring carts to collect rice (「找雇贱价车辆」), but no price. The 河防一覽 脚价 passage is Ming straw haulage, which does not bear. The web query 清代 雇车 每日 价 京钱 骡车 脚价 每里 银 乾隆 则例 returned nothing relevant.

**Not usable:** the archived kotobank 大八車 and ja.wikipedia 大八車 pages (no hire rate); kotobank 駄賃, 日雇, 日傭取り and 賃金 (definitions only); the Saito 1993 and Saito–Takashima 2020 papers (a wage index only, and medieval wages); the Ōiso and postal-museum PDFs. The guessed kotobank address for 御定賃銭 led to an unrelated entry, and kotobank 相対賃銭 returned a 404. All of these are recorded on the ledger.
