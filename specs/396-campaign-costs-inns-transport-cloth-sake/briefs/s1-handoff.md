## Figures and verdicts

### 0259 - a night at an inn
- Hatago, Tokaido: 200 mon at the better inns, 120 at the lesser; Nakasendo better inns about 150 - ATTESTED (undated,
  a historian's dictionary entry). Mid-19th-century going rate about 200 mon a night on the Tokaido road to Ise, lunch
  70-80 - ATTESTED, hedged by its source ("seems").
- Meals included: how many - SILENT (pages say only "with meals"; "two meals" only on an unsourced popular table).
- In a town: Tsu 1790, 130 mon each at the Kami-ya - ATTESTED ONLY FOR Tsu. In a big city - SILENT.
- Hatago five times a kichin-yado or more, Tenpo era - ATTESTED.
- Kichin-yado: 1611 law 3 mon a person; 1658 6 mon; 1663 order 6 mon with firewood; 1665 master 16, servant 6 - ATTESTED;
  1686 intendant's clerks' firewood fee in villages, master 12, servant 6 - ATTESTED (not an inn's charge). Tsu 1823 firewood
  fee 50 mon; Tokaido kichin 50 or less, northeast 16 (undated) - ATTESTED. Edo 1843: no firewood fee, roof fee 24 mon a
  day, bedding 16-24 more - ATTESTED.
- Horse: only the firewood fee, 6 mon in 1611 (twice a person's, for the hot water), 8 in 1663 - ATTESTED ONLY FOR the
  firewood fee. Feed or stabling - SILENT.
- Wage: unskilled in mon - SILENT. Nearest: Kyoto day laborer (Mitsui), grams of silver: 1791 2.55 (1 year after Tsu
  1790; 1762-1790 missing from the series), 1823 4.17, 1843 2.80 (Edo level with Kyoto from 1818, 0250), 1850 3.00.
- Qing inn and fodder - SILENT.
- Left out: Tsu 1867, 6 monme (or 1.6) at the Wakasa-ya, in the same Mie table (silver; the source-reader found it after the
  page was written); Arai 1711 (horse 35 mon, equal to the master's), Tachibana's 1658 per-horse 10 mon and 1665 16 mon -
  each a candidate for a later edit.

### 0260 - hiring a horse or a porter
- Fares paid stage by stage, horses changed at each station - ATTESTED (freight). Shogunal warrant or senior councilor's
  certificate free; shogunate business at the low official fare - ATTESTED. Negotiated fare "seems" about twice the
  official - ATTESTED, hedged.
- 1665 Hamamatsu, after the stations' rise: pack horse 83 / light horse 54 to Maisaka, 151 / 104 to Mitsuke - ATTESTED;
  the 20% rise of the 10th month withdrawn within the year - ATTESTED. Per ri about 30 / 19 - project's arithmetic, at the
  1820s distance (2 ri half and 10 cho = 2.78 ri).
- 1665 Takamiya (Nakasendo), 1.5 ri to Toriimoto: 40, 27, 20 mon - ATTESTED; as pack horse, light horse, porter - this
  project's reading; about 27, 18, 13 a ri - arithmetic.
- 1665 Nakasendo order: porter up to 5 kan; up to 5 kan at the light horse's fare, above at the pack horse's; light horse
  at night as a pack horse - ATTESTED.
- 1711 base fare (the 1690 fare + 30% of 1707) - ATTESTED. Maisaka-Hamamatsu pack horse: base 125 mon (about 45 a ri),
  with the 1819 and 1824 surcharges (50%) 186 mon (about 67 a ri) - ATTESTED; per ri arithmetic.
- Return: one way on the relay - this project's reading of the stage-by-stage rule; Shinagawa 1853, no earnings on the way
  back - ATTESTED. Return on a day hire - SILENT. Horse with driver by the day, cart by the day - SILENT.
- Wage: unskilled in mon - SILENT. Nearest: Osaka 1628 0.8-1.2 monme a man (0257; 37 years before 1665); Kyoto 1741
  2.57 g silver (30 years after 1711); Kyoto 1824-1829 3.02-3.97 g.
- Qing: Michie, Peking to Kalgan, August 1863, 8 pack mules at 4 taels, 3 litters at 8, four days, 400 li, "slightly
  exorbitant" - ATTESTED ONLY FOR one private foreigner's hire; about 1 tael a mule a day, about 11 days of Beijing's 1700
  wage (0.0897 tael) - arithmetic. The 1865 rural Beijing series (0.026) is named but not used for a ratio (the study uses
  its trend only). Return - SILENT. Official per-li fares - SILENT.

### 0261 - cloth and clothing
- Tan: 9 sun 5 bu wide (about 36 cm), 2 jo 6 or 2 jo 8 shaku long (9.85-10.6 m) before metric bolts - ATTESTED (undated);
  fixed in the Edo period as one kosode's cloth, revised many times - ATTESTED; hiki = two tan - ATTESTED. Mitsui's 1790s
  orders: at least 9 sun by 2 jo 7 shaku (about 34 cm by 10.2 m) - ATTESTED.
- Cotton a tan: Ako 1781 7 / 5.4-5.5 / 4 monme, 1790 9 / 7-8 / 6; Nishinomiya 1790 9-13 - ATTESTED. Edo wholesalers 1791
  about 3 to 17.5 (Owari 6-12, Mikawa 6-8, Kawachi 12, Mooka 13.6) - ATTESTED, probably understated (its historian).
- Wage: Ako and Edo 1781-1791 - SILENT; nearest Kyoto 1791 2.55 g = 0.82 monme (same metal); Ako cotton 5-11 days, Edo 4-21
  - arithmetic.
- Bassino and Ma's cotton linen 5.83 g a meter (Osaka 1840s price used for Kyoto 1745-54; 0249) = about 19 monme a 10.2 m tan,
  at the top of the Edo range, 2-4 times Ako's - arithmetic.
- Habutae 1692, 45 monme a tan - ATTESTED ONLY FOR a price in Saikaku's stories (Seken munezan'yo), via a compiler.
- Silk-to-cotton, Japan about 6:1 - weak (1692 fiction against 1716 white cotton 14-15 monme a hiki in Yotsuho silver).
  Beijing 1736 official: silk of various colors 0.6 tael a zhang (1.4 chi wide), red Hangzhou 0.4; blue biaobu 0.188 (9 cun),
  coarse white 0.09 - ATTESTED; 2 to 7 times by length - arithmetic. Laborer 75 cash a day = 0.094 tael at the author's 800
  cash - ATTESTED; silk zhang about 6 days, blue cotton 2, coarse 1 - arithmetic.
- Pongee: Japan - SILENT; Songjiang, late 1600s, Shandong-type pongee by then woven in Jiaxing, Huzhou, Suzhou and
  Songjiang, best about 0.1 tael a chi, some 0.03-0.04 - ATTESTED ONLY FOR Songjiang ("now", dated by context). Nearest
  wage Suzhou 1700, 0.09 tael.
- New plain kimono: Japan - SILENT. Beijing 1736: unfaced sheepskin jacket 1.2 tael, cloth shoes 0.18, cloth boots 0.6;
  its silk shirts (2.2) and printed-cotton padded jacket (0.54) are yangge dance costumes, which the author says differ
  somewhat from everyday dress (the pass had taken them as ordinary garments).
- Used kimono, sewing, silk bedding - SILENT.
