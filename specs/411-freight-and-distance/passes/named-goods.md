## A. China: Wuyi ("Bohea country") Congou tea at origin against Canton and London

**Source:** Samuel Ball, *An Account of the Cultivation and Manufacture of Tea in China* (London 1848), archive.org full text, https://archive.org/stream/accountofcultiva00ballrich/accountofcultiva00ballrich_djvu.txt (saved at `/tmp/l7r-pass/ball1848/`, parts p26, p27, p29). The quotes keep the OCR text as it stands.

**Date of evidence:** Ball was the EIC tea inspector at Canton from 1804 to 1826. He says the transit passage is "taken almost literally" from his paper printed at Macao in 1816. The cost table is for "the period of the East India Company's charter", which ended in 1834. Pigou's 11-tael estimate is from "about fifty years ago" before 1848, so c. 1800.

**Route, mode and time (pp. 352-353):**
> "They are first transported down the river Min in Fokien to the small town of Tsong-gan-hien, whence they are carried, by porters, an eight days' journey, over mountain passes to Ho-keu, and the rivers of Kiang-sy, which conduct to Nan-chang-foo and Kan-chew- foo ; and then, sufi'ering many transship- ments on their way, to the pass of Ta-moey-ling ... At this pass the teas are again carried by porters — the journey ... occupies one clay — ^ when they are re-shipped in large vessels which convey them to Canton. The time occupied in the entire transport from the Bohea country to Canton is about six weeks or two months ; and the whole expense is 3 tales, 9 mace, 2 candareens the pecul, or ''2d. the lb : which amounts to no less than about one-third of the cost of Congou tea at its seat of growth."

> "The expense of transit across these mountains amounts to no less than 1 tale, 2 mace, 5 candareens the pecul ; which is more than one third of the whole expense of carriage, though the pass is not one seventh of the whole distance, nor the time occupied one fifth of the whole time"

**Cost at origin, at Canton and in London (pp. 353-355):**
> "the cost of growth and ma- nipulation of Congou tea, of good or average quality, may be estimated at 12 tales the pecul ... Mr. Pigou about fifty years ago estimated the cost of the same description of tea at 11 tales."

> "Cost of growth and manipulation - - 12 0 0 0 Cost of chests, canisters, and packing - - 1 3 1 6 Expense of transport from the black tea districts to Canton - - - - -3920 Charges at Canton on account of government duties. Hong merchants' expenses, and boat hire to the ships - - - -3000 20 2 3 6 The cost of this description of tea to the East India Company was about 27 tales the pecul ; thus leaving the Hong merchant 6 tales, 8 mace, the pecul"

> "This tea usually sold at the Company's sales in England at three shillings the pound or sixty tales the pecul"

**The shorter sea-port route (p. 356):**
> "the port of Foo-chew-foo possesses the great advantage of a direct and uninterrupted water communication with the Bohea country at a distance of about 270 miles. The transit of the teas occupies about four or five days ; and the expense of conveyance does not exceed 4 mace, 3 candareens the pecul"

**Premium grades at origin (Appendix, p. 379):**
> "Cost of Tea in the Bohea District, The teas which the Chinese class as Paochong and Sou- chong are said to sell from 42 to 92 tales the pecul ... Such as the Chinese class as Kong-fu or Congou, sell from 22 to 32 tales the pecul ; and the teas known to us as Congou, sell from 7 to 13 tales the pecul."

**Wages in the same trade and region (p. 341-342):**
> "A labourer who can plough is a yearly servant ; he has his dinner found him, but his other meals he takes with his family ; and receives about 10s. the month. The packers of Bohea are allowed three meals a day, and five pence wages. The Hong merchants' coolies or porters are supplied with three meals a day, their lodging, and 155. the month. ... A common carpenter or bricklayer re- ceives his food and about seven pence the day. These calculations are framed after the accustomed mode of calculating the dollar at 5<§."

The "155." is OCR for 15s. Ball converts at a tael of 6s. 8d.

**Reading: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** Congou of good or average grade from the Wuyi/Bohea district, c. 1800-1834.
- At origin: 12 taels a picul, or 13.3 with chests.
- Carriage to Canton: 3.92 taels a picul, about a third of the origin cost. The trip takes six weeks to two months by river boat, with two porter stages over the mountain passes (8 days and 1 day).
- Delivered to the Hong merchant at Canton: about 20.2 taels. The EIC paid about 27 taels, and the tea sold in London for 60.
- Ball gives no mileage for the Canton route, only time and stages. The only distance he states is about 270 miles to Fuzhou (4-5 days, 0.43 taels).
- The premium grades (Paochong/Souchong at 42-92 taels in the district) have an origin price but no Canton price in what I read.
- Wages are attested for south China in the same period.

This is a contemporary witness (EIC inspector), not a modern reconstruction.

## B. Japan: Nada sake (下り酒), Edo price, freight per cask, carriage

**B1. Kobe City Archives, 「灘の酒造業」 (特集),** https://www.city.kobe.lg.jp/documents/60534/tokusyu-nadanosyuzogyo.pdf. A modern municipal text built on documents of the period.

(a) Normal and slump prices in Edo, Bunka-Bunsei (1804-1830):
> 「江戸では供給過剰の状態となり、必然的に酒価の暴落をもたらし、灘酒の相場が、普通10 駄(20 樽)が 15～20 両程のところ、この時期４～６両という惨たんたる状態です。」

(translation) "In Edo supply exceeded demand, which inevitably brought a collapse in sake prices. The going rate for Nada sake, normally about 15-20 ryō per 10 da (20 casks), fell in this period to a disastrous 4-6 ryō."

(b) Table 10, 「安政 5 年(1858)の柴田家造酒売付仕切り(鹿嶋利右衛門)」. These are the settlement accounts of the Shibata brewery (Shinzaike, Nada) with the Edo wholesaler Kashimaya. I checked the page image (PDF p. 21). Sample rows:
> 「3 月 29 日 丹頂 10 ｜19 両｜9 両 2 歩」; 「18 日 同 80 ｜18 両 2 歩(160 樽)｜148 両」; 「10 月 8 日 同 20 ｜23 両｜149 両 2 歩」; 壽海 「22 日 同 60 ｜14 両 2 歩(140 樽)｜101 両 2 歩」

The price column is headed 「2樽値段」, but the arithmetic only works per 10 駄 (20 樽). For example, 10 樽 at 19 両 gives 9両2歩, and 160 樽 at 18両2歩 gives 148 両. So in 1858 the Edo settlement price of the Tanchō brand ran 17両2歩 to 23 両 per 20 casks, and Jukai ran 13両2歩 to 18両2歩. The text says these casks went 「江戸の酒問屋鹿嶋屋に送られ」 ("were sent to the Edo sake wholesaler Kashimaya"). The 1850 Shibaya shipping book (Table 9) gives transit times by sea, for example shipped 正月24日, arrived 2月3日-2月14日. It also shows losses: 「難船」 (shipwreck), 「皆変酒、水上」 (all spoiled in transit).

(c) Tradition, Manji period (1658-61), quoted from 「船方曰御定並諸方聞書」 in 『灘酒沿革史』:
> 「何程に売仕切り参り候と尋ね申され候処に、元付六両位にて、江府付の時分に五十五両より六十両の仕切見せ申され候」

(translation) "When asked at what price the sales were settled, he showed settlements of about 6 ryō at origin, and 55 to 60 ryō on arrival in Edo."

The passage gives no unit. It is a founding story about the first ship-borne sake (Kitakaze Hikotarō of Hyōgo), not a price series.

(d) Nada sake was made for Edo only:
> 「灘酒造家は、天保３年新規株が交付されたとき、大坂市場を侵さないという一札を取られています。…灘の新規株はあくまでも江戸送りの酒として交付を受けたものでした。」

(translation) "When new licences were issued in Tenpō 3 [1832], the Nada brewers were made to sign a pledge not to encroach on the Osaka market ... the new Nada licences were granted strictly for sake sent to Edo."

**B2. 加藤慶一郎「樽廻船の加入形態に関する一考察」** (Kato Keiichirō), 流通科学大学論集 20-1 (2007), https://ryuka.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/181/files/n20-1kato.pdf. It discusses a contract dated 安政2年 (1855):
> 「毎回 40 駄の積み込みの約束がある。酒荷物の運賃がおよそ 10 駄につき銀 60 匁が基準であったから 8）、40 駄ならば銀 240 匁で大体計算が合う。」

(translation) "There is a promise to load 40 da each time. Since the standard freight for sake cargo was about 60 monme of silver per 10 da, 240 monme for 40 da roughly fits."

Note 8 cites 柚木学『酒造りの歴史』, so the figure is second hand and Kato gives no year for it.

**B3. Archived copy, Gakken 「大江戸」 Nada page** (https://www.gakken.jp/kagakusouken/spread/oedo/04/haiken2.html, archive `3a/3a774ca2481c`). A modern museum text (Kiku-Masamune).
> 「大きな船では、一度に1000石（一升びんで10万本分）の酒を積み、10～14日かけて江戸に運んだ。」

(translation) "A large ship carried 1,000 koku of sake at once (100,000 one-shō bottles) and took 10-14 days to bring it to Edo."

**Reading: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the Edo price and the freight, not for a brewery-gate price against an Edo price in the same year.
- Edo: normally 15-20 ryō per 10 駄 (20 four-to casks) in Bunka-Bunsei. Actual settlements in 1858 were 13.5-23 ryō.
- Freight on the Nada-Edo run by tarukaisen: about 60 monme of silver per 10 駄, a late-Edo figure taken second hand. At the nominal 60 monme = 1 ryō that is about 1 ryō per 20 casks, roughly 5-7% of the Edo price. The real gold-silver rates in Bunka-Ansei moved away from 60, so this ratio is approximate.
- Carriage: by sea, 10-14 days in 1,000-koku ships, with real losses from shipwreck and spoilage.
- No brewery-gate price was found. The only origin price is the undated-unit Manji tradition (6 ryō against 55-60).
- Structurally, Nada sake had no home market price: its licences were for Edo shipment only. The brewer's price was the Edo settlement minus freight and commission (Table 9 total: 「口銭等引〆金 1528 両」, "net after commission etc., 1,528 ryō").
- No Nada or Edo wage turned up in this pass.
