# Feature 282 - what is in a threshing yard, and where - the GM's request

The GM, 2026-09-28, on the threshing yards of the settlement maps:

> I have a question about the threshing yards on our maps. The way the threshing yard is rendered, there is kind of a rectangular space in front of the farmhouse, and then there are some drying racks, or at least that's what I think that they are. which are rendered there. And then there is a smaller rectangle inside the threshing yard, which I believe is the drying mat. Now my question is about scale. I understand that the threshing yard is an example of something where our glyph rendering conventions need to make the drawing legible, which prevents us from doing everything completely to scale. because, for example, if we are rendering a drying rack, then with one pixel being one foot, then you kind of just can't render the drying rack accurately because you would end up making it look like the wood that makes up the drying rack is made from logs which are one foot thick, which is not actually accurate. So, I mean, this is a just glyph rendering convention, which is the kind of thing that we have documented, and that's all fine. But what I'm basically wondering is whether the layout still gives an accurate sense of what these threshing yards were like. And here's what I mean by that. Um, every yard has exactly one drying mat kind of in the center. And then the ratio of the size of that drying mat to the rest of the threshing yard is widely variable. And there's only ever one drying mat. And also, the drying racks are always on the southern end of the threshing yard such that the threshing yard is a bunch of open space around a single drying mat with a line of drying racks at the southern edge. And that's just always what they look like. And I wonder whether that is accurate. Now, again, I get that in order to have legible glyphs, then we cannot be fully accurate because certain things are simply not renderable at this scale. But if it was the case that, for example, drying racks were strewn throughout the threshing yard, then us always putting them at one side of the yard kind of gives the wrong impression. And if there were sometimes multiple drying mats instead of one, then just always putting one in the very center gives the wrong impression, etc. So do we already have research that captures these details about what was in a threshing yard? If not, then it sounds like we should actually do a real research pass on this and then figure out how to maintain the legibility of our threshing yards as they are rendered as per our glyph rendering conventions while still giving a more or less accurate impression of what they were like.

The session's answer (summarized): the record covers the yard's SIZE (homesteads 020), its sun corridor (030) and
the drying racks (500, 145), but nothing on the yard's interior layout; the glyph (`homestead_parts/yards.py`) is a
fixed 14 x 9 ft mat at the center and a yard-wide rack on the south edge, and its modal is labeled accurate with no
research behind the layout. It contradicts the record in places: Kitamoto (the yard-size source) says mats were
spread over the whole yard at harvest, 40-60 of them, each 3 x 6 ft; entry 500 says racks stood mainly on the
paddies and bunds, and only in some regions (tall racks) by the house; a tall rack on the yard's south edge would
shade the yard entry 030 keeps sunlit. The session proposed a research pass on the yard's layout and on where a rack
by the house stood, then a glyph that tiles mats sized to the yard, and racks by the house only where a rolled knob
says so.

The GM, 2026-09-28:

Like, it's already the case that the rice paddies themselves would look very different in different seasons, as would the dry crop fields and so forth. I mean, showing snow on the ground is a good example of something that we might need to be able to do if we were rendering maps of different types, or having glyphs that denote whether or not a pond is frozen or not. Stuff like that. But that's all totally out of scope for this phase of development that we are in. Right now, we're just trying to get a solid baseline.

So our threshing yard glyphs show a smaller number to give the impression that there are many of them. I think that your tiling of three to six foot mats size to the yard makes sense. And then tall rack regions with racks by the house, place where the research says only in settlements rolled for tall racks, where that is a tunable knob because it sounds like some settlements had them and some didn't.
