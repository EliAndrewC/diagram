# Pass - plays and entertainers (#4 Q4) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

I found priced seat classes for Edo kabuki and Beijing theatres, but only scattered figures for noh, temple-fair and village shows. Same-period wages could be paired only partly. All pages fetched through `make source-pages` went on the ledger. Pages that had nothing are recorded as `nothing-found`, `rejected:` or `unreadable`. Pages that bear on the question are left uncited for you to cite. Two PDFs had no text layer that `source-pages` could read: the Rikkyo repository paper and the Saitama repository paper. I downloaded both with curl and read them as page images, so those quotes are my own transcriptions from the images.

## Sources

**1. Björk Tove (ビュールク・トーヴェ), 「二代目市川団十郎と劇場経営―享保十九年の江戸歌舞伎―」.** Rikkyo repository, issue 109, page 105. https://rikkyo.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/5105/files/AN0024904X_109_13.pdf . Evidence covers Edo, 1714–1803.
- 「享保五（一七二〇）年二月二十九日の『御用覚帳』によると、市村座の間取りは…東側桟敷一五間、西側桟敷一六間、向桟敷九間、桟敷は合わせて四〇間。…切落・人溜（土間）は八間×一三間で一〇四間」 (translation: "By the Goyō oboechō of 1720/2/29, the Ichimura-za had east boxes 15 bays, west boxes 16, facing boxes 9, 40 boxes in all … the kiriotoshi/hitodamari (pit) was 8 × 13 bays, 104 bays.")
- 「服部氏…このとき桟敷一間当たりには平均六人、切落・人溜（土間）には九人を収容したと推測し」 (translation: "Hattori estimates an average of six people per box bay and nine per pit bay.")
- 「服部氏（B論考）は『戯場訓蒙図彙』や『近世風俗志』などにより、享和期以降、切落は一三二文、中の間（向桟敷の階下、切落の後ろの方）は一〇〇文、そして追込（中の間よりさらに後ろ）は一六文で幕見ができた。桟敷の「本値」は、一階桟敷が内格子・太夫・平、下桟敷が内翠簾・外翠簾・新格子に分かれ、銀二五匁～三五匁（約一貫六七五文～二貫三四五文）だったとして引用紹介している。」 (translation: "Hattori, citing Gekijō kinmō zui and Kinsei fūzokushi, gives from the Kyōwa era (1801–04): kiriotoshi 132 mon; naka-no-ma (under the facing boxes, behind the kiriotoshi) 100 mon; and the oikomi (further back) let you watch a single act (makumi) for 16 mon. The box 'list price', by sub-class, was silver 25–35 monme (about 1,675–2,345 mon).")
- 「正徳四年に行われた改鋳直後の桟敷代は一間当たり一貫二〇〇文（一六匁）であり、切落一人分は六四文」 (translation: "Right after the 1714 recoinage a box cost 1,200 mon (16 monme) per bay and one kiriotoshi place 64 mon.")
- From the 1737 Nakamura-za sales return: 「東西桟敷代は三二間で一六両、一間当たり金二分（二貫文）…「人溜・切落」は八〇三人で銭一三一貫七〇〇文、一人分は一六四文」 (translation: "East and west boxes, 32 bays, 16 ryō, i.e. 2 bu gold (2,000 mon) per bay … pit and kiriotoshi, 803 people, 131 kan 700 mon, i.e. 164 mon per head.")

## Reading

**ATTESTED** for kabuki seat classes and prices in Edo. In 1803 the kiriotoshi crowd seats cost 132 mon and the naka-no-ma 100 mon. A box cost silver 25–35 monme for 7 people, about 3.6–5 monme a head. Beside the 1802–04 Kinai and Kyoto unskilled wage of 0.92–1.0 monme a day, a box seat was 4–5 days' wages. The wage is from Kinai and Kyoto, not Edo. The 132 mon cannot be set against the silver wage without a mon-to-monme rate for 1803, which no source I read gives.

- **一幕見:** **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the Edo makumi, the single-act standing view at the oikomi for 16 mon (Kyōwa era, via 戯場訓蒙図彙). The compound 一幕見 itself was not found in any Edo source.
- **Noh:** **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** kanjin-nō. Ground money was 30 mon c.1614–24 and boxes "one gold piece" in Saikaku's 1688 text. In 1848 tickets were forced on Edo townsmen, 52,500 of them. I found no seat-class price list. Ordinary noh does not appear as a ticketed show in anything I read.
- **Temple-fair 宮地芝居:** SILENT on figures. The sources establish only the hundred-day temporary licence.
- **Rural shows:** **ATTESTED** at troupe-hire level, not per seat. Funai paid 839–6,530 monme a run (1690–1735). In 1728 that was 285 monme a performance, 5.7 monme per performer, and ticket money of 2,392 mon over 15 shows. Village 花/祝儀 gifts are SILENT; everything I saw on them described modern revivals.
- **Troupe composition:** **ATTESTED**. Kabuki companies included resident nagauta musicians from late Kyōhō. Touring kabuki troupes averaged 43 performers and jōruri troupes 26. Acrobats, misemono and street performers were separate trades that borrowed from kabuki; they did not travel inside kabuki troupes. Street performers collected coin from the crowd, but no Japanese amount was found.
- **Qing:** **ATTESTED**. The pit was charged per head and the gallery per table, with a 130-cash fixed seat price before 1900. A rural Henan troupe cost ten-odd thousand cash for three days, and a street act about 3 cash (c.1720s). The only Beijing wage I have is 0.077 taels a day from 1769–95, a century off and in silver, so it does not fit beside the 130 cash.
- **England (comparative):** **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** gathering at the door. That includes a touring company at an inn yard in 1583, whose gatekeeper was still taking coin after the play began. The playhouses charged a further penny at each further door (Platter, 1599). I found no primary evidence of a hat passed mid-play. Lawrence's "secondary gathering in the galleries" is a 1912 inference, not a source of the period. No English wage was read from a usable source.
