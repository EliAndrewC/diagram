# Pass - arrows (#4 Q1) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

I found per-arrow prices for Ming and Qing China, but nothing for Japan. No source anywhere gave how many arrows a fletcher made in a day. On recovery after battle I found only an indirect Qing legal point, and nothing Japanese.

## Sources that bear on the question

**1. Tan Lun's memorial, 譚襄敏奏議 卷07** – Jizhou frontier (薊鎮), c. 1568–72, an estimate of what it cost to equip Qi Jiguang's (戚繼光) troops.
https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/譚襄敏奏議_(四庫全書本)/卷07
> 弓箭一千六百二十副每副弓一張一條箭三十枝撒袋一副共工料銀一千六百二十兩

"Bows-and-arrows, 1,620 sets; each set is one bow, one string, thirty arrows and one quiver; materials and labour together 1,620 taels" (translation). That is 1 tael per complete set, so each arrow cost well under 0.033 tael.

> 火箭一十四萬枝連盛箭蓆桶一千四百箇共工料銀四千五百三十三兩一錢三分

"Fire-arrows, 140,000, with 1,400 mat barrels to hold them, materials and labour 4,533.13 taels" (translation). That is about 0.032 tael per fire-arrow, my arithmetic, barrels included.

**2. Ming wage benchmark: 武編 前集卷5** (Tang Shunzhi 唐順之, before 1560), a costing of matchlock-making.
https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/武編_(四庫全書本)/前集卷5
> 煉鐵爐每爐六人煉該四日共二十四工内鉗手每工四分散匠三分

"At each smelting furnace six men work four days, 24 workman-days; the tongs-man is paid 4 fen a day, the ordinary hand 3 fen" (translation).
> 木匠二工銀六分

"Carpenter, two days, 6 fen" (translation).

So an ordinary craft hand earned 0.03 tael a day. At that rate Tan Lun's 1-tael set is about 33 days' pay, and one arrow is under a day's pay. The place does not match exactly: Tang's costing is not tied to Jizhou.

**3. Late-Ming assessed price, Zhenjiang prefecture (Jintan county)** – the gazetteer's 田賦考 section in the 古今圖書集成 imperial encyclopedia, 職方典 第0731卷.
https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/方輿彙編/職方典/第0731卷
> 此項查舊刊《全書》原辦攢箭九千枝，每枝價銀一分三釐五毫六絲三忽二微零，共銀一百二十二兩六分八釐八毫零。……於順治三年六月內奉。 旨改折每枝折銀一錢。

"Checking the old printed Fiscal Book: originally 9,000 composite-shaft arrows were supplied, each priced at 0.0135632 tael, 122.07 taels in all… In the sixth month of Shunzhi 3 [1646], by edict, the levy was commuted at 0.1 tael per arrow" (translation).

The Ming book price is about 0.0136 tael, roughly half a day of the 3-fen wage. The 1646 figure of 0.1 tael is a tax commutation rate, not what an arrow cost to make. Dengzhou (Qixia county) used the same commutation rate, 箭六十四把，每把三十枝，每枝銀一錢 (職方典 第0276卷).

**4. Early-Qing statute price for provincial troops: 欽定大清會典則例 卷130**
https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定大清㑹典則例_(四庫全書本)/卷130
> 七年議準棉甲每副工料銀二兩弓每張銀一兩箭每枝銀四分腰刀每把銀九錢二分鳥槍每杆銀一兩三錢

"Shunzhi 7 [1650] ruling: padded armour, materials and labour 2 taels a suit; bow, 1 tael each; arrow, 0.04 tael each; sabre, 0.92 tael; musket, 1.3 taels" (translation).

**5. Qing Beijing fixed prices, ordinary and fine arrows: 欽定大清會典則例 卷122**
https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定大清㑹典則例_(四庫全書本)/卷122
> 雍正五年議準靑鶴翎一等每箭五枝價銀一兩八錢二等一兩六錢三等一兩四錢蟬鵞翎一等每箭五枝價一兩七錢……鸛翎一等每箭五枝價一兩二等八錢鴇翎一等價七錢二等六錢芝麻鵰戰箭每百枝價八兩鴈翎每百枝價五兩定價之後舖戸有任意高擡時價者照把持行市律治罪

"Yongzheng 5 [1727] ruling: arrows fletched with blue-crane feathers, first grade 1.8 taels per five arrows, second 1.6, third 1.4; … stork feathers, first grade 1 tael per five, second 0.8; bustard feathers, first grade 0.7, second 0.6; sesame-eagle feathered war arrows, 8 taels per hundred; goose-feathered, 5 taels per hundred. Once prices are fixed, shops that raise them at will are punished under the law on cornering the market" (translation).

Per arrow: ordinary war arrows 0.05–0.08 tael; fine arrows 0.12–0.36 tael. The same 卷122 notes that the 兵部 (Board of War) kept ten 箭匠 (arrowsmiths) to make official arrows: 兵部設箭匠十名以造官箭. It gives no output figure.

**6. Qing Code, 大清律例 卷十九, article 毀棄軍器** (archived: `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/22/22e8a8914322/20261002T121911Z/text.txt`)
> 並驗〈毀失之〉數追賠〈還官〉其曾經戰陣而有損失者不坐不賠 ……一箭上不書姓名及書他人姓名者杖八十

"…all to be checked by the number destroyed or lost and the cost recovered for the state; but losses incurred in battle are neither punished nor repaid. … Sub-statute: an arrow not inscribed with its owner's name, or inscribed with another's name, is punished with eighty blows" (translation).

**7. Japanese wage benchmark only** – coin-walk.site/J020.htm, a secondary page citing 松延康隆 and others:
> １６世紀後半、小田原北条氏の領内で人夫は１日２０文、職人（木挽き職、鍛冶職など）は５０文、との記録があります。

"In the late 16th century, in the Odawara Hōjō domain, a labourer was paid 20 mon a day and a craftsman (sawyer, smith) 50 mon" (translation).

**8. A historian's inference, not evidence** – Kawato Takashi (川戸貴史) in gendai.media/articles/-/72981, excerpted from his 『戦国大名の経済学』:
> 弓矢は自作したと考えられるが、自給がむずかしい金属製品の鏃は、槍のそれと同じくらいの価値で取引されただろう。

"Bows and arrows are thought to have been self-made, but iron arrowheads, hard to make for oneself, were probably traded at about the value of spearheads" (translation). This is a guess. He prices a spear at about 1 kanmon (1,000 mon), from the 朝倉孝景条々 phrase 百疋之鑓.

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** Ming and Qing China, and only for price. In late Ming an ordinary war arrow cost about 0.0136 tael (Zhenjiang book price), or under 0.033 tael within a 1-tael set of bow, string, 30 arrows and quiver (Jizhou, c. 1570). An ordinary craft hand earned 3 fen (0.03 tael) a day, so one arrow cost roughly half a day's wage, and a full set about a month's. In early Qing the provincial statute price was 0.04 tael (1650). In 1727 Beijing, war arrows cost 0.05–0.08 tael and fine feathered arrows 0.12–0.36 tael each. I found no same-place unskilled wage for Qing Beijing.

**SILENT for Japan.** I found no per-arrow or per-arrowhead price in mon for Sengoku or Edo; the only Japanese statement is Kawato's inference. The only Japanese figure is the Hōjō day wage of 20 mon.

**SILENT for fletcher output per day** in both countries. The sources give arrowsmith headcounts (Ming 箭匠 421; Qing Board of War 10) but no daily quota.

**Recovery is not attested** in Japan or China. The Qing Code instead writes off battle losses ("neither punished nor repaid"), while requiring each soldier's name on his arrows.

## Leads not followed

- 工部廠庫須知 (1615): the line 箭…每百枝銀一錢一分 came only as a garbled search-result snippet; it needs a clean edition.
- 崇禎四年兵部題本 (1631 Board of War memorial), relayed second-hand by the zhihu article: 大箭五千支 工價四十五兩 (about 0.009 tael labour per arrow).
- 職方典 0089 and 0412: early-Qing post-station labourers paid 5 fen and 2 fen a day; the county was not identified (a possible Qing wage benchmark).
- 雑兵物語 (1657–83), its 矢箱持 (arrow-chest carrier) and 弓足軽 (archer foot-soldier) chapters, for arrow resupply and recovery. NDL digital copy.
- 雍州府志 (1684), its 土産 (local products) 弓矢 entry on Kyoto 矢師.
- 太平御覽 卷349, citing 宋書 (5th c.): 修之使軍人緣水拾箭 – soldiers picking up arrows along a river, outside the period.
- Korean 時務策 (Hwaseong, 1790s): exam arrows picked up and sold – Korea, not in scope.
