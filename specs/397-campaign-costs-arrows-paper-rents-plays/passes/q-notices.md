# Pass - notice boards and paper (#4 Q2) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

**Verdict: ATTESTED ONLY FOR part of the question.** What a notice was made of, and who could post one, is now attested for Edo. A complete price set is attested only for Qing Beijing in 1736. For Edo the prices of 半紙, 美濃紙, 奉書 and an ink stick, by size and grade, are **SILENT**: the only figures I found come from a hobby site and an anonymous Q&A, and they disagree.

Question 0190 already covers sites, sizes, contents, the headman and the Chinese yamen gate walls. Below is only what it lacks.

## Sources

**1. Kotobank 高札** (archived, 828b0aa6300f; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%AB%98%E6%9C%AD-64337). Describes Edo practice and medieval use.
- 世界大百科: 「ヒノキ，梅などの板札に法令を墨書して高く掲げたもの。」 (translation: "plaques of cypress, plum or similar wood, with the law written in ink, hung high.") 「大名は独自に高札を立てることもあり，これを幕府の公儀高札と区別して，自分高札と呼ぶ。」 (translation: "Daimyo sometimes put up boards of their own; these are called *jibun kōsatsu*, as distinct from the shogunate's *kōgi kōsatsu*.")
- ニッポニカ: 「通常、幕府の高札を公儀高札、大名・旗本のものを自分高札という。」 (translation: "Usually the shogunate's boards are called *kōgi kōsatsu*, and those of daimyo and hatamoto *jibun kōsatsu*.")
- 精選版 日本国語大辞典, たかふだ: 「中世・近世で、禁制・法度・掟書・犯罪人の罪状などを記し、寺社の門前や人通りの多い場所に立てた板札。私事によるものもあった。」 (translation: "In medieval and early modern times, a wooden board giving prohibitions, laws, rules, criminals' offences and the like, set up before temple and shrine gates or where people passed. Some were put up for private matters.")

**2. ja.wikipedia 高札** (archived, de4cd4d835b5). Describes the Edo period.
- 「木製の板札に墨書きで表題・本文・年月日・発行主体が書かれ」 (translation: "On a wooden board, in ink, were written the title, the text, the date and the issuing authority.")
- 「また高札の文字が不明になったときでも許可なくしては墨入れもできなかった（…札は風雨や日光に晒されがちで木製の板に墨で書かれた札は読みにくくなった）。その代わり幕府や諸藩では「高札番」という役職を設けて常時、高札場の整備・管理・修繕・新設にあたらせた。」 (translation: "Even when a board's letters became illegible, no one could re-ink them without permission (…the boards were exposed to wind, rain and sun, and ink on wood became hard to read). Instead the shogunate and domains created a post, the *kōsatsu-ban*, to maintain, manage, repair and build board sites at all times.")
- 「高札に書かれた内容を出版することも町人に許可し」 (translation: "Townsmen were also permitted to publish what was written on the boards.")

**3. Watanabe Hiroshi, 「江戸の高札と都市社会」**, report of 2009 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/cuhreview/28/2/28_KJ00005996505/_pdf/-char/ja). Describes Edo in the 18th century. The quotes are from OCR, with the OCR's spaces removed.
- 「高札場の建て替えは町方の負担であったと思われる。」 (translation: "Rebuilding the board sites appears to have been borne by the townspeople.")
- 「江戸町方の高札（場）は100ヶ所をはるかに超え…これらの保全は近隣の特定の町（地縁団体）に任されており」 (translation: "Edo's commoner districts had far more than 100 board sites… their upkeep was entrusted to a particular neighbouring ward.")
- On the open-ground board at Edobashi (c.1720), the ward itself asked for it: 「預り町の方から明地高札を求めた」 (translation: "the custodian ward itself requested the open-ground board"), as proof of its right to manage the plaza.

**4. Chūō City, 一石橋迷子しらせ石標** (https://www.city.chuo.lg.jp/a0052/bunkakankou/rekishi/tokyobunkazai/sekihyo.html). A stone set up in 1857.
- 「西河岸町…の家主17人の惣代・重兵衛…が石標建立の願書を南町奉行所に提出・許可され」 (translation: "Jūbei, representing 17 landlords of Nishigashi-chō, petitioned the South Magistrate's office to set up the stone, and was permitted.")
- 「たづぬる方には迷子の親が「迷子の詳細…」を記した紙を貼り置き、しらする方には迷子を留め置く町内から…記した紙を貼り置いた」 (translation: "On the 'searching' side the lost child's parents pasted a paper with the details; on the 'informing' side the ward holding the child pasted a paper with its details.")
- The recessed panels for the papers measure 「縦約30.5cm・横約22.5cm」 (translation: "about 30.5 cm tall and 22.5 cm wide").

**5. Iwahashi Kiyomi, 「近世都市江戸における迷子の保護」**, *Chiba Keizai Ronsō* 48, 2013 (https://lib.cku.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1020/files/KJ00008682043.pdf). Quotes a town ordinance of 1726.
- 「倒死病人水死、其外異死迷ひ子等有之節、其所より訴出次第、年頃并衣服等之品認、自今芝口町河岸ニ七日之内札を建置候条、心当有之者ハ右札場江罷越、文言見候而…」 (translation: "When there are people dead in the street, sick, drowned, otherwise strangely dead, or lost children, as soon as it is reported, their age, clothing and so on shall be written down and a notice set up for seven days at the Shibaguchi-chō riverbank; anyone who knows them is to go to that notice site and read the text…")
- The paper also says the stones were paid for by townsmen, not from ward funds: 「町入用に依拠しない町人たちの出資」 (translation: "townsmen's own contributions, not reliant on ward funds").

**6. Gao Shouxian, 「清代《九卿议定物料价值》的分类整理」**, Palace Museum journal, 2022 (https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2023/02/10/u63e60647c73ae.pdf). These are official purchase prices for Beijing, compiled 1729–1736.
- Units: 「均指白银，单位为两」 (translation: "all in silver, unit taels").
- Paper, per sheet:
  - 「头号高丽纸每张0.08」 (translation: "No. 1 Korean paper, 0.08 per sheet")
  - 「江南纸每张0.027」 (translation: "Jiangnan paper, 0.027 per sheet")
  - 「各色毛边纸每张0.01；各色榜纸每张0.01」 (translation: "*maobian* paper of all colours, 0.01 per sheet; placard paper (榜纸) of all colours, 0.01 per sheet")
  - 「棉料连四纸每张0.0063；竹料连四纸每张0.0055；各色连七纸每张0.0015」 (translation: "cotton-stock *liansi*, 0.0063; bamboo *liansi*, 0.0055; *lianqi* of all colours, 0.0015")
  - 「三白纸每张0.0003」 (translation: "*sanbai* paper, 0.0003 per sheet")
- Ink: 「顶烟徽墨每斤3.6；徽墨每斤0.6；蓝墨每斤0.342；青烟墨每斤0.04」 (translation: "top-soot Hui ink, 3.6 per *jin*; Hui ink, 0.6; blue ink, 0.342; green-soot ink, 0.04 per *jin*").
- Wages, per day: 「壮夫…不论长、短给钱75文」 (translation: "able-bodied labourers… 75 cash, long-term or short-term alike"). Craftsmen got 154 cash.
- Exchange rate, 1737: 「每纹银一两，换大制钱八百文」 (translation: "one tael of fine silver exchanges for 800 large cash").
- My arithmetic, not the source's: a placard or *maobian* sheet is about 8 cash, about a ninth of a labourer's day. A *lianqi* sheet is about 1.2 cash. The source prices ink by the *jin*, not the stick.

**7. coin-walk.site, 江戸時代値段史** (https://coin-walk.site/J078.htm). An Edo-period price table; the site calls itself 「私的な試作品です。決して学術的な利用はされないよう」 (translation: "a private draft; please do not use it academically"). It lists its sources as 江戸物価事典, 近世賃金物価史史料 and others.
- Sugihara paper, one 帖: 文政3年 (1820) 「２０～３０文」 and 天保元年 (1830) 「３０～４０文」 (translation: "20–30 mon"; "30–40 mon").
- Its note: 「１束＝１０帖で、１帖は４８枚（２０枚とする説もある）。半分に切ったのが「半紙」」 (translation: "1 bundle = 10 *jō*; 1 *jō* is 48 sheets (some say 20). Cut in half, it is *hanshi*.")
- Labourer's day wage: 寛延3 (1750) 「１００文」, 文化7 (1810) 「２００～３５０文」, 慶応元 (1865) 「４００～５００文」 (translation: "100 mon"; "200–350 mon"; "400–500 mon"). Its note: 「通常、お米２～３升分のことが多い」 (translation: "usually the value of 2–3 *shō* of rice").
- I read the table rows by position. The 1820 and 1830 paper rows and the 1810 wage row are unambiguous.

**8. Yahoo Chiebukuro Q&A, 2014** (https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q10137853745). An anonymous answer claiming to cite 江戸物価事典, for the late Edo period.
- 「半紙（和紙）1枚12文、質の悪いもので10文。その後14文、16文、20文に至る。（江戸物価事典）」 (translation: "*hanshi* 12 mon a sheet, poor grade 10 mon; later rising to 14, 16 and 20 mon.")
- 「還魂紙…浅草紙…価格は百枚百文」 (translation: "recycled Asakusa paper… 100 sheets for 100 mon").

**9. JAANUS, hanshi** (https://aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/h/hanshi.htm). Defines the counter, used in the Edo period and since.
- "approximately 25cm by 35cm… 20 sheets equal one jou 帖, 10 jou equal one taba 束."

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** what a notice was made of and who posted it, in Edo:
- **Official notices were wooden plaques written in ink.** The Edo plaques were cypress or plum, written in ink and re-inked only with permission. The shogunate posted *kōgi kōsatsu*, and daimyo and hatamoto their own boards. Edo's wards paid for and guarded the board sites.
- **Pasted paper notices were made by townspeople, and only on a structure the magistrate had permitted.** The attested case is the 1857 lost-child stone, whose recesses held papers about 30×22 cm.
- **Private wooden notices are attested only in passing.** The medieval-to-early-modern dictionary says 「私事によるものもあった」 ("some were for private matters"), without saying who or how.
- **The magistrate's own temporary notices are attested once.** In 1726 a 札 was set up for seven days at a fixed riverbank site in Shibaguchi; the source does not say whether it was wood or paper.
- No page I read says what happened to unpermitted paper posted at a village board.

**Prices:**
- **Qing (Beijing, 1736) is attested.** There are official per-sheet prices by grade, ink by the *jin*, and wages on the same list.
- **Edo is not attested to a standard I could use, and the two figures conflict.** The hobby site gives Sugihara at 20–40 mon a 帖 in the 1820s–30s; at 48 or 20 sheets that is under 1 to 2 mon a sheet. The anonymous Q&A gives *hanshi* at 10–20 mon a sheet. These cannot both be right. The Q&A may be misreading a per-帖 price as per-sheet; that is my guess. No source I read gives 美濃紙, 奉書 or an ink stick a price in mon. The Edo wage range of 100 to 350–500 mon a day is from the hobby site only.

**Archive and searches.** In the archive I ran the 高札, 半紙, 美濃紙, 奉書, 張り紙, 一帖, 墨書, 日雇 and 手間賃 terms and read the passages. The archived 和紙 entries (Kotobank and ja.wikipedia) give no prices. The web searches were:

## Leads not followed

- 江戸物価事典 (Ono Takeo, 1979) and 近世賃金物価史史料 (Koyaizu Nobuo): the primary price tables behind both Edo figures; only reachable through NDL Digital.
- 三井文庫『近世後期における主要物価の動態』: a scholarly price series that may include paper.
- 《钦定物料价值则例》 (1768), the provincial counterpart to source 6, with paper and ink prices by province.
- The archived Miyashiro PDF (3f99c69e5170) holds 文久二年 御高札場御普請出来形帳, an 1862 itemized timber cost for rebuilding a village board site (8.5 shaku high), in 永 accounting units. It covers the structure, not the notices.
- Edo *harigami* rules, such as bans on anonymous pasted petitions (張訴) and on placards in 撰要永久録: not searched.
