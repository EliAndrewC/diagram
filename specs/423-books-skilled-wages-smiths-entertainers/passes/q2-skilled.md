## (a) Literate clerk

**1. Mitsui Echigoya, Kyoto head shop: clerk pay by rank and year, early 1800s.** **ATTESTED**, third-hand. The live page is now 404, so I read the Wayback copy: `https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://www.pref.kumamoto.jp/uploaded/attachment/13947.pdf` (Kumamoto prefecture statistics page, 2013). It quotes 厚生労働通信, which quotes 西坂靖「越後屋京本店手代の小遣・年褒美・割銀について」. The page itself calls the table an estimate (「給金推計」).
- Table: 「「大店」における手代等の給金（銀）年額 単位：匁 … 平手代（１年目）160 … 中輩（次座以下の平手代）180～190 … 次座 200 … 筆頭 210 … 上座 270 … 役頭 330 … 組頭（２年目）650 … 組頭（３年目）900 … 組頭（６年目）1800 … 支配役並 1800 … 支配役（１年目）1800 … 支配役（３年目）2100 … 支配役（５年目）2700 … 支配役（６年目）3240」 (translation: the yearly silver pay of clerks in a great house, in monme, from a first-year clerk at 160 up to a sixth-year manager at 3,240).
- How it was paid: 「月給８万円が毎月もらえたわけではなく、盆・正月の年２回に小額程度の現金が支給され。残りは退職金名目などとして積み立てをし、社内の運転資金に消えたり」 (translation: it was not paid monthly. A small amount of cash came twice a year, at Bon and New Year; the rest was held back as a retirement fund and used as the firm's working capital).
- In kind: 「小僧（丁稚）は…毎月の賃金は住み込みの「衣食住」という現物給付のみ」 (translation: the apprentices' only pay was board, clothing and lodging, living in).
- Arithmetic (mine): at the page's 60 monme to the ryō, 160 monme is 2.7 ryō (the page rounds it to about 3 ryō) and 3,240 monme is 54 ryō.

**2. Promotion, the coming-of-age step (元服), the branch house (別家), and the Kōnoike bonus funds.** **ATTESTED** for structure; the Kōnoike sums are deferred bonuses, not a yearly wage. Source: https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%89%8B%E4%BB%A3-100991 (世界大百科事典, entry by 宮本又郎; Edo period).
- 「丁稚の期間は無給…17～18歳で元服して名前も変わり，手代となった。手代昇進の際に主人は…木綿の紋服や羽織などを与えた。手代になると…衣食のほか給金を与えられるのがふつうであった」 (translation: apprentices were unpaid; at 17-18 they came of age, took a new name and became clerks, and the master gave them a crested cotton robe and a haori. As clerks they usually got a wage on top of food and clothing).
- 「大坂の両替商鴻池家では，〈名付銀〉〈催合銀〉…1回の支給額は銀500目～1貫目で，これらは本家で積み立てられ，月1歩（1%）の利息が付された。催合銀は毎年200目ずつ…独立して自分家業を始めるとき，元手金として手渡された」 (translation: at the Osaka money-changer Kōnoike, each naming-silver grant was 500 monme to 1 kan of silver, held by the main house at 1% interest a month; the joint fund added 200 monme a year. Both were handed over as start-up capital when the man set up on his own).

**3. Clerk of a shogunal intendant's office (代官所手代), the one office clerk with a fixed pay, from 1725.** **ATTESTED**. Same kotobank page.
- 世界大百科事典: 「書役より昇格した…1725年（享保10）以降の給与は20両五人扶持で，役所の事務を総括した元締手代になると30両五人扶持」 (translation: promoted from scribe (書役); from 1725 paid 20 ryō and rations for five, and 30 ryō and five rations as chief clerk).
- ニッポニカ: 「代官の赴任先で村役人や商人の子弟などを書役…という見習に採用し…手代に昇任させた」 (translation: sons of village officials and merchants were taken on as apprentice scribes and promoted to clerk).
- Arithmetic (mine): five rations at 1.8 koku each (the archived kotobank 扶持 entry) is 9 koku of rice a year, plus 20 ryō.
- Against the canon: this is a commoner clerk on a fixed salary, not a samurai. It is the closest historical comparison to "samurai clerk stipend = rank squared."

**4. Qing private secretaries (幕友), c. 1785.** **ATTESTED**. Source: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E4%BD%90%E6%B2%BB%E8%97%A5%E8%A8%80 (汪輝祖『佐治藥言』). 金 here means taels; quoted as the Wikisource text has it.
- 「吾輩從事於幕者，類皆章句之儒，為童子師，歲修不過數十金，幕修所入，或數倍焉，或數十倍焉。」 (translation: men like us were schoolteachers paid no more than a few tens of taels a year; a secretary's fee is several times, or several tens of times, that).
- 「然幕中數席，惟刑名、錢穀歲修較厚。余則不過百金內外，或止四五十金者」 (translation: of the secretary posts only law and revenue pay well; the rest get about 100 taels, some only 40-50).
- 「歲修百金，到家亦不過六七十金。八口之家，僅足敷衍。」 (translation: a 100-tael fee brings only 60-70 home, barely enough for a family of eight).
- 「千金大多幕」 (translation: the great posts of a thousand taels).
- 「相佐遊幕之土，月修或至數十金」 (translation: a travelling secretary's monthly fee may reach several tens of taels).
- The 「月修百兩／歲修千金」 an online op-ed attributes to this book is **not in this text**.

## (c) Skilled servants

**8. Household cook in a townsman house.** **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the live-in rice-cooking manservant. A trained 料理人 or 板前 is **SILENT**. Source: archived copy `31280-edo-rakugo-kuchiire-ya.html` (a blog transcribing 『守貞謾稿』巻之四, record of the 1830s-1850s). The blog's text is not checked against the original.

**11. A lady's personal maid.** **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the shogun's own women's quarters (大奥) under the 13th shogun Iesada (1853-58). A daimyo or hatamoto house is **SILENT**. Source: https://www.nippon.com/ja/japan-topics/c10811/, citing 松尾美恵子「江戸幕府女中分限帳について」.
- Table: 「役職 切米（石）扶持（人）合力金（両）上臈御年寄 50 10 60 御年寄 50 10 80 … 御中臈 12 4 40 … 御右筆 8 3 25 呉服之間 8 3 20 御三之間 5 2 15 御中居 5 2 7 … 御半下 4 1 2」 (translation, columns are rice stipend in koku, rations, and allowance in ryō: a senior lady-in-waiting (御中臈) got 12 koku, rations for 4 and 40 ryō; the lowest maid got 4 koku, rations for 1 and 2 ryō).

**12. Ordinary maid (下女).** No direct evidence. https://kotobanomado.jp/column/1094/ says a half-year's 1 ryō 2 bu, with seasonal clothing, was good pay, but the columnist frames it as a guess (「であったろう」). His other details paraphrase an 1810 comic novel I did not read.

## Reading

Clerks are the best covered:
- In Japan there are three levels. Mitsui's estimated ladder runs from 160 monme in a clerk's first year to 3,240 monme for a sixth-year manager, with most of it held back rather than paid out. A commoner clerk in an intendant's office got 20 ryō plus rations for five from 1725.
- In China, a minor secretary got about 40-100 taels a year (c. 1785), the law and revenue secretaries up to about 1,000, and a village schoolteacher a few tens.

Servants who cooked earned about 3 ryō a year in Edo, live-in. A samurai house's servant was on 3 ryō 2 bu in 1828. The only lady's-maid figures are for the shogun's women's quarters.

For guards I found only how biaoju were paid: once a year, at the first lunar month, for three or four trips of about three months each. No rate turned up, and nothing on Japanese guards.

The Kanazawa lead (0.7-1 koku a year for servants, 3 for a young steward) is still **unverified**. Its likely source is the Inoyama household accounts in 磯田道史『武士の家計簿』, but no copy of the figures is online. I tried the serai and smbc pages, a Shimane paper (404), and two queries.
