I found prices for a hoe, for a sickle and for re-tipping a hoe, but no smith's yearly takings or net income from before 1868, Japanese or Chinese. The tool-against-input comparison only works cleanly for Qing Beijing in 1736. Crew size is attested. Payment in rice by the household is attested only from 20th-century surveys.

## 1. Sources

### A. Tool prices against iron and charcoal

**A1. Qing Beijing, 1736: tool and iron on one list.** Archive copy: `https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2023/02/10/u63e60647c73ae.pdf` (Gao Shouxian's study of 《九卿议定物料价值》, 乾隆元年 1736; already cited in 0275 and 0276 as gao-shouxian-jiuqing-wuliao).
- 「镰刀（重12两）每把0.07」 (translation: "sickle, weighing 12 liang, 0.07 tael each")
- 「钢每斤0.085；生铁每斤0.004；好熟铁每斤0.027；平常熟铁每斤0.0225」 (translation: "steel 0.085 a jin; pig iron 0.004 a jin; good wrought iron 0.027 a jin; ordinary wrought iron 0.0225 a jin")
- 「菜刀（重1斤）每把0.1」 (translation: "kitchen cleaver, weighing 1 jin, 0.1 tael each")
- 「黑炭每百斤0.5/0.6；白炭每百斤1.1」 (translation: "black charcoal 0.5 or 0.6 tael per 100 jin; white charcoal 1.1 per 100 jin")
- **Our arithmetic:** 12 liang is 0.75 jin. That much wrought iron costs 0.017–0.020 tael, about a quarter to three-tenths of the sickle's price. This leaves out the steel edge, the charcoal and the iron lost in forging, whose quantities are unknown.
- **Limits:** this is a government purchase list, not a village retail price. The list's craftsman wage covers 「木石砖瓦油画等匠」 (carpenters, masons, bricklayers, tilers, painters), not smiths.

**A2. Sakai and Osaka, 1822 (『農具便利論』), a hoe's price.** Source: Kawashima 2010, URL above, read from the PDF text.
- 「最初の鍬には「摂州西成郡の辺にて真土に用る鍬」と添え書きされ、「代銀五匁六分」と値段も書かれている」 (translation: "the first hoe is captioned 'hoe used on loam around Nishinari district, Settsu', and its price is written too: 'price 5 monme 6 bu of silver'")
- 「第 3 図では「五匁五分」となっている」 (translation: "in figure 3 it is 5 monme 5 bu")
- Ōkura Nagatsune's own note on the price table: 「右ハ泉州堺又は大坂辺の農具鍛冶のつくり出す所の直段なり」 (translation: "these are the prices at which the farm-tool smiths of Sakai in Izumi or around Osaka make them")

**A3. Echizen, 1841, a village smith's charges.** Fetched: `https://www.library-archives.pref.fukui.lg.jp/fukui/07/kenshi/T4/T4-3-01-02-03-13.htm` (『福井県史』通史編4).
- 「野鍛冶の手仕事の値段は、天保十二年の「鍛冶細工物値段取極連印帳」（馬面武治家文書資４）によれば、鍬真先作りが銀一一匁より一五匁まで、三鍬刃先作り四匁、五升鍋つる作り三匁、草取り鎌目立て銀一分五厘などであった。」 (translation: "The charges for village smiths' handwork, by the 'sealed agreement on prices for smiths' work' of 1841, were 11 to 15 monme of silver for making a hoe's new point [鍬真先作り], 4 monme for making the blade-edge of a three-pronged hoe, 3 monme for making a handle for a 5-shō pot, 0.15 monme for resharpening a weeding sickle, and so on.")
- **These two figures do not reconcile.** A hoe's new point at 11–15 monme is two to three times a whole Sakai hoe at 5.6. The kenshi calls the list 手仕事 (handwork) charges. I did not resolve which explains the gap: a different province, 1841 against 1822 silver, or what 真先 covers.

**A4. Iron per kan, in rice.** Source: Arai Hiroshi, BOUNDARY 2000.9, URL above. Its table 23 is Oka Mitsuo's series; Arai disputes only the rows before 1620.
- 「表２３ 鉄 1 貫価格（米換算）」 (translation: "Table 23, price of 1 kan of iron, converted to rice"). Rows, in to of rice per kan of iron:

  | Year | Place | Rice per kan of iron |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1736 | Osaka | 0.46 |
  | 1812 | Osaka | 0.29 |
  | 1826 | Edo | 0.43 |
  | 1843 | Osaka | 0.52 |
  | 1844 | Edo | 0.68 |

- The one figure in silver: 「1620 年の打鉄価格（山元）を 6 匁／10 貫と言っている」 (translation: "it gives the price of forged iron at the works in 1620 as 6 monme per 10 kan"), from 『学己集』 by way of 『広島県史』.

**A5. Hoe weights.** Fetched: `https://www.tosyokan.pref.shizuoka.jp/data/open/cnt/3/50/1/ssr3-32.pdf`; the figures are drawn from 『農具便利論』.
- 「〈図２〉の備中鍬（700～800目）が〈図１〉の平鍬（350目）より２倍の重さ」 (translation: "the Bitchū hoe in fig. 2 (700–800 me) is twice the weight of the flat hoe in fig. 1 (350 me)")
- A flat (furo) hoe is a wooden body with an iron edge, so 350 me may be the whole tool, not its iron.
- **Our arithmetic:** a Bitchū hoe of 0.7–0.8 kan holds iron worth about 0.2–0.4 to of rice at the 1812–1826 rates. I did not convert rice to silver; the record would need an Osaka rice price for those years.

**A6. Who paid for the charcoal: the farmer, in the itinerant model.** Archive copy: Kawashima 2012, `https://www.ritsumei.ac.jp/acd/cg/lt/rb/627/627PDF/kawashima.pdf`.
- 『清良記』 itself: 「四人役は、鍛冶すみきり、鍛冶の手傳いに入、年中農具をする故、なくて叶わず。」 (translation: "The fourth man's duty is cutting charcoal for the smith and serving as the smith's helper, making the farm tools through the year, so he cannot be done without.")
- Furushima's reading as Kawashima reports it: 「農家は農具製造用の鍛冶炭を蓄うべきものとされ、農民は自ら向う槌を打つ」 (translation: "the farm household was to store smith's charcoal for making its tools, and the farmer himself swung the striker's sledge")
- Kawashima dates this to 1661–1681 at the latest. In this model the travelling smith's charcoal cost is zero.

### B. Output per year

**B1. Takefu (Fuchū), Echizen: a sickle-making town in aggregate.** Archive copy: `https://www.library-archives.pref.fukui.lg.jp/fukui/07/kenshi/T4/T4-3-01-02-03-04.htm`.
- 「慶応の頃福井藩が諸物産に課した雑税のうちでは、鎌・菜刀八〇万七〇〇〇丁に永三貫七五〇文が課せられており」 (translation: "among the miscellaneous taxes Fukui domain laid on products in the Keiō years, 807,000 sickles and vegetable knives were taxed at 3 kan 750 mon in ei")
- 「文久二年に鍛冶仲間から「制産役所」に出した願書によると、一年間に最小限二〇〇〇個（推定鎌数約五七万丁）を役所に納品できるとしている」 (translation: "a petition the smiths' guild sent to the production office in 1862 says they could deliver at least 2,000 bundles a year (an estimated 570,000 sickles)")
- The same page says 「元治・慶応年間に…売上高は二万両」 (translation: "sales in the Genji and Keiō years were 20,000 ryō"). It does not say this is yearly, so I did not divide it.

Archive copy: `.../T4-3-01-02-03-02.htm`.
- 「鍛冶職の数は寛政九年には七二軒、嘉永三年には一一一軒になり、慶応二年（一八六六）には一二九軒に増加した」 (translation: "smith households numbered 72 in 1797, 111 in 1850 and 129 in 1866"). The same page says the guild controlled 「使用人の雇い入れ」 (the hiring of workers), and about a quarter of houses held two bellows shares.

## 2. Reading

- **Tool price against inputs: ATTESTED ONLY FOR Qing Beijing 1736.** There the sickle and wrought iron are on one list, and the iron is roughly a quarter of the price. For Japan the hoe price (1822 Sakai, 5.5–5.6 monme) and the repair charges (1841 Echizen) are attested, and iron is attested only in rice per kan. Any Japanese ratio is our arithmetic across sources, with the 350 me weight caveat. Where the smith travelled, the farmer supplied the charcoal.
- **Items per year: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** a specialized town trade in aggregate (Takefu, about 4,400–6,250 sickles per household a year by our arithmetic) and for Sakai tobacco knives per night (1761). For a village smith, SILENT.
- **Gross and net income: SILENT** for any smith. Only the Edo carpenter's year exists, and it is third-hand.
- **Crew: ATTESTED.** A master with one or two helpers, or with grown sons (Mino and Hida, 1834–1840). Qing small workshops hired one or two, mostly when busy.
- **Smith's rice: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** 20th-century practice. For before 1868 it is SILENT. Searches: archive terms 鍛冶米, 村鍛冶, 先掛, 鍬先; web queries 「村 鍛冶 給米 一軒 升 江戸時代 鍬 先掛 年 米で支払」 and 「江戸時代 鍬 値段 文 鍛冶 先掛け 代 村方文書」. Neither found a village document.
