I found prices for cheap booklets and lending fees in Japan, and Ming book prices and a Qing copying quota in China. Two parts came up SILENT: the price of a single volume of a Japanese classic, primer or reference work, and what a Japanese copyist was paid per leaf or per day. Most Japanese figures are secondary sources quoting 長友千代治 or 鈴木俊幸. The best one is 佐藤's article, built on Bakin's letters.

The archive holds no book prices. I searched 貸本, 草双紙, 黄表紙, 筆耕, 写本, kibyoshi, Kornicki, 赤本, 合巻, Sibao, 书价 and 往来物. The only hit that bore on the question was the archived Brook excerpt (source 7). Chow, J-STAGE and NCCU are PDFs that `make source-pages` could not read, so I fetched them with curl and pdftotext. The J-STAGE article is OCR of a two-column scan; I quote only clean runs and give corrections in [ ].

## Sources

**1. Lending fee per loan**, from 長友千代治『近世貸本屋の研究』(1997, p.29), quoted at https://intojapanwaraku.com/rock/culture-rock/128664/ (Edo; period not given in the quote)
「見料は四、五冊物で銀三分から四分、現在の金で二百四十円から三百二十円ぐらい。一冊物で銀二分、百六十円ぐらい、十冊物以上になると銀一匁前後、八百円ぐらい」…「多くの人は半年から1か月ぐらい借りて読んでいたようです。」
(translation) "The reading fee was 3 to 4 fun of silver for a work in four or five volumes, about 240-320 yen today; 2 fun of silver, about 160 yen, for a one-volume work; about 1 monme of silver, about 800 yen, for works of ten volumes or more." "Most people seem to have borrowed for anything from half a year to a month."
- 分 here is silver fun, a tenth of a monme, not the gold bu. So a one-volume loan is 0.2 monme (labelled arithmetic). I have not converted it to copper.

**2. Lending fee in 蔦屋重三郎's time** (c.1770s-1790s), from https://www.nippon.com/ja/japan-topics/c14903/, attributed to 長友千代治『江戸時代の図書流通』
「料金は時代によって異なるが、蔦重が生きた時代は1冊6〜30文。蕎麦一杯の値段が16文だから、リーズナブルだったといえよう。」
(translation) "The fee varied with the period, but in Tsutaju's lifetime it was 6-30 mon a volume. A bowl of soba cost 16 mon, so it was reasonable."
- This is a lending fee, not a purchase price.

**3. Kibyōshi purchase price and leaf count** (An'ei-Bunka, 1772-1818), from https://serai.jp/news/magazine-news/1213187 (magazine, no citation)
「●体裁中本型（美濃本二つ折り）５丁（10ページ）を１巻として、通常２〜３巻（冊）で構成 ●価格１冊６文（約330円）」
(translation) "Format: chūbon size (Mino paper folded in two), 5 leaves (10 pages) to a volume, usually 2-3 volumes to a work. Price: 6 mon a volume (about 330 yen)."

**4. Kibyōshi price, second figure**, from https://note.com/daiwadou/n/n9825ff587b92, citing 鈴木俊幸『蔦屋重三郎』
「黄表紙は非常に安価で、1冊が 8文、3冊（1作品）で 24文ほどでした。」
(translation) "Kibyōshi were very cheap: 8 mon a volume, about 24 mon for a three-volume work."
- Sources 3 and 4 disagree (6 vs 8 mon), and both are tertiary. I have not chosen between them.

**5. Leaf count of 草双紙**, from https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/草双紙, citing 『草双紙事典』2006
「1冊5丁単位で、2-3冊ないし5-6冊で1編となることが多い」…「紙数は5丁（10ページ）が基本」(赤本)…「5丁1冊の冊数が増え、10 - 15丁を纏めて綴じたのが合巻」
(translation) "In units of 5 leaves a volume, often 2-3 or 5-6 volumes to a work." "The basic length is 5 leaves (10 pages)" (of akahon). "As the number of 5-leaf volumes grew, gōkan bound 10-15 leaves together."

**6. Gōkan and yomihon trade prices**, from 佐藤［given name illegible in the OCR］「馬琴の潤筆料と板元」『近世文藝』59 (1994), https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kinseibungei/59/0/59_21/_pdf/-char/ja (Tenpō era, Edo, from Bakin's letters)
- 「天保期の合巻の卸値は四十丁もので百七十六文、袋入本の卸値は二匁である。」
  (translation) "In the Tenpō era the wholesale price of a 40-leaf gōkan was 176 mon; of a bagged edition, 2 monme."
- Yomihon, Bakin's letter about 『松浦佐用媛石魂録』後編: 「仲ケ問［仲間］うり直段十二匁位…中ケ間うり正味十五匁ニうり出し」
  (translation) "The trade price was expected to be about 12 monme … [the publisher] put it out at a net trade price of 15 monme." The note gives the work's length as 「全百六十六丁、そのうち挿絵が二十一丁」 (translation: "166 leaves in all, 21 of them illustrations").
- 佐藤's own summary: 「一部二朱（七匁五分）が相場であったようであるが、このころの新板は一分（十五匁）以上」
  (translation) "2 shu (7.5 monme) a set seems to have been the going rate [for reprints of an older yomihon], but new editions of this time were 1 bu (15 monme) or more."
- All of these are wholesale or trade prices, not retail.
- Labelled arithmetic: 40 leaves is 8 volumes of 5 leaves, so 176 mon is about 22 mon a volume, wholesale.

**6a. Agreeing figures**, from https://www.fumikura.net/other/column.html (高木元, citing 浜田啓介 1953 and 服部仁 1974)
「読本は元手がかかるうえに1集15～20匁（約9000円）と高く…一方合巻は安上がりのうえ1編1匁7、8分（約900円）で4000～5000部売れた」
(translation) "Yomihon were costly to make and dear at 15-20 monme a set … while gōkan were cheap to make and sold 4,000-5,000 copies at 1.7-1.8 monme a work."
- A gōkan work is 8 volumes here. This probably comes from the same Bakin correspondence as 佐藤, and it agrees with him.

**7. Ming books** (Wanli, 1573-1620), from Brook『崩盘』, the Chinese edition of his book, excerpted at https://www.yicai.com/news/102960940.html (archived). The quote below is the Chinese translation, not Brook's English.
「能为大众消费的书籍每卷仅售6分，但这只是一套书的一卷分册而非全集。最便宜的书是福建书坊出版印刷的大量廉价图书。…我所搜集的78部书的价格，均价为2两，其中四分之一书的价格低于1两。」
(translation) "Books for mass consumption sold for only 6 fen a juan, but that was one juan of a set, not the whole work. The cheapest were the mass of cheap books printed by Fujian publishers. … The 78 book prices I collected average 2 taels; a quarter of them were under 1 tael."
- A 卷 is a section of a text, not a bound volume (册).

**8. Ming and early Qing book prices**, from 周启荣 (Kai-wing Chow)「明清印刷书籍成本、价格及其商品价值的研究」『浙江大学学报』40:1 (2010), https://www.zjujournals.com/soc/CN/Y2010/V40/I1/5 (late Ming to early Qing)
- 「沈津…共找到２７种上面有标价的明刊本…其中最贵的是１０册的《本草》，４．９两，最便宜的是３０多卷的《万宝全书》，１钱。…２７种中有１６种的价格是１两或低于１两的」
  (translation) "Shen Jin found 27 Ming imprints with marked prices … the dearest a 10-volume Bencao at 4.9 taels, the cheapest the Wanbao quanshu of 30-odd juan at 1 qian … 16 of the 27 were 1 tael or less."
- 「一般的书籍标价大概不会高于１两银，不少单册书只卖两三钱。」
  (translation) "Ordinary books were probably not priced above 1 tael; many single volumes sold for only 2 or 3 qian."
- On hand copies in 毛扆's catalogue: 「对于单册的手抄本来说，其价格应该比印刷本要贵，但不超过０．４两。」
  (translation) "A single-volume manuscript copy should have cost more than a printed one, but not over 0.4 tael."
- Cost of a printed 24-page pamphlet: 「每一本的成本也不过是０．０１１两银」 (translation: "each copy cost no more than 0.011 tael of silver").
- The 刻工 rates in the same article (0.02-0.05 tael per hundred characters, about 100 characters a day) are for carving blocks, not copying.

**9. A copyist's daily output in Qing China** (Qianlong, 1770s-80s), from https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/四庫全書
「四库馆建立了"稽核字数考勤簿"，规定"每人每日写一千字"，"五年共限写一百八十万字"。…"缮写二百万字列为一等，一百六十五万字列为第二等"，以誊写字数作为议叙的方式。」
(translation) "The Siku office kept an attendance register checking characters written, setting 'a thousand characters per man per day' and '1.8 million characters in five years' … 'two million characters ranks first class, 1.65 million second', the characters copied being the basis for rewards of official rank."
- The reward was official rank (议叙), not a wage. I have not converted characters to leaves because I have no page format.

**10. A Japanese fair copy, 筆工 and 画工 costs together** (Kōka 3, 1846), from source 6
「筆エ［工］・画工潤筆、彼是ニ一向凡十金余も費用懸り候」
(translation) "The copyist's and the illustrator's fees and the rest came to some ten-odd ryō in all."
- This is 木村黙老's fair-copy manuscript (板下本) of the 40-leaf 『新編金瓶梅』第十集, illustrated by Kunisada.
- 佐藤 says the total included Bakin's 8 ryō fee, and that it was 「どんなに高くても十五両以下」 (translation: "fifteen ryō at most").
- Labelled arithmetic: the copyist and illustrator together come to about 2-7 ryō for 40 leaves. That cannot be split between them.
- The same article's 「筆耕部分が一丁一分二朱」 is the block-cutter's rate for a text leaf, not the copyist's pay.

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** cheap popular booklets, lending fees, and Chinese book prices.

- **Japanese booklets** have prices and a 5-leaf volume: a kibyōshi at 6 or 8 mon a volume retail (tertiary). For the Tenpō era there are wholesale and trade prices from Bakin's letters: a 40-leaf gōkan at 176 mon wholesale, and a yomihon set at 7.5 to 15 monme trade.
- **Lending** cost 6-30 mon a volume in the late 18th century. 長友's scale, as quoted, runs 0.2 monme of silver for a one-volume work to about 1 monme for ten volumes or more, borrowed for a month to half a year.
- **China:** late-Ming single volumes ran 0.1 to 1 tael, often 2-3 qian; mass-market juan cost 6 fen; a hand copy under 0.4 tael.
- **SILENT:** the price of one volume of an 往来物, the 四書 or a 節用集 in Japan, and a Japanese copyist's rate per leaf, per volume or per day, or leaves a day. The only Japanese figure is one deluxe fair copy where the copyist's and illustrator's fees are lumped together (source 10).
- **Copyist output and pay, China:** the Siku quota of 1,000 characters a day is attested, but the pay was official rank, not cash.
