### 0255 - the sanctuary (Tama district, Musashi)
- 1841 Shibasaki one-bay sanctuary: winning carpenter bid over 30 ryo against 50-80 - ATTESTED; that it was for the
  sanctuary alone - this project's reading (the worship hall was contracted separately).
- Worship hall 11 ryo 2 bu; wall-panel carving 3 ryo a panel outside the contract; building funds over 120 ryo - ATTESTED.
- Timber and village labor bought and paid by the headman - ATTESTED; outside the bid - reading.
- 30 ryo = about 600-1,100 craftsman-days, or about 2,000 Kyoto laborer-days - project's arithmetic (60 monme a ryo,
  wages from other years and places).
- Craftsman wage, Shibasaki 1841 - SILENT; nearest Kumehara 1830 (Saitama district, 11 years): carpenter 2 monme + 1
  monme allowance (the allowance reading is the project's). Unskilled - SILENT; nearest Kyoto 1802-1804, 0.92 monme
  (spring rate), about 38 years.
- 1749 Kasuga repair: 187 carpenter-and-sawyer man-days + 24 Edo roofer man-days; rates 1-1.67 monme and 3 monme -
  ATTESTED (same job). Unskilled - SILENT; nearest Kyoto 1745-1754, 0.91 monme.
- 1805 Kasuga repair about 61 ryo 2 bu 3 shu, wages 44.2%, materials 16.2% - ATTESTED. Wages - SILENT; nearest Kumehara
  1818 carpenter 200 mon (13 years later); Kyoto 1802-1804 laborer 0.92 monme.
- Mitake 1764-1770 repair (bay just over 4.2 shaku) about 9 ryo 2 bu, wages and rice 66.3%, materials 29.1% - ATTESTED.
  Nearest wages: Tama 1749 (15-21 years), Kyoto 1745-1754.
- Kumehara 1818: carpenter 200 mon, sawyer 180, carter 180 (39 villages) - ATTESTED; three graded rates 116/100/80 mon
  have their trade blank in the transcription; unskilled - SILENT.
- Kumehara 1830 notice board: prints 50 carpenter-days at 2 monme, but its sums fit 20 - ATTESTED as printed.
- New-build carpenter-days - SILENT. Hokora - SILENT.

### 0256 - the roof
- 1749 copper re-roofing (Kasuga, bay 7.8 shaku): total about 43 ryo 1 bu; materials 53.1%, wages 14.6%, rice etc.
  12.3%, ridge-raising/enshrinement/sundries 20.0% - ATTESTED (page image settles the order; the roof pass, and the
  interim Q2 comment on #6, had 12.3% and 20.0% swapped). Same job's wages ATTESTED (see 0255); unskilled - SILENT,
  nearest Kyoto 1745-1754.
- 1772-1773 copper tiles: 11,000 (1 x 1.2 shaku) for just under 30 kan of silver, about 2.7 monme a tile - ATTESTED ONLY
  FOR a daimyo's mansions bought in Osaka. Wages - SILENT; nearest Tama 1749 roofer 3 monme (23 years), Kyoto 1745-1754
  laborer 0.91.
- Edo carpenter: 5.4 monme meals included, 1804-1830 prices - ATTESTED, third-hand (blog of a popular book); 0.125 ryo
  (7.5 monme) meals included 1853-1860, and the 1854 market rates 4 / 3.75 + 1.2 meals, after 1855 45 / 37.5 + 1.5 -
  ATTESTED. Edo unskilled - SILENT (only "broadly equal to Kyoto" from 1818).
- The 1751 shogunate standard (ja.wikipedia 積算基準) was left out: the 1749 job's own rates stand beside it, and the
  key cap was spent.
- Cypress bark, shingle, roofer-days a tsubo, persimmon tannin, roof area - SILENT (absence notes).

### 0257 - torii, stone, earthwork
- Osaka 1628 earthwork: 7, 9 and 10 monme a tsubo of earth - ATTESTED ONLY FOR castle work; the tsubo of earth is
  undefined. Same ledger: unskilled 0.8-1.2 monme a man (per day is a reading); five carpenters 6.5 monme (1.3 each if
  one day, arithmetic). The 17.922 southern-moat line was dropped: it combines several kinds of work.
- Rubble 1620: 23 men a tsubo, 16 loads a man, no distance - ATTESTED.
- Stone hauling 1620: 1.3 and 1.5 monme a man against quarry labor 0.8-1 and the Nakagawa reckoning 1; 1621 Osaka 1,
  Fushimi 0.5 - ATTESTED.
- Pack-horse load: two Oya-stone blocks (5 sun x 1 x 3 shaku); one man carried a 150 kg block - ATTESTED (museum page).
- Great stone torii, Hatogamine (Iida, Shinano), 1708-1709, "said to have cost" 3,200 ryo - ATTESTED ONLY FOR a great
  torii, an upper bound. Wages - SILENT; nearest Tama 1749 and Kyoto 1745-1754, about 40 years.
- Wooden torii, fence posts, stepping stones, mason-days, burial mound, carrying stone a ri - SILENT.

### 0258 - rites and furnishings
- Fushimi Inari installation fees: before 1864, six grades (one gold ban; 5 and 3 silver pieces; 500, 300, 200 hiki);
  1864, seven grades (20 ryo, 7.5, 3, 2, 1.25, 3 bu, 2 bu) - ATTESTED ONLY FOR installing Fushimi's Inari, paid to
  Fushimi. Of 46 certificates, 28 the abbreviated grade - ATTESTED. 100 hiki = 1 bu - the project's reading.
- About 1800 the cheap grade = about 50 Kyoto laborer-days or 10 Osaka carpenter-days - arithmetic, on a 60-monme ryo
  borrowed from the 1850s Edo table (labeled a reading). 1864 - wages SILENT; nearest Edo carpenter 0.125 ryo (1853-1860),
  so 3 bu = 6 carpenter-days.
- A local monk's or priest's dedication, the ground rite, depositing remains, the bell and rope, the plaque, the
  offering table - SILENT.
