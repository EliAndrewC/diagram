# Pass - small sanctuary cost (#6 Q1) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

I found no record of the carpenter-days for building a new one-bay sanctuary, and nothing on hokora. What two Tama-district papers do give:

- the contract price for a new one-bay nagare-zukuri sanctuary in 1841;
- the craftsman-days and cost shares of two repairs;
- day wages, including a craftsman and unskilled pair from a nearby Musashi village.

Questions 0215–0218 have no cost, wage or 人工 (craftsman-day) figures at all; their only numbers are building and ground sizes. So everything below is new to the record.

There is a second paper beyond the lead: the No. 420 paper (Kawakami 1991, read on 2026-10-18 by an earlier pass) has a sequel, No. 435 (1992), and the new-build figures are in the sequel. Both PDFs are scanned images with no text layer. `make source-pages` refused them as unreadable, so I fetched them with WebFetch, ran Japanese OCR (I installed tesseract-ocr-jpn on this machine), and checked the quoted passages against the page images. Only quote 4 was checked against the OCR text alone. Nothing was written into the clone.

## 1. Sources

**1. 河上信行「江戸時代の村落における寺社造営と名主の役割」, 日本建築学会計画系論文報告集 No. 435 (1992-05), pp. 87–95** – https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/435/0/435_KJ00004076856/_pdf. It describes 1749–1842, Tama district.

- Note 25 (the new sanctuary, Shibasaki village, Tenpō period):
  「八幡神社本殿は一間社流造軒唐破風付，身舎桁行柱間6尺。文政7年（1824）4月4日前身本殿焼失後，同10年3月に「伐木削木発起」し，天保12年8月15日に竣工した。造営費用は「焼枯」の杉檜売却金30両余，氏子日掛の積金16両余，6ヶ年にわたる多摩川・秋川の筏師奉納金20両余，村の家別の奉加金40両，有力の信者金17,8両，計金120両余からなる。」
  (translation) "The Hachiman shrine sanctuary is one-bay nagare-zukuri with a cusped gable at the eaves, its core bay 6 shaku. After the earlier sanctuary burned on 1824-4-4, timber cutting began in 1827-3 and it was finished on Tenpō 12 [1841]-8-15. The building funds were: over 30 ryō from selling fire-killed cedar and cypress, over 16 ryō from parishioners' daily savings, over 20 ryō offered by Tama and Aki river raftsmen over six years, 40 ryō from the village's household subscription, and 17–18 ryō from leading believers. In all, over 120 ryō."
- Note 29 (carpenter bids for that job, from 「鎮守八幡宮建立記」):
  「八王子の大工小町土佐（粂次郎）が80両余，そのほかの大工が50,60両余を提示した中，拝嶋の臼井粂次郎が30両余で落札した。」
  (translation) "The Hachiōji carpenter Komachi Tosa (Kumejirō) bid over 80 ryō and other carpenters over 50 and 60 ryō; Usui Kumejirō of Haijima won the bid at over 30 ryō."
- Note 38 (the worship hall was contracted separately, which suggests the 30 ryō was for the sanctuary):
  「拝嶋大工粂次郎江，八幡宮拝殿方建方造作共杣木挽一式金拾壱両弐分ニ而渡し方取究候事」
  (translation) "Agreed with the Haijima carpenter Kumejirō to pay 11 ryō 2 bu for the Hachiman worship hall, raising and joinery together with the woodcutters' and sawyers' work, as one lump sum."
- Note 44 (OCR text, not checked against the image): 「胴羽目の彫刻作料分1面3両は大工請負分に含まれずに中島家，井上家ら少数の有力者の寄進に依存」
  (translation) "The carving fee for the wall panels, 3 ryō per panel, was not in the carpenter's contract and depended on donations from a few leading families."
- Note 32: materials were bought separately by the headman, e.g. 「八幡宮用杉松四本壱駄…八王子より引取，品切大高直」
  (translation) "four cedar and pine logs, one horse-load, for the Hachiman shrine, fetched from Hachiōji – scarce and very dear."
- Note 14 (the repair benchmark, Kasuga shrine sanctuary, 1749):
  「大工・木挽に対して1人工あたり金1分に付9人（銀1.67匁），10人，15人の手間賃で計187人工分，江戸の屋根屋に対してはより高額の1人工あたり銀3匁，計24人工分が数度に分けて支払われた」
  (translation) "Carpenters and sawyers were paid at 9, 10 or 15 workers per 1 bu of gold (the top grade being silver 1.67 monme per day), 187 craftsman-days in all; the Edo roofers were paid more, silver 3 monme per day, 24 craftsman-days, in several instalments."

**2. 河上信行「江戸時代後期における中小寺社造営の費用調達」, 同 No. 420 (1991-02)** – https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/420/0/420_KJ00004076677/_pdf. It describes 1749–1858, the Ōme area.

- Kasuga shrine sanctuary, 1749 repair (bay 7 shaku 8 sun):
  「支出(約金43両1分)は材料代(53.1%)，大工を含む職人の手間賃(14.6%)，米代ほか(12.3%)，上棟・遷宮費用・「諸費用」(20.0%)」
  (translation) "Expenditure, about 43 ryō 1 bu: materials 53.1%, craftsmen's wages including carpenters 14.6%, rice etc. 12.3%, ridge-raising and enshrinement costs and 'sundries' 20.0%."
  The 1805 repair cost about 61 ryō 2 bu 3 shu: 「大工・彩色の手間料(44.2%)，材料代(16.2%)」 (translation) "carpenters' and painters' wages 44.2%, materials 16.2%."
- Mitake shrine sanctuary, the target size:
  「本殿は桁行柱間4尺2寸余という小規模な一間社流造であり，棟札写によれば元禄2年(1689)に建立され」
  (translation) "The sanctuary is a small one-bay nagare-zukuri, its bay a little over 4 shaku 2 sun; by the copy of its ridge plaque it was built in 1689."
  The paper costs only its 1764–70 repair: 「支出(約金9両2分)は職人手間・飯米(66.3%)，材料(29.1%)，酒代ほか(4.6%)」 (translation) "Expenditure, about 9 ryō 2 bu: craftsmen's wages and their rice 66.3%, materials 29.1%, sake etc. 4.6%." There is no figure for the 1689 build.

**3. Archived copy of the Kumehara village (Musashi, Saitama district) documents, okayasukebunsyo.pdf** – `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/53/539d0fda2d5b/20261002T162356Z/text.txt`. It describes 1818 and 1830. This is a neighbouring district, not Tama.

- Doc. 31, 1818 craftsmen's wage rule agreed by 39 villages:
  「弐百文 大工手間代／百八拾文 木挽手間代／百八拾文 車力手間代」
  (translation) "200 mon carpenter's day wage; 180 mon sawyer's; 180 mon carter's."
- Doc. 44, 1830 estimate for rebuilding the village notice board, a small timber structure:
  「一五拾人 大工手間賃 但壱人ニ付銀二匁ツゝ 此銀四拾匁…銀弐拾匁 夫持壱人ニ付銀一匁ツゝ」
  (translation) "50 [carpenter-days]: carpenter's wages at silver 2 monme per person; and an allowance at silver 1 monme per person." The text also gives the total as silver 40 monme, which does not match 50 × 2.
  As printed, 銀二匁ツゝ is the wage per carpenter-day. 夫持 is most likely the per-person food allowance (扶持) paid on top of the wage, not an unskilled worker's wage. In that reading it puts the carpenter's full day at 3 monme, which the derivation below uses. If it were instead a labourer's wage, the 600 floor below would not hold.

## 2. Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** three narrower things:
- **Repair labour and cost shares.** The 1749 Kasuga sanctuary repair took 187 carpenter and sawyer days plus 24 Edo roofer days, about 211 in all. That is inside your 190–250 band for a minor repair, so the ledger's repair figure looks normal. Materials were about half the cost when Edo copper was involved (53%), and 16–29% otherwise.
- **The contract price, but not the day count, for a new one-bay sanctuary.** Shibasaki's new sanctuary (1827–41) is bigger than yours: a 6-shaku bay, a cusped gable and carved panels. The winning carpenter bid was "over 30 ryō", against rival bids of 50–80. Timber, the panel carvings (3 ryō each) and village labour were paid for outside that bid.
- **Day wages.** Tama 1749: carpenter and sawyer up to silver 1.67 monme a day, Edo roofer 3 monme. Kumehara 1818: carpenter 200 mon, sawyer and carter 180 mon. Kumehara 1830: carpenter silver 2 monme plus 1 monme food allowance.

**My own derivation, not attested:** at about 60 monme to the ryō, 30 ryō is about 1,800 monme. At 1.67–3 monme a day, with or without food, that would buy about 600–1,100 craftsman-days. This assumes the contract was almost all wages, which fits the materials being bought separately. If so, a plain 4-shaku sanctuary at 480–640 craftsman-days is plausible, not inflated, as long as it is about half or less the work of a 6-shaku carved one.

**SILENT for hokora.** I searched 石祠 建立 代金 両 江戸時代 石工 村 文書, 宮建立入用帳 大工 手間 人 金 両 村文書 翻刻 小宮 祠, and an English query on small-shrine costs, with no result. The archive had 0 hits for 造営帳, 普請帳, 祠 建立 and 社殿 新築. The nearest small-structure comparators are the 1830 notice board (50 carpenter-days) and, from paper 1, a pair of stone lanterns at 4 ryō 3 bu.
