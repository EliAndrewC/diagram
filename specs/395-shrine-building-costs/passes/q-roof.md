# Pass - shrine roof and finish (#6 Q2) (research-pass, 2026-10-18; its reply verbatim)

I found one Edo-period per-job cost for a small shrine roof, and it is for copper. Nothing turned up for cypress bark, shingle or persimmon tannin.

**Archive:** no archived page gives a price, a roofer-day figure or a roof area. I searched 檜皮葺, 檜皮, 杮葺, 栩葺, 銅板葺, 柿渋, 屋根屋, 葺替, 流造, nagare-zukuri, shingle roof and 本途帳 one at a time, then grepped the hits for 両, 匁, 文, 坪, 人工 and 費.

## Sources that bear

**1. 河上信行, 「江戸時代後期における中小寺社造営の費用調達 ―名主吉野家文書にみる旧武蔵国多摩郡の中小寺社造営―」, 日本建築学会計画系論文報告集 420 (Feb 1991), p.96.** https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/420/0/420_KJ00004076677/_pdf. The evidence is from 1749 (寛延2) and the shrine is the Kasuga honden at Nogami village, Tama district. The PDF is a scan; the text below is its OCR, with spacing normalized and nothing else changed.
- 「流造の本殿は，棟札によれば慶安元年（1648）に建立され，寛延2年（1749），文化2年（1805）ほかに修理された。…寛延期修理は屋根の銅板葺替を中心とする破風や懸魚の取替…江戸の屋根葺職人…」 — "The nagare-zukuri honden, by its ridge tag, was built in 1648 and repaired in 1749, 1805 and other years. … The 1749 repair centred on re-roofing in copper plate, with replacement of the bargeboards and gable pendants … roofers from Edo …" (translation)
- 「本殿は桁行柱間7尺8寸と一間社としては規模が大きく」 — "The honden, with a bay of 7 shaku 8 sun, is large for a one-bay shrine." (translation)
- 「支出（約金43両1分）は材料代（53.1％），大工を含む職人の手間賃（14.6％），上棟・遷宮費用（12.3％），米代ほか「諸費用」（20.0％）…屋根葺替のために江戸から調達した銅板・銅釘の占める比重が大きい。」 — "Spending (about 43 ryō 1 bu): materials 53.1%, craftsmen's wages including carpenters 14.6%, ridge-raising and enshrinement costs 12.3%, rice and other costs 20.0%. … The copper plate and copper nails bought from Edo for the re-roofing make up a large share." (translation)
- The OCR's two-column layout scrambles the order of the four shares. I have paired each share with its label as the layout version of the page reads.
- The paper also lists the itemized account books behind these totals: G30「宮葺替諸入用帳」 and 7-4「金峰神社修覆并上屋工手間其外入用」.

**2. 宮崎勝美, 「大名屋敷の作事・普請と江戸遺跡」 (江戸遺跡研究会, 2000).** https://edo.jpn.org/cyber/2000/miyazaki2000.htm. The evidence is from 1772–73: the Hagi domain's Edo mansions, from the 「江戸三御屋敷新御作事記録」.
- 「銅瓦は１尺×１尺２寸規格のもの11,000枚で銀高は30貫弱である」 — "Copper tiles, 1 shaku × 1 shaku 2 sun, 11,000 pieces, for just under 30 kan of silver." (translation)
- 「檜皮３寸５分釘80,000本、檜皮３寸釘3,000本」 — "Cypress-bark nails, 3 sun 5 bu: 80,000; cypress-bark nails, 3 sun: 3,000." (translation) This shows only that some cypress bark was laid in that job. No bark price is given.

**3. 斎藤修, 「幕末–明治の賃金変動再考」, 経済研究 44(4), Oct 1993.** https://econ-review.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/files/1995/keizaikenkyu4404330.pdf. The evidence is Edo carpenters' wages, 1853–56. The text is an extracted text layer.
- 「『我国商品相場統計表』の大工賃金をみると，1853年から1860年まで飯料込で一律0.125両（銀7匁5分）となっている．これにたいして，東京商法会議所の大蔵省商務局宛上申書に附された大工賃金表によると，1854年の相場は上手間料4匁，並手間料3匁7分5厘，飯料1匁2分であったが，大地震の翌年には上手間料45匁，並手間料37匁5分，飯料1匁5分に騰貴した．」 — "In the 『我国商品相場統計表』 the carpenter's wage is a flat 0.125 ryō (7 momme 5 fun of silver), meal allowance included, from 1853 to 1860. By contrast, the carpenters' wage table attached to the Tokyo Chamber of Commerce's report gives 1854 rates of 4 momme for top-grade work and 3 momme 7 fun 5 rin for ordinary work, plus a 1 momme 2 fun meal allowance. In the year after the 1855 earthquake these rose to 45 momme, 37 momme 5 fun, and 1 momme 5 fun for meals." (translation)

**4. ja.wikipedia 「積算基準」, which summarizes 西和夫『江戸建築と本途帳』 (1974).** https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A9%8D%E7%AE%97%E5%9F%BA%E6%BA%96. The evidence is the shogunate's 1751 labour standard.
- 「大工の手間賃の基準は、米１升5合、となっている。…１日当たり米１升5合に相当する額が「銀」で支払われていたという。」 — "The standard carpenter's wage is 1 shō 5 gō of rice; … the silver equivalent of 1 shō 5 gō of rice per day was reportedly paid." (translation)
- I recorded this page as rejected because its per-tsubo figures (e.g. 102人/坪 for a copper-roofed mansion) count carpenter-days for the whole building per floor tsubo, not roofing. That rejection covers the roofing part only. The wage line is usable, but it is a wiki summary of 西 1974, not the 本途帳 itself.

**5. 不二考匠, 「【歴史小話】大工の家計」 (note.com, 2023).** https://note.com/takamasa_jindoh/n/nef9a0e8fdb30. The evidence is an Edo carpenter in the Bunka–Bunsei era (1804–30). This is third-hand: a blog quoting 『江戸のお勘定』, which it believes draws on 『文政年間漫録』.
- 「大工の手間賃（食事代込み）：銀5匁４分」 — "Carpenter's day wage (meals included): 5 momme 4 fun of silver." (translation)

## My reading, one verdict per part

- **Copper, per job: ATTESTED.**
  - In 1749 the re-roofing in copper of a one-bay nagare-zukuri honden in a Tama village was the core of a repair costing about 43 ryō 1 bu in all. Materials were 53.1%, mostly copper plate and nails bought in Edo. All craftsmen's wages were 14.6%, which works out to about 6.3 ryō (my arithmetic).
  - That total also covers the bargeboards and gable pendants, the enshrinement and food. It is not a price for the roof alone.
  - The bay is 7 shaku 8 sun, about twice the GM's 4 shaku.
- **Copper, per unit: ATTESTED ONLY FOR copper roof tiles on a daimyo mansion.** In Osaka in 1772–73, 11,000 tiles of 1 × 1.2 shaku cost just under 30 kan of silver, about 2.7 momme each (my arithmetic). The record gives no coverage per tsubo, so I derive no per-tsubo price. The 80-per-tsubo coverage rate in the same article (「八拾枚物」) is for clay tiles, not copper.
- **Cypress bark (檜皮葺) per tsubo or per job: SILENT.**
- **Shingle (杮葺 / 栩葺) cost: SILENT.**
- **Roofer-days per tsubo (檜皮師, 屋根屋 人工): SILENT.**
- **Persimmon tannin (柿渋) per 升: SILENT.** The Asahi Group Foundation report (2006) gives no Edo price. It names a price relation without figures (aged tannin cost 7–8 times new) and the number of tannin sellers in Edo (渋屋: 17 in the 1700s, 300–400 in the 1800s).
- **Roof area of a one-bay, ~4 shaku nagare-zukuri: SILENT.**
- **Unskilled day wage: SILENT.** Saito names a Mitsui day-labourer (日雇賃) series but gives no figure. The ledger had already rejected that paper for this point.
- **Wages beside the prices are mismatched in time and place:**
  - **1749 Kasuga job (Tama):** there is no carpenter rate for Tama. The nearest is the 1751 shogunate standard of 1 shō 5 gō of rice a day, paid in silver.
  - **1772 Hagi tiles:** those were bought in Osaka. The only Edo carpenter rates I could read are from 1804–30 (5 momme 4 fun, third-hand) and 1853–56 (3.75–4 momme plus 1.2 momme meals, or 7.5 momme meals included). Both are 30–80 years later and from another city.

## Leads not followed
- 清水寺本堂's 1741 (寛保元年) document on its cypress-bark roof (Nikkei article) may give bark sizes or quantities.
- 「出雲大社延享造営傳」 (1744) is a carpenter's record of the Izumo rebuilding, which was roofed in cypress bark.
- 西和夫『江戸建築と本途帳』 (1974) may hold a shogunate labour standard for shingle or bark roofing.
- 「東京業別平均賃銀累年表」 (1830–79, in 『我国商品相場統計表』) has a roofer (屋根葺) wage series, according to Saito.
- 河上 1991's source books, G30「宮葺替諸入用帳」 and 7-4「金峰神社修覆并上屋工手間其外入用」, are the itemized roofing accounts behind the percentages.
- 今井敬潤『柿渋』 (2003) and 高橋幹夫『江戸あきない図譜』 (1993) are the likeliest places for a persimmon-tannin price.
- 『文政年間漫録』 and 『馬琴日記』 (1831: 3 momme 7 fun 5 rin a day) were seen only in search summaries, not read.
