# Research - 205 tsubo in the glossary

## R1. What a tsubo is, in the record's own words

`research/homesteads.html`, "How big was the work yard, and how did the sizes spread?":

- footnote 12 (`kitamoto-mushiro-niwa`, READ): "two mushiro come to one tsubo" - the yard was counted
  in straw mats, two to the tsubo.
- footnote 15 (`tobunken-mushiro`, READ): a mushiro mat is "about 3 shaku in width and roughly 6 shaku
  in length (90 x 180 cm)". Two of those side by side are 180 x 180 cm - one ken square, about 3.3 sq m,
  36 sq ft - which is the conversion the entry itself uses (18 tsubo = 59.5 sq m; 20-30 tsubo = 66-99
  sq m).
- footnotes 19 and 21 (`kamikanai-1771-houses`, READ): house floors were counted in tsubo ("the 20 to
  29 tsubo band, 14 houses"; "if the holding increases by about 5 koku, the floor area increases by
  about 10 tsubo").

So the definition can say, from read sources only: a Japanese unit of area, a square about six feet
on a side, the floor of two straw mats laid side by side, about 3.3 square meters (36 square feet);
house floors and work yards were counted in it. Label: accurate.
