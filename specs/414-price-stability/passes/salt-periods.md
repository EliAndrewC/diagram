**Search pass, feature 414: salt and other goods across the dynasties and Edo periods (the GM's addition of 2026-10-24)**

The sources do let salt be compared across Song, Ming and Qing, but not in a single measure. The Song can be measured only in rice, because the one Song wage I found is second-hand. Ming and Qing can be measured in days of a hired worker's wage, from two Beijing Board of Works price lists. Edo is already in day-wages in record question 0273.

Four price levels are kept apart below: what the state paid the producer, what merchants paid the state, the official retail price, and the price of smuggled salt.

Wage-day figures marked "by this pass's arithmetic" are my division, not a source's.

I wrote nothing in any repository. Pages are on the ledger, and I recorded nothing as cited. I did not redo what 0273, qing.md or japan.md already hold; I cite them.

## SALT

### Song

**宋史 卷182, 食貨下四 (鹽中)**, https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/宋史/卷182 (fetched this pass)
- **Rice measure, before 1068 against 1122.** A Monopoly Goods Office memorial of 宣和4 (1122): 「古有斗米斤鹽之說，熙、豐以前，米石不過六七百，時鹽價斤為錢六七十；今米價石兩千五百至三千，而鹽仍舊六十。崇寧會定鹽價，買鹽折算，酌以中價，斤為錢四十，今一斤三十七錢，虧公稍多。」
  - (translation) "Of old it was said a dou of rice for a jin of salt. Before Xining and Yuanfeng [before 1068] rice was no more than 600–700 cash a shi, and salt then 60–70 cash a jin. Now rice is 2,500–3,000 a shi, and salt is still 60. In the Chongning years [1102–06] the salt price for the purchase reckoning was set at a middle price of 40 cash a jin; now it is 37 cash a jin, a considerable loss to the state."
  - The 60–70 and the 60 are the official retail price. The 40 and the 37 are what merchants paid the state.
- **The state's buying price and its markup.** 「鹽之入官，淮南、福建、兩浙之溫、台、明斤為錢四，杭、秀為錢六，廣南為錢五。其出，視去鹽道里遠近而上下其估，利有至十倍者。」
  - (translation) "Salt bought into the government paid 4 cash a jin in Huainan, Fujian and Wen/Tai/Ming of Liangzhe, 6 at Hang and Xiu, 5 in Guangnan. Sold out, the price was set up or down by distance from the saltworks, and the profit reached as much as tenfold."
- **Hebei, monopoly imposed in 1084, protested in 1085.** Wang Yansou: 「河北二年以來新行鹽法，所在價增一倍……聞貧家至以鹽比藥。」 (translation) "Since the new salt law in Hebei two years ago, the price has doubled everywhere … I hear poor households now reckon salt like medicine."
- **Cai Jing's vouchers.** 「蔡京更欲巧籠商賈之利……裁定買官鹽價，囊以三百斤，價以十千」 (translation) "Cai Jing wished to capture the merchants' profit … fixed the price of buying official salt at 10,000 cash a sack of 300 jin." That is merchant level, about 33 cash a jin, by this pass's arithmetic.

**宋史 卷181, 食貨下三 (鹽上)**, https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/宋史/卷181
- At 至道3 (997): 「末鹽之直，斤自四十七至八錢，有二十一等。」 (translation) "The price of sea salt ran from 47 cash down to 8 a jin, in 21 grades."

**宋史 卷183, 食貨下五**, https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/宋史/卷183
- **Official against private, Sichuan, 元祐1 (1086).** Su Zhe: 「四川數州賣邛州蒲江井官鹽，斤為錢百二十……而梓、夔路客鹽及民間販小井白鹽，價止七八十，官司遂至抑配」 (translation) "Several Sichuan prefectures sell official Pujiang-well salt at 120 cash a jin … while merchants' salt and people's small-well white salt are only 70–80, so officials resort to forced allotment."
- **Official against private, Fujian, 淳熙8 (1181).** 「鈔法罷而綱運興，官價高而私價賤，民多食私鹽而官不售，科抑之弊生矣。」 (translation) "When the voucher system was ended and official convoys began, the official price was high and the private price cheap; the people mostly ate private salt, official salt did not sell, and the evil of forced quotas arose."
- **Sichuan, 太平興國2 (977).** 「劍南諸州官糶鹽，斤為錢七十……豪民黠吏……賤市於官，貴糶於民，至有斤獲錢數百」 (translation) "Official salt in Jiannan sold at 70 cash a jin … powerful men and clerks bought cheap from the state and sold dear to the people, getting several hundred a jin."
- **Hedong, mid-11th century.** 「其入官，斤為八錢或六錢，出為錢三十六」 (translation) "Bought in at 8 or 6 cash a jin, sold out at 36."

**Song wage (second-hand only).** The Paper / 瞭望智庫, on 程民生《宋代物價研究》, https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_18498645: 「北宋中期的东南地区"丁男日佣，不过四五十文"」 (translation) "In the southeast in the mid-Northern Song, 'a grown man's day hire is no more than 40–50 cash'." The quoted phrase's original text is not named, and an exact-phrase search found nothing.

**Reading: ATTESTED.**
- Measured in rice, salt was dear in the early-to-mid Northern Song: one jin of salt cost about one dou of rice. By 1122 it was about 0.2–0.24 dou, because rice roughly quadrupled while the decreed salt price stood still. That arithmetic is the memorial's own figures. The memorial does not say why rice rose.
- Official retail ran 40–120 cash a jin depending on place and date, against a producer price of 4–8 cash; the source's own words are "profit up to tenfold".
- Monopoly reach drove the jumps: Hebei doubled when the monopoly was imposed in 1084. Private salt undercut official salt by about a third in Sichuan in 1086, and Fujian's convoy system after 1181 produced the same gap.
- If the second-hand wage is accepted, a laborer's day bought less than 1 jin of salt, about 1.2–1.75 days per jin by this pass's arithmetic.

### Ming

**明史 卷80, 食貨四 鹽法** (archived: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明史/卷80)
- Guan Huaili, 嘉靖13 (1534): 「定價太昂，息不償本，取贏之難也。私鹽四出，官鹽不行，市易之難也……嘗考祖宗時，商人中鹽納價甚輕，而灶戶煎鹽工本甚厚，今鹽價十倍於前，而工本不能十一，何以禁私鹽使不行也？……大抵正鹽賤，則私販自息。」
- (translation) "The fixed price is too high, so the return does not repay the outlay … Private salt comes from every side and official salt does not move … In the founders' time merchants paid very little for salt and the boilers were paid generously; now the salt price is ten times what it was, while the boilers get not a tenth of it. How can private salt be stopped? … In general, when official salt is cheap, smuggling stops of itself."
- The "tenfold" is the price merchants paid for licenses, not retail.
- The license price: 「引價，淮南納銀一兩九錢，淮北一兩五錢」 (translation) "license price: 1.9 taels in Huainan, 1.5 in Huaibei" (Jiajing). Not converted per jin, because the Ming license weight was not read in a source.
- **1617 綱法:** 「巡鹽御史龍遇奇立鹽政綱法，以舊引附見引行，淮南編為十綱，淮北編為十四綱」 (translation) "the salt censor Long Yuqi set up the gang system, moving old licenses with current ones; Huainan in ten gang, Huaibei in fourteen". No price effect is given.

**Ming retail and the wage beside it.** 高壽仙 2022, 《故宮學刊》 (archived: https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2023/02/10/u63e60647c73ae.pdf)
- Table 22, Ming against Qing: 「盐每斤[0.006-0.007]//0.012」. The bracketed Ming figure is from 萬曆會計錄 and 宛署雜記; 0273 already has it.
- Ming wage: 「明朝万历年间工部雇用的劳动者……最高者每工0.07两，最低者每工0.03两」 (translation) "Board of Works hires under Wanli … highest 0.07 tael a day, lowest 0.03."
- Both are Board of Works rates.

**Reading:**
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the price the state charged merchants: tenfold over the early Ming by 1534, said by a contemporary to be what drove smuggling.
- Retail, Beijing, Wanli: 0.006–0.007 tael a jin. Against the lowest Board of Works day of 0.03 tael, a day bought about 4.3–5 jin, about 0.2 days per jin, by this pass's arithmetic.
- **SILENT** on the 1617 綱法's effect on price, and on a Ming private-salt price per jin. A Bilibili column citing 萬曆會計錄 for confiscated private salt valued at 4 li a jin appeared only in a search summary; it was not read and is not evidence.

### Qing

**清史稿 卷123 食貨四 鹽法** (archived: https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/清史稿/卷123). The Changlu price of 1688, the 1728 memorial and the Jiaqing edict of 1800 are already in 0273 and qing.md and are not repeated.

**Tao Shu's Huaibei ticket reform (道光12, 1832).**
- 「蓋淮北舊額未嘗不輕，而由暢運至口岸，每引成本已達十餘兩，價不償本，故官不敵私。今票鹽……每包百斤，出場更不改捆，直抵口岸，除鹽價錢糧外，止加運費一兩，河湖船價一兩，每引五兩有奇，減於綱鹽大半。」
- (translation) "Huaibei's old quota was never heavy, but by the time salt reached its markets the cost per license was over 10 taels; the price did not repay the cost, so official salt could not compete with private. Now ticket salt … in 100-jin packages, not rebaled after leaving the works, goes straight to market; besides the salt's price and tax, only 1 tael carriage and 1 tael boat hire are added, a little over 5 taels a license, more than half below the gang salt."
- On the private trade: 「是法成本既輕，鹽質純淨，而售價又賤，私販無利，皆改領票鹽。」 (translation) "Under this law cost was light, the salt pure and the selling price cheap; smugglers had no profit and all switched to taking tickets."
- I did not compute this per jin. The source says the reform was "change the route, not the baling" (改道不改捆), and the old license's weight is not established (0273 declined for the same reason).

**Huainan ticket reform (道光29, 1849).** 「既裁浮費，又多運鹽二百斤，成本輕減過半。故開辦數月，即全運一綱之引，楚西各岸鹽價驟賤，農民歡聲雷動。」 (translation) "With surcharges cut and 200 jin more carried per license, cost fell by more than half; within months a whole year's licenses were shipped, and salt prices in the Hubei, Hunan and Jiangxi markets fell abruptly, to the farmers' loud joy."

**Changlu, cutting the price to beat smugglers.**
- 道光28 (1848): 「官鹽價貴，私鹽乘之……其鹽價每斤減制錢二文以敵私」 (translation) "official salt was dear and private salt exploited it … the salt price was cut by 2 cash a jin to beat the smugglers."
- 咸豐8 (1858): restored: 「奏准將道光二十八年減價二文起徵，名鹽斤復價」 (translation) "approved re-collecting the 2 cash cut in Daoguang 28, called restoring the salt price."
- 乾隆56 (1791), Jianchang in Jiangxi: 「依閩省時價斤減二文以敵私」 (translation) "cut 2 cash a jin, to Fujian's current price, to beat the smugglers."

**Official against private retail, c. 1830s (new to the record).**
- 吳海波, 〈清代私盐盛行的经济视角思考〉, 《鹽文化研究論叢》第四輯, p. 44 (archived: https://tsjyb.jsbc.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/75/69/7346c62b453cbd4126bdc45fd5a0/bca8ad31-2170-4285-b227-c25c94f43143.pdf). Read from the page image.
- Its footnote [5]P244 is 陶澍集, so the evidence is Tao Shu's, Daoguang era; Wu is the vehicle.
- 「才会出现"江西湖广各州县，官盐价昂，每斤制钱六十文至七十余文。而私盐每斤不过三十余文"的局面。在同一个行盐口岸，官盐价格是私盐的两倍甚至更多……道光年间，在两淮盐区的不少口岸，甚至出现了"官私各半"的局面。」
- (translation) "Hence the situation where 'in the counties of Jiangxi and Huguang official salt is dear, 60 to over 70 standard cash a jin, while private salt is no more than 30-odd a jin.' In the same market official salt was twice the private price or more … In the Daoguang years many Lianghuai markets were 'half official, half private'."
- Ni Yuping 2005 (archived, jds.cssn.cn) adds only the ticket's 0.64 tael for 400 jin, already in 0273.

**Reading: ATTESTED.**
- Qing retail ran from about 12–16 cash a jin at the administered Changlu and Beijing price (1688–1736) to 60–70+ cash in the far Huai markets of Jiangxi and Huguang by about 1830. Smuggled salt sold at about half the official price in the same markets.
- Measured against the Beijing list of 1736 (0273): a laborer's day bought about 8 jin, about 0.13 days per jin.
- At about 1830, against the rural Beijing copper wage of 88–91 cash (qing.md, a different region): official salt in the Huai markets is about 0.7–0.8 days per jin and private about 0.35, by this pass's arithmetic.
- The drivers the sources name:
  - surcharges and carriage costs on the license (浮費);
  - silver growing dearer against cash, which squeezed the merchants and led to 1–2 cash surcharges;
  - smuggling, met by 2-cash cuts in 1791 and 1848;
  - the ticket reforms, which more than halved merchants' cost per license in 1832 (Huaibei) and 1849 (Huainan) and made prices "fall abruptly".

### Edo Japan, for contrast

**kotobank 藩専売制 (日本大百科全書)**, https://kotobank.jp/word/藩専売制-118212
- 「初期専売の代表的なものとして、金沢藩における塩専売制の実施をあげることができる。この塩専売制は初期以降、幕末までほぼ一貫して継続されている。ほかに初期専売制の例としては、仙台藩での塩専売制の実施……などがある。」
- (translation) "A representative early monopoly is Kanazawa domain's salt monopoly, continued almost without break from the early period to the end of the shogunate. Other early examples include Sendai domain's salt monopoly …"

**kotobank 赤穂塩田 (改訂新版世界大百科事典)**, https://kotobank.jp/word/赤穂塩田-814801
- 「赤穂藩では浅野氏時代には1680年から，森氏時代には1809年から塩を藩の専売としたが，1821年生産者側の反対で中止された。」 (translation) "Ako domain made salt a domain monopoly from 1680 under the Asano and from 1809 under the Mori; it was ended in 1821 by the producers' opposition."
- Producer cost: 「1820年ころの勘定書によると，一軒前の年生産量4000俵，価格12貫匁，このうち燃料費50%」 (translation) "By an account of about 1820, one standard holding produced 4,000 bales a year worth 12 kanme of silver, fuel being 50%." That is about 3 monme a bale at the works, by this pass's arithmetic.

**Reading: ATTESTED ONLY FOR** "no shogunate-wide monopoly". The premise that Japan's salt was unmonopolized is only half right:
- Kanazawa held a salt monopoly from the early period to the end of the shogunate, and Sendai also ran one.
- Ako ran domain monopolies in 1680– and 1809–1821.
- The inland Sanriku and Ichinoseki prices in 0273 (up to 300 mon a sho) lie in Sendai territory, so they may reflect a domain monopoly as well as carriage.

Edo per-sho prices against the wage are already in 0273, by this pass's arithmetic:

| Kashimo, 1773–74 | about 0.5 |

Shinbo's salt CV is in japan.md (22% in 1820–58, 98% after 1859, from inflation).

**SILENT** on the 休浜 output cartel's effect on price, for the reasons given under "Searched and blocked" below.

**Common measure for salt.** China is priced by the jin (weight) and Japan by the sho (volume). I found no source for the weight of a sho of Edo salt, so the two are given side by side in wage-days per native unit, not converted.

## OTHER GOODS, Ming 1615 against Qing 1736 (the one source read that compares dynasties)

**高壽仙 2022**, archived as above. Both lists are Beijing official purchase prices: 工部廣庫須知 for the Ming, 九卿議定物料價值 for the Qing.

| Good | Ming 1615 (tael) | Qing 1736 (tael) |
|---|---|---|
| Wheat flour, per jin | 0.008 | 0.014 |
| Glutinous rice, per shi | 1 | 2.52 |
| Firewood, per 100 jin | 0.125–0.35 | 0.162–0.3 |
| Coal, per 100 jin | [0.1–0.12] | 0.26–0.27 |
| Coarse white cotton cloth, per zhang | 0.094 | 0.09 |
| Pig iron, per jin | 0.006 | 0.004 |
| Best wrought iron, per jin | 0.016–0.02 | 0.027 |

- In the source's own words: 「白面价格《价值》是《须知》的175%，江米价格更是高达252%」, and 「煤和煤砟的价格……高出一倍有余」 (translation: wheat flour 175%, glutinous rice 252%, coal more than double).
- Gao's conclusion: 「清朝雍乾之际北京市场的物价水平，比明朝万历年间有较大幅度的上升」 (translation) "Beijing's price level around 1730s–40s was considerably above Wanli's."
- Peng Xinwei's index, as Gao cites it from 中國貨幣史 p. 629: 「15世纪为100，16世纪为61.83，17世纪为41.80，18世纪为27.78，19世纪为17.37。看来白银购买力呈现出长期持续的下降趋势。」 (translation) "Silver's purchasing power in rice, 15th century 100, 16th 61.83, 17th 41.80, 18th 27.78, 19th 17.37: a long, continuous decline."
- On wages: 「从明朝后期到清朝中期，工资的增长幅度要比粮价增幅更高一些，民众生活水平可能有所提高。当然这只是一个初步判断」 (translation) "From the late Ming to the mid-Qing, wages rose somewhat more than grain prices; living standards may have improved. This is only a preliminary judgment." His figures: flour per day's pay went from 3.75–8.75 jin (Ming) to 6.7–13.75 jin (Qing).

**Reading: ATTESTED** for Beijing official prices, 1615 against 1736:
- Food and fuel roughly doubled in silver.
- Cotton cloth stayed flat, and pig iron fell.
- The driver Gao names is silver's long fall in purchasing power. The only other named driver is 全漢昇's American silver plus population growth, already in qing.md.

**Song rice:** 600–700 cash a shi before 1068 and 2,500–3,000 in 1122 (宋史 182, above). The source names no driver.
