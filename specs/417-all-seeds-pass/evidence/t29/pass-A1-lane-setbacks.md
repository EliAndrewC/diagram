## Reading

- **Water lines and banks — NOT ATTESTED.** The evidence puts the way on the bank, the bund or the levee crest, not 14 ft off it. That covers the Zhouli path on every grade of channel, bunds as paths, the towpath, the Ayase levees and the highway beside the river. The limits: the Zhouli is an ideal scheme, and the towpath and levee cases are highways and haulage routes, not a village lane beside a brook.
- **Wet ground and marsh — ATTESTED ONLY FOR the direction.** Ways kept to dry levees through wet ground (0058, MLIT Ayase). No distance is given anywhere, so the 7 ft and 8 ft figures are conventions.
- **Brooks and ditches, and the crop rule — NOT ATTESTED, and the record goes the other way.** 0084 and 0035 draw lanes crossing ditches and brooks, and land crossings on cultivated ground.
- **Graves:**
  - That a way ran to the burial ground: **ATTESTED** (the 1635 Qingming roads, plus the sōren-michi in a modern folk record).
  - That a way kept off a grave: **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the grave's ground being bounded by statute and protected from ploughing (Tang Code art. 168), and burial being kept off roads (Yoro code). No page speaks to a path over a grave, or to the 14 ft.
- **Yards — NOT ATTESTED.** The one passage has the lane itself widened into a drying ground (undated), which leaves no gap between lane and yard.
- **Shelter belts and ponds — SILENT.**
