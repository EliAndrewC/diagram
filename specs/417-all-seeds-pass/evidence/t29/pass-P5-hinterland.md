### 1. Sources that bear on the claims

**A. The record's own pages**

- **0075 Bamboo groves** (`/diagram/research/questions/0075-bamboo-groves-chikurin*.html`)
  - *Qimin Yaoshu*, 533-544, quoted in the notes: 「宜高平之地，（近山阜，尤是所宜。下田得水即死。）」. (translation) "It suits high, level ground; near hills and mounds is best; in a low field, once it gets water, it dies."
  - yashikirin-jawiki, undated custom: 「At houses near rivers and in flood-prone areas, alder and root-spreading bamboo are planted as flood protection」 (the record's translation). Bu nongshu, Ming-Qing: 「bamboo and trees behind」 the house. Gifu, mid-Meiji: a persimmon stood 「in the bamboo grove behind the house」.
  - The notes record an earlier search that found nothing: 「nothing saying who held a village's thicket or setting one at a paddy's edge.」
  - The drawing page already labels the thicket's seat ("dry ground just beyond the back row"), its side and its 84×58 ft size as GUESS.
- **0072 Shelter belts** (`...0072-shelter-belts...`)
  - Tarama's village belt, made in 1742, 「rings the settlement on the west, the south and the east」.
  - The notes record two earlier silent searches: 「None of the pages we read shows a gap left in a planted side, says how a lane or an entrance was cut through a planted grove, or gives an opening's width or angle.」 Also 「Hokkaido's ... windbreak manual ... gives the width of the belt itself and nothing for an opening」.
  - The drawing page's 37-262 ft from a farmhouse is labeled a calibration.
- **0036 Homestead groves**
  - The grove 「stands against the house」.
  - Mito domain register, 1684: homestead woods of about 6,000-28,000 sq ft.
  - Drawing rule: every farm that keeps a wood has 6,000-28,000 sq ft of trees, counting its share of the copse.
- **0071 Fengshui woods**
  - Hong Kong's 115 measured village woods: median about 1 ha, range 0.06-5.3 ha. These are modern measurements of old groves.
  - Korean village-entrance groves (an analogue, not Japan or China): mean 0.22 ha, range 0.025-0.63 ha.
- **0077 Satoyama**
  - Ruling maps of 1612 and 1649 drew boundaries as lines that bent. 「No page we read describes a coppice lot laid out as a surveyed rectangle.」
  - The notes record an earlier silent search: 「none says a village or commons boundary followed a ridge, a stream or a path」.
- **0078 Grass hills**
  - Mid-to-late Edo: grass ground 「more than ten times the area of the fields」 was needed.
  - satoyama-jawiki: 「特定の水田に専用の小規模な草山を設定することもあり「田付草山」と呼ばれた。」 (translation) "A small grass hill was sometimes set aside for one particular paddy's sole use, called a tazuke kusayama." The archived weblio copy (`/diagram/.specify/source-archive/cb/cbf6368040d7/.../text.txt`) has the same sentence and nothing on shape or which paddy.
- **0038 Sunlight**
  - *Qimin Yaoshu*: no grain grows in the elm's shade, and the shade reaches east, west and north as far as the tree is tall.
  - The record's working windbreak height is 10 m (about 33 ft).

**B. New this pass: 農政全書 卷39, 竹 section (Xu Guangqi, compiled 1639)** — https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%BE%B2%E6%94%BF%E5%85%A8%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B739 (saved at `/tmp/l7r-pass/p5-nzqs39/01-zh.wikisource.org.txt`)

- Quoting 《種樹書》 (Ming): 「種竹處當積土，令稍高於傍地二三尺，則雨潦不浸損。」 (translation) "Where bamboo is planted, heap up the earth so it stands 2-3 chi above the ground beside it, and heavy rain will not soak and harm it."
- Quoting the *Qimin Yaoshu* (the record cites this chapter, but not these sentences): 「不用水澆。（澆則淹死。）」 (translation) "Do not water it; watered, it drowns." Also 「諺云：『東家種竹，西家治地』，為滋蔓而來生也。」 (translation) "The proverb: 'the east house plants bamboo, the west house tills the ground', because it spreads and comes up there."
- In the compilation's own text: 「筀竹根多，穿害堦砌。惟聚皂莢刺埋土中障之，根則不過。」 (translation) "Gui bamboo has many roots that bore into and damage steps and paving; only honey-locust thorns buried in the soil as a barrier stop them." Xu's own note says its roots harm *other bamboo* (能害他竹，不宜雜種) and calls for a deep ditch. That note is not about fields.
- Xu Guangqi, late Ming, on Guangxi: 「篥竹為藩，可禦寇……惟有村居者，家有此藩而已。」 (translation) "Li bamboo made into a hedge can keep off bandits... only village dwellers have such a hedge at their houses." The page itself shows 可大寇, apparently a typo for 可禦寇.

**My reading:**
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR the direction.** Bamboo is kept off waterlogged ground and set on raised, dry ground. It spreads into the neighbor's ground, and its roots damage paving. In south China it stood as a hedge against village houses.
- No source gives any distance in feet.
- No source says bamboo was kept off dry crop plots. The *Qimin Yaoshu* plants it in a garden's northeast corner, and the proverb implies only that it spreads.

**Flags**
- **`belt_polygon#off the crop` conflicts with a record source.** The belt is refused only within 20 px of a crop (belt.py line 362), and the 22-60 px are the steps it is pushed back. The *Qimin Yaoshu* (0038) says no grain grows in a tree's shade, which reaches as far as the tree is tall: about 33 ft at the record's 10 m windbreak. A belt 20-33 ft east, west or south of a field stands in that shade.
- **`no copse without a belt or a reserved seat` conflicts with the record's own drawing rule.** 0036.drawing gives every farm that keeps a wood 6,000-28,000 sq ft of trees, counting its share of the copse. An engine that draws no copse on a map with no belt does not draw that.
- **The `parcel count and size` reading** is contradicted only if the 250 ft parcel is meant as a village's whole grazing commons.
