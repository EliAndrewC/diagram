**The track out of a hamlet and the field path (feature 417 T29, D32), research pass D**

The record already settles or speaks to most of the 29 claims. The web pass added nothing citable: six searches, no page bore on any claim, so no page was fetched or written to the sources-consulted ledger.

Four findings matter most:
- **The edge clearances are not research figures.** All six field-edge clearances (the 17 ft setbacks and the 12 ft margins) contradict the record, which puts the path on the field's edge with no clearance.
- **The gable margin disagrees with an attested figure.** The engine keeps a track 6 ft off a house wall; the record's attested figure is 3 ft.
- **The engine's ford is something the record does not draw.** At a hamlet's brook the record crosses every way on a timber deck at a crossing place.
- **The "keep clear of the brook" rule cuts against the record.** The Chinese evidence puts ways beside water.

## Sources that bear

1. **Wang Zhenzhong, 「"里至源头，外至水口"：明清以来徽州村落空间的建构」**, in 徽学 第14辑. Archived copy: `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/97/97fb5ef6d1d1/20261002T144603Z/text.txt` (https://m.read.qq.com/read/1043461189/7). The evidence is Ming–Qing Huizhou; the road passage rests on 《酬世汇编》, a Qing household reference book.
   - 「水口是村落通往外界的要冲，因此道路和桥梁必不可少，分别称为水口路和水口桥。」
     (translation) "The water mouth is the village's key passage to the outside world, so roads and bridges there were essential, called the water-mouth road and the water-mouth bridge."
   - 「歙县鸿飞村西的路亭叫"四角亭"，又称水口亭。一条笔直的石板路穿亭而过，是以前通往深渡和徽州府的要道。」
     (translation) "The road pavilion west of Hongfei village in She county is called the Four-Corner Pavilion, also the water-mouth pavilion. A straight flagstone road runs through the pavilion; it was formerly the main road to Shendu and Huizhou prefecture."
   - The Fangkeng passage, from a villager's account (Jiang Yitong) that is undated: 「土坝上栽满高大的柏、梓、桐、椿，组成一片浓阴苍翠的树林，像一堵城墙，仅溪流石板路绕坝」
     (translation) "the earth dam is planted full of tall cypress, catalpa, tung and toon, forming a dense green grove like a city wall; only the stream and the flagstone path go round the dam."
   - **Reading: ATTESTED ONLY FOR southern China (Huizhou).** The way out leaves at the water mouth, where the stream leaves the village, which is its low end. The road runs with the stream and crosses it on the water-mouth bridge. The path goes round a village grove, not through it.

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR southern China:**
- **Gateway direction:** the way out leaves at the water mouth, the stream's low exit, and runs with the stream, crossing it on the water-mouth bridge (Wang, Ming–Qing Huizhou). There is no Japanese tiebreaker. The Tonami "the east side was the entrance" is about one farm's grove, not slope.
- **Groves:** a path goes round a village grove (Fangkeng) or in through a gap in a farm's own grove (Otsubo, modern). Nothing shows a way crossing either freely.

**NOT ATTESTED as a rule of the world:**
- **Keeping clear of water:** Hongcun's lanes run mostly beside the water, the Fangkeng path goes round the dam with its stream, and Fei's road (1936) runs along a stream bank.
- **Keeping off the field:** the record puts the field path onto the outer bund and lets a lane touch a plot's boundary. Every 12–17 ft field-edge clearance is therefore a drawing convention, not a research figure.

**SILENT**, for the ford landing, the pond berth, commons, and when a path crosses a ditch rather than going round.

The ford needs flagging rather than more searching. 0035 has every hamlet way cross its brook on a deck at a crossing place, and 0086 knows fords only as highway crossings of great rivers. So `FORD_LANDING_FT`, and the "crossing made off a ford" rule in `_run_from`, name something the drawing pages do not draw.
