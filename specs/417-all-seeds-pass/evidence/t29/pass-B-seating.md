## Sources

**1. Takamine Akiko, 「農村集落に関する一考察」, 東北農業研究 19 (1977), pp. 246-247.** https://www.naro.go.jp/org/tarc/to-noken/DB/DATA/019/019-246.pdf. It surveys Tominaga, Furukawa, in Miyagi's paddy plain. The 1640 order and the lots laid out by the end of Meiji are premodern evidence; the rest is a 1977 survey. I read the quotes from the page image, not the faulty text layer.
- 「伊達藩寛永17年の令に「百姓新屋敷として，表20間，裏20間の2反歩を渡す。」の記録がある。」 (translation) "A Date-domain order of Kan'ei 17 (1640) records: 'as a peasant's new homestead, 20 ken of frontage and 20 ken at the back, 2 tan, is granted.'" The page prints 20 and 20. But 20 x 20 ken is 400 bu, which is 1.33 tan, not 2. So one of the two figures is probably wrong in the paper; 20 x 30 would make 2 tan.
- 「明治末期までに屋敷を構えたものをチェックしてみると，ほぼ2反歩以上の屋敷規模をもっていた。」 (translation) "Checking the homesteads laid out by the end of Meiji, nearly all had lots of 2 tan or more."
- 「屋敷の規模は…平均では1,650〜2,400㎡である。」 (translation) "Homestead lots average 1,650-2,400 m² (about 17,800-25,800 sq ft)."
- 「調査集落の屋敷は，おおむね水路にむかって構えられていた。…屋敷は水の得やすい水路ぎわに求められ，水路ぞいの集落形成がなされたと思われる。」 (translation) "The surveyed homesteads were for the most part built facing the irrigation channels... homesteads were sought at the channel's edge, where water was easy to get, and the settlement is thought to have formed along the channels." The second sentence is the author's inference.
- 「2反歩〜3反歩ある屋敷は，ひば，杉の生垣，屋敷林にかこまれており…5畝から1反歩ぐらいの自給畑」 (translation) "Homesteads of 2-3 tan were enclosed by hiba and cedar hedges and a grove... with a kitchen field of about 5 se to 1 tan."

**2. Namihira Emiko, 「会津芦の辺むらの社会構造」, 九州人類学会報 11/12 (1984), pp. 25-27.** https://catalog.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/2235343/p025.pdf. It describes a paddy village of about 90 households in the Aizu basin. The layout was observed in 1984; its dates come only from village tradition, which reaches back to the mid-Edo period. I read the quotes from the page image.
- 「総本家の真正面に最初に分家したと言われる家があり、そのほか、総本家の屋敷の周囲をかこむ形で東西南北に13戸の分家が配置されている。米沢イッケの屋敷地は一カ所の飛び地もなく、ムラの居住地域のほぼ中央にかたまって存在している。」 (translation) "Directly in front of the senior main house is the house said to have branched off first, and 13 branch households are placed around the main house's lot on all four sides. The Yonezawa lineage's lots lie together, without one outlier, near the middle of the village's living area."
- 「分家は原則として本家の東側ないし西側に屋敷を構え、本家の屋敷地を通り抜けて通りに出なければならない。」 (translation) "A branch house as a rule sets its homestead east or west of the main house and must pass through the main house's lot to reach the street."
- 「田中イッケはムラ内のあちこちに分散し…あとから芦ノ辺に入ってきて、耕地と屋敷地を開いたり買い取ったりして戸数を増やしていった」 (translation) "The Tanaka lineage is scattered here and there in the village... having come later, it grew in households by opening or buying fields and house lots."
- 「どの家も北側および北西側に屋敷森をめぐらしている。」 (translation) "Every house keeps its homestead grove on its north and northwest sides."
- The main house of the senior lineage stands 「南向きに千坪の屋敷を構え」 (translation) "on a 1,000-tsubo lot, facing south."

**3. Otsubo Kazuro, 「千葉県中山間農家のアプローチ『じょうぼ』の分析と考察」.** Archived copy: `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/0d/0daf2a20a0e7/20261003T014401Z/text.txt` (m-sugi.com; already cited for 0036). It is a modern essay on standing farmhouses in the valleys of hilly southeast Chiba. It gives no date, and the "in the old days" claims are hearsay.
- 「谷の底辺に最大限に水田を取った。さらに、畑での自給自足用の農作物が水はけの良い、水田より少し高い位置に必要になる。そして、母屋より日当たりが良い家の前の斜面に来ることになり、家屋はその後ろの高い位置に来る。したがって道路と母屋の間に距離が生じ」 (translation) "As much of the valley floor as possible was taken for paddy; the kitchen crops needed well-drained ground a little above the paddy, so they came on the sunny slope in front of the house, and the house came higher up behind them. So a distance arose between the road and the main house."
- 「水はけを良くするために1．5mほど敷地を高くする。」 (translation) "The site is raised about 1.5 m (~5 ft) for drainage."
- 「谷津の一番奥には必ず貯水池があり」 (translation) "At the very head of each valley there is always a reservoir."

**My reading.** Houses at the foot of the slope, set up off the paddy with dry field between, are **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the hilly valleys of southeast Chiba, in a modern undated essay; none of the three sources gives a distance in feet. A plain-paddy homestead fronting and facing its water channel is **ATTESTED** for Miyagi: the lots go back to before the end of Meiji (1912), and the grant to 1640. The founding along the channels is the author's inference. The lot size is **ATTESTED** at 2 tan or more: by the 1640 order, which is internally inconsistent, and by lots laid out before the end of Meiji. That is far larger than the 90-180 tsubo the record has in 0285, so lot size varied widely by region. A cluster growing by branch houses gathered round the stem house, the first one directly in front of it, is **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** one Aizu village, with dates from tradition only. Late arrivals there filled in scattered gaps.

A hard 45° cap on turning a house off its slope is **NOT ATTESTED**. 0029 already quotes the Okinawan slope survey: "of the 21 houses on the slope descending to the northeast, 15 face southeast and 3 face south-southeast; southerly orientations differing by 90 degrees from the slope direction make up nearly all" (translated). 0029's ±34° is a different quantity: how far a house turns from its neighbors' common bearing.

The pond berth (no record found of houses kept out of the ground below a pond, before 1868 or since) and the clustered-hamlet spacing (in Japanese and in Chinese) came back **SEARCHED-SILENT**. Queries:

The Chinese hits were modern morphology studies with no distance figures, and none was read in full. The archive holds Chen Fu's 1149 quotation 「農居五畝，以二畝半在鄽…以二畝半在田…『中田有廬』」, in a 2019 paper (`/diagram/.specify/source-archive/e0/e099412c103f/20261002T144848Z/text.txt`). It is a classical ideal of living near or in the field, with no distance.
