## Sources

**1. Record 0031 (Okamoto 1956, Kanto lowland; also kotobank-shuson).** Evidence date: a modern survey (1950 census) and a modern definition.
- 「この場合集落数の定め方は集落相互の間隔200m以上離れているものをもつてし，200m以内の離隔はこれを連続と見なした．」 (translation) "Here settlements are counted as separate when they are 200 m or more apart; gaps within 200 m are treated as continuous."
- 0031 itself says (in English) that no page gives "from before Japan began to modernize in 1868, any rule or measure of how far apart its houses stood."
- **Reading: SILENT** on the extent of a 10-20-household cluster. The 200 m rule is a geographer's counting convention, not the villagers' practice. It measures the gap between settlements, not a settlement's own reach.

**2. Record 0033 (Ome Shinmachi, opened 1596-1615; Santome, 1694-96).** Evidence date: the Edo-period layouts.
- 「南北中央に青梅街道を通し、その両側に…屋敷割を一軒分、間口九間として三三区画ずつの短冊形に分け」 (translation) "The Ome road was run north-south through the middle; on each side of it the lots were divided into strips of 33 plots each, each house's frontage 9 ken."
- 0033 says (in English) "No page we read says how long a row ran where it grew by itself."
- **Reading: ATTESTED ONLY FOR planned rows.** Frontages ran 9 to 40 ken (about 54-240 ft). By my own arithmetic, not a source's, a 10-20-farm row on one side would run about 540-4,800 ft. That is the only extent the record gives for a row.

**3. Record 0033 (Musashino plateau rows and the Ogawa farm, settled 1661-73).** Evidence date: the Edo layout, as described in modern accounts and in a 1980s survey.
- 「台地南部は街道にそって掘られた用水路の方向に、北部は水源が井戸のため比較的自由な方向に、列村が形成されている。」 (translation) "In the south of the plateau the rows formed along the canals dug beside the highways; in the north, where the water came from wells, they ran in comparatively free directions."
- 「Ｂ家の土地利用は立川街道にそって間口１３～１５間で、南側にある玉川上水まで約５００間ある長方形の土地を北から屋敷地ー畑地ー雑木林ー玉川上水という配列で利用していた。」 (translation) "Farm B's land fronted the Tachikawa road for 13-15 ken and ran about 500 ken south to the Tamagawa canal, used from the north as house lot, field, woodland, then the canal."
- **Reading: NOT ATTESTED as a standoff.** The street and the canal were dug together, side by side, and no gap is given. Where a canal lay behind a farm, the whole holding stood between road and water.

**4. Arthur H. Smith, *Village Life in China* (1899).** Archived copy: `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/74/741832efa52b/20261002T125132Z/text.txt`. Evidence date: Qing Shandong, 1870s-1890s, so premodern.
- "Sometimes in a village a quarter of a mile long, there may not be a single crossroad … Whether land is dear or cheap, a village always has the same crowded appearance"
- Two pages earlier: "the average size was taken as eighty families"
- "The village mud-hole … These excavations are the hole whence the material for the village houses was originally dug. During the summer time these pits, many of them as large as a dry-dock, are filled to the brim with dirty water, and at such times they are sure to be surrounded by groups of children"
- **Reading: ATTESTED ONLY FOR North China villages of about 80 families.** These are tightly packed, with a long dimension of about a quarter mile (about 1,320 ft). Smith gives no figure for 10-20 households. The pond is a pit dug for the houses' own material, so it lies among them, with no standoff stated.

**5. Go! NAGANO, 海野宿 (Unno-juku).** https://www.go-nagano.net/tradition-and-culture/id22471. Saved at `/tmp/l7r-pass/t29q-unno/01-www.go-nagano.net.txt`. Evidence date: a post town opened in 1625. The channel's age rests on this tourism page's word that it is unchanged since then.
- 「北国街道の宿場として江戸時代初期・1625年（寛永2年）に開かれました。」 (translation) "It was opened as a post station on the Hokkoku road in 1625, early in the Edo period."
- 「街道中央を流れる用水と石橋も、当時から変わらず受け継がれています。」 (translation) "The channel flowing down the middle of the road, and its stone bridges, have also come down unchanged from that time."
- **Reading: ATTESTED ONLY FOR a post town's own channel running in its street.** This is 0035's "own small channel through the middle". It is not a stream or pond beside a farm row, but it points the same way: no standoff.

## Reading

- **Claim 1 (the seating's reach): SILENT.** Nothing measures how far a 10-20-household clustered hamlet spread from its center, in Japan or China, before 1868 or 1912.
  - The nearest evidence is Smith's crowded North China villages (about 80 families, about a quarter mile long) and Okamoto's modern 200 m counting rule.
  - For a row, the only figures are planned frontages of 54-240 ft per farm.
  - The 1.15 and 2.5 multipliers are our own engine settings, not claims about the world.
- **Claim 2 (street off the pond and the water): NOT ATTESTED** as a standoff. The evidence runs the other way:
  - Musashino streets were dug together with their canals.
  - Unno-juku's channel runs in the street.
  - Huizhou ways run with the stream, and Hongcun's lanes run beside the water.
  - The Guangfu front lane runs straight along its pond.
  - Smith's pits lie among the houses.
  - The one form with a gap is 前街后河, where a single row of houses stands between street and river.
  - No source gives a clearance in feet. Pond-specific searches stay SEARCHED-SILENT (pass-A1, pass-D).
