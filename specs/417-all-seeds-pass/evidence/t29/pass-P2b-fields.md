I ran the pass: the record first, then the web on five grouped questions. Of the 85 claim keys, 56 are tolerances or drawing choices that no source could speak to. Two figures are contradicted by sources the record already holds:
- **Tea row spacing:** Fortune (1843) gives about 4 ft between rows; the code draws 8 px.
- **Water-wheel size:** the mill page 0064 says it has no wheel size, but a page it already cites gives one.

A third claim, the **whole fallow field**, goes against the resting-paddy page 0013.

## Sources that bear

**1. Fortune, *Three Years' Wanderings* (1843).** Archived copy: gutenberg 54720 (`/diagram/.specify/source-archive/d7/d71fce34d07c/…/text.txt`), already cited on 0020. The evidence describes the 1840s.
- 「The tea plantations in the north of China are always situated on the lower and most fertile sides of the hills, and never on the low lands. The shrubs are planted in rows about four feet apart and about the same distance between each row」
- **ATTESTED, and CONTRADICTS `tea row spacing`.** The code draws rows 8 px apart in raw pixels. At 1 px = 1 ft that is 8 ft, twice Fortune's 4 ft, and more on a coarser map. Fortune says nothing about which way the rows ran.

**2. Mizu no kenkyū, kikanshi no. 28.** Archived copy: mizu.gr.jp/kikanshi/no28/03.html (`e7/e7a6f75e859e`), already cited on 0064 as nka-28-suisha. The mill was founded in 1808; the wheel measured is a 1959 rebuild.
- 「「新車（しんぐるま）」と呼ばれるその水車は、水輪の直径4.8m、幅0.97mで、精米製粉用の水車としては大規模なものだ。」 (translation) "The wheel called Shinguruma, its water wheel 4.8 m across and 0.97 m wide, is a large one for a rice-polishing and milling wheel."
- 「今の新車の水輪は、1959年（昭和34）につくられたものです。…水輪は8〜10年で新しくつくり直されます」 (translation) "The present wheel was made in 1959 … a wheel is rebuilt every 8 to 10 years."
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR a traditionally built wheel in a line of rebuilds going back to 1808**; it is not a measurement taken before 1868. The 4.8 m (about 15.7 ft) is called large, and the claim's 18 ft is bigger still, so it **CONTRADICTS `wheel glyph`** mildly.
- The same page's Yodobashi wheel (6.67 m) dates from 1887, Meiji, so it does not count.

**3. 明史 卷60 禮十四.** zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明史/卷60. These are Ming rules of 1370 and 1372 capping grave size by rank.
- 「墳塋之制，亦洪武三年定。一品，塋地周圍九十步，墳高一丈八尺。…七品以下二十步，高六尺。」 (translation) "The rule for graves was also fixed in Hongwu 3 (1370): first rank, grave ground 90 bu round and mound 18 chi high … seventh rank and below, 20 bu, mound 6 chi high."
- For commoners (1372): 「塋地圍十八步」 (translation) "grave ground 18 bu round."
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR an upper limit.** 18 bu round is about 94 ft, roughly 23.5 ft a side if square. That is the grave's ground, not the mound itself. The drawn 13 x 9 ft and 18 x 12 ft mounds fit inside it.
- No mound footprint for commoners is given.
- **Ledger fix needed:** I recorded this page as `rejected:` but it bears on the grave claims. Overwrite it with `cited:` if you cite it.

**4. Chen Fu, 農書 卷上 (1149).** Archived copy: zh.wikisource 農書/卷上 (`88/8801883e2e4e`), already cited on 0009, 0042 and 0061. The evidence is Song-era Jiangnan.
- 「或謂土敝則草木不長…凡田土種三五年，其力已乏。斯語殆不然也…若能時加新沃之土壤，以糞治之，則益精熟肥美，其力常新壯矣」 (translation) "Some say worn soil grows nothing, that after three to five years a field is spent. Not so … manure it with fresh rich soil and its strength stays ever new."
- He recites the ancient resting of land as a past order of the former kings: 「一易之地，中地也，家二百畝，謂間歲耕其半，以息地氣」 (translation) "Land rested once is middle land, 200 mu a household: half worked in alternate years to rest the soil's vigor."
- 「下地多肥饒，易以渰浸」 (translation) "Low land is mostly fertile, and easily flooded."
- **NOT ATTESTED for `fallow field`**: no whole field lies fallow in the Song practice described. My own inference, not a statement in the text: this fits low plots never resting.

**5. Resting-paddy page 0013** (its originals page), quoting the 2023 review. The evidence describes the Heian period and Kamakura on.
- 「休耕地はふつう一円地ではなく，耕作中の土地と混在している。」 (translation) "Resting land was usually not one solid tract but mixed in with the land under crop."
- 「鎌倉時代以降，用水の整備や耕地の改善が進んで不安定耕地が克服され」 (translation) "From the Kamakura period on, water works and fields were improved and the unstable land was overcome."
- **NOT ATTESTED for a whole field drawn fallow.**

**6. Paddy page 0005** (its originals page), the Kami-Baba survey register of 1684.
- 「(2)拾(十)六間・拾間半 (3)中田(4)五畝拾八歩」 (translation) "16 ken by 10.5 ken, middle-grade paddy, 5 se 18 bu."
- The note beside it: 「※土地の区画を方形に換算して測量」 (translation) "the parcel is measured by converting it to a rectangle."
- **ATTESTED ONLY FOR one register parcel.** 10.5 / 16 = 0.656, which matches the claim's 0.66. But the sides are a converted rectangle, not a measured basin shape. The record's other parcels run from about 0.21 (8 x 39 ken, 1849) to 0.86.
