## Sources that bear on the claims

**1. Kakimochi village house list, Table 18 and text** (archived: `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/e7/e776f86566f1/20261002T132655Z/text.txt`, https://adeac.jp/oamishirasato-city/texthtml/d100010/mp100010-100010/ht011770). The list is from 1885; the town historian reads it back to the last years before 1868, and page 0028 already accepts that.
- The eight retirement-house (隠宅) rows of Table 18, in ken (about 6 ft each), as printed: 「隠宅 1 3.5 4.5」「隠宅 1 2 1.5」「隠宅 1 4 2」「隠宅 1 4 2」「隠宅 1 2 1.5」「隠宅 1 3.5 2」「隠宅 1 6 2.5」「隠宅 1 3.5 2」
- 「九坪という広さは、表18の八戸の隠宅が三坪から一五坪までの広さであったことから、ちょうど中ぐらいの規模をもった隠宅といえる」 — "Nine tsubo is about a middling retirement house, since the eight households' retirement houses in Table 18 ran from 3 to 15 tsubo" (translation). The undated Figure 7 house is 「三間四方の建坪九坪」, "3 ken square, 9 tsubo of floor" (translation); the text dates it to the end of the shogunate or the Restoration.
- 「八戸もの家が隠宅を構えている…比較的小規模な本家しかもたないものでも、大部分の家が隠宅を設けている」 — "as many as eight households kept a retirement house… even most houses with a fairly small main house had one" (translation).

**2. The Ohara house plot plan, 1849 (premodern), same archived page:** 「板間を挾んで六畳二間の離れ間がある。この離れ間は、多分隠居部屋と思われる」 — "across a plank-floored room there are two detached six-mat rooms; these are probably the retirement rooms" (translation). Here the retirement quarter is a wing joined to the main house, not a separate building, and the text itself hedges with "probably".

**3. Yokota house, a samurai house in Matsushiro; retirement house built 1820** (archived: `.../25/257f8060f424/20261002T132652Z/text.txt`, https://adeac.jp/nagano-city/texthtml/d100040/ct00000004/ht002070): 「主屋の左がわにある隠居屋は、桁行七・七メートル、梁行四・六メートル。庭に面した八畳の座敷と六畳の次の間があり、その北側に台所が突きでていて」 — "The retirement house on the left of the main house is 7.7 m long and 4.6 m deep, with an eight-mat room facing the garden and a six-mat next room, and a kitchen jutting out to its north" (translation). That is about 25 by 15 ft, standing on a flank. No distance from the main house is given.

**4. Wang and Ochiai 2022, already in the record at 0047 notes; evidence is Meiji-era layouts (1868–1912), so just after the cutoff:** 「storage buildings and retirement houses in the southwest and west directions tended to be placed perpendicularly to a vertical main house, thus forming wind fences to protect the open space in front of the house entrance」 and 「Among the 11 retirement houses, 63.6% were located in a westerly direction.」 It is in English.

**5. Yanagidani village history (柳谷村誌, Ehime); describes houses built in the Meiji period, so after 1868** (archived: `.../99/99ab9b6ad2c6/20261003T012916Z/text.txt`, https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:3/60/view/14448): 「隠居部屋が一緒にとられていない場合は、近くに隠居屋が建てられた。」 — "Where no retirement room was included in the house, a retirement house was built nearby" (translation). It gives no distance.

**6. Kotobank, 屋敷 entry; general and undated, though it says the pattern was stronger the further back you go** (archived: `.../5a/5a429c550872/20261002T121417Z/text.txt`, https://kotobank.jp/word/屋敷-145028):
- 「隠居屋やりっぱな長屋門などを備えているもの，戌亥の隅などに小祠を建てて屋敷神を奉斎しているものも多い」 — "Many have a retirement house or a fine gate-house, and many enshrine the homestead god in a small shrine at the northwest corner or the like" (translation).
- 「一般的には別棟になっていることが多く，時代をさかのぼるほどそれが顕著であった」 — "generally [the outbuildings] are separate buildings, and the further back in time the more so" (translation).
- The same entry's land-survey sense of 隠居屋 (a dependent farmer's dwelling) is a different meaning and is not used here for siting.

**My reading.** **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** the retirement house's floor area and its seat on a flank:
- **Size.** The engine's 18 × 15 ft is 3 × 2.5 ken, or 7.5 tsubo. That sits inside Kakimochi's 3–15 tsubo range and near its middle. But no row of the table has that shape: the commonest rows are 4 × 2 ken (24 × 12 ft) and 3.5 × 2 ken (21 × 12 ft), and 7 of the 8 are 2.5 ken deep or less. Yokota's 15 ft depth does match.
- **Seat.** The flank is attested: Yokota stands on the left of its main house (1820, a samurai house), and Wang–Ochiai find mostly westerly placements (Meiji). Nothing I read puts a retirement house "off the back wall".
- **Gap.** No source gives a distance between the retirement house and the main house, so the 6 / 12 ft gaps are unattested.

## Flags (no figure contradicted)
- **Cardinal wind's second face (#38, #41).** The engine rolls either flank at even odds, or per settlement. Page 0036 gives only the attested Sendai pair: 「Two sides are the windward pair, as on the Sendai plain, where the groves … were planted on the north and west」. For a north wind, the record gives west.
- **Retirement house as a separate building.** The 1849 Ohara plan shows it as a wing joined to the main house. That is one case: the Kotobank 屋敷 entry and the 1885 table support separate buildings as the general form.
