I found an Osaka back-tenement rent tied to room size, but for 1814-1852, not c. 1745. I found nothing for Kyoto.

## Sources that bear on it

**1. Tani Naoki 谷直樹, 「近世大坂の居住事情 ―借家と長屋建を中心に―」, 『大阪市立大学生活科学部紀要』37 (1989)**
https://ocu-omu.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2015149/files/111H0000001-37-14.pdf
`make source-pages` could not read this PDF, so I downloaded it and extracted the text myself (saved in /tmp/l7r-pass/420q3-ocu-dl/). The page is printed in two columns and the extract runs them together; I rebuilt the right-hand column from the layout extract. Obvious OCR slips are kept as printed and read in brackets.

- **Mitsui's Osaka rental houses, 1814 (Bunka 11) and 1846 (Kōka 3). Rent is set per mat (畳) of floor, per month, in silver.**
  「三井の借家の家賃は，嘉永年間のものを欠いているが，文化11年以降しばらくは表借家が1畳につき9分(空地は1坪につき9分)裏借家が7分(空地は1坪につき7分)になっている。また同じ三井の借家で弘化3年(1846)新築の南小幡町建家の「宿賃町入用差引見積額」によると，表借家は1畳につき9分(空地は1坪につき9分)裏借家が7分(空地は1坪につき5分)である。」
  "The Mitsui rental houses' rents are missing for the Kaei era, but from Bunka 11 [1814] for some time the street-front rental houses were 9 bu [0.9 monme] per mat (vacant ground 9 bu per tsubo) and the back rental houses 7 bu (vacant ground 7 bu per tsubo). In another Mitsui rental house, the Minami-Obata-machi building newly built in Kōka 3 (1846), its 'estimate of rents less ward charges' gives front houses 9 bu per mat (vacant ground 9 bu per tsubo) and back houses 7 bu (vacant ground 5 bu per tsubo)." (translation)

- **A general average for Kyoto/Osaka rents, per half-tsubo (one mat's area).**
  「当時家賃に関しては幕府の統制があって極端な値上げは難しく，また『所以者何』によると「家賃銀高下平均裏底[裏店]にて半坪約七分位，表応[表店]九分位J[」]とあることから，三井の借家家賃は嘉永年間においてもこの額を踏襲していたと考えることができる。」
  "Rents were then under shogunal control, so a steep rise was hard. And the 『所以者何』 says 'house rents in silver, high and low averaged: back shops about 7 bu per half-tsubo, front shops about 9 bu'. So Mitsui's rents can be taken to have stayed at this level into Kaei [1848-54]." (translation)
  Tani's note 11 explains the 『所以者何』: Tamiya Nakanobu's answers to Ōta Nanpo, who had come to Osaka as a shogunal official, put into letters in Tenpō 3 (1832). Nanpo served in Osaka in 1801-02, so its evidence describes about 1801-1832.

- **The rate is monthly, and the floor is counted in mats.** Tani works the rates out for the Kyōmachibori 4-chōme houses rebuilt in Kaei 5 (1852):
  「裏借家の家賃 建家 220.5匁(0.7匁×315.0畳) 空地 33.67匁(0.7匁×48.1坪)となり，計算上は表借家151.97匁，裏借家254.17匁，合計406.14匁が1カ月の家賃収入になる予定である。」
  "Back houses: buildings 220.5 monme (0.7 monme × 315.0 mats), vacant ground 33.67 monme (0.7 × 48.1 tsubo). On paper, 151.97 monme from the front houses and 254.17 from the back, 406.14 monme in all, was to be the month's rent income." (translation)

- **How much was actually collected (no rent amount given).** 「常安裏町の家賃記録を整理すると，表借家の回収率は平均95%，裏借家は80%となっている」
  "From Jōan-uramachi's rent records, front houses' rent was collected at 95% on average and back houses' at 80%." (translation)
  These records are Meiwa-era (1764-72), per Tani's note 25.

- **The size of a laborer's household, Osaka 1834 (no rent).** In the 1834 inventory of a 27-year-old 「働渡世」 (day laborer) tenant in Goike-dōri 6-chōme, who left a mother and sisters behind, the tenant owned 「畳四畳半」, four and a half mats. Tani adds 「一人当たり1畳にも満たない生活はおそらく借家人でも最下層に属する者にちがいない」: "living on less than one mat a person must belong to the very lowest of tenants" (translation). In Osaka, mats and fittings belonged to the tenant (裸貸).

**2. Matsue city history column no. 48, from the 『大保恵日記』 (1826-54)**
https://www.city.matsue.lg.jp/material/files/group/34/column48.pdf
This is a castle town, not Kyoto or Osaka. I include it only as the nearest back-tenement rent printed beside a room size.
「裏借家は大通りから小路を入った先にある長屋で、間口が1間から1間半（約2mから3ｍ）、奥行き2間程度の狭い部屋です。…表借家では月920文（文政9年頃）であったのに対し、裏借家では月260文（嘉永5年（1852）頃）でした。」
"The back tenements were row houses down a lane off the main street, rooms 1 to 1.5 ken wide and about 2 ken deep. …a front house cost 920 mon a month (c. 1826), a back house 260 mon a month (c. 1852)." (translation)
The size is the column's general description, not a measurement of that particular unit. The same family also paid an 8-kanmon deposit plus 7,670 mon for fittings.

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** Osaka, 1814-1852 (with a general Kyoto/Osaka average from about 1801-32), not c. 1745.

- **Osaka.** Mitsui's rental houses charged 0.7 monme of silver per mat per month for a back tenement and 0.9 for a street-front one, from 1814 and again in 1846. Tani assumes the same through Kaei (1848-54). The 『所以者何』 gives the same rate as the general average, about 7 bu per half-tsubo for back shops.
- **What it gives for a reference room.** A 九尺二間 room is 3 tsubo, or 6 mat-areas. At that rate it would rent for about 4.2 monme a month. That is this pass's arithmetic, and it assumes the whole floor, earth-floored entry included, is billed by the mat-area. Tani's figures count buildings in mats but do not say whether the entry is included.
- **Nearest to 1745.** Fujimoto 1978 below, on Osaka's Meiwa-era (1764-72) rent register, would be the closest, but I could not read it. Tani also notes the Kyōmachibori lot fell to Mitsui as forfeited collateral pledged in 延享4年 (1747), but he gives no rent for that year.
- **Kyoto: SILENT.**
  - Searches: Japanese queries on 京都 町家 / 裏借屋 / 借家人 家賃 銀 匁, and on 町触 rent orders.
  - Pages read: a Kyōto University lease bond of 1820 (catalogue record only, no rent in it; recorded nothing-found).
  - 所以者何 says only that Kyoto rentals came with their fittings: 「京都ハ借家建具大体家付…大坂ハ建卸家の侭にて，内造作ハ家借り主より自分仕候」.
- **Ground rent (地代) for Kyoto or Osaka with a house size:** none found. Washizaki 2007 (in the archive, jstage sehs/73/2) uses Edo's 寛政度地代店賃書上 (1790) for front-lot and back-lot rents. That is the Edo comparison, and I did not read it closely because its archived text is badly garbled.
