- **Salt:** plain salt is $1.28/kg.

## Q3 modern: housing per m² a year

**ATTESTED** for the rents and construction costs below. The per-m² figures are my divisions, and each mixes two sources.

1. **Census HVS, Q2 2026** (https://www.census.gov/housing/hvs/files/currenthvspress.pdf): "In the second quarter 2026, the median asking rent for vacant for rent units was $1,531." That is $18,372 a year.
   - Divided by the RECS 2020 mean rented unit → **$182/m²-year**. RECS Table HC10.1 (https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/hc/pdf/hc%2010.1.pdf) has "Rented 40.61 [million units] 44.22 [billion sq ft]", which is 1,089 sq ft or 101.2 m². Its note says "Rented includes households that occupy their primary housing unit without paying rent."
   - Divided by the AHS 2023 median for all occupied units → **$132/m²-year**. The AHS workbook (https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/ahs/2023/AHS%202023%20Table%20Specifications%20and%20PUF%20Estimates%20for%20User%20Verification.xlsx), Table C-02-AO, gives "Median (square feet)" 1500, about 139 m². That median is mostly owner-occupied units.
   - I could not rebuild the snapshot's $269. It would imply a unit of about 68 m².
2. **HUD Fair Market Rent, FY2027.** The notice (https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-17891.pdf) says:
   - "For FY 2027, the national non-metropolitan rent is $1,014."
   - "efficiency FMRs are constrained to fall between approximately 0.70 and 0.88 of the two-bedroom FMR"
   - "HUD derives FMRs for single-room occupancy units by subtracting 25 percent from the zero-bedroom FMR (i.e., they are set at 0.75 times the zero-bedroom [efficiency] FMR)"
   - → In the cheapest areas, an efficiency is about $710–892 a month and an SRO about $532–669 a month ($6,400–8,000 a year).
   - No room size is sourced, so no per-m² figure. The county schedule itself was blocked (HUD's firewall).
3. **Construction cost, land excluded.**
   - NAHB 2024 survey (https://nahb.org/-/media/AB4EFC742624475A97A0A62189986FF8.ashx): "Average Finished Area: 2,647"; "B. Total Construction Cost $428,215 64.4%" → $162/sq ft, **$1,741/m²**. This excludes the lot, overhead and profit.
   - NAHB on Census data (https://eyeonhousing.org/2025/10/square-foot-prices-moderate-in-2024/): "For custom, or contractor-built, homes, the median price was $166 per square foot of floor space"; "Contract prices of custom homes do not include the value of an improved lot" → **$1,787/m²**.
   - Both are a capital cost. A rate per m²-year needs a building life or depreciation assumption, which the session must choose.
4. **Allen's poverty-line housing.** Cited from LIS Working Paper 706 (July 2017), not the AER text (https://www.lisdatacenter.org/wps/liswps/706.pdf):
   - "I set the quantity of housing at three square metres per person."
   - "In some cases, like New York, rents were found for illegal, substandard apartments, although, in this case, the rent per square did not differ greatly from the US Department of Housing Fair Market Rent for New York County in 2011."
   - "in the USA, UK, and France, rents were on the order of $25 per square metre per month. In the USA, rent on 3 square metres worked out to be $1.76 per day"
   - → $214–300/m²-year in 2011 dollars.
   - His Table 10 is an image and could not be read.
   - The NBER 2016 conference draft instead set housing "as 5% of the budgeted food and non-food spending".
   - This is the comparator closest to a back-tenement room: a substandard New York apartment at Allen's crowding of 3 m² a person.

## Q6: US annual quantities, all per capita

**ATTESTED** for flour, other grains, dry beans, fish, sodium, fuel and floor area. **ATTESTED ONLY FOR** 2010 for rice and cooking oil (the series stop). **SILENT** for soybeans as food and for textiles.

**USDA ERS Food Availability (Per Capita) Data System** (https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-availability-per-capita-data-system):
- **Wheat flour, 2022** (grains.xlsx, sheet Pcc): "White and whole wheat flour" **121.2 lb** (55.0 kg); with durum 132.9 lb (60.3 kg).
- **Other grains:** corn products 35.5 lb (2022); oat products 4.81 lb (2022); barley products 0.73 lb (2020; "Data for barley products food availability were discontinued in 2021").
- **Rice:** **20.4 lb milled** (9.27 kg) in 2010, the last year. The note reads: "Annual data and per capita estimates for rice are unavailable beyond 2010 due to a large and unexplained decline in the implied total domestic and residual use estimate."
- **Dry beans, 2022** (vegetables-pulses.xlsx): "Dry edible beans" **6.82 lb**, farm weight (3.09 kg).
- **Fish, 2018** (fish-and-shellfish.csv): "Total fish and shellfish: Supply and use - edible meat weight ... Per capita availability-Pounds, 16.12" (7.31 kg). Canned sardines are 0.226 lb of that.
  - NOAA (https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/national/sustainable-fisheries/fisheries-united-states): "In 2023, the per capita consumption of seafood in the United States was 19.1 pounds." The basis is not stated on that page.
- **Cooking oil, 2010** (fats-and-oils-added.csv): "Salad and cooking oils" **53.6 lb, product weight** (24.3 kg). The note: "data on added fats and oils (except butter) cannot be updated beyond 2010". I did not convert this to liters because no density is sourced.
- **Soybeans as food:** NOT in the system. The pulses workbook lists bean types only (pinto, navy, black, garbanzo and others), with no soy.

**Salt.** FDA (https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-education-resources-materials/sodium-your-diet): "Americans eat on average about 3,400 mg of sodium per day." Converting sodium to salt (× 58.44/22.99 = 2.542) → 8.64 g of salt a day, **3.15 kg a year**. This is total dietary sodium, mostly from processed food, not household salt bought.

**Fuel, EIA RECS 2020** (2024 RECS consumption data are due in spring 2027):
- **Space heating.** Table CE3.1 (https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/c&e/pdf/ce3.1.pdf), "All homes 123.53 [million] ... Space heating 4,026 [trillion Btu] ... 34.4 [million Btu per household using the end use]". All homes → **32.6 million BTU per household**.
  - Its footnote a: "Consumption and expenditures for biomass (wood), coal, district steam, and solar thermal are excluded."
- **Wood, kept separate.** Table CE7.2: "All homes 11.1 [million] 345.1 [trillion Btu] 31.2 [million Btu per household]".
- **Cooking**, per household using it (CE5.4 and CE5.3b): natural gas 2.02, propane 2.09, electric 0.99 million BTU.
- **Per person.** From the household-member rows, with "6 or more" counted as 6, there are at least 299.7 million people, at most 2.43 per household. → heating without wood about 13.4 million BTU per person; with wood about 14.6 (an upper bound).

**Floor area per person.**
- AHS 2023, Table C-02-AO: "Square Feet per Person — Median" **725 sq ft (67.4 m²)**; mean 943 (87.6 m²).
- RECS cross-check: 224.62 billion sq ft over at least 299.7 million people is about 749 sq ft (69.6 m²).

**Textiles:** **SILENT**. ERS Cotton and Wool Outlook (cws-26a.pdf) gives only trade in "Raw-fiber-equivalent pounds", with no per-capita row. No figure in m² of cloth exists.
