## R2 - was a draw-well ever on a guest's roji? (search pass, 2026-10-11)

Archive first (`make archive-find`: roji, 露地, 蹲踞, 露地+井戸, 中門+井戸, 露地+中門, 武家屋敷+井戸), then the web in Japanese and
English. Found: NO draw-well on a residence's guest roji. The Koseki house (Shiroishi, 1730) - the middle gate and roji to the
zashiki's veranda this sheet's form follows - names no well. Working wells stood by the kitchen or bath (Matsue's
Shiomi-nawate house; Kaminoyama's Miwa house; Kitsuki's Ohara residence; the Urasenke house, "kitchen ... with well") or in an
official compound's front court beside the formal entrance (Iwama jin'ya, from an 1827 drawing; Kakunodate's Aoyagi house).
The one tea-roji well curb found (Jo-an, Uraku-en, inscribed 1615) is an homage copy of Murata Juko's, not shown to be a working
well. Not readable online: the Edo garden manuals (Tsukiyama teizoden; Kokon sado zensho vol. 5). The finding is recorded,
cited and checked on research 0104's drawing page (task T03, the page sessions); plan D6 follows it.
