Feature 431, pass C: the fiber chain in hours.

I found working-time figures for cotton cloth in both Japan and China, but none for the whole chain from plant to garment. The Japanese cotton figures start from ginned cotton, and no source counts growing the plant, dyeing or sewing. Nothing found states that cloth-making took more of one person's time than farming. What the sources do show is that one weaving woman put in about 200 days a year. I edited nothing; I recorded outcomes on the ledger only for pages that did not bear (as unreadable or nothing-found), never as cited.

## Sources

**1. Tanimoto Masayuki, "Cotton and the Peasant Economy" (LSE GEHN Helsinki paper)** — https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/Helsinki/HELSINKITanimoto.pdf (read through the fallback fetch and pdftotext). English source; no translation needed.

- **Japan, Niikawa district, mid-19th century; unit: days per tan of cotton, starting from ginned cotton.** "Table 2 Production, payment in kind and consumption of cotton cloth in peasant household (Niikawa district, middle of 19th century) … Annual production for sale 39.4 tan … Annual production for self consumption 12.8 tan (Working days per tan) Spinning 2 day / Preparation 1 day / Weaving 1 day / Total 4 day (Annual working days per household) Production for sales, per year 157.6 day / Production for self consumption, per year 51.2 day / Total 208.8 day"
  - "The average household devoted one woman for about 7 months to prepare the clothing for the household members, supplying 39.4 tan to the market and 12.8 for her own family." Households averaged "slightly less than 5" members.
  - He defines the unit: "Normal size of one tan is about 0.35 meter wide and 11 meter long."
- **Japan, Akita, 1808–1810; how much cloth was bought and how much made.** "Table 1 Provision for clothing (Annually, average of 1808-1810, Akita region) Inflow Cotton Cloth 174,397 tan … Inflow Ginned Cotton 6,400 gan 180,000tan in cloth / Inflow Used Cloth 117,101 mai … Production Ramie,Hemp 105,161 kin 84,000 tan in cloth / Population 450,000 Person". He adds: "Peasant household ordinarily ran the whole production processes, namely cultivation, spinning and weaving, in self-use basis."
  - My arithmetic, not his: the total is about 555,500 tan for 450,000 people, about 1.23 tan per person per year.
  - By source: new cotton cloth bought, about 31%; bought ginned cotton spun and woven at home, about 32%; used cloth, about 21%; home-grown hemp or ramie, about 15%.
- **Hemp/ramie against cotton (secondhand).** "According to the estimation by Keiji Nagahara, the labour input for producing the cloth from raw ramie per tan, including spinning and weaving, reached nearly ten times more than that of cotton cloth" (his footnote 9: Nagahara, 苧麻・絹・木綿の社会史, p. 212).

(Huang 2002: 535) … (Assuming no men at all ever wove of spun, it would require slightly less than ¼ of females working a 210-day year.)"
  - Footnote 3: "Total production of a bolt from raw cotton to cloth earned 42 jin of rice over 7 days; now it's 1.9 for a day's weaving, plus 1.1 for each of 3 days spinning, plus about the same for the other 2 days, which would give you 7.4 jin over 6 days"

**4. 郑天挺, 鸦片战争前清代社会的自然经济 (1982), quoting 杨光辅《淞南乐府》 (Qing, about 1800)** — https://www.aisixiang.com/data/176107.html (saved page)

- The poems (primary, Qing):
  - 「淞南好，妇苦最农家。午汗花田锄蔓草，宵饥蚊窟纺棉纱」 (translation: "Songnan is fine — the women suffer most in farm households: sweating at noon hoeing weeds in the cotton field, hungry at night spinning cotton yarn in the mosquito den")
  - 「淞南好，耕织不辞劳，刷布经车沿架走……辛苦利如毛」 (translation: "…tilling and weaving without shirking toil, sizing the cloth and walking the warp along the frame… bitter toil, profit thin as a hair")
- Zheng's own 1982 statement, not a period source; unit 端, not 匹: 「一个人一天可以织布一端，有的能织两端」 (translation: "one person could weave one *duan* of cloth a day, some could weave two").

**5. 宋應星《天工開物》 (1637), Ming Wikisource transcription** — https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/天工開物/治絲 · /夏服 · /布衣

- **Silk reeling (治絲):** 「窮日之力，一人可取三十兩。若包頭絲，則只取二十兩」 (translation: "with a full day's strength one person can reel thirty *liang*; for headband silk only twenty *liang*").
- **Ramie (夏服):** 「紡苧紗，能者用腳車，一女土並敵三工。惟破析時，窮日之力只得三、五銖重」 (translation: "for spinning ramie yarn, a skilled woman using a treadle wheel equals three workers; but in the splitting, a full day's strength yields only three to five *zhu* in weight").
  - 一女土 is almost certainly a transcription error for 一女工.
  - The 3–5 *zhu* figure is for splitting the fibre (破析), not for joining it into thread (績).
  - My conversion: 24 銖 = 1 兩, and taking a Ming 兩 as about 37.3 g, that is about 4.7–7.8 g of fibre a day.
- **Cotton (布衣):** this chapter gives the steps and says looms were everywhere, but gives no times: 「去子取花，懸弓彈化……以登紡車……然後繞籆牽經就織……織機十室必有」 (translation: "remove the seeds, bow the cotton… to the spinning wheel… then reel, warp and weave… of ten households, every one has a loom").

**6. Mannering, "Fashioning the Viking Age", *Archaeological Textiles Review* 63 (2021), p. 139** — https://tidsskrift.dk/atr/article/download/166728/207807/371498. **Comparative only; modern experiment.**

- "The average weaving speed was 16 to 29 threads per hour – or approximately 3 cm per hour depending on the number of threads per cm in the weave. Each sample was woven to a size of 60 cm x 60 cm."
- With machine-spun yarn: "The weaving speed for this sample was 10 cm to 15 cm per hour."
- My arithmetic: about 33 hours to weave 1 m of cloth 60 cm wide, weaving alone.

## Reading

**ATTESTED ONLY FOR** cotton from ginned fibre to cloth, plus a few single steps:

- **Japan:** 4 days per tan (2 spinning, 1 preparation, 1 weaving) in Niikawa in the mid-19th century.
- **China:** about 7 days per bolt with hand-spun yarn around 1750 (6 or 7 in Pomeranz's draft), 3 days with machine yarn.
  - My arithmetic, different cloths: about 2.3 days per m² in China (bolt ≈ 3.04 m²) against about 1 day per m² in Niikawa (Tanimoto puts a tan at about 4 m²).
- **Single steps (1637, China):** silk reeling at 30 *liang* per person-day, and ramie splitting at 3–5 *zhu* per day.
- **Days per year:**
  - A five-person household weaving for itself needed 12.8 tan, or 51.2 days a year, at cotton rates.
  - A household also weaving for market kept one woman at it for about 7 months, 208.8 days.
  - A Jiangnan woman's practical maximum was about 200 days.
- **Bought against made (Akita, 1808–10):** about 31% of the cloth supply was finished cotton cloth bought in, and about 21% was used cloth.

The GM's hypothesis is not stated anywhere I found. What bears on it:
- **Cotton:** a self-supplying cotton household needed about 50 woman-days a year for clothing cloth, which is probably less than its farm work.
- **Hemp:** with hemp at Nagahara's roughly tenfold rate, the same 12.8 tan would take about 512 days — more than one woman's year. That is my arithmetic: it combines figures from different sources and is not a sourced number. It suggests that before cotton, cloth-making could well have rivalled farming for the women's time.
- **Weaving for market:** Pomeranz's "slightly less than ¼ of females working a 210-day year" (China around 1750) and the Niikawa 7 months show it filling most of a woman's working year.
