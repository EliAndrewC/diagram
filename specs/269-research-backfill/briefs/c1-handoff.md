## Outcomes (B38, one line per part)

- B38 moat KNOB - new 100: the dug ditch scales with rank, from about 13 ft by 13 ft deep at a county seat (Pingyao, Qufu) to 55-100 ft at a provincial capital (Kaifeng 16.7 m, Xi'an 18 m, Jingzhou 30 m), 10-20 ft deep. A second form, the broad shallow water moat (Xiangyang 130-250 m, Guide 25-500 m), is attested. 020 now points at 100 and no longer calls 66 ft a generous guess. - Kinds: moat. The provincial seat's 66 ft stays; a county-seat walled map (if any) should draw about 13 ft. The broad form is a new knob, rolled per city. Its weight (2 of 7 read) and its restriction to low ground beside a river are labeled GUESSES; no generator draws it yet. Depth is not drawn.
- B38 gate-tower footprint ACCURATE - 040: gate towers are measured at Pingyao (county, 46x33 ft), Chaozhou (prefectural, 85x43 ft) and Beijing (Yongding 24x10.5 m; Zuo'an, a lesser gate, 16x9 m = 52x30 ft). - Kinds: gate tower. No change: the drawn 52x30 ft is exactly Zuo'an's three-bay tower and sits inside the band. A provincial seat's main gate could go up to 85x43 ft if the generator ever scales towers by rank.
- B38 gatepost, guard-house and inspection-hall footprints SILENT - 040: the absence note is updated with the 2026-09-28 search. It covered Hakone's rebuilding pages, Arai's heritage-register entry (single story, faces south, long east-west, no size), kotobank 辻番所, Hikone's street guard house and the Edo Castle guard-house pages. - No change: the gatepost stays a GUESS, and the guard house and inspection hall stay estimates.
- B38 facing guard and inspection stations ACCURATE (was a GUESS) - 050: at the rebuilt Hakone barrier (rebuilt from its late-Edo repair records), the foot soldiers' guard house stands opposite the main guard house, beside the Edo-side gate. That the road runs between them is this page's reading. - Kinds: gate guard house, inspection station. The layout is unchanged. At Hakone both stood within the 59 ft between the gates, while the map seats them 105-135 ft inside the opening, about twice as far. The distance still rests on no source, and moving them nearer the opening is the orchestrator's call.
- B38 tower count against wars fought ACCURATE in direction - new 110: Mongol raids drove Ming Shanxi's three waves of wall-building (enemy platforms, corner towers, dredged moats). The most-raided basin rebuilt 91% of its walled cities; Pingyao added 94 platforms in 1567-70 and Yuci 20 in 1541; Guide carries 9. Pingyao's 72 are said to be symbolic. 060's spec now says the direction is attested and only the proportion is a calibration. - Kinds: wall tower (mamian). No drawing change; a city's setting (SIEGE/GARRISON/PEACEFUL) may now be keyed to its canon history of raids with a cited ground.
- B38 bowshot ranges CONTRADICTION-RESOLVED (partly) - new 120: the one range read is the Tongdian's drill (archers loose at 60 paces, about 92 m; crossbowmen at 150, about 230 m, using a 30.7 cm Tang foot and a five-foot pace). The Shoucheng Lu's old rule spaced mamian every 60 paces. 060's unsourced "aimed-lethal ~60 m / full reach 100-150 m" sentence is removed. - Kinds: wall tower. No drawing change: GARRISON's 100 m sits within a tenth of the archers' 92 m and counts as attested. SIEGE's 60 m is shorter than any range read and is labeled a calibration against Pingyao's 50 m spacing.
- B38 patrol road ACCURATE (existence) / width a GUESS - 080: Tongzhou's wall-side street was a Ming patrol road ("West Horse Road"). Chang'an's wall-side streets ran on all four sides inside the outer wall, 20-25 m wide, against 35-65 m for its avenues. Whether houses were barred from it is still an absence note. - Kinds: patrol road. The drawn 20 ft width and 102 ft inset are now labeled GUESSES; the one measured wall-side street, a capital's, is 66-82 ft wide. Widening the road is a possible generator change and is not made here.
- B38 fence at the rampart CONTRADICTION-RESOLVED - 090: at Tang Chang'an every ward was walled on four sides and the wall-side street ran between the outer wards and the rampart, so no ward wall met the city wall (this page's reading of the two statements together). The maps' rule, where the fence ends on the rampart and borrows it as the ward's fourth side, is now labeled a GUESS against the Tang form. - Kinds: ward fence, patrol road. This is a map-level question for the GM, because their 2026-07-27 ruling is behind the current rule. The Tang form would close the ward fence along the inner edge of the patrol road instead of crossing it to the rampart (080's "a ward fence may cross it, gated" and 060's exemption "within 186 ft of the gate of a walled ward where the ward's fence meets the rampart" both assume the current form).
