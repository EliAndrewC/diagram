B07 ACCURATE - fields/160's catena is now cited piece by piece: houses and dry fields on the natural levee because it is sandy, higher and drier; paddy in the backswamp; dry fields and settlements on river terraces, later partly irrigated; the fan middle too water-poor for paddy and long left as coppice or wild ground, dry-farmed only from late Edo, with the spring-fed toe settled early with paddy (the "dry plots around each house" half stays this page's reading, with its existing absence note) - for the dry_band knob, nothing changes on the levee, terrace or slope; one thing a map could do differently: a fan's MIDDLE drawn before late Edo would be coppice or rough grass rather than dry fields, so a fan settlement's dry band belongs at the toe's rises and edges, not across the fan middle.
B08 ACCURATE (with the estate a labeled DEVIATION) - fields/140 now sets the setting's country estate against Japan: Edo samurai lived gathered in the castle town, even landholding retainers (Sendai the exception), though some domains kept retainers in the countryside to the end; the setting's estate is closer to the medieval lord's residence with its own paddy (kadota) before its gate. fields/150 now cites the historical counterparts: Edo-period village re-allotment (warichi), regional and chiefly on unstable land, whose lots lay in pieces across every kind of ground by design, and tax-exempt temple and shrine fields (jochi); where a temple's fields lay and how large they were is an absence note - for maps, a samurai estate may rightly be drawn with paddy at its gate (the rule in 140 gives it none today; the orchestrator may put that to the GM, since estate placement is canon-led); nothing changes for the glebe marking, whose scatter stays the setting's allotment, labeled as such.
B09 ACCURATE (bracketed; the exact point stays calibrated liberty) - fields/110's coarse-grain third is no longer an unlabeled guess: no source gives a whole-diet share, but the everyday pot brackets it - a paddy district about one part barley in five, an Ina (Shinano) village c.1830 a winter midday rice of 40% rice, dry-field districts 70-80% barley - and the third sits between the first two, as fits a village among its paddy - no change to the acreage-from-population sizing (69-86 acres per 350 inhabitants stands).
