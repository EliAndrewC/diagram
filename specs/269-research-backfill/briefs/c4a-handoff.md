B41a is four questions, so there is one line for each part:

- B41a-1 KNOB - Near-city retreats are attested. New question 060 cites the Edo lower mansion (a garden retreat in the suburbs, with its vegetable plots and bamboo, sometimes on bought farmland), the dictionary's "there are cases" in other castle towns, and Suzhou's Lingering Garden outside the Changmen gate. It also records a second form: Sumoto's street of lower mansions in the castle town's outer town. 010 now points to 060, and 015 names its nearest drawn estate as that retreat - What it means for the maps: the nearest estate a city map draws is the first form (a garden retreat on its own ground outside the town), which is what the maps draw now. The second form, a street of lower mansions at the town's outer edge, has no rule yet. 060 records the two forms as a knob to roll per city, with every city taking the first until the generator can draw the second. Write-ups for the estate kinds can now say "a lord's or senior retainer's retreat with gardens and kitchen plots" as accurate.
- B41a-2 SILENT - How far an estate reached. The 010 absence note is widened (searched 2026-09-28) and 060 has its own absence note: no source read gives a distance from a castle or wall to a retreat or country estate beyond the Ningbo chapter's ten kilometers, so "within a mile or few" and "out to about fifteen miles" stay labeled guesses - What it means for the maps: nothing changes (the estates on a city map are off-sheet or at the frame edge either way).
- B41a-3 ACCURATE - Moat and fields (030). The old absence note on "the moat is the city's storm drain as well as its reservoir" is replaced with citations. For the drain: the Jin capital's storm water ran through a water gate into its south moat. For the reservoir: Yanagawa's moat is filled through a sluice and holds stored water, and its canals store fresh water and flood water. Moat water reaching fields is also cited: a medieval lord's moat irrigated his gate fields, Ueda's channel drawn at the castle town watered three villages, and Yanagawa's canal mud was spread on the fields. Which way the water flows between a given field and the moat stays the GM's 2026-08-11 ruling - What it means for the maps: no change. The moat taps, the upstream fields that drain into the moat, and the in-wall field's underground outfall are now backed by attested practice. The write-ups for moat channels can call the moat's two roles accurate.
- B41a-4 ACCURATE - Size of the strip outside a gate (040). Two measured places now bracket the guess. Odawara's ward at the castle-town entrance on the Tōkaidō had inns and tea shops and held 124 houses in the early 1830s. Nanguanxiang in Xiashi is a street of shop-houses about 350 m by 50 m. The drawn belt within about 1,560 ft of the gate is a slice of about the right length, and ten to forty structures is on the low side. The count itself stays a calibration - What it means for the maps: no change is required. The six-structure floor is conservative, and a generator could draw a denser belt at a busy gate without going past the evidence. Whether to raise the ceiling is a GM call and was not made here.
