# Step-back - where 2026-10-18's time went (feature 385, T05 to T10 and the tooling tasks)

Asked by the GM, 2026-10-18 evening: break each task down by how long it took and what it spent the time on, ask what was
wasted and what should change - and build a checklist for doing this analysis again. A change that relies on anyone (human,
LLM or agent) remembering to behave differently is not a fix and is not proposed; every change below is either an enforced
tool or a checklist item.

## How the numbers were measured

`where_time.py` (a scratch script, offered below as `make where-time`) reads the feature's event log and gives each gap between
events to the task the session had started (`make task-start`) at that moment - so nothing is counted twice. Categories are
`feature_report.split_time`'s, grouped: **model** = the session thinking and writing between tool calls; **tools** = make targets,
edits, git; **background run** = the session waiting on a gate or a page session; the check columns = the session waiting on that
kind of agent; **idle** = gaps over an hour. Agents run in the background, so their own time is listed separately (second table).

**Two limits, both themselves findings:** (1) the active-task mark did not follow the work: while T08's record sessions ran, the
mark named T46, so 37 minutes of T08's record checks are booked to T46, and the hour T08 itself shows is far below its real three;
(2) work before the 03:30Z conversation compaction (T05's start) is partly outside the event window.

## Task by task: what happened, and what was not worth it

- **T05 (the wells).** 240 of its 464 minutes were the overnight idle: a refused check dispatch was never re-sent and the session
  waited on nothing for four hours. The fix (T22, a Stop guard on refused dispatches) is in. The rest: 18 plan-review rounds, most
  of them BLOCKED and re-written - the plan was written before the measurement it needed.
- **T06 (single file).** About 20 minutes went to an experiment that the arithmetic (households x farmstead width against the
  margin's length) answers in two; two arbiter asks followed. Worth it: the fallback that takes no house back.
- **T07 (the tread margin).** The research pass and two record sessions were the job; five claims rounds found three real
  defects (the joint gap, a mis-cited threshing yard, the corner-only wall test). Waste: one wrong measurement handed to the
  arbiter (half of a caller's skip condition read), costing a ruling and a correction.
- **T08 (the pond bank).** Worth it: the glyph check caught every tree standing in the marsh. Waste: (a) a 64-claim round
  (372 s, 1.29M input tokens) because one key added to a claimed dict re-opened all 19 of its claims; (b) two red gates whose causes
  a cheap check finds before the gate (a mark under 0.95 opacity not in `see_through.py`; a branch no test reached); (c) a
  triage agent sent on a bundle with nothing to rule; (d) two part B blocks stamped in the future by hand; (e) every part B and
  re-answered Approach block went through its own spec-fidelity round, each adding a round-trip before a tick.
- **T09 (the row pitch).** Two experiments (pitch to 92 everywhere, then the row offers alone) before reading what the constant
  actually measured; the answer was a relabel with no map change. Reading the uses first would have saved about 40 minutes and
  two cohort runs. Then a full gate and a landing cohort (about 11 minutes) for a docstring-only change - fixed by T57.
- **T10 (the dike-pond split).** 109 agents. Most of them checked five near-identical title-card pop-ups as five separate units,
  three checks each, round after round, while their shared paragraph was edited by hand twice. Widening three splits to five
  mid-task (right, on the record) re-owed the whole record pass. My first edit missed half the inset's readers (the claims round
  caught it). Three preflights reviewed the wrong plan section (an umbrella heading named T10) until T56 fixed the lookup.
- **The tooling tasks (T20-T59, about 30).** About 13 hours across the day, roughly 40% of it. Most were real fixes. But the
  hourly step-back produced a new tooling fix almost every hour, and each one holds the next tick until it is built, so a
  ranking-row task cannot close while the queue is non-empty; and several fixes repaired earlier fixes or my own slips (T51 a test
  race, T55 a scope error in T52, T49 a dry run of mine, T50 my future stamps, T56 a collision I first renamed around).

## A checklist for this analysis (proposed for `docs/`)

When a task, a batch of tasks or a day of work closes and the GM asks where the time went:

1. **Measure.** Run `make where-time F=<n> [SINCE=<ISO>]` for both tables (time by bucket, agents by kind), and `make
   feature-report F=<n>` for gates, plan-review blocks, tripwires and rounds.
2. **Check the attribution.** For each task, does its booked time match its span from start to tick? Name every gap where the
   active-task mark did not follow the work, and correct the table before reading it.
3. **Split every task's time into four kinds:** the job itself (the research, the code, the map); verification that found
   something (a check round with findings acted on); verification that found nothing (rounds that came back clean or were
   superseded); rework (anything redone, reverted, re-decided or re-checked because of our own earlier step).
4. **Name each false lead:** an experiment discarded, a route reversed, a wrong measurement corrected, a review on stale text -
   with its minutes.
5. **Name each tooling-induced cost:** a guard that refused correct work, a review owed by a process rule rather than a change,
   a fix that repaired a fix.
6. **For each cost in 3-5 over ~10 minutes, propose one change** - an enforced tool, or a checklist item where no tool can do it.
   A change that relies on remembering is not allowed. Price each: minutes or agent runs saved per task, against its building cost.
7. **Put the proposals to the GM** in one table, ranked by saving, before any of them is built.
