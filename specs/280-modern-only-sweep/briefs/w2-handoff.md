## Outcomes

M32 MIXED - water/610 (new, split from 260 because 260 went over the size cap) now says the separated layout is an Edo form at the scale of the trunk canals and the drain: the Kanto school had combined channels, the Kishu school separated them, and the Minuma reclamation of 1728 put the supply trunks on the terrace edge above the paddies and the drain on their lowest ground (Konuma 1729-31 the same). A supply ditch reaching every plot with its own outlet is found only as the modern consolidation standard. 260 now points at 610 and no longer calls the layout modern - for the kinds and maps: KEEP the supply canals along the high margin and the one collector on the low line (IrrigationDitch, DrainageDitch, `hamletgen/sink.py`, `waterfields/comb.py`, all scripted hamlets and legacy comb maps). The DrainageDitch kind's Why ("as modern field consolidation lays them out", labeled `accurate`) is now wrong and owes a rewrite from 610. Wherever the comb's delivery ditches give every plot its own inlet, that part is modern-only and goes; plot-to-plot (tagoshi) flow between delivery ditches is the older form that replaces it (fields/070). That now agrees with 610.

M33 MIXED - 0054 now says the downstream lean is an Edo form: Edo-period weirs were classed by their angle to the flow, and the slanting (minote) weir put its intake on the side the water flows toward (kotobank-seki, Kitamura Toshio's section in the Heibonsha encyclopedia), and slanting weirs were common before Meiji. No premodern source gives the angle at which a canal left its river or a ditch its parent, so the 30-45 degree figure (ignou-silt-control) is modern-only. The drawn angle is a guess that the modern figure cannot warrant - for the kinds and maps: KEEP the downstream-leaning offtake (the engine's offtake; legacy Tango and Nagahara taps; the scripted hamlets' head-race offtake). No premodern figure was found to calibrate to. The 47-48 degree taps and the hamlet angle stay labeled guesses; nothing should cite 30-45 degrees as their warrant. If the maps want a number, Murakami 2014 (Landscape Research Japan 77(5), read as page images, not registered) measured surviving historic slanting WEIRS at an average of 30 degrees off the square across the river, which is the weir's angle and not the canal's. The engine phase can use it only as a labeled guess - searched: 取水口 斜め 下流向き 角度 近世 用水 分水 江戸時代 取入口; 斜め堰 取水口 角度 鋭角 下流 伝統的 堰 水路 分岐; 歴史的斜め堰の取水口; 地方凡例録 堰 箕の手 取水口 斜め 一文字堰 (Japanese); 古代 渠首 引水口 斜向 顺水 夹角 农书 开渠 引水 (Chinese); web search, Kotobank, J-STAGE, Ehime prefectural history (i-manabi), Murakami 2014, a 2023 JSIDRE lecture (first six slides); 2026-09-28. No premodern source places a reed marsh or undrained waste along a drain's line.
