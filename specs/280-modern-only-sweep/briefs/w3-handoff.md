M36 PREMODERN-ATTESTED - new water/640 says the woven stake fence across water is ancient: the shigarami, stakes in a river with brushwood or bamboo twined on them to hold back the flow, first written in the Man'yoshu (8th century) and the Heike monogatari (13th century); ARIC reads a Man'yoshu shigarami as the Kodera weir's build; the oldest excavated weir (Itazuke, Final Jomon or Early Yayoi) is stakes and sheet piles in a channel, Kodera (Kofun) stakes and crosspieces in three tiers; Edo weirs were classed (Kenrei Shuchi) into grass weirs and washing weirs; and the Kanoya weir, early Edo (about 1638), is said to have been first plants woven onto wooden stakes. The reed (kaya) weave in particular is found only in the present-day Saga weir - Weir (inashiro, kashikawa, mizuguchi, sawada): keep the fence form, drawn as now; rename its material from woven reed to woven brushwood or bamboo in the rule and the modal; no geometry change - searched for the reed weave before 1868/1912: しがらみ 柵 杭 竹 水をせき止める 万葉集, しがらみ堰 弥生時代 杭 横木 水路 遺跡 井堰, 草堰 江戸時代 杭 萱 堰 用水 農業土木, しがらみ 堰 杭 竹 編む 井堰 近世 農書 百姓伝記, 草堰 くさぜき 杭 柴 江戸, 古照遺跡 堰 しがらみ (Japanese, web search), Kotobank しがらみ/柵/堰, Gakken kobun and Kanjipedia しがらみ, Ehime prefectural history on weirs, Rekihaku's Yayoi-weir dating paper (PDF, no text layer here), 古代 灌溉 木桩 编 竹 柴 拦水 堰 农书 王祯 (Chinese, web search), 2026-09-29.
