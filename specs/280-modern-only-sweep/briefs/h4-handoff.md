M22 MIXED - the record now says the bath is premodern but as part of the house, not as a shed. The Nikko house-plan registers (Tsuda 1991) show a bath of 0.25-0.5 tsubo in "a very small part" of houses in 1770-71, beside the main door or under the eaves. In 1824 (Shimohasuda, 21/76) and 1842 (Ukiya, 15/62) there was a 1-2 tsubo bath in 2-3 houses in 10, mostly beyond the stable wing. The Hyakusho Denki (c. 1680, through a blogger's report) calls a bath a luxury for a farm. M23 PREMODERN-ATTESTED - the record now says the privy outside the house is premodern. Hasuda 1824: "most houses have a well and a privy outside the main house". Kakimochi (1885, read back to the bakumatsu): main house plus privy was the basic form, and all 16 households had one. A privy joined to the house was an official's in the registers of the 1720s-1771, and was in about 1 in 10 houses by 1824 and 2 in 10 by 1842. The Hyakusho Denki (c. 1680, through a blogger) puts the farm privy southeast of the house, in the sun, to rot the night soil quickly. - For the kinds and maps: Privy (`homestead.py:156`) and ManureHeap stay in all scripted hamlets, and the 85-95% band stays; it sits between "most" and 16/16.
