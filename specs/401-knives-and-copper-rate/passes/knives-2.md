# Pass 2 - kitchen knives, the leads (research-pass, 2026-10-19; its reply verbatim)

I found prices for Edo kitchen knives (1851, and 1665 in Tsuyama), one per-day output figure for Sakai tobacco-knife smiths (1761), and a separate polishing trade at Sakai in 1855. Nothing found names a separate handle-maker for kitchen knives. I wrote nothing in the repository; the saved pages are under `/tmp/l7r-pass/`. The project archive had nothing on any of this: `庖丁` matched only a Wikipedia page and a Chinese gazetteer, and `研屋` matched only a 1771 village trades survey that lists 研屋 as a village trade with no fee.

## 1. Sources

**A. Edo, 嘉永4年 (1851) 9月, knife prices.** 『諸問屋再興調』24巻 [41] 二十「諸色引下直段」, 「大工道具打物直段引下ケ書」. Viewer: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/2548713/1/57 and /58. I read the OCR from https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/fulltext-json/2548713 and checked it against the page images R0000054, R0000057 and R0000058.
- The document is the tool and blade wholesalers' (大工道具打物問屋) list of mandated price cuts, filed when the wholesale guilds were restored in 1851. Black ink is the old price and red ink the reduced one. These are **wholesale** prices.
- **Unit:** "十枚ニ付" sits beside the 料理庖丁 entry (image R57). Before it, 小刀 is "十本ニ付"; after it, 針 switches back to "十本ニ付" (R58). So the knife block, through 畳屋庖丁, is priced **per ten blades**. That is my reading of the page layout.
- OCR as the page has it: 「十枚ニ付…一料理庖丁薄母中八拾五匁…一差身庖丁中五拾匁之四十七匁…一弐匁庖丁中弐拾匁之十九匁…一菜切包丁中八匁五分八匁之大拾弐匁之十壱匁五分小七匁五分七匁」「一多葉粉庖丁中三拾五匁之三十三匁小拾五匁々十四匁…大六拾五…六十三匁」
- Corrected from the image: 薄母 → 薄刃; 弐匁庖丁 → probably 出刃庖丁 (cursive, my reading); 之 = 之処.

**B. Tsuyama, 寛文5年 (1665) 8月, knife price per piece.** 『吉備群書集成』第2輯「作州記」〔七三〕「諸職人作料値段定帳」, printed p.116 (frame 120). Viewer: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1912986 ; read via https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/fulltext-json/1912986 and checked against the page image.
- From the image, under the town's three sword smiths: 「一、爪打包丁 大小 銀五匁六分 / 一、包丁 一本 同 八匁 / 一、髮剃 一對 同 三匁二分 … 右三人の外鍛冶打物は直段下直に相對仕請取可申候」
  - (translation) "爪打 knife, large or small, 5.6 匁 silver; a kitchen knife, each, 8 匁; razors, a pair, 3.2 匁 … other smiths shall agree prices for forged goods below these."
- These are **ceilings**, and the makers are sword smiths. The tariff does not say whether the iron is included.
- The same page lists 「▲研屋 助左衞門・平右衞門…安兵衞」: eight named polishers with a tariff for swords only (「古身研賃 刀 代十匁…」). The facing pages give separate tariffs for 白銀屋 (metal fittings), 鞘師 (scabbard makers), 塗師 (lacquerers) and 柄巻 (hilt binders).

**C. Sakai, 宝暦11年 (1761), tobacco-knife guild rules.** 『日本経済史』第9巻 (1935) pp.36–38. Viewer: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1448699 ; read via https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/fulltext-json/1448699 and checked against image R42.
- 「一、夜細工之鍛冶は、一日之出庖丁、大には二十一枚、中にては二十七枚、小にては三十枚。右先年より定之通急度相守可申候、但し是より上之出來は晝鍛冶に可致事。」
  - (translation) "For smiths who work at night, the knives turned out in one day: large 21, medium 27, small 30. Keep to this as fixed in earlier years; anything made beyond it is to be done as daytime smithing."
  - The 1935 author reads this as a cap on night-work output and says the daytime quota is unknown. It could also be read as a whole-day cap for night-working smiths.
- 「庖丁一枚に付三厘宛口錢」 (translation) "a levy of 3 厘 per knife". 堺市史 gives 一厘 for the same levy (next source); the two disagree.
- The rules also restrict hiring 手間取 (hired hands): 「手間取抱申候節は、雙方親方互に届け合…縦一日之日雇にても、無斷て者遣ひ申間敷」.

**D. 『堺市史』第3巻 (1930), pp.716–717 (OCR pages 282–283).** Viewer: https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1049487 ; read via https://lab.ndl.go.jp/dl/api/book/fulltext-json/1049487.
- On 1730: 「享保十五年四月、庖丁に堺極の極印を打つことを許され、仲間株三十一、冥加一年三十兩、庖丁一枚につき一厘の口錢を徵して…」
  - (translation) "In 1730 the guild was allowed to stamp its knives 堺極; it had 31 shares, paid 30 ryō a year to the shogunate, and levied 1 厘 per knife."
- On 1855: 「鍛冶業者と密接の關係にある、番子、中買、諸國問屋…幕末に至つては、磨業者、仲買人この間に、値上賃に關して紛擾を重ね(安政二年覺書)…下職等に生活費を借用せしめん」
  - (translation) "Closely tied to the smiths were 番子 (forge helpers), middlemen and provincial wholesalers. By the end of the period the polishers and the middlemen were repeatedly in dispute over raising their pay (memorandum of 1855) … loans to live on for the 下職 (subcontracted workers)."
- On 1851: Miki smiths asked to set up a branch limited to 「鍛冶七軒火窪七個」 (seven smithies, seven hearths).

## 2. Verdicts

- **Kitchen-knife prices: ATTESTED.** Edo 1851, wholesale, per ten blades, in silver 匁 (A). Tsuyama 1665, a price ceiling per piece for sword-smith work: 包丁 8 匁 (B). The two differ by about ten times per knife. B is a ceiling for top-grade work in a castle town; A is wholesale bulk.
- **A smith's daily output: ATTESTED ONLY FOR the Sakai tobacco-knife guild's night-work rule of 1761:** 21, 27 or 30 knives a day by size (C). There is no daytime figure and nothing for kitchen knives. Takefu gives only totals: about 807,000 sickles and vegetable knives taxed against 129 smithies or 151 bellows (E). Any per-smith rate from that is arithmetic, not evidence.
- **The trade split: ATTESTED ONLY FOR Sakai tobacco knives, 1855.** Polishers (磨業者) were a separate trade that bargained over pay with the smiths' side, and forge helpers (番子) and middlemen (中買) are also named (D). No fee figure is given. For swords, Tsuyama 1665 shows separately licensed polishers, scabbard makers and hilt binders, each with a tariff (B). A separate **handle-maker for kitchen knives: SILENT.** I found no sharpener's fee per kitchen knife. The 守貞謾稿 search was **SILENT**: the benricho index does list a 磨師 entry, but I could not reach the text, and the NDL full-text search finds no printed 類聚近世風俗志.
- **Inui's two papers: SILENT.** Not located. Searches under 乾宏己 and 乾宏巳 with the titles found nothing. I did not search CiNii or the Osaka Kyoiku University repository directly.
- **Miki: SILENT** apart from the 1851 branch petition in D.
