# Pass - kitchen knives (#8 question 1) (research-pass, 2026-10-19; its reply verbatim)

I found no Edo-period price for any Japanese kitchen knife, no figure for how many a smith made in a day, and nothing for Seki, Takefu or Tosa. The work being split between smith, sharpener and handle-maker is attested only for Sakai around 1757, and only at second or third hand. The Chinese comparison is well attested: Qing official price lists for Beijing value a kitchen cleaver at 0.1 tael of silver, and the same paper gives the Beijing day wages.

## Sources that bear on the question

**1. 欽定大清會典則例 (四庫全書本) 卷130, 一雜料價直.** A Qing statute book compiled in the Qianlong reign (the exact year is not given here); these are Beijing official procurement prices. It is in the archive at `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/82/82e8e4c96cb7/20261019T092448Z/text.txt` (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定大清㑹典則例_(四庫全書本)/卷130).
> 庫刀菜刀各重一斤均銀一錢

"Storehouse knives and kitchen cleavers, each weighing one jin [about 600 g], are alike 0.1 tael of silver [apiece]." (translation)

For scale, from the same list: 「大斧每把重一斤銀一錢八釐」 "large axe, 1 jin, 0.108 tael" (translation), and 「鋼剪每把銀二錢」 "steel shears, 0.2 tael each" (translation).

**2. Gao Shouxian (高寿仙), 《九卿议定物料价值》的分类整理, 故宫博物院 2022.** This is a modern study of the 《九卿议定物料价值》, a price list completed in Qianlong 1 (1736) for the Ministry of Works in Beijing. It is in the archive at `/diagram/.specify/source-archive/d7/d7234ae5c7ec/20261019T074210Z/text.txt` (https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2023/02/10/u63e60647c73ae.pdf).

- Its Table 7 gives the same price as the statute book:
  > 菜刀（重1斤）每把0.1

  "Kitchen cleaver (1 jin), 0.1 tael each." (translation)
- Its Table 22 sets Ming prices before the `//` and Qing prices after it:
  > 切刀//菜刀每把[0.05]//0.1

  "Cutting knife // kitchen cleaver, per knife [0.05] // 0.1." (translation)

  The table's note says a bracketed price comes from 《萬曆會計錄》 or 《宛署雜記》, which are Wanli-era sources (1580s–1590s, Beijing/Wanping), not from 《工部廠庫須知》:
  > 《宛署杂记》的价格加"[]"标示

  "prices from the 宛署杂记 are marked with []." (translation)

  So the Ming figure is about 0.05 tael, at second hand. I could not find the 宛署雜記 text online to check it.
- Its Table 23 gives the Qing day wages, set in 1736, Beijing, paid in copper cash:
  > 木石砖瓦油画等匠 … 不论长、短给钱154文 / 壮夫 … 不论长、短给钱75文

  "Carpenters, masons, brick- and tile-workers, oilers and painters, whether long- or short-term: 154 wen; labourers: 75 wen." (translation)
- The conversion to silver is Gao's own, based on a Qianlong 2 (1737) memorial, not part of the price list:
  > 纹银一两，换大制钱八百文 … 154文折合白银0.1925两、75文折合白银0.09375两

  "One tael of fine silver exchanges for 800 cash … 154 wen equals 0.1925 tael, 75 wen equals 0.09375 tael." (translation)
- It also gives the Ming day wages, from the Ministry of Works' hired workers in 1615:
  > 分为匠和夫两个等级 … 其中最高者每工0.07两，最低者每工0.03两

  "Divided into craftsmen and labourers … the highest 0.07 tael per day's work, the lowest 0.03 tael." (translation)

**3. 河島一仁, 日本における鋳物師・鍛冶に関する研究の進展, 立命館大学.** This is a modern review summarising 山口氏's 1960s economic-geography study. The evidence it describes is Sakai around 1757. https://www.ritsumei.ac.jp/acd/cg/lt/rb/627/627PDF/kawashima.pdf (the project's page fetcher could not read the PDF, so I downloaded it with curl and read the extracted text).
> 鉄砲の生産で知られる堺において、山口氏によると、刃物工業の地域集団の基礎が確立したのは宝暦年間（1751 〜 1763）のことであり、宝暦 7 年（1757）頃には刃物工業は鍛冶・研磨・柄屋によって担われており、この当時には鍛冶がみずから問屋機能も保有していたといわれている。

"In Sakai, known for its gun production, according to Yamaguchi the cutlery industry's local group was established in the Hōreki era (1751–1763). Around Hōreki 7 (1757) the industry was carried by smiths, grinders and handle-makers, and at that time the smiths are said to have also acted as wholesalers themselves." (translation)

On Seki and Takefu it gives only when the trades formed: 「関では、打刃物を主とする刃物工業の地域集団の基盤が形成され始めたのは、享保年間（1716 〜 1735）」 "in Seki, the base of the forged-cutlery group began forming in the Kyōhō era (1716–1735)" (translation). It also says 武生 (Takefu) became a sickle-making centre in the 18th century.

**4. 堺市立図書館, 堺と包丁.** A modern library page; its evidence is from the Genroku era (1688–1704) and 1834. https://www.lib-sakai.jp/kyoudo/kyo_digi/monodukurisakai/monodukuri_hotyo.htm
> 元禄時代に刀工・山之上文殊四郎一門が料理包丁を鍛えて非常にすぐれた出刃包丁や薄刃包丁をつくって、堺包丁の名を高めました。

"In the Genroku era the swordsmith Yamanoue Monjushirō's school forged cooking knives, making excellent deba and usuba, and raised Sakai knives' name." (translation)

> 「左海（堺）たばこ庖丁鍛冶」天保5年（1834）建立

"Stele 'Sakai tobacco-knife smiths', erected 1834." (translation)

It gives no prices.

## Verdict on each figure

- **Chinese cleaver price: ATTESTED.** A 菜刀 weighing 1 jin cost 0.1 tael of silver in the Beijing official price lists: the 1736 九卿议定物料价值 and the Qianlong 會典則例.
  - At Gao's 800 cash to the tael, that is about half a day's wage for a Beijing building craftsman (154 wen, about 0.19 tael) and about one day for a labourer (75 wen, about 0.094 tael).
  - Late Ming, Beijing (Wanli): a 切刀 cost about 0.05 tael, against 0.07 tael for a craftsman's day and 0.03 for a labourer's (1615). The knife price is at second hand, through Gao's bracketed figure.
  - These are government procurement prices, not market retail prices.
- **Japanese knife prices, ordinary or from a noted maker: SILENT.**
  - Archive terms: 包丁, 庖丁, hocho, 出刃, deba, 刃物, 打刃物, 研師, togishi, 守貞謾稿, kitchen knife, knives, cleaver. The only hits were unrelated: a Sakata seed-company komatsuna page, kotobank 鍛冶町, ja.wikipedia 鎌 and 和紙, a Tōkaidō 水口 page, and the GM setting file `l7r.md`, which mentions kitchen knives and togishi but gives no historical figures.
  - Web queries: 江戸 包丁 値段 文; 出刃 一挺 銀/文; 庖丁 代 匁 with 文政/天保/元禄; 江戸物価事典 包丁; 煙草庖丁 代銀 一挺; 研屋 研ぎ代; 守貞謾稿 研屋; English "Edo kitchen knife price mon". None returned an Edo knife price.
  - Pages read: kotobank 堺打刃物 is a stub with nothing found. The rekishikaido article describes today's practice with no date. The lib-sakai page has no prices.
- **Smith's daily output: SILENT.** I searched 鍛冶 一日 何挺 鎌 包丁 and found no premodern figure.
- **Division of labour: ATTESTED ONLY FOR Sakai around 1757**, at third hand (Kawashima summarising Yamaguchi, worded as "it is said"): smith, grinder and handle-maker were separate trades, and the smiths also acted as wholesalers.
- **Seki, Takefu/Echizen and Tosa: SILENT** on price, output and division of labour. Only when the Seki (Kyōhō) and Takefu (18th century) trades formed is attested.
