What the record already holds (0277): Japan's legal rate (1 ryo = 60 monme = 4,000 mon from 1700; 6,500 mon from 1842),
the Osaka floor's daily rates 1787-1859, Edo's copper rates 1751-1842. Japan first; Qing (and Ming where found) beside
it. These questions ask for the working of the system, beyond the rates.

### 1. Legal rates against market rates

- **Question**: in each era, was there a legal rate between the metals (and within one, e.g. 4 bu to the ryo), who set
  it, was it enforced, and how far and how long did the market rate stray from it? Which ratios were fixed by count
  (gold's ryo-bu-shu; the counted silver coins such as the nanryo nishu after 1772) and which floated?
- **Figure needed**: per era, the legal rate, the market range, and the fixed-by-count ratios, with dates of changes.
- **Canon beside it**: Rokugan's fixed decimal coinage, 1 koku = 10 bu = 100 zeni = 1,000 sen (`setting/l7r.md`, "Money").

### 2. Who priced and paid in which metal

- **Question**: which transactions were priced and paid in gold, which in silver, which in copper (wholesale against
  retail, Edo's gold against Osaka's silver, wages, rents, taxes, a samurai stipend, a shop purchase)? How did a buyer
  holding one metal pay a seller who priced in another: money changers, their fees and spreads?
- **Figure needed**: the pattern by kind of transaction and region, and the changers' fees as a percentage.
- **Canon beside it**: in Rokugan daily life is copper, silver bu is for planned household sums, gold koku is for
  landlords, merchant houses and treasuries (skill's SKILL.md, "Coins and who uses them").

### 3. Did a price depend on the coin it was paid in?

- **Question**: did one good carry different prices by the metal paid (a copper price and a silver price; a premium
  or discount for paying in gold or in debased coin)? When the copper-silver rate moved, did shop prices quoted in
  copper stay put (so a copper amount's purchasing power drifted from its silver value), or follow the rate?
- **Figure needed**: examples with dates; how fast copper-quoted prices followed a move in the rate.
- **Canon beside it**: the conversion table converts each coin through its era's rate into silver, then by the basket;
  if copper prices were sticky, a mon amount should be measured against copper prices instead.

### 4. Who won and lost when the rates moved

- **Question**: when copper fell against silver (the Meiwa iron and brass coins; Tenpo; the Qing "silver dear, copper
  cheap" crisis of the 1830s-1850s) or a recoinage changed a metal's content, who gained and who lost: wage earners
  paid in copper, taxpayers owing in silver, merchants, samurai on rice stipends? What did people do about it?
- **Figure needed**: the size of the effect on a laborer's real wage and a tax burden in named episodes.
- **Canon beside it**: none yet; this is the material for any play the GM decides to import.
