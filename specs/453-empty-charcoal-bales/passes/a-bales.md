## 1. Price of an empty bale

### 1a. Empty bales (明俵; the kind of bale is not stated) bought by the ward for flood dikes: Edo (Honjo, Nakanogo, Yokokawa), 享和2 (1802)
https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/2548494 (出水一件 第54冊, 旧幕府引継書), frames 72, 74, 82 (frame 72 checked against the scan). Evidence date 1802; the volume dates itself 「享和二戌年九月四日」.
- p.72: 「土俵壱俵並一明俵弐百四拾五俵代銭壱貫文百三拾文但壱俵二付但銭六文 … 人足三拾六人此賃銭四貫五百文 … 但壱人に付銭百弐拾四文宛」
  (translation) "earth bags: 245 empty bales, price [1 kan 5]30 mon in cash, that is 6 mon a bale ... 36 laborers, their wage 4 kan 500 mon ... 124 mon a man."
- p.74: 「一明俵六拾俵代銭三百七拾弐文一まぐし縄代銭百四拾八文但壱俵ニ付銭六文」
  (translation) "60 empty bales, price 372 mon; tying rope 148 mon; 6 mon a bale." [372/60 = 6.2 mon (my division)]
- p.82: 「土俵葉土切に水防方被仰付候人足賃幷明俵保くし縄杉ノ葉等之為御入用 … 享和二戌年九月四日」
  (translation) "for the laborers' wages and the empty bales, rope, cedar leaves etc. used in the flood defense with earth bags ... Kyowa 2 (1802), 9th month, 4th day."
- Measure: about 6 mon for one empty bale (the text does not say whether it was a rice bale or a used one), bought in the hundreds, against a laborer's 124 mon a day in the same account. That is about 1/20 of a day's wage (my division).

### 1b. Empty bales for relief-shed roofs after the great fire: Edo, 文化3 (1806)
https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/2587102 (町会所一件書留 [38] 文化三寅年 類焼之部), frames 54, 65, 71-72 (frame 54 checked against the scan).

  (translation) "1,150 straw mats (mushiro), 29 mon each ... 370 bundles of rope, 67 mon a bundle [the stated totals divide to about 30.2 and 69.8 mon, so the unit figures are OCR-uncertain] ... about 3,410 empty bales at 12 mon each, 42 kan 624 mon, used for the shed roofs; about 17,712 handfuls (把) of straw ..." [42,624/3,410 = 12.5 mon a bale (my division)]
- p.65: 「明俵五拾枚且百文に付拾二枚」 and 「俵合五百六拾枚但百文に付拾弐枚之積」
  (translation) "50 empty bales at 12 for 100 mon"; "560 bales, reckoned at 12 to the 100 mon." [about 8.3 mon each (my division)]
- p.71: 「早出居残人足七拾八人賃銀百九拾五匁」
  (translation) "78 laborers working early and late, wages 195 monme [of silver]." [2.5 monme a man (my division)]

## 2. Who made charcoal bales

### 2a. Tama mountains (Nippara village), 文化11 (1814): 武蔵名勝図会, quoted in
https://www.city.akiruno.tokyo.jp/cmsfiles/contents/0000005/5474/33.pdf (五日市郷土館『郷土あれこれ』33号; archived 50/500bd79126cb)
- 「（日原村は）一歩も平かなる地はなし。男は山へ入りて木を伐り炭竈の業を第一とし、女は、萱を刈りて炭俵を作り、或は背負いて毎日氷川村まで負い出す。この地は牛馬の通う道なきゆえなり。中婦は二俵、小婦は一俵、日永きときは二遍も往復すと云。その運賃を得て米穀、塩、味噌などを求めて、帰る」
  (translation) "(Nippara) has not one step of level ground. The men go into the hills to cut wood, charcoal-burning being their first trade. The women cut kaya grass and make charcoal bales, or carry [charcoal] on their backs every day down to Hikawa village, because no road here takes oxen or horses. A middle-aged woman carries two bales, a young one one bale, and when the days are long they make the round trip twice. With the carrying fee they buy grain, salt, miso and the like and return."
- Same article: 「江戸時代の炭俵は、炭焼きと同じ山間で、女性たちが、各々の村の萱で製作した炭の容器です。」
  (translation) "The Edo-period charcoal bale was a container that women made in the same mountain villages as the charcoal-burning, from each village's own kaya." (This is the modern author's own summary.)

### 2b. Tosayama, Kochi, 20th century (date not given), anonymous oral account
https://scrapbox.io/chouroudaigaku/%E7%82%AD%E4%BF%B5%E3%81%AE%E4%BD%9C%E3%82%8A%E6%96%B9
- 「炭俵は茅（カヤ）で編む。… 最初は藁で縄を編む。… 母から習って編んで、土佐山の炭屋さんが買いに来てくれていた。１つ作るのに、１時間もかからなかった。」
  (translation) "Charcoal bales are woven of kaya. ... First you twist rope from straw. ... I learned from my mother, and the Tosayama charcoal dealer came to buy them. One took less than an hour to make."
- The project's filter judged this page earlier as "NOT KEPT (unreliable-kind, no-substance)". It is listed because it is the only text found that has charcoal bales made by women and BOUGHT SEPARATELY by a charcoal dealer.

### 2c. Village straw work by men (neighbor). The record already cites this source (okayasukebunsyo) for other lines.
https://www.town.miyashiro.lg.jp/cmsfiles/contents/0000002/2922/okayasukebunsyo.pdf (須賀村戸田家文書八七, a Musashi village account; date not given in the extract)
- 「一農業之間男を縄を索莚を織、女を木綿を織」 (translation) "Between farm work the men twist rope and weave mats, and the women weave cotton."

## 3. Time per bale; material

- Material, 1814: kaya (萱), from the Nippara passage in 2a.
- Material and output, 20th-century folklore (same Itsukaichi article, citing 大館勇吉『奥多摩の民俗』):
  「背負梯子なしで背負う炭俵は、婦人たちが萱を刈り、簾をあむ要領で編んだものです。炭を多く焼出す、短い冬の一日でも、14、5枚は仕上げたといいます。萱は、村の家々の屋根を葺くために大切に育てていた「萱処」の萱を刈って使いました。」
  (translation) "The charcoal bales, carried without a back-frame, were woven by the women from kaya they cut, the way a reed blind is woven. Even in a short winter day, when much charcoal was being burned, they are said to have finished 14 or 15. The kaya was cut from the 'kaya fields' each household kept for thatching its roof."
  The count is in 枚 (woven pieces), not in finished bales, and the evidence is 20th-century oral, not the 1814 text.
- Standard of 1935 (Tokyo prefecture), same article: 「包装はまず「丸俵」で風袋重量2.5㎏、口当ては葉なき粗朶等、口縄は藁縄２重六ツ割掛、胴縄は同３箇所括（掛）、正味量15㎏、編目４箇所」
  (translation) "Packing first in a 'round bale', tare 2.5 kg; end caps of leafless brushwood or the like; end ropes of straw rope, doubled, in six divisions; body rope of the same, tied in three places; net weight 15 kg; four rows of weaving."
- Tosayama oral account (2b): less than an hour a bale; kaya body with straw rope.
- Kameyama, Mie (Showa oral; https://kameyamarekihaku.jp/sisi/MinzokuHP/jirei/bunrui4/data4-2/index4_2_2_1.htm): 「一尺の長さにして萱製の角俵（四貫）に詰めた」 (translation) "cut to one shaku and packed in square bales of kaya (4 kan)". Material only; the ledger outcome is rejected for maker, output and price.

- How a tax-rice bale was made, Seki village, Musashi, early 18th century (village report; the date is inferred from 「当拾八年已前千川御上水」, the Senkawa aqueduct being of 1696):
  https://www2.i-repository.net/contents/myc/text_2rekishihen/rekishihen_honcho7.xhtml (archived 78/78ee895d1916): 「一　御城米　三斗七升入  但シ俵拵ハ五ツ所結立縄拾ヶ処ニ掛ケ口ハ茶カ々りニ仕候関村ゟ浅草御蔵江馬付ニ而御座候」
  (translation) "Castle rice, 3 to 7 sho to the bale. The bale is made tied in five places, with lengthwise rope run at ten places, and the ends stitched in the 'tea-kagari' way; it goes from Seki village to the Asakusa granary by packhorse." Construction only, no price or time.
- Ehime, rice hulling (modern oral; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/49/view/6481): 「俵装二人 … 一時間の工程五、六俵であった」 (translation) "two people baling ... the work went at five or six bales an hour." This is filling and closing bales, not weaving them.

## 4. Empty bales traded as a separate good

- Edo 1802, 1806, 1808 (1a-1c): wards and contractors bought empty bales (明俵; kind not stated) by the hundreds and thousands, at 6-12 mon.
- Edo 1859: the shogunate's contractor was told to deliver 5,000 empty rice bales after the Honmaru fire. They were short in the city, so the rice dealers sold them direct.
  https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/2547702 (炎上一件 4巻 [3] 御本丸炎上一件 安政六年), p.20: 「右清七江米明俵五千俵相納候様申渡候処此節市中払底ニ而買入方差支候義を以右表数之分差向米屋共相対を以て買受度 … 代料其外都而相対を以差支無之候様市中米商共え早々御触有之候様致度」
  (translation) "Seishichi was ordered to deliver 5,000 empty rice bales, but they are at present scarce in the city and hard to buy, so he wishes to buy that number directly from the rice dealers by private agreement ... we ask that the rice merchants of the city be notified at once that the price and all else may be settled by private agreement."
- Edo, Kodenmacho jail, 1840s-1860s: the jail sold its empty bran bales (糠明俵). Until 天保14 (1843) the proceeds were a perquisite of its staff.
  https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/2548603 (七十冊物類集 71巻 [43] 牢屋御入用減省方), p.34: 「牢屋敷賄所糠明俵之儀前々は絹役之者共役徳ニ相成居候処天保十四卯年」
  (translation) "The empty bran bales of the jail kitchen were formerly the perquisite of the staff, but in Tenpo 14 (1843) ..."
  p.40: 「昨年分牢扶持糖明俵払代金六両三分弐朱銭七百十八文」 (translation) "last year's sale of the empty bran bales of the prisoners' rations: 6 ryo 3 bu 2 shu and 718 mon."
  Also https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/2587751 p.11: 「糠明俵払代并参葉嫁車薪代金拾弐両永百五拾七文弐歩」 (translation) "sale of empty bran bales together with [other items]: 12 ryo ..." (for 1859; this total includes other sales).
- Tokyo 1915 (modern): a trade in empty rice bales with wholesalers and buyers walking the city.
  https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/920967 (実行容易無資本小資本実業成功法, 1915), p.36: 「◎空俵屋 … この三百萬人の一日に食する米の俵數は、殆んど、壹萬の上である。即ち一日に、壹萬餘の空俵が出るわけである。この空俵は、其れ〓〓又用途があつて、これを一々買ひ集める處がある。此の問屋では、皆二三人位づゝ空俵買ひを出して、東京市內を買ひ集めて居る … 上手に買つて歩けば、一日二百俵位は買へる。」
  (translation) "Empty-bale dealers ... the three million people [of Tokyo] eat nearly 10,000 bales of rice a day, so over 10,000 empty bales come out every day. They have further uses, and there are places that buy them up one by one. These wholesalers each send out two or three buyers to collect them across the city ... a good buyer can buy about 200 bales a day."
- Charcoal bales sold empty to a charcoal dealer: only the 20th-century Tosayama account (2b).

## 5. Share of a full charcoal bale's price

- No source of the Edo period states the share.
- Cross-date division, my own: the Edo empty RICE bale at 6-12 mon (1802-08) against Edo charcoal at 112-466 mon a bale (1842, record 0279) is about 1-11%. The dates and the goods differ.
- Japan 1914-26, white charcoal, per 100 bales of 10 kan, the 内地 (home islands) row. Ministry survey:
  https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1178389 (農林省山林局『本邦木炭生産並移動状況調査書』1931), p.8:
  「白炭標準生産費(十貫目百俵當) … 標準仕場切相128.31 408.91 346.12 … 俵裝費8.38 18.84 18.78 … 計115.67 346.40 319.92」
  (translation) "standard production cost of white charcoal (per 100 bales of 10 kan) ... standard settlement price 128.31 / 408.91 / 346.12 yen [Taisho 3, 9, 15] ... packing cost (俵装費) 8.38 / 18.84 / 18.78 ... total 115.67 / 346.40 / 319.92."
  Packing comes to 6.5% (1914), 4.6% (1920) and 5.4% (1926) of the settlement price (my division). This is the 内地 row; the 全國平均 row on the same page reads 127.14 / 399.50 / 340.35 with packing 8.38 / 18.66 / 18.95, which gives the same shares to within 0.2 points. This is packing as a whole, bale plus rope plus labor.
- Japan c.1931-33 (modern): https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1077861 (木炭関係資料 第1号, 1933), p.13: 「立木代製炭費俵裝費運搬費雜費金利 白炭41% 31% 13% 9% 3% 3% 黑炭43% 31% 12% 9% 3% 2%」
  (translation) "standing timber / burning / packing / carriage / sundries / interest: white charcoal 41, 31, 13, 9, 3, 3%; black 43, 31, 12, 9, 3, 2%". This is the planted-forest row, taken from the search highlight, so its frame is unverified. Frame 14, which I read, gives packing at 10.0-10.4% for white charcoal from natural forest (oak 10.3, kashi 10.0, nara 10.4).
- Ishikawa, 20th century, second-hand: https://www.seiryo-u.ac.jp/u/new/2023/0701.html (citing『石川県林業史』1997, p.368): 「製炭者は原木代金として収入の40%近くを山主に納め、包装・雑費に10~20%を使用」
  (translation) "the burner paid nearly 40% of his income to the forest owner for wood and spent 10-20% on packing and sundries." Packing and sundries are counted together.
- Qing Beijing 1736: see item 7. A basket for 60 jin of black charcoal comes to 5.4-6.5% of its charcoal (my division).

## 6. Neighbors: mats, 菰 bale-matting, straw, rope, earth bags

### 6a. Edo, 天保13 (1842): ward headmen's price returns under the copper-rate order
GM copy `gm-copies/keizai_2_2_69_128.pdf` (大石慎三郎「天保13年8月銭相場公定に伴う物価引下げ令による『物価書上』について」). Read from the page images, pp.108-113 of the article (PDF pp.39-44).
- (38) 藁菰直段書上 (Hongo 4-chome headman), "price before / after the cut":
  - 「武州二合半綾瀬川辺出 上藁/中藁/下藁 10把ニ付」: 240 / 140 / 80 mon, cut to 224 / 132 / 72.
  - 「武州大森玉川筋稲毛大師河原辺出 上/中/下藁 10把ニ付」: 164 / 100 / 48 mon, cut to 153 / 90 / 45.
  - 「畳薄菰 上糸網/中/下 10枚ニ付」: 372 / 348 / 248 mon, cut to 348 / 324 / 232.
  - A second 菰 (first character uncertain in the scan, printed like 婦菰), 上/中/下 per 10 sheets: 212 / 184 / 164 mon, cut to 200 / 172 / 156.
  (translation) "Straw and straw-matting prices: straw from the Ayase river near Nigohan, Musashi, per 10 handfuls, top/middle/low ...; thin straw matting [komo] for tatami, per 10 sheets ..."
  That is 菰 at about 16-37 mon a sheet and straw at 5-24 mon a handful (my division). 畳薄菰 is matting for tatami, not the bale's own sheet. What the second kind of 菰 was used for is not said.
- (41) 莚値段書上: 「上総本間莚 … 10枚」 at silver 4匁3分4厘, 4匁7厘, 3匁8分1厘, 3匁5分, 3匁2分5厘, and 上総中間莚 3匁8厘. (translation) "Kazusa full-size mats, per 10 sheets: 4.34, 4.07, 3.81, 3.5, 3.25 monme; Kazusa mid-size mats, 3.08 monme." That is about 0.3-0.43 monme a mat (my division).
- (42) 子年 (天保11, 1840) and (43) 丑年 (天保12, 1841) 新古莚売値段書上, 伊勢町 dealers, per 10 sheets. NEW and USED mats are priced side by side:
  - 1840: new 本間莚 5.85 / 5.4 / 5.1 / 4.7 / 4.5 monme, new 中間 4.7; used 本間 4.9 / 4.7 / 4.5 / 4.25 / 3.9, used 中間 4.0.
  - 1841: new 6 / 5.5 / 5.2 / 4.8 / 4.65, new 中間 4.8; used 5 / 4.8 / 4.6 / 4.4 / 4.1, used 中間 4.1.
  (translation of the headings) "Prices of new and used mats sold in the Rat year / Ox year." Used mats sold at about 83-92% of new, item by item (my division). This is direct evidence that USED straw goods were a priced trade in Edo.
- (25) 草履類 in the same returns: bleached-surface 草履 (表付草履), 9.5-11 mon a pair. These are not waraji, so they are not taken as the straw-sandal neighbor.

### 6b. Mats: Kashimo village, Mino (Owari domain forest), 明和2 (1765), with a day wage of 1774
https://rinseishi.tokugawa.or.jp/pdf_file/booklet_09.pdf (徳川林政史研究所 林政史ブックレット9; archived c4/c47e027b4ab9), table of the Naiki Hikoshichi household's purchases:
- 「112 筵 8枚 200文 明和2年 12/26」 (translation) "No.112, straw mats, 8 sheets, 200 mon, Meiwa 2, 12/26." That is 25 mon a mat (my division).
- p.11: 「日数廿七日相働候賃銭一日六拾文充之積壱分ト四百文」 (translation) "for 27 days' work, wages reckoned at 60 mon a day: 1 bu and 400 mon." (Autumn farm work, 安永3 (1774).) A mat is therefore about 0.4 of a farm laborer's day.

### 6c. Rope: Hanno, Musashi, 宝暦13 (1763), second-hand
https://ghosts.xrea.jp/daylight/yore/yore-092.htm (昔の飯能「江戸城の薪炭(5)」, from the 大野喜資家文書)
- 「29日(宝暦13年〈1763〉2月)飯能へ出て縄を買入れる。一、縄三百四拾八房此代壱メ八拾文この縄は炭俵をつくるためのものと思われ、後述する出荷量に見合うほどの数量です。」
  (translation) "29th (2nd month, Horeki 13 = 1763): went out to Hanno and bought rope. 'Rope, 348 bundles, price 1 kan 80 mon.' This rope seems to have been for making charcoal bales; the quantity matches the shipments described below."
- That is about 3.1 mon a bundle (房) (my division). Calling it bale rope is the web author's guess. The contractor shipped about 4,930 bales between the 8th and 12th months.

### 6d. Rope and mats: Edo 1806 (1b above)
Rope 67 mon a 束 and mushiro 29 mon a sheet (both OCR-uncertain: the totals divide to 69.8 and 30.2); 酒筵 28 mon; straw about 1.4 mon a handful.

### 6e. Earth bags: Osaka castle works, 寛永5 (1628)
http://archives.tuad.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/tuad-iccp-R7bulletin-2.pdf (北野博司「徳川期大坂城石垣の普請労働力」, table 1 from 「萬日用銀四組割符帳」; archived 32/324bc3ca5c32)
- 「5 （同）重ねて水通さらえの日用賃銀 994人 1,192.50 1.200 1人 … 7 水通の川除に使う土俵の代銀 1,700袋 70.00 1袋 1袋当たり4厘1毛…」
  (translation) "(5) day wages for clearing the channel again: 994 men, 1,192.5 monme, 1.2 monme a man ... (7) price of the earth bags for the channel's river guard: 1,700 bags, 70 monme, about 0.041 monme a bag."
  That is about 1/30 of a laborer-day a bag (my division). Whether the bags were bought empty or filled is not said.

## 7. China: how charcoal was packed and what the packing cost

### 7a. Beijing, 乾隆元年 (1736): 《九卿议定物料价值》, as tabulated by 高寿仙 (Palace Museum journal)
https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2023/02/10/u63e60647c73ae.pdf (archived d7/d7234ae5c7ec). The record already cites this paper for charcoal prices; the basket lines are new.
- Table 12 (日用杂品): 「黑炭筐（装60斤）每个0.0195；红螺炭筐（装10斤）每个0.0066」
  (translation) "basket for black charcoal (holding 60 jin): 0.0195 tael each; basket for Hongluo charcoal (holding 10 jin): 0.0066 tael each."
  The same table: 「大蒲包每个0.01」 (translation) "large cattail-matting bag: 0.01 tael each"; 「席筐（高1尺径1.6尺）每个0.03」 (translation) "mat basket: 0.03."
- Table 2: 「黑炭每百斤0.5/0.6；白炭每百斤1.1；红螺炭每百斤1.06」 (translation) "black charcoal 0.5/0.6 tael per 100 jin; white charcoal 1.1; Hongluo charcoal 1.06."
- Table 23 and text: 「壮夫 每日长工给钱80文、短工60文 → 不论长、短给钱75文」 and 「按照前述银1两兑钱800文的比例」 (translation) "able-bodied laborer: 80 cash a day long-term and 60 short-term, now 75 cash for either"; "at the rate of 1 tael to 800 cash."
- My division:
  - 60 jin of black charcoal costs 0.30-0.36 tael, so its basket is 5.4-6.5% of the charcoal.
  - 10 jin of Hongluo charcoal costs 0.106 tael, so its basket is about 6.2%.
  - The black-charcoal basket is about 15.6 cash, about 0.21 of a laborer's day.
- These are official purchase prices for the Board of Works, based on the Beijing market.

### 7b. Ming
The paper compares the Ming 《工部广库须知》 (1615) item by item, but no charcoal basket appears among the Ming items it shows. Nearest Ming neighbor found:
https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明㑹典_(四庫全書本)/卷136 (archived 63/6332374c4bd7), the Board of Punishments' valuation list: 「灰炭每十斤一貫 … 蘆蓆一領一貫」 (translation) "charcoal, 1 guan per 10 jin ... a reed mat, 1 guan each." These are paper-money valuations for assessing stolen goods, not market prices.

## Verdicts

1. **Price of an empty bale: ATTESTED ONLY FOR empty bales of unstated kind (明俵), bought in Edo for flood dikes and relief sheds.** Two frames were checked against the scans; the rest is machine OCR.
   - Edo 1802: 6 mon a bale against a 124-mon laborer, about 1/20 of a day.
   - Edo 1806: 8-12.5 mon, against 2.5 monme of silver a day.
   - Edo 1808: 10-11 mon, against 141-300 mon a day.
   - Hamura, dated by inference to 1868-69: 1 monme, against 2 monme a day.
   - Osaka 1628: earth bags at 0.041 monme, against 1.2 monme a day.
   - No Edo-period price for an empty CHARCOAL bale bought on its own was found (SILENT on that). Whether these bale prices stand in for one is for the session or the GM to decide.
2. **Who made them: ATTESTED** for the Tama mountains in 1814: the women of charcoal-burning households wove the bales from village kaya while the men burned. It is ATTESTED ONLY in 20th-century oral accounts (Tosayama) that women wove them for sale to a charcoal dealer. Men twisting rope and weaving mats between farm work is attested for a Musashi village (undated in the extract).
3. **Time and material: material ATTESTED** (kaya body, 1814; straw rope and brushwood or straw end caps in the 1935 standard). **Time: ATTESTED ONLY FOR the 20th century:** 14-15 woven pieces in a short winter day (Okutama folklore), and under an hour a bale (Tosayama). Nothing of the Edo period was found.
4. **Separate trade: ATTESTED for empty bales in Edo.**
   - The wards bought them, kind unstated, by the hundreds and thousands, 1802-1808.
   - In 1859, empty RICE bales (米明俵) were to be bought from the rice dealers.
   - The Kodenmacho jail sold its empty bran bales, about 7-10 ryo a year from the bales alone, 1840s-1860s.
   - Used mats sold beside new at about 83-92% of the new price, 1840-41.
   - The empty-bale wholesalers of 1915 Tokyo (modern) fill in the trade.
   - For CHARCOAL bales traded empty, only the 20th-century Tosayama account was found.
5. **Share of a full bale: SILENT for Edo.**
   - Packing was 4.6-6.5% of the settlement price for white charcoal, nationally, 1914-26.
   - It was 10-13% of production cost in 1933, and 10-20% "packing and sundries" in 20th-century Ishikawa.
   - The Qing Beijing basket was 5.4-6.5% of its charcoal in 1736.
   - The Edo rice-bale and charcoal prices of different dates give about 1-11% (all divisions mine).
6. **Neighbors: ATTESTED.**
   - Mushiro: 25 mon (Mino, 1765; a farm day wage was 60 mon); about 29-30 mon (Edo 1806); 0.3-0.6 monme silver in Edo 1840-42; used mats about 83-92% of new.
   - Komo matting (tatami and one other kind): 16-37 mon a sheet (Edo 1842).
   - Straw: 5-24 mon per handful (Edo 1842).
   - Rope: about 67-70 mon a 束 (Edo 1806); about 3 mon a 房 (Hanno 1763).
   - Waraji, kamasu and rice bales woven per day: SILENT.
7. **China: ATTESTED (Qing).** Beijing 1736: a black-charcoal basket (筐) holding 60 jin cost 0.0195 tael, and one holding 10 jin 0.0066 tael. A large 蒲包 cost 0.01 tael. A laborer earned 75 cash a day at 800 cash to the tael. Ming: SILENT on charcoal packing.
