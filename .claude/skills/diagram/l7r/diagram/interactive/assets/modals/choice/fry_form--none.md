About: Most dike-pond villages of the Pearl River delta kept no nursery ponds. The carp that stocked their ponds were not bred
there: the young fish, the fry, 魚苗 (yumiao, "fish fry"), were netted wild in the West River and sold. From the middle of the
Ming one township, Jiujiang in Nanhai, held the river's fry landings and supplied the other pond districts. A writer of 1678
says fry ponds were found only in Jiujiang; elsewhere the ponds raised grown fish, and where villages tried to raise fry, the
fry did not thrive.

Such a village's ponds were all grow-out water: bought fry went in and were raised to market size, the ponds drained and
their mud dredged onto the dikes in turn. Fry dealing was old: in the 13th century dealers carried fry from Jiangzhou to other
provinces, rearing them a few weeks in a cloth pen hung in open water before selling them.

How many villages bought their fry is not counted, but by the 1678 account it was nearly all of them outside Jiujiang.

Guesses:
- That about three dike-pond hamlets in four buy their fry: no source gives the odds, so the fry village is made the rarer form, by the 1678 writer's word that fry ponds were found only in Jiujiang.

Depiction: The map draws every pond of the hamlet in the same clear grow-out water. No small nursery pond is drawn beside a
big one: that is the method of inland Jiangxi in a farming compendium of 1639, away from the delta whose ponds the map draws.
