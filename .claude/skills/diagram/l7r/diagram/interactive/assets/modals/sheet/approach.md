About: The approach, the sando, was the way up to a shrine's hall from where it entered the shrine's ground. An open
gateway arch, the torii, stood over its start, marking where the kami's ground began, and further arches might stand along it
toward the hall; on a long approach they were counted first, second and third from the outermost in. Gravel laid along an
approach was meant to purify the ground it covered, and beside it stood the place where worshippers rinsed their hands and
mouth before they prayed.

An arch was a gift, from a shrine's parishioners together or from a single donor, so a shrine's arches recorded what its
worshippers gave, not its size. Before 1868 every dated arch found at an ordinary shrine is a single gate; a row of donated
arches is known only at the great Inari shrine of Fushimi, where the custom arose in the Edo period. In Rokugan any shrine's
approach may pass under a row of arches, one, three or seven of them, numbers that carry power in the setting, seven most of
all.

How long or how wide a village shrine's approach ran is not recorded. The approach of Nagao Shrine in Nara, a great shrine,
is a little over 23 ft wide; it was once a course for mounted archery.

Guesses:
- Its width on the plan, about 10 ft: no village shrine's approach was found measured, and the one width found is a great
  shrine's.

Depiction: The plan draws the approach as a straight band of bare ground from the outermost arch to the hall's step, on the
line of the hall and its sanctuary; that it runs on that line is a drawing convention, since where a village shrine's approach
ran is not recorded, and its course is drawn to fit the ground it crosses. Its arches stand evenly spaced along it, the
innermost as far off the hall's face as the arches stand from one another, so that the avenue reads as that hall's; how far
a real innermost arch stood from its hall is not recorded.

Name: approach
Covers: the path from the precinct's edge to the hall
