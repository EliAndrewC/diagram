About: A small watch hut at the head of the landing, manned from the office, from which the watch inspects and records the boats,
people and goods that come to it.

In the Edo period, domains kept boat watch posts (funabansho) at the landings of busy boat routes and other key points, to inspect
passing boats and collect taxes. The best recorded, the shogunate's Nakagawa post of 1661 on the river way into Edo, was a
palisaded compound of about 155 by 100 ft with ten spears and a watch hut at the river's edge, manned by the retainers of the
bannermen who held the duty; its inspection seems to have become, little by little, a formality.

In Rokugan goods are taxed where they are sold, not where they pass through, so this watch takes no toll.

Guesses:
- A watch at a local office's own landing, and its hut's size, about 12 by 10 ft: no account of one was found, nor the size of
  any watch hut.

Depiction: The plan draws the hut alone, without the Nakagawa post's fenced compound, which belonged to a checkpoint on the way
into a capital.
