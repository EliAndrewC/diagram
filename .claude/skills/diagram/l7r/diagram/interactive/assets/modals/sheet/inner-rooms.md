About: In a lord's palace the private rooms, the oku, lay at the far end from the front where guests were received, beyond the
lord's own rooms. They held the lord's private rooms and the quarters of the lord's wife, and because women of high status lived
there, who came and went was strictly limited; at Edo the shogun's great oku was walled off from the rest of the palace and
joined to it by a single corridor. A chief retainer's great house at Aizu ran from the hall where the lord was received through
the office rooms and the inner rooms to the kitchen and the bath. A smaller house could run the other way: at one samurai
house at Hagi the deepest rooms seem to have been the formal ones, the zashiki and the inner zashiki.

The household's altar room, the butsuma, where it kept its Buddha images and the memorial tablets of its dead, is not recorded
among them. Where a house gave it a place of its own, it stood by the formal rooms: beside the alcove of the zashiki, or in a
small room behind the inner room or the zashiki.

Like nearly every room of a samurai house, they were matted. What they held room by room, and how large they were, is not
recorded.

Guesses:
- The size of the bay the plan draws: how large a household's private rooms were is not recorded, so the bay takes the far end
  of a house sized to a district magistrate's house of about 1,740 sq ft.

Depiction: The plan draws the inner rooms as one labeled bay standing for several rooms, at the far end of the residence from
