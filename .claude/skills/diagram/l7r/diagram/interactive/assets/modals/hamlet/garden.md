About: A farming household grew vegetables for its own table on a bed of its own, the yashikibatake, the "homestead
field". It might lie in the yard before the house, all round the house, or on spare ground away from it, such as a corner
beside the fields.

What the bed's surface and rows were like is not recorded. In one hillside settlement the ground round a house was made a
vegetable field, with no edge of its own; in 17th-century China a garden was to be fenced with a thorny hedge. In the early
Edo period a household's greens ran month by month - daikon, burdock and water dropwort in the first month; eggplant, cowpeas,
gourds and chives in the sixth; taro, turnips, yams and daikon in the tenth - though some were gathered wild, and which grew in the bed and
which in the fields is not recorded.

A house lot, with its house, sheds and yard, was a few thousand square feet; in one Japanese prefecture about
3,200 sq ft (300 sq m) was usual. The bed's own size is not recorded: in one district in the 1920s, the ground a lot had left
after its buildings and drying yard came to about 1,600 sq ft (150 sq m), and the vegetable ground on it was small. In
17th-century China a garden of one mu - about 6,600 sq ft on a later standard - was reckoned enough to keep a household of
ten in vegetables all year.

Guesses:
- Its size on the map, about 110 to 1,500 sq ft (10 to 140 sq m) for a household's beds together: no record gives a bed's
  area, so the range is reckoned from what a house lot could hold, its upper end near the ground one 1920s district's lots had
  left beside their buildings and yard.
- That the bed held the daily greens while the bulk crops grew out in the household's dry fields: no account says which
  vegetables grew beside the house and which in the fields.

Depiction: The map draws a household's garden as one or two beds of tilled earth with three rows of greens, so a bed reads as
worked ground at a glance, and bends each bed a little out of square so it reads as hand-worked; how a real bed's rows and
outline ran is not recorded. The beds are drawn to scale, and kept in the
sun, clear of the shade a house, a tree or bamboo casts in late autumn.
