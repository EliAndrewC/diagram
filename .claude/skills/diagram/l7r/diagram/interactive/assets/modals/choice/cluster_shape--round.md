About: A clustered village took its shape from its site, its density and its roads, and one shape was the lump: the
houses gathered irregularly into one compact clump. Among clustered villages generally, in Japan and beyond, this block
village was the commonest form, ranked above the village strung along a road. A lump of houses grew at the foot of a hill or plateau, on a river
terrace or other narrow ground where water could be got, or where two highways crossed. In the hills of southwest Zhejiang,
in China, too, villages were mostly clusters and bands, held in by the ground.

A visitor saw roofs bunched together rather than strung out, the fields lying around them. How tightly they bunched differed
by region: around the old capital and the Inland Sea, farmhouses stood side by side behind walls; in the Kanto and the
Tohoku each house stood on a wide lot in its own grove, the houses well apart.

No count of how many villages took each shape is recorded, nor any measure of a lump village's size on the ground.
