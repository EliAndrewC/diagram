About: A samurai house received and lodged its guests inside the house. The residence at the Takayama intendant's office
had a room for receiving guests among its rooms, and a middle-rank house kept its guest space - the formal entrance with its
low board step, the shikidai, the anteroom where a visitor was announced, and the zashiki, the guest room - within its one
main house, apart from the family's living rooms.

A guest of rank came by palanquin through the gate and across open ground, and the palanquin was brought right alongside
the step, so that the guest went into the building without setting foot on the ground. A middle-rank house could do without
such an entrance: its guest passed through a middle gate and along a walled garden path straight to the veranda of the
zashiki. No guest house standing apart is known at a samurai house; a traveling lord or official lodged instead at a honjin,
a post town's house for guests of rank, with its raised room of honor.

How large a guest room was is not recorded.

Guesses:
- A guest house drawn as a building of its own: none is known at a samurai house, and the nearest form, the honjin's suite
  of honor, belonged to a post town's lodging house, not to an office.
- A garden between the gate and a guest's door, where a plan draws one: none is recorded there, and the one drawing of that
  ground shows it open.

Depiction: The plan draws the guest quarters as the guest room of the residence, by the reception, or as a guest house of
its own. A guest's door opens onto open ground a palanquin could cross, never straight from the street or against the side
of a building.

Name: guest quarters
Covers: the guest room of the residence with its floor and label, or a detached guest house and its label
