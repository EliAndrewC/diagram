About: Apart from the farmsteads, bamboo stood in thickets of its own, the take-yabu, "bamboo thicket". In the Edo period
the countryside round Kyoto was painted with its settlements ringed by bamboo, and the villages of one district southwest
of the city owed bamboo as a tax from the start of the period, managed their own groves, and had farmers who kept bamboo
shops on the side. A sixth-century Chinese farm manual wanted bamboo on high, level ground, near hills best, and said it
died in a low field once water reached it. Who held a village's thicket is not recorded.

A thicket is one plant spreading by shoots from an underground rootstock: its stems are clones, most often of nearly one
species, and it shades out almost everything else. Madake, the common timber bamboo, grows canes up to about 66 ft
(20 m) tall and at most about 4 in (10 cm) across; the thickets painted round Kyoto are taken to be madake or hachiku.
Kept as a resource, a thicket was cut in moderation and renewed itself from its own shoots. Many still stand today on
the outskirts of the plain, where it meets the worked hills.

How large a village's thicket ran is not recorded.

Guesses:
- Its size, about 84 by 58 ft, a working stand: no source gives the size of a village's thicket.
- Which side of the settlement it stands on: no source says, beyond high, dry ground.
- That the thicket belongs to the village as a whole: no source says who held one, of any period.

Depiction: The map draws the thicket as an area true to scale in its place and size, filled with paired cane strokes and
a leafy fork in jade green over a light wash, one small block of marks repeated across it, as it draws a farm's bamboo;
a real cane, a few inches across, is far too slim to see at the map's scale. The mark is the one modern Japanese map
legends give bamboo; before then map-makers painted the growth itself. The thicket stands on dry ground just beyond the back row of
houses, never on a paddy. Whether a settlement keeps a thicket, bamboo on its farms, or both, the title card says.
