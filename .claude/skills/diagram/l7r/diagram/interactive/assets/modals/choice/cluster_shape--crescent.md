About: A clustered village took the shape of the dry ground it stood on, and that ground could bend with the land: the foot
of a fan, the bank of a river's old channel. On a river's lowland a village on the natural levee could
run in a curve beside the old channel, and at the foot of one alluvial fan in Iyo, where the water sunk into the fan rose
again as springs, many villages lined up in an arc.

A crescent village would show its houses curved round the edge of its fields, the paddy in the hollow of the curve. The crescent
is not recorded as a village shape of its own; the shapes recorded are the lump and the line.

How many villages took such a curve, and how large they were, are not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a cluster may bend into a crescent along its field's edge: no account names the crescent as a village shape, so it is
  an extension of the attested lump and line.
- How often a cluster comes out a crescent: no count of village shapes was found, so the share is set for the maps.

Depiction: The map seats the houses in a band that follows the dry edge of the fields round its bend. The title card calls a
cluster a crescent only when the finished houses run about two to four times as far one way as the other.

Name: The cluster's shape: a crescent
