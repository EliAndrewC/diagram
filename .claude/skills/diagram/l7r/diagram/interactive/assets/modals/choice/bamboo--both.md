About: A farming settlement below the frost line could keep bamboo in two places at once: a stand on each household's own
farmstead, and a thicket of the village's own, the take-yabu, apart from the houses. On the farmstead it grew in the
household's grove, low under the tall trees on the windward side, beside the storehouses on some farms, or where its roots
held a riverbank. Of the thickets, in the Edo period the villages of one district southwest of Kyoto owed bamboo as a tax
and managed their own groves, and a screen of the countryside round Kyoto paints its settlements ringed by bamboo.

Each stand was a thicket of its own, nearly all of one kind, spreading by shoots from an underground rhizome and shading
out almost everything beneath it. Madake, the common timber bamboo, grew a culm about 66 ft (20 m) tall and at most about
4 in (10 cm) across. It went into baskets, tools and building, and its shoots were eaten.

How many farmsteads kept their own bamboo, and how large a village thicket was, are not recorded; on the Tonami plain the
farmstead groves once held many stands, so it was probably common but not universal.

Guesses:
- That about three farmsteads in five keep a stand of their own, about 22 by 16 ft: no source gives a share or a size.
- A thicket of about 84 by 58 ft, on dry ground just beyond the back row of houses and held in common: no source gives its
  size, its side of the settlement or who held it.
- How often a settlement keeps bamboo in both places rather than one or none: no source counts them.

Depiction: A bamboo culm is far too slim to see at the map's scale, so the map draws each stand as an area, its place and
size to scale, filled with paired culm strokes and a leafy fork in jade green over a light jade wash - a mark taken from
modern map legends, which stands for the bamboo and does not count it. A farm's stand is a strip among its sheds and garden,
or a patch inside its own grove where it shows between the tree crowns; the thicket stands on dry ground behind the houses.
No stand is drawn on a paddy, since bamboo dies in standing water.
