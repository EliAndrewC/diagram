About: A rack held weapons ready where samurai and their guards kept them, and there were two kinds. The sword rack
(katanakake) was a stand or shelf with forked supports - deer antlers, for one - set at the same height left and right, so
that the swords lay level, usually in two tiers for the long and the short sword. A guardroom kept one, and a man took off
his sword and hung it there.

At checkpoints and guard posts the "three tools" of arrest were kept, the tsukubo, the sasumata and the sodegarami, all on
long shafts. In the early Edo period they could be seen stood upright in a row along a bamboo fence beside a theater's
entrance, probably used as a set from early on, as symbols of order and authority.

A rack for practice weapons at the edge of a practice ground is not recorded, nor its size.

Guesses:
- That a practice ground kept a rack for its practice weapons at its edge: joined from the guardroom's sword rack and the
  guard post's long weapons, since no account describes one.
- Its size, about 8 by 2 ft: no rack's size was found.
- That weapons stood upright in the open: taken from the painting of the arrest weapons at a theater's entrance.

Depiction: The plan draws the rack to scale from above, at its guessed size, as a bar at the practice ground's edge with
a label; the weapons on it are not drawn.
