About: A rack held weapons ready where samurai kept them, and two real kinds are recorded. The sword rack (katanakake) was
a stand or shelf with forked supports - deer antlers, for one - set at the same height left and right, so that the swords
lay level, usually in two tiers for the long and the short sword. It stood indoors: a story of 1914 has a man take off his
sword and hang it on the rack of a guardroom.

At checkpoints and guard posts the "three tools" of arrest were kept, the tsukubo, the sasumata and the sodegarami, all on
long shafts. A screen painting of the early Edo period shows them stood upright in a row along a bamboo fence beside a
theater's entrance, probably as a set and as symbols of order and authority.

A rack for practice weapons at the edge of a practice ground is not recorded, nor its size.

Guesses:
- That a practice ground kept a rack for its practice weapons at its edge: joined from the guardroom's sword rack and the
  guard post's long weapons, since no account describes one.
- Its size, about 8 by 2 ft: no rack's size was found.
- That weapons stood upright in the open: taken from the painting of the arrest weapons at a theater's entrance.

Depiction: The plan draws the rack as a small marker at the practice ground's edge, beside the building next to it, with a
label.

Name: weapon rack
Covers: the rack at the practice ground's edge and its label
