About: The kennel housed a household's hunting dogs. Warrior households hunted with dogs: the great warlords loved hawking,
and it spread widely among the daimyo, the dogs driving the game out of the thickets for the hawk, kept and trained by
dog-handlers under the chief falconer. Raising hunting dogs was a trade of its own, and in the Kii mountains boar was hunted
with packs of dogs, three or four to each hunter.

What a household's kennel looked like, how big it was and where it stood are not recorded. The only dog houses known are
the shogunate's shelters for sick and ownerless dogs, the first of them inside an official's compound at Kitami: in 1693
about forty dogs were kept there, in a care house, a nursing house and sleeping quarters for sick dogs and pups. Those were
dogs in care, not a hunting pack.

Guesses:
- Its form and size, a small building about 11 ft square: no source describes a household's hunting kennel.
- That it stands beside the stables: no source puts the two together, so it is set where both share the household's animals'
  yard.

Depiction: The plan draws the kennel as a small building beside the stables in the service yard, sharing the yard's well
and the service gate, away from the ceremonial ground of the forecourt.
