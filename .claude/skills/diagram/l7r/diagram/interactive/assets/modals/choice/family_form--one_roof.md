About: A farming household of the later Edo period averaged about five, across two or three generations: most often a stem
family, the parents living with one married heir and the heir's children. In this form the generations stayed together
under the farmhouse's one roof when the old couple handed the farm to their heir, sharing one house, one table and one purse.
That was the common way along the Sea of Japan coast and in the northeast, where the large family held together; elsewhere
the retired couple often moved out to a small house of their own in the yard.

On the ground nothing marked it: one farmhouse stood for each household, its old couple living in the same rooms as their heir's
family.

How common the one-roof form was, against the retirement house, is not counted village by village; in one village counted in
1885, 8 of its 16 households kept a retirement house. Across a whole region the share is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a settlement keeps its old couples under one roof or in retirement houses at even odds: no source weighs the two forms against each other.

Depiction: The map draws one farmhouse for each household and no second roof beside it.
