About: A cart yard is open working ground inside a compound's cart gate, where carts stand to load and unload at the
stores - rice bales into a granary, charcoal into its store. It is Rokugan's own: there wagons and carts use the roads
between towns, and hay goes in wagons behind teams of oxen, so a compound that ships or receives bulk goods keeps a cart
gate and a yard. Edo Japan kept its carts to the towns and barred them from its highways to the end of the shogunate;
it sent its heavy freight by water, and overland a load went on a packhorse, about 300 lb to a horse.

The Edo hand cart, which spread through the castle towns and beyond them late in the period, had a bed about 8 by 2.5 ft.
What surface a loading ground had is not recorded. A yard beside a charcoal store needed no ground for cooling, since
charcoal was cooled at the kiln before it traveled, and no fire gap of a set width round a charcoal store is recorded
before modern times.

How large a compound's cart yard was is not recorded. At the one intendant's office measured, the buildings covered
at most about three-tenths of the ground and the rest lay open, and its largest building was its rice storehouse, about 11,200 sq
ft.

Guesses:
- Its width, about 15 to 20 ft: no loading apron's width was found, so it is reckoned as no wider than carts and draft
  animals need to stand and turn.

Depiction: The plan draws the yard as open court ground inside the cart gate, marked only by its label.
