About: A cart yard was open working ground inside a compound's cart gate, where carts stood to load and unload at the
stores - rice bales into a granary, charcoal into its store. In Rokugan wagons and carts use the roads between towns, and
hay goes in wagons behind teams of oxen. Edo Japan kept its carts to the towns and barred them from its highways to the
end of the shogunate; it sent its heavy freight by water, and overland a load went on a packhorse, about 300 lb to a
horse.

The Edo hand cart, which spread through the castle towns and beyond them late in the period, had a bed about 8 by 2.5 ft.
What surface a loading ground had is not recorded. A yard beside a charcoal store needed no ground for cooling, since
charcoal was cooled at the kiln before it traveled, and no fire gap of a set width round a charcoal store is recorded
before modern times.

How large a compound's cart yard was is not recorded.

Guesses:
- Its width, about 15 to 20 ft: no loading apron's width was found, so it is reckoned as no wider than carts and draft
  animals need to stand and turn.

Depiction: The plan draws the yard as open ground beside the stores it serves, inside the cart gate, with a label.
