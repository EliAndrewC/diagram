About: A farm that plowed with a beast kept an ox, a water buffalo or a horse - in Japan horses in the east and cattle in the
west, in China's rice country the ox and the water buffalo. Every arrangement recorded ties the beast to one keeper: it
lived in its keeper's farmhouse or in a stable of the keeper's own. A shed several households kept in common, on ground
between their houses, is not recorded.

Sharing a beast is recorded, though. Not every household kept one: in one western province from the early 1700s only about
half did, and fewer as time went on. In the mid-1600s some beasts there were placed in a farmer's care rather than bought;
one headman's household of the early 1800s owned about a thousand cattle and placed them with borrowers near and far; and
Sanuki, short of cattle at plowing time, hired them from the mountain villages of neighboring Awa for a fee in rice.

A stable recorded on its own was a single-story timber shed; two surviving from the Edo period cover about 320 and 370 sq ft
(30 and 34 sq m).

Guesses:
- That several households kept their beasts in one shed on the common ground among the houses, about one shed to every four or five households: no source describes such a shed.
- That a settlement takes this form about one time in ten, the rarest of the three: no source counts the forms.

Depiction: The map draws each shared shed as a small one-room shed of about 16 by 11 ft, open-fronted under a plank roof, on
open ground among the houses where several households can reach it, never alone beyond the last house.
