About: A clustered village whose dry ground ran in a line took that line's shape. Settlement geography names houses in a line
along a natural levee or the spring line at a hill's foot, and houses in a line along a road, beside the lump of houses
gathered irregularly. On a river's lowland the old villages took the natural levee, a long, slight rise that drained well
above the wet ground behind it, and one such village ran in a curve beside the river's old channel. In the hills of southwest
Zhejiang, in China, villages formed clusters and bands and grew along rivers and roads.

A visitor saw the houses drawn out along the dry ground, longer than they were deep, the paddy lying beside them on the low
side.

No count of how many villages took a long shape survives, nor how long such a village ran or how deep.
