About: Where fields were won from the water behind dikes, the dike itself could be the dry ground a village stood on. Around
Lake Tai, in China, the margin of each polder was left higher than the fields inside it, a strip 33 to 98 ft (10 to 30 m)
wide for mulberry with a wider space for houses, and that raised margin also served as the farm's dike. In Japan, on land
reclaimed from the sea, the farmhouses lined the dike; and in the delta where the three Kiso rivers meet the sea, where storm
surges struck and natural levees were few, the houses crowded onto the highest dike inside the village's ring dike. The
saying handed down there was "if the levee breaks, flee to the levee".

A visitor saw a single line of farmhouses along the bank, the polder's fields spread below on one side and the open water
on the other. Around Lake Tai the houses stood near the water because the boat was how the villagers traveled, and
mulberry grew on the raised ground beside them.

How old such dike-top houses were is not recorded, nor how many households a dike carried or how far apart they stood.

Guesses:
- That ten to twenty households shared a small polder: no figure for a small polder's households was found.
- That each house stood on its own stretch of crest widened for it, with spoil piled from the ditches: the margin is
  recorded as wider where the houses stood, but no account of a house's ground or its size was found, so the platform is a
  reconstruction.
- That the houses stand at least a little more than a house's width apart along the crest: no figure for their spacing was
  found.

Depiction: The map seats the farmhouses in single file on the crest of the polder's dike, each on a widened stretch of bank so
the dike bulges at every house instead of the house overhanging the water. No house is set over a sluice notch. The crest
serves as each house's yard and lane, so no threshing yard or garden is drawn up on it. The map draws this form only for a
polder with water on every side and no shore to build on; a polder against a dry shore has its village on the shore.
