About: A manure pit was an earthenware jar sunk in the ground, in which a household kept its night soil until it went out
to the fields; human manure was the most important fertilizer a farm had. In one silk village by Lake Tai in 1936 the
jars stood half buried behind the houses, and the public road along the stream was lined with them. In the 18th century a
British visitor saw large earthen vessels buried in various parts of a farm and by paths and roads, for a laborer or a
passer-by to use. Out by the fields, a Chinese farming book of 1313 already has southern households ripening manure in a
brick-lined pit at the head of the field. In Japan a storage jar for night soil is on record by 1681, and in three
villages in 1959 it was kept in a tank beside the privy or in a pit out by the fields, moved there to make manuring
easier.

What showed was the jar's open mouth at the ground: buried to the rim by a road, half buried behind a Lake Tai house. A
farm manual of the 17th century wanted its pits deep and large.

How big a jar was is not recorded. How many households kept theirs out by the fields varied widely from village to
village: in 1959, 2 households of 83 in one village and 15 of 18 in another.

Guesses:
- That a settlement keeps its manure in sunk jars or in heaps at even odds: both forms are recorded, but no source says
  how many villages used each.
- That the silk village's jar stood out by a field or a road as Japan's field pit did, sought within 160 ft of the house:
  the field pit is found in Japan, and the field-head pits of south China were lined with brick, not jars.
- The jar's mouth on the map, 3.5 ft across: no manure jar's size was found.

Depiction: The map draws each jar as its mouth seen from above, a dark disc inside a pale rim, flush with the ground; a
jar buried to its rim and one half buried look the same from above, so both are drawn as one mouth, a map drawing
convention. A jar stands beside its household's privy, or out at the edge of the household's nearest field or by a road;
how many stand out by the fields differs from settlement to settlement.
