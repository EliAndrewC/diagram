About: A clustered village's shape followed its ground and its roads. Houses gathered in a lump at a hill's foot or on a
river terrace, or strung out in a line along a natural levee, a spring line or a road. A Y of lanes is two ways coming in
from above and merging into one stem that runs on down through the houses, perhaps where two approaches to a village met. A worn
path takes the shortest or the easiest way, so two such ways would join where their walkers' routes ran together.

Each lane was a strip of bare earth, trodden hard and muddy in rain, made for feet, carrying poles and the odd barrow or
packhorse rather than carts. How wide a village lane ran is not recorded; the one old figure for a field road is about 3 ft.

What shapes a village's lanes took, and how common each was, is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a village's lanes took one of a few shapes - a spine, a T, a Y, a crossroads or a lane along the water - and that a Y formed where two approaches merged: no description of the shapes a village's lanes took was found.
- How often each shape comes up: no source counts them.

Depiction: The map seats the houses first and lays the Y after, fitted to where they stand: two arms from the upper side of
the cluster joining in a stem that runs down to its foot; the households' own ways join it. Each arm is drawn as a track of
bare earth a little wider than a footpath, pulled as straight as the plots allow.
