About: The official study was the magistrate's own working room in the office hall, where the office's papers were read
and answered. A magistrate's household lived in the same compound, but a private study in the residence most likely
stood on the wrong side of the line between office and home for official business, so the office hall kept a working
room of its own. At Takayama, the one intendant's office that survives, the entrance hall, the examination room, the
working office and the great hall were rebuilt together in 1816, most likely as one block, and the officials sent by
the shogunate worked in a room of the office beside the room of the officials hired from the district.

Takayama's office is a single-story timber hall roofed with wooden shingles, built with great formality to show the
office's rank. What a magistrate's own working room looked like inside, and how large it was, is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That the official study stood behind the dais, in the same hall: no account places such a room there; it is reckoned
  from where an office's paperwork had to be done.

Depiction: The plan draws the official study as a room of the office hall behind the dais, beside the day office, its
floor drawn in the hall's own color.
