About: The official study was the magistrate's own working room in the office hall, where the office's papers were read
and answered. At Takayama, the one intendant's office that survives, the entrance hall, examination room, working office
and great hall were rebuilt together in 1816, most likely as one block. The officials sent by the shogunate worked in a
room of the office, partitioned from the room of those hired from the district, and the documents sent to the shogunate
were drawn up in a room used only for writing and bound in the room beside it. An intendant governed with about ten
retainers of the shogunate and a few local hires.

Takayama's office was a single-story timber hall roofed with wooden shingles, built with great formality to show the
office's rank. What a magistrate's own working room looked like inside, and how large it was, is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That the official study stood behind the dais, in the same hall: no account places such a room there; it is reckoned
  from where an office's paperwork had to be done, since a private study in the residence would stand on the household's
  side of the line between office and home.

Depiction: The plan draws the official study as a room of the office hall behind the dais, beside the day office, its
floor drawn in the hall's own color.
