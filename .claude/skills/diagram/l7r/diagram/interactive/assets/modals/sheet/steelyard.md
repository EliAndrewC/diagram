About: The scale on Ubame's weighing floor where bales of charcoal are weighed before the tally is written: a wooden beam with a
large hook at one end for the bale and a weight hung on the other, slid along the beam until the two balance.

China's traditional market scale was the steelyard, and some were built for heavy loads: one wooden steelyard more than 200 years
old, from Shaoxing, is about 7 ft (2.2 m) long and weighs up to about 386 lb (175 kg). Japan used steelyards from the Edo period,
in sizes up to one for 16 kan, about 132 lb (60 kg). One account gives the steelyard food and everyday goods and the two-pan
balance valuables, and calls the steelyard easy to cheat with.

A straw bale of charcoal had no standard size, so the bales were most likely weighed when they were sold.

Guesses:
- The frame or post the steelyard hangs from: no account describes how a heavy steelyard was hung at a depot.

Depiction: The plan draws a 6 ft beam hung near its hook end, the hook over the bales and the weight at the far end.
