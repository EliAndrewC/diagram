About: An earthen bridge (dobashi, "earth bridge") was built of logs laid side by side with no gaps between them, with earth
thrown over them and trodden down until it filled the hollows and the surface was level. It was a different thing from a
plank bridge, not another name for one. Until the Edo period it was by far the commonest bridge over Japan's rivers, and the
small bridges over a waterway in a Hiroshige print were mostly built this way. What crossed a paddy ditch, earth over logs,
a single log or a plank, is not recorded.

A visitor crossed on a level band of trodden earth, carried over the water on its bed of logs.

How long or wide a village's earthen crossing ran, and how often one stood along a ditch, are not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a settlement's ditch crossings are all earth over logs, at even odds with a single log and a planked deck: no count
  of the forms over a farm ditch was found; for river bridges, the one proportion found would make this form far commoner
  than the planked deck.

Depiction: Every ditch crossing on the map takes this form, drawn as a deck of trodden earth between two dark edges, the
ends of the logs under it ticked along each side, with no seams, so it does not read as planked. Each is drawn about 4 ft
wide and 8 ft long so it reads as a single-file crossing and not a road bridge. Where a lane or a road crosses water, the
map draws a planked deck as wide as the way, whatever form the ditch crossings take; over real rivers, logs under earth
were the likelier.
