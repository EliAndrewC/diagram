About: The residence was the house where the magistrate's own household lived, inside the walled compound of the office
they ran, so that the office was also a home. Its rooms were grouped by use: a formal side where guests were received, and
a private side where the family lived. In a Chinese county office the home stood behind the office by regulation; at
Takayama, the one Japanese intendant's office that survives, the residence stood beside it.

Such a house was usually one block under one roof; halls joined by corridors belonged to the mansions of shoguns and
daimyo, and at the Katsura villa four halls went up one at a time over about half a century, each stepped back from the
last. In a great house the rooms ran from the reception at the front, through the master's own rooms, to the family's
beyond; a smaller house could set its formal room deepest in. The formal garden lay on the sunny south side, before the
formal rooms. A guest of rank crossed open ground inside the gate to the step of the formal entrance, the genkan.

A district magistrate's house built in 1794 at Matsushiro, for a samurai of 150 koku, had about 1,740 sq ft of floor on
grounds of about 0.8 acre; a thousand-koku samurai's house at Hagi ran about 1,470 to 2,630 sq ft, by two
measures that disagree. The restored residence at Takayama, of an intendant who governed a whole province, is about 6,400
sq ft.

Guesses:
- That the household's service - the servants' range, a well, the family privy - stood on the shady ground behind the
  house: only a storehouse is recorded there, so the rest is reasoned from the garden's place in the sunny south.

Depiction: The plan sets the residence in an inner court behind the office hall, with a gate between the two courts - the
Chinese county office's order, kept for every plan, though at Takayama the residence stood beside the office. It draws the
house either as one block under one roof or as two or three halls stepped back one behind the next, each joined to the
last by a corridor; how Katsura's halls were joined is not recorded. The reception stands at one end of the house, with
the garden before it on the sunny side where the buildings allow, and each labeled room - the master's, the family's, the
inner rooms - stands for a suite of several rooms.
