About: At the far end of the change, a pond district gave nearly all its ground to ponds. In the Pearl River delta in the 18th
century, fields were abandoned to build ponds, and at Longshan "outside the dwellings all was pond": the dike-ponds reached
30,000 mu, three-quarters of the cultivated land. At Longjiang and Longshan every pond was a mulberry pond.

Such a place was pond after pond, each ringed by its planted dike, the dikes running up to the houses and the creeks and
canals of the polder winding between them, with no paddy among them.

The highest shares on record are a township's: three-quarters of Longshan's cultivated land, and in 1678 eight tenths of the
land of Jiujiang, another pond township. Whether any village gave up its rice entirely is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That every parcel of the block is a pond: the highest shares recorded are three-quarters and eight tenths of a township's land, so a block with no rice at all goes beyond them.
- That a wholesale dike-pond hamlet keeps no rice about half the time: no source counts the hamlets of either kind.

Depiction: The map lays the whole block out as dike-ponds and converts every parcel to a pond, so no paddy is left among them.
