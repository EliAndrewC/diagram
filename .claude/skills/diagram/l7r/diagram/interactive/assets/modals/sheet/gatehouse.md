About: The gatehouse was the guardroom where the gatekeepers kept the main gate, the compound's door for everyone who
came on business; even a branch office of three officials had its gatekeepers' room by the main gate. The gatekeepers
sat in one of two places. At the Kashiwara domain's seat at Kaibara the guardroom, a room of eight tatami mats with an
earth floor beside it, is a room of the gate range (nagaya-mon), the long building the gate passes through; at Matsue a
samurai residence's gate range was also where its gatekeepers and servants lived. At Takayama the gate and the
gatekeepers' house were both built in 1832 and stand apart, each a gabled building of one story. Edo castle's gates had
guardrooms, and the Akamon in Edo has one on each side.

The Kaibara gate range, built in 1714, is about 81 by 12 ft, with rooms on both sides of the gate. A guardroom of its
own measured at the temple Kita-in is a small gabled building of about 18 by 12 ft, and Takayama's gatekeepers' house
has a floor of about 400 sq ft. Both kinds ran about 12 ft deep. How many gatekeepers kept a gate is not recorded.

Guesses:
- The length of a gatehouse of its own on the plan, from about 18 ft, the one measured, up to about 40 ft, the scale of a
  gate range: no account gives a magistracy's gatehouse.

Depiction: The plan draws the gatekeepers in one of the two places, a gatehouse beside the main gate or a guardroom in
the gate range, never across the gate's opening; each plan takes one. Both are drawn about 12 ft deep.

Name: gatehouse
Covers: the guard post beside the main gate, or the guardroom in the gate range, and its label
