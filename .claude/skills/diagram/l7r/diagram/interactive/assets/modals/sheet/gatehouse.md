About: The gatehouse was the guardroom where the gatekeepers kept the main gate and watched who came and went; even a
branch office of three officials had its gatekeepers' room by the main gate. At a samurai residence the gatekeepers lived
there too: at Matsue the gate range housed the gatekeepers and the household's menservants.

The gatekeepers sat in one of two places. One was a room of the gate range (nagaya-mon), the long building the gate
passes through, under the range's one unbroken roof: at the Kashiwara domain's seat at Kaibara the guardroom is a room of
eight tatami mats with an earth floor beside it, and the Matsue gate range had lookout windows onto the street. The other
was a gatehouse of its own: at Takayama the gate and the gatekeepers' house were both built in 1832 and stand apart, each
a gabled building of one story. Edo castle's gates had guardrooms, and the Akamon in Edo has one on each side. What their
walls were made of is not recorded.

The Kaibara gate range, built in 1714, is about 81 by 12 ft, with rooms on both sides of the gate. A guardroom of its
own measured at the temple Kita-in is a small gabled building of about 18 by 12 ft, and Takayama's gatekeepers' house
has a floor of about 400 sq ft. Both kinds ran about 12 ft deep. How many gatekeepers kept a gate is not recorded.

Guesses:
- The length of a gatehouse of its own on the plan, about 40 ft, the scale of a gate range: the one measured, a temple's,
  is about 18 ft, and no account gives a magistracy's gatehouse.

Depiction: The plan draws the gatekeepers in one of the two places, a gatehouse beside the main gate or a guardroom in
the gate range; each plan takes one. Both are drawn about 12 ft deep.
