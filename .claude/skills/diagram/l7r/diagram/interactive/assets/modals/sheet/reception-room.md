About: The zashiki was a samurai house's formal room, where the master received guests. It was built for formality, unlike the
master's own living room, and a guest was received inside the one house: a middle-rank house's guest side ran from its formal
entrance with its step, through an anteroom where a visitor was announced, to the zashiki. A traveler of rank lodged instead at
a post town's house for guests of rank.

In a lord's palace the reception rooms stood at the front, the omote, with the entrance and the great halls where the lord
received retainers and envoys and held audiences and ceremonies; the lord's own rooms lay behind them and the family's beyond. A
smaller house could run the other way, setting its zashiki deepest in, past the living rooms. Either way the formal rooms sat
at one end of the house. The formal garden lay on the sunny south side, before them and beyond the veranda, and the window of
the raised seat faced south for its light. A house's altar room could stand beside the zashiki's alcove, or in a small room
behind it.

How large a zashiki was is not recorded.

Depiction: The plan draws the reception as one labeled bay at the end of the residence nearest the approach, in the palace's
order, and never between the master's rooms and the family's. It faces the garden where the plan's buildings allow, and
otherwise the court or the service ground before its block.
