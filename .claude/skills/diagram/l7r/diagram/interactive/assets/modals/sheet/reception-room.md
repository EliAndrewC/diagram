About: The zashiki was a samurai house's formal room, where the master received guests. It was built for formality, unlike the
master's own living room, and a guest was received inside the one house: a middle-rank house's guest side ran from its formal
entrance with its step, through an anteroom where a visitor was announced, to the zashiki.

Like nearly every room of a samurai house, it was floored with tatami mats. A zashiki with a tokonoma, the alcove, was a mark
of a house's standing, as were its formal entrance and its step. On the garden side a wide veranda served as a secondary space
of the room, and the window of the raised seat faced south for its light.

In a lord's palace the reception rooms stood at the front, the omote, where the lord received retainers and envoys; the lord's
own rooms lay behind them and the family's beyond. A smaller house could run the other way, setting its zashiki deepest in,
past the living rooms. Either way the formal rooms sat at one end of the house, with the formal garden before them on the
sunny south side. A house's altar room could stand beside the zashiki's alcove, or in a small room behind it.

How large a zashiki was is not recorded.

Depiction: The plan draws the reception as one labeled bay at the end of the residence nearest the approach, in the palace's
order, and never between the master's rooms and the family's. It faces the garden where the plan's buildings allow, and
otherwise the court or the service ground before its block. Where one lineage holds the posting, the household's altar alcove
is drawn at this formal end, beside the zashiki or behind it.
