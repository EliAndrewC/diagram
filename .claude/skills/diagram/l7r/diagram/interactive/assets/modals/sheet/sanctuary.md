About: The sanctuary, the honden, was where a shrine's kami was kept: a small closed building at the back of the shrine,
behind the hall where the villagers prayed and on the same line. Nobody lived or gathered in it. A shrine often kept several
kami, one of them the main one, housed together in one sanctuary or each given a bay of its own; which was usual at a village
shrine is not recorded. In Rokugan the deity a village shrine houses is a Fortune.

The commonest form of sanctuary across Japan was the "flowing-roof" style, nagare-zukuri, most often one bay wide or three.
A small sanctuary was sometimes housed under a covering building, to keep off the wind and rain.

A village shrine's sanctuary was tiny. One built in 1789 at a Hachiman shrine in the Ehime highlands measures about 6 ft
square, a fifth of the width of its worship hall; another village shrine's, two bays by one, comes to about 12 by 6 ft.

Depiction: The plan draws the sanctuary about 6 ft square, one bay wide, the size of the village sanctuary that was
measured, behind the hall on the approach's line, and names the Fortune it serves beside it. That the approach runs on the
same line as the hall and the sanctuary is a drawing convention: a sanctuary stood behind its hall on the hall's line, but
where a village shrine's approach ran is not recorded.
