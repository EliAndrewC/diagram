About: A watari-roka was a corridor built between two buildings to join them. Such corridors grew up in the mansions of the
shoguns and the daimyo of the late 1500s and the Edo period, built as many halls joined one to another. Farmhouses,
townhouses and the houses of middle and lower-ranking samurai had almost none: such a house was one block under one roof.

A great house could also grow hall by hall. At the Katsura villa in Kyoto four halls stand stepped back one behind the next,
like geese in flight, and they went up in three phases over about half a century, from about 1615 to about 1662, so halls in
echelon are the form of a house its holders built onto.

How such a corridor was built, how wide or how long it ran, and how Katsura's own halls are joined, is not recorded.

Depiction: The plan draws the corridor joining each hall of a residence set in echelon to the hall before it, putting the great
houses' corridors together with Katsura's stepped halls; a residence drawn as one block has none. A residence drawn this way
reads as a house that has been added to.

Covers: the corridor between the residence blocks
