About: A watari-roka was a corridor built between two buildings to join them. Such corridors grew up in the mansions of the
shoguns and the daimyo of the late 1500s and the Edo period, built as many halls joined one to another. Farmhouses,
townhouses and the houses of middle and lower-ranking samurai had almost none: such a house was one block under one roof.

A great house could also grow hall by hall. At the Katsura villa four halls stand stepped back one behind the next,
like geese in flight, and they went up in three phases over about half a century, from about 1615 to about 1662, so there halls in
echelon were the form a house took as its holders built onto it.

How such a corridor was built, how wide or how long it ran, and how Katsura's own halls are joined, is not recorded.

Guesses:
- How wide the plan draws the corridor: no account of a watari-roka's width or build was found.
- That a short covered corridor joined the kitchen to the house, as some plans draw it: no account was found of a kitchen's
  own way to carry food to the rooms.

Depiction: The plan draws the corridor joining each hall of a residence set in echelon to the hall before it, putting the great
houses' corridors together with Katsura's stepped halls; a residence drawn as one block has none between its halls. A residence
drawn this way reads as a house that has been added to. Some plans also draw a short corridor from the kitchen to the house's
veranda.

Name: residence corridor
Covers: the covered corridors between a residence's blocks, and from its kitchen to the house
