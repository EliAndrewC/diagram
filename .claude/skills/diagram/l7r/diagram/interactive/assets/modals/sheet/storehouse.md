About: The household storehouse, the dozo or kura, kept a household's valuables and stores safe from fire and theft. A
samurai house might keep more than one: the Yokota house at Matsushiro, a 150-koku district magistrate's, kept two. At the
Higuchi house, also at Matsushiro, the storehouse stood behind the main house, beyond an open yard.

It was the one building made to resist fire. Its walls were earth, laid coat after coat on a bamboo lattice to 8 to 12 in
thick and finished in lime plaster, under a tile roof, and by the end of the Edo period its doorway could be shut by a pair
of plastered doors. In a fire, valuables that could not be carried out were put into the storehouses, and from 1720 Edo
permitted and then encouraged earthen-storehouse building against fire.

A household storehouse was small. The Yokota house's two measure about 9 by 12 ft and, of two stories, about 18 by 15 ft,
and the common size was 2 by 3 ken, about 12 by 18 ft. Takayama's kitchen storehouse, at an office that governed a whole
province, has about 740 sq ft of floor.
