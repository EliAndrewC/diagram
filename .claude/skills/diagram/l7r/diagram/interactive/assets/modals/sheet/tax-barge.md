About: The tax barge is a river barge moored at Hayakawa's dock with bales of tax grain aboard, taken on for the run
downriver. River landings were set up under the domain lords to carry tax rice and other goods to the great cities, and
the boatmen and the porters who loaded and unloaded the cargo lived and worked at them.

Japan's long, flat river boat, the takasebune, ran about 31 ft (9 m) long in a record of the Heian period and 73 ft (22 m) in one
of the Edo period, and the two-hundred-koku boats of the Tone River ran to some 89 ft (27 m). The shogunate's tax rice went by sea
on ships it hired directly, flying an official pennant and inspected at the ports of call.

The grain traveled in straw bales (tawara), which held no standard amount before modern times: anything from 2 to 5 to, a to
being a tenth of a koku, by time and place. Today's 132 lb (60 kg) rice bale is about 2.5 ft long and 1.5 ft across.

Guesses:
- The size of a bale before modern times: none was found measured; scaled from what the recorded bales held, one would be within
  a tenth of today's size.
- That a county's grain went downriver on boats hired for the run, as the shogunate's rice went by sea on hired ships: tax
  rice is known to have been carried to river landings to be shipped, but no account says whose boats took it on.

Depiction: The plan draws the barge about 47 by 7 ft, within the river boats' recorded range, lying along the bank and tied to
it. The bales aboard are drawn about 4 ft long so that they read; a real rice bale was about 2.5 ft long.
