About: A stone lantern, the ishidoro, began as a votive light: it came from China and Korea with Buddhism as a light offered
to shrines and temples, where at first a single lantern stood at the center of the front. Lanterns were gifts. In 1799 the
parishioners of Ishigami village, in Saitama, gave their Hikawa Shrine a pair, inscribed with the gift.

A stone lantern was built of six stacked stone parts: from the top, a jewel, a cap, the firebox that held the light, a
middle platform, a post and a base. It came in many named kinds, the Kasuga among them, and from the Momoyama period on
lanterns were set in gardens as well.

How many lanterns a village shrine had, and how tall they stood, are not recorded.

Guesses:
- That a village shrine has lanterns only where its parish is richer than average: no count says how many village shrines
  kept them.
