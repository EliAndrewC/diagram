About: A scattered village had no lanes among its houses, because it had no gathered houses to run them between. In a
scattered village, the sanson, each farmhouse stood apart among broad fields, on the Tonami plain in the middle of its own
rice fields so that the household could manage the water for them. A farmhouse there was most likely reached from the field
paths, a way running in to the hamlet from outside and nothing laid between the farmhouses.

Among the paddies the path ran on the bunds between the plots: a bund heaped up from the paddy's mud on the boundary between
one field and the next, which served also as the path round them, the azemichi, a narrow way between one field and another.
It was bare earth, trodden by feet and made for walkers, carrying poles and packhorses rather than carts.

How wide such a path ran is not recorded; the one old width given for a field road is about 3 ft.

Depiction: The map draws no lanes among the farms. The track in from outside joins the settlement to the wider world, and
each farm is joined to the rest by the paths along the field bunds; this is a value of the settlement's form, set out on the
title card.
