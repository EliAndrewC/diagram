About: The tax archive was the earth-walled storehouse (dozō) where an office kept its papers - for a county office,
its tax ledgers - in a building made to come through the fires that took wooden halls. Nobody lived or worked in it.
Office halls were timber, and they burned: the Sado magistracy burned and was rebuilt five times, though Takayama's
office never did.

Its walls were thick earth, plastered, often a foot thick or more, its doors faced with earth and plaster or made to be
shut in a fire, and most such storehouses were roofed with tile. In a fire, Edo's townspeople put what they could not
carry out into storehouses of this kind, and from 1720 Edo encouraged earthen building against fire.

The one office's records store that was measured, the book storehouse of the intendant's office at Takayama, which
governed the whole province of Hida, was built in 1841 and has a floor of about 450 sq ft; it survives. The same office's
kitchen storehouse is larger, at about 740 sq ft. A county office, governing less, probably kept a records store no
larger. How long and how deep any records storehouse ran is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That an office kept its papers in a storehouse rather than a room of its hall because of fire: no account says why.
- The proportions the plan draws: no account gives a records storehouse's length and depth.

Depiction: The plan draws the archive as a plastered storehouse, pale and solid. No fire-water tub stands at it: the tubs
stand at the wooden buildings, and the archive is the one kind of building made not to burn. It stands well below the
staff's rowhouse in size, as Takayama's small storehouses did.
