About: A polder was low ground walled in all round by a dike, so its water could not simply run in off a brook: it came through a gate in the dike from the water outside. In Japan's ring-diked polders, the waju, the intake was generally set at the upstream head of the ring and the outlet at its downstream tail, and a Chinese polder's gates were opened in drought to let the river in and shut against it in flood. In this form the water came in at the polder's high corner.

The intake was a culvert or gate through the dike, and few were built, because a gate strong enough to let river water in safely was very hard to make; after one blew out in the flood of 1722 the Fukutsuka waju set no other for a long time, and many of its paddies waited on the rain. Inside the dike the water ran in channels of its own.

Between 1789 and 1872 the villages of the Fukutsuka waju had four intake culverts against 34 culverts and a water gate for drainage, and the Chinese Wanchun polder, rebuilt from 1061, had five water gates.

Guesses:
- That a village's polder has one inlet and one outfall: the one count found is of a large waju, and two openings are reckoned enough for a small polder.

Depiction: The map draws the polder's inlet sluice high on the polder, cut through its dike, with the open water that feeds it just outside the dike beside it, and the outfall low on the polder; water crosses the dike nowhere else.
