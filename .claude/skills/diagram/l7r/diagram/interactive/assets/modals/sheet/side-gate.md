About: A walled compound could have lesser gates beside its main one: even a magistrate's
branch office of three officials, at the end of the shogunate, had a small gate beside its main gate. The formal entrance
was kept for the head of the household and honored guests, the family came and went by an inner entrance, and the
kitchen had its own door from outside, the katteguchi.

Night soil was a good with a price. Farmers bought a town household's night soil by the year and came with carts to
collect it, and a merchant house on a back street kept a back gate for them. A compound probably let its collectors in
by a service gate too, though no such gate is recorded at a samurai house.

In Rokugan wagons and carts travel the roads between towns, so a compound that ships or receives bulk goods, rice bales or
charcoal, keeps a gate a cart can pass. Edo Japan kept its carts to the towns and barred them from its highways to the
end of the shogunate. On a river a samurai residence stood back from the bank behind its wall, and it is thought none
had a gate opening straight onto a landing.

A guest's way was different: a guest of rank came through the gate across open ground, and the palanquin was brought
right alongside the entrance step. How wide a side gate was, and how it was built, are not recorded.

Guesses:
- A guests' door that opens into a garden rather than an open court, where a plan draws one: no account puts a garden
  between a gate and a formal entrance.
- That a side gate is a pair of posts, narrower than the main gate: no account gives a side gate's width or how it was
  built.

Depiction: The plan draws each side gate as a pair of posts in the compound wall, narrower than the main gate: a kitchen
postern, a service gate by the stables or the stores, a gate to a river landing (opening onto the bank street, with the
landing's steps across it), a cart gate, or a guests' door. Service
gates are set where the night-soil collectors reach the outer privies from outside without crossing the inner court where
the household lives.

Name: side gate
Covers: the posts of the posterns, service gates, landing gate, cart gate and guests' door
