About: The day office was the room of the office hall where the office's daily business was done - the villages'
suits and petitions received, orders written, accounts kept. At Takayama the officials sent by the shogunate worked in a
room of the office, with the room of the officials hired from the district partitioned off beside it, and the office's
entrance hall, examination room, working office and great hall were rebuilt together in 1816, most likely as one block.
Takayama kept two hearing courts: at one the villages' suits and petitions were received, as at an office's service
window today; at the other, those who had committed crimes were examined and judged.

Takayama also had an examination room (ginmisho), roofed like its court, though its torture is reported to have been
done in the jail in the town. In Rokugan torture to win a confession is forbidden by Imperial decree, so a magistracy
keeps no room built for interrogation, and questioning happens in the day office or the hearing court like any other
business.

Takayama's office is a single-story timber hall roofed with wooden shingles. What its working rooms looked like inside,
and how large they were, is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That the day office stood behind the dais, in the same hall: no account places such a room behind the court; it is
  reckoned from what a working office had to hold.

Depiction: The plan draws the day office as a room of the office hall behind the dais, between the clerks' room and the
official study, its floor drawn in the hall's own color.
