About: The stone facing of the riverbank at Hayakawa's landing, with the landing's steps cut into it, across the bank street from
the compound's wall. A river rises in its flood season every year, and a stepped landing was built on the slope of a faced bank
so that boats could be met and cargo handled at any level of the water.

The first stepped landings were of timber, which rotted standing in the water, so stone is thought to have come into use early
in the Edo period. Hiroshima's six rivers came to be lined with some 300 to 400 of them, public and private. Samurai residences
on those rivers stood back from the revetment behind their walls, which is why the compound's wall stands back behind the bank
street. How far a river rose in a year is not recorded.

Guesses:
- That this faced bank and its steps are the compound's own rather than a public landing: no account of an official's compound
  with a landing of its own was found.

Depiction: The plan draws the facing as a gray band along the water's edge, following the river's own line, with the landing's
steps notched into it.

Name: revetment
Covers: the stone facing along the landing's bank
