# Ways: roads, lanes, streets, bridges

*Part of the Mode B settlement docs. The index, the `meta()` knobs, the workflow and the validator contract live in [`../settlements.md`](../settlements.md).*

**Load this file when:** laying a road, lane, street, or a crossing over water.

---

- **Where a WAY crosses water, a BRIDGE carries it over** (`roads_bridge_water`, universal): a way does not simply run through open water. After laying ALL ways (road, **ring road**, streets, lanes) and ALL water (streams, irrigation channels, field ditches, the **cargo canal**, the city moat), call **`s.bridges()`** once - it scans for every place a carried path intersects a watercourse and drops an `s.bridge(...)` (a planked timber deck with side rails, drawn on the TOP layer so it sits above the water and the roadbed) at each crossing, oriented along the way. The classic case is a **walled city's approach road crossing the moat at each gate** (Tango gets two, N and S); a country road crossing a stream gets a timber bridge the same way. The check re-derives the crossings from the polylines and fires if any lacks a bridge within ~40px - so a watercourse added *after* the `s.bridges()` call (leaving a fresh unbridged crossing) is caught; put the call last. A way merely running *alongside* water (never intersecting) needs no bridge and is left alone. An **undrawn** channel (`topo_channel`'s `drawn: False`) is a buried conduit recorded for water topology only - there is no seam on the ground, so a way over its line crosses nothing and needs no deck (Tango's ring road runs over three).
- **SOLVE a crossing, never eyeball it** (`bridges_align_with_their_way`; GM 2026-07-27, Minami's cargo basin: *"the bridge by the cargo basin is NOT aligned with the road, which results in not only the bridge being crooked but the road just running over the water"*). A deck must lie **ON** its crossing (within 8px) and run **ALONG** the way it carries (within 8 deg, mod 180 - a plank has no forward direction); a deck with no way-over-water crossing under it at all fails the same rule. *Why the older check was not enough:* `roads_bridge_water` is satisfied by ANY deck within 40px of the crossing, so a deck sitting beside the crossing at the wrong angle passed it - and that is precisely what the reader sees as the road running through the water past a crooked plank. *Why it happened:* the ring road was not a carried way here and the cargo canal was not a watercourse, so `bridges()` could not see the ring-over-canal crossing at all, and both cities hand-placed that deck at design coordinates. Hand-placed coordinates then **drift**: when Minami's ring was re-derived from the budget its deck stayed put, ending 17px and 39 deg off (Nagahara's 15px / 24 deg). *Tolerances, from measurement:* every deck `s.bridges()` solves lands 0.0-1.0 px and 0.0-1.0 deg off its crossing (rounding only), so 8/8 separates solved decks from eyeballed ones by two orders of magnitude. Standalone `foot` planks are exempt - no way carries them and they cross their ditch perpendicular by construction. **Consequence for a gen: do not write bridge coordinates by hand.** Lay the water and the ways, then call `s.bridges()`; hand-place only a crossing the pass genuinely cannot see, and expect this check to test it.
  - Where a cargo canal enters through a **shuimen** the ring road necessarily crosses it a few paces inside the arch (the ring runs a fixed inset from the rampart), so the deck and the water gate share ground - the Suzhou pattern, where the wall road bridges each canal at its water gate. That adjacency is a stated mount in `_FIXTURE_MOUNTS["bridges"]`, not a drawing error: shortening the deck does not separate them.
- **Lanes stay OFF the dry crop plots** (`lanes_clear_of_dry_plots`, GM 2026-07-21, caught on Hikari no Sato's west field-spur): a trodden path never crosses row crops - historically it runs on the baulk BETWEEN plots or along the hem's edge. A lane may TOUCH a plot's boundary (paths hug field margins by design; only points >3px inside a plot fire). When a dry-plot staircase blocks the way to the paddy and no clear baulk exists, the correct answer is usually to STOP the field track at the hem's edge (past it, farmers walk the plot baulks) rather than force a route around - Hikari's detour around the staircase foot fought the homestead packing on every seed, while the hem-edge stop kept the known-clean packing untouched.
- **PLANK BRIDGES - two kinds.** (a) A **carried-way crossing** (`s.bridges()`; `roads_bridge_water`): where a road, town street, or village LANE crosses a watercourse (stream, channel, field-ditch, moat) it must be carried over on a railed timber deck oriented ALONG the way; `bridges()` auto-spans every such crossing, on the TOP layer, and the check fails on any unbridged one. (b) **Standalone ditch FOOTBRIDGES** (`s.channel_footbridges(spacing, min_len)`; `long_ditches_have_a_footbridge`, opt-in via `meta.field_footbridges`): the planks field-workers use to cross an irrigation ditch while walking the paddy BUNDS - **NOT tied to any lane** (people reach them along the bunds, so NO path leads to them). Any ditch stretch longer than `min_len` gets a plank about MIDWAY; a long stretch gets one roughly every `spacing` px; each crosses PERPENDICULAR to the ditch. **Deck size (1px=2ft): an *itabashi* footplank (板橋, ita + hashi, "plank bridge" - what the glyph actually draws, a planked timber deck with seams across it; this file and three engine comments called it a *dobashi* 土橋 until 2026-08-19, which is the wrong object: a dobashi is an EARTHEN deck, bundles of faggots or small logs over a timber frame surfaced with earth and gravel and edged with turf, so the name failed the Principle XI kanji/romaji/meaning triangle) is a small single-file crossing, so `plank_w=2.0` (~4ft wide) and the deck spans only the ditch width + a SHORT abutment (`span = w + PLANK_ABUTMENT`, +6px total - GM 2026-07-22: was 2.5 wide and +15, both too large; a footplank over an ~8ft ditch is short, not a long jetty).** It is kept both NARROWER and SHORTER than a cart lane (~5-6px): the wider `bridges()` carried-way deck matches the lane it carries (a road bridge is road-width), a footplank is not, so don't let it read as a road bridge. Because each ditch is a separate manifest entry, the northern trunk (several segments) gets several planks, each branch between two paddy strips gets one, the village-edge channel gets one (Hoshigaoka adds a second on its heavily-trodden northern stretch). **A footplank must reach USEFUL ground on BOTH banks** (`footbridges_reach_useful_ground`; GM 2026-07-22, Hikari no Sato): a standalone plank exists so field-workers can cross to the FIELD, so both banks must land on cultivated ground (wet paddy or dry crop), the village (a dwelling within `PLANK_VILLAGE_REACH`), or a walked dike - never reed marsh, scrub commons, or off-map. The placement slides each plank to a useful, house-clear spot and DROPS it if none is near, so a drain/toe stretch running along the field's OUTER edge (marsh or scrub on the far side) carries no plank; `long_ditches_have_a_footbridge` correspondingly EXEMPTS such a MARGIN ditch (`_ditch_plankable` - nothing to cross TO needs no crossing). *Why it matters:* the useless-plank defect was widespread, not a one-off - the water-first maps' drainage toes AND the diagonal edge-drains along every field's outer boundary all carried planks stepping straight into the swamp or scrub; the check + placement rule cleared them pool-wide (Hikari 13->11, honda/kikuta/akagahara/ikegami/moritono/ueda each lost their toe/edge planks) while keeping every field-to-field, dry-to-wet, and village-to-field crossing. Placement and the check read the SAME cultivation source (the manifest `fields` outlines + `dry_plots`, NOT the blocking-only `field_polys`) so they can never disagree. **A lane must NOT run through the flooded paddy** - the village-to-field spur STOPS at the paddy edge; people cross into the fields on foot along the bunds. Call `channel_footbridges()` AFTER the ditches are placed. **A plank must never sit ON a farmhouse** (`bridges_clear_of_houses`): a bridge is overlap-exempt in general (it deliberately sits on the water it spans) but a rotated-rect SAT of its deck against every house must be clear - so `channel_footbridges` SLIDES each plank along its own ditch (a few arc-length offsets each side of the midpoint) until its deck clears every house, keeping it ON the channel while dodging a home near the village edge.
- **A deck LANDS past its banks - every corner on dry ground** (`bridges_span_their_water`, tightened GM 2026-08-09 from ends-reach-the-edge). A real bridge does not stop at the water's edge: the girder bears on an **abutment sill set BACK from the channel edge** - scour (the current undercutting the bank) must never reach the bearing, and the seat itself needs bearing length - so a modest timber bridge lands **~5-15 real ft of deck past the water on each side**; `s.bridges()` draws `LANDING_FT` (10 ft, mid-band) per side, converted by `ftpx`. It also **solves the OBLIQUE crossing exactly**: along the deck the water is `w/sin` wide and the deck's own width adds `deck_w*|cos|/sin` before a corner clears the bank - the old flat `+28px` slack was eaten by obliquity, which is how the capital's east deck over the diagonal river landed **0.0 ft** at its worst corner (the GM's catch). The check is a gap VERDICT on the deck's **four real corners**: >= 6 ft of landing for a carried deck (floored under the drawn 10 so local water curvature cannot flap the gate), >= 2 ft for a `foot` plank - an itabashi's deliberately short `PLANK_ABUTMENT` stands (GM 2026-07-22; its ends simply rest on the bank, and 10 ft rests would turn a footplank back into the jetty it was already cured of).
- **Village lanes and connecting paths - UNPAVED (or narrow-paved) NARROW single track (researched + source-verified 2026-07; CHINA-first per the core principle, Japan corroborating).** **Re-read 2026-08-27 (feature 133 T50, the GM: "I just want to make sure that we did not set it arbitrarily"): the FORM is READ, the WIDTHS are drawing conventions.** A `source-reader` pass (23 fetches) read: the Edo hand-cart (daihachiguruma) was by law confined to Edo, Owari and Sunpu (ja.wikipedia "大八車"); a purpose-built Edo ox-cart stone route was 2.7 m / 9 shaku wide with a 90 cm track for the beast (Wikipedia "Stone routes") - a CEILING for wheeled ways; the kaidō trunk roads ~9 m, 4-7 m in the mountains, 2 ken at Hakone (MLIT road history); and China's rural network was "narrow paths, scarcely wide enough for foot passengers and wheelbarrows", cart-tracks only on the eastern plains (Low-Tech Magazine on the Chinese wheelbarrow, citation-backed). NOT FOUND, in any language: a numeric width for an ordinary village lane or farm path - the "里道 = 1 ken" figure a search summary offered traces to a page that does not say it, and the 里道 article and Osaka's own FAQ give no number. So the GM's two-carts-passing picture is not what the record shows for a village lane (carts were the exception, and even the cart road gave one beast one track); the drawn widths - 3 ft footpaths, 5 ft spine, 6 ft connector at 1 px = 1 ft - are DRAWING conventions inside the read bounds (a footpath to a wheelbarrow's width; the connector under the 9 ft cart road), neither corroborated nor contradicted by a measured lane. Labeled so; a measured survey of a surviving hamlet lane would settle it and is on the SOURCES.md queue. The conclusion is the SAME in both cultures - a village lane is a **NARROW single track, not a wide 2-lane road** - so the "dashed-centerline 2-lane" reading was wrong either way. The reasons, China leading:
  - **CHINA (the guiding star):** the Chinese deliberately built a **network of NARROW PATHS for the WHEELBARROW** (the characteristic Chinese one-wheel vehicle, which carries 3-6x a European barrow and needs only a footpath), plus **carrying-pole porters, packhorses, and litters** - NOT wide cart roads. Two-wheeled **cart-tracks predominated only in the flat NORTHERN/eastern plains**, which is NOT our rice-south analog. So the vehicle to picture is the **wheelbarrow and the shoulder-pole porter**, and the track is one-wheelbarrow / one-packhorse wide. *Paving nuance (a real China/Japan difference):* China had MANY narrow **stone-slab / flagged** paths, especially in the wet rice south (a paved causeway keeps porters and barrows above the mud) - MORE paving than Japan, but still **narrow foot/wheelbarrow paths, never wide cart roads**. A modest village's internal lanes are earth; an important inter-village or pilgrimage path may be a narrow stone causeway (an available upgrade, `worn=True` earth is the humble default).
  - **WATER as the freight highway - true for NATURAL water, but ARTIFICIAL canals are a Rokugan EXCEPTION (GM canon).** In Ming China bulk goods (above all tribute rice) moved by **canal and river boat**, and the man-made Grand Canal + dense artificial canal net were central. **Rokugan deliberately DIVERGES here:** artificial transport canals are NOT Empire-wide - they are a **LION-lands feature** (the Lion deliberately embrace the Chinese precedent), while the other clans rely on NATURAL water or land and simply do not need canals: **Crane** lands are coastal (never far from the ocean), **Dragon** has the Drowned Merchant River, **Unicorn** has the Firefly River valley and is intensely horse-focused, **Scorpion** lands are smaller, etc. So the RULE for a Rokugani village: where there is **navigable NATURAL water** (a river, the coast), that water is the main freight route and the village gets a **boat landing / jetty** as its primary link; but do NOT default to an artificial **transport canal** - add one only for a **Lion-lands** map or when the GM says so. This is a case where explicit GM setting canon **overrides** the China-first historical default. Make the transport canal + boat landing a **tunable, opt-in feature**, not the rule. (Hoshigaoka: irrigation pond + a minor drain, NOT navigable and NOT canal-served, so its land track legitimately IS the main connector - no canal here.)
  - **JAPAN (corroborating):** wheeled carts were near-absent rurally - ~70% mountains, horses too small to pull carts, carts even prohibited in places to prevent rutting and opposed by the packhorse/porter guilds; peasants traveled on foot, goods by packhorse/porter, heavy freight by boat. Paving was highways-only (even the Gokaido was stone-paved only on steep passes). Same bottom line: narrow, unpaved, single-track.
  - *Render:* `s.lane(pts, width=5, clearance=18, worn=True)` = a narrow packed-earth tread + soft worn shoulder, **NO center marking**. Widths at 1px=2ft: a village lane ~5px (~10 ft), the out-track ~6px - clearly narrower than any constructed road. **`clearance` must be wide enough that a house CORNER clears the tread** - a plain house's half-diagonal is ~14px (a well-off one ~15), so `clearance=18` keeps houses fronting the lane rather than sitting on it. `houses_clear_of_lanes` gates this (fires if any house footprint corner falls within a lane's tread half-width): a lane is a no-build corridor, houses FRONT it, so in a HAND-AUTHORED gen **lay lanes before the packs** and give the corridor real width (the scripted hamlet tier inverted this in feature 126: there the internal lanes are derived from the placed houses and only the connector and field spur come first) (11 was too tight - house corners overlapped the tread).
  - *The connecting path to the wider world IS marked, RUNS OFF THE EDGE, and the main lane does not dead-end.* A village connects to the nearest district / Imperial road (or, in the rice south, a **canal landing**) by a **trodden dirt track** worn by feet, wheelbarrows, packhorses, and porters - NOT a constructed road. Mark it `s.lane(..., connector=True)`: the village's main lane **continues OUT** of the cluster as this track and **runs OFF the map edge** (UNLABELED - a connector is an ordinary track, and only Imperial roads get labeled) - it must NOT stop mid-landscape (that reads as a path to nowhere). **AND IT KEEPS OFF THE WET GROUND** (GM 2026-08-12: "there's supposed to be a rule that paths don't pass through marshland"). None of these maps draws a causeway, so a track does not cross the reed toe - but it does NOT have to leave sideways either: the toe is only as wide as the ground the fan WATERS (researched; [`../research/water.md#the-wet-toe-is-as-wide-as-the-fan-not-as-wide-as-the-valley`](../research/water.md#the-wet-toe-is-as-wide-as-the-fan-not-as-wide-as-the-valley)), so there is dry footslope at BOTH lateral ends of it. A connector leaves DOWNSLOPE, past the toe's end, on the flank the cluster already sits on; it turns along the contour only where the fan itself reaches the frame and leaves no dry ground to pass by. (This paragraph briefly said the opposite, while the toe was still being drawn canvas-wide - if you find a map whose track turns away over the settlement's back, that is why, and it can probably be routed downslope now.); `roads_clear_of_marsh` and `fields_clear_of_road` both read every drawn way now (road, street, lane, alley), not just the road. `connector_lane_runs_off_edge` gates the off-edge part: a lane flagged `connector` must have an endpoint at/off the map boundary. Internal lanes (the spine, the field spur) are exempt - they legitimately end in the cluster or at the paddy. The field-ward spur (village to paddy) and the road-ward connector are the two ends of the same through-route.
  - *Rokugan nuance (setting, not history).* The Imperial TRUNK network is unusually well-kept - `government.md`: ~50,000 mi of Imperial roads, ~5,000 staffed waystations, maintained by **corvee** (peasants owe ~10-20 days/yr, "most often called for road repair after the spring thaws and autumn storms"), and Hantei X outlawed road tolls (see [[project_rokugan_unusually_well_run]]). But that is the trunk network; a village's own lanes and its local connector are still humble narrow tracks. An actual Imperial road, if shown, may read as a maintained (earth/gravel, wider) road distinct from the village track.
  - *Legacy:* `worn=False` (the old wide dashed lane) is kept ONLY so the not-yet-rebuilt dispersed pool maps (Hikari, Kikuta) render unchanged; new/rebuilt maps use `worn=True`. `M["lanes"]` records each lane (classified LINEAR for overlap - structs avoid it).
- **A LANE WEB IS ONE NETWORK IN INK - a junction is where two treads MEET** (`lanes_form_one_network`; GM 2026-08-27, feature 133 T31, Inashiro: *"random scattered lanes strewn about without much rhyme or reason ... a short section of lane, between three farmhouses. It does not really connect to anything on either end"*). Researched and ACCURATE for a nucleated cluster: the record's "interconnected system of narrow lanes and alleys" ([`../research/homesteads.md`](../research/homesteads.md) "Is every farmhouse reached by a lane", revisited under T31) - and the GM's other expectation, a track to the hamlet and nothing between the houses, is the DISPERSED form, a `settlement_form` knob value, not a correction. What had gone wrong was drawing, not form: every web pass (`_join_orphan_ways`, the stub trimmer, the service trim, `lanes_reach_something`) counted an end within `_LANE_JOIN_FT` (30 ft) of another way as joined, so a map passed every gate with nine lanes in six components. Two mechanisms, both measured on Inashiro: the fabric margin (`WEB_FABRIC_GAP`, 7 ft) clips a lane where it passes a garden fence, which beside a plot is exactly where the junction is - every refused link was 29 ft long and 6-15 ft from a bed; and `_trim_to_service` shortened the orphan-joiner's own links. Now (`hamletgen/ways.py` `_touch_junctions`, the LAST web pass): every non-connector end within 30 ft of another way is extended to touch it, tested at footprint margins (`_clear_touch`, `_TOUCH_GAP` 1 ft: a link may run along a fence, never across a house, a bed, a yard or water); a component still stranded is linked back by a routed link within `_ORPHAN_REACH` (150 ft) or LEFT - a first cut deleted it, which stranded a farmhouse, tripped the driver's re-roll and shipped a different map (attempt 4, connector reversed) with nothing saying why. The check reads the ink's own record with a 4 ft tolerance (two rounded treads), never 30. Inashiro after: 10 lanes, 1 component, every house within 100 ft of a way, the GM's attempt-1 layout kept. Labels: the web ACCURATE; the 7 ft margin, the 30 ft reach and the 1 ft touch gap are DRAWING conventions, not findings.
- **A LANE BENDS THE WAY FEET WEAR A PATH - no hairpin, no zigzag, one node per junction** (`lanes_bend_like_paths`; GM 2026-08-27, feature 133 T32: *"a loop-de-loop, which isn't how a lane would look ... zig-zags just below the loop de loop for no apparent reason"*). Researched ([`../research/homesteads.md`](../research/homesteads.md) "How does a village lane bend?"): a footpath is the line feet wear, walkers minimize the number and severity of turns, and a village lane bends at a plot corner and runs straight between - so a switchback within a few paces is a path nobody walks and nothing wears. The defect was an ABSENCE: the web is assembled from fragments (clear runs, links, touches, trims) and no pass read the result as a shape. `hamletgen/ways.py` `_smooth_web` is now the LAST pass of `stage_web`: string-pull each lane (at the web's margins, or at footprint margins for a chord that only removes jogs within `_JOG_FT` 6 ft - a first cut took every footprint-legal chord and ran a lane through a house's corridor); cut a hairpin arm (turn past `_HAIRPIN_DEG` 140, arm under `_ARM_FT` 40 ft); collapse ends within `_KNOT_FT` 25 ft onto one node (the end already standing on a through lane, else the centroid); cut a tail that runs on past a crossing for under 40 ft. And `_touch_junctions` makes each junction ONCE - linking a free end to a way the lane already met drew a 180-degree retrace - and joins END to END by running the other lane's end onto this one rather than by a link (an 8 ft tread-to-tread link read as a loop). The gate holds it: a turn >= 140 deg, or two turns >= 50 deg within 40 ft of path, on any non-connector lane. Inashiro after: 9 lanes, sharpest turn 94 deg, no lane longer than 1.9x its chord. Labels: the principle ACCURATE; every threshold a DRAWING convention.
