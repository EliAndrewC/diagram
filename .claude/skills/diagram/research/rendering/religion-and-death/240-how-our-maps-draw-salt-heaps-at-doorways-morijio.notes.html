• kotobank-morijio - 「In customer trades such as restaurants, assignation houses and variety halls, heaping salt small at the doorway to celebrate good fortune.」 (translated; ); its earliest cited use: 「a ninjōbon, Shunshoku Magaki no Ume (about 1838-40), book 5」 (translated; ) (the Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry)
  • no publicly readable source The pages found were present-day explainers of the custom, none citing a period record of its shape. The encyclopedia article gives no date for the shape and dates the custom to the Nara and Heian periods without a source. Neither the late-Edo encyclopedia of the customs of Edo, Kyoto and Osaka nor the Edo essay collection on customs of 1830 was found in a readable text.
  • no publicly readable source The dictionaries, the encyclopedia article and a ritual-goods seller's page place it at shops, restaurants, assignation houses, variety halls and present-day homes, and none at a samurai residence, a magistrate's office or a government building.
