How our maps draw the country monk's dwelling and writing room

On our maps a village shrine is kept by a country monk who lives there. By default the monk's dwelling is one end of the worship hall's own building, under the same roof; the district's registers are kept in a writing room in that dwelling, and no office building is drawn. The shrine's land and its income are not drawn at all.

  • Our maps have the country monk live at the shrine.
    The setting's fusion of the Fortunes and Shinsei is read as Japan's fusion of the kami and the Buddhas before 1868, when many shrines had a small temple attached whose monk lived there and performed the shrine's rites. So the form is accurate, and no knob is needed for where the monk lives. How common it was in the villages is uncounted, and the received view in one region puts shrines without monks as the rare case.
  • The game's published setting puts caretakers at its shrines and a resident monk at most temples.
    As its fan wiki summarizes the rulebook, "There was no village without some manner of shrine", and "Priests were the shrine's administrators and primary caretakers". Of temples, the smallest "relied on visiting monks, priests, shugenja, or even local volunteers", "but most had at least one resident monk".
  • Our maps do not draw the two other forms history had.
    A Shinto village shrine with no one living at it, kept by the parishioners in turn, and a rural temple with no resident, served by a priest who comes from far away, are both left undrawn.
  • By default the dwelling shares the worship hall's roof, at one end of the building.
    A temple's main hall and dwelling under one roof is attested once, and called unusual there, so the form is accurate but rare. The building's size is at How our maps draw a village shrine's precinct: halls, basin, sacred tree and offerings (keidai).
  • In the two-building form, the dwelling is a farmhouse-sized house beside the hall.
    It is drawn at the map's own farmhouse footprint, 46 by 28 ft. An ordinary temple's kuri was much like an ordinary house, and one measured kuri resembles its region's farmhouse, so the form is accurate; no small kuri has been measured, so the size is a guess.
  • The district's registers are kept in the monk's dwelling, never in an office building.
    A temple certified its parishioners under the Edo registration, and a small shrine often had no office of its own: "business was frequently carried on at the private home of the administrator". So the registers are kept in a writing room or a record chest in the dwelling (accurate). The writing room itself, its size and its place along the dwelling's front are a guess.
  • Nothing about the shrine's land or its income is drawn.
    The precinct's size is at How our maps site and size a village shrine.
