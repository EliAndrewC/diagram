How our maps draw a village temple

Our maps draw no village temple. A village draws its shrine and no separate temple, and a hamlet draws neither and is served by its village's shrine. This follows the setting our maps are drawn for, in which each village district, a village and the hamlets round it, has one country monk, who serves in the village's shrine and keeps the district's records of births and deaths.

  • Leaving the temple out is a deliberate deviation from the history.
    A real village might keep no temple of its own and use its neighbors', keep one, or keep several beside its shrine. The setting gives every village the one hall.
  • The village's shrine is its parish temple.
    In the setting the shrine does the work a village temple did, the registers of the dead included. Whether a graveyard stands beside it is at Does a graveyard stand beside its temple or its shrine?
  • The setting's arrangement is two of the historical ones at once.
    A village has its own hall, as a village with a temple of its own did, and its hamlets are served from the village's hall, as a village without a temple was served by its neighbor's.
  • A village temple's halls are the measure for the country shrine's hall and the monk's dwelling.
    They do the temple's work, so they are sized against it. A village temple's main hall of about 24 to 36 ft a side, with a kuri of about 12 by 15 ft, is of the order of a hall and a dwelling under one roof some 66 ft long.
