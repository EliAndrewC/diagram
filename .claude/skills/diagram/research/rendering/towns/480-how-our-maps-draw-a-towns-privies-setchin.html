How our maps draw a town's privies (setchin)

The only privy a town map draws is the back tenements' shared one, a small shed of its own at the far end of their lane. A street-front house's privy is inside the house, and nothing on the map marks the carrying-off of the night soil.

The rule the map follows:
  • A block of back tenements draws one shared privy shed, or two in a long block, at the far end of its lane beside the shared well.
    A back tenement had no privy for each unit but one or two shared ones within the block, and in Edo the shared well and privy stood at the far end of the lane.
  • How big is the shed?
    About 6 by 9 ft, one ken by a ken and a half. That is a GUESS: no page we read measures one.
  • A street-front house draws no privy outside its own footprint.
    Its privy stood at the back, where the through-passage reached the back yard.
  • The map draws nothing for the collection.
    The night soil went to the farms as fertilizer, and no page we read says where a night-soil yard stood.
  • The setting's owaiya change nothing on the map.
    The setting gives the night soil to the owaiya, a burakumin trade, but does not say whose privies they empty in a town; whoever empties them, the privies and the lanes are drawn the same.
