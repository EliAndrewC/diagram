How our maps draw laborers' housing (uradana)

Our maps draw two kinds of laborer's house: an ordinary one, 34 by 24 ft, which stands for a whole row of back-tenement homes, and a master laborer's, 50 by 34 ft. In a city both stand behind the shops on the street, on the lanes of the back lots. In a town, the share of laborers who live in back tenements rather than on plots of their own is chosen at random for each town.

  • An ordinary laborer's house stands for a whole tenement row, not one family's home.
    That is a drawing convention. It is drawn 34 by 24 ft, about 816 sq ft, and one back-tenement unit was about 9 by 12 ft, some 108 sq ft, so the drawn house is the size of a row of seven or eight units.
  • In a city, our maps put laborers in back tenements on the lanes behind the street-front shops.
    A back tenement was a row of one-story units, built one row deep or two to the width of the shop in front.
  • In a town, the share of laborers who rent is a knob, chosen at random for each town.
    The share of urban residents who rented ran from 20 to 30% in Japan's north-east and on the Japan Sea coast to 50 to 60% around the Inland Sea. In a town at the low end most laborers have a small house on a plot of their own, and a back tenement appears only behind the larger shops. In a town at the high end the back tenement is the laborer's usual home, as in a city.
  • How many of a town's laborers rented?
    Our maps take a town's share of renters as the share of its laborers who rent, and give the rest plots of their own. The shares are of all urban residents, not of laborers alone, so that is this project's calibration, not a finding.
  • The larger laborer's house is a master laborer's.
    The setting has a small band of rich master laborers among its laborers, one in twelve in a town and one in eight in a city.
  • Our maps make a master laborer a labor boss who finds laborers work and houses some of them.
    That follows the early modern placement broker (hitoyado), who found hired hands their places and lodged them until they were placed.
  • A master laborer's house stands among the laborers' housing, not on the shop street.
    It takes a lot on a back street, among the tenements. Where those lots front is set out at Where do the shops stand, and where does the poor housing go?.
  • Our maps draw a master laborer's house 50 by 34 ft, a GUESS.
    No page we read gives the size of the house of any of the master laborer's nearest historical counterparts: a placement broker, a tenement's house manager or a post town's porters' office.

The tenements, tenant shares and master laborers above come from Japanese towns. For a town on the Chinese model there is nothing to go on.

  • Our maps house the laborers of a town on the Chinese model by the same rules as a Japanese town's, a GUESS.
    No page we read says where a Chinese county seat's laborers lived.
