How our maps draw gardens behind a town house (tsubo-niwa)

On our maps a town's garden follows the house, not a roll per town: a merchant's house keeps a small open court inside its footprint and a planted garden behind, a farming household keeps vegetable beds, and a tenement row keeps none.

The rule the map follows:
  • A merchant's town house shows a small open court inside its footprint, near the back.
    It is about 6 by 6 to 6 by 12 ft, the one to two tsubo of a Kyoto town house's court.
  • The plot behind a merchant's house is drawn as a planted garden, not vegetable beds.
    Shrubs and a tree or two, as in the back garden many Kyoto town houses kept.
  • Both are carried over from Kyoto, a GUESS for a provincial town.
    No page we read says how common the court garden was in a provincial market town.
  • A farming household's plot stays a vegetable garden, and a tenement row draws none.
    A farming household's lot often ended in its vegetable garden (How a town is zoned: shops on the street, housing behind), and a back-tenement unit was a single room on a shared lane (Laborers' housing: back-lot tenements and the master laborer's house (uradana)).
