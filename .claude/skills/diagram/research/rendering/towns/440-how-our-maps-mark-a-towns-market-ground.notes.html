  • kotobank-sansaiichi - 「Markets were held mainly before temple and shrine gates, at hubs of land and water transport, and near a manor's office or the steward's residence.」 (translated; ) (the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry, on the medieval three-day market)
  • miaohui-zhwiki - 「The temple fair, also called the temple market or the festival ground, is a gathering near a temple for offerings to the gods, contests, performances and the like」 (translated; ) ... 「while keeping its rites, the temple fair gradually took in market trading」 (translated; ) ... 「It was first held at the celebrations of the various religious festivals (chiefly Buddhist and Daoist), and later developed into certain fixed dates」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-ichigami - 「It is often enshrined at places that mark a boundary: a village boundary, a landing, a bridge foot, a crossroads. From where it stands, it is inferred that a market was once held there.」 (translated; ) (the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry, by Kamata Hisako)
  • ichigami-jawiki - 「Most market sites were on old highways, and the market god was worshipped at the roadside」 (translated; ) ... 「It may be enshrined at the boundary of the market or the market ward, or at the boundary between the market settlement and its neighboring settlements」 (translated; ) ... 「The god's body is most often a round natural stone」 (translated; )

  • lifang-zhwiki - 「The Song abolished the Tang's ward-and-market system and the night curfew, producing commercial streets, entertainment quarters and night markets.」 (translated; )
  • otabisho-jawiki - 「The otabisho is the place where, in a shrine's festival (the procession of the god), the god (generally the portable shrine carrying the god's body) rests or stays the night partway along its procession, or the destination of the procession.」 (translated; ) ... 「Besides those with a shrine building, such as the original shrine, a subsidiary shrine or a shrine to the god's consort, a temporary altar may be set up at the god's former seat and the like, or the god may be received at the house of the representative of the ujiko (the tonin).」 (translated; )

  • tokaido-qa-mlit - 「For this reason each post station was placed under an obligation to keep permanently on hand porters and horses for carrying the baggage.   • sukego-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, the villages in the neighborhood of a highway post station that supplied men and horses supplementarily when the station's permanently kept men and horses alone were insufficient for the relay; or the levy or system itself.」 and 「In 1694 (Genroku 7) the shogunate newly laid down the sukego system, revising the former sukego, which had been bounded by domain borders and by province and district, and designating the villages in the neighborhood of each post station as attached sukego, and setting the men-and-horses duty at 2 men and 2 horses per 100 koku of assessed yield.」 (translated; ) and, from another work on the same page, 「At first the extent of the sukego villages was 2 to 3 ri around the station, but it was gradually widened to more than 10 ri, and when the supply of men and horses was impossible it was commuted into money, becoming a kind of tax.」 (translated; ) (the station that could not absorb what arrived widened its catchment; it did not enlarge its yard.)
