• kotobank-omotedana - 「Most are land-renters or house-renters, but it is a term symbolizing a stratum one rank above the common folk of the uradana, corresponding roughly to the wholesaler and broker classes.」 (translated; ); on where each stands, 「On the front part of each lot, along the main street, about five ken deep, either the landowner opened a shop or - under what was called jigari - a comparatively wealthy merchant rented the land from the landowner and built a shop with his own funds.」 (translated; ) and 「the back part, entered by the alleys between the omotedana」 (translated; ) (frontage held by the wholesaler and broker class, the back lots by the common folk a rank below; the sorting attested is by wealth)

  • kyoto-machiya-isan - 「The Kyo-machiya, founded on the coexistence of work and dwelling.」 (translated; ); on which part is which, 「A long narrow earth-floored passage running from the entrance to the back, which draws light and air into the house. The front part facing the street, which serves for receiving customers and as a workspace, is called the 'shop yard'; the cooking place where the range stands is called the 'running yard'.」 (translated; ); and one surviving house, 「The Nagae house, wholesale drapers for generations, has main buildings to the north and to the south, each with a low second story - a large Kyo-machiya of the work-and-dwelling-in-one type, made up of shop and residence.」 (translated; )
  • choninchi-jawiki - 「Among the chonin there was a class gulf running from the danna-shu, the bourgeois class of house-owners who kept a shop on the main street, down to the lower chonin of the back-shop stratum on the back streets.」 (translated; )
  • eta-jawiki - 「Where they lived was in many cases at the edge of the village, beside a river, or some other place unsuited to agriculture.」 (translated; ) (the outcast settlement at a village's edge, on ground unsuited to farming; the outcast quarter in a town's plan is on no page read)
  • kotobank-machiya - 「A machiya usually runs the ridge of its main house parallel to the street (hirairi), and many are of the form with the roof of the main house and its pent eave (hisashi) projected in two tiers and a low second story used for storage, but there are also tsumairi types, with the ridge run at right angles to the street and a triangular gable showing on the street side.」 (translated; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry)
  • hirairi-tsumairi-jawiki - 「Hirairi and tsumairi are names classifying a building by which of its faces holds the main front entrance. The face parallel to the main ridge of the roof is called the hira, the hira side, and the face perpendicular to it the tsuma」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「The lot division on both sides of the highway was of planned strip shape, the depth kept nearly constant, with differences in the frontage. Apart from the stratum of station officials, it was usual to combine farming, and the back side of the lot was in many cases a vegetable garden.」 (translated; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, the section by Tamai Tetsuo)
