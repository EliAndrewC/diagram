• kotobank-machikaisho - 「In Sakai, Imai, Hiranogo and Kaizuka, which developed urban self-government at the end of the middle ages, and in Kyoto, and in Osaka, which flourished greatly in the early modern period as an economic city, each separate ward within each city also had a machi-kaisho, and above them a general office for the whole, the sokaisho, was set up for each group, for each street, or for the town as a whole.」 (translated; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry)
  • kotobank-machikaisho - 「Among castle towns there are some, like Utsunomiya, where a kaisho was set up in each ward, but they are exceptional; on the contrary, there are places where the town officials were posted at the town magistrate's office and did their work there, and no machi-kaisho was set up.」 (translated; ) and 「Among post stations too there are cases that set up a machi-kaisho, such as Sukagawa on the Oshu-Matsumae road and Yokkaichi on the Tokaido, but they are unusual examples.」 (translated; )
  • kinsei-kaisho-jawiki - 「in Edo the official residence of the ward headman doubled as the machi-kaisho」 (translated; )
  • machidoshiyori-jawiki - 「The town elders' lots were granted by the shogunate; Naraya, Taruya and Kitamura each had a corner lot facing Honcho street, in Honcho 1-chome, 2-chome and 3-chome. As with samurai, the residence doubled as the office; it was called the town elders' office」 (translated; )
  • huiguan-zhwiki - 「The huiguan, also called the gongsuo, is an industrial and commercial organization dating from China's Ming and Qing」 (translated; ) ... 「The earliest huiguan now known is the Wuhu Huiguan in Beijing, built in the Yongle reign of the Ming. Huiguan were set up at first only to provide fellowship, entertainment and lodging for people from the same home region; later some developed the function of uniting the merchants of one trade」 (translated; )
