How our maps draw a channel narrowing along its run

Every stroke on our maps narrows along its run as a real channel does: as a square root, not a straight line, so a ditch keeps its working width while it still has most of its water. At true size the narrowing is too gentle for the eye to follow along a stroke, and the maps accept that rather than enlarge the net.

  • The width squared runs evenly along the stroke.
    That is the square-root law put to use, and one shared rule gives the local width everywhere the maps need it - the drawn stroke and the room the bunds keep from its bank alike - so no second formula can lay a bund inside the water.   • A drain widens the same way in reverse.
    From 3.0 to 12.0 ft it is 8.75 rather than 7.50 at mid-run, so a drain gains body in its first stretch. The law barely moves a shallow taper, which is right: it bites only where a stroke really sheds most of what it carries.
  • Why not a straight line?
    Narrowing the width evenly quietly says the water goes as the width, a law nothing in the hierarchy obeys. It also looks wrong: the stroke thins imperceptibly along its whole length and then stops dead at a still-substantial width. Under the square root the ditch keeps its working width while it has most of its water, then dwindles hard.
  • Can a reader see a ditch narrow at true size?
    Hardly: what the eye follows is the rate of narrowing, and at true size it is tiny. A delivery ditch sheds one foot of width over five hundred, and the high-margin canal three over fifteen hundred. The two ends of a canal differ plainly side by side, but only the short far-margin canal visibly narrows along its length. A real ditch narrowing from about 1.4 m (~5 ft) to 0.45 m (~1 ft) over 460 m (~1,509 ft) is no more dramatic from above.

  • Enlarging the net to make the taper show was declined.
    A taper the eye can follow comes from enlarging the net, not from the law, so true size and a visible taper pull against each other. Two single multipliers were priced against true size - one and a half times (a delivery 3.75 to 1.8 ft, 0.39 ft lost per 100) and twice (5.0 to 2.4 ft, 0.52) - and true size was kept.
