How our maps draw washing places at the water's edge

Where a stream or ditch passes a settlement's houses, the map draws a place to wash at the water's edge, and a town draws its water by one of three forms. Both are decided at random for each settlement.

  • A village's washing place is shared, or one at each house - a knob.
    The record shows two forms: a shared one on the channel or stream bank, like the shared kawado of Gujo's channels, and one at each house where a channel runs past the houses, like the landing at nearly every door of the Jiangnan water towns. So which a settlement has is rolled for each settlement where water passes its houses.
  • A shared washing place is a footing of steps down the bank, about 4 ft wide.
    The record names the footings built for washing but not their size, so the width is a GUESS.
  • How many shared washing places does a settlement draw?
    One for every few households on that bank - a GUESS, since no page we read counts them.
  • A town's water is a second knob: wells alone, street channels, or pipes.
    A castle town may have channels led from its river through the streets, with washing places along them; or it may pipe its water to shared and household wells, which on the map shows only as wells.

The rule the map follows: where a stream or ditch passes a settlement's houses, the map rolls from its seed a shared washing place - a footing of steps down the bank, about 4 ft wide (a guess), one for every few households on that bank (a guess) - or, where a channel runs past the houses, one step at each house on it. A town rolls wells alone, or street channels drawn from its river with washing places along them; piped water draws as wells.
