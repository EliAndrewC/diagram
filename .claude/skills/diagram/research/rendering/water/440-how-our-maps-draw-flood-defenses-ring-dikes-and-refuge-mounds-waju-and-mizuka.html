How our maps draw flood defenses: ring dikes and refuge mounds (waju and mizuka)

A village on a flood plain is drawn on its natural levee, and on the lowest plains it may be ringed by a dike. At the farmstead, the better-off households have a refuge storehouse on a mound, and the rest of the village a mound in common.

  • A flood-plain village seats its houses on the natural levee, the highest ground by the river.
    That is where the villages of the Kiso plain stood from early on, so every flood-plain map does it, with no choice made at random.
  • Whether a ring dike encloses the village and its fields is a knob, chosen at random only on the lowest plains.
    The ring dike is a regional form of the lowest, most flood-prone plains, and a late one: such dikes came in the seventeenth century, and before that and elsewhere levees were built only where they were needed. They survive along the Kiso, the Shinano, the Arakawa, the Tone and the Yodo. The map chooses from its seed whether a village on such ground is ringed. The record gives no share of ringed villages, so the odds are arbitrary.
  • A ringed village draws its water from wells as well as through an intake at the upstream head of its dike.
    An intake through the dike so often caused a breach that wells came into use beside it.
  • Which farmsteads carry a refuge storehouse?
    The richest: the headman's and one or two more. Only fairly wealthy houses had one, and no source counts how many, so the number is a GUESS.
  • The refuge stands to the northwest of its house, on a stone-faced mound 1 to 10 ft higher.
    The mizuya stood northwest of a south-facing house, on a stone base a step higher, on fill some tens of centimeters to 3 m (~10 ft) above the house.
  • A village without enough refuges has one mound in common, or its shrine on raised ground.
    The life-saving platforms were raised by the community or a landlord for those who had no mizuya, and a raised shrine could serve instead.

The rule the map follows: a flood-plain village seats its houses on the natural levee, the highest ground by the river. On the lowest plains the map chooses from its seed whether a ring dike is drawn round the village and its fields; a ringed village draws its water from wells as well as through an intake at the upstream head of the dike. The richest farmsteads (a guess at the share: the headman's and one or two more) carry a mizuya: a storehouse on a stone-faced mound 1 to 10 ft higher than the house, to its northwest; a village without enough of them has one communal refuge mound, or its shrine on raised ground.
