How our maps name rivers

A river on our maps keeps one name along its whole course, across every province and clan border it passes. That is a deliberate deviation from the history: before the modern era a river changed its name from reach to reach, at borders and where other rivers joined it, as the Chikuma became the Shinano and the river through Edo was the Sumida, the Miyato-gawa and the Okawa in different places.

  • Why one name?
    So that players have fewer names to remember. It is the same choice the setting makes when it names a province's city after the province: the extra names would be historically accurate, but keeping track of them would cost the players more than it would give them.
  • The choice costs a map the local names of its river's reaches.
    A map loses the local texture of a river named by the people who live on that reach of it, and a river that crosses a border keeps a name its downstream neighbors would not have used. The alternative, a name for each reach as the history has it, was weighed and declined for the names it would add.
  • The same river keeps its name from one map to the next.
    The Hayakawa, which runs south out of the Fox Clan's province of Minami past its provincial city, is still the Hayakawa where it passes Nagahara, downstream in Crab lands, and no map invents a local name for it.
  • The rule covers the words around a map.
    It holds wherever a river is named - in a map's notes, in its titles and in the record's prose.
