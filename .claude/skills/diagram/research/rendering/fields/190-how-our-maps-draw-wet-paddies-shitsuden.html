How our maps draw wet paddies (shitsuden)

Our maps draw a wet paddy as a rice basin tinted blue-green, open water showing where the other basins are green. The tint marks the ground, not the season: a wet paddy stays waterlogged even when no rice is growing, so it is a kind of plot, not a moment in one. It goes on the low plots at the foot of the field, along the drain that carries the water away.

  • The tint goes on the plots along the field's drain.
    In a traditional paddy the water passes from plot to plot, and no line can be drawn between irrigating and draining, so the plots at the foot, on the drain, are taken to be the wettest ground. That siting is our inference from the mechanism, not a finding, and it is drawn anyway.
  • Which plots wear the tint is a drawing convention, not a survey.
    A comb field tints a random 45% of its lowest rank of plots, the ones whose foot lies on the drain, so the Inashiro map shows 2 blue plots among its 24 low ones. A polder or terraced field tints every low plot: 22 of 22 on the Enokida map, 40 of 40 on the Tanada map.
  • On a comb map, blue is a sample of the wet ground, not the whole of it.
    The low plots left green are as wet as the blue ones; only some wear the tint.
  • The blue shows wetter ground, not deeper water or water seen between the plants.
    No page we read says whether a paddy's water shows between the rice plants until their leaves close over it, so the tint rests on the ground being wetter, not on what a viewer would see.
