How our maps mark temple fields

A village map may mark the country monk's tax-free fields, or leave them unmarked. Where it marks them, it tints them vermilion and scatters them among the common fields as the setting's headman scatters every household's strips. No page we read says where a real village temple's fields lay or how large they were, so the scatter follows the setting's allotment, not an attested temple holding.

  • Marking the tax-free plots is optional.
    A village map that marks none is correct and complete.
  • A map that marks them marks two or three.
    They lie scattered non-contiguously across the common fields rather than blocked together. It marks no more: the setting gives the monk a single plot, and a larger holding would say something about the temple that the setting does not. The count is this project's calibration, a GUESS.
  • How much land do the marked plots hold?
    About two households' worth, all told. The setting's notes give the plot no size, and neither they nor the sources read support a figure; the bound is this project's calibration, a GUESS.
  • The plots are tinted vermilion and outlined.
    Vermilion stands out against the greens of the fields, and this project chose it to read as the shrine's color.
  • A thin gold outline was tried first and declined.
    On a rendered comparison it blended into the tan bunds and was hard to pick out at map view.
