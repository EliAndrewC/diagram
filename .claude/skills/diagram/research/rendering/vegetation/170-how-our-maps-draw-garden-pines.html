How our maps draw garden pines

A residence's inner garden on our building plans holds a cluster of old pines, drawn as three crowns and labeled. Pines in such a garden follow the record, where the black pine is the garden's lead tree, and so does a pine at the wall showing over it to the lane. The size of their crowns is a GUESS.

  • How wide are the drawn crowns?
    4 to 6 ft, which reads as young or closely pruned trees. No page we read gives the crown of a pruned garden pine, so the size is a GUESS.
  • Would an old garden's pines be wider?
    Likely, a GUESS. A garden kept by generations of magistrates would hold older trees whose crowns, even pruned, would likely be wider. A crown of 15 to 30 ft, the figure the map's own notes give a mature garden pine, is no less a guess.
