How our maps draw roofed playhouses

Only a capital keeps roofed playhouses, three to five of them. Towns and provincial cities stage their plays in a temporary hut or on a shrine's stage (where a town's stage stands).

  • A capital keeps three to five roofed playhouses.
    The count is a knob rolled per capital, between Edo's three and Osaka's five.
  • Each capital draws its playhouses in one of two forms, rolled per capital.
    In the Japanese form they stand side by side on one theater street of the entertainment district, each about 80 by 145 ft, the size of the oldest surviving playhouse. In the Song form the halls stand inside the entertainment precincts.
  • Why does no town or provincial city keep a roofed playhouse?
    It is a GUESS. Roofed playhouses stood mostly in the great cities, but the Kanamaruza shows that a pilgrimage town could keep one; our maps draw none below a capital so that the playhouse stays a mark of the great city.
