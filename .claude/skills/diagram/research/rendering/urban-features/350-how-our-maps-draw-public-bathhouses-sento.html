How our maps draw public bathhouses (sento)

A county seat on our maps keeps exactly one bathhouse for its townspeople, while the farmhouses around it bathe at home. A city keeps one for every 1,300 to 2,000 inhabitants, and never fewer than one. Each bathhouse is drawn as a street-front building with a furnace at its back and a fenced fuel yard behind that.

  • A county seat draws exactly one bathhouse.
    Hachijo, a village of 856 near Edo, kept the only bathhouse of nineteen villages, and a county seat of about 1,200 is larger and, like it, the chief place and traffic hub of its district. The farmhouses around it draw no bathhouse: country households each had a bath.
  • How many bathhouses does a city draw?
    One for every 1,300 to 2,000 inhabitants, a degree rolled for each settlement within that band, and never fewer than one. The band runs from the provincial castle town of Takada, about one per 1,300, to Edo, one per 1,700 to 2,000. The city maps' present count of one per full 2,000 (How our maps draw shops and trades in towns and villages) sits at the sparse end of that band.
  • A bathhouse is drawn as a furnace with its stock of firewood beside it.
    Its costs were firewood and water, so the firewood is what shows on the ground: one street-front building, a furnace at its back and a fenced fuel yard behind that, the fuel yard its visible extra.
  • How big is a bathhouse and its fuel yard?
    The building about 40 by 30 ft, the fuel yard about 1,000 to 2,000 sq ft, sized to hold a single mixed bath with its changing floor and a season's stacked firewood. Both are a GUESS: no page we read gives either size.
