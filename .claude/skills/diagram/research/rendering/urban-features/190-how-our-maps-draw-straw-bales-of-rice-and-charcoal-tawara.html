How our maps draw straw bales of rice and charcoal (tawara)

A bale on our maps stands for the straw bales that carried a domain's tax rice and a town's charcoal. No bale from before modern times was found measured, so the maps take their real sizes from twentieth-century bales, and draw them larger than that so that they read.

  • A rice bale is taken to be about 2.5 ft long and 1.5 ft across.
    That is the size of today's 60 kg (~132 lb) bale, 75 cm (~30 in) long and 47 cm (~19 in) across. Scaled from what the Edo bales held, an Edo bale would be within a tenth of that size either way - a GUESS.
  • A charcoal bale is taken to be about 2 by 1.3 ft.
    That is the size of a mid-twentieth-century charcoal bale, 63 cm (~25 in) by 39 cm (~15 in) across its two larger sides; no older one was measured.
  • A bale is drawn about 4 ft long, by convention.
    At its real length of about 2.5 ft a bale is too small to read at the map's scale, so the bales aboard a tax barge and stacked on a charcoal yard's weighing floor are drawn larger.
