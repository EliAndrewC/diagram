How our maps draw sake breweries (sakagura)

Our maps put a sake brewery in the county town and in every provincial city, and in no village. A brewery is drawn as a shopfront with its vat hall and stores behind it, the hall sized from the brewery buildings measured from before 1868 (Sake breweries (sakagura)). What a city's brewery holds besides - its rice storehouse and private well - is at How our maps draw shops and trades in towns and villages.

  • A county town keeps one brewery.
    The setting's county has "the" sake brewer, one house.
  • How many breweries does a city seat of about 3,000 keep?
    One or two, this project's estimate, a GUESS: no page we read counts the brewers of a town of a given size.
  • A village keeps no brewery, a deliberate departure from the history.
    Villages were allowed, and even encouraged, to brew after the early eighteenth century, but the setting names the sake brewer as the county's, so the maps keep the brewery in the county town. A village has one only where its map says it has grown into something more.
  • The vat hall is drawn about 50 to 62 ft on its long side and about 23 to 49 ft across.
    That is the span of the three measured brewery buildings of the Edo period, at Itami, in Tochigi and at Kanazawa. It stands behind the brewer's shopfront.
  • The county town's brewery is a shopfront at the center with its stores behind it on a deep plot.
    Brewers are commonly pictured with a solid shop at the center of the town. The Kanazawa store gives the size of one of the stores, about 62 by 23 ft. Placing the brewery this way is a GUESS drawn from that picture and from the one measured plot.
  • The long Meiji-era hall of about 96 ft is not drawn.
    The maps draw nothing attested only in modern times, so a vat hall is sized from the Edo-period buildings alone.

The rule the map follows: a county town keeps one brewery - a shopfront at the center with its vat hall and stores behind it on a deep plot, the hall about 50 to 62 ft on its long side and 23 to 49 ft across, one store about 62 by 23 ft. A village keeps none.
