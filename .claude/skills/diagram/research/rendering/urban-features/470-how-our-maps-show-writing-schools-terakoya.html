How our maps show writing schools (terakoya)

No village, town or city on our maps draws a building for commoners' schooling. A writing school needed only a master and one room, so a village's school is a room in a house or a temple the map already draws, and a town's or a city's many schools are rooms in its houses. The domain school of a capital is a building, and is at the domain school.

  • How many villages keep a school?
    About three in four, rolled for each village from the map's seed. The share is a GUESS, set a little below the saying that there were about as many schools as villages.
  • Where does a village's school sit?
    In the headman's house, the village temple or another house - a knob rolled for each village from the seed. The three seats are inferred from who the masters were, a GUESS, and their weights are a GUESS too.
  • The school changes what a building's description says, not what is drawn.
    The chosen building's description names the school.
  • A hamlet sends its children to the village's school.
    This is a GUESS.
