How our maps show writing schools (terakoya)

No village, town or city on our maps draws a building for commoners' schooling. A writing school needed only a master and one room, so a village's school is a room in a house or a temple the map already draws, and only that building's description names it; our towns and cities draw no school building either. The one school our maps do draw is a capital's domain school (hanko), where the lord's retainers' sons were taught, which had buildings of its own.

  • How many villages keep a school?
    About three in four, chosen at random for each village from the map's seed. The share is a GUESS, set a little below the saying that there were about as many schools as villages.
  • Where does a village's school sit?
    In the headman's house, the village temple or another house - a knob chosen at random for each village from the map's seed, each about one time in three. The three seats are inferred from who the masters were, a GUESS, and the equal shares are a GUESS too.
  • A hamlet keeps no school; its children go to a nearby village's.
    A hamlet of a few houses is taken to be too small to keep a master of its own. This is a GUESS.
