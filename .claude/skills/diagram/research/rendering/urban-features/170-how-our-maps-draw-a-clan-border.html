How our maps draw a clan border

A clan border is drawn on our maps as a line with no width: it takes up no ground, and nothing on the map need stand clear of it. A border was a line two domains had agreed, and the period's own provincial maps drew their boundaries as lines.

  • Our maps draw the line, not the mounds that marked it.
    The period's large border markers were lines of earth mounds, large and sparse at first, smaller and closer together later. Leaving them out is a deliberate deviation. A mound is a structure, which everything else on the map would then have to stay clear of - the opposite of what a frontier magistracy wants, since its whole design stands its east wall on the line, with a parley room built into that wall.
  • How big are the boundary pillars at the Ubame magistracy?
    About 1 to 1.5 ft of shaft, up to about 3 ft with a plinth. They mark where the line runs, not a structure to keep clear of. The size is an estimate, not a figure read in any source: a GUESS.
  • The parley room stands on the same line.
    The magistracy's parley room, with the line across its floor and kneeling mats on each side, says in a building what the drawn line says on the map: a border exists where two authorities have agreed it. How the room is drawn is at How our maps draw a parley room on a border.
