• banta-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, those hired by towns and villages who kept night watch and watched over fires, sluice gates and the like. Many were of hinin status.」 (translated; ) / 「in some villages, guarding the hills, fields and sluice gates, running errands, night watch, controlling vagrants and so on」 (translated; ) / 「this Edo banta was a commoner」 (translated; ) / 「elsewhere many were of hinin status and were also called ban-hinin」 (translated; ) / 「in some regions many were of hinin status, and they took charge of grave-digging, cremation, the disposal of those who died on the road and so on」 (translated; ) (several encyclopedias on the page; "elsewhere" is set against Edo, where the banta, one says, was the keeper of the jishinban and of commoner status)
  • shukuba-jawiki - 「Kido - set up at the ends of the post station (near the mitsuke), the ground between kido and kido (between mitsuke and mitsuke) being taken as the post town. Besides the kido, a standing night lamp was sometimes set up. And in most cases they were closed at night for crime prevention and the like.」 (translated; )
  • kidoban-kotobank - 「these kido were set up one per machi in a large town and one per several machi in middle and small towns; in the case of Edo, their form was double doors hung between posts set about 2 ken apart, with a fence or board wall on each side running to the edge of the road, and beside it the kido keeper's watch hut. At night, from yotsu (10 p.m.) to mutsu (6 a.m.), they were shut, and passage was possible only through the small side gate (or wicket) under the keeper's watch」 (translated; ) (Heibonsha's entry on the page)
  • kidoban-jawiki - 「The watch hut was fixed at a beam span of 6 shaku, a length of 9 shaku, and an eave height of 1 jo, with the ridge height to correspond.」 (translated; )
  • jishinban-jawiki - 「The watch house was usually set at the south corner of a crossroads facing the main street running east and west. The rule was 9 shaku by 2 ken, but in practice they were about 2-3 ken」 (translated; ) / 「Both the kido watch hut and the jishinban were built against the kido set at the two ends of each machi's main street, the kido keeper at one and the jishinban at the other.」 (translated; )
  • shukuba-mitsuke-kaido-walking - 「At the entrances of a post town were set up mitsuke, kido and a boubana.」 (translated; ) / 「the mitsuke of a post town is an earthen bank built on both sides of the entrance with a fence set on top of it.」 (translated; )
  • jishinban-jawiki - 「In Edo it was under the supervision of the town magistrate, and was set up in each machi of the townsmen's ground in the Kyoho era (depending on the size of the machi, two or three machi set one up jointly; this was called moai), and in September of Kaei 3 (1850), at the end of the shogunate, there are said to have been 994 watch houses.」 (translated; )
  • tsujiban-kotobank - 「a facility for keeping order set up mainly in castle towns in the Edo period」 (translated; ) / 「In Edo, by who bore the cost, they were divided into three kinds: the shogunate's public tsujiban, the wards' tsujiban (jishinban), and the samurai houses' tsujiban kept by daimyo and hatamoto.」 (translated; ) / 「Of these, the samurai houses' tsujiban numbered 936 in the Genbun era.」 (translated; ) / 「Usually, in a tsujiban hut of 2 ken frontage (about 3.6 meters) and 9 shaku depth (about 2.7 meters), built by cutting open the wall of a mansion, the three arresting tools were kept, and four or five men were on duty day and night.」 (translated; ) / 「There were tsujiban in Kyoto too.」 (translated; ) (several encyclopedias on the page)
