How our maps leave out firebreaks (hiyokechi)

A firebreak is open ground kept against the spread of fire: the absence of building, and that is what makes it hard to draw. Our maps leave it off, and let the fire towers carry the story of fire alone (How our maps draw a town's fire watch and fire gear).

  • No map draws a firebreak, or marks a gap as one.
    Two ways were tried and dropped. A drawn plaza, a shaded clearing placed before the houses packed in, made the housing flow round an invented clearing that read as a designed square. A label on a gap the finished map happened to leave only named dead space, adding a word rather than a feature, and cost real effort in placing the labels for no gain. The omission is on the map, not a claim that these cities had no firebreaks - a map drawing convention of omission.
  • A map whose subject is a fire plaza would draw it as a real feature.
    Such a map builds it as a deliberately placed feature with its own glyph; the marked gap is not revived.
