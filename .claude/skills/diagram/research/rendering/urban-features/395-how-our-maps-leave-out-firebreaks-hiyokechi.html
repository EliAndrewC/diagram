How our maps leave out firebreaks (hiyokechi)

A firebreak is open ground kept against the spread of fire: it is made of the absence of building, which is what makes it hard to draw. Our maps leave it off, and let the fire watch towers and fire gear carry the story of fire (How our maps draw fire watch towers and firefighting gear (hinomi yagura)).

  • No map draws a firebreak, or marks a gap as one.
    Two other ways were weighed and declined. A drawn plaza, a shaded clearing laid down before the houses were placed, bent the housing round an invented open space that read as a designed square. A label on a gap the finished map happened to leave named only dead space: it added a word, not a feature.
  • Leaving firebreaks off is a drawing convention, not a claim that towns had none.
    The reason lies in the drawing, not the history: open ground cannot be shown as a feature without inventing one, so a gap in a map's housing is neither a firebreak nor labeled as one - a map drawing convention of omission.
  • A map made to show a firebreak would draw one on purpose, as a feature of its own.
    Where the firebreak is the point of the map, such as an open plaza kept clear against fire, it is placed deliberately and given its own glyph, so it reads as the open ground it was rather than as a gap the housing happened to leave.
