How our maps draw oil pressers (aburaya)

A county seat on our maps keeps one oil presser, and a city at least one. Each is a press house of about 40 to 60 by 25 to 30 ft, standing toward the edge of town, with a mill of one of the three kinds the record knows.

  • How many oil pressers does a county seat have on our maps?
    One. Hachijo, a crossroads village of 856 inhabitants in what is now Yashio city, kept one, which was also a pawnbroker, and a county seat is larger.
  • An oil presser is drawn as one trade among the town's others.
    It is not a works standing alone: Hachijo's presser was a household that also kept a pawnshop.
  • A city on our maps has at least one oil presser.
    Every city keeps one by the rule at How our maps draw shops and trades in towns and villages. No page we read counts a city's oil pressers.
  • The mill is a knob, chosen at random for each settlement among the three recorded kinds.
    A hand trough worked by two men, or a stone ox mill for a presser with the capital to build one, the trough drawn inside the press house and the ox mill's circular track in the yard as a GUESS, since no page we read says where either stood, or a water-wheel mill on a stream or channel, the last only where one runs by. The three kinds are recorded; the choice among them is ours.
  • A press house on our maps is about 40 to 60 by 25 to 30 ft.
    That is room for a press log, a roasting hearth and the mill. The size is a GUESS: no page we read gives one.
  • A press house on our maps stands toward the edge of town, for its roasting fire.
    The siting is a GUESS: no page we read says where a press house stood.
