  • kabu-ido-commons - 「The Kabu-ido system was a set of rules, including regulation of the number of wells per village, introduced to de-escalate the conflict. Under the system, groundwater uses were controlled not by external authority, but by the community residents themselves.」 (p. 169); the cap itself, from the 1812 agreement: 「Article 1: The number of artesian wells is confined to 45. No additional wells are permitted. If any upper village breaches this article, all existing wells in that village must be eliminated;」 (p. 176); and what the quarrel was actually about: 「commons studies have focused on the lowering of groundwater tables caused by excessive groundwater development (Blomquist 1992; Ross and Martinez-Santos 2010; Boone and Fragaszy 2018). The background problem of the Kabu-ido system was, however, excessive drainage water from artesian wells.」 (p. 182)
  • seiyo-karihama-ido - 「In Karihama, where water sources were once scarce, the main sources of water until simple waterworks were laid in 1952 were dug wells and springs. The dug wells were made at a rate of about one to every 5 to 10 houses, and their number reaches about 150」 (translated; ) and 「it was found that 11 in Honura and 1 in Edaura were used in common. Around the communal wells, many had stones laid into the ground, creating a wellside space.」 (translated; ) (Honura and Edaura, the "main cove" and the "branch cove", are the two parts of Karihama; that the neighbors met there is this page's reading of 井戸端空間 (idobata kukan, "wellside space"))
