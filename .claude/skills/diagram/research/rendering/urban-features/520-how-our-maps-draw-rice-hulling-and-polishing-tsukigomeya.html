How our maps draw rice hulling and polishing (tsukigomeya)

Our maps draw rice polishing in town and hulling nowhere in it, since the rice reached a town already hulled (Rice hulling and polishing (tsukigomeya)). Two forms of polishing are attested, the polisher's mortars in a shop and a mill with a waterwheel driving pestles on a stream, so the maps draw both.

  • Every town's rice polisher is an ordinary shop in the shop rows.
    Its mortars are inside, so nothing sets it apart on the map.
  • Whether a town also has a waterwheel mill is a knob.
    It is rolled from the map's seed, and only where a stream with some fall runs past the town's edge.
  • Where does the mill stand, and how big is it?
    On the bank, a small shed with its wheel in the water, outside the built-up blocks. Its siting and its size are a GUESS: no page we read gives a mill building, its size or its distance from the houses.
  • Hulling draws nothing in town.
    It was done on the farm.
