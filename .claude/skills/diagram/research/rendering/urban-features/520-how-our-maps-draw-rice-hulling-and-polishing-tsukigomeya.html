How our maps draw rice hulling and polishing (tsukigomeya)

Our maps draw rice polishing in town and hulling nowhere in it, since the rice reached a town already hulled. Two forms of polishing are attested, the polisher's mortars in a shop and a mill with a waterwheel driving pestles on a stream, so the maps draw both.

  • A town's rice polishers are ordinary shops in the shop rows.
    Their mortars are inside, so nothing sets them apart on the map.
  • Whether a town also has a waterwheel mill is a knob.
    It is rolled from the map's seed, and only where a stream that drops enough to turn a wheel runs past the town's edge.
  • Our maps guess a waterwheel mill as a small shed on the stream bank, outside the built-up blocks.
    Its wheel sits in the water. Its siting and its size are a GUESS: no page we read gives a mill building, its size or its distance from the houses.
  • Our maps draw no hulling in town.
    Rice was hulled on the farm and reached a town as brown rice.
