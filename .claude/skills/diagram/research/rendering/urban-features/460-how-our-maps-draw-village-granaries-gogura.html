How our maps draw village granaries (gogura)

A village on our maps keeps its grain in one of three ways, the three that are recorded: a storehouse of its own, one shared with a neighboring village, or the headman's own storehouse. Which one is a knob, rolled for each village from the map's seed.

  • How often does each form come up?
    A storehouse of its own in about half the villages, a shared storehouse in about a quarter, and the headman's storehouse in about a quarter. The shares are a GUESS: no page we read says how many villages shared rather than built.
  • A shared storehouse is drawn in only one of the villages that share it.
    It stands in one of them.
  • Where the headman's storehouse serves, it is drawn as part of the headman's compound.
    The village then draws no storehouse apart from it.
  • How big is a village's own storehouse?
    One building between about 11 by 9.5 ft, the surviving storehouse at Yamaji, and about 37 by 20 ft, the largest measured village storehouse at The granary and the tax rice (gokura). Each village's is sized along that range from the seed, a calibrated liberty.
  • The storehouse stands free among the houses, on dry ground rather than paddy, with its door to the lane.
    That is where a manual for rural officials sets it, and the Yamaji storehouse turns its door to the road.
  • A hamlet draws no storehouse of its own.
    This is a GUESS.
