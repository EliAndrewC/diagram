How our maps draw village granaries (gogura)

A village on our maps keeps its tax rice and its reserve against famine in one of three ways, the three that are recorded: a storehouse of its own, one shared with a neighboring village, or the headman's own storehouse. Which one is a knob, chosen at random for each village from the map's seed.

  • About half our villages have a storehouse of their own, a quarter share one, and a quarter use the headman's.
    The shares are a GUESS: no page we read says how many villages shared a storehouse or used the headman's rather than building their own.
  • A shared storehouse is drawn in only one of the villages that share it.
    Where villages shared a storehouse, it stood in one of them.
  • Where the headman's storehouse serves, it is drawn as part of the headman's compound.
    The village then draws no storehouse apart from it.
  • How big is a village's own storehouse?
    It is one building, anywhere from about 11 by 9.5 ft, the size of the surviving storehouse at Yamaji, in Yabu city, to about 37 by 20 ft, the largest village storehouse measured (see Storehouses for the tax rice). Each village's storehouse is sized along that range from the seed, a calibrated liberty.
  • The storehouse stands free among the houses, on dry ground rather than paddy.
    That is where a manual for rural officials sets it.
  • Which way does the storehouse's door face?
    Toward the lane: the surviving storehouse at Yamaji turns its door to the road.
  • A hamlet draws no storehouse of its own.
    This is a GUESS: the storehouses recorded belong to villages, so a hamlet's grain is taken to be kept in its village's storehouse.
