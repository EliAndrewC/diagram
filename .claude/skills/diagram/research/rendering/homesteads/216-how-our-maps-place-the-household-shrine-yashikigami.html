How our maps place the household shrine (yashikigami)

Our maps draw a household shrine on only a few homesteads, three to eight in a hundred, as the god of the house's ground was kept where only certain old families had one. Both patterns are recorded - a shrine at every house in some places, at only certain old families' houses in others - and the one count we have found the shrine rare, at one household in a hundred. This project draws the rarer pattern, so that a shrine is notable where it stands, and does not draw the every-house pattern.

  • The number drawn never exceeds the share.
    So chance alone cannot make shrines common on a map.
  • A shrine stands in the plot's northwest, northeast or southwest corner, a knob.
    All three are attested: the northwest and the northeast as the commonest, the northeast especially, and the southwest as usual in Tokushima. Each shrine's corner is rolled: the northwest 45 times in 100, the northeast 35 and the southwest 20. The weights are a GUESS, ordered after the Japanese Wikipedia article's commonest corners; no count we could read gives them.
  • The shrine is drawn at 6 by 6 ft, larger than life, so that it can be seen.
    That is the size of the map's small sheds; the one measured stone shrine is about 40 cm (~16 in) wide, deep and high, and at its true size it could not be made out at the map's scale. It is drawn vermilion, with a ridge line and a torii before its door, to mark it apart from the sheds - a drawing convention.
