How our maps draw and label the half-moon pond (banyuechi)

Where a village digs one, our maps draw the half-moon pond at the front of the settlement, its flat bank toward the houses, and, unusually for these maps, give it a label.

  • The pond is a half-disk, its flat bank facing the houses across open ground.
    The ground between is left bare as a forecourt, as the drying floor lay open between a Hakka house and its pond.
  • The pond carries a label: "geomantic pond".
    These maps do not label what a reader can see for themselves - no "gate", no "north", no name on an ordinary road - and the pond is the one deliberate exception. It is culturally specific, and to a reader who has not met one it reads as nothing but an ordinary pond. The label names what the pond is for, geomancy, rather than calling it a religious feature. It is a map drawing convention.
  • Trees keep clear of the pond and the open ground before it, and scrub and reeds of its water.
    The groves keep clear of the full circle the half-disk is cut from, so the forecourt between the pond and the houses stays bare too, and the scattered scrub and reeds skip the pond as they skip any other water.
  • The pond is drawn apart from the field channels, fed by nothing the map shows.
    This is a DEVIATION: on the one page we read about the pond's water, a channel led field water into it. The map does not yet draw an inlet.
