How our maps draw and label the half-moon pond (banyuechi)

Where a village digs one, our maps draw the half-moon pond at the front of the settlement and give it a label, the one feature of its kind they name.

  • The pond is a half-disk with its flat bank toward the houses.
    The flat bank is left open as the forecourt, as the drying floor lay open between a Hakka house and its pond.
  • The pond labels itself "geomantic pond".
    These maps do not label what a reader can see for themselves - no "gate", no "north", no name on an ordinary road - and the pond is the one deliberate exception. It is culturally specific, and to a reader who has not met one it reads as nothing but a pond-shaped pond. The label names what the pond is for, geomancy, rather than calling it a religious feature. It is a map drawing convention.
  • No vegetation touches its water.
    The groves keep clear of the full circle the half-disk is cut from, so the open forecourt stays bare too, and the scrub and reed scatter skips the pond as it skips any other water.
  • The pond is drawn apart from the field channels, fed by nothing the map shows.
    This is a DEVIATION: on the one page we read about the pond's water, a channel led field water into it. It stands until the map draws an inlet.
