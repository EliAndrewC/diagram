How our maps draw bath sheds (furo)

Our maps draw no bath shed standing on its own. Before 1868 a farm's bath was a small room at the main house, and no bath standing as a building of its own is found until the twentieth century, so the higher shares of the twentieth-century counts are not used.

  • A bath, where drawn, is a room of 6 by 6 to 6 by 12 ft joined to the main house.
    The baths of 1824 and 1842 were 1 to 2 tsubo, a tsubo being about 6 by 6 ft.
  • Two or three homesteads in ten have a bath room.
    That is the share of houses with a bath in the registers of 1824 and 1842. The earlier registers, with fewer, are not followed.
  • A bath room takes one of three attested places, a knob.
    Beside the house's main door, at the far end of its stable wing, or joined to its floored rooms, a place found in only a few houses. How often each place is drawn is a GUESS, and so is giving the floored-room place to a headman's house.
