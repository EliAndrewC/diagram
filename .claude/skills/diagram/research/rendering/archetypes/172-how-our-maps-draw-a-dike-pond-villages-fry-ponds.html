How our maps draw a dike-pond village's fry ponds

On our maps most dike-pond hamlets keep no nursery ponds: they raise grown fish from fry bought in. About one hamlet in four is drawn instead as a fry village of the Jiujiang kind, whose smallest ponds are given to fry until they hold up to seven-tenths of its pond water, and whose fry ponds are drawn in grayer, muddier water than the clear ponds where the fish are grown on.

  • Each hamlet is either a fry village or a hamlet that buys its fry.
    The delta knew both: Jiujiang, whose ponds were mostly nursery water, and the villages elsewhere, whose ponds mostly raised grown fish. These are two forms of village, not one share of nursery ponds that every village kept, so each hamlet takes one form or the other, chosen at random (Fish fry and nursery ponds (yumiao)).
  • How many hamlets are fry villages?
    One in four, a GUESS. Qu Dajun says fry ponds were found only in Jiujiang, so the fry village is made the rarer form; no source gives the odds, and one in four is arbitrary.
  • A fry village's nursery water is seven-tenths of its pond area.
    Qu Dajun gives seven parts in ten of Jiujiang's pond water to fry; reading that as a share of the ponds' area is this project's reading.
  • The smallest ponds are the nursery ponds.
    They are taken smallest first, for as long as their area stays within seven-tenths of the whole, so the nursery water comes to about that share and never more. Which of Jiujiang's ponds held the fry is on no page we read; taking the smallest follows the graded ponds of the late-Ming Jiangxi method, where the fry start in the smallest (Fish fry and nursery ponds (yumiao)), and is a GUESS.
  • A fry pond is drawn in grayer, muddier water than a grow-out pond.
    In Jiujiang the fry water was turbid and the grown-fish water clear, so the color of a pond told what it held, and a fry village reads as one on the map at a glance. The exact shade is a map drawing convention.

Some forms of nursery the record holds are not drawn.

  • No nursery pit is drawn beside a pond.
    The small pond about ten feet square beside a big one is the Jiangxi method of a late-Ming compendium, away from the delta, and the ponds our maps draw are the delta's (Fish fry and nursery ponds (yumiao)).
  • The modern manual's fry-pond sizes and nursery share are not used.
    A fry pond of one to three mu, and nursery ponds a quarter to three tenths of a farm's ponds, are a modern design for a farm that stocks itself (Fish fry and nursery ponds (yumiao)), and this project draws nothing found only in modern times. A fry pond is whatever size its parcel is.
