How our maps draw parcels and bunds inside a polder (aze)

Our maps draw a polder's parcels at their real size, not enlarged to read better: about half an acre each in a rice polder, laid out in square modules about 190 ft on a side. A field ditch runs one way along the lines between modules, and a walking bund the other way. Each parcel is its own shape, and the gap between two parcels is only as wide as what divides them on the ground: a walking bund about 3 ft wide, or a field ditch with its banks, about 8 ft.

  • How large is a rice polder's parcel?
    About three mu (~half an acre) on average. That is the rough mean of the fish-scale registers of 1897 for the polders of Taipingqiao, near Suzhou, about 3,700 parcels in nineteen polders, and since the count is rough the size is a figure the drawing is set to, not a surveyed mean (Parcels and bunds inside a polder (aze)).
  • The parcels are laid out in square modules about 190 ft on a side.
    A whole module holds about 5.5 mu (~0.8 acre), and a third of one about 1.8 mu.
  • The module lines run straight, while the parcels inside them vary.
    The canal lattice was the surveyed part of a polder (How our maps draw polders (weitian, waju)), so the module lines hold their line. Inside them most modules split into two or three oblong strips, a few merge along the fall of the ground, and each bund line wanders on its own, as a hand-piled bund's did (How our maps draw bunds between the paddies (aze)).
  • Our maps never draw the parcels as a grid of uniform rectangles.
    Plots of one large, regular rectangle are the look of modern consolidation for machine working (Parcels and bunds inside a polder (aze)), and this project draws nothing that is only modern.
  • How wide is the walking bund between one row of modules and the next?
    About 3 ft, a GUESS. The map draws it as a ridge of piled earth with the footpath along its top. The bund dividing two paddies was two feet wide or less (Parcels and bunds inside a polder (aze)), and no page we read says how much wider one ran where it also carried a path, so 3 ft is ridge and path together, within the 2 to 5 ft the maps give a walking bund elsewhere.
  • Between one column of modules and the next, the gap is a ditch corridor of about 8 ft.
    A field ditch runs there with its spoil banks on either side.
  • A module split into strips is cut by a walking bund, not a ditch.
    A wide gap tells the reader that water runs there. Where a module is split lengthwise into strips of one holding, the cut between two strips takes the plain 3 ft walking bund between rows of modules, not the 8 ft corridor, which would be wider than any plain bund and promise water that is not there. Only the lines between columns of modules, where a ditch is drawn, keep the corridor. This project chose it so that a gap's width always says what runs in it.
  • Who held or rented a plot is not drawn.
    No household's plots are gathered into a block, no line is drawn round a holding, and nothing sets a tenant's plot apart: a household's land lay scattered among its neighbors', and nothing read marks tenure on the ground (Parcels and bunds inside a polder (aze)). The irregular patchwork of small plots is itself what scattered, intermixed holding looks like from above.
  • Japan's warichi, the redistribution of village land by lot, gives the map nothing to draw.
    Redistribution by lot changed who worked which plot, not the plots (Parcels and bunds inside a polder (aze)).

Where a polder's low fields were dug out into fishponds, with mulberry planted on the dikes between them (the dike-pond, as in the Pearl River delta), the map lays the block out on a different grid.

  • A dike-pond block runs a smaller module, about 160 ft on a side, most of them merged in pairs.
    So most ponds stand in a parcel about 160 by 320 ft, about 0.48 ha (~1.2 acres), with gaps of about 22 ft both ways.
  • Are the drawn ponds as large as real ones?
    Somewhat smaller. The water inside such a parcel comes to about 0.29 ha (~0.7 acre), against the 0.4 to 0.6 ha (~1-1.5 acres) a summary of a modern survey gives for most ponds, a figure that reaches us at second hand and is kept as a comparison, not a target.
