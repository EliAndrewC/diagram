• iwatsuki-tokinokane-jawiki - 「In Kanbun 11 (1671) of the Edo period, Abe Masaharu, the fourth lord of the Iwatsuki domain, placed the first bronze bell at Shibue-guchi as the bell of Iwatsuki castle」 (translated; ), 「as of Jokyo 4 (1687), four men such as ashigaru and chugen served as bell-ringers」 (translated; ) and 「in the Edo period the bell was struck 12 times a day」 (translated; )
  • kokucho-tokinokane-jawiki - 「In the Edo period, around the time of Tokugawa Hidetada, the time bell was placed in the west bailey of Edo castle. Because the bell tower was close to the lord's quarters and a nuisance, in 1626 Tsuji Genshichi moved the time bell to Honkokucho 3-chome (now Nihonbashi Honcho 4-chome) and built a bell tower, and a drum came to be used for the time signal within the castle. The other time bells rang on hearing the sound of the bell that began here.」 (translated; ) and 「it was rung every toki, taking the sound of the drum from Edo castle as the standard」 (translated; ) (the article says the shogunate recognized nine time bells in all and then lists nine besides this one, so whether Edo had nine or ten is unclear on the page; either way it had many)
  • xian-zhonglou-zhwiki - 「first built in the seventeenth year of Hongwu of the Ming (1384)」 (translated; ) and 「it first stood at the Yingxiang monastery at the mouth of Guangji street north of West Street, opposite the Xi'an drum tower, about 1,000 m from its present position」 (translated; )
  • xian-gulou-zhwiki - 「the drum tower stands west of the Xi'an bell tower」 (translated; ), 「the two buildings reported the hours by the sound of the drum and of the bell respectively」 (translated; ) and 「first built in the thirteenth year of Hongwu of the Ming (1380)」 (translated; )
