How our maps draw the Imperial Magistrate's compound

A capital map draws the Imperial Magistrate's compound as one walled compound of about 90,000 square feet (~2 acres), holding the magistrate's court, office and residence, in an ink of its own so that it reads as the Emperor's ground and not as another of the domain's offices. Where its staff live is rolled per map.

  • How big is the compound?
    About 90,000 square feet, the size of an Edo town magistracy. The two anchors are about 89,000 to 92,000 square feet for a magistracy and over 106,700 square feet for the intendant's office at Takayama, which held its rice storehouse too (the two offices). A size is a degree, not a choice between forms, so it is calibrated rather than rolled.
  • The compound and the Emperor's granaries together come to a little over the Takayama grounds.
    Where the granaries stand beside the compound - one value of their siting knob - the two take about 117,000 square feet, a little over the Takayama office that held office and storehouse together. That the two figures agree is the check on both.
  • Where do the magistrate's staff live?
    Either inside the compound or beside it. The record supports both forms, so where they live is a knob, rolled per map from its seed, with neither value favored.
  • Staff inside: the staff households live within the compound's own walls.
    The compound's dozen staff households take row housing along its perimeter, as a domain's estate in Edo housed its people.
  • Staff beside: the staff households live in a walled group quarter against the compound.
    The compound holds the magistrate's household and office only, and the staff households take walled plots of their own in a group quarter against its wall, as the Edo magistracy's officers and constables did.
  • Why a dozen staff households for roughly 25 assistants?
    The map allots the compound 12 households, on the reading that not every assistant keeps a household of their own there. The count is a GUESS.
  • A group quarter of the magistrate's staff is drawn in the compound's ink.
    It is the Emperor's ground too, so the reader sees one foreign presence rather than a magistracy and an unrelated samurai block. This is a drawing convention.

The rule the map follows: the Imperial Magistrate's compound is one walled compound of about 90,000 square feet, drawn in its own ink as the Emperor's ground, holding the magistrate's court, office and residence. Whether its dozen staff households live inside its walls or in a walled group quarter against them is rolled per map; in the second case the quarter's households each take a samurai plot of the capital's own sizing, and the quarter is drawn in the compound's ink.
