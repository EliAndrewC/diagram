A capital's map draws nothing inside the castle (How our maps draw the castle in a capital), but what a castle held is known, so what a reader should not expect to find drawn is known too.

  • Four things in the inside column are a GUESS: the treasury, the guard's barracks, the wells and the gardens.
    No page we read names them among a castle's contents. None of them is drawn, so the guess costs the map nothing.
  • The domain's rice is drawn once and implied once.
    A castle kept reserve rice inside its walls against war and famine, and a domain collected its tribute rice at a granary of its own, at Kumamoto on the water. So the siege stock is implied inside the castle and the tribute granary stands at the wharf, a separate feature; that the stipends are paid from the wharf granary is this project's reading.
  • A capital draws no House Chancellery compound.
    A domain's senior retainers worked in a room of the domain's office, in the castle, so the council meets in a room of the implied palace. A deliberative body of five to ten lineage representatives makes no street traffic and keeps no archive of its own: it is a chamber, not a compound, and a chancellery compound drawn anywhere in the city is an error.
  • The ministries stand outside the castle, on the grand avenue.
    A Japanese domain kept its offices inside its castle, in the palace or an outer bailey; our maps put the ministries outside, as China's Six Ministries stood outside the palace, for the reasons at The ministries sit outside the castle, flanking the approach avenue.
  • The lineage compounds stay outside the castle, though great retainers' estates stood inside some.
    At Fukuoka they stood in the castle's third bailey through the Edo period, but a compound behind the castle's wall would not be drawn, and its labeled lineage would vanish from the map. Whether a capital may take that form is not yet decided; until it is, the compounds stand in the town: How big is a lineage's compound in the capital, and where does it stand?
  • The castle's own plan would hold a mid-sized domain's palace.
    Seven buildings of about twenty rooms, public in front and private behind, in whichever bailey the plan puts it, with the council's chamber a room of the palace, not a building of its own.
