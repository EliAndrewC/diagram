How our maps hang a capital's time bells (toki no kane)

Every walled seat on our maps draws one bell-and-drum tower at its main crossing, and a capital, the largest seat, draws one time signal station too, never a second elsewhere in the city.

  • A capital draws one time signal, not Edo's many.
    A domain's castle town kept one time bell, the domain's own, and the relay of many bells belonged to Edo, the shogun's city, far larger than the seats these maps draw. That comparison is this project's, not one a source states.
  • The castle's own drum is not drawn.
    Edo castle kept a drum for its own time signal, but it stood inside the castle, and the castle is drawn blank.
  • A capital may draw the Chinese pair of towers instead - a knob.
    Xi'an kept a drum tower and a bell tower apart, reporting the hours one by drum and one by bell, the bell tower first standing opposite the drum tower. So a capital draws either the single combined tower every walled seat draws or the pair, rolled from the map's seed.

The rule the map follows: a capital draws exactly one time signal station at its main crossing - either the single combined bell-and-drum tower every walled seat draws (about 36 ft square), or, as a per-capital roll, a drum tower and a bell tower of that size facing each other along the main street, one on each side of the crossing. It never draws a second station elsewhere in the city.
