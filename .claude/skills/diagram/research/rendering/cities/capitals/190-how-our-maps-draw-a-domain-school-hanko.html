How our maps draw a domain school (hanko)

A capital's map draws its domain school as one walled compound of about 1 ha (~2.5 acres), in the violet the map gives state buildings, with nothing drawn inside it. It stands beside the castle or in the town, chosen at random for each capital. A provincial city draws the school's martial hall but no school of letters.

  • The domain school is drawn as a school, not as a government office.
    A domain school was a school of letters with its martial arts taught on the same grounds, not a bureau of clerks, so the map draws it in the family of the martial halls rather than as one of the ministries' offices.
  • How big is the compound?
    About 400 by 260 ft, about 1 ha (~2.5 acres, some 3,000 tsubo): a mid-sized school. It lies inside the recorded range - above Matsushiro's school of about 0.31 ha (~0.8 acres), below Aizu's Nisshinkan at 2.4 ha (~6 acres), and far below Mito's Kodokan at about 10.5 ha (~26 acres). That a middling domain built a school of about this size is a GUESS: the schools whose grounds are recorded here were not chosen by the size of their domains.
  • Every capital's school is drawn at the same size.
    A school's ground varied by degree, not by kind, so every capital draws the one size.
  • Nothing is drawn inside the compound.
    A real school was dense with buildings - Aizu's school, the Nisshinkan, fitted a swimming place and an observatory into its ground - so a faithful interior is a dozen buildings, and that belongs to the school's own building plan. Showing nothing is better than showing the wrong thing, the same convention that leaves the castle blank.
  • Does a school's own plan draw a shrine to Confucius?
    It is open. The historical schools went both ways - Mito set a shrine to Confucius beside a Shinto one, while Matsushiro's list of buildings names none - so on a school's own plan the shrine would be a knob, chosen at random for each school. But the setting's canon names no Confucian temple, which argues for a school with no Confucian hall at all. No school's plan has been drawn yet.
  • The school stands beside the castle or in the town, chosen at random for each capital.
    Domain schools stood in all three places - inside the castle, beside it, or in the town - so the siting is a knob, chosen at random from the map's seed. The inside-the-castle form cannot be drawn while the map leaves the castle's interior blank - the same limit that keeps the great samurai families' compounds out of the inside of the castle - so the map draws the other two.
  • Does a provincial city's map draw a school?
    Only its martial hall. This project reads the provincial city's martial hall (Martial training is an urban institution), a long plank-floored hall with its kamiza at the head, as the domain school's martial wing, one rank of settlement down; the capital shows the whole school. The setting sends a county town's samurai children to the provincial city or the domain capital for schooling, and history gives both Japan's domain school at the castle and China's official school in every county seat (Temples of the state cult: the City God and Confucius). No map yet draws a provincial city's school of letters. That a provincial city holds one school compound for samurai children is a GUESS, and whether its map draws one, and on how much ground, is still to be decided.

The rule the map follows: a capital's domain school is one walled compound of about 400 by 260 ft, about 1 ha (~2.5 acres), in the state violet, standing either beside the castle or in the town; the choice between the two is made at random for each capital from the map's seed. Nothing is drawn inside it.
