How our maps draw a barbican (wengcheng, masugata)

On our maps a barbican is a walled court before a city gate, round or square, its own gate opened in its side wall rather than in line with the main gate, as the Song manual describes it. Whether a gate has one is a knob rolled per gate, and its size is drawn between the two courts the record measures.

  • A provincial city gives each of its main road gates a barbican.
    The Ming built them at prefectural and county cities alike.
  • A city's barbican is rolled round or square.
    The Song manual says a barbican was round or square as the ground allowed.
  • A city's barbican is drawn about 125 to 230 ft along the wall.
    The range runs from the widest surviving masugata at Edo castle, about 27 m (~89 ft) by 38 m (~125 ft), to the south barbican of Xi'an, 70 m (~230 ft) east to west. Any size between the two measured courts is allowed.
  • A walled county town on the Chinese model gives a barbican to some of its gates or to all of them.
    The towns differ: at Pingyao only the east and west gates have one, while at Zhenhai the five open gates each had one.
  • A county town's barbican is drawn about 80 to 120 ft along the wall, a GUESS.
    It is scaled down from the city's, since no page we read gives a county town's barbican's size.
  • A town on the Japanese model draws no barbican; only a castle's gate draws a masugata.
    The masugata the record describes guarded a castle's gates.

Where a barbican stands, the drawbridge over the moat is drawn in line before the barbican's own gate (How our maps draw a city's gates (chengmen)).

The rule the map follows:

  • A barbican or none is a knob rolled per gate.
  • A provincial city: a barbican at each main road gate, its court rolled round or square, about 125 to 230 ft along the wall, its own gate in its side wall, off the line of the main gate.
  • A walled county town on the Chinese model: a barbican at some of its gates or at all of them, about 80 to 120 ft along the wall.
  • A town on the Japanese model: none. Only a castle's gate draws a masugata.
