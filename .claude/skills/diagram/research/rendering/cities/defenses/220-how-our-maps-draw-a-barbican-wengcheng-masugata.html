How our maps draw a barbican (wengcheng, masugata)

On our maps a barbican is a walled court before a city gate, round or square, its own gate opened in its side wall rather than in line with the main gate, as the military manual Wujing zongyao describes it. Cities and walled towns on the Chinese model draw them: a provincial city at each main road gate, about 125 to 230 ft along the wall, and a county town at some of its gates or all of them, smaller. Towns on the Japanese model draw none, and the Japanese form, the masugata, is drawn only at a castle's gate.

  • A provincial city gives each of its main road gates a barbican.
    The Ming built them at prefectural and county cities alike. Giving one to every main road gate of a city, rather than to some, is this project's choice.
  • Each of a city's barbicans is drawn round or square, chosen at random.
    The Wujing zongyao says a barbican was round or square as the ground allowed.
  • A city's barbicans are drawn about 125 to 230 ft along the wall.
    The record measures two such courts, a Japanese castle's masugata and a barbican at Xi'an, and a city's barbicans are drawn between them. The range runs from the widest surviving masugata at Edo castle, whose longer side is about 38 m (~125 ft), to the south barbican of Xi'an, 70 m (~230 ft) east to west.
  • A walled county town on the Chinese model gives a barbican to some of its gates or to all of them.
    The towns differ: at Pingyao the barbicans stand outside the east and west gates only, while at Zhenhai the five gates left open each had one. That both were county towns is quoted at Barbicans (wengcheng, masugata).
  • A county town's barbicans are drawn about 80 to 120 ft along the wall, a GUESS.
    They are scaled down from the city's, since no page we read gives a county town's barbican's size.

Japan built the same kind of court, the masugata, but at castles.

  • A castle's gate is drawn with a masugata.
    The masugata the record describes guarded a castle's gates.
  • A town on the Japanese model has no barbican on our maps.
    Every masugata the record describes stood at a castle, so this project draws none at a town's own entrances.

Where a barbican stands, the drawbridge over the moat is drawn in line before the barbican's own gate (How our maps draw a city's gates (chengmen)).

The rule the map follows:

  • For a county town, whether a gate has a barbican is a knob, chosen at random for each gate.
  • A provincial city: a barbican at each main road gate, its court round or square, chosen at random, about 125 to 230 ft along the wall, its own gate in its side wall, off the line of the main gate.
  • A walled county town on the Chinese model: a barbican at some of its gates or at all of them, about 80 to 120 ft along the wall.
  • A town on the Japanese model: none. Only a castle's gate draws a masugata.
