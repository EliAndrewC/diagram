  • nagayamon-kotobank - 「The nagayamon, made by putting a door in part of this row-house to serve as its entrance and placing a lookout watchman's (chugen's) room beside it, was what expressed the formal rank of a warrior residence. In the villages, this nagayamon was permitted to be built only by the upper stratum of farmers who served as village officials such as nanushi and shoya.」 (translated; ) and 「the nagayamon is distinguished by having guard posts projecting on either side of the gate, and its form and structure were laid down according to the house rank of the daimyo or hatamoto. ... In time, besides warrior residences, there appeared farmhouses whose owners had served as shoya or nanushi, townhouses such as those of the machi-doshiyori, and temples too, that took the nagayamon form. These have no guard post, and the rooms on either side of the doorway were used as men's rooms, attendants' rooms, sheds, stables and so on.」 (translated; ) (two published encyclopedias reprinted on the same page. Both put the gate in the language of permission, and both tie it to rank and to office rather than to means.)
  • myoji-taito-kotobank - 「From the middle of the early modern period onward, in line with the various domains' financial distress, they issued the qualification indiscriminately to peasants and townsmen as well, in exchange for monetary contributions and the like.」 (translated; ) and, from the historical dictionary on the same page, 「The shogunate and the domains gave this privilege as a favor even to the ruled statuses other than the warrior status - to those who had rendered special social service, those who made large monetary contributions, and those who performed auxiliary duties connected with rule, such as ogashoya and machi-doshiyori.」 (translated; ) (two works, same page, both making money one of the recognized grounds for the grant in the later Edo period. Neither says anything of a wall or a gate being obtainable that way.)
  • bunka-sugimoto-omoya - 「The Sugimoto house is a machiya in the center of the city of Kyoto, and occupies a broad estate facing Ayanokoji street.」 and 「The main house is in the omoteya-zukuri form, joining the shop part at the front and the living part at the back by the entrance hall; behind the main house the Okura, the Sumigura and the Nakagura stand in a row, and a high wall is run round the estate.」 (translated; ) (the three storehouse names mean the great, the corner and the middle storehouse)
