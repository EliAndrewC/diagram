How our maps draw the ground outside a city gate

Every main-road gate of a city on our maps has a gate market outside it: at least six shops within about 1,560 ft of the gate, strung along the approach road, the outermost running off the edge of the map. A city may also draw a small tile works just beyond its built ground, on water, and draws no lime kiln near its wall.

  • Every main-road gate gets a gate market, not only the busiest.
    The study of Chinese walled cities finds a suburb outside at least one gate of nearly every city, and the largest in the direction of the main traffic. That each main gate earns one, at a scale that differs from gate to gate, is this project's choice.
  • In the drawn cities the southern gate has the bigger market.
    The wider southern approach carries the bigger market and the northern gate a row of stalls; a river city's gate on the water carries the whole wharf instead.
  • How many buildings does a gate market hold?
    Ten to forty at a busy gate, a GUESS the maps were calibrated against. The one count found for a gate suburb is forty-eight horse inns outside a capital's gate on its trunk road north, one trade alone, so a provincial seat's strip sits sensibly below it.
  • The drawn strip is about as long as a measured one, and has fewer buildings.
    The one measured street, Nanguanxiang, runs about 350 m (~1,148 ft); the drawn strip, within about 1,560 ft of the gate, is of the same order of length, not a shorter slice of it. The one count, Odawara's entrance ward, is 124 houses; ten to forty is on the low side of it, which is where a thinned strip belongs.
  • At least six shops stand within about 1,560 ft of each main gate.
    The floor is set low because the strip on a map is only a slice of a longer suburb.
  • The outermost shops run off the edge of the map.
    The cut says the suburb continues past what the map shows - a drawing convention.
  • A lodging house stands in the same strip outside each gate, and a second inside the walls.
    The town's market-day lodging house is at The market-day flophouse - who actually stays over.
  • A gate kept only for sorties has no market.
    A purely military sally gate, opening onto empty field with no road, carries no market, no lodging house and no caravan cluster. No city on our maps has one yet.

No city map draws the works outside the wall yet; when one does, these are the rules.

  • A city may draw a small tile works just beyond its built ground, on water.
    It is drawn as a cluster of kiln households rather than one shop, as at Imado, where Edo's roof tiles were fired. How many kilns a provincial city kept is on no page we read, and is a GUESS.
  • A city draws no lime kiln in its near hinterland.
    Edo's castle lime was burned where the stone was and carried in. This rests on Edo alone; that a provincial city's lime also came from away is a GUESS by extension.
