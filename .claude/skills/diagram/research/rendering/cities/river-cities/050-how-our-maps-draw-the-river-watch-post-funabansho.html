How our maps draw the river watch post (funabansho)

At an official's landing, as at Hayakawa, our maps draw a small watch hut at the head of the landing, manned from the office. It is the Nakagawa post's hut at the river's edge, not the whole fenced post.

  • The fenced compound is left to a checkpoint on a capital's approach.
    The Nakagawa post's compound of about 155 by 100 ft guarded the people and goods entering and leaving the shogun's city, so a local office's landing keeps only the hut.
  • How big is the hut, and why at the office's own landing?
    Both are a GUESS: no page we read describes a watch kept at a local office's own landing, or gives its size.
  • The watch takes no toll.
    The domains' boat watch posts collected taxes as well as inspecting boats. This setting taxes goods where they are sold, not where they pass through, so a river watch on our maps inspects and records who and what comes to the landing and takes no toll from boats passing by - a deliberate deviation from the domains' posts.
