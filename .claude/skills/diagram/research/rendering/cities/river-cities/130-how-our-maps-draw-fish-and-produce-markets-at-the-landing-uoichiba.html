How our maps draw fish and produce markets at the landing (uoichiba)

The rule the map follows for a city on navigable water:

  • A city on navigable water has a fish market at its landing.
    It is a short row of piers at the bank, a line of storage sheds along the bank behind them, a row of selling sheds and open stalls facing the street behind those, and a well among them - the parts of Edo's fish market in its plan of 1842.
  • A produce market is a separate site at another stretch of the bank.
    Osaka kept its fish and its produce at separate markets.
  • How big is a provincial city's market?
    Its size is a GUESS, scaled down from Edo's: no page we read says how a smaller city's market was laid out.
