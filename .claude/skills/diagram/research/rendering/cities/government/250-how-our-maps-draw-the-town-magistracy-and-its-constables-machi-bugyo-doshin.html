How our maps draw the town magistracy and its constables (machi-bugyo, doshin)

A provincial city map draws no town magistracy of its own. In this setting a city's Ministry of Justice oversees its street policing, magistrates, courts and jails, so this project reads that ministry's office as the city's magistracy, and its constables live outside it, as Edo's did, in a group residence of equal plots.

  • A provincial city draws no town magistracy beside its Ministry of Justice office.
    The Ministry of Justice office is drawn like the city's other ministry offices (How our maps draw the offices of the six ministries (liubu)).
  • The constables live in a group residence of equal plots near the Ministry of Justice, never inside its walls.
    Edo's officers and constables lived together at Hatchobori, apart from the magistracies they served.
  • A senior constable's plot is about 100 ft square and a junior constable's about 60 ft square: the Edo sizes.
    An Edo officer's plot was about 300 tsubo (~10,670 sq ft) and a constable's about 100 tsubo (~3,560 sq ft).
  • A domain capital's Imperial Magistrate keeps a compound of its own.
    How it is drawn is at How our maps draw the Imperial Magistrate's compound.
  • The Clan and Family magistrates' offices are not drawn as compounds of their own.
    They are the setting's own offices, which hear disputes between houses and families.
