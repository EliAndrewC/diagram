How our maps draw the town magistracy and its constables (machi-bugyo, doshin)

In this setting a city's Ministry of Justice oversees its street policing, magistrates, courts and jails, so this project reads that ministry's office as the city's magistracy and draws no second one. Its officers and constables (yoriki and doshin) live in a group residence near it, as Edo's lived together at Hatchobori.

  • A provincial city draws no town magistracy beside its Ministry of Justice office.
    In this setting that ministry already oversees street policing, magistrates, courts and jails, so its office serves as the magistracy. It is drawn like the city's other ministry offices (How our maps draw the offices of the six ministries (liubu)).
  • The officers and constables live in a group residence near the Ministry of Justice, never inside its walls.
    Edo's officers and constables lived together in group residences at Hatchobori.
  • An officer's plot is about 100 ft square and a constable's about 60 ft square, as in Edo.
    An Edo officer's plot was about 300 tsubo (~10,670 sq ft) and a constable's about 100 tsubo (~3,560 sq ft).
  • The Clan and Family magistrates' offices are not drawn as compounds of their own.
    They are the setting's own offices, which hear disputes between houses and families, so this project does not draw them for now.

A domain capital also holds the Emperor's own magistrate, whose compound is drawn as How our maps draw the Imperial Magistrate's compound describes.
