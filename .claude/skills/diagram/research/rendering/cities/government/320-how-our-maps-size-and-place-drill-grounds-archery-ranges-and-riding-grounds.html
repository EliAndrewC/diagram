How our maps size and place drill grounds, archery ranges and riding grounds (jiaochang, yaba, baba)

Our city maps draw the open grounds where a garrison trained to march, shoot and ride. A provincial city or county seat has one walled drill ground of some 5 to 6 acres. A castle town or provincial city has a long, fenced riding ground about 600 ft long in its samurai ground, and a capital may have more than one. An archery range, about 250 ft long, appears on the city map only when a settlement puts it at the thinly built edge of town; inside a castle or a residence it belongs to that compound's own plan.

The rule the map follows:

  • A drill ground is closed by an earth wall, with a gate on one side and a review hall facing it across the ground.
    The hall, with its commander's platform, stands on the side opposite the gate, as at Chongming, a county seat.
  • How large is a drill ground?
    About 5 to 6 acres, calibrated from the only two drill grounds whose size we found: Chongming's 37.5 mu (~6 acres) and Fuzhou's more than thirty mu (~5 acres).
  • Whether the drill ground lies outside a gate or in a corner inside the wall is chosen at random for each settlement.
    Both are attested: Chongming's great ground lay outside its east gate, and Xi'an's garrison drilled inside the walls, in its garrison quarter at the city's northeast corner. Inside the wall it counts against the cap on how much of a walled city is left unbuilt (How our maps fill a city's quarters and leave its empty ground).
  • Whether a city with its great ground outside also draws a small practice ground in a corner inside the wall is a knob, rolled per settlement.
    Chongming kept one as well as its great ground outside the east gate, though the page gives it no size. Where a map draws one, it too counts against the cap on unbuilt ground.
  • A riding ground is a banked, fenced strip about 600 by 60 ft.
    That is the common shape of a castle town's riding ground. Where it runs along a moat or a river bank it may be up to about 900 by 30 ft, as the castle town of Ako's was.
  • A riding ground has a small viewing stand at the middle of one long side.
    Ako's stood below the bank, just south of the ground's middle.
  • A riding ground lies in the samurai ground, near the castle or the governor's compound.
    That is where a castle town's samurai rode.
  • An archery range is about 250 by 8 ft, with an earth butt (azuchi) for the arrows at one end.
    That is the fixed size of a samurai's range.
  • Whether an archery range stands inside a compound or at the thinly built edge of town is chosen at random for each settlement.
    A range stood inside the castle or a residence, or in the thinly built outskirts. Inside a compound it is part of that compound's own building plan; at the edge, the city map draws it.
