How our maps draw the offices of the six ministries (liubu)

Our maps draw each of the six ministries as a walled compound of its own, in a provincial city as in a domain capital, all six alike in size. History gives a province no such compounds - its six offices were rooms in the governor's yamen - so a provincial city's ministry compounds, and their size, are a GUESS at the setting's own arrangement. A domain capital sets its six in a government ward outside the castle, along the approach avenue to the castle's main gate.

  • How big is a provincial city's ministry office?
    About 110 by 80 ft to 140 by 95 ft, the range the drawn provincial cities give: a GUESS, since history puts these offices inside the governor's yamen rather than in compounds of their own. Minami draws each ministry at 114 by 78 ft, Nagahara and Tango at 130 by 90 ft, and Tango its Ministry of Rites at 140 by 95 ft.
  • A ministry office holds a hall of business, its bureaus' and clerks' rooms and a document storehouse, and no treasury or granary.
    A ministry worked through its bureaus, and the things it managed - a mint, treasuries, granaries - were offices of their own under it.
  • A provincial city's ministries stand around the governor's compound.
    Where they stand, and how they front the street, is set with the compound (How our maps place and draw the governor's compound (yamen)).

A domain capital is larger, and its government ward stands outside the castle.

  • A domain capital's six ministries stand in a government ward outside the castle's outermost gate, flanking the approach avenue (ote-suji): a deliberate deviation.
    A Japanese domain kept its offices in the lord's palace; our maps take the Chinese capital's arrangement instead, for three reasons that reinforce one another:
    • It is where both traditions go as a government grows: Beijing's ministries flanked the approach to the palace, outside its walls, and Matsumoto moved its county and town offices out of the castle into the town when the palace proved too small. Where so much of a capital is reconstruction, the answer both traditions point to is the one taken.
    • It makes the castle's front the map's axis, as the castle town's main roads ran to the castle's front (Domain capitals: the daimyo's castle town (jokamachi)). A ceremonial avenue lined with paired ministry compounds is the plainest way for a map to say the lord's seat rather than merely a large city.
    • It keeps the castle's interior implied, as the capital maps draw it, and the ministry compound is drawn the same way it is in a provincial city.
  • The domain school stands on the same avenue.
    A domain school was founded in the domain's own territory to educate its retainers' children, so our maps set it with the government it serves.
  • Is a capital's ministry office larger than a province's?
    This project's rule is that it is not. A larger city's government grows by the institutions it adds - the castle, the chancellery, the Imperial Magistrate's compound, the domain school, the granaries - and not by larger offices, since a ministry at any level is a bureau of clerks and papers. A capital's ministry is drawn at 224 by 148 ft, a size the rule took to be the provincial one; the provincial cities as drawn give theirs 110 by 80 to 140 by 95 ft, about a third of that area, so the drawn sizes do not yet follow the rule.
  • All six ministries of a city are the same size.
    This project reads the six ministers as peers of one rank, so their offices take peer form, as a county yamen's six rooms were alike, two bays each. A city where one ministry plainly outbuilds the others - a Ministry of War swollen by a border command, a Ministry of Revenue fat on a boom - would be saying something about that city, so it is drawn only as a declared exception for that city, with its reason written down, never as the default.
  • A capital's ministry captions sit on the compound; a provincial city's sit beside it: a map drawing convention.
    On a capital map each caption sits on the compound in near-black, in two stacked lines, "Ministry of" over the ministry's name - as an estate's caption sits on its own compound - which a 224 by 148 ft compound drawn at 3 ft to the pixel has room for. A provincial city's compound stands in tighter streets, so its caption stays beside it.
