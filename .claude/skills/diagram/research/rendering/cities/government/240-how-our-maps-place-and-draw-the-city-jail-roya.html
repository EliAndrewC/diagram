How our maps place and draw the city jail (roya)

Two forms of city jail are attested, and they differ in where the jail stands: inside the governor's compound, as in China, or in a compound of its own, as in a Japanese castle town. Each city's map takes one of the two, rolled from its seed - a knob. Either way the jail belongs to the city's Ministry of Justice, which runs the setting's jails.

  • In the first form, the jail is a walled court at the southwest of the governor's compound, outside its ceremonial gate.
    That is where a Chinese yamen kept its jail. The court holds an inner ward and an outer ward, with a walled women's ward beside the outer, as a Qing department or county jail did. How the rest of the compound is drawn is at How our maps place and draw the governor's compound (yamen).
  • How big is the jail court inside the governor's compound?
    Its size is a GUESS: no page we read gives the area of a prefectural jail.
  • In the second form, the jail is a compound of its own on a samurai residence plot, near the Ministry of Justice.
    It takes the plot's size, as the Hagi jails were built on the sites of two confiscated residences. The ministry's office is drawn as How our maps draw the offices of the six ministries (liubu) describes.
  • In the second form, a holding cell is kept in the governor's compound for the day a case is heard.
    Edo's North town magistracy kept such a temporary cell beside its court.
  • A provincial city's jail is never drawn at the scale of Edo's Tenmachō.
    Tenmachō, the shogunate's largest jail, covered about 8,850 square meters (~95,000 sq ft), a size for the shogun's own capital, not a provincial city.
