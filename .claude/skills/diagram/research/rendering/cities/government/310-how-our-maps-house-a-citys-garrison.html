How our maps house a city's garrison

Our maps give a provincial city's garrison barracks, as the setting's own city has, and draw no castle town's quarter of foot soldiers' row-houses. These are the rules a map follows when it draws them.

The rule the map follows:

  • Where a city's barracks stand is chosen at random for each city.
    Both of the two ways a Chinese city housed its standing troops are drawn. One is a walled garrison quarter in one corner of the city, laid out in rows of barracks with its own drill ground, stables and armory, as the Qing's banner garrisons lived; the other is barracks flanking the drill ground at a gate, as at Fuzhou. Both are attested and neither was the rule, so neither is our maps' only form.
  • A city large enough to have districts may spread its barracks one to a district.
    That is how the setting's own city houses its garrison.
  • Each gate carries its guard post, with the guard commander's house beside it.
    At Fukui the head of the company that held a gate had their official residence beside it.
  • The armory and the powder store are not drawn in the city.
    They stand inside the castle or the governor's compound, whose interior the city map leaves to that compound's own plan (How our maps draw the castle in a capital).
  • A capital may add a powder magazine outside its wall, on a main road.
    The one such store found in the sources was the shogun's, on a military highway out of Edo.
  • Our maps draw no quarter of foot soldiers' row-houses.
    A castle town's foot soldiers lived in blocks of their own at its edge, often at its entrances, but they were samurai of the lowest rank. The setting's foot soldiers are peasants, levies who live as the peasants they are, so they have no claim on a samurai foot soldiers' quarter, and the city's garrison lives in its barracks.
  • Ranked small houses, if a map draws them, stand outside the samurai ward.
    The edge of the town is where uniform small dwellings in ranks belonged, never inside the fence of the samurai's ward, where a samurai household's servants are drawn only against the household they serve (How our maps draw servants' quarters in the samurai ward).
