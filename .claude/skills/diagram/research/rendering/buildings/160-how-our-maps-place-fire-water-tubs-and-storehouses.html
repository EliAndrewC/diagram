How our maps place fire-water tubs and storehouses

On a compound plan our maps draw about eight to twelve fire-water tubs, one at each major wooden building and two at the kitchen, and none at the earth-walled storehouses. An office's records storehouse is drawn as an earth-walled storehouse (kura) of about 450 sq ft at most. The tubs are drawn only on building plans: a town or city map shows its fire defense as a watchtower instead.

  • Why put the tubs where the fire risk is?
    Standing water was kept at the wooden buildings, at the entrance and on the roof, and the Forbidden City's vats stood wherever a timber hall was far from water. So the maps give the flammable wooden buildings the tubs, and the kitchen, whose open cooking fire burns all day, the most. No page read ranks the buildings for fire water; the ranking is this project's reasoning.
  • About eight to twelve tubs: one at each major wooden building and two at the kitchen.
    A token pair of tubs clustered at one storehouse would be too few for a tub at each building.
  • The plastered storehouse keeps no tub.
    The plastered storehouse was the fire-resistant building, where valuables went in a fire, so a tub there would defend the one building made not to burn.
  • A wooden granary may keep one.
    Unlike the plastered storehouse, a vented wooden granary can burn, so one tub may stand at it to cover its part of the yard.
  • How big is a tub?
    About 2.5 ft across, a GUESS: no page read gives a rain tub's size.
  • The tubs are drawn on compound plans only.
    A town or city map draws its buildings as footprints and no fixture of a single building - no house's well, privy, hearth or tub; it keeps even the rain barrels feeding a well off the map, showing only the shared wellhead. A building's fire water is presumed there, not missing. A tub is smaller than the line that would draw it at that scale, so tubs are not scattered onto a settlement map.
  • A town or city map shows fire defense as a watchtower.
    Walled towns and cities carry the communal fire-watch tower by default (How did a dense wooden city watch for fire?): the tubs fight a fire at the building, the tower spots it for the town.
  • How big is a county office's records storehouse?
    No larger than Takayama's. Takayama's book storehouse, the one records store measured, has a floor of 41.46 m² (~446 sq ft), and it served an office that governed a whole province, so a county office's was no larger - this project's reading.
  • What shape is a county office's records storehouse?
    About 24 by 18 ft, a GUESS: no page read gives a records storehouse's length and depth.
  • The records storehouse the plans draw now is too big.
    The plans draw it about 32 to 36 ft long, larger than Takayama's; a storehouse that size matches the office's kitchen storehouse of 68.82 m² (~741 sq ft), not its records store.

The rule the map follows: a county office's records storehouse is an earth-walled kura with a floor of no more than about 450 sq ft - accurate as a ceiling set by the one measured office; its proportions (for instance about 24 by 18 ft) are a guess.
