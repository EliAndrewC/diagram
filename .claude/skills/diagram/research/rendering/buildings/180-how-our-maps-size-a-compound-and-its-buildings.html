How our maps size a compound and its buildings

Our maps give every building of a compound a ceiling on its size, and check the order of the buildings as well. A compound in which every building is plausible on its own can still show an impossible order - a kitchen as large as the house it serves, a stable larger than the barracks, a shrine hall larger than the residence - so the order has a check of its own.

A compound is also judged by how much of its walled ground its buildings cover, since a courtyard compound is meant to be mostly open.

  • The office hall is the largest hall, the residence next, and the staff's rowhouse below the residence.
    That is the order of the one intendant's office measured building by building, at Takayama, so it is historically accurate. The office hall may out-size the residence because it is not a domestic building but the office's working core.
  • The records store and the other small storehouses stand well below the rowhouse.
    At Takayama the book storehouse and the kitchen storehouse are a small fraction of the rowhouse.
  • The grain storehouse is ranked by how much rice its posting holds.
    At a county office that ships its rice on, it stays below the residence. At a posting that stores a wide territory's rice it may be the largest building in the compound, as Takayama's rice store was. So the maps do not hold every storage building below the residence. Tying the rank to the rice held is a GUESS from the one office measured.
  • The residence is the largest domestic building.
    No kitchen, bath or shrine hall out-sizes it, and a kitchen stays a fraction of the living quarters. The order among these buildings is this project's reading, a GUESS: no page we read ranks them.
  • The stable stays below the barracks.
    Its place in the order is a GUESS: no page we read says where a stable ranked among an office's buildings.
  • How much of a compound's walled ground do its buildings cover?
    About 37-42%, the rest courtyard. This is this project's own measurement of plans, a GUESS: no page we read gives a jin'ya's built area.
  • A compound that looks empty is not shrunk.
    At about 37% cover a compound is not oversized: its walls stay where they are, within the range of sizes the size audit allows, from the small Joge branch office up to Takayama, and shrinking them is the wrong fix.
  • A plan that looks sparse is fixed by gathering its buildings together.
    A real jin'ya gathered its rooms into a few connected buildings - Takayama's entrance, examination room, office and great hall are one office, its storehouses one numbered run - where a schematic plan tends to scatter a dozen separate labeled pavilions with loose ground between them. The maps tighten that loose ground and leave the rest open.
  • Two wooden service buildings on a shared edge abut, or stand about 6-8 ft apart.
    Not 12-16 ft apart. A plaster storehouse (kura) keeps a fire gap of about 6-10 ft. Both gaps are this project's figures, a GUESS: no page we read gives a spacing rule.
  • The forecourt, the hearing court, the garden and the ground between the courts stay open.
    They are features of the plan, not slack to be filled.
  • An empty stretch kept as a feature must be measured, not only named.
    A loading apron for carts and draft animals is taken as about 15-20 ft wide, a GUESS: no page we read gives an apron's width. A stretch wider than its purpose is slack, whatever it is called.
  • The packing is judged part by part, not only over the whole compound.
    A sparse quarter can hide inside a compound whose overall cover is in the band.
  • Our maps keep buildings a little further apart than a real jin'ya did.
    Each building stays a distinct, labeled footprint so the map can be read, but the gaps are held to the width of a fire gap. This is a deliberate deviation, for legibility.
  • Ochiba's and Hayakawa's compounds cover 38% and 35% of their ground.
    Every building is at its audited size, and both fall between Takayama and Joge. Hayakawa's west column of service buildings - the archive, the clerks' room and the stables - stands at fire gaps of 9 and 6.7 ft. Ochiba's cell and barracks form one guard range below the granary, with a 16 ft loading apron beside it.
