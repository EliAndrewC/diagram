How our maps decide where an ancestral alcove is drawn

On our building plans of a magistrate's compound, the ancestral alcove is drawn inside the residence, at its formal end by the reception room, and only at a posting that one lineage holds from generation to generation. A house kept the tablets of its own heads, and no page we read puts the tablets of predecessors in office at an office or its residence, so the alcove holds past magistrates only where they were the present one's kin.

  • The alcove stands at the formal end of the residence, in one of two places - a knob.
    Both are attested for houses: beside the tokonoma of the zashiki, or in a small room of its own behind the zashiki or the inner room. Each plan takes one. The farmhouse form, the altar built out beyond a room, is not drawn at a magistrate's residence.
  • The alcove is never drawn among the family's private rooms.
    No page we read sets a butsuma there, so the inner rooms at the far end of the residence hold none.
  • An alcove holding past magistrates' tablets is drawn only at a posting one lineage holds.
    In this setting some provinces are held by one provincial lineage across generations. Where that lineage holds a magistracy, the past magistrates are the present one's forebears and the alcove is ancestral in the plain sense. The Hayakawa plan is such a posting, held father to son, and its guest-wing annex "added by the 9th magistrate" shows the same long tenure.
  • A posting filled by appointment is drawn with no ancestral alcove.
    Its past holders are not the incumbent's kin, and the one hall for past officials we found, China's shrine of eminent officials, stood at the Confucian temple, not inside the office. The Ochiba plan, whose relics and duties pass with the office rather than a family, and the example county magistracy, a freshly appointed office, have none.
  • Where a post passes cousin to cousin within one lineage, the alcove is drawn as the lineage's.
    The Ubame plan, in a province one lineage holds, labels its alcove a lineage alcove, holding the tablets of the kin who held the post before. A house keeps the tablets of its own line, and the research's GUESS is that a cousin heading a branch house would keep only their own line's; so tablets of kin who were not the house's own forebears are this project's deliberate extension, beyond what the record shows.
