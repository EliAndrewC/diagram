How our maps draw holding cells

Our maps give a county magistracy a small cell, where one or two of the accused wait for their hearing, and no prison block. The jails of the period held the accused until judgment, and a magistracy that judged cases kept a temporary cell inside its own compound for the prisoners called before its court, so ours does too. It is drawn about 12 by 10 ft in the office's outer court, with bars across its front.

  • A magistracy keeps a cell, not a prison block.
    Prison as a sentence was imposed only by exception, so the rule does not rest on the rare sentences of confinement: a county cell holds its prisoners only until their hearing. That is accurate.
  • How many of the accused does a cell hold?
    One or two, on our maps. The count is this project's GUESS: a county office holds only a few until their hearing, where each cell room of the great jails was shared by several.
  • How big is a cell on our maps?
    About 12 by 10 ft, a GUESS at the small end of the single cell rooms measured, which run from Osaka's 6 mats (~12 by 9 ft) to Tenmachō's 18 (~18 by 18 ft). No page we read gives the size of a magistracy's temporary cell. A county cell belongs at the small end because it holds only a few until their hearing.
  • A cell drawn at 23 by 17 ft would be larger than any single cell room measured.
    At about 22 mats, it would be exceeded only by the commoners' great cell of 30 mats.
  • A cell may stand anywhere in the compound.
    Chinese county offices set their jails to the south or on the west side by rule, but this setting keeps no such rule, so a map places its cell wherever the compound's plan wants it: a deliberate deviation.
  • Our maps draw no room built for interrogation.
    In this setting torture is unusual, though not strictly forbidden, so a magistracy keeps no room purpose-built for it, and questioning happens in the day office or the hearing court like any other business: a deliberate deviation. Edo justice tortured a suspect who kept refusing to confess, though never in the hearing court, and at Takayama, whose examination room was roofed like its court, the torture is reported to have been done in the jail in the town.
