How our maps draw a samurai's country manor

A samurai's country manor on our maps stands apart from any village, beside its own fields, as the setting has it. The manor drawn so far is about 1 acre, which is smaller than every moated residence measured in the record: those run from about 1.2 acres to about 3, and far more for a great house.

The rule the map follows:

  • A manor's form is a knob rolled per manor: moated or garden.
    Both forms are recorded - the medieval moated residence and the Edo-period garden estate - so each manor rolls one.
  • The moated form is a near-square enclosure of about 1.2 to 3 acres, about 230 to 360 ft a side.
    Its size is rolled along that range, the range the measured residences give. It stands inside a moat and an earthen bank, with one gate. Inside are the master's house at the center, a stable, a storehouse and a row of servants' houses, and before the gate the manor's own paddy.
  • The garden form has no moat, and its size is a guess.
    It is a walled house with a garden, vegetable plots and a bamboo grove. No page we read gives the size of such an estate kept by a single samurai family, so it is kept at the drawn 1 acre, a GUESS, until a source is found.
