How our maps draw the office hall and the clerks' rooms

Our maps draw a county magistrate's office hall as one long building in the office's outer court, with a floor of about 1,600 to 6,750 sq ft, and the clerks' room as a room inside it, beside the day office. The clerks have no building of their own at the office, to work or to live in.

  • How big is the office hall on our maps?
    Between about 1,600 and 6,750 sq ft. That is always well below the 8,100 sq ft of a province's intendancy office, and above a small branch office's whole central building of about 2,850 sq ft only at its top end. The ceiling is accurate, calibrated against the two measured offices; the floor, below the smaller of them, is a GUESS.
  • How long and deep is the office hall?
    80 to 150 ft long and 20 to 45 ft deep, a GUESS: no page we read gives an office hall's length and depth.
  • The clerks' room is a room of the office hall, beside the day office.
    That follows the record: at the Takayama Jin'ya, the workroom of the officials hired from the district was partitioned off beside the room where the shogunate's own officials worked.
  • The clerks have no building of their own at the office.
    No page we read gives the clerks a building of their own to work in; what an office's staff had apart was a house, in huts and row houses inside the compound. This setting's clerks live in town and come in each day, so our maps give them no housing at the office either, and a clerks' building of their own there would be a GUESS.
