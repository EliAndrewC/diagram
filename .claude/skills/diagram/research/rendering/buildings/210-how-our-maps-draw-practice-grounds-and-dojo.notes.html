• hagi-meirinkan-guide - 「The Yubikan moved and enlarged the fencing hall and spear hall of the old Meirinkan: a wooden single-story building, hip-and-gable, roofed in pantile, a building long north to south, 37.8 m along the ridge and 10.8 m across. Inside, the north half is a board-floored 39-mat fencing hall and the south half an earth-floored 54-mat spear hall, the west side of each being the lord's viewing place, and between them is a waiting room used on such occasions as the lord's attendance. The Yubikan was, besides the domain's samurai's martial practice, the hall for bouts with trainees in the sword and spear from other domains - that is, the 'fencing and spear hall receiving trainees from other domains'.」 (translated; )

  • dojo-jawiki - 「Practice was often held outdoors or on an earthen floor, but from the mid-Edo period onward, because bamboo-sword practice in swordsmanship and randori practice in jujutsu became central, board-floored and tatami practice halls came to be provided. In populous places such as Edo - the three great dojos of Edo, for example - the entrants grew many, and the small private practice rooms of before changed into large practice halls. In each domain, too, many domains attached a practice hall to the domain school. The practice hall of this time had a floor from which the master watched over the pupils' practice, and there hung scrolls bearing the names of two gods from Japanese myth held to be 'gods of the sword, gods of arms': Kashima Daijingu (Takemikazuchi) and Katori Daimyojin (Futsunushi).」 (translated; )
