• oshirasu-jawiki - 「The oshirasu of a magistracy either had a roof built over it, or an indoor earth floor spread with gravel was used as the oshirasu; it was not the scene of open-air white gravel seen in period dramas and the like.」 (translated; ) and 「In the oldest times the earth floor was used as it was; it was later that it became a floor of white gravel.」 (translated; )
  • takayama-gh-shirasu - 「The province of Hida has no sea, and not enough white sand for spreading in the oshirasu could be had. So cobbles from the riverbed were spread in its place.」 (translated; ) and 「as for the Takayama Jin'ya's oshirasu in particular, Hida is snow country, so for the practical reason that without a roof it would be buried in snow in winter and become unusable, the oshirasu was made inside the building.」 (translated; ) (the words of the jin'ya's guide, interviewed)
  • takayama-jinya-jawiki - 「The gundai office was rebuilt in 1816 (Bunka 13) in such parts as the entrance hall, the examination room, the government office and the great hall.」 (translated; ) (the examination room is named as one part of the gundai office)
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「According to a floor plan of the Edo South Town Magistracy made in Bunka 7 (1810), on the topmost level was a tatami room called the "kujiba", where the officials, the town magistrate first among them, sat, while on the lowest level was a "jarijiki" (gravel floor), and the plaintiffs, the defendants and others sat on the straw mats spread on it.」 (translated; )
  • shirasu-kotobank - 「Those who spread mushiro on the shirasu in the narrow sense (also called the gravel) and sat in the open were peasants, townsmen, ronin and the like.」 (translated; ) (Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia, which seats ronin of good lineage on the lower veranda) and 「The accused peasants, townsmen and lesser ronin sat on mushiro spread on it.」 (translated; ) (MyPedia)
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「The suspect was usually made to sit at the center of the shirasu, roped, together with a servant holding the end of the rope, with the soles of the feet down.」 (translated; ) and 「Behind the suspect on the left, the town officials, the town headmen and the landlords sat on komo matting, and behind on the right sat the plaintiff.」 (translated; )
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「The "kujiba" was partitioned into three rooms; at the very front was a board veranda of 3 shaku, and in its middle a stair of three steps, 1 ken wide.」 (translated; ) and 「In the innermost of the three rooms sat the town magistrate, in the room one in front of it the examining yoriki and the clerk doshin, in the room nearest the shirasu the witnessing doshin, and at the left and right of the shirasu the crouching doshin, each in attendance.」 (translated; )
  • shirasu-kotobank - 「Further, on the uwadori of the upper level sat the examiners and the scribes, and the magistrate was seated in the room of the topmost level.」 (translated; ) (Heibonsha's concise encyclopedia, MyPedia) and 「There are also magistracies that set apart a further upper room (uwadori), but the magistrate sat in the room of the topmost level.」 (translated; ) (Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia, Jinbo Fumio)
