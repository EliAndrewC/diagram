How our maps draw shrines kept inside government offices

A magistrate's compound, the walled office of a jin'ya in Japan or a yamen in China, kept a place to worship its gods inside its walls, as a household kept its own god in a corner of its lot. Every compound our maps draw has one modest shrine, in a corner of its grounds beside a tree. That an office had a shrine follows both offices the record holds; which god it serves by default is a guess, and so is any garden planted around it.

  • Every compound our maps draw has a shrine inside its walls.
    A shrine is accurate for a magistrate's office down to the smallest: the branch post at Joge, with three officials, had shrines inside its walls. A Chinese county yamen kept a courtyard for worshiping the god of the land and the god of the office.
  • Our maps draw one modest shrine, however many a real office kept.
    Real offices kept more than one shrine or god - the Joge post an Inari shrine, a Tenjin shrine and others, a Chinese county yamen a courtyard for worshiping both the god of the land and the god of the office - but what holds across them is that every office had a shrine, so a map draws one, a deliberate deviation.
  • Which god does a compound's shrine serve by default?
    Inari: an Inari shrine was among the Joge post's. Making it the default is a GUESS resting on that one post. No yamen we read kept an Inari; Neixiang's courtyard served the god of the land and the god of the office.
  • Ochiba's shrine serves two kami, a deliberate deviation for its priest-magistrate.
    Ochiba's magistrate is also a priest, so its shrine serves a second kami. Keeping two or more kami in one shrine hall has a name of its own, aidono, and a second kami could instead keep a shrine of its own inside the precinct, as a shrine moved into another's grounds did; which form Ochiba's takes is rolled from the map's seed, as set out at When one shrine hall serves several kami, does each have its own altar?
  • Why does the shrine stand in a corner, beside a tree?
    A household's god was kept in a corner of its lot, in a small shrine or at an old tree or a stone taken as the god's seat. Our maps set the compound's shrine where a household's stood, and give it the tree as well.
  • A garden laid out around the shrine is a GUESS.
    A map may plant ornamental ground around its shrine, but no page we read describes a garden around a household's shrine.
