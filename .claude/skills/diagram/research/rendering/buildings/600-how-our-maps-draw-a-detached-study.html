How our maps draw a detached study

A magistrate on our building plans may have a writing pavilion: a small study standing apart in the inner garden, a room of their own set away from both the office hall, where the county's business is done, and the family's house.

  • A study standing apart in a garden is a recorded form.
    A scholar's estate in Kyoto had one, built in 1828 with its garden on its west side, and a Chinese compound set its study in a quiet part of the house, beside the rear garden. So the pavilion's form is accurate.
  • Giving one to a magistrate, in the inner garden of the posting, is a GUESS.
    It is an extension from the scholar's study and the Chinese one: no page we read describes a detached study at a magistrate's or any other official's compound.
  • The pavilion is drawn about 18 by 14 ft, a GUESS.
    No page we read gives a detached study's size.
  • At Ubame the pavilion stands at the inner garden's west edge, before the magistrate's quarters.
    The kitchen garden is beside it.
