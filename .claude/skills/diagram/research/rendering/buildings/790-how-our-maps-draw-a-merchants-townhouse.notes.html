• okeihan-kyomachiya - 「The plan of the machiya has changed little by little with the times, but most have as their basis the 'one row, three rooms' type, in which a long, narrow earth floor called the 'toriniwa' is lined by three rooms, the 'shop room', the 'kitchen' and the 'back room'.」 (translated; ); of the tsubo-niwa, 「A garden of about one tsubo set at the back or the middle of the building. It serves both for ventilation and for light」 (translated; ); of the back room, 「The formal zashiki, farthest in.」 (translated; ) (the page speaks of Kyoto's townhouses)
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「At the back of the lot were placed detached rooms, outbuildings, storehouses and the like. In Kyoto it was the rule that the storehouse was never built on the street side but set at the back of the lot, the very opposite of Edo's storehouse-built machiya. The storehouses standing at the backs of the lots made the boundary of the block and at the same time worked as a kind of firebreak.」 (translated; )
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「Many machiya were built in provincial castle towns, post towns, ports, temple-gate towns, country market towns and the like too, and there too machiya peculiar to the region arose. Their plans divide into Kyoto's through-earth-floor type, Edo's front earth floor made a little wider, and a fusion of the two. In regions near Kyoto the Kyoto through-earth-floor type is common, in regions farther off the front-earth-floor type and the fused type.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-omoteyazukuri - 「One of the forms of machiya often seen in Kyoto. The shop is built on the part facing the road and the dwelling behind it as separate buildings; the two buildings are separated by an inner garden and joined by a narrow entrance building.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-machiya - 「many take the form with a low second story used as a storeroom」 (translated; ) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, by Suzuki Mitsuru)
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「Throughout the early modern period the second floor of the Kyoto machiya did not develop much except in large merchant houses, and the mezzanine 'zushi-nikai' was usual」 (translated; ); 「The second floor was used as a storeroom and as rooms for live-in servants」 (translated; ); 「At first the second floor was built only on the street side, in the form of a 'tsushi-nikai' with low eaves」 (translated; ); 「As time went on it changed to a full two stories, the ground and upper floors nearly equal in area, and the second floor was used as guest rooms.」 (translated; )
