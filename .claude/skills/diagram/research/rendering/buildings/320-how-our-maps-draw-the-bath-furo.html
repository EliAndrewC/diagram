How our maps draw the bath (furo)

On our building plans a posting's bath is a room of the residence or a small addition to it, never a pavilion standing apart, since no page we read shows a samurai compound's bath as a building of its own. A house of middle rank or below may have none, as most such samurai washed from a tub or went to the public bath.

  • How do our maps place the bath?
    On the house's service side, as a small addition abutting the kitchen. The Takayama residence listed its earth floor and kitchen, its well and its bath together, and around 1600 a warrior family's bath stood in a corner of the doma; setting the bath with the doma, the kitchen and the well is this project's reading of the two. On the magistrate's compound our maps generate, it abuts the kitchen's face toward its court, and never stands in a garden or a court, where it would be the pavilion the record does not find.
  • The kitchen's well serves the bath too.
    A kitchen well may stand as far as 20 ft out, past the bath, as the Takayama residence's bath stood with its well and kitchen.
  • A fire-water tub stands at the bath's yard face.
    A bath's hearth was a fire risk, so a tub of water stands ready beside it.
  • The bath is drawn 10 by 8 ft - a GUESS.
    No page we read gives a bath's size. It is drawn small so that the whole house keeps to the size of the residence our maps draw (How our maps lay out samurai residences and their rooms (buke yashiki)).
