How our maps draw the kitchen

On our building plans a residence's kitchen is board-floored, with a small earth-floored doma at its edge, and it cooks on a kamado. It has one door to the outside, and the family comes and goes by the residence's own inner entrance. The kitchen's size is at How our maps lay out samurai residences and their rooms (buke yashiki).

  • A residence's kitchen cooks on a kamado, never a sunken hearth.
    A samurai's kitchen cooked on a kamado. The sunken hearth is the common house's, and no page we read puts one in a samurai kitchen, so drawing one there would be a GUESS, and our plans do not.
  • Where the kamado stands is a knob between two forms.
    It stands on the small doma at the kitchen's edge, the rule for houses of the period, or on the kitchen's board floor as a movable kamado on legs. Each plan takes one.
  • The kamado on the board floor is a GUESS by analogy.
    The legged kamado on the boards is shown in a commoner's tenement kitchen of the Edo period, as a museum reproduces it. In a samurai house it rests on a house-builder's history and a museum's reproduction, both modern, and no earlier page we could read puts it there.
  • The kitchen has one door to the outside, on its doma - a GUESS.
    The kitchen door is recorded, but no page we read places it on the doma, and that there was only one rests on no page showing two. On the magistrate's compound our maps generate, it opens west, onto the way to the kitchen's yard.
  • The family's everyday door is the residence's inner entrance, not the kitchen's.
    The inner entrance was made for the family and those of the house to come and go by. On the generated compound it opens on the residence's face toward the kitchen.
  • The map draws the kitchen door but does not say who came in by it.
    That those of lower status came in by it is found only on the modern plan of a fictional house, and tradesmen at the kitchen door only of houses today; no page from before 1868 we could read says who used it.
  • A second outside door on a kitchen, or a route by which food reached the rooms, is a GUESS wherever a plan draws one.
    No page we read draws either.
