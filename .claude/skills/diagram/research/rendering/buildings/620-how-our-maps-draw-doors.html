How our maps draw doors

A door on our building plans is a small dark block set on the wall it opens through, and its width is a drawing convention, not a measurement. The working doors are drawn two to three times the width of a real ordinary door, and wider than a farmhouse's one-ken main door, so that they read at the sheet's scale.

  • How wide are the doors on the three magistracy sheets?
    From about 3.3 ft to 10 ft. The kitchen doors and the doors of the lodgings, the barracks and the servants' rows are mostly about 8 to 8.7 ft, and Hayakawa's servants' door 5.3 ft. The storehouse doors are about 4.7 ft. The chief retainer's side door is 3.3 ft at Ochiba and Ubame but 8.7 ft at Hayakawa, and Hayakawa's guest-quarters entry 10 ft. The writing pavilion's door is 6 ft and each parley-room door about 7.3 ft.
  • The real widths are about 3 ft for an ordinary door and 6 ft for a farmhouse's main door.
    An ordinary door was up to half a ken, about 3 ft - this project's reading of how the big door was defined, as a door wider than half a ken. A farmhouse's main sliding door, with a wicket set in it, was one ken, about 6 ft.
  • A plan drawn by the generator gives each lodging block a door 6 ft wide.
    It is the same convention, set flush inside the building's face as the hand-drawn sheets draw one, and 1.33 ft deep, the ink the hand-drawn sheets give a door.
  • A storehouse's paired leaves are accurate for the Edo period; their drawn width is a convention.
    Paired plastered leaves are attested at the end of the Edo period, but their width is known only as today's standard of about 3.5 ft each. No drawn storehouse door is set from that figure, and the drawn width stays a convention with no premodern size behind it.
  • A door glyph sits flush to the wall it opens through.
    A door is an opening in a wall, so a program checks every plan for a small dark door block inside a building, close to one of its walls but not touching it, and flags it. The check reads only the dark door color, so a storehouse's single door is checked, while a raised granary's stilts, which straddle its wall, and a kitchen hearth, too big to be a door, are not flagged.
