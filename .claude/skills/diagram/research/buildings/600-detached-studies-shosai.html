Detached studies (shosai)

A study (shosai) was a room in a private house for reading and writing, and in older houses it could be a building of its own. A scholar's detached study survives in Kyoto, and in China the study drew apart from the living rooms into a quiet corner of the compound, beside its garden.

  • A study was whichever room or building held the built-in writing desk.
    That room or building, the one with the tsuke-shoin, was the study, and was called a shoin or a gakumonsho, "place of learning"; either could be a room or a building for the purpose.
  • Rai San'yō's detached study, built late in the Edo period, survives in Kyoto.
    Rai San'yō was a Confucian scholar, poet and historian of the late Edo period. In 1828 he built a new study-cum-tea room, Sanshi Suimei-sho, on his Kyoto estate. The estate passed out of the family after his death, and by about the middle of the Meiji period its other buildings were lost, so the study alone stands. Its garden is on its west side; there is none to its east.

Chinese houses moved the study apart as well.

  • In China the study drew apart from the living rooms, into a quiet part of the compound beside the rear garden.
    Over time it took on a function and a layout of its own. It generally stood in a quiet part of the house compound, and where there was a rear garden, always next to it.
