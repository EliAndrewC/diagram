• shirobito-612-omote-oku - 「In the "omote" were placed the entrance, the hiroma, the shoin and so on, and audiences with retainers and envoys, events and ceremonies were held there.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「「表」には玄関、広間、書院などが配置され、家臣や使者との対面、行事や儀式などが行われていました。」)
  • shirobito-612-omote-oku - 「The living room where the castle lord did his work by day, the bedroom and so on were located behind the omote palace, and were called the "naka-oku".」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「城主が日中に執務をする居間や寝室などは表御殿の後方に位置しており、「中奥」と呼ばれていました。」) and 「Incidentally, the word naka-oku is not, strictly, a historical term, and in the drawings of the Edo period and so on the shogun's office and bedroom are included in the omote palace.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「ちなみに、中奥という言葉は正確には歴史用語ではなく、江戸時代などの絵図では将軍の執務室や寝室は表御殿に含まれています。」)
  • shirobito-612-omote-oku - 「The "oku" held, besides the castle lord's private rooms, the residences of his principal wife and his consorts.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「「奥」は城主の私室の他、正妻や側室の住居がありました。」) and 「Because women of high status lived there, the coming and going of people was strictly limited」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「身分の高い女性達が住まうため、人の出入りは厳しく制限され」)
  • shoinzukuri-kotobank - 「The great complexes of halls were broadly divided into the omote-muki and the oku-muki, and the main buildings - the hiroma, taimenjo and goza-no-ma of the omote-muki, and the okami (the lady's palace) of the oku-muki - were alike in the developed shoin-zukuri form.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大規模な殿舎群は表向きと奥向きに大別され，表向きの広間，対面所，御座間と，奥向きの御上（奥方御殿）の主要建物は，ひとしく発達した書院造形式のものであった。」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Kawakami Mitsugu)
  • touken-world-buke-madori - 「First, from the "genkan-no-ma" there is the "hikae-no-ma", then come the "ima" and the "ni-no-ma", then the "zashiki" and the "oku-zashiki"; beside the ima is the kitchen, projecting to the east.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「まず、「玄関の間」から「控えの間」があり、「居間」と「二の間」と続き、「座敷」と「奥座敷」、居間の横には東側に突き出た台所があります。」) (the Kuchiba house at Hagi, of about 1,000 koku)
  • matsue-bukeyashiki - 「Master's living room: in contrast to the front of the main house, it is playfully built, with nageshi of menkawa (timber keeping the log's rounded corners) and nail covers in the shape of a bird (fukura-suzume).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「当主居間 主屋の表側とは対照的に、面皮(角に木の丸みを残した木材)の長押や鳥の形(ふくら雀)の釘隠しなど遊び心のある造りとなっています。」) and 「Zashiki: the room where the master receives guests, built with an eye to formality, with a shoin, straight-grained nageshi and nail covers bearing the nine-star crest.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「座敷 当主が来客を迎える部屋で、書院や柾目(平行な木目)の長押、九曜紋の釘隠しなど格式を重んじた造りになっています。」) and, among its other rooms, 「family room, wife's living room, tea room, butsuma」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「家族部屋 奥方居間 茶室 仏間」)
