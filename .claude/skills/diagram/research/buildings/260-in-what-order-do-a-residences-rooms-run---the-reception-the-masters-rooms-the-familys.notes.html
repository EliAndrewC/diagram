  • edojo-kotobank - 「The ooku (the great oku), where the shogun's children and the women in service lived around the midaidokoro, was the shogun's private residence; it was the building on the north side of the naka-oku, but it was partitioned off by a strict wall and joined only by the Osuzu corridor, and no man but the shogun could enter the ooku.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大奥は御台所を中心に将軍の子女や奥女中たちの生活する場所で，将軍の私邸にあたり，中奥の北側の建物であるが厳重な塀で仕切られ，御鈴廊下だけでつながっており，将軍以外の男子は大奥に入ることはできなかった。」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Murai Masuo)
  • shoinzukuri-kotobank - 「At the start of the early modern period, in the Keicho and Genna eras (1596-1624), the residences of warrior daimyo were built in many places.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世初頭の慶長・元和年間（1596-1624）には武家大名の邸宅が各地で造立された。」) and 「The great complexes of halls were broadly divided into the omote-muki and the oku-muki, and the main buildings - the hiroma, taimenjo and goza-no-ma of the omote-muki, and the okami (the lady's palace) of the oku-muki - were alike in the developed shoin-zukuri form.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大規模な殿舎群は表向きと奥向きに大別され，表向きの広間，対面所，御座間と，奥向きの御上（奥方御殿）の主要建物は，ひとしく発達した書院造形式のものであった。」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Kawakami Mitsugu)
