• shirobito-1717-takayama - 「The residence had rooms for the intendant or district intendant and family, a room for receiving guests, a garden, and also rooms for the yonin and maids who looked after them, an earth floor and kitchen, a well and a bath.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「役宅には代官・郡代とその家族の部屋、客人応接の部屋、庭のほか、彼らの身の回りの世話をする用人や女中の部屋、土間や台所、井戸や風呂もありました。」)
  • boso-no-mura-takei - 「It can be divided into living space used in daily life (ima, dining room, katte (kitchen), doma) and guest space (shikidai (genkan), yoritsuki (toritsugi), zashiki (guest room)).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「日頃の生活に利用する「居間」「食事室」「勝手（台所）」「土間」という居住空間と「式台（玄関）」「寄付（取次）」「座敷（客間）」という客用空間とに区別できます。」) and 「The Takei house is a single-story house in yosemune-zukuri built in the late Edo period, and the main house is made up of the doma, the katte (kitchen), the dining room, the living room, the yoritsuki (toritsugi) and the zashiki (guest room).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「武居家は，江戸時代後期に建築された寄棟造（よせむねづくり）の平屋建てで，主屋は土間・勝手(台所）・食事室・居間・寄付(取次）・座敷（客間）で構成されてます。」) and 「It is reproduced on the model of the Takei house, of a middle-rank samurai, in Miyakoji, Sakura City.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「佐倉市宮小路にある中級武士の武居家をモデルに再現しています。」)
  • honjin-jawiki - 「Used by daimyo, hatamoto, shogunate officials, imperial envoys, imperial-prince abbots and others.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大名や旗本、幕府役人、勅使、宮門跡らが利用した。」) and 「[Honjin] had a front gate, a genkan with a shikidai, and a jodan-no-ma, which ordinary inns were not allowed to build.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一般の旅籠屋には設置が許されていない表門、式台付玄関（しきだいつきげんかん）、上段の間を備えた。」) and, from the article's opening sentence on the honjin, 「a building in a post town, from the Edo period on, where persons of high status stayed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代以降の宿場で、身分が高い者が泊まった建物。」)
  • yakage-honjin - 「as honjin facilities it had guest-receiving facilities such as the o-zashiki with a jodan-no-ma and an onari-mon, and further the main house for the family's living and for running the family sake brewery, an inner storehouse, sake storehouses, a west storehouse and so on, more than ten buildings in all」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「本陣施設として上段の間を備えた御座敷、御成門などの迎客施設を整え、さらに家人の生活の場や家業の酒造業を営むための主屋、内倉、酒倉、西倉など、合わせて十数棟が建っており」)
