Every administrative compound keeps a shrine

Sources: fuchu-joge-pamphlet (the Jōge branch office and its two shrines), henan-neixiang (the yamen-god and earth-god shrines), neixiang-yamen-zhwiki (the jail-god shrine)

Even a Japanese branch office of three officials had two small shrines inside its walls, one of them an Inari shrine, on the drawing of it made in 1869 (What did the smallest branch office hold inside its walls?); a full Chinese county yamen, Neixiang's, had three (yamen-god, earth-god, jail-god). So the shrine is generic equipment, not a particular; what varies is scale and dedication. Ochiba's two-altar hall is the deliberate exception, justified by its priest-magistrate. The rule (a modest shrine, Inari default) does not depend on the count.
