Rooms for a parley across a border

Where two powers dealt with each other regularly across a border, each kept to its own ground. The forms recorded are two: a post on each side of the line, facing each other a short way apart, or a compound one side set aside for the other's officers, with its own reception hall beside it.

  • Was a room ever built across a border line, so that neither side stood on the other's ground?
    No page we read describes one, or two parties meeting on the line itself.
  • At Kyakhta, Russia and Qing China each built a post on its own side of the border.
    From 1727 Russia and China traded through Kyakhta, on the Mongolian border south of Lake Baikal. The Russians built their post at the border, and the Manchus built the fort of Maimaicheng a few hundred feet south, on their own side. Officials on both sides dealt with the diplomatic exchanges, the trade disputes and the police matters of the border, and much barter was done at Kyakhta.

Between Japan and Korea the arrangement was different: one side kept a compound on the other's ground.

  • In the Edo period, Tsushima's officers dealt with Korea from a compound at Choryang, the Japan House.
    The Japan House (Wakan) held the commander's house and the trade hall in its east section and inns in its west, and officers sent from Tsushima conducted diplomacy and trade there.
  • The Korean side's banquet hall and guest houses stood beside the Japan House.
    They were used to feast the envoys from Tsushima and to lodge the Korean receiving officers and interpreters.
