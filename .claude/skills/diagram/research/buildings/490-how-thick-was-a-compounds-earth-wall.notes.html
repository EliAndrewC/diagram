  • kojodan-dobei - 「the sill and the posts form the framework; between post and post, lath (komai) is tied, and earth is plastered onto it to finish the wall」 (translated; , of the 塗塀)
  • kojodan-dobei - 「because it has no framework, the wall's thickness seems to have been thinner than a tsuijibei, about 30 cm」 (translated; , of the 練塀)
  • kunishitei-toyonaga-neribei - 「A neribei of head-sized stones laid in red earth, 0.6 m wide at the base.」 (translated; ), 「1.6 m high overall」 (translated; ) and 「The top is capped with pantiles.」 (translated; ), from the entry for 「Toyonaga dye shop earthen wall」 (translated; ), dated 「late Edo」 (translated; )
  • kojodan-dobei - 「a form is made of wooden boards, a few cm of earth and sand is put into it and packed down with a rod」 (translated; ), 「doing this over and over is called hanchiku (rammed earth)」 (translated; ) and「repeating this and removing the forms, an earthen wall as much as 1 m thick and 3 m high is completed」 (translated; , of the 築地塀)
  • mlit-kanazawa-dobei - 「Tsuchi-kabe were constructed with a “rammed earth” technique that resulted in a heavy, solid wall.」 and 「The ashigaru or foot soldiers, for example, were not permitted walls at all, and instead used hedges. Samurai of middle rank (heishi) or above, however, surrounded their residences with earthen walls (tsuchi-kabe).」 (the sign's English, of which the Japanese on the same page is a translation)
