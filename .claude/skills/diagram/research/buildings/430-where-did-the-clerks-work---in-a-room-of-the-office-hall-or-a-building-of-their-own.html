Where did the clerks work - in a room of the office hall, or a building of their own?

Sources: mapple-takayama-jinya (the Takayama office's workrooms: the day office of the local officials and the writing room), edo-ashigaru-bugyosho (two sections' duty rooms in the Edo town magistracy's court quarter), jinya-kotobank (the tedai housed in huts and row houses inside an intendant's compound)

At the Takayama Jin'ya the clerks worked in rooms of the office. Beside the goyakusho, the room where the officials sent from the shogunate worked, and partitioned from it, was the goyoba, the workroom of the jiyakunin, officials hired mostly from the district. The kakiyaku-beya was a room used only for writing, where the documents sent to the shogunate were drawn up, and they were bound in the room beside it. At the Edo town magistracy the duty rooms of two of its sections, the shacho-sen'yokata and the reikurikata, stood in the same quarter of the compound as its court, among the rooms given to suits and examinations.

What stood apart as buildings of their own were the staff's houses: the tetsuki and tedai of an intendant's office lived in huts and row houses inside the compound. No page read gives the clerks a building of their own to work in.

What this means for a plan: a clerks' room inside the office hall, beside the day office, is ACCURATE. A freestanding clerks' building has no attested counterpart as a place of work; what a clerk had apart was a house, and our clerks live in the town (Clerks are few, local, and heimen), so a building of their own at the office is a GUESS.
