Privies attach to the house; night-soil drives their placement

Sources: tajima-2007-night-soil (the annual contract paid in rice or cash; farmers paying the estates in kind; how far the trade reached), guernica-night-soil (night soil as a good with a price; the carter at the tenement); kotobank-benjo, sinyoken-madori (the privy built into the house; the farm case). The sand box for inspection under the master's privy has no readable source

What the research found: the samurai-residence kawaya/setchin was built INTO the house (at the rear of the guest parlor; the pull-out sand box under the master's for health inspection is unsourced), not a free-standing block in the yard; and a well-appointed house had more than one (a guest privy by the reception suite, a family privy toward the back). Night-soil was a contracted, premium commodity - well-off farmers supplied the castle and the daimyo estates with firewood, charcoal and seedlings for the right to empty their privies - so collection was by outside carters emptying cesspits, which means pits sit toward a service edge/wall/gate for cart access - and, our rule rather than a finding, never so deep that a collector crosses the inner court. The trade stayed close: night soil moved within about 3 to 5 km of most towns and up to 20 km around Edo, by fertilizer boat where a canal allowed it. The decision: privy count scales with occupancy (~1 per functional zone, ~3-4 at a county manor), the residence privy attaches to the house with its cesspit to the rear/service wall, and servants'/outer privies line service walls near a gate.
