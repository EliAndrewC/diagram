• oshirasu-jawiki - 「According to a floor plan of the Edo South Town Magistracy made in Bunka 7 (1810), on the topmost level was a tatami room called the "kujiba", where the officials, the town magistrate first among them, sat, while on the lowest level was a "jarijiki" (gravel floor), and the plaintiffs, the defendants and others sat on the straw mats spread on it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「文化7年（1810年）作成の江戸南町奉行所の平面図によれば、最上段には町奉行をはじめとする役人が座る「公事場」と呼ばれる座敷が設けられており、対して最下段には「砂利敷」が設置され、その上に敷かれた莚に原告・被告らが座った。」)
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「The "kujiba" was partitioned into three rooms; at the very front was a board veranda of 3 shaku, and in its middle a stair of three steps, 1 ken wide.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「「公事場」は三間に仕切られ、一番手前は3尺の板縁、その中央に1間幅3段の階段が設けられていた。」) and 「In the innermost of the three rooms sat the town magistrate, in the room one in front of it the examining yoriki and the clerk doshin, in the room nearest the shirasu the witnessing doshin, and at the left and right of the shirasu the crouching doshin, each in attendance.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「三間の一番奥に町奉行が、一つ手前の間に吟味方与力と書役同心、一番白州に近い間に立合同心、白州の左右に蹲居同心がそれぞれ座して控えた」)
  • shirasu-kotobank - 「Further, on the uwadori of the upper level sat the examiners and the scribes, and the magistrate was seated in the room of the topmost level.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「さらに上段の上通り(うわどおり)に吟味役や書役が座り，奉行は最上段の座敷に着座した。」) (Heibonsha's concise encyclopedia, マイペディア) and 「There are also magistracies that set apart a further upper room (uwadori), but the magistrate sat in the room of the topmost level.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「さらに上段の間（上通（うわどおり））を区分して設ける奉行所もあるが，奉行は最上段の座敷に着座した。」) (the 世界大百科事典, Jinbo Fumio)
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「However, people of certain status such as samurai (excluding ronin), Shinto priests, monks and purveyor townsmen did not sit on the "jarijiki", but sat on the verandas of the room, divided into two steps.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「もっとも、武士（浪人を除く）や神官・僧侶・御用達町人などの特定の身分の人々は「砂利敷」には座らず、2段に分かれた座敷の縁側に座った。」) and 「Samurai, Shinto priests and monks sat on the upper veranda (the upper of the two verandas) and so were called uwamono; the others sat on the lower veranda (the lower of the two) and so were called shitamono.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「武士・神官・僧侶は上縁（2段のうちの上側の縁側）に座ることから上者、それ以外は下縁（2段のうちの下側の縁側）に座ったために下者と呼ばれた。」)
  • shirasu-kotobank - 「Those who spread mushiro on the shirasu in the narrow sense (also called the gravel) and sat in the open were peasants, townsmen, ronin and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「狭義の白洲（砂利ともいう）にむしろを敷いて露座するのは百姓，町人や浪人等である。」) (the 世界大百科事典, which seats ronin of good lineage on the lower veranda) and 「The accused peasants, townsmen and lesser ronin sat on mushiro spread on it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「被疑者の百姓・町人・下級浪人はその上にむしろを敷いて座った。」) (マイペディア)
  • shirasu-imidas - 「For commoners, mushiro were spread; samurai and monks who had committed crimes were made to sit on the board veranda.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平民の場合は、筵（むしろ）が敷かれ、罪を犯した武士や僧侶は板縁の上に座らされた。」)
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「The suspect was usually made to sit at the center of the shirasu, roped, together with a servant holding the end of the rope, with the soles of the feet down.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「罪が疑われる容疑者は、通常、白州中央に縄尻をかけられて、縄尻を持った下男と共に、足裏をつけて座らされた。」) and 「Behind the suspect on the left, the town officials, the town headmen and the landlords sat on komo matting, and behind on the right sat the plaintiff.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「容疑者の後方左は町役人や町名主、家主が薦敷きに座り、後方の右側は原告が座った。」)
