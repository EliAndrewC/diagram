• oshirasu-jawiki - 「According to a floor plan of the Edo South Town Magistracy made in Bunka 7 (1810), on the topmost level was a tatami room called the "kujiba", where the officials, the town magistrate first among them, sat, while on the lowest level was a "jarijiki" (gravel floor), and the plaintiffs, the defendants and others sat on the straw mats spread on it.」 (translated; )
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「The "kujiba" was partitioned into three rooms; at the very front was a board veranda of 3 shaku, and in its middle a stair of three steps, 1 ken wide.」 (translated; ) and 「In the innermost of the three rooms sat the town magistrate, in the room one in front of it the examining yoriki and the clerk doshin, in the room nearest the shirasu the witnessing doshin, and at the left and right of the shirasu the crouching doshin, each in attendance.」 (translated; )
  • shirasu-kotobank - 「Further, on the uwadori of the upper level sat the examiners and the scribes, and the magistrate was seated in the room of the topmost level.」 (translated; ) (Heibonsha's concise encyclopedia, マイペディア) and 「There are also magistracies that set apart a further upper room (uwadori), but the magistrate sat in the room of the topmost level.」 (translated; ) (the 世界大百科事典, Jinbo Fumio)
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「However, people of certain status such as samurai (excluding ronin), Shinto priests, monks and purveyor townsmen did not sit on the "jarijiki", but sat on the verandas of the room, divided into two steps.」 (translated; ) and 「Samurai, Shinto priests and monks sat on the upper veranda (the upper of the two verandas) and so were called uwamono; the others sat on the lower veranda (the lower of the two) and so were called shitamono.」 (translated; )
  • shirasu-kotobank - 「Those who spread mushiro on the shirasu in the narrow sense (also called the gravel) and sat in the open were peasants, townsmen, ronin and the like.」 (translated; ) (the 世界大百科事典, which seats ronin of good lineage on the lower veranda) and 「The accused peasants, townsmen and lesser ronin sat on mushiro spread on it.」 (translated; ) (マイペディア)
  • shirasu-imidas - 「For commoners, mushiro were spread; samurai and monks who had committed crimes were made to sit on the board veranda.」 (translated; )
  • oshirasu-jawiki - 「The suspect was usually made to sit at the center of the shirasu, roped, together with a servant holding the end of the rope, with the soles of the feet down.」 (translated; ) and 「Behind the suspect on the left, the town officials, the town headmen and the landlords sat on komo matting, and behind on the right sat the plaintiff.」 (translated; )
