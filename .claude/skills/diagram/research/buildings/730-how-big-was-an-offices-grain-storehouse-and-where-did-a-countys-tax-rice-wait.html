How big was an office's grain storehouse, and where did a county's tax rice wait?

Sources: jinya-kotobank (a rice storehouse and a levy storehouse in an intendant's office), takayama-jinya-gifu (the floor area of a province's rice store), tomiyama-1961-gokura (where the rice went after the village store), sagami-2012-gogurasho (the storehouses of a three-village tax-rice site, measured)

An intendant's office kept its own rice storehouse. The encyclopedias list, among what stood in an intendant's office, a rice storehouse and a levy storehouse, beside the residence, the office, the staff's houses and the jail. The one that survives, at Takayama, was a single earth-walled building of numbered storehouses with a floor area of about 11,200 sq ft (1,042.56 m²). That store served a whole mountain province (The granary is a staging node), so it is the top of the range.

But much of the tax rice did not wait at an office at all. A historian of the village storehouses of Gunma found that the village store only received the baled tax rice and held it for a short time: soon after, the rice was carried to the rice storehouses in the castle town that the lord named, or to a river landing to be shipped. So the tax rice of a district gathered in one of two places - an office's own storehouse, or storehouses at the landing where it was loaded.

A store for a few villages is measured. In Echigo, groups of villages - from two or three to several tens - kept the tax rice of the shogun's estates through the winter at a shared storehouse site. A drawing of 1769 of one such site, serving three villages, gives each storehouse's size: one for each village, of about 37 by 20 ft, 34 by 22 ft and 21 by 21 ft, and a measuring storehouse the three villages shared, about 38 by 19 ft - four storehouses of some 440 to 750 sq ft each.

A county office's storehouse is not measured. The drawn granary, about 43 to 50 by 25 to 27 ft (some 1,100 to 1,350 sq ft), is larger than any one storehouse on the three-village site and about a tenth of Takayama's rice store; no page read gives the size of a county-scale office's grain storehouse, or how many it kept.

The rule the map follows: where a county's tax rice waits is a knob, rolled per county: in the office's own grain storehouse, or in a row of storehouses at the town's river landing, beside the loading place - both attested. On this record's reading, a county on navigable water leans to the landing and an isolated one to the office's storehouse. A levy storehouse beside the rice storehouse is attested for intendants' offices. The county storehouse's size, about 1,100 to 1,350 sq ft, and the count of one, are a guess, set above a three-village storehouse's 440 to 750 sq ft and well below the 11,200 sq ft of a province's store; a landing's storehouse row is drawn as several storehouses of about the three-village site's size, their number a guess.
