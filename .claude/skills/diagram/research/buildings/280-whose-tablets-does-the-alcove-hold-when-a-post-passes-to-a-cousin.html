Whose tablets does the alcove hold when a post passes to a cousin, not a son?

Sources: sosen-saishi-kotobank (whose tablets a Japanese house keeps). Tablets of predecessors in office have no readable source

The alcove question at a lineage-held posting is answered when the post passes father to son: the past holders are then the holder's own forebears. When it passes to a cousin the history speaks only of the house, not the office. One common type of Japanese tablet veneration keeps, in principle, the tablets of the house's successive heads and their spouses, and follows the same line as the inheritance of the house's property. Nothing readable was found on tablets of predecessors IN OFFICE being kept at an office or its residence, in Japan or at a Chinese county seat.

What this means for a plan, labeled a GUESS: the alcove follows the house, not the post. Where a cousin takes the post as heir of the same house, the house's tablets - and so any past holders who were its heads - come with them; where the cousin heads a branch house of their own, their alcove holds their own line's tablets, and the past holders of the post stand in no alcove at the residence. Which of these a given posting was is a question of the setting, for the GM.
