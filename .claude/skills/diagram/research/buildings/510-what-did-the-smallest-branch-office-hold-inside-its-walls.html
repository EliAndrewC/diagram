What did the smallest branch office hold inside its walls?

Sources: fuchu-joge-pamphlet (the Jōge post's drawing and its excavation)

The shogunate's post at Jōge, in Bingo, was an outpost: an office set up in territory far from its intendant's office, something like a branch, where at the end of the shogunate, in 1867, three officials were posted. A drawing of it made in 1869, reproduced by the city of Fuchū from the town's history, shows what those three kept inside its walls: an Inari shrine and a Tenjin shrine, a central building of about 80 tsubo, and a court spread with white gravel where trials were held. Its main gate, with a small gate and a gatekeepers' room beside it, is taken up at How wide was the main gate of a magistrate's post?, and the entrance facing the ground inside it at How did a guest of rank arrive, from the gate to the entrance?

Excavation from 2008 to 2010, with the drawing in hand, found the post's stone steps and stone walls, ditches thought to have bounded its buildings, and their foundation stones, and put the site at about 70 m by 27 m, close to the drawing's 35 by 14 ken. The shrines are not among what the ground gave up; they rest on the drawing.
