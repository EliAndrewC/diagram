Staff housing spans a real spectrum

Sources: hatchobori-jawiki (the yoriki/doshin 組屋敷, kumi-yashiki, apart from the office), takayama-jinya-jawiki (the storehouse-keepers' rowhouse; it gives no staff count and does not say who else lived there), machi-bugyo-jawiki (the magistrate's residence inside the office), jinya-jawiki

Rural jin'ya housed staff on-grounds in rowhouses (the sources name the storehouse keepers' rowhouse and retainers' residences inside the ramparts; that the rest of the staff lived on the grounds is a reconstruction); it was Edo's big town magistracy that externalized the workforce to a separate constables' district (Hatchōbori), while the magistrate's own residence stood inside the magistracy. Both ends are historical; the county-manor default is on-grounds, per the setting's staffing (a working platoon, mostly without dependents). How long and how deep such a rowhouse was is its own question (How big was the rowhouse a magistrate's staff lived in?).
