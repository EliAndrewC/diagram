The size of a compound and the rank of its buildings

A magistrate's or a lord's compound was a walled site of halls, a household, storehouses and service buildings set around open courts. At the one intendant's office in Japan measured building by building, the rice storehouse was the largest, then the office hall, the intendant's residence and the staff's rowhouse, with the small storehouses and the gatehouse far below.

How the other buildings of a compound ranked - its kitchen, its stable, its shrine - no page we read records, and the order given for them below is this project's reading of the few sizes that are known.

  • The Takayama Jin'ya is the one intendant's office that survives.
    It was the office the shogunate set up to govern Hida province as its own territory, and it is preserved as the only jin'ya still standing. Its residence and its storehouse-keepers' rowhouse are restorations of the 1990s, not the old buildings themselves.
  • At Takayama the office hall out-measured the residence, and the residence the staff's rowhouse.
    The office is about 8,100 sq ft and the intendant's residence about 6,400 sq ft, so the working office is about a quarter again the size of the household's. The storehouse-keepers' rowhouse is about 3,700 sq ft. The kitchen storehouse (about 740 sq ft), the book storehouse (about 450 sq ft) and the gatehouse (about 400 sq ft) are far below.
  • The largest building at Takayama was a storehouse.
    Its rice store was built as a rice storehouse in the third bailey of Takayama castle and moved to the jin'ya in 1695. At about 11,200 sq ft it is larger than the office and about three-quarters again the size of the residence. This project reads it as one long building divided into the numbered storehouses among its surviving buildings. It held the rice of a whole province (The granary is a staging node), so it is the top of the range, not a county's granary - but it shows that a storehouse could out-size every hall.
  • A small branch office had one central building of about 80 tsubo.
    The Joge branch office, set up in the governed territory away from the intendant's office, to which three officials had come by the end of the shogunate in 1867, had one central building of about 80 tsubo (about 2,850 sq ft).
  • How big was a retainer's house?
    About 49 to 67 tsubo, on the two houses measured. A main house of about 67 tsubo (about 2,380 sq ft) at Matsue was lived in, in turn, by retainers of about 500 to 1,000 koku, and a middle-rank retainer of 150 koku at Matsushiro had one of about 49 tsubo (about 1,740 sq ft). More on the size of a house against its owner's rank is at how big a samurai's house was.
  • How big was a kitchen against the house it served?
    A fraction of it, not its equal: an elite kitchen (daidokoro) was large, but this is a GUESS, since no page we read gives a kitchen's area against its house's.
  • How big was a county office's stable beside its barracks?
    A fraction of it, on this project's figures: a stable of two to four horses, at about 55-70 sq ft a stall, beside a barracks for ten to twenty men. The horse count, the stall area and the barracks' strength are a GUESS: no page we read gives the horses of a posting or a stall's depth, and none says where a stable ranked among an office's buildings. What a warrior's stable and its stalls were like is at the samurai's stable.
  • How large were a shrine's halls?
    Small, even when grand. A provincial shrine's worship hall (haiden) is taken here as 3 to 5 ken across, a GUESS: no page we read gives it a figure. The national outlier, the main sanctuary (honden) at Izumo, stands 24 m (~79 ft) tall on a near-square plan divided into four rooms, which this project reads as about 2 by 2 ken, a GUESS. So in this project's reading a shrine hall larger than the residence would be out of place in a county compound.
  • Compounds were laid out around open courts.
    The county yamen at Neixiang, in China, covers more than 47,000 m² (~11.6 acres) with 18 successive courtyards, and a visitor going north from its main gate crosses two rectangular courts in turn.
  • How much of a jin'ya's site was open forecourt?
    At Takayama the plaza in front of the jin'ya is about an eighth of the designated site, some 15,200 sq ft of about 120,800. The forecourt and the sanded court of the hearing (oshirasu) together are taken here as about 12-18% of a site, a GUESS: no page we read measures them.
  • At Takayama the office's rooms were one building, and its storehouses one run.
    The entrance, the examination room, the office and the great hall were rebuilt together in 1816 as parts of the one intendant's office, and the storehouses stand as a numbered run, nos. 1 to 4 and 9 to 12.
