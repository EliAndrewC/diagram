How big was a samurai's house, and what rank is a 67-tsubo house?

Sources: matsue-bukeyashiki-about (the Matsue main house's size and the rank of its households), matsushiro-bukeyashiki (the Yokota house at Matsushiro, its main house and its grounds)

The 67-tsubo house is an upper retainer's. The Matsue samurai residence's main house is about 67 tsubo (about 2,380 sq ft), and retainers of about 500 to 1,000 koku lived in it in turn; it was rebuilt after the fire of 1733, altered several times, and restored to its Meiji plan. A middle-rank house was smaller: the Yokota house at Matsushiro, the representative residence of a middle-rank samurai of that domain, a 150-koku man who served as district magistrate among other posts, has a main house of 1794 of about 49 tsubo (about 1,740 sq ft) on grounds of 1,012 tsubo. How much of either house was kitchen is not given.

What this means for a plan: a posting's kitchen is measured against a whole house of about 49 tsubo at middle rank and about 67 at upper-middle rank; a kitchen that approaches either figure is the tell the size hierarchy looks for. The kitchen's own size is a GUESS.
