How big was a samurai's house, and what rank is a 67-tsubo house?

Sources: matsue-bukeyashiki-about (the Matsue main house's size, the rank of its households, and the plan it was restored to), matsushiro-bukeyashiki (the Yokota house at Matsushiro, its main house and its grounds), nagano-shishi-yokota (how the Yokota house is dated and measured, and how much of it was altered), bunka-kuchiba and yamaguchi-kuchiba (the Kuchiba house at Hagi, its rank, date and size)

The 67-tsubo house is the Matsue samurai residence's main house, about 67 tsubo (about 2,380 sq ft), lived in by turns by retainers of about 500 to 1,000 koku; it was rebuilt after the fire of 1733, extended and altered several times, and restored from Meiji-period drawings, so the figure measures the house as it stood after 1868, not as it was built. The firm figure from before 1868 is smaller: the Yokota house at Matsushiro, the representative residence of a middle-rank samurai of that domain, a 150-koku man who served as district magistrate among other posts, has a main house of 1794 of about 49 tsubo (about 1,740 sq ft) on grounds of 1,012 tsubo. The town history dates it by an ink inscription on a pillar and by its style, and gives it as 17.1 by 9.6 m; by about 1868 its plan had been considerably altered, with rooms added and the kitchen changed, though little from the entrance to the parlor. A house of the Matsue rank from before 1868 stands at Hagi, the Kuchiba house, and the two official descriptions of it do not agree. The national register calls it the house of a Hagi domain retainer of about 1,000 koku, dates it only to the late Edo period, 1801-1900, and gives the main house as 12.9 by 10.0 m with an east wing of 2.0 by 3.9 m, about 41 tsubo (about 1,470 sq ft) in all; the prefecture, which reports that the household is said to have held 1,018 koku at the end of the period and dates the house, with no documents, to the late 18th or early 19th century, gives it as 14.9 by 11.9 m with a west wing of 3.0 by 8.9 m and an east wing, the kitchen, of 5.9 by 6.9 m, about 74 tsubo (about 2,630 sq ft) in all, and says it was little altered apart from later extension, the entrance now being in an addition on the north. By the prefecture's measure the Kuchiba kitchen wing is about 12 tsubo, about a sixth of the house, though the register's east wing is far smaller and neither says whether the wing is original; how much of the other houses was kitchen is not given.

What this means for a plan: a county magistracy's residence is measured against the whole house of about 49 tsubo built in 1794 for a 150-koku household, the one figure from before 1868 that is firm and whose household held the office the posting holds. The 67-tsubo figure is a Meiji plan and is not used as a measure; a house of about 1,000 koku before 1868 is somewhere between about 41 and about 74 tsubo, as the Kuchiba house's two measures stand. A kitchen that approaches the whole house is the tell the size hierarchy looks for. The kitchen's own size is a GUESS, the one kitchen measured being the Kuchiba wing's sixth of the house.
