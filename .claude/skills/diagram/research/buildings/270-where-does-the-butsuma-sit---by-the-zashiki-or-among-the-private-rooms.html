Where does the butsuma sit - by the zashiki, or among the private rooms?

Sources: butsuma-kotobank (what a butsuma holds, and the places it was set), touken-world-buke-madori (a samurai house with its butsuma beyond the audience room)

The butsuma is the room where the household's Buddha images and ihai, the memorial tablets of its dead, are kept. In ordinary houses it stood in one of three places: the altar's place built out beyond the room, which was common; a place made beside the tokonoma of the zashiki; or, in devout regions and above all where Jodo Shinshu was strong, a room of about six mats behind the oku-no-ma or the zashiki. In the imperial palace it was a separate building standing beside the everyday residence hall. One surviving upper-samurai house at Kanazawa, the Nomura house, has its butsuma beyond the audience room - though that audience room was moved in from another house, so the sequence is not the house's own.

What this means for a plan: the butsuma's place is a knob with two forms attested for a house - beside the zashiki, at its alcove; or a room of its own behind the zashiki or the inner room - and both put it at the formal end of the residence, by the zashiki. A butsuma among the family's private rooms was not found in any source read; the palace's separate hall beside the everyday residence is the nearest case, and it is an emperor's.
