What grew around the compound's own shrine?

Sources: kotobank-yashikigami (the household god and where it is kept), jawiki-yashikigami (its corner and its origin in the woods), boso-no-mura-takei (an Inari at a samurai house)

The god of a household's own ground, the yashikigami, was kept in a corner of the residence lot or on a small plot joined to it, either in a small shrine or at an old tree or a stone that served as the seat the god came down to (a yorishiro). It often stood at the northwest or the northeast of the lot. Its origin is thought to be a place of worship in the woods near the house, marked at first by no shrine at all, only an old tree or a natural stone used at festival time, which drew closer to the house over time until the shrine stood inside the lot. Which farmsteads kept one, and in which corner, is taken up at Which farmsteads had a household shrine, and in which corner?, and the shrine every compound keeps at Every administrative compound keeps a shrine.

At a samurai house the shrine could be one of the fixtures of the grounds. The Takei house, a middle-rank samurai's house in Sakura rebuilt at an open-air museum, has besides its main house an Inari shrine, a gate and a vegetable garden, the whole enclosed by an earth bank and hedges. None of these pages describes a garden laid out around such a shrine.

What this means for a plan: the compound's shrine in a corner of its ground is ACCURATE, and so is a tree beside it, the older seat of a household god. An ornamental garden planted around the shrine is a GUESS.
