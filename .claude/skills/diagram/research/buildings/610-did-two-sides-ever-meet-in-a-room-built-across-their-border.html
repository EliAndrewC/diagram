Did two sides ever meet in a room built across their border?

Sources: kyakhta-trade-enwiki (a post on each side of the Russo-Qing border), wakan-kotobank (the Japan House at Pusan, where Tsushima traded with Korea)

No page read describes a room or a building built across a border line, or two parties meeting on the line itself so that neither stood on the other's ground.

Where two powers dealt with each other regularly across a border, the forms read are two, and in both each party kept its own ground. At Kyakhta, where Russia and Qing China traded from 1727, the Russians built their post at the border and the Manchus built the fort of Maimaicheng a few hundred feet south, on their own side; officials on both sides dealt with the diplomatic exchanges, the trade disputes and the police matters of the border, and much barter was done at Kyakhta. Between Japan and Korea one side kept a compound on the other's ground: in the Edo period the Japan House (Wakan) at Choryang held the commander's house and the trade hall in its east section and inns in its west, and officers sent from Tsushima conducted diplomacy and trade there. Adjoining it, on the Korean side, stood a banquet hall and guest houses, used to feast Tsushima's envoys and to lodge the Korean receiving officers and interpreters.

What this means for a plan: a parley room built into a border wall, with the line across its floor, remains a DELIBERATE DEVIATION, made by the GM's ruling that the door which receives the Kitsune is the border. The historical forms it departs from are a post on each side of the line, facing each other across it, or a compound one side sets aside for the other's officers, with its own reception hall beside it. The kneeling mats set two a side across the line belong to the same deviation.
