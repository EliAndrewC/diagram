• kotobank-umaya - 「A warrior's stable, that is, one attached to a shoin-style residence, has the tachiba where horses are tied, a zashiki called tozamurai, and an earth-floored kusa-no-ma. The Rakuchu rakugai screens and the Shomei, the manual handed down in the Heinouchi family, the shogunate's master carpenters, show a gable-entered stable 3 ken long, considered the most formal of its time. The tachiba is board-floored, not earth」 (translated; ) (the Heibonsha world encyclopedia entry)
  • jaanus-umaya - 「The stables used by the warrior class bushi 武士 were substantial freestanding structures in the shoin style」 and 「The classic 16th-17th century examples, as recorded in the master carpenter's manual *Shōmei 匠明, and excavated at the site of the Asakura 朝倉 mansion at Ichijōdani 一乗谷 in Fukui Prefecture were 3, 5 or 7 bays in length.」 and 「None survive」 and 「In the warrior class stable, separate stalls of one bay width were provided.」 and 「the stalls had raised timber floors itabari 板張」 and 「In front of the stalls was an earth-floored passage that ran the length of the building called the *kusa-no-ma 草の間.」 and 「On the other side of the kusa-no-ma, opposite the stalls, there was a suite called tōzamurai 遠侍 floored with straw mats *tatami 畳.」 and 「Illustrated handscrolls *emaki 絵巻 indicate that the quarters for grooms and retainers in warrior class stables go back to the Kamakura period, but these rooms were also used by senior household members when they came to view their horses.」
  • kotobank-umaya - 「Hikone castle's stable is a large one, L-shaped, with 21 small bays and a grooms' rest room at the end.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-umaya - 「In the Tohoku region and elsewhere, where winters are long, stables are wide; those of the Kinki region and elsewhere are narrow, about 3 m square.」 (translated; ); 「A farmhouse's stable divides into the inner stable, inside the main house, and the outer stable, standing on its own; the L-shaped house of the Nanbu region is the form in which the outer stable was joined to the main house.」 (translated; ) (both from the entry's passage on the farmhouse's stable)
