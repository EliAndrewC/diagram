Did the private rooms of an ordinary posting have a garden of their own?

Sources: oniwa-takayama-jinya (the garden of the one surviving intendancy office, and the rooms that look onto it), takayama-jinya-jawiki (the Edo-period plan the residence was rebuilt from), takayama-jinya-city (the excavation of the residence's site)

At the Takayama Jin'ya, the shogunate's intendancy office at Takayama and the one such office whose main buildings survive, a single garden is seen both from the shoin-style great hall at the heart of the office and from the Arashiyama-no-ma, the room where the intendant lived. The living room has a tea room, stepping stones lead into the garden from it and from the great hall alike, and a pond garden lies further in; the site's pamphlet says the garden was remade in the 1780s. Most of the buildings seen there today are reconstructions of the late Showa and Heisei years (the late twentieth century and after), the front gate of 1832 and the office range of 1816 excepted. The rebuilding does not make the layout modern: the great hall stands in the range of 1816, and the intendant's residence and the inner rooms were rebuilt in 1996 to almost their Edo-period state from a plan of 1830, with the city's excavation of the residence's site in 1991 as a further reference. So the one garden shared by the hall and the living room is the layout of an office before the Meiji Restoration, as far as the rooms rebuilt from that plan show it; none of these sources says the plan draws the garden itself. A separate small garden kept for the private rooms alone was not found at any posting.

What this means for a plan: the shared garden is ACCURATE for Takayama, an Edo-period layout rather than only a modern rebuild. Carrying it to other postings is a GUESS from this one compound: at an ordinary posting the private rooms share the one garden with the formal hall, with their own stepping stones into it, rather than having a garden of their own.
