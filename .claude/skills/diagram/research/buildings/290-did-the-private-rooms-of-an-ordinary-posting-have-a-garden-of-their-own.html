Did the private rooms of an ordinary posting have a garden of their own?

Sources: oniwa-takayama-jinya (the garden of the one surviving intendancy office, and the rooms that look onto it)

At the Takayama Jin'ya, the shogunate's intendancy office for Hida and the one such office whose main buildings survive, a single garden is seen both from the shoin-style great hall at the heart of the office and from the Arashiyama-no-ma, the room where the intendant lived. The living room has a tea room, stepping stones lead into the garden from it and from the great hall alike, and a pond garden lies further in; the site's pamphlet says the garden was remade in the 1780s. Most of the buildings seen there today are late-twentieth-century reconstructions, the front gate of 1832 and the office range of 1816 excepted. A separate small garden kept for the private rooms alone was not found at any posting.

What this means for a plan: at an ordinary posting the private rooms share the one garden with the formal hall - they look onto its far part, reached by their own stepping stones - rather than having a garden of their own.
