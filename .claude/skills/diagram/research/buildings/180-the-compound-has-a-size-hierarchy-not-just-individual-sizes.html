The compound has a size HIERARCHY, not just individual sizes

Sources: matsue-bukeyashiki-about (a retainer's main house in tsubo); no readable page gives the other anchors - the Izumo sanctuary plan, the county stable and a magistracy's office footprint - and each carries its note in the text

What the research found: in a lord's walled compound the domestic footprints rank in a fixed order - the residence is the dominant DOMESTIC building (the administrative office hall may exceed it, being the institution's working core, but no service/utility/storage/sacred building should). Anchors: an elite daidokoro is large but a fraction of the living quarters, not their equal (the main house of a retainer of 500 to 1,000 koku at Matsue is about 67 tsubo, and a kitchen that size is a tell; the house by rank is How big is a samurai's house lot, and the house on it?); a county stable held ~2-4 horses (~55-70 sq ft/stall, this page's figure) so it is a fraction of a ~10-20-man barracks; a shrine's buildings are small even when grand (Izumo's main sanctuary, the national outlier, is ~2×2 ken on this page's reading, its pages giving only the 24 m height; a provincial haiden is 3-5 ken), so a shrine hall with a bigger footprint than the residence is impossible at county tier. So the ranking matters as much as the sizes: a compound where every building is individually plausible can still show an impossible order (a kitchen at or above the living block, a stable over the barracks, a shrine over the residence). The deliberate departure: the office hall is allowed to be the largest building - it is not domestic, and a magistracy's public working block was the reason it existed (this project's reading: no source comparing a magistracy's office footprint with its residence was found).
