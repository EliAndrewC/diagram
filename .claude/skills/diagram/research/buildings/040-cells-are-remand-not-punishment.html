Cells are remand, not punishment

Sources: tenmacho-jawiki, chuo-royashiki; neixiang-yamen-zhwiki, henan-neixiang (the southwest jail). The "west is the death direction / western ghost gate" gloss has no readable source

Edo jails held the accused pending judgment; sentences were exile, flogging, fines, or execution - not, in the ordinary case, prison time (ja.wikipedia, the Tenmachō jail (伝馬町牢屋敷) names 永牢 (eiro) and 過怠牢 (katairo) as real, if exceptional, prison-as-sentence categories held there alongside the remand prisoners) - and light offenders were consigned to their home village. Purpose-built prisons existed as separate metropolitan compounds, the largest being Edo's Tenmachō (there were also jails at daikan offices and other magistracies) (~8,850 m2, its own moated block). Cell PLACEMENT is deliberately a free variable: Chinese regulation put the jail in the southwest corner (Neixiang; Pingyao "west side"), though the gloss that west is the death direction and that the condemned left by a western "ghost gate" is not something the record can support. Rokugan has no equivalent directional rule, so a magistracy's cells stand wherever the compound wants them (GM decision, 2026-07). The rule (small remand cells, no prison blocks) does not depend on the exceptions: the ordinary sentence was not confinement.
