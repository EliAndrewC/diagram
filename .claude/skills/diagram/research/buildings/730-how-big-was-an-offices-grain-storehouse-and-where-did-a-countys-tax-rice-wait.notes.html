• jinya-kotobank - 「Generally an intendant's jin'ya had within its grounds the honjin (the intendant's official residence), the office for public business, the official residences of subordinate officials, rice storehouses and levy storehouses, a white-sand court, a jail and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一般に代官陣屋では敷地内に代官の官舎である本陣，公用執務の役所，属僚の役宅，米蔵・運上蔵や白洲・牢屋などが設けられていた。」) (the Hyakka Jiten Mypedia entry on the page; the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry on the same page lists the rice storehouse and the levy storehouse too)
  • takayama-jinya-gifu - 「Earthen-storehouse construction: ... onkura 1,042.56 m², Keicho era (about 1600)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「土蔵造 … 御蔵 1,042.56㎡ 慶長年間(1600年頃)」) (that the onkura is the building of numbered storehouses is this project's reading, beside ja.wikipedia 高山陣屋's list of storehouses nos. 1-4 and 9-12; the conversion to square feet is this project's)
  • tomiyama-1961-gokura - 「The function of the village storehouse for receiving and keeping tax rice was only to receive the baled tax rice and hold it temporarily. Soon after it was received, the tax rice was carried to the rice storehouses of the castle town designated by the lord, or to the river landing for shipment.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「年貢米収納保管用の郷蔵の機能は俵装された年貢米を収納し、一時的に保管するだけであつた。収納後間もなく年貢米は領主の指定する城下の米蔵や、廻米のため河岸に輸送される。」) (page 797, read from the page rendered as an image; the paper covers two districts of Joshu, modern Gunma)
  • sagami-2012-gogurasho - 「An important role of Gôgura-syo was to store the rice collected as tax from Shogunate's estates during wintertime」 and 「Since a storehouse group was made up of anything from two or three villages to several tens of villages, we can see that there were many store sites in Echigo province at the time.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「蔵組は、2、3ヶ村から数10ヶ村によって構成されていたので、当時の越後国には多くの郷蔵所があったことがわかる。」) (the first passage is the paper's own English abstract; the second is page 1180, read from the page rendered as an image)
  • sagami-2012-gogurasho - 「Iwata storehouse: length 6 ken 1 shaku, width 3 ken 2 shaku. Iizuka storehouse: length 5 ken 4 shaku, width 3 ken 4 shaku. Fudosawa storehouse: 3 ken 3 shaku, 3 ken 3 shaku. Measuring storehouse: 6 ken 2 shaku, 3 ken 1 shaku.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「岩田蔵 立六間壱尺 横三間弐尺」「飯塚蔵 立五間四尺 横三間四尺」「不動沢蔵 三間三尺 三間三尺」「計蔵 六間弐尺 三間一尺」) (the legend of figure 4, 「明和6年（1769）の飯塚村郷蔵所」, "the Iizuka village store site in 1769") and 「Figure 4 shows the storehouses of the storehouse group's villages and one 'three-village measuring storehouse'. The tax-rice storehouses were built for each village, but the measuring storehouse was used by the three villages.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「図4には、蔵組の村々の蔵と1棟の「三ヶ村計蔵」が見られる。積蔵は村毎に設けたが、計蔵は3ヶ村で使用していた。」) (pages 1180-1181, read from the pages rendered as images; the conversions to feet, at six shaku to the ken, are this project's)
