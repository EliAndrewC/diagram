Those who used it as their office were the jiyakunin, mostly local hires.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「御役所と仕切られた隣の空間は御用場。」 and 「ここを執務室にしていたのは、地元採用を中心とした地役人。」) and, of the goyakusho, 「the room where the officials directly under the intendant, sent from the shogunate (what would now be called the career track), did their work」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「幕府から派遣されてきた代官直属の役人（現代でいうキャリア組）が仕事をする部屋。」)
  • mapple-takayama-jinya - of the kakiyaku-beya, 「A room only for writing, where the important documents to be submitted to the Edo shogunate and others were drawn up here.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸幕府などに提出する大切な書類をここで作成する、書き物専用の部屋。」) and 「The documents were bound in the chotojiba next door」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「隣の帳綴場で書類を綴じた」) (the guide gives no size for either room)
  • edo-ashigaru-bugyosho - 「In this area were concentrated the functions concerned with lawsuits and examinations: the saikyosho and the shirasu, the inner sengisho and the sengisho, the duty rooms of the shacho-sen'yokata and the reikurikata, the litigants' waiting room and benches, the jail doshin's duty room and the temporary jail, and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「このエリアには、裁許所と白洲、内詮議所、詮議所、赦帳撰要方や例繰方の詰所、公事人溜りや腰掛、牢屋同心の詰所や仮牢など、公事吟味に関する機能が集中していました。」) (the page describes the North Town Magistracy from a plan of 1841, and says the South was built much the same)
  • jinya-kotobank - 「The daikan jin'ya was the base of the shogunate's local administration, and is the collective name for the honjin, the intendant's official residence; the office where public business was carried on; the rice kura; the levy kura; the koya and nagaya that were the official houses of the tetsuki and tedai; and the jail.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「代官陣屋は幕府の地方行政の拠点であり、代官の官舎の本陣、公用を執務する役所、米蔵、運上蔵、手付(てづき)や手代(てだい)の官舎である小屋や長屋、牢屋(ろうや)を総称する。」) (Nipponica, Murakami Tadashi)
