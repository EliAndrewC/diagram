  • liq-takayasu-daidokoro - 「Basically the kamado was placed on the doma, but in a narrow nagaya it apparently sometimes had to go on the board floor between the tatami and the doma.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「基本的には土間に竈が置かれるのですが、狭い長屋の場合は畳敷きと土間の間の板間に置かなければならないこともあったようです。」) (the sentence is written of the Edo period's rowhouses; the page's general rule is its first clause)
  • boso-no-mura-takei - 「The katte (kitchen) is board-floored, with a movable kamado and a sink. The ceiling, to let smoke out, is the mesukashi saobuchi type, with gaps between the ceiling boards.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「「勝手（台所）」は板敷きで、移動式のかまどと流しが設置されています。また、天井は煙抜きのため、天井板の間に隙間をあける「目透（めす）かし竿縁（さおぶち）天井」という形式をとっています。」) (the house is a reproduction modeled on a late Edo house in Sakura, which the page calls a middle-rank samurai's)
  • matsue-bukeyashiki - 「inner entrance, formal shikidai entrance, anteroom, family room, wife's sitting room, tea room, Buddhist altar room, bath, miso room, kamado (kudo), privy」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「内玄関 式台玄関 次の間 家族部屋 奥方居間 茶室 仏間 湯殿 味噌部屋 竃（くど） 雪隠」) (names only, under the page's "other features"; the page describes none of them)
