Where does a walled compound keep its vegetable garden, and does the sun decide it?

Sources: boso-no-mura-takei (a plot west of the house), sanada-higuchi (the field south of the pond), matsushiro-bukeyashiki (half the grounds), kasama-iwama-jinya (inside a jin'ya), kotobank-kisaku-daikansho (a daikan office's field), gujin-xie-yamen (a county yamen's)

What the research found. A walled residence or office kept its vegetables in more than one place. One samurai house's plot lay west of the house. Another's field lay south of its pond, on the sunny side the formal garden holds - the woods, bamboo and garden there were a field in the Edo period. A third gave about half its grounds to vegetables. An official compound kept one too: a jin'ya had about two tan of vegetable garden inside its grounds, worked by its own officials, and a daikan's office had a vegetable field of 30 by 20 ken beside its residence lot, a parcel of its own in the land survey. A Chinese county yamen listed its vegetable plot last in its rear service ground, after the kitchen, the stable and the archery ground. So the formal garden's hold on the south was not exclusive: a working plot shared the sunny side at one house, and stood west, in the rear, or apart at others. No source read says why a household put its plot where it did.

The rule a plan follows. The vegetable garden's seat is a knob (accurate as seats): west of the residence, south beside the formal garden, in the rear service ground, or a parcel of its own. The sun takes out any seat where a bed of sun crops gets under its six hours in the shoulder month (under "How many hours of direct sun does a kitchen bed need?"; a guess that a household chose among its seats by the sun, since no source gives the reason), and among the seats left a scripted plan rolls one and a hand-drawn plan takes one. At Ochiba, Hayakawa and Ubame the west and the rear lie in the shade of the kitchen range and the residence, so the only seat left inside the walls is the south: each bed takes the sunny edge of the south court beside the formal garden, as the field south of the pond did.
