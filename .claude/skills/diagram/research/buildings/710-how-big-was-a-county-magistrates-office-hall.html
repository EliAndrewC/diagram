How big was a county magistrate's office hall?

Sources: takayama-jinya-gifu (the office of a whole province's intendancy, measured), takayama-jinya-jawiki (what the office block held), fuchu-joge-jinya (the central building of a small branch office)

Two offices give the range. At the top, the office of the Takayama Jin'ya, which governed Hida province for the shogunate, has a floor area of about 8,100 sq ft (754.75 m²); it is the block rebuilt in 1816 that held the entrance hall, the examination room, the working office and the great hall together (The courtroom is a room of the office hall). At the bottom, the Joge branch office - an outlying office to which three officials came at the end of the shogunate - had a central building of about 80 tsubo, some 2,850 sq ft.

A county office sits between them. A county magistracy, with a platoon of staff, is larger than a three-man branch and far smaller than a province's intendancy. The drawn office hall - about 80 to 150 ft long and 20 to 45 ft deep, some 1,600 to 6,750 sq ft - stays below the intendancy, but only its upper part is larger than the branch office's whole central building of about 2,850 sq ft; its lower end is smaller than that branch. How long and how deep an office hall was is on no page read: both sources give a floor area and nothing else.

The rule the map follows: a county office hall has a floor area between about 1,600 and 6,750 sq ft - always well below the 8,100 sq ft of a province's intendancy office, and above a small branch office's whole central building (about 2,850 sq ft) only at its top end. The ceiling is accurate, calibrated against two measured offices; the floor, below the smaller of them, is a guess. Its proportions, 80 to 150 ft long and 20 to 45 ft deep, are a guess.
