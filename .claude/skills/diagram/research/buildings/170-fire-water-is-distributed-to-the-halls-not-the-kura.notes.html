• tfd-hongou-fire-history - 「As a matter of daily habit, fire-fighting equipment such as fire-prevention water and fire-beaters was kept on the roof. The fire-beater was soaked in water and used to beat out sparks when they fell on one's own house; the names vary - yosui-oke (water bucket), tensui-oke (rainwater bucket), chinka-sui (fire-quenching water) - but the common people habitually kept water ready and placed it at the front entrance and on the roof.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )

  • thepaper-menhai - 「It is said that these great vats also had a popular name, "men-hai", taking the meaning of a great sea before the gate, in the hope that the "great sea" before the gate would overcome fire with water and the palace would suffer no more fires」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • thepaper-taipinggang - 「In the Qing dynasty there were specific regulations governing which kind of great vat was set out in which courtyard of the imperial palace and how many great vats were placed there. Those set out on both sides of the Taihe Hall, the Baohe Hall and the Qianqing Gate were the highest-grade gilt bronze taiping vats ... while those placed in the various courtyards of the rear palace were ordinary bronze or iron vats.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • thepaper-taipinggang - 「Because most of the halls of the Forbidden City are timber structures and catch fire extremely easily, great vats were placed everywhere far from a water source, so that when a hall caught fire it could be put out with water in time.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
