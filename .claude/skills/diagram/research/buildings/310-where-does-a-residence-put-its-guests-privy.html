Where does a residence put its guests' privy?

Sources: sayama-jinya-uematsu (the upper privies of the Sayama domain's council hall, and the bath and privy added on to its middle-rank houses), kotobank-benjo (the privy at the rear of the guest parlor)

That a residence's privies are built into the house, an upper privy for the head of the household and the men and a lower one for the women, is set out in Privies attach to the house. Where a house has a guest parlor, townhouses and farmhouses alike put a urinal and a privy at its rear. An official building of the Sayama domain's jin'ya, its council hall (hyojosho), had with its genkan, its shikidai and its zashiki with a tokonoma two upper privies with board verandas, and the author surmises, because the genkan, the shikidai and the tokonoma mark a samurai house's standing, that the domain's upper-rank houses had the same plan. At the domain's middle-rank houses the bath and privy were meant to be added on, whether the domain supplied an old house, which it repaired, or money for one.

What this means for a plan, as this project reads the two sources together (neither shows the whole arrangement in one house - the dictionary puts the guest privy at the rear of the guest parlor, and the Sayama hall gives its upper privies board verandas): the guests' privy is part of the formal side of the house, at the rear of the zashiki and reached along a board veranda, and the household's own privy stands apart from it, toward the living rooms, as the section on privies sets out. Neither is a building of its own in the yard.
