Guest doors feed courts, not flanks

Sources: kotobank-shikidai (the genkan as the place a palanquin was set down), kominkai-genkan (the rider stepping from palanquin to building), kotobank-katteguchi (the kitchen door); the GM's rule (2026-07)

Historical arrival was STAGED: gate, then open ground a palanquin could cross, then the formal stepped entrance (genkan), where the palanquin was set down against the step so that its rider went straight into the building - a visitor of rank never stepped from the street into a room or against a building's side. The finding is set out at How did a guest of rank arrive, from the gate to the entrance?; that the ground could be a garden rather than a court is not read. Service circulation is the deliberate inverse (the kitchen postern opens into work space). On the Hayakawa sheet the guests' river door opens into the guest garden, not the sliver beside the guest house's flank.
