Guest doors feed courts, not flanks

Sources: kotobank-shikidai (the genkan as the place a palanquin was set down), kominkai-genkan (the rider stepping from palanquin to building), kotobank-katteguchi (the kitchen door); the GM's rule (2026-07)

Historical arrival was STAGED, and it ended at the formal stepped entrance (genkan): in a warrior's residence that was where the palanquin was set down, brought right alongside the step so that its rider went straight into the building without getting down onto the ground. The gate and the open ground before the entrance are set out, with their own sources, at How did a guest of rank arrive, from the gate to the entrance?; no source found says the ground could be a garden rather than a court. The GM's rule (2026-07) draws from this: a guest of rank never steps from the street into a room or against a building's side. Service circulation is the deliberate inverse (the kitchen postern opens into work space). On the Hayakawa sheet the guests' river door opens into the guest garden, not the sliver beside the guest house's flank.
