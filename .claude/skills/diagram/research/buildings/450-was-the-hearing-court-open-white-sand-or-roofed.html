Was the hearing court open white sand, or roofed?

Sources: oshirasu-jawiki (magistracies' courts roofed or indoor, and the gravel coming late), takayama-jinya-city (Takayama's examination room and court paved with cobbles and roofed), takayama-gh-shirasu (why Takayama's court is not white, and why it is under a roof), shirasu-imidas (courts at intendants' offices and barriers), takayama-jinya-official (Takayama's two courts)

Roofed. A magistracy's court either had a roof built over it or was an earth floor spread with gravel inside the building; the open-air court of white gravel is the image of the period dramas, not the history. In the oldest times the earth floor was used as it was, and the white gravel came later. Intendants' offices and the barrier checkpoints (sekisho) had their courts too. The one that survives at an intendant's office, Takayama's, is paved with cobbles and roofed, with its examination room. Its guide gives the reasons: Hida province has no sea and not enough white sand, so cobbles from the riverbed stood in for it, and Hida is snow country, where an open court would be buried in winter, so the court was made inside the building. Takayama has two such courts: one received the villages' suits and petitions, as an office's service window would now, and the other was where those who had committed crimes were examined and judged.

What this means for a plan: the hearing court is roofed - a court under the office hall's roof or a roof of its own - and that is ACCURATE; an open court of sand is not. What covers its floor is a knob: white gravel, or cobbles from the local riverbed where white sand is scarce, both attested.
