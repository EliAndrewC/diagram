Did a granary stand on posts, and why raise its floor away from a river?

Sources: takayukashiki-jawiki (the storehouse on posts, why its floor was high, and where it was still built), takayama-onkura-heritage (the Takayama rice storehouse, an earth-walled grain store with ventilation gaps), dozo-jawiki (the rice and grain storehouse among the kinds of kura, and what a kura guards against), kotobank-goura (where a village storehouse stood, and where its rice went)

Two storehouses are attested, and only one stands on posts. The raised-floor storehouse, the takakura, was built with its floor high to keep rats from the grain, with rat-guards on its posts, and to let the air through against damp; in Japan such storehouses were still built on Amami Oshima, on Hachijojima and among the Ainu of Hokkaido into modern times. Neither reason is a river's: on this project's reading they hold wherever grain is kept, by the water or away from it. The grain store of an Edo intendancy was the other kind: the rice storehouse at Takayama, built in the castle's third bailey and moved to the intendancy in 1695, is an earth-walled grain storehouse, a dozo, its walls sloping inward and set with gaps for ventilation. A kura was sorted by what it held, the rice store and the unhulled-grain store among its kinds, and it guarded them against fire, damp and theft.

The village storehouse, the goura, stood within the village and mostly on dry-field ground, and the tax rice stored in it was sent on to the shogunate's stores at Edo and Osaka, or to the domain's stores or to market. How far an earth-walled grain storehouse's floor sat above the ground, and whether on posts or on a stone base, was searched for and not found.

What this means for a plan: a granary is one of two forms, a knob between them. A takakura on posts is attested, but for the southern islands and the Ainu north; an intendancy's grain store on the Takayama model is an earth-walled kura with ventilation gaps. Drawing posts under an earth-walled kura is a GUESS, because nothing read says how its floor was raised; where the posts stay, the reason to give is rats and damp, which holds away from a river as well as beside one.
