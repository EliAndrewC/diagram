Where did a provincial city keep its tax rice, and how many storehouses did it take?

Sources: kawashiri-komegura-kunishitei (a domain's tax-rice store at a river port: its buildings, its intake, its landing), takayama-jinya-city (a rice storehouse built in a castle's third bailey), tomiyama-1961-gokura (the castle town's storehouses or the river landing), kuramae-jawiki (the shogun's storehouse ground at Edo), l7r-budgets (the setting's Imperial granaries)

The rice went to the castle town or to the river. Tax rice left the village store for the rice storehouses in the castle town that the lord named, or for a river landing to be shipped. Both ends are attested as buildings. Takayama castle kept a rice storehouse in its third bailey, the outer ward of the castle. The Kumamoto domain gathered its tax rice at Kawashiri, a river port that was also its naval port: the domain kept a magistrate's office, a lodge and storehouses there; normally 200,000 bales of tax rice came in from five of the domain's districts and were shipped on to the domain's warehouse estate at Osaka; the old drawings show nine storehouse buildings under three names; and a landing about 150 m (some 490 ft) long survives on the river.

At the very top, a storehouse town. The shogun's own rice store at Edo, on the Sumida river, was a ground of 67 storehouses bounded by the river on one side and moats on the other three - the capital's scale, not a provincial city's.

In the setting, the Emperor's share of the taxes does not travel to the capital: it is stored in Imperial granaries in each domain's capital and spent there. How large any one storehouse was, at the castle or at the port, is on no page read.

The rule the map follows: where a provincial city keeps its tax rice is a knob, rolled per city - both forms attested: a rice storehouse inside the castle's outer ward, or a storehouse ground at the city's river landing with its own quay and a small office beside it. A large domain's port store ran to about nine storehouse buildings, which is the top of the range for a provincial city; the count for a smaller city, and every storehouse's size, are a guess.
