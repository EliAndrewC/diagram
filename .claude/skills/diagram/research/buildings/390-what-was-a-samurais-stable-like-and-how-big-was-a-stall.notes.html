• kotobank-umaya - 「A warrior's stable, that is, one attached to a shoin-style residence, has the tachiba where horses are tied, a zashiki called tozamurai, and an earth-floored kusa-no-ma. The Rakuchu rakugai screens and the Shomei, the manual handed down in the Heinouchi family, the shogunate's master carpenters, show a gable-entered stable 3 ken long, considered the most formal of its time. The tachiba is board-floored, not earth」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「武家の馬屋，すなわち書院造の邸宅に設けられた馬屋は，馬をつないでおく立て場，遠侍と呼ばれる座敷と土間の草の間をもつ。《洛中洛外図屛風》や幕府大棟梁平内（へいのうち）家の伝書《匠明（しようめい）》には，妻入りで桁行3間の馬屋が描かれ，当時もっとも正式のものとされている。立て場は土間でなく板張りで」) (the Heibonsha world encyclopedia entry)
  • jaanus-umaya - 「The stables used by the warrior class bushi 武士 were substantial freestanding structures in the shoin style」 (the source's own English) and 「The classic 16th-17th century examples, as recorded in the master carpenter's manual *Shōmei 匠明, and excavated at the site of the Asakura 朝倉 mansion at Ichijōdani 一乗谷 in Fukui Prefecture were 3, 5 or 7 bays in length.」 and 「None survive」 and 「In the warrior class stable, separate stalls of one bay width were provided.」 and 「the stalls had raised timber floors itabari 板張」 (the source's own English)
  • kotobank-umaya - 「Hikone castle's stable is a large one, L-shaped, with 21 small bays and a grooms' rest room at the end.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「彦根城の馬屋はL字形の平面をもち，21の小間，端部に馬丁の休息所をもつ大規模なものである。」)
  • kotobank-umaya - 「In the Tohoku region and elsewhere, where winters are long, stables are wide; those of the Kinki region and elsewhere are narrow, about 3 m square.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「冬が長い東北地方などでは，馬屋は広く，近畿地方などのものは狭く，約3m四方ほどである。」) (of the farmhouse's stable)
