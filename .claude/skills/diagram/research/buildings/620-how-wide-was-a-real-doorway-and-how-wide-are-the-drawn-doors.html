How wide was a real doorway, and how wide are the drawn doors?

Sources: madoken-odoguchi (the big door and the ordinary one), hongofuji-koiwai (a farmhouse's one-ken entrance and its wicket), s-kent-kuratomae (a storehouse's doors)

The sources give doorways in ken and shaku; a ken is about 6 ft, half a ken about 3 ft, and a shaku about a foot. A farmhouse's main way in was the large opening at its front that led to the earth floor, closed by a big board door that slid to one side or parted to both; the big door was wider than an ordinary door, and the word for it originally meant a door wider than half a ken. Read the other way, that puts an ordinary door at up to half a ken, about 3 ft - this project's reading of the definition, not a width the page states. At a farm or fishing-village house the everyday entrance to the earth floor was usually a sliding door one ken wide, about 6 ft, with a wicket set in it, and the wicket was what the household normally used; a wicket is a low, narrow doorway one ducks through. A storehouse's doorway can be closed by double hinged doors, each leaf of standard size 3.5 shaku wide and 6 high, about 3.5 by 6 ft. No page read gives the width of a kitchen door, of a guest entrance, or of the opening a storehouse's leaves close over.

On the three magistracy sheets a door is drawn from about 3.3 ft to 10 ft wide. The kitchen doors and the doors of the lodgings, the barracks and the servants' rows are mostly about 8 to 8.7 ft, Hayakawa's servants' door 5.3 ft; the storehouse doors about 4.7 ft; the karo's side door 3.3 ft at Ochiba and Ubame but 8.7 ft at Hayakawa; Hayakawa's guest-quarters entry 10 ft; the writing pavilion's door 6 ft and each parley-room door about 7.3 ft.

What this means for a plan: a drawn door's width is a MAP DRAWING CONVENTION, not a measurement. The working doors are drawn two to three times the width of an ordinary half-ken door, and wider than a farmhouse's one-ken main door, so that they read at the sheet's scale. The real widths are about 3 ft for an ordinary door (this project's reading of the big door's definition), about 6 ft for a one-ken main sliding door with a wicket in it, and a storehouse's paired leaves about 3.5 ft each.
