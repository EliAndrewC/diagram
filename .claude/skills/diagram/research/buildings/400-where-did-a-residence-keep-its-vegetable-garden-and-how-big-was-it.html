Where did a residence keep its vegetable garden, and how big was it?

Sources: boso-no-mura-takei (the small plot west of a middle-rank house), matsushiro-bukeyashiki (the vegetable field over half the Yokota grounds, and where Matsushiro houses put their garden)

Samurai grew their own vegetables, on anything from a kitchen plot to half their grounds. The Boso-no-mura house, a reproduction of a middle-rank retainer's house of the Sakura domain, has its vegetable garden on the west side of the house, about 1 se (about 100 m2, about 1,070 sq ft); farmers from nearby came to work it, and it was called the soup-greens field, planted mainly with leafy greens. At Matsushiro the Yokota house of a 150-koku middle-rank retainer keeps a vegetable garden among its buildings, and about half of its 1,012-tsubo grounds was vegetable field, a sign of the effort to feed the household itself. The formal garden of a Matsushiro house lay on the south side of the building, generally with a pond, and the farm ground was a working space for farm work and housework. A vegetable garden seated north of the house was searched for and not found; the shady rear puts it there by reasoning from the sun.

What this means for a plan: a residence has a vegetable garden, off the south side that the formal garden holds. Its size is a knob, rolled per settlement between the two attested forms, a soup-greens plot of about 1,000 sq ft and a field over about half the grounds. Seating it to the north is a GUESS; the one plot whose side is given lay to the west.
