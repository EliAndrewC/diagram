• amado-kotobank - 「A board door set outside the openings of Japanese buildings; it runs on a one-groove sill and is usually stored in a tobukuro when not in use.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「和風建築の開口部の外側に設ける板戸で，一本溝の敷居の上を走り，不用時には戸袋に納められるのが普通である。」) and 「The amado was devised in the Momoyama period; its use at Toyotomi Hideyoshi's Jurakudai is an early example.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「雨戸が創案されたのは桃山時代で，豊臣秀吉の聚楽第で用いられたのはその早い例である。」) (the Heibonsha world encyclopedia entry)
  • amado-kotobank - 「Its purposes are protection against wind and rain, watchfulness at night, and keeping warm. In the 16th century it took the form of board doors sliding past each other, set between the posts at the veranda edge; in the 17th century the form with a tobukuro, set in a one-groove sill and lintel, was created.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「風雨に対する保護、夜間の用心、保温を目的とする。16世紀には縁先の柱間に建てられた引き違いの板戸であったが、17世紀に入って戸袋(とぶくろ)をもち、一本溝の敷居、鴨居(かもい)に建てる形式がつくられた。」) (the Nipponica encyclopedia entry)
  • amado-kotobank - 「it is stored in the tobukuro in the daytime」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「昼間は戸袋に格納するようになっている」) (the Britannica entry)
