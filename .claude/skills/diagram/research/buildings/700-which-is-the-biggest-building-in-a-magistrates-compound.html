Which is the biggest building in a magistrate's compound, and how do the rest rank?

Sources: takayama-jinya-gifu (the floor area of every building of one intendant's office), takayama-jinya-jawiki (what the office was, and the numbered storehouses in the rice store), japan-heritage-takayama-onkura (the storehouse was built as a rice store), fuchu-joge-jinya (the one main building of a small branch office)

One office was measured building by building, and its order is clear. The Takayama Jin'ya, the shogunate's intendancy for Hida province, is the one such office that survives, and its buildings have these floor areas: the rice storehouse about 11,200 sq ft (1,042.56 m²), the office about 8,100 sq ft (754.75 m²), the intendant's residence about 6,400 sq ft (597.30 m²), the storehouse-keepers' rowhouse about 3,700 sq ft (342.83 m²), the kitchen storehouse about 740 sq ft, the book storehouse about 450 sq ft and the gatehouse about 400 sq ft. The residence and the rowhouse are restorations of the 1990s, not the old buildings themselves.

So the office out-measures the residence, and the residence the staff's rowhouse. That is the order the size hierarchy on this page already draws (The compound has a size hierarchy), and here it rests on a measured office instead of on this project's reading: the working office is the largest hall, about a quarter again the size of the household's.

But the largest building of all was a storehouse. Takayama's rice store, which this project reads as a single long building divided into numbered storehouses, is larger than the office and about three-quarters again the size of the residence. It held the rice of a whole province (The granary is a staging node), so it is the top of the range, not a county's granary; but it shows that the rule "no storage building out-sizes the residence" is not a historical one. A small office was different again: the Joge branch office, to which three officials came at the end of the shogunate, had one central building of about 80 tsubo (about 2,850 sq ft) holding its rooms together.

Where the stable ranked, and how a barracks compared with it, is on no page read here.

The rule the map follows: the office hall is the largest hall, the residence next, the staff's rowhouse below the residence, and the records store and the other small storehouses well below the rowhouse - that order is accurate. The grain storehouse is ranked by how much rice the posting holds: at a county office that ships its rice on it stays below the residence; at a posting that stores a wide territory's rice it may be the largest building in the compound. The stable's place in the order is a guess.
