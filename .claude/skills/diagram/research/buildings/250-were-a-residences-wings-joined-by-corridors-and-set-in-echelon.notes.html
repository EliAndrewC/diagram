• watariroka-kotobank - 「A corridor between two buildings, for joining them.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「二つの建物の間にあって、これをつなぐための廊下。」) (Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten)
  • irikawa-kotobank - 「It was in the Momoyama and Edo-period residences of the shoguns and daimyo that corridors inside buildings developed remarkably: besides corridors joining buildings to one another, tatami-floored corridors set so as to surround the outside of the guest zashiki (sometimes called hiroen or irikawa), and corridors with many rooms ranged along one or both sides, were used.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「建物内部の廊下が著しく発達したのは桃山･江戸時代の将軍や大名等の邸宅で，建物を相互に結ぶ廊下のほか，客座敷の外側をとりまくように設けられた畳敷きの廊下(広縁，入側（いりがわ）と呼ぶことがある)や，片側や両側に多くの部屋を並べた廊下などが用いられた。」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, old edition, from its article on the corridor)
  • irikawa-kotobank - 「Corridors are hardly seen in the farmhouses and townhouses of the Edo period, or in the houses of middle and lower samurai」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の農家･町家や，中･下級の武士住宅には廊下はほとんど見られない」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, old edition, from its article on the corridor)
  • katsura-rikyu-jawiki - 「The shoin of Katsura Rikyu, the four shoin halls the "Ko-shoin", "Naka-shoin", "Gakki-no-ma" and "Shin-goten", stand in echelon.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「桂離宮の書院は「古書院」「中書院」「楽器の間」「新御殿」の4つの書院群が雁行している。」) and 「On the flat ground on the west bank of the pond the Ko-shoin, Naka-shoin and Shin-goten stand in echelon from northeast to southwest.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「池の西岸の平坦地には古書院、中書院、新御殿が北東から南西へ雁行形に並ぶ。」)
  • katsura-rikyu-jawiki - 「built one after another from the first years of Genna to the first years of Kanbun (about 1615 - about 1662)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「元和初年から寛文初年（1615年頃 - 1662年頃）にかけて順次建立された」) and 「over nearly half a century, in three phases」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「半世紀近くにわたり、三期に分けて」)
  • touken-world-buke-madori - 「Looking at the plan of the main house, it is a hojo form with a six-room plan divided front and back, a form long seen in Zen temples.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「主屋の間取りを見てみると、前後に仕切られた六間取り（むまどり）の方丈形式で、これは古くから禅宗寺院に見られた形式です。」) and 「First, from the "genkan-no-ma" there is the "hikae-no-ma", then come the "ima" and the "ni-no-ma", then the "zashiki" and the "oku-zashiki"; beside the ima is the kitchen, projecting to the east.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「まず、「玄関の間」から「控えの間」があり、「居間」と「二の間」と続き、「座敷」と「奥座敷」、居間の横には東側に突き出た台所があります。」) (the Kuchiba house at Hagi, of about 1,000 koku)
