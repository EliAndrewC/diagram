• kotobank-shikidai - 「In warrior residences the projecting board floor for setting down a palanquin was called the genkan and the room of reception the shikidai, but in the Edo period the whole came to be called the genkan, and shikidai came to mean, as in sense 3, the lower board floor where one bows in greeting and seeing off guests.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「武家屋敷で駕籠をおろすための突き出た板床を「玄関」、取次ぎの部屋を「敷台」と呼び分けていたが、江戸時代になって全体を「玄関」と呼ぶようになり、「敷台」は③のように客の送迎に際して礼をする一段低い板敷を指すようになった。」) (from the note on the word in the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Great Dictionary of the Japanese Language)
  • kominkai-genkan - 「The shikidai entrance of that time had a step; the palanquin was brought right alongside it, and by stepping up onto it one could go straight from the palanquin into the building without getting down onto the ground.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「当時の式台の入口には段差があり、駕籠を直接横付けしてこの段差に上がることで地面に降りることなく駕籠からそのまま建物の中に入ることができるようになっていました。」)
  • kominkai-genkan - 「The shikidai was a special entrance room for receiving formal guests」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「式台は公式のお客さんを迎える特別な入口部屋」)
  • jaanus-uchigenkan - 「Whereas use of the main genkan was restricted to the head of the household and honored guests, the uchigenkan was used by other household members and senior household staff.」
  • fuchu-joge-pamphlet - the labels 「entrance, step, main gate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「玄関・敷台・大門」) on the Meiji 2 (1869) drawing the pamphlet reproduces, the entrance and its step set on the side of the central building that faces the ground inside the main gate
  • bjd-qing-yamen - 「stepping through the main gate is the open front court, planted with dozens of stout junipers」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「步入大门就是空阔的前庭，内植数十株粗大的桧柏」) (of the Zhili governor-general's yamen, 直隶总督署, whose gate plaque the article quotes just before)
