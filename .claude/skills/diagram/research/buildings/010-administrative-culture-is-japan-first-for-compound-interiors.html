Administrative culture is JAPAN-first for compound interiors

Sources: takayama-jinya-city, takayama-jinya-jawiki (Japan); neixiang-yamen-zhwiki, henan-neixiang (China), machi-bugyo-jawiki, daikan-jawiki (an intendant's small staff), xuli-zhwiki (a Chinese county's many clerks)

The primary models for a magistrate's manor are the Edo-period daikansho/jin'ya (rural intendant's office; Takayama Jin'ya is the surviving anchor) and machi-bugyōsho (urban magistracy). The Chinese county yamen documents a fuller compound grammar - office-front/residence-rear, a court hall over kneeling litigants, in-compound shrines, granary + archive (in this page's guess; on no page read) + jail - and is drawn on for enrichment, but where the two traditions differ (jail scale, clerk counts, a codified central axis) the Japanese civilian form leads, scaled to the setting's demographics.
