• kotobank-kuchimai - 「Rice that the intendant managing a lord's lands collected besides the main annual tax, at a fixed rate according to the cultivated area or the assessed tax.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) - 「In the early Edo shogunate, kuchimai and kuchiei went solely to the intendant's office expenses, and their use - officials' wages, the cost of brush, ink and paper and so on - was left to the intendant.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten entry on the page)
  • kotobank-kuchimai - 「At the start of the Edo period it was granted to the intendants as the expenses of the intendants who collected the annual tax, but because the intendants' expenses were cheaper than that kuchimai, from 1725 on kuchimai was paid to the shogunate and the intendants' expenses came to be provided by the shogunate.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Hyakka Jiten Mypedia entry on the page; the other entry there gives the intendants' misuse of public money as the reason for the change)
  • kuchimai-jawiki - 「at intendant offices whose kuchimai/kuchiei income was poor and could not be expected to grow, it became one cause of diverting the main annual tax to office expenses and of bribery」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-kuchimai - 「the system was changed in 1725 (Kyoho 10): kuchimai and kuchiei were all to be paid directly into the shogunate's storehouse together with the annual tax, and the office expenses were to be provided separately by the shogunate.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • bushijyoshi-daikan-jinya - 「The personnel costs and business-trip travel expenses of the people who worked at the jin'ya were all paid to the intendant, but it seems the amount was not sufficient. It is said that 90% of intendants carried a debt called hikioi, from diverting the annual tax.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (a blog post whose references are Nishizawa Atsuo's Daikan no nichijo seikatsu, 2014, and Yamamoto Hirofumi's 2018 book on the intendants; it hedges both sentences, and neither book was read)
  • bushijyoshi-daikan-jinya - 「Drafts blow in through the double shoji, and when he reads at night the sound of the wind is eerie; on snowy days, if even a little wind blows, snow piles up on the veranda; and besides that the freezing-cold toilet and so on - he wrote that, though bewildered by the differences in customs and climate, he was laughing it off cheerfully together with his family.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) - 「Toyoda Tomonao, the "gundai" of Hida Takayama, wrote of how he lived in letters to his father.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
