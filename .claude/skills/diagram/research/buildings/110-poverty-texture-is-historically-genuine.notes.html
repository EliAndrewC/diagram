  • kotobank-kuchimai - 「At the start of the Edo period it was granted to the intendants as the expenses of the intendants who collected the annual tax, but because the intendants' expenses were cheaper than that kuchimai, from 1725 on kuchimai was paid to the shogunate and the intendants' expenses came to be provided by the shogunate.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代当初は年貢徴収に当たる代官の経費として代官に下付したが，代官の経費はその口米より安いものだったので1725年以後口米は幕府に納入され，代官の経費は幕府からの支給となった。」) (the Hyakka Jiten Mypedia entry on the page; the other entry there gives the intendants' misuse of public money as the reason for the change)
  • kuchimai-jawiki - 「at intendant offices whose kuchimai/kuchiei income was poor and could not be expected to grow, it became one cause of diverting the main annual tax to office expenses and of bribery」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「口米・口永収入が乏しく増収も望めない代官所では本年貢の代官所経費への流用や贈収賄の一因になったり」)
  • kotobank-kuchimai - 「the system was changed in 1725 (Kyoho 10): kuchimai and kuchiei were all to be paid directly into the shogunate's storehouse together with the annual tax, and the office expenses were to be provided separately by the shogunate.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「1725年（享保10）に制度を改め，口米，口永はすべて年貢とともに幕府蔵へ直納されることとなり，代官所経費は別に幕府より給付されることとなった。」)
  • bushijyoshi-daikan-jinya - 「The personnel costs and business-trip travel expenses of the people who worked at the jin'ya were all paid to the intendant, but it seems the amount was not sufficient. It is said that 90% of intendants carried a debt called hikioi, from diverting the annual tax.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「陣屋で働く人々の人件費、出張旅費は全て代官に支給されていましたが充分な額ではなかったようです。 代官の9割が「引負（ひきおい）」という年貢流用による負債を抱えていたそうです。」) (a blog post whose references are Nishizawa Atsuo's Daikan no nichijo seikatsu, 2014, and Yamamoto Hirofumi's 2018 book on the intendants; it hedges both sentences, and neither book was read)
  • bushijyoshi-daikan-jinya - 「Drafts blow in through the double shoji, and when he reads at night the sound of the wind is eerie; on snowy days, if even a little wind blows, snow piles up on the veranda; and besides that the freezing-cold toilet and so on - he wrote that, though bewildered by the differences in customs and climate, he was laughing it off cheerfully together with his family.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「隙間風が二重の障子から吹き込んで夜に読書をしていると風の音が気味が悪い 雪が降る日は少しでも風が吹いたら縁側に雪が積もる そのほか極寒のトイレのことなど習慣や気候の違いに戸惑いながらも家族とともに明るく笑い飛ばしていると書き送っています。」) - 「Toyoda Tomonao, the "gundai" of Hida Takayama, wrote of how he lived in letters to his father.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「飛騨高山の「郡代」豊田友直は生活の様子を父親への手紙に書いています。」)
