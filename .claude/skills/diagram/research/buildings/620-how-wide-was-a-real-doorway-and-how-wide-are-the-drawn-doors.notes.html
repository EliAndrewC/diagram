  • madoken-odoguchi - 「A large opening set at the front entrance leading to the earth floor of a folk house.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「民家の土間に通じる表入口に設けられる大きな開口部。」) and 「A big door such as a board door is fitted, as a door sliding to one side or sliding apart to both sides.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「板戸などの大戸を建て込み、片引き戸や両引き戸とする。」) and 「The big door is wider than an ordinary door」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大戸は通常の戸より幅が広く」) and 「"Ōdo" is a word that originally referred to a door wider than half a ken」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「「大戸」は本来、半間幅より広い戸を指す語であり」)
  • hongofuji-koiwai - 「The houses of farming and fishing villages had entrances at the front of the earth floor (for daily use) and at the back (for cooking).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農漁村の民家の出入口は土間回り正面(日常)、背面(炊事)の出入口がありました。」) and 「Usually the entrance to the earth floor was fitted with a one-ken-wide big door sliding to one side (with a wicket), and ordinarily the wicket was used.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「通常土間の出入口は一間巾の片引きの大戸(潜り戸付)を建て、普段は潜り戸を利用していました。」)
  • hongofuji-koiwai - 「A door set in a low, narrow entrance that cannot be passed without lowering one's head is called a kugurido (wicket).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「頭を下げなければ通れないような、高さが低く、幅の狭い出入口に設ける戸を潜り戸と云います。」)
  • s-kent-kuratomae - 「Here there are three kinds of door, from the outside: the double hinged doors, the urajiro door and the screen door.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「ここには外側から、観音扉、裏白戸、網戸の三種の戸が存在します。」) and 「The size of one leaf of the double hinged doors is 3.5 shaku wide and 6 shaku high as the standard size」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「観音扉の一枚の大きさは、幅3.5尺、高さ6尺を常寸とし」) (said of a storehouse's doorway, the tomae-guchi)
