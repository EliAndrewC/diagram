• jigen-ryu-jawiki - 「it is characterized by single-mindedly repeating practice aimed at real combat, such as "tategi-uchi", striking fiercely left and right at a standing timber with a shout from the stance called "tonbo"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-tategi-uchi - 「its practicality of the single attack, its basic technique of "tategi-uchi", practicing against a standing timber with a round staff, and the like - a regional character preserved conservatively in Satsuma - are a singular presence」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「The founder was Tōgō Tōbei Shigetaka (1561-1643), a retainer of the Shimazu.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia, old edition, under Jigen-ryū)
  • nodachi-jigen-ryu - 「The practice ground is based on the open air and bare feet, and practice mainly repeats "tategi-uchi".」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Incidentally, though the two are easily confused, it is a different school from the other Jigen-ryū (示現流), whose founder is Tōgō Shigetaka, a Satsuma domain retainer of the early Edo period.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • bujutsukarate-tategi - 「the main line's is a training method just as the word says: a log of a little over 2 meters is buried vertically about 70 centimeters in the ground, and the wooden sword is struck at that "standing timber" in kesa and reverse-kesa cuts, and the height struck is about from one's own shoulder to the pit of the stomach.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「A "yokogi" is a bundle of a dozen or more long thin branches, laid down at knee height, and the wooden sword is struck into it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「In short, this school's "yokogi-uchi" was a "children-only" training method, and the number of "yokogi" used was not a dozen or more as now, but "one to a few", enough to act as a spring.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • aikidoshibuya-yokogi - 「Then the teacher told me to make a stand for training strikes, so I set two Y-shaped natural poles in the ground.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It was to lay bundled tree branches on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Saitō Morihiro, quoted from his book) and 「"Such a thin thing is no use," he said, and split the Y-shaped natural poles clean in two with his wooden sword.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Saitō, quoted) and 「It was in 1942 that the founder built the Aikien at Iwama and moved there.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It was in 1946 that Saitō Morihiro sensei entered at Iwama.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It is said the Satsuma domain samurai of old struck standing trees or bundles of brushwood ten thousand times a day, but I did about five hundred at most.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Tada Hiroshi, quoted)
