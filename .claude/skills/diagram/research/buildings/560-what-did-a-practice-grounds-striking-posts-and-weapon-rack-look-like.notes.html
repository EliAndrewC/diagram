• jigen-ryu-jawiki - 「it is characterized by single-mindedly repeating practice aimed at real combat, such as "tategi-uchi", striking fiercely left and right at a standing timber with a shout from the stance called "tonbo"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「『蜻蛉（とんぼ）』と呼ばれる構えから、立木に向かって気合と共に左右激しく斬撃する『立木打ち（たてぎうち）』など、実戦を主眼に置いた稽古をひたすら反復する事に特徴がある」)
  • kotobank-tategi-uchi - 「its practicality of the single attack, and its basic technique of "tategi-uchi", practicing against a standing timber with a round staff, make it a singular presence, a regional character preserved conservatively in Satsuma」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「その攻撃一本の実戦性と，丸棒で立木に向かって稽古する〈立木打ち〉の基本技法など，薩摩に保守的に存続した地域性は特異な存在である。」) and 「The founder was Tōgō Tōbei Shigetaka (1561-1643), a retainer of the Shimazu.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「流祖は，島津氏の家臣東郷藤兵衛重位（しげたか）(1561‐1643)。」) (Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia, old edition, under Jigen-ryū)
  • nodachi-jigen-ryu - 「The practice ground is based on the open air and bare feet, and practice mainly repeats "tategi-uchi".」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「稽古場は、露天・素足を基本とし、主に「立木打ち」を反復して稽古する。」)
  • bujutsukarate-tategi - 「the main line's is a training method just as the word says: a log of a little over 2 meters is buried vertically about 70 centimeters in the ground, and the wooden sword is struck at that "standing timber" in kesa and reverse-kesa cuts, and the height struck is about from one's own shoulder to the pit of the stomach.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「本家の方はその言葉通りの鍛錬法なのであり、２メートル強ほどの丸太を７０センチほど垂直に地中に埋め込み、その「立木」に向かって木刀を袈裟や逆袈裟に打ち込むのであり、打ち込む高さは自身の肩から水月くらいの高さなのである。」) and 「A "yokogi" is a bundle of a dozen or more long thin branches, laid down at knee height, and the wooden sword is struck into it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「「横木」とは、細長い木の枝を十数本束ねたもので、これを膝の高さに横たえて、それに木刀を打ち込むのである。」)

  • kotobank-katanakake - 「A stand or shelf on which swords are hung lying sideways. Forked things such as deer antlers are stood at the same height left and right. It is usually in two tiers so as to hang the long and short swords.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「刀を横にして掛けておく台や棚。左右同じ高さに叉(また)のある鹿の角などを立てる。普通大小を掛けるよう二段になっている。」) and 「"First he took off his sword and hung it on the sword rack of the guardroom"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「「先づ刀を脱して詰所の刀架(カタナカケ)に懸ける」」) (Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, quoting Mori Ōgai's Ōshio Heihachirō, 1914)
  • kotobank-mitsu-dogu - 「In the Edo period, the tsukubō, the sasumata and the sodegarami, a kind of long-shafted weapon said to have been used in arresting criminals and the like, taken as a set.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸期に、犯人逮捕の際などに用いたという長柄(ながえ)の武器の一種である、突棒(つくぼう)、刺股(さすまた)、袖搦(そでがらみ)を一組としていう。」) and 「Because they were kept at checkpoints, guard posts and the like in the Edo period, they are also called "the guard post's three tools".」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸期に関所、番所などに常備したので「番所の三つ道具」ともいう。」) and 「since the Shijōgawara pleasures screen, already a work of the early Edo period, depicts them stood in a row along the bamboo palisade beside the tower (entrance) of the kabuki and noh playhouses, they were probably used as a set from early on as tools symbolic of public order and dignity.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「すでに江戸初期の製作である『四条河原(がわら)遊楽図屏風(びょうぶ)』の歌舞伎(かぶき)の小屋、能楽の小屋の櫓(やぐら)（入口）の脇(わき)の竹矢来(たけやらい)に沿って立て並べているようすが描かれているので、治安・威儀の象徴的な道具として早くから一組として用いられていたものであろう。」) (Nipponica)
