• engawa-kotobank - 「One set inside the building is called the irikawa or irikawa-en, and in recent years is called the engawa.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「建物内に設けられたものを入側(いりかわ)あるいは入側縁といい、近年は縁側とよんでいる。」) and 「One set on the outer edge of the building is called the en or nure-en, and because its floor is usually one step lower than the interior it is also called the ochi-en.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「建物外周に設けられたものは縁、濡(ぬ)れ縁といい、通常床(ゆか)が内部より一段下がっているので落ち縁ともいう。」) (Nipponica, Hirai Kiyoshi)
  • engawa-kotobank - 「In the shoin-style house too that tradition remained: a wide veranda was set between the main room and the garden and used as a secondary space of the main room.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「書院造の住宅でもその伝統は残り，主室と庭園との間に広い縁を設け，主室の副次的な空間として使用していた。」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Suzuki Mitsuru)
  • engawa-kotobank - 「In farmhouses, though it differed by region, from about the middle of the seventeenth century engawa came to be set outside the zashiki, and further from the middle of the eighteenth century engawa were set on the front of the main rooms, and the form with white shoji lined up behind the engawa spread nationwide.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「民家においては地域により違いはあるが，17世紀の中ごろから座敷の外側に縁側が設けられるようになり，さらに18世紀中ごろから，主要室の表側に縁側が設けられ，縁側の奥に白い障子が並ぶ形式が全国的に普及した。」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Suzuki Mitsuru)
  • shoinzukuri-kotobank - 「In the Keicho and Genna years (1596-1624) at the start of the early modern period, the residences of the warrior daimyo were built in every region. Their great groups of halls were divided broadly into the front and the interior, and the main buildings - the hiroma, the taimenjo and the goza-no-ma of the front, and the oue (the lady's hall) of the interior - were all of the developed shoin-zukuri form.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世初頭の慶長・元和年間（1596-1624）には武家大名の邸宅が各地で造立された。」「大規模な殿舎群は表向きと奥向きに大別され，表向きの広間，対面所，御座間と，奥向きの御上（奥方御殿）の主要建物は，ひとしく発達した書院造形式のものであった。」) and 「Their rooms took as the upper seat a zashiki fitted with the zashiki decorations of tokonoma, shelves, tsuke-shoin and nando-gamae (often built as a jodan-no-ma), with lower seats such as the ni-no-ma and san-no-ma and a nando attached, and were accompanied on all four sides by hiroen and irikawa-en.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「それらの屋内諸室は床（とこ），棚，付書院，納戸構（なんどがまえ）の座敷飾を装置した座敷（上段間につくる場合が多い）を上座とし，二の間，三の間などの下座，納戸などを付属した構成をもち，四周に広縁，入側縁をともなった。」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Kawakami Mitsugu, of the daimyo mansions of the Keicho and Genna years)
  • irikawa-kotobank - 「Corridors are hardly seen in the farmhouses and townhouses of the Edo period, or in the houses of middle and lower samurai, but at the end of the Edo period there appear houses with a continuous engawa set along two or three sides, used as a passage to the privy and the bath, which comes to have a function like a corridor.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の農家･町家や，中･下級の武士住宅には廊下はほとんど見られないが，江戸時代末期には，家の二方あるいは三方に連続した縁側を設けて，便所や風呂場への通路に用いるものが現れ，廊下に似た機能をもつようになる。」) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, old edition, from its article on the corridor)
  • irikawa-kotobank - 「A passage one ken (about 1.8 meters) wide between the zashiki and the engawa.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「座敷と縁側との間にある一間（約一・八メートル）幅の通路。」) (Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten)
  • katsura-rikyu-jawiki - 「To the east of the ichi-no-ma and ni-no-ma there is a hiroen one ken wide. The veranda continues at a right angle along the south of the ichi-no-ma too, but on the south side its width becomes half a ken.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一の間・二の間の東には1間幅の広縁がある。縁は矩折れに一の間の南にも続くが、南側では幅が半間になる。」) (of the Ko-shoin)
  • katsura-rikyu-jawiki - 「From the east face of the building round to the south face runs a half-ken-wide, tatami-floored "bent irikawa-en".」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「建物の東面から南面にかけて、半間幅、畳敷の「折曲り入側縁」をめぐらす。」) (of the Naka-shoin)
