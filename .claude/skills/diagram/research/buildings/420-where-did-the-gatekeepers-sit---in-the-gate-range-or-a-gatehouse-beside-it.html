Where did the gatekeepers sit - in the gate range, or a gatehouse beside it?

Sources: tamba-kashiwara-jinya (the Kashiwara domain seat's gate range and its guardroom), matsue-bukeyashiki (the gate range as the gatekeepers' dwelling), takayama-jinya-city (the Takayama gate and its gatehouse, of 1832), takayama-jinya-jawiki (the gate and gatehouse among the buildings that survive), bansho-jawiki (the guardrooms at Edo castle's gates and at the Akamon), kitain-bansho (a freestanding guardroom beside a gate, and its size)

Both are attested. In the first form the guardroom is part of the gate range, the nagayamon, a long building with the gate through its middle. At the Kashiwara domain's seat the gate range is 13.5 ken long and 2 ken deep (about 81 by 12 ft), with three eight-mat rooms on one side of the gate and on the other an eight-mat room and an earth floor, the guardroom, the bansho. At Matsue the gate range was also the dwelling of the gatekeepers and the household's servants.

In the second form the gatehouse stands beside the gate as a building of its own. At Takayama the gate and the gatekeepers' house were both built in 1832, and each is listed separately, as a gabled building of one story; both survive. Edo castle's gates had their guardrooms, and the Akamon, now at the University of Tokyo, has one on each side. One such guardroom, a building of its own, is measured: the one built against the side of the Kita-in temple's gate at Kawagoe, a small gabled building 3 by 2 ken (about 18 by 12 ft). The Takayama gatehouse's size, and a freestanding gatehouse about 40 ft long, were searched for and not found.

What this means for a plan: a gatehouse beside the opening is attested, and it is the Takayama intendancy's form; the guardroom inside a long gate range is the other form, a knob between them. A depth of about 2 ken (about 12 ft) is attested for both. A freestanding gatehouse about 40 ft long is a GUESS: the one measured is about 18 ft long, and a building of 40 ft or more beside a gate is the gate range's scale.
