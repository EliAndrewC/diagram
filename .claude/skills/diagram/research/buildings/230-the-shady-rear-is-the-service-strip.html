The shady rear is the service strip

Sources: shoinzukuri-jawiki (the garden south of the formal rooms; the north side unaddressed), matsushiro-kankou-higuchi (what stood north and south at one house), kojodan-higuchi (the storehouse beyond a yard behind the house), bunka-yokota-dozo (one household storehouse measured), suumo-kura-size (the common size), matsushiro-bukeyashiki (two storehouses at one house), bunka-yokota-dozo-2 (the other one measured). The rest of the north service strip has no readable source and is reasoning from the sunny-south, shady-north rule

What the research found: a buke property's prized formal garden sat on the sunny SOUTH side facing the reception rooms, so the shady NORTH rear carried the household's service economy - storehouses, a vegetable garden only where it gets its sun (under "Where does a walled compound keep its vegetable garden, and does the sun decide it?"), and - by reasoning from that rule, no source placing them - a well, the family privy and the servants' quarters (the retainers' often in the nagayamon range). The decision: the north band behind the residence is drawn as a service strip (servants' nagaya + utility + attached privy) or narrowed to a ~6-10 ft cart/servant alley; a wide empty band there is internal dead space and also leaves the ~10 on-grounds servants - counted in the household's staff - drawn nowhere. The magistracy plans drawn so far each draw a servants' quarters and an attached family privy in that band.

The storehouse behind the house. At the Higuchi house of Matsushiro a watercourse runs through the grounds, the wood, the bamboo and the garden to its south, and to its north the main house, the storehouse, the rowhouse, the household shrine and the front gate; behind the main house lies a garden, and the storehouse stands beyond it. A household storehouse was small: the Yokota house at Matsushiro kept two, one 2.7 by 3.6 m (about 9 by 12 ft) and one of two storeys, 5.5 by 4.5 m (about 18 by 15 ft), and a plasterer who builds them puts the common size at about 2 by 3 ken, 3.64 by 5.46 m (about 12 by 18 ft). So a plan draws the household's storehouses behind the house at about 12 by 18 ft, the ground between them and the house named as working yard (two, as the Yokota house kept two; the number for any one sheet a guess).
