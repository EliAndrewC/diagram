How big was the rowhouse a magistrate's staff lived in?

Sources: jinya-kotobank (the staff's huts and rowhouses in an intendant's office), takayama-jinya-gifu (Takayama's storehouse-keepers' rowhouse), omori-daikansho-kunishitei (the gate rowhouses of an intendant's office), kaibara-jinya-tamba (a small lord's rowhouse gate and its rooms), shibata-ashigaru-nagaya (an eight-household foot soldiers' row), kanra-ashigaru-nagaya (the smallest unit of a foot soldiers' row)

An intendant's staff lived in rowhouses on the grounds. The intendant's office took in, besides the office and the residence, the huts and rowhouses that were the official houses of its junior officials (Staff housing spans a real spectrum).

Every rowhouse measured is long and narrow. The gate rowhouses of the intendant's office at Omori, which governed a silver mine and some 150 villages, run 33 m (about 108 ft) at a width of about 4 m (13 ft). The rowhouse gate of the Kaibara jin'ya, the seat of a 20,000-koku lord, is 13.5 ken long by 2 ken deep (about 81 by 12 ft), with three rooms of eight mats on one side of the gateway and one of eight mats and an earth-floored room on the other; the city's own description gives it as about 13 ken long, with a guardroom on one side and a horse-viewing room and a gun store on the other. The foot soldiers' row of the Shibata domain, built for eight households under one roof, is 43.6 by 7.3 m (about 143 by 24 ft). In the Obata domain's row, each unit is 2 ken wide and 3.5 ken deep (about 12 by 21 ft) - one living room, an entrance and a kitchen, the smallest dwelling - and a row held ten of them. At Takayama, the storehouse-keepers' rowhouse has a floor area of about 3,700 sq ft (342.83 m²), a restoration of 1992.

So a staff rowhouse is 12 to 24 ft deep and 80 to 145 ft long, built as a row of bays about 12 to 18 ft wide (the Obata unit's 2 ken; the Shibata row's length shared among its eight households, on this project's arithmetic). How many unmarried men shared a bay, as the setting's working platoon of about fifteen mostly would, is on no page read: every unit whose occupants are named housed a household.

The rule the map follows: the staff's barracks is a rowhouse - a long, narrow range 12 to 24 ft deep, built of bays about 12 to 18 ft wide, and up to about 145 ft long; that form and those limits are accurate. A square or squat barracks is not. The number of bays a platoon of about fifteen needs, and so the drawn length, is a guess.
