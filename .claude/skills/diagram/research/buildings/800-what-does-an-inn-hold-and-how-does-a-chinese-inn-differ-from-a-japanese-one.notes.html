• machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「The main facility of a post town was the hatago, basically a building of the same plan as the machiya. Past the big door the doma ran to the back, beside it the board-floored mise and daidokoro, and upstairs or at the back several rooms for lodging were fitted out. In the Tokaido post towns of the late Edo period, a projecting lattice on the road-side second floor was a mark of the hatago」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「宿場町の主要施設は旅籠屋であり、基本的には町屋と同じ平面形式の建物であった。大戸を入るとドマが奥まで伸び、その脇にはミセやダイドコロの板の間、二階あるいは奥に宿泊用部屋がいつくか設えられていた。江戸時代後期の東海道宿場町では、旅籠屋の往還側二階には出格子が特徴となっていた」)
  • toyokawa-akasaka-juku - 「A hatago consisted of an earth floor, a board-floored room, rooms, zashiki, the kitchen quarters, a bath and a privy, and where it faced the highway two stories was usual.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「旅籠屋は、土間・板の間・部屋・座敷・勝手向・湯殿および雪隠からなり、往還に面したところは二階建てが一般的でした。」); 「The Ohashiya was a large hatago, with a frontage of about 9 ken and a depth of about 23 ken.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大橋屋は、大旅籠で間口9間、奥行23間ほどありました。」)

  • jiemian-ancient-inns - 「The Ming had inns of the 'drink in front, lodge guests behind' kind, eating and lodging two in one」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「明朝有“前面吃酒，后面宿客”这样吃饭住宿二位一体的店」); 「But what existed in great numbers in the Ming were still the ordinary lodging inns spread through town and country, or, called together 'mule-and-horse', the 'great inns'. The mule-and-horse great inns mostly stood in the open country, and their lodgers were mostly laborers who came driving carts, who often cooked their own meals, so though the service was simple the price was cheap」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「然而在明朝大量存在的，还是分布在城乡的普通住宿客店，或统称为“骡马”的“大店”。一般骡马大店多设立于荒野，住宿者也多为驾车而来的劳动者，往往自己动手做饭菜吃，因此虽招待条件简陋，但价格便宜」)
  • netease-dachedian - 「On the land of the Northeast in the late Qing and the Republic, along the muddy main roads, one could always see courtyards with thatched roofs and earth walls」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「晚清和民国时期的东北大地上，沿着泥泞的交通要道，总能见到一座座草顶土墙的院落」); 「The siting of a cart inn was particular: mostly beside a main road, on high ground near water」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「大车店的选址都有讲究，多在要道旁、水源近的高地上」); 「The yard was fenced round with tall wooden stakes; inside, besides the mud-brick houses where people lived, there was always a spacious stable, and bundles of hay were piled in the corners of the walls.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「院子用高木桩围起来，里头除了住人的土坯房，必有宽敞的马厩，墙角堆着成捆的草料。」); 「the three or five rooms were all great kang running north and south, one kang sleeping more than twenty」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「三五间房里全是南北大炕，一铺炕能睡二十多人」)
