In what order do a residence's rooms run - the reception, the master's rooms, the family's?

Sources: shirobito-612-omote-oku (the castle palace from front to back), shoinzukuri-kotobank (the daimyo mansion's omote and oku, and the goza-no-ma), touken-world-buke-madori (an upper samurai's house with its zashiki at the far end), matsue-bukeyashiki (a middle-rank house with the master's living room apart from the room where he received)

A castle lord's palace ran from front to back in three parts. The omote in front held the entrance, the great halls and the shoin where the lord met retainers and envoys. Behind it lay the lord's own rooms - the room where the lord worked by day, and the bedroom - which are now called the naka-oku, though the word is not strictly a historical one and Edo drawings count those rooms as part of the omote. Beyond them was the oku, the lord's private rooms and the quarters of the lord's wife and consorts, where coming and going was strictly limited. A daimyo mansion was divided the same way, the goza-no-ma - the lord's own sitting room - standing with the great hall and the audience hall on the omote side, and the lady's palace on the oku side.

So at the palace scale the master's own rooms stand between the reception and the family, adjoining both, and the reception is at the front end of the house. The lord's suite beyond his study is, in these sources, a working room and a bedroom.

A smaller house sets the formal room at the far end instead. The Kuchiba house at Hagi, of an upper samurai of about a thousand koku, runs from the entrance room through an anteroom, the living room and a second room, to the zashiki and the inner zashiki. At the Matsue samurai house the master's living room is finished playfully, in contrast to the front of the house, while the zashiki where the master received guests is built for formality; the house keeps a family room and the wife's living room besides.

What this means for a plan: the room order is a knob with two attested forms - the palace order, reception in front, the master's rooms behind it, the family beyond; and the small house's order, the formal zashiki deepest in, the living rooms between it and the entrance. In both the reception room sits at one END of the sequence. A reception room set between the master's rooms and the family's is in neither form, and no source found supports it.
