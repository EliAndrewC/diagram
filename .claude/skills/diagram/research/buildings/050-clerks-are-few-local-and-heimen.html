Clerks are few, local, and heimen

Sources: the setting's own caste notes (scribes are heimen artisans) and budgets; tedai-jawiki (who the tedai were), xuli-zhwiki

Both traditions ran the paperwork on locally-hired commoners under a tiny elite staff: Japanese tedai were hired from the peasants and townsmen who knew the district, most of them the children of village and town officials; Chinese counties ran on dozens of statutory (hundreds of actual) local clerks and runners. Scaled to an L7R county (~6,800 inhabitants, vs ~100,000-200,000 for a Chinese county), that becomes ~3-4 clerks at ~4-5 koku/yr. They are scribes by caste (heimen artisans, in the setting's caste notes), live in town, commute to the manor, and as permanent locals are the office's institutional memory under successive magistrates - and its natural tax-farming pressure point.
