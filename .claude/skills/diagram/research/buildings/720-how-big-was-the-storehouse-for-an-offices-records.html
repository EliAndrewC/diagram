How big was the storehouse for an office's records?

Sources: takayama-jinya-gifu (the floor areas of Takayama's book storehouse and kitchen storehouse), takayama-jinya-jawiki (one book storehouse among the surviving buildings)

The one measured records store is small. The Takayama Jin'ya kept its documents in a book storehouse built in 1841, an earth-walled storehouse with a floor area of about 450 sq ft (41.46 m²); its other small storehouse, the kitchen's, is about 740 sq ft (68.82 m²). The book storehouse is among the buildings that survive. This was the records store of an office that governed a whole province, so on this project's reading a county office's was no larger. Why the records went into an earth-walled storehouse at all is the fire rule (Fire discipline).

The drawn records store is too big. The records storehouse the plans draw, about 32 to 36 ft long, is larger than Takayama's; a storehouse that size is the size of that office's kitchen storehouse, not its records store. How long and how deep a records storehouse was is on no page read.

The rule the map follows: a county office's records storehouse is an earth-walled kura with a floor area of no more than about 450 sq ft - accurate as a ceiling set by the one measured office; its proportions (for instance about 24 by 18 ft) are a guess.
