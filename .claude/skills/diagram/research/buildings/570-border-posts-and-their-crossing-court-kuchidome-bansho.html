Border posts and their crossing court (kuchidome bansho)

In the Edo period each domain kept guard posts, the kuchidome bansho, where its roads crossed into a neighboring domain or the shogun's land. They watched the people and goods going in and out - above all to stop the domain's produce leaving - and sometimes taxed the goods at a fixed rate.

The one surviving post read here, at Nuruyu, was laid out around what crossed: a gate across the road, an inspection hall facing it, and the officer's house behind. The ground between gate and hall was the road itself, so whoever crossed passed before the hall.

  • Few border posts survive; the Nuruyu post of Sendai domain is one.
    Its buildings of the time still stand, which is rare for such a post anywhere in the country. It commanded a mountain gorge where the road left Sendai for Akita domain.
  • At Nuruyu the road ran through the gate and past the front of the inspection hall.
    The front gate stood across the road itself. Facing it was the inspection hall (kendansho, where travelers and goods were examined), and the officer's residence stood to the right, behind the hall. The road entered by the gate and ran on in front of the hall.
  • The officer's house at Nuruyu is a farmhouse in form, but with a formal reception room.
    It measures 23.19 m (~76 ft) by 11.86 m (~39 ft), with an earth-floored side like any farmhouse, but it is furnished with a reception room in the shoin style.
  • A great checkpoint held far more than a domain's post.
    Hakone, the shogunate's own checkpoint on the Tokaido from 1619 to 1869, held within its fence a front post with an upper guardroom, a guards' room, a rest room and a bath; an opposite post with a lockup; stables, a street watch and a notice board.
  • Did a border post keep a court for receiving the neighboring domain's officials?
    No page we read describes one. What is recorded is ground for those crossing: the road inside the gate, before the inspection hall.
