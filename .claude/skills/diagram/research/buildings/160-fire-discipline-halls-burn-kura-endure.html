Fire discipline: halls burn, kura endure

Sources: takayama-jinya-gifu (the office listed among the timber buildings, the storehouses as earthen), takayama-jinya-city (the office's roof of wooden boards and shingles; it never burned), sado-bugyosho-fires (the Sado magistracy burned and was rebuilt five times), tfd-hongou-fire-history (Edo encouraged earthen-storehouse and earth-plastered building)

Administrative halls were timber buildings - Takayama's office is listed among the site's timber buildings, its storehouses as earthen ones - and they often burned: the Sado magistracy burned and was rebuilt five times, though Takayama's office, by good fortune, never did. Earth-walled building was the townsman's answer to fire: Edo came to permit, and then encourage, earthen-storehouse (dozo) and earth-plastered (nuriya) construction for townhouses that had been forbidden it. That, we judge, is why tax grain and documents got storehouse buildings of their own instead of rooms in the hall - a guess at the reason, since no source read says why. How big the records storehouse was is its own question (How big was the storehouse for an office's records?).
