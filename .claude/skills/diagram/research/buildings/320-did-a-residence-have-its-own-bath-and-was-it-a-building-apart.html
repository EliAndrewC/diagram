Did a residence have its own bath, and was it a building apart?

Sources: furo-kotobank (who had a bath in the Edo period, and what one cost), yokushitsu-kotobank (the family bath of warrior houses around 1600), shirobito-1717-takayama (the bath among the rooms of the Takayama residence), kanagawa-hatamoto-kaso (the bath named on a hatamoto's house plan), sayama-jinya-uematsu (the bath added on to a middle-rank house)

A bath of one's own was a mark of rank. In the Edo period a bath room, the yudono, was built in the few shoin-style residences of upper-rank samurai, while middle and lower samurai and ordinary people washed from a tub or went to the public bath; in a city a home bath meant digging a well and buying fuel and was a great luxury, and home baths spread only gradually in the latter half of the period. Around 1600 the family bath that noble and warrior houses kept was only a tub one person could get into, with a hearth built on, in a corner of the doma, the earth-floored part of the house, and it was seldom used for fear of fire and for the cost of fuel.

At a posting the bath went with the residence. The residence at the Takayama Jin'ya had, besides the rooms of the intendant, the family and the servants, an earth floor and kitchen, a well and a bath. The 1843 plan of a hatamoto's house in Edo names its yudono and its privy among the rooms it draws, though the page does not say whether the yudono stood in the house or apart from it. At the Sayama domain's middle-rank houses the bath and privy were meant to be added on to the house. A bath standing as a building of its own in a samurai compound, and the size of a residence's bath, were searched for and not found.

What this means for a plan: a posting's bath is a room of the residence, or a small addition to it, not a pavilion standing apart; placing it on the service side, with the doma, the kitchen and the well, is this project's reading of the Takayama list and of the bath in a corner of the doma; a house of middle rank or below may have none. Its size is a GUESS: nothing read gives one.
