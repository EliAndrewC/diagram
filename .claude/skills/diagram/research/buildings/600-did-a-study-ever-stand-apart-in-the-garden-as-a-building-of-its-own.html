Did a study ever stand apart in the garden, as a building of its own?

Sources: shosai-kotobank (a study as a room or a building), kyoto-ga-sanshisuimeisho (Rai San'yō's detached study in Kyoto), chinesepen-shuzhai (where a Chinese study stood in the compound)

A study is, by the dictionary, a room in a private house for reading and writing. Historically it could be a building as well as a room: the shoin, or the gakumonsho, the place of learning, was a room or a building for that purpose, and whichever room or building had the built-in writing desk was the study.

A study built as a building of its own survives in Kyoto. Sanshi Suimei-sho is the study-cum-tea room of Rai San'yō, a Confucian scholar, poet and historian of the late Edo period, who built it new on his estate in 1828; the estate's other buildings were lost after his death, and it alone stands, with its garden on its west side. In China the study drew apart from the living rooms over time into a place with a function and layout of its own, and it usually stood in the quiet part of the house compound, always next to the rear garden where there was one.

No page read describes a detached study at a magistrate's or any other official's compound, or gives a detached study's size.

What this means for a plan: a study that stands apart in the garden as a building of its own is ACCURATE as a form - a scholar's estate in Kyoto had one, and a Chinese compound set its study in the quiet part beside the garden. Giving one to a magistrate, in the inner garden of the posting, is a GUESS by extension from those, and its size is a GUESS: no readable source gives a detached study's size.
