• hanko-jawiki - 「As a nationwide tendency, the martial arts too were encouraged at the domain schools: pupils entered at seven or eight, learned letters first and studied the martial arts afterward, and graduated between fourteen or fifteen and about twenty. The educational content centered on sodoku (recitation) of the Four Books and Five Classics and on calligraphy, and in the late Edo period rangaku (Dutch learning) and, as martial arts, various arts such as swordsmanship were added.」 (translated; )
  • edo-three-dojos-jawiki - 「The three great dojos of bakumatsu Edo (bakumatsu Edo sandai dojo) refers to the following three representative dojos among the swordsmanship dojos of Edo in the bakumatsu period.」 (translated; ); of the Shigakukan, 「Kyoshin Meichi-ryu. In 1773 (An'ei 2), Momonoi Naoyoshi (the first Momonoi Shunzo) opened it at Nihonbashi Minami-Kayabacho (now Nihonbashi Kayabacho, Chuo ward)」 (translated; ); of the Genbukan, 「Hokushin Itto-ryu. In the autumn of 1822 (Bunsei 5), Chiba Shusaku opened it at Nihonbashi Shinagawacho. It later moved to Kanda Otamagaike.」 (translated; ); of the Renpeikan, 「Shinto Munen-ryu. In 1826 (Bunsei 9), the first Saito Yakuro opened it near Manaita bridge at the foot of Kudanzaka.」 (translated; )
  • dojo-jawiki - 「Practice was often held outdoors or on an earthen floor, but from the mid-Edo period onward, because bamboo-sword practice in swordsmanship and randori practice in jujutsu became central, board-floored and tatami practice halls came to be provided.」 (translated; ) (with 「Masters traveled the country visiting their pupils, and the practice place was a private residence or the like - there was no purpose-built facility」 (translated; ); the first describes the time before the mid-Edo period, the second the end of the Warring States period (the page opens that passage with the end of the Warring States period), and neither the countryside)
  • hagi-meirinkan-guide - 「The Hagi domain school Meirinkan is a domain school that the fifth lord Mori Yoshimoto opened inside the moats in Kyoho 4 (1719) for the education of the sons of the Mori house's retainers.」 (translated; ) (the page's 家巨 is its own misprint for 家臣, retainers)
  • kodokan-mito-jawiki - 「It was built inside the third bailey of Mito castle by Tokugawa Nariaki, the ninth lord of the Mito domain (before the Kodokan was founded, the ground was residences of the senior-retainer class, such as the Yamanobe family).」 (translated; )
