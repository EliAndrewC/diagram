• hanko-jawiki - 「As a nationwide tendency, the martial arts too were encouraged at the domain schools: pupils entered at seven or eight, learned letters first and studied the martial arts afterward, and graduated between fourteen or fifteen and about twenty. The educational content centered on sodoku (recitation) of the Four Books and Five Classics and on calligraphy, and in the late Edo period rangaku (Dutch learning) and, as martial arts, various arts such as swordsmanship were added.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「全国的な傾向として、藩校では武芸も奨励され、7歳・8歳で入学して第一に文を習い、後に武芸を学び、14歳・15歳から20歳くらいで卒業する。教育内容は、四書五経の素読と習字を中心として、江戸後期には蘭学や、武芸として剣術等の各種武術などが加わった。」)
  • edo-three-dojos-jawiki - 「The three great dojos of bakumatsu Edo (bakumatsu Edo sandai dojo) refers to the following three representative dojos among the swordsmanship dojos of Edo in the bakumatsu period. Shigakukan: Kyoshin Meichi-ryu. In 1773 (An'ei 2), Momonoi Naoyoshi (the first Momonoi Shunzo) opened it at Nihonbashi Minami-Kayabacho (now Nihonbashi Kayabacho, Chuo ward) ... Genbukan: Hokushin Itto-ryu. In the autumn of 1822 (Bunsei 5), Chiba Shusaku opened it at Nihonbashi Shinagawacho. It later moved to Kanda Otamagaike. Renpeikan: Shinto Munen-ryu. In 1826 (Bunsei 9), the first Saito Yakuro opened it near Manaita bridge at the foot of Kudanzaka.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「幕末江戸三大道場（ばくまつえどさんだいどうじょう）は、幕末期の江戸の剣術道場のうち、代表的な以下の3道場を指す。士学館 : 鏡新明智流。1773年（安永2年）、桃井直由（初代 桃井春蔵）が日本橋南茅場町（現 中央区日本橋茅場町）に開設し… 玄武館 : 北辰一刀流。1822年（文政5年）秋、千葉周作が日本橋品川町に開設。のちに神田於玉ヶ池に移転。 練兵館 : 神道無念流。1826年（文政9年）、初代斎藤弥九郎が九段坂下の俎橋付近に開設。」)
  • dojo-jawiki - 「Practice was often held outdoors or on an earthen floor, but from the mid-Edo period onward, because bamboo-sword practice in swordsmanship and randori practice in jujutsu became central, board-floored and tatami practice halls came to be provided.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋外や土間を稽古場とすることが多かったが、江戸時代中期以降、剣術では竹刀稽古、柔術では乱取り稽古が中心になっていったため、板張りや畳の稽古場が整備されるようになった。」) (with 「Masters traveled the country visiting their pupils, and the practice place was a private residence or the like - there was no purpose-built facility」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「師範は全国を廻って弟子のところに訪ねており、稽古場は個人屋敷を利用するなど専門の施設はなかった」); 「on shrine grounds」 and the rural framing are this page's)
