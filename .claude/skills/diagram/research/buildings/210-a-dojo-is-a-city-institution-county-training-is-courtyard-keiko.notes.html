• hanko-jawiki - 「As a nationwide tendency, the martial arts too were encouraged at the domain schools: pupils entered at seven or eight, learned letters first and studied the martial arts afterward, and graduated between fourteen or fifteen and about twenty. The educational content centered on sodoku (recitation) of the Four Books and Five Classics and on calligraphy, and in the late Edo period rangaku (Dutch learning) and, as martial arts, various arts such as swordsmanship were added.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「全国的な傾向として、藩校では武芸も奨励され、7歳・8歳で入学して第一に文を習い、後に武芸を学び、14歳・15歳から20歳くらいで卒業する。教育内容は、四書五経の素読と習字を中心として、江戸後期には蘭学や、武芸として剣術等の各種武術などが加わった。」)

  • dojo-jawiki - 「Practice was often held outdoors or on an earthen floor, but from the mid-Edo period onward, because bamboo-sword practice in swordsmanship and randori practice in jujutsu became central, board-floored and tatami practice halls came to be provided.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋外や土間を稽古場とすることが多かったが、江戸時代中期以降、剣術では竹刀稽古、柔術では乱取り稽古が中心になっていったため、板張りや畳の稽古場が整備されるようになった。」) (with 「Masters traveled the country visiting their pupils, and the practice place was a private residence or the like - there was no purpose-built facility」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「師範は全国を廻って弟子のところに訪ねており、稽古場は個人屋敷を利用するなど専門の施設はなかった」); the first describes the time before the mid-Edo period, the second the end of the Warring States period (the page opens that passage with the end of the Warring States period), and neither the countryside)
