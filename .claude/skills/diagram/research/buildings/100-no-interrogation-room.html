No interrogation room

Sources: tenmacho-jawiki (torture as a step of the Edo procedure), oshirasu-jawiki (no torture in the hearing court), takayama-jinya-official (Takayama's torture done in its jail in the town), takayama-jinya-city (the examination room beside the roofed court); the Rokugan decision is the GM's (2026-07)

Edo justice used judicial torture as a step of procedure for suspects who went on refusing to confess, with the senior councillors' (roju) approval, but not in the hearing court: the instruments set out there are thought to have been meant to overawe the parties, and no torture was done in it. At Takayama, whose examination room (ginmisho) and court are both roofed, the torture is reported to have been done in the jail, which stood in the town, not in the jin'ya. In Rokugan torture is unusual though not strictly forbidden, so magistracies keep no room purpose-built for it - questioning happens in the day office or the hearing court like any other business.
