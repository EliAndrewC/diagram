Tax rice was carried out from village granaries - temporary stores for its shipment, in places also a reserve against bad harvests; that it flowed on toward central stores, that it passed through the administrative compound, and that the office kura held rice in transit plus a local reserve, is this page's reading. Remote, high-transport-cost territory, this page reads, kept large on-site granary rows instead (Takayama's numbered storehouses, read here as its onkura, staged for all of Hida, the province it administered - the staging is unsourced). Which end a county sits on is readable from its settlement map - water access means through-flow, isolation means storage. That the rice waited either in an office's own storehouse or at a river landing, and how big those storehouses were, is its own question (How big was an office's grain storehouse?). The rice moved on HIRED boats flying an official pennant and inspected at the ports of call - the magistracy owns no hulls.
