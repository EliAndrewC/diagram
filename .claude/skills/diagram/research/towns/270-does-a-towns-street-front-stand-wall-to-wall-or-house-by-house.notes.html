  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「In post towns with heavy traffic of men and horses, such as those of the Tokaido and the Nakasendo, the main house was built along the highway across the full frontage of each lot, and in the late Edo period two-story, board-roofed inns with the same through-passage plan as the machiya of the great cities came to stand eave to eave.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「東海道，中山道など人馬の往来の盛んな宿場では，各敷地の街道沿いに間口いっぱいに主屋が建てられ，江戸時代後期には2階建て板葺きで，大都市の町家と同じような通りにわ式平面の旅籠屋が軒を連ねるようになった。」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, the section by Tamai Tetsuo)
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「Among the machiya of the Edo period there were ones with the structure of raising an udatsu with a small roof on the roof at the boundary with the neighbor, to prevent fire spreading from next door」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の町屋には、隣との境の屋根に小屋根付きのうだつをあげて、隣からの延焼を防ぐ構造を持ったものや」)
  • unnojuku-jawiki - 「on both sides of the old Hokkoku highway, 650 m long and 10 m wide, about 100 historic buildings used at the time remain, in the inn style, the silkworm-room style, with thatched roofs and so on」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「延長650 m、幅10 mの旧北国街道の両側には、旅籠屋造り、蚕室造り、茅葺屋根など当時使用された約100棟の歴史的な建物が残る」) ... 「"udatsu", walls for fire protection, can be seen on the roofs」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋根に防火用の壁である「うだつ」が見られ」)
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「By contrast, on side roads and in mountain stations and the like, inns with plans not much different from the thatched farmhouses around them often take a front garden on the highway side and stand in a row with a space between them and the neighboring house too.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「これに対し脇街道や山間の宿などでは，茅葺きの周辺農家とあまり変わらない平面の旅籠屋が街道側に前庭をとり，隣家との間にも間隔をとって並んでいる場合が多い。」)
