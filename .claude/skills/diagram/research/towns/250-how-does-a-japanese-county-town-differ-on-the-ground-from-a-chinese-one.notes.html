• zaigomachi-jawiki - 「In many cases the basis is a strip land division with townhouses lined up in one or two rows along a highway or a river, and no functional arrangement of samurai ground, townsmen's ground and temple ground like the castle town's is seen.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「多くの場合、街道や河川沿いに町家が一列ないし二列に並ぶ短冊形の地割を基本とし、城下町のような武家地・町人地・寺社地の機能別配置は見られない」)
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「At stations with heavy traffic of men and horses, such as the Tokaido and the Nakasendo, the main house was built along the highway across the full frontage of each plot, and in the later Edo period two-story, board-roofed inns with a through-passage plan like the townhouses of great cities came to stand eave to eave.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「東海道，中山道など人馬の往来の盛んな宿場では，各敷地の街道沿いに間口いっぱいに主屋が建てられ，江戸時代後期には2階建て板葺きで，大都市の町家と同じような通りにわ式平面の旅籠屋が軒を連ねるようになった。」)
  • siheyuan-zhwiki - 「its layout is a courtyard with buildings built on its four sides, usually made up of the main rooms, the east and west side rooms and the reverse-facing rooms, enclosing the courtyard in the middle from the four sides, whence its name」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「其格局为一个院子四面建有房屋，通常由正房、东西厢房和倒座房组成，从四面将庭院合围在中间，故得其名」) ... 「The reverse-facing rooms are the southernmost row of houses in the whole siheyuan; their eaves wall fronts the hutong and usually has no windows.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「倒座房是整个四合院中最南端的一排房子，其檐墙临胡同，一般不开窗。」) ... 「the doors of the south rooms open to the north, so they are called the "reverse-facing rooms".」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「南房门向北开，故称为“倒座房”。」) ... 「A large siheyuan is enclosed from outside by walls, the walls tall and without windows, to show its privacy.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「大四合院从外边用墙包围，墙壁高大，不开窗户，以显示其隐秘性。」) ... 「At that time two-court siheyuan and larger ones were usually the residences of officials and gentry.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「其时两进四合院以及更大的四合院通常是官宦和士绅的住所。」)
  • jinya-kotobank - 「In general, within the grounds of an intendant's jin'ya were set the honjin that was the intendant's official residence, the office for public business, the official houses of subordinate staff, rice stores and levy stores, the white-sand court [shirasu], the jail and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一般に代官陣屋では敷地内に代官の官舎である本陣，公用執務の役所，属僚の役宅，米蔵・運上蔵や白洲・牢屋などが設けられていた。」)
  • jinyamachi-jawiki - 「It is a town formed around a jin'ya in a domain or fief of the Edo period, or a daikansho or the like in the shogun's lands.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の藩・知行所における陣屋、もしくは天領における代官所などを中心に形成された町である。」) 「Samurai residences stood in rows, and merchants and craftsmen doing business with the warriors and others connected with the jin'ya gathered, and it developed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「侍屋敷が建ち並び、また陣屋に関わる武士などを相手に商売を営む商工業者が集まり発展した。」)
  • jinya-jawiki - 「The siting of jin'ya and their relation to the surrounding settlements is not uniform. Studies of early-modern jin'ya towns point out that while a 'jin'ya town', a small city in which jin'ya and settlement are one, is sometimes formed, at the seats of detached territories and the like there are also many examples built adjoining an existing settlement that can hardly be called planned towns proper.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「陣屋の立地や周辺集落との関係は一様ではない。近世の陣屋町研究では、陣屋と集落が一体化した小都市としての「陣屋町」が形成される場合がある一方、飛地領の拠点などでは既存集落に隣接して築かれ、本来の計画都市とは言い難い例も多いことが指摘されている」)
