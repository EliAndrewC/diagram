• zaigomachi-jawiki - 「In many cases the basis is a strip land division with townhouses lined up in one or two rows along a highway or a river, and no functional arrangement of samurai ground, townsmen's ground and temple ground like the castle town's is seen.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「At stations with heavy traffic of men and horses, such as the Tokaido and the Nakasendo, the main house was built along the highway across the full frontage of each plot, and in the later Edo period two-story, board-roofed inns with a through-passage plan like the townhouses of great cities came to stand eave to eave.」 (translated; )
  • siheyuan-zhwiki - 「its layout is a courtyard with buildings built on its four sides, usually made up of the main rooms, the east and west side rooms and the reverse-facing rooms, enclosing the courtyard in the middle from the four sides, whence its name」 (translated; ) ... 「The reverse-facing rooms are the southernmost row of houses in the whole siheyuan; their eaves wall fronts the hutong and usually has no windows.」 (translated; ) ... 「the doors of the south rooms open to the north, so they are called the "reverse-facing rooms".」 (translated; ) ... 「A large siheyuan is enclosed from outside by walls, the walls tall and without windows, to show its privacy.」 (translated; ) ... 「At that time two-court siheyuan and larger ones were usually the residences of officials and gentry.」 (translated; )
  • jinya-kotobank - 「In general, within the grounds of an intendant's jin'ya were set the honjin that was the intendant's official residence, the office for public business, the official houses of subordinate staff, rice stores and levy stores, the white-sand court [shirasu], the jail and the like.」 (translated; )
  • jinyamachi-jawiki - 「It is a town formed around a jin'ya in a domain or fief of the Edo period, or a daikansho or the like in the shogun's lands.」 (translated; ) 「Samurai residences stood in rows, and merchants and craftsmen doing business with the warriors and others connected with the jin'ya gathered, and it developed.」 (translated; )
  • jinya-jawiki - 「The siting of jin'ya and their relation to the surrounding settlements is not uniform. Studies of early-modern jin'ya towns point out that while a 'jin'ya town', a small city in which jin'ya and settlement are one, is sometimes formed, at the seats of detached territories and the like there are also many examples built adjoining an existing settlement that can hardly be called planned towns proper.」 (translated; )
