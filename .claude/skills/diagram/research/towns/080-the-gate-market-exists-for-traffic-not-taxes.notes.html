• guanxiang-zdic - 「the streets outside the city gate and the area nearby」 (translated; ) (the entry gives the gate suburb its location and its name; that through-road traffic trades there without entering the walls is this record's own reading and is on no page read)
  • jishi-zhwiki - 「A periodic market (jishi, xushi) is a place of periodic trade in goods located in the countryside, usually opening once every other day or every few days」 (translated; ) ... 「The periodic market is local: buyers and sellers are chiefly the nearby villagers」 (translated; ) ... 「Many rural districts are far from a city, and for villagers to travel a long way to a city to trade is very uneconomical, and so the periodic market arose」 (translated; ); caoshi-zhwiki - 「The forerunner of the caoshi was the rural periodic market under the Tang ward-and-market system.」 (translated; ) (the market outside the walls descends from the market where the surrounding villagers traded; that it sits at the gate as a market-day chokepoint is this page's reading - the jishi page itself places the periodic market in the countryside, as the villagers' alternative to traveling to a distant city)
  • thepaper-night-gates - 「At dusk the drum sounds eight hundred times and the gates are closed」 (translated; ) ... 「at the palace and ward gates, the streets and the outer wall's gates, checkpoints were set up layer upon layer at night, and no one was let through」 (translated; ) (Tang Chang'an, the capital; the gates the passage reopens at dawn are the ward and market gates, and the outer wall's gates are barred by night checkpoints rather than said to shut; a county town's gate is not described)
  • caoshi-zhwiki - 「It refers to the commercial districts that developed outside the compass of the city walls in each city, beginning in China's Song dynasty.」 (translated; ); lifang-zhwiki - 「At sunrise, after the street drum has been struck 600 times, the ward gates are opened; at sunset, after the street drum has been struck 600 times, the ward gates are closed.」 (translated; ) ... 「Within the city, the residential areas were divided off by the low walls of the wards (fang), to control the coming and going of their people and goods.」 (translated; ) (the city's residents live in walled wards inside it, which is where the commoners belong; that its laborers and servants are among them is this page's reading; the market outside the walls, descended from the rural periodic market, and gates shut at sunset - but the gates the lifang page closes are the WARD gates of a Tang city, not a city gate; that a town's gate shut at dusk too, and the late arrival stranded outside, are this page's inference)
