How is a town farmstead laid out?

Sources: chengchi-zhwiki; the GM's decision of 2026-07; the homestead group's own grounding is on the homesteads page, chinese-city-wall-enwiki, yashikirin-jawiki

A town's farmhouses are not a looser version of a hamlet's. They stand in a nucleus and run the same to-scale homestead group the villages do - house, threshing yard strictly to its south, an adaptive sunny garden, a storehouse plot reserved to the north - and the whole group is tested against the paddies by its footprint.

That footprint test is the GM's direction of 2026-07, and what it guards against is the reason to keep it. Placing the house first and testing the fields by their centers is a 14 ft error in the wrong direction at a town's grain: a 44 ft town house passes such a test with a corner already sunk into the crop. Testing what the building actually occupies, against what the field actually occupies, is the only version of the rule that holds at this scale.

The nucleus also changes the greenery. A dispersed farmstead is sheltered by its own grove; a nucleus shelters itself, so the town's farms get no per-farm groves at all and the green instead comes as one communal windbreak on the windward side, with a leafy copse scatter through the rows.34

The rule the map follows: town farmsteads use the nucleated to-scale homestead group - house, threshing yard, garden and storehouse plot - footprint-tested against the fields, with no per-farm grove; the shelter is communal. They stand outside the rampart, ringing the paddies at roughly village density, because a farmer builds close to the ground they work (this page's reasoning), and these maps keep a town's fields outside its walls - a drawing decision, since the Chinese walled city listed farmland among its basic intramural land uses and Suzhou's wall still enclosed farmland in the twentieth century.33
