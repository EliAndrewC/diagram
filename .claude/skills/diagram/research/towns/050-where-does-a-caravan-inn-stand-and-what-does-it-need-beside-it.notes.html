  • jishi-zhwiki - 「Teidian provided lodging and also let traveling merchants store their goods; they were at once inn and warehouse.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (a merchants' lodging that also warehoused their goods; the single prominent inn is this page's; the stable and the open ground behind it are the wagon inn's, cited at the next sentence)
  • dachedian-jilin-daily - on when it stood, 「The dachedian of the northeast began in the late Qing and early Republic」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) and 「The dachedian lasted right up to the 1970s; when I was small I went along with the production team's big horse cart and once stayed a night at a dachedian.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ); on what the institution is and where it stands, 「The dachedian was the shop that received passing carts and horses. It was generally set up on the main thoroughfares and in the belt around a city's outskirts, and provided simple board and lodging at relatively low cost.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ); on the yard and the stable, 「The open ground of the back yard was for parking the carts, and there was also a long stable. The fittings were simple: one very long wooden feeding trough, above it a horizontal round timber, which was for tying the halters to. The stable stank, and in summer flies and mosquitoes flew about everywhere. Beside the stable was a small lean-to, where the grooms lived.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ); and on the well, 「In the yard there was a well, with several wooden buckets standing beside it.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • dachedian-jilin-daily - 「In the yard, uniformly single-story buildings; likewise built of mud brick and reed thatch.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) 平房 names a single-story building as against a multi-story one, so for the wagon inn the account says the opposite of a second story.
  • okabe-hatago-jawiki - of the Ohatago Kashibaya, 「a hatago (inn) of the Edo period, in the former Tokaido post station of Okabe in Okabe-cho, Fujieda City, Shizuoka Prefecture」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ), whose 「present building is thought, from an old record of the cost of its rebuilding in 1835, to be a rebuilding of about 1836」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ): 「Wooden, two stories, tile-roofed」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ), and of its upper floor, 「A space that was used as guest rooms」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ). The same article puts the main parlor, the kitchen and the proprietors' living space on the ground floor, so the upper floor is guest lodging above a working ground floor rather than the whole of the guest accommodation.
  • kotobank-umayado - the Japanese dictionary Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten defines the sense, 「when a person traveling on their own horse lodges at an inn, the inn's taking the horse into its keeping; also, an inn having such facilities」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ), and cites as the word's earliest attested example, from a novel of 1913, 「the front is a travelers' inn, and at the back there is a large stable, and it also does umayado」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ). The definition bears the institution; only the 1913 sentence bears the stable-behind arrangement, and the earliest-example label means the compilers found no older written use of the sense. The older senses on the same page are 「a place that keeps horses ready for use as post horses and relay horses」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ), earliest example from a haikai collection of 1677, and 「laborers working at an umayado; packhorse drivers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ), earliest example 1682.
  • park-yeolha-ilgi-dianshe - in the travel notes covering 「from Xinguang to inside Shanhaiguan, 562 li in all」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ), the section on inns: 「An inn's courtyard is broad, surely not less than several hundred paces; otherwise it could not hold the carts, horses and crowds. ... In the galleries, thirty to fifty sets of chairs and tables. In the stable the stone troughs are sometimes two or three bays long and half a bay wide; where not stone, the trough is built of brick, in the same form as the stone. In the courtyard, too, dozens of wooden troughs are set in rows, propped at both ends on forked timbers.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (the section describes the inns of that road in general, not one stop; it gives no height and names no grooms' quarters and no well)
  • yeolha-ilgi-zhwiki - 「In 1780 Park Ji-won's cousin Park Myeong-won was appointed chief envoy of the embassy to China to congratulate the Qing Qianlong Emperor on his seventieth birthday.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「the first part (chapters 1-7) records in diary form the 57 days of what the author saw and heard from crossing the Yalu River on 24 June 1780 to reaching Rehe at the end of August and returning to Beijing」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
