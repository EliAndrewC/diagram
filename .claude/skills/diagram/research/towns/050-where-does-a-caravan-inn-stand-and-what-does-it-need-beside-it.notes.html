  • jishi-zhwiki - 「Teidian provided lodging and also let traveling merchants store their goods; they were at once inn and warehouse.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「邸店提供住宿，也讓客商堆存貨物，既是客棧又是貨棧」) (a merchants' lodging that also warehoused their goods; the single prominent inn is this page's; the stable and the open ground behind it are the wagon inn's, cited at the next sentence)
  • dachedian-jilin-daily - on when it stood, 「The dachedian of the northeast began in the late Qing and early Republic」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「东北的大车店起始于清末民初」) and 「The dachedian lasted right up to the 1970s; when I was small I went along with the production team's big horse cart and once stayed a night at a dachedian.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「大车店一直延续到上世纪70年代，我小时候跟随生产队的大马车，曾经住过一夜大车店。」); on what the institution is and where it stands, 「The dachedian was the shop that received passing carts and horses. It was generally set up on the main thoroughfares and in the belt around a city's outskirts, and provided simple board and lodging at relatively low cost.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「大车店便是接待过往车马的店铺，一般设置于交通要道和城郊一带，提供简单的食宿，费用也相对低廉一些。」); on the yard and the stable, 「The open ground of the back yard was for parking the carts, and there was also a long stable. The fittings were simple: one very long wooden feeding trough, above it a horizontal round timber, which was for tying the halters to. The stable stank, and in summer flies and mosquitoes flew about everywhere. Beside the stable was a small lean-to, where the grooms lived.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「后院的空地供停放马车用，还有长长的马厩，设施简单，有一个很长的木制喂马槽，上边一根横着的圆木，是拴马缰绳用的。马厩里臭气熏天，夏季里苍蝇、蚊子乱飞。马厩旁边有一个小偏厦子，住的是马夫。」); and on the well, 「In the yard there was a well, with several wooden buckets standing beside it.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「院里有一口水井，旁边放着几只木桶。」)
  • dachedian-jilin-daily - 「In the yard, uniformly single-story buildings; likewise built of mud brick and reed thatch.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「院里清一色平房，同样是用土坯和苇草盖成」) 平房 names a single-story building as against a multi-story one, so for the wagon inn the account says the opposite of a second story.
  • okabe-hatago-jawiki - of the Ohatago Kashibaya, 「a hatago (inn) of the Edo period, in the former Tokaido post station of Okabe in Okabe-cho, Fujieda City, Shizuoka Prefecture」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「静岡県藤枝市岡部町の旧東海道岡部宿にある江戸時代の旅籠（宿）」), whose 「present building is thought, from an old record of the cost of its rebuilding in 1835, to be a rebuilding of about 1836」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「現在の建物は1835年（天保6年）の再建費用の古記録から、1836年（天保7年）ごろの再建と考えられている」): 「Wooden, two stories, tile-roofed」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木造2階建、瓦葺」), and of its upper floor, 「A space that was used as guest rooms」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「客間として使用された空間」). The same article puts the main parlor, the kitchen and the proprietors' living space on the ground floor, so the upper floor is guest lodging above a working ground floor rather than the whole of the guest accommodation.
  • kotobank-umayado - the Japanese dictionary Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten defines the sense, 「when a person traveling on their own horse lodges at an inn, the inn's taking the horse into its keeping; also, an inn having such facilities」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「自分の馬に乗って旅をする人が宿屋に泊まるとき、その馬を宿で預かること。また、そういう設備のある宿屋。」), and cites as the word's earliest attested example, from a novel of 1913, 「the front is a travelers' inn, and at the back there is a large stable, and it also does umayado」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「表は旅人宿(はたごや)で、裏には大きい厩(うまや)があって馬宿(ウマヤド)もする」). The definition bears the institution; only the 1913 sentence bears the stable-behind arrangement, and the earliest-example label means the compilers found no older written use of the sense. The older senses on the same page are 「a place that keeps horses ready for use as post horses and relay horses」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「駅馬、伝馬に使用する馬を準備しておく所。」), earliest example from a haikai collection of 1677, and 「laborers working at an umayado; packhorse drivers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「馬宿で働く人足。馬方。」), earliest example 1682.
  • park-yeolha-ilgi-dianshe - in the travel notes covering 「from Xinguang to inside Shanhaiguan, 562 li in all」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「自新廣至山海關內。共五百六十二里。」), the section on inns: 「An inn's courtyard is broad, surely not less than several hundred paces; otherwise it could not hold the carts, horses and crowds. ... In the galleries, thirty to fifty sets of chairs and tables. In the stable the stone troughs are sometimes two or three bays long and half a bay wide; where not stone, the trough is built of brick, in the same form as the stone. In the courtyard, too, dozens of wooden troughs are set in rows, propped at both ends on forked timbers.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「店舍庭廣。必不下數百步。不如此。難容車馬人衆。...廊廡間。椅卓三五十副。廐中石槽長或二三間。廣半間非石槽。則甎築為槽。如石制。庭中亦列置木槽數十坐。兩頭叉木而支之。」) (the section describes the inns of that road in general, not one stop; it gives no height and names no grooms' quarters and no well)
