What is the large laborer's house, and how big was it?

Sources: l7r-budgets (the setting's master laborers), kotobank-hitoyado (the broker who placed hired hands and lodged them until placed), tehaishi-jawiki (the broker's boarding house), kotobank-uradana (the house manager living on the tenement lot), kotobank-shukubamachi (the post town's porters' office)

In the setting, a master laborer's house. The setting's budgets set a small band of rich master laborers above the ordinary and the poor ones: about one laborer in twelve in a town, one in eight in a city. The larger laborer's house on the map is theirs.

In history, a boss who housed his men. Three houses of early-modern Japan fit someone who ran laborers rather than labored. The placement broker who grew up with the cities stood guarantor for the many who came in from the country to take service in Edo, found them places, and had to put them up until a place was found; one account says such a broker gave work-seekers lodging - a boarding house, like a laborers' bunkhouse - and found them work. On a city's tenement lot the house manager lived on the lot, collected the rents and was the real ruler of its tenants, the day laborers among them. And a post town kept a porters' office among its inns and teahouses near the center. None of the pages read gives the size of any of them.

The rule the map follows: the large laborer's house is a master laborer's - a labor boss who finds work for laborers and houses some of them - and stands among the laborers' housing, not on the shop street. Its drawn size, 50 by 34 ft, is a guess: no page read gives the size of a labor broker's house, a house manager's or a porters' office.
