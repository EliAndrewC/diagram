A street is access infrastructure for the buildings it serves

Sources: hutong-enwiki, machiya-shoka-jawiki, kotobank-uradana, desire-path-enwiki (a path worn by traffic, as a shortcut)

The why is historical: a street is access infrastructure for the buildings it serves, and where it is not paved it is worn into the ground by the traffic it carries - Beijing's hutong alleys were 「formed by lines of siheyuan, traditional courtyard residences」, and a desire path forms as the shortcut to where walkers are actually going. A planned grid line that never gets built up simply isn't drawn (an undeveloped block).

Note the asymmetry the rule inherits from that: a building with no street is perfectly fine - wide street frontage took great wealth, and the poor were tenants in row houses let by the shop-house owners - while a street with no buildings is not. So an empty lane is fixed by demand rather than by deletion first: if there are dwellings the lane could serve, or households the census still owes, the lane is routed to them or they are moved onto it, lower-status dwellings readily lining lesser back lanes; only when the population is already fully housed does the street have no reason to exist, and then it is trimmed back to its last building or removed.

What counts as frontage. Frontage counts only to the street a building was actually turned to face, and a fence or a wall between the two breaks it. Laborers housed on the far side of a samurai ward fence serve their own side of it: they cannot reach a government avenue beyond the fence, however near they stand, so they do not line it, and an avenue with only them beside it is empty. A looser rule that credits any building near a lane fails the same way: a building that fronts the cross-street it stands nearer to keeps a dead-ending lane alive.

The rule the map follows: inside the walls, no stretch of street longer than about 130 ft is empty of buildings that front that street - the street each was turned to face, not merely one it stands near - and a building with a fence or a wall between it and the point it would front serves its own side instead. A town's lesser lanes exist to be fronted: the laborers' quarters are given their own back lanes off the avenue, so their dwellings front a street without standing on the main commercial frontage.
