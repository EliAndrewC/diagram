Gate markets outside a town (guanxiang)

Outside the gates of a walled Chinese city, the streets just beyond the gate and the area around them were called the guanxiang. Because a gate funneled the traffic of the countryside beyond it, markets and inns grew up just outside. By the nineteenth century few walled cities lacked such a suburb outside at least one gate. The study these findings come from covers walled cities of every rank and speaks of them in general, not of the county seat by name; it calls the built-up ground outside a gate a suburb, and this section calls a town's a gate market.

In the setting our maps draw, a town's gate market is there for its traffic, not to escape a toll or a rule at the gate: the setting collects its import tariffs at the gates of provincial cities and capitals, not at a town's, and our maps take a county's magistrate to govern the whole county, outside the wall as well as in. This project takes a walled town's gate market to be the same thing as a city's, on a smaller scale. The city's strip outside its gate is at What stands outside a city gate?, and the periodic market the villages come in for, and where in the town it meets, at Market days and the market ground (ichi).

  • The ground outside a Chinese city's gate drew markets for the country people.
    A study of Chinese walled cities says so of walled cities in general: since a gate channeled all the traffic to and from one sector of the city's hinterland, the area just outside it was a favored site for markets and for businesses serving the rural population.
  • China's periodic markets met every few days, for the villagers nearby.
    A periodic market opened every other day or every few days, its buyers and sellers chiefly the villagers around it. It arose because a long journey to a distant city to trade did not pay.
  • Did the villages' market-day trade meet a Chinese town at its gate?
    The study of walled cities puts markets for the country people just outside the gates. That the villages' market-day trade met the town there is this project's reading; the Chinese Wikipedia article on the periodic market places the market itself in the countryside.
  • A Chinese city's gates on long-distance routes drew inns for travelers.
    Inns and other services for travelers and traders were set up outside the particular gates that opened onto long-distance routes. A town's own inn for the road is at Where does a caravan inn stand, and what does it need beside it?
  • We read the through road's travelers as trading outside the gate without going in.
    That carters, peddlers and other travelers on the through road bought in the gate market without going inside the walls is this project's reading, from where the inns stood.
  • When did commercial districts grow outside China's city walls?
    From the late Tang or the Song onward, by different accounts. The Chinese Wikipedia dates the districts of trade outside the walls, the caoshi, from the Song, and traces them to the rural periodic markets of the Tang. The study of walled cities dates the growth of suburbs from the later Tang and the Southern Song.
  • Outside a Chinese city's busiest gates, the suburb often grew before the ground inside the wall had filled.
    The study of walled cities says so of many, if not most, of them.
  • Where a Chinese city's walled suburb rivaled the city, it lay toward the main traffic.
    Where a suburb's own wall grew to rival the city wall in size, it lay in the direction of the main flow of traffic on an important route.
  • A Tang city's residents lived inside its walls, in walled wards.
    Under the Tang ward-and-market system, a city's residential areas were divided off by the low walls of the wards, to control the coming and going of their people and goods. That its laborers and servants lived there, not in the suburb outside the gate, is this project's reading.
  • Did a Chinese town's gate shut at dusk, leaving late arrivals outside?
    Probably, though no page we read says so of a county town. In Tang Chang'an, the capital, the gates of its walled wards closed at dusk to the drum, and at night checkpoints barred passage through the outer wall's gates; under the ward-and-market system the wards' gates opened at sunrise and closed at sunset. That a county town's own gate shut at dusk too, leaving late arrivals to spend the night in the gate market outside it, is this project's inference.
  • What shops stood in a Chinese county town's gate market?
    The study of walled cities names two kinds: the market and the shops that sold to the villagers, at every gate that carried traffic, and, at a gate on a through road, inns and services for travelers. No page we read lists a county town's gate market trade by trade, so which shops stood in it, within those two kinds, is a GUESS.
  • No page we read counts the buildings of a Chinese county town's gate market or says how far along the road it ran.
    The study of walled cities gives no count and no length for any suburb, and no other page we found gives either. The one counted strip the record has is a capital's, at What stands outside a city gate?
