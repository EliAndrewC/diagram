• hutong-enwiki - 「In Beijing, hutongs are alleys formed by lines of siheyuan, traditional courtyard residences.」 ... 「Many neighbourhoods were formed by joining one siheyuan to another to form a hutong, and then joining one hutong to another.」 (lead) ; also 「Between the main hutongs, many tiny lanes ran north and south for convenient passage.」 (section "Historical hutongs")
  • desire-path-enwiki - 「A desire path ... is an unplanned small trail formed by erosion caused by human or animal traffic.」 ... 「the width and severity of its surface erosion are often indicators of the quantity of traffic it receives.」 (lead) (a path worn by the traffic along it; the paved street is not on the page)
  • desire-path-enwiki - 「The path usually represents the shortest or the most easily navigated route through a natural area or greenspace」 ... 「Desire paths typically emerge as convenient shortcuts where more deliberately constructed roads and footpaths take a longer or more circuitous route, have gaps, or are non-existent.」 (a desire path is the shortcut to where walkers are going)
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「To lay out a wide frontage required great financial means. As a result the machiya was built to the full frontage width of the plot and came to have a floor plan running long toward the rear.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「間口を広く構えるには多大な財力を要した。その結果、町屋は敷地の間口いっぱいに建てられ、奥に長い間取りを持つことになった。」) (frontage as wealth, and the rented tenement of the poor behind the shop-house - 「The ura-nagaya was a rented house of which the master of the omotedana was the landlord, let out to tenants.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「裏長屋は表店の主人が大家となり、店子に貸し出される借家である。」); the tenement stands on a back alley behind the shop-house, below a street's rank but not without access)
  • kotobank-uradana - 「A house built on a back street of a built-up area, or on the ground behind a merchant house. In particular it referred to the crude partitioned row houses built facing a back street.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「市街地の裏通りや、商家の背後の地所に建てられた家。とくに、裏通りに面して建てられた粗末な棟割長屋をいった。」) (the back-street row house as the entry's primary sense; the encyclopedia entry on the same page has them as 「In the Edo period, in the townspeople's quarters of great cities such as Edo and Osaka, the rented dwellings of petty traders, artisans, day laborers and other lower commoners, built in back alleys not facing the main street.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代に江戸・大坂などの大都市の町人居住地で，表通りに面していない路地裏に建てられた小商人・職人・日雇いなど下層庶民の借家住居のこと。」))
