• kotobank-shukubamachi - 「The lot division on both sides of the highway was of planned strip shape, the depth kept nearly constant, with differences in the frontage. Apart from the stratum of station officials, it was usual to combine farming, and the back side of the lot was in many cases a vegetable garden.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「街道両側の敷地割は計画的に行われた短冊形のもので，奥行きはほぼ一定とし，間口に違いがあった。宿役人層を除けば，農業を兼ねるのが普通で，敷地裏側は菜園になっている場合が多い。」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, the section by Tamai Tetsuo)
  • kotobank-machiya - 「A machiya usually runs the ridge of its main house parallel to the street (hirairi), and many are of the form with the roof of the main house and its pent eave (hisashi) projected in two tiers and a low second story used for storage, but there are also tsumairi types, with the ridge run at right angles to the street and a triangular gable showing on the street side.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町家はふつう主屋の棟を街路と平行に通し（平入り），主屋の屋根と庇（ひさし）を二重に出し，物置に使われる低い二階を備えている形式が多いが，棟を街路と直角に通し，街路側に三角の破風が見える妻入り（つまいり）型のものもある。」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry)
  • hirairi-tsumairi-jawiki - 「Hirairi and tsumairi are names classifying a building by which of its faces holds the main front entrance. The face parallel to the main ridge of the roof is called the hira, the hira side, and the face perpendicular to it the tsuma」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平入（ひらいり）と妻入（つまいり）は、建物をそのいずれの面に正面出入口があるかによって分類した呼び方。屋根の大棟と平行な面を平、平側といい、これと直交する面を妻」)
