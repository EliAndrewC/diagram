What does a town keep for the market crowd and the festival?

Sources: l7r-market-day (the troupes' market day), otabisho-jawiki (where a shrine's procession rests), kotobank-ichigami (the market god's festival), miaohui-zhwiki (the Chinese temple fair)

The market crowd. The crowd comes on market day, and the setting's acting troupes make most of their money then. What the town keeps for that crowd is already on the map: the ground the market meets on, under where a town's market was held; the flophouse for those who stay over, under the market-day flophouse; and the stage the troupes play on, beside a temple, under where a town's theater stage stands.

The festival. A Japanese shrine's festival generally carries the god out in a portable shrine, which rests or stays the night on its way, or sets out to reach, a place called the otabisho; that may have a shrine building of its own, a temporary altar, or be the house of the representative of the shrine's ujiko. The market's own festival was the market god's, held as a rule at the first market of the New Year. In China the temple fair gathered near a temple for offerings, contests and performances, and took in trade. So a festival's ground is the shrine's, the temple's and the route of the god's procession.

No page read describes a square or festival ground that a Japanese or Chinese county town kept open for the purpose. A town map therefore draws no festival ground of its own: the festival uses the shrine and temple precincts and the procession's route, and an otabisho, where one is drawn at all, is a small shrine or a house.
