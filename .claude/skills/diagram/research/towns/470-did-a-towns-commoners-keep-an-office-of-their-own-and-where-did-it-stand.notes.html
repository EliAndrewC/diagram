• kotobank-machikaisho - 「In Sakai, Imai, Hiranogo and Kaizuka, which developed urban self-government at the end of the middle ages, and in Kyoto, and in Osaka, which flourished greatly in the early modern period as an economic city, each separate ward within each city also had a machi-kaisho, and above them a general office for the whole, the sokaisho, was set up for each group, for each street, or for the town as a whole.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry)
  • kinsei-kaisho-jawiki - 「Within the ward, the machi-kaisho was used as an office and a meeting place. The ward officials were posted there; they gathered the ward's residents and passed on rules such as the machi-bure notices from the magistrate's office and elsewhere, and, the other way, did the clerical work of drawing up the papers the residents submitted.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-machikaisho - 「Its duties covered the whole of the town's government, and it is usual for them to reach, besides administration and civil justice, into policing, such as searching for and arresting criminals concerned with the townspeople. For this reason it sometimes had a white-sand court or a small jail. There were also places where it was used as lodging and reception for the domain's guests and the shogunate's officials.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-machikaisho - 「Among castle towns there are some, like Utsunomiya, where a kaisho was set up in each ward, but they are exceptional; on the contrary, there are places where the town officials were posted at the town magistrate's office and did their work there, and no machi-kaisho was set up.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Among post stations too there are cases that set up a machi-kaisho, such as Sukagawa on the Oshu-Matsumae road and Yokkaichi on the Tokaido, but they are unusual examples.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kinsei-kaisho-jawiki - 「in Edo the official residence of the ward headman doubled as the machi-kaisho」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • machidoshiyori-jawiki - 「The town elders' lots were granted by the shogunate; Naraya, Taruya and Kitamura each had a corner lot facing Honcho street, in Honcho 1-chome, 2-chome and 3-chome. As with samurai, the residence doubled as the office; it was called the town elders' office」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • huiguan-zhwiki - 「The huiguan, also called the gongsuo, is an industrial and commercial organization dating from China's Ming and Qing」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「The earliest huiguan now known is the Wuhu Huiguan in Beijing, built in the Yongle reign of the Ming. Huiguan were set up at first only to provide fellowship, entertainment and lodging for people from the same home region; later some developed the function of uniting the merchants of one trade」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
