  • jinya-jawiki - 「Also, at small domains' jin'ya one sees a layout in which, as a facility ranking next to a castle, earthworks and moats are run around the perimeter but no tall stone walls are provided, and a residence, offices, retainers' official houses, earth-walled storehouses, a drill ground and so on are arranged.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また、小藩の陣屋では、城に準ずる施設として周囲に土塁や堀をめぐらせつつも高い石垣は備えず、居館・役所・家臣の役宅や土蔵、調練場などを配置する構成がみられる」)
  • jinya-jawiki - 「For this reason, even among jin'ya, there was a range in how far they were accompanied by retainers' residential quarters and townhouses around them, in the composition of their internal facilities, and in the defensiveness of their perimeter.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「このため、同じ陣屋でも、周辺に家臣団の居住区や町屋敷を伴う度合い、内部施設の構成、外周の防御性には幅があった」)
  • bushijyoshi-daikan-jinya - 「The jin'ya was the intendant's residence, and his office, court, and the place the annual tax was stored.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「陣屋は代官の住まいであり役所、裁判所そして年貢の保管場所でした。」) (a blog post written from two published books on the intendants)
  • takayama-jinya-jawiki - 「The earth-walled storehouses within the grounds were originally built inside Takayama Castle in the Keicho era (around 1600) and moved to the present site in 1695 (Genroku 8).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「敷地内の土蔵は、元々慶長年間（1600年前後）に高山城内に建設され、1695年（元禄8年）に現在地に移築されたものである。」) and, of the surviving buildings, 「storehouses no. 1-4 and no. 9-12」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一～四番蔵・九～十二番蔵」)
