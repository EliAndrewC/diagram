What is in a town's gate market, and how far out along the road does it run?

Sources: chang-morphology-walled-capitals (what grew outside a walled city's gate, at which gates, and how common it was)

A town's gate market is the same thing as a city's, smaller; the city's strip is What stands outside a city gate?, and why the market is there at all is The gate market exists for TRAFFIC, not taxes. What the record can say about the strip itself comes from a study of Chinese walled cities of every rank, which speaks of walled cities in general rather than of the county seat by name. A gate funneled all the traffic of one sector of the countryside, so the ground just outside it was a favored site for markets and for businesses serving the country people, and the gates that opened onto long-distance routes drew inns and other services for travelers and traders. By the nineteenth century few walled cities lacked a suburb outside at least one gate, and outside the busiest gates the suburb commonly grew before the ground inside the wall had filled.

What it held. The trades the record can name are of two kinds: the market and the shops that sold to the villagers who came in to trade, at every gate that carried traffic; and, at a gate on a through road, the inns and services for travelers - for a town, the caravan inn of Where does a caravan inn stand, and what does it need beside it?. No page read lists a county town's gate strip trade by trade, so which shops stand in it is a guess within those two kinds.

How many, and how far. No readable page counts the buildings of a county town's gate strip or says how far along the road it ran; the study gives no count and no length for any suburb, and the one counted strip the record has is a capital's, in the city question. The four to eight premises within about 420 ft of the gate that the town maps draw are a calibration, and a guess.

What it means for a map: the town's rule in The gate market exists for TRAFFIC, not taxes stands. Its premises are shops selling to the villagers on every gate that carries a road, with an inn among them only where the gate opens onto a through road; and the gate that carries the town's main through road has the larger strip, since that is the gate the traffic uses.
