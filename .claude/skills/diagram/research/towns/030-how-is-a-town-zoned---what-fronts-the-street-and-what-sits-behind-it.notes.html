• kyoto-machiya-isan - 「The Kyo-machiya, founded on the coexistence of work and dwelling.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「職住共存を基本とする京町家。」); on which part is which, 「A long narrow earth-floored passage running from the entrance to the back, which draws light and air into the house. The front part facing the street, which serves for receiving customers and as a workspace, is called the 'shop yard'; the cooking place where the range stands is called the 'running yard'.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「玄関から奥へ続く細長い土間で、家の中に光や風を取り込みます。通りに面した表は客人の対応や作業場になる「店庭」、おくどさんを置く炊事場は「走り庭」と呼び分けます。」); and one surviving house, 「The Nagae house, wholesale drapers for generations, has a main building with a low second story to north and south alike - a large Kyo-machiya of the work-and-dwelling-in-one type, made up of shop and residence.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「代々呉服卸商を営んできた長江家は、南北それぞれに厨子二階の主屋を持ち、店舗と住居からなる職住一体型の大型京町家。」)
  • kotobank-omotedana - 「Most are land-renters or house-renters, but it is a term symbolizing a stratum one rank above the common folk of the uradana, corresponding roughly to the wholesaler and broker classes.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「多くは地借・店借だが，裏店(うらだな)の民衆より一段高い階層を象徴する呼称で，問屋・仲買商人層にほぼ相当する」) (frontage held by wholesalers and brokers, the back lots by artisans and day laborers; the sorting attested is by wealth, and a trade choosing its place by its need for a front is this page's mechanism)
  • choninchi-jawiki - 「Among the chonin there was a class gulf running from the danna-shu, the bourgeois class of house-owners who kept a shop on the main street, down to the lower chonin of the back-shop stratum on the back streets.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町人は特に家屋敷を所有する家持層で表通りに店を構えるブルジョワ階級である旦那衆から、裏通りの裏店層の下層町人まで階級的落差があり」); eta-jawiki - 「Where they lived was in many cases at the edge of the village, beside a river, or some other place unsuited to agriculture.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「居住していたのは村外れや川の側など、農業に適さない場所であったことが多い。」) (the banding from street shop to back-street tenement, and the outcast settlement on ground unsuited to farming, at a village's edge; the gap between merchants and laborers, and the outcast quarter in a town's plan, are on no page read)
