• kotobank-ressen - 「as against the road village, where houses mainly in primary industry stand along a road, a long narrow settlement facing a highway with transport and commercial functions is called a street village」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「道路に沿い第一次産業を主とする家が並ぶ路村に対し、街道に面し交通的・商業的機能をもつ細長い集落を街村という。」) (the Nipponica entry; the page defines the form and names no town, and that a town's edge takes it along its roads is this record's reading)
  • kotobank-edo - 「after this great fire the daimyo mansions and the temples and shrines near Edo Castle were moved out to the suburbs, firebreaks were made in the town ground too, and town ground spread along the highways」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この大火以後，江戸城近くの大名屋敷や寺社の郊外への移転が行われ，町地でも火除地がつくられ，街道ぞいに町地が拡大していった。」) and 「after the Meireki fire, town ground formed in great numbers along these suburban highways」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明暦の大火以後はこれらの郊外の街道ぞいに町地が数多く形成されていった。」) (the fire of Meireki 3, 1657; Edo, the shogun's city)
  • kotobank-edo - 「in 1662 (Kanbun 2) the surrounding districts that had become built up were placed under the town magistrate as town-row ground」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「1662年（寛文2）には周辺の宅地化した区域を町並地として町奉行(まちぶぎょう)支配とした。」) (the Nipponica entry; the same entry adds a further 259 town wards (machi) in 1713)
  • chang-morphology-walled-capitals - 「Inns and other services catering to travelers and traders were established outside the particular gates that gave access to long-distance routes.」 (p. 99) and 「the overdeveloped suburb inevitably lay in the direction of the major traffic flow on an important transport route」 (p. 100; said of the suburb that grew large enough to be walled itself, the chapter's subtype of the multiple city, not of every suburb)
