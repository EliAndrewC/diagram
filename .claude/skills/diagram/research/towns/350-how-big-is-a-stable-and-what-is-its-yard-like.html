How big is a stable, and what is its yard like?

Sources: hikone-umaya-bunka (a dated castle stable of the Edo period: its wings' length and depth and the horses it held), hikone-umaya-city (the same stable's count of stalls and its room for managing the horses), umaya-kotobank (a Japanese farmhouse stable: inside the house or standing apart, how it is closed and walled, what is above it, and how big a stall is), l7r-budgets (the setting's waystation horses)

The one stable the record can both date and measure before modern times is a lord's. The stable of Hikone Castle, built about 1688-1703, is two single-story wings joined in an L, one 25.2 m and the other 31.1 m long, each 5.9 m deep, with fittings for nineteen horses; the city counts 21 places to stand and tie a horse, and a small room at the east end for managing the horses. Taking the two wings as one run of about 50 m once the corner they share is counted once, and less again once that room is taken out of it, that is at most about 2.4 to 2.7 m a horse - about 8 to 9 ft a stall, in a building about 19 ft deep. The division is this record's, not the sources', and it assumes the stalls fill the wings' length in one row. It is a castle lord's stable, not an inn's or a relay's, but it is the premodern figure the maps are calibrated to.

A farmer's stable is described by a modern encyclopedia, which gives no period for what it describes. A Japanese farmhouse kept its horse either in an inner stable, a corner of the house's own earth-floored work room, or in an outer stable standing apart. Its entrance was closed with removable wooden bars and its other sides were plank walls; it usually had a ceiling, with a servant's room or a storeroom above. In the long-wintered north it was roomy, and in the Kinki region (around Kyoto and Osaka) narrow, about 3 m (about 10 ft) square; an outer stable usually had a privy and a shed built on with it. That 10 ft stall is an undated modern description, which could as well be of a house of the Meiji era as of the Edo period, so by the GM's rule of 2026-09-28 against what only modern sources show, the maps do not size a stall from it: they use the castle stable's 8 to 9 ft.

The bigger stables the maps draw are the record's other questions: the wagon inn's long stable with its single feeding trough is in Where does a caravan inn stand, and what does it need beside it?, and the Chinese station's horse compound in What did a post station keep for its relay horses and porters? Neither is measured on any page read. A stable for the waystation's 8 to 10 horses, at about 8 to 9 ft a stall, would run about 65 to 90 ft of stalls; that length is a guess built on the castle stable's stalls, not a measured relay stable.

What a stable yard's ground was like is no better known. No page read describes the ground of an inn's, a relay's or a gate's stable yard; the beaten earth, the hitching rails and the litter the maps draw are this record's reading of a picture, set out in How our maps draw stable yards and their troughs.
