• edo-sanza-jawiki - 「By about the beginning of the Enpo era (the 1670s) the shogunate had come to permit only four troupes - the Nakamura-za, the Ichimura-za, the Morita-za and the Yamamura-za - to raise a yagura.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「延宝の初めごろ（1670年代）までには中村座・市村座・森田座・山村座の四座に限って「櫓をあげる」ことが幕府によって認められるようになった。」); shibaigoya-jawiki - 「In the late Edo period, kabuki theaters (shibai-goya) with roofs and seating were established, centered on Edo, Kyoto and Osaka.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代後期、江戸・京都・大阪を中心として屋根や客席が付いた歌舞伎劇場（芝居小屋）が成立した」) (licensed permanent houses, in a tier of three cities rather than one capital; the festival crowd as the reason is this page's)
  • kotobank-miyaji-shibai - 「In the Edo period, a booth-built play performed under a temporary license in the precincts of a shrine or temple.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、臨時に許可を受けて、社寺の境内で興行した小屋掛け芝居。」); edo-sanza-jawiki - 「Conversely, a playhouse without a yagura was called a miyachi-shibai, and various restrictions were imposed on it: that it be a simple booth structure, that no roof be put on anything other than over the stage, that it not use fittings such as a draw curtain, a revolving stage or a hanamichi.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「逆に櫓のない芝居小屋は宮地芝居と呼ばれ、簡略な小屋掛けであること、舞台の上以外には屋根をつけないこと、引幕・回り舞台・花道などの装置を使わないことなど、さまざまな制限が設けられた。」) (the play in a precinct, with the regulation beside it that put no roof on anything but the stage; the Chinese temple opera stage could not be reached, and the audience's position between stage and hall is this page's)
