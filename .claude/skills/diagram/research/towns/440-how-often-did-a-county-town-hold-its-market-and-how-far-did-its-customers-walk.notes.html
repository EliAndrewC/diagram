  • kotobank-rokusaiichi - 「The earlier hikiri-ichi was normally three times a month, but with the growth of trade, markets came to be held six times a month, every five days.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「それ以前の日切市(ひぎりいち)は月三回を普通としたが、商品流通の発展に伴って五日おきに月六回、市が立つようになった。」) (the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry) and 「The market days too were held in combinations such as 1 and 6, 2 and 7, 3 and 8, 4 and 9, 5 and 10, two days in each ten-day period, six days a month.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「市開催日も1と6，2と7，3と8，4と9，5と10といった組合せで，旬日に2日，月に6日開かれた。」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, by Sasaki Gin'ya)
  • kotobank-rokusaiichi - 「In the Edo period, in the Kinai, where the commercial economy grew markedly, many rokusai-ichi became zaimachi towns and the name rokusai-ichi vanished. In Tohoku and Kanto the rokusai-ichi kept its function as the center of the regional economy.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代，商品経済の発展の著しい畿内地方では，六斎市の多くは在町として町場化し，六斎市の名は消滅した。東北や関東では依然六斎市が地域経済の中心としての機能を保ちつづけた。」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry; the Kinai is the region around Kyoto and Osaka)
  • kotobank-sansaiichi - 「Market days were set by reference to the market days of each region's central parent market; in each region the days were adjusted so the markets would not compete.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「市日が各地域の中心的な親市の市日を基準に決定されるなど，地域ごとに各市の競合を避けるために市日が調整されていた。」) (the Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten entry, on the three-day market)
  • jishi-zhwiki - 「The market opens periodically, roughly one day in every two or several days, or three times in ten days」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「集市周期性開市，大約每兩日或三數日開市一日，或每十日三次」) and 「The periodic market is local: buyers and sellers are chiefly the nearby villagers」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「集市是地區性的，買賣雙方都以附近村民為主」)
