  • kotobank-hitoyado - 「a house that brokers hired hands and the like. A placement lodging.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「it appeared from the beginning of the early-modern period with the growth of the cities, and is thought to have arisen of necessity because, especially for the many migrant servants flowing into Edo, there was need to give their personal guarantee, to broker a place of employment, and to lodge them until their employment was settled.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • tehaishi-jawiki - 「In the Edo period this master-and-follower relation spread even to the common people, and became the relation between the kuchiireya, who stood as guarantor for those coming to the cities to work away from home or seeking work, provided a hitoyado (a lodging, a dwelling like a tako-beya) and brokered work, and those seeking work」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (an article the encyclopedia itself marks as unsourced)
  • kotobank-uradana - 「in most cases a yamori (also called ōya or yanushi) kept a dwelling within the lot, collected the ground rent and house rent on the owner's behalf, and also carried out the registration of the back-tenement residents and the passing on of town ordinances, and became the de facto ruler of the back-tenement residents - small traders such as peddlers, craftsmen such as carpenters and plasterers, and the lower simple laborers called day laborers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, by Tamai Tetsuo, on Edo)
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「the relay office that ran the station's business, such as the relaying of official men and horses and rest and lodging, and the honjin and waki-honjin where daimyo on alternate attendance and others stayed, stood at about the center, and before and after them lined up the inns, teahouses and cook-shops used by ordinary travelers, and the porters' offices and packhorse offices」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, the section by Tamai Tetsuo)
