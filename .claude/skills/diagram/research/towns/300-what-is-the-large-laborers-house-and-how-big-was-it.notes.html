  • kotobank-hitoyado - 「a house that brokers hired hands and the like. A placement lodging.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「雇人などの周旋をする家。口入宿。」) ... 「it appeared from the beginning of the early-modern period with the growth of the cities, and is thought to have arisen of necessity because, especially for the many migrant servants flowing into Edo, there was need to give their personal guarantee, to broker a place of employment, and to lodge them until their employment was settled.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世初期以降，都市の発展にともなって出現したもので，とくに江戸に流入する多数の出稼ぎ奉公人に対し，その身元保証，雇入先の斡旋，そして，就職先がきまるまでの宿泊を行う必要があったことから必然的に発生したものと思われる。」)
  • tehaishi-jawiki - 「In the Edo period this master-and-follower relation spread even to the common people, and became the relation between the kuchiireya, who stood as guarantor for those coming to the cities to work away from home or seeking work, provided a hitoyado (a lodging, a dwelling like a tako-beya) and brokered work, and those seeking work」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この主従関係は江戸時代には庶民にまで広がり、都市部に出稼ぎや職を求める者の身元引受人となり、人宿（下宿、たこ部屋のような住まい）を提供し仕事を斡旋するといった口入屋と職を求める者の関係になり」) (an article the encyclopedia itself marks as unsourced)
  • kotobank-uradana - 「in most cases a yamori (also called ōya or yanushi) kept a dwelling within the lot, collected the ground rent and house rent on the owner's behalf, and also carried out the registration of the back-tenement residents and the passing on of town ordinances, and became the de facto ruler of the back-tenement residents - small traders such as peddlers, craftsmen such as carpenters and plasterers, and the lower simple laborers called day laborers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「多くは家守（やもり）（大家，家主ともいう）が屋敷地内に住居を構え，地代・店賃徴収を代行し，また裏店住人の人別改め（戸籍調べ）や町触（法令）通達などを行い，棒手振（ぼてふり）などの小商人，大工・左官などの職人，そして日雇いなどと呼ばれた下層単純労働者など，裏店住人の事実上の支配者となっていた。」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, by Tamai Tetsuo, on Edo)
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「the relay office that ran the station's business, such as the relaying of official men and horses and rest and lodging, and the honjin and waki-honjin where daimyo on alternate attendance and others stayed, stood at about the center, and before and after them lined up the inns, teahouses and cook-shops used by ordinary travelers, and the porters' offices and packhorse offices」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「公用人馬の継立てや休泊などの宿業務を運営する問屋場や，参勤交代の大名などが宿泊する本陣，脇本陣がほぼ中央部に位置し，その前後に一般旅行者の利用する旅籠屋，茶屋，煮売屋，そして人足問屋，馬借問屋などが並び」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, the section by Tamai Tetsuo)
