• kotobank-gaison - 「The post towns, market towns, temple-gate towns and the like that developed in the Edo period almost all show a linear settlement form with houses lined up on both sides of a central street. Among them, the stations of the Fifty-three Stations of the Tokaido form typical street villages. The land division in many is a neat division of house lots into strips, in the direction at right angles to the central street.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代に発達した宿場町，市場町，門前町などは，ほとんど中軸となる街路の両側に家が立並んだ列状の集落形態を示している。そのなかでも，東海道五十三次の宿駅は典型的な街村を形成している。土地割は中軸となる街路に直角の方向に，整然とした宅地割が短冊型に施されたものが多い。」) (the Britannica Japan entry)
  • zaigomachi-jawiki - 「In many cases the basis is a strip land division with townhouses lined up in one or two rows along a highway or a river, and no functional arrangement of samurai ground, townsmen's ground and temple ground like the castle town's is seen.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「多くの場合、街道や河川沿いに町家が一列ないし二列に並ぶ短冊形の地割を基本とし、城下町のような武家地・町人地・寺社地の機能別配置は見られない」); and what a zaigo-machi is, 「a general term for places that, while belonging legally to the countryside (the rural districts), in substance existed as towns」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「法制上は在方（農村部）に属しながら、実質的には都市として存立したものの総称である」)
  • kotobank-gaison - 「On highways running along narrow coasts where the mountains press close to the sea, one-sided street villages are often seen.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「山地が海に迫った狭い海岸を走る街道などでは、片側街村が多くみられる。」) (the Nipponica entry)
