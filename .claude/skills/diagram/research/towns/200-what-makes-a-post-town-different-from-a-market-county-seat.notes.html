  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「The toiyaba, which ran the station's business of forwarding official men and horses and of lodging, and the honjin and waki-honjin where daimyo on alternate attendance and the like stayed, were located at about the center; before and behind them stood the inns, teahouses and cookshops used by ordinary travelers, and the porters' offices, pack-horse offices and the like, and further outside were placed the various merchants and craftsmen; in some cases the station was divided into several wards.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「The width of the highway inside the station was a comparatively wide 2 to 4 ken, and a flowing channel was laid in the middle of the road or at the road's edge, for fire prevention and for watering men and horses.」 (translated; ) (one ken is about 1.8 m, 6 ft)
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「The post towns of the five highways had mitsuke of stone walling, earth banks and the like set at the town's ends, and the toiyaba and honjin were placed at the town's center.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「Because the post town takes the street-village form, from a military-defensive standpoint the entrances of the highway into the post town curve, or form a masugata street, to block the long view.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「The division of the lots on both sides of the highway was a planned one into strips, the depth kept nearly constant, with differences in frontage.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「The people making up a station were allotted house plots of a roughly average size, and in many cases were exempted from ground rent in return for providing horses or porters.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukueki - 「Because plots were given anew along the highway with a fixed division of frontage, the plots became even.」 (translated; ) ... 「Also, the station's entrances were made masugata, and a channel was run along both sides or down the middle of the street, and the like.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「Apart from the stratum of station officials, it was usual to farm as well, and in many cases the back of the plot was a vegetable garden.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「At stations with heavy traffic of men and horses, such as the Tokaido and the Nakasendo, the main house was built along the highway across the full frontage of each plot, and in the later Edo period two-story, board-roofed inns with a through-passage plan like the townhouses of great cities came to stand eave to eave. By contrast, on side roads, at mountain stations and the like, inns whose plan differed little from the thatched farmhouses round about in many cases stood in a row taking a front yard on the highway side and keeping a space from the neighboring house as well.」 (translated; )
