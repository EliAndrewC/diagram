• kotobank-zokibayashi - 「Zokibayashi, then, generally often means secondary woods of deciduous broadleaf trees, including woods for firewood and charcoal. It refers to young woods near habitation, of konara, shide, ryobu, yamahannoki and the like」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Heibonsha article by Tsutsumi Toshio; a modern definition, undated)
  • rinya-satoyama - 「satoyama woods, mostly of broadleaf trees such as konara and kunugi, have been used since long before the Meiji period for many purposes: fuel such as firewood and charcoal, food such as wild greens, and gathering fertilizer and materials for farming」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • rinya-satoyama - 「such satoyama woods were renewed by cutting and sprouting again at intervals of about 20 to 30 years, and were woods of trees relatively thin in diameter and low in height, growing in clumps from the stump」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • maff-musashino-giahs - 「on land thickly covered with volcanic ash and poor, trees have been planted since the Edo period to raise flatland woods, and the fallen leaves gathered and put on the fields as compost」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「This area had its start in the reclamation the Kawagoe domain carried out from 1654, against the food shortage that came with Edo's rapid growth in population.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「trees were planted on grassland as far as the eye could see to make woods on the flat, and they were given several functions, such as the use of fallen leaves as compost and keeping the soil from blowing away」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Musashino area of Saitama)
  • jfs-inagawa-daiba-kunugi - 「the kunugi woods of the upper Ina River, which flows along the border of Hyogo and Osaka prefectures, were a production area of Ikeda charcoal and Hitokura charcoal that can be traced back at least to the Muromachi period」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「that it was used as charcoal for the tea ceremony is written in documents of the Edo period」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and, as the date of origin, 「the Muromachi period on (old documents, tradition)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • hattori-ikedazumi - 「As records concerning charcoal in old documents and traditions, 27 items could be confirmed between 1145 and 1833. Rotational cutting, charcoal for the lord, Toyotomi Hideyoshi's praise of it and the like were recorded.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「the Enpo land survey registers record that 'kunugi hills' existed in villages such as Hitokura, Kurokawa, Kunisaki and Yoshikawa」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「comparing the old documents and books with the present state of Ikeda charcoal production, they agreed on the wood of Ikeda charcoal, its production area, the distribution of the kunugi woods, the planting of kunugi, the presence of daiba kunugi, the rotation period and other points」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the abstract)
  • jfs-inagawa-daiba-kunugi - 「a patchwork landscape made by rotational cutting (on an 8-9 year cycle)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (present-day practice)
  • ffpri-kunugi - 「the late-Edo farm book Koeki Kokusanko」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) quoted: 「from about one jo up, hundreds of branches come out. Of these branches, those that have come from the stump and grown to between half a shaku and one shaku around are cut off in winter and burned into charcoal, which is called Ikeda charcoal and used in the world's tea ceremony.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
