  • kyoto-machiya-isan - 「The Kyo-machiya, founded on the coexistence of work and dwelling.」 (translated; ); on which part is which, 「A long narrow earth-floored passage running from the entrance to the back, which draws light and air into the house. The front part facing the street, which serves for receiving customers and as a workspace, is called the 'shop yard'; the cooking place where the range stands is called the 'running yard'.」 (translated; ); and one surviving house, 「The Nagae house, wholesale drapers for generations, has main buildings to the north and to the south, each with a low second story - a large Kyo-machiya of the work-and-dwelling-in-one type, made up of shop and residence.」 (translated; )
  • nagaya-jawiki - 「Merchant houses of the middle stratum and above kept independent shops on the main street, but the other townspeople and craftsmen almost all lived as tenants in the nagaya of the back streets.」 (translated; ); nagaya-jawiki - 「A nagaya of dwelling units lined up with a frontage of 9 shaku (about 2.7 m) and a depth of 2 ken (about 3.6 m) is called a nine-shaku-two-ken nagaya.」 (translated; )
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「In Kyoto, as in Edo, the stratification of the townsmen was marked: they were divided into townsmen holding large machiya with a frontage of 10 ken or more, townsmen holding ordinary machiya with a frontage of 4 or 5 ken, and townsmen holding small machiya with a frontage of 1 and a half to 2 ken, or renting, and the division had been advancing since the middle of the Edo period.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-shukubamachi - 「The lot division on both sides of the highway was of planned strip shape, the depth kept nearly constant, with differences in the frontage.
