Why is the magistrate's manor drawn as a plain walled box?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-07-27 (the box is a convention); jinya-jawiki (what a jin'ya held, and how much jin'ya differed); takayama-jinya-jawiki (the storehouses inside the Takayama grounds); bushijyoshi-daikan-jinya (a jin'ya as residence, office, court and tax store at once)

On a settlement map a walled compound is drawn as walls, a gate and an empty court, and the GM ruled on 2026-07-27 that this box is a simplification of the compound, not a scale reduction of it. It is a map drawing convention, and it has three consequences. First, the footprint on the settlement map need not match the compound's own detailed sheet in shape, proportion or size. Real seats were not one plan: a small domain's jin'ya ringed itself with earthworks or moats and held a residence, offices, retainers' houses, storehouses and a drill ground, and jin'ya differed in what they held and in how strongly they were enclosed. Second, features the detailed sheet draws outside the walls - notice boards at the gate, a bounty board, an approach fork to a cart gate or a parley door - need not appear on the settlement map at all. Third, the glyph is presumed to contain everything the detailed sheet shows, drawn or not; that is the same reasoning that puts a county seat's tax-rice granary inside the magistrate's compound rather than beside it as a separate building. That placement is the historical one: an intendant's jin'ya was at once their residence, their office, their court and the place the year's tax was stored, and the intendancy at Takayama kept its numbered earth-walled storehouses inside its own grounds. A difference between the two artifacts is therefore the convention working rather than an error.

The rule the map follows: a town draws the magistrate's compound as walls, a gate and an empty court, and nothing it is presumed to contain is drawn a second time beside it - which is why a county seat draws no separate granary unless it is one of the rice-transit towns that keeps a storehouse row of its own.
