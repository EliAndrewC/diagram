Why is the magistrate's manor drawn as a plain walled box?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-07-27

On a settlement map a walled compound is drawn as walls, a gate and an empty court, and the GM ruled on 2026-07-27 that this box is a simplification of the compound, not a scale reduction of it. It is a map drawing convention, and it has three consequences. First, the footprint on the settlement map need not match the compound's own detailed sheet in shape, proportion or size - the real compound may not even be rectangular. Second, features the detailed sheet draws outside the walls - gate boards, a bounty board, an approach fork to a cart gate or a parley door - need not appear on the settlement map at all. Third, the glyph is presumed to contain everything the detailed sheet shows, drawn or not; that is the same reasoning that puts a county seat's tax-rice granary inside the magistrate's compound rather than beside it as a separate building. A difference between the two artifacts is therefore the convention working rather than an error.

The rule the map follows: a town draws the magistrate's compound as walls, a gate and an empty court, and nothing it is presumed to contain is drawn a second time beside it - which is why a county seat draws no separate granary unless it is one of the rice-transit towns that keeps a storehouse row of its own.
