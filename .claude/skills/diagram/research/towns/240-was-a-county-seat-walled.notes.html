  • city-wall-grades-zhwiki - 「In the Ming and Qing most county cities, departmental cities, prefectural cities, guard cities and battalion cities had city walls」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「However, the walls of most county cities were only 4 to 10 m high」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ); and of the smallest grade, 「Circuit under 2 km. Cities of this kind were the most numerous, such as most of the country's county seats」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )

  • jokaku-toshi-jawiki - 「In self-governing towns such as Hakata (Chikuzen province), Sakai (Izumi province), Hirano (Settsu province) and Imai (Yamato province), the town's surroundings were enclosed with earth banks and moats (moated towns)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「Also, the temples of the Ikko sect were defended by moats and earth banks (temple towns).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「When the Warring States period came and castles grew in importance as the central bases of rule in the Warring States lords' management of their domains, then, as seen at Odawara Castle and elsewhere, castles appeared that took an outer-enclosure structure placing natural rivers, moats and earth banks around the castle town.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • jinya-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, the residence on the granted lands of a small daimyo without a castle or of a kotai-yoriai, the residence outside the castle town of a senior retainer of a large domain, the station of the irrigation works officers, and the office of the local officials who governed a domain's detached territory or the fief of a high-stipend bannerman were called jin'ya, and the daikansho, the official residence in the lands ruled by the shogunate's gundai and daikan, was also called a daikan jin'ya.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「In the early Edo period they were often placed at key points of communication and the like as military bases, and earth banks and moats were run around them after the manner of a castle, but in many cases high stone walls were not built.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「Where a settlement developed with the jin'ya as its core and became a town, it was also called a jin'ya-machi, and in many cases flourished as the core town of the area under its rule.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Heibonsha Encyclopedia Mypedia entry)
