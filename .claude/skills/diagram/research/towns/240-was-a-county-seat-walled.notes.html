  • kotobank-ikaku-toshi - 「Very many in China; each county town and provincial city, and villages too gathered together, had enclosures, and as a whole it has shown the aspect of one great society of enclosures.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「中国には非常に多く，各県城，省城はじめ農村も集合して囲郭をもち，全体として一大囲郭社会の様相を示してきた。」) (the Heibonsha Encyclopedia Mypedia entry)

  • jinya-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, the residence on the granted lands of a small daimyo without a castle or of a kotai-yoriai, the residence outside the castle town of a senior retainer of a large domain, the station of the irrigation works officers, and the office of the local officials who governed a domain's detached territory or the fief of a high-stipend bannerman were called jin'ya, and the daikansho, the official residence in the lands ruled by the shogunate's gundai and daikan, was also called a daikan jin'ya.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代には無城の小大名や交替寄合の拝領地における居館，大藩重臣の城下町外の屋敷，用水方普請役の詰所，藩飛地領や高禄旗本の知行地の支配にあたった地方役人の役所などを陣屋とよび，幕府郡代・代官の支配地における役宅である代官所も代官陣屋の別称があった。」) ... 「In the early Edo period they were often placed at key points of communication and the like as military bases, and earth banks and moats were run around them after the manner of a castle, but in many cases high stone walls were not built.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代初期には軍事上の拠点として交通の要衝などに設置されることが多く，城に準じて周囲に土塁や堀をめぐらしたが，高い石垣は築かれない場合が多い。」) ... 「Where a settlement developed with the jin'ya as its core and became a town, it was also called a jin'ya-machi, and in many cases flourished as the core town of the area under its rule.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「陣屋を核に集落が発達し，町場化した場合には陣屋町ともよばれ，支配領域の中核都市として活況を呈する場合が多かった。」) (the Heibonsha Encyclopedia Mypedia entry)
