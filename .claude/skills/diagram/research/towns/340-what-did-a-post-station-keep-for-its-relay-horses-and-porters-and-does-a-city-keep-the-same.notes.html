• toiyaba-kotobank - 「Its position differs from station to station: there are cases where there is one place in the middle of the post station, one place each at the upper and lower ends, or three places, upper, middle and lower. It was presided over by the toiya officer, assisted by the toshiyori officers, and under them were the chōtsuke, the umasashi and ninsokusashi, the kozashi and others.」 (translated; ) and 「The toshiyori was the toiya's assistant, the chōtsuke logged the men and horses going in and out each day, and the umasashi assigned the loads to the station's and the sukego's men and horses.」 (translated; )
  • toiyaba-kotobank - 「Sometimes a part of the house of the toiya, the head of the station officials, was used for it, and sometimes it was set up separately. If there were 2 toiya, there were 2 toiyaba, in keeping with the number of toiya.」 (translated; )
  • toiyaba-kotobank - 「About 1 or 2 toiyaba to one post station.」 (translated; ) and 「There are also stations, such as Okazaki, where the relay of porters and of horses was done at separate toiyaba.」 (translated; )
  • toiyaba-kotobank - 「when the station's men and horses were short, the men and horses of the sukego were gathered together there, and at times men and horses reaching several hundred head and several thousand people were directed, so that it became an uproar like a battlefield.」 (translated; )
  • toiyaba-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, the office at a post station that did the relaying of men and horses and the like.」 (translated; )
  • tenmayaku-kotobank - 「In Keicho 6 (1601) the shogunate made the post stations of the Tokaido keep 36 post horses each on hand, and requisitioned horses and men from the residents of the post station. This later spread to the Five Highways, and 100 men and 100 horses at each station of the Tokaido, 50 men and 50 horses on the Nakasendo, and 25 men and 25 horses on the other highways became the rule for the men and horses kept on hand.」 (translated; )
  • tenmayaku-kotobank - 「The Tokaido relay-horse system dates from 1601 (Keicho 6); many post stations kept 36 post horses on hand, and from Kan'ei 15 (1638) onward 100 men and 100 horses were fixed as the men and horses to be kept in place.」 (translated; )
