Theater stages on shrine ground (miyaji shibai)

In Edo-period Japan, the permanent roofed theater with seating was centered on the great cities, Edo, Kyoto and Osaka, and in Edo only a few licensed troupes were allowed to raise a yagura. Beside them, plays were put on in booths on the grounds of shrines and temples, under a temporary license, with nothing roofed but the stage. Such a play was a 宮地芝居 (miyaji shibai, "shrine-ground play"). The great cities' theaters are at Roofed playhouses (shibai goya).

  • By the 1670s, only four troupes in Edo were allowed to raise a yagura.
    By about the beginning of the Enpo era, the 1670s, the shogunate permitted only the Nakamura-za, the Ichimura-za, the Morita-za and the Yamamura-za to raise one.
  • Roofed theaters with seating arose late in the Edo period, centered on Edo, Kyoto and Osaka.
    The Japanese Wikipedia dates the kabuki theater with a roof and seating to the late Edo period, in those three cities.
  • A shrine-ground play was a booth play in a shrine's or temple's precinct, under a temporary license.
    A Japanese dictionary defines it so for the Edo period.
  • Where did a provincial town see its plays?
    The record's comparison of a capital's institutions with a provincial city's finds the permanent theater house belonging to the capital and, below it, the playing place on a shrine's or temple's ground (how a capital's institutions scale down); the licensed houses were in the three great cities.
  • Only the stage of a shrine-ground play could be roofed.
    A playhouse without a yagura, which is what a shrine-ground play was, was held to a simple booth, with no roof over anything but the stage, and no draw curtain, revolving stage or hanamichi.
  • Where did a shrine-ground play's audience stand?
    No page we read says where. The rule that nothing but the stage be roofed puts the audience in the open; this project's reading, a GUESS, is that it stood on the open ground between the stage and the shrine's or temple's hall, inside the precinct the play was put on in.
  • We guess that plays went to shrine and temple grounds because their festivals already drew crowds there.
    The pages we read give the license, not the reason. This project's reading, a GUESS, is that a precinct was where the festival crowd already gathered.
