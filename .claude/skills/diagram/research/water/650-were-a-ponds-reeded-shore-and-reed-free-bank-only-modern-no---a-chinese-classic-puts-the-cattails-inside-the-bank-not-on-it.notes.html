  • maoshi-zhengyi-juan7 - 「By the embankment of that marsh there are cattail and lotus. (An evocation. The embankment (bei) is the marsh's barrier. He is the lotus.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「彼澤之陂，有蒲與荷。（興也。陂，澤障也。荷，芙蕖也。」) ; 「The correct meaning: the marsh's barrier means the bank at the marsh's edge that holds back the water. Because these two plants are inside the embankment, the poem names the embankment's edge; the two plants do not grow on the embankment.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「○正義曰：澤障，謂澤畔障水之岸。以陂內有此二物，故舉陂畔言之，二物非生於陂上也。」) (the poem 澤陂, "The marsh's embankment", with the Mao commentary and the subcommentary headed 正義; the page names no author or date for the subcommentary)
  • chenfu-nongshu-juanshang - 「For a high field, look at the lie of its land, and where the high water gathers, reckon what it needs and dig a pond, giving up about two or three mu in every ten mu of field to store the water; at the turn of spring and summer, when the rains come in season, raise its bank high and large and dig its middle deep and broad, so that it is wide enough to hold them; on the bank plant mulberry and cudrania sparsely, to which cattle can be tethered. The cattle get cool shade and thrive, the bank is trodden by the cattle and becomes firm, the mulberry gets rich water and grows lush, in drought the water can be let out to irrigate, and in floods it does not spread over and harm the crop.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「若高田視其地勢，高水所會歸之處，量其所用而鑿為陂塘，約十畝田卽損二三畝以潴畜水；春夏之交，雨水時至，高大其隄，深闊其中，俾寬廣足以有容；隄之上，疎植桑柘，可以繫牛。牛得涼蔭而遂性，隄得牛踐而堅實，桑得肥水而沃美，旱得決水以灌溉，潦卽不致於瀰漫而害稼。」) (the chapter on the lie of the land, 地勢之宜篇)
  • nagano-shishi-kawayoke - 「Flood control in the Edo period is thought to have served both to protect fields and settlements from floods and, together with it, the water use of new-field development and irrigation-weir building.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の治水は、田畑と集落を洪水から守るとともに、新田開発・用水堰開発をおこなう利水と併せた機能を果たしていたと考えられる。」) ; 「In the Matsushiro domain embankments were called "tsutsumi" or "dote"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「松代領では、堤防を「堤(つつみ)」「土堤（手）(どて)」とよび」) ; 「On the slopes of the embankments, turfing was applied to prevent the soil washing away. Instead of turf, the haguchi method was also used, in which thatch grass, brushwood, pine needles and the like were layered and built up to protect the slope.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「堤防の法(のり)面には、土の流失を防ぐ芝付けをほどこした。芝のかわりに萱(かや)・粗朶(そだ)・松葉などを重ね組み、法面を保全する羽口(はぐち)工法も用いられた。」) (the section on the Matsushiro domain's flood-control measures)
