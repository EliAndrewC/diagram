The bund runs along the channel bank, and the mizuguchi is too small to draw

Sources: hattori-site-yayoiken (an early paddy's large bund along its canal, and its bund widths), sena-site-shizuoka (channel banks and bunds built and reinforced together), zhouli-suiren-wikisource (a path along every channel), suido-ishizue-yayoi-suiden and kotobank-minakuchi-matsuri (the inlet as an old form), kotobank-azebiki (the Edo survey's bund width), nougyoudoboku-suikou (the modern inlet's size), aze-standard (the modern standard bund), aze-jawiki

The question (GM 2026-08-17). We draw the earthen bunds running along the banks of the irrigation channels. Is that correct - and is the water reaching the paddies through gaps too small to render?

What the research found: yes to both, and the second is precisely quantified.

  • A continuous bund along the channel is right. The paddy's own aze IS the channel's bank - a basin has to be watertight on every side, and the side that faces the ditch is no exception, or the plot would simply drain into it. Early paddies dug up in Japan are built this way. At the Hattori site the central canal is "enclosed by large bunds", which ran along it and seem also to have served as paths for farm work; at the Sena site, from the Late Yayoi into the Kofun period, the channel banks and the large bunds were reinforced together with stake rows and yaita sheet boards. The Rites of Zhou put a path on every channel in its graded series of channels, smallest to largest.
  • The opening is the mizuguchi (水口), and it is sub-pixel. The opening itself is old: the excavated paddies had intakes and outlets, and a rite held at a paddy's inlet is in a poem of about 1134. Its size is known only from modern design practice, which asks for one intake per plot (two or more only when they would stand 50 m or more apart), of width within 50 cm, its sill 0-10 cm above the field surface so the inflow does not scour the bed, with an allowable flow velocity of about 0.4 m/s. The plot's outlet - the shirimito (尻水戸; the source's 水じり / 落水口), an overflow set 5-10 cm below the field surface - is the same size. A notch of up to 50 cm is at most about 1.6 ft wide: a gap of a pixel or two in the bund at the finest scale, and half a pixel at the coarsest.
  • How wide the bund is, before modern times. The Edo land survey reckoned a bund at one shaku, about 30 cm, and took it out of the taxed area; the dictionary's example, a shogunate order of 1726, reckons that deduction at one shaku five sun (a sun is a tenth of a shaku, so about 45 cm in all) with the adjoining land. The Hattori paddies' small bunds are 20-60 cm wide and "were not for people to walk on"; their large bunds, the ones along the canal, are 80-150 cm. A modern standard bund is 40 cm across its crest and 30 cm high, at 1:1 side slopes, about 1.3 ft at the crest and 3.3 ft at the base between plots at one level, inside the same range. Our drawn bund of ~1.5 real ft sits inside the older figures for a bund between plots. The bund along a canal is the one the old figures make wider: a large bund of about three to five feet.

The decision it drove (a deliberate NON-render). The mizuguchi and shirimito are never drawn on any settlement map. A notch that size does not read as an opening at any scale the maps use, and a legible one would be drawn larger than life on the most numerous feature on the map - one per paddy, on a map with hundreds. The bund is therefore drawn CONTINUOUS along every channel bank, and the reader is expected to understand that water crosses it through openings below the resolution of the sheet, exactly as they understand that a drawn farmhouse has a door. The unbroken bund is not a claim that the paddies are unwatered.

What stands on a record from before modern times. The continuous bund along the channel, the opening in it, and a dividing bund of about a foot and a half all do. The opening's 50 cm and its sill and outlet levels are modern design figures only, and they decide nothing on the map, since the opening is not drawn. One width does change: at the one early site measured, the bund along the canal was a large bund, wider than the bunds between plots.

The rule the map follows: a bund between plots, or along a delivery ditch, is drawn about 1.5 ft wide; the bund along the head race or a canal is a large bund, drawn about 3 ft wide, inside the 80-150 cm read at the Hattori site. This is a calibration against one excavated site, not a measured Edo figure: no width was found for an Edo bund along a canal.
