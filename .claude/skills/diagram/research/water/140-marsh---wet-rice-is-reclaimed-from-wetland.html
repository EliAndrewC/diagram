Marsh - wet rice is reclaimed FROM wetland

Sources: aas-rice-technology (the earliest domesticated rice found in naturally marshy areas,18 and the poldered field reclaimed from swamp or lake behind a high solid dyke), cao-2019-abandoned-paddy (marshland converted to paddy, and abandoned paddy returning to a wetland plant community)

This is grounded in the fact that wet rice is reclaimed FROM marsh: a poldered field is a large area of land reclaimed from a swamp or a lake behind a high solid dike, divided inside into blocks of fields with drainage channels running down the middle, the dike itself wide enough in places to build houses on and planted with trees against erosion.16 Naming the lower-Yangzi and Tai-Lake polders in particular, and the characters 圩田/围田 (weitian), is this record's own; the source describes the poldered field as a Chinese type without them. And where reclamation stops (or the ground is too wet to manage) it stays reed wetland: on lowland plain where farming turned most of the marsh into paddy, an abandoned paddy goes back to a wetland plant community by natural succession.17
