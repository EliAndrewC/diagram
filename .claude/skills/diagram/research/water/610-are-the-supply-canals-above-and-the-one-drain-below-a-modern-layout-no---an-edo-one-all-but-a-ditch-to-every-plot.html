Are the supply canals above and the one drain below a modern layout? No - an Edo one, all but a ditch to every plot

Sources: suido-ishizue-kishu (the combined Kanto-school channels and the Kishu school's separation), suido-ishizue-minuma (the 1728 reclamation with supply on the edge and the drain in the center), matsuura-2017-minuma (where the trunks and the drain lay against the paddies), saitama-museum-izawa (the Konuma drainage gathered down the center)

The question. A hamlet's water is drawn as supply canals along the high edge of its fields and one collector drain along the low line. Modern consolidation separates supply from drainage (where the paddies' water goes); is the separated layout only modern?

What the record found. Not at the scale of the trunk canals and the drain. On the Kanto plain the older practice, the Kanto school, ran irrigation and drainage in the same channels, so that "securing water for the downstream always made drainage difficult upstream"; the Kishu school that the shogun Yoshimune brought there in the 1720s "separated the drainage rivers". Its reclamation of the Minuma reservoir in 1728 "set supply canals along the edge of the former reservoir and a drainage channel in its center": the supply canals, split into two trunks, ran on the terrace edge a step higher than the paddies, and the drain on the paddies' lowest ground - though, as the historian of the works adds, not everything was neatly separated. At the Konuma marsh, reclaimed from 1729, the drainage was gathered down the center, and the villages below used it again as their irrigation water. What these sources do not show is the field end of the separated type: a supply ditch reaching every plot and an outlet from each into the drain is the standard of modern consolidation (where the paddies' water goes), and no source read shows it before modern times.

What it means on the map. The supply canals along the high edge and the one collector on the low line are an Edo form, and stay. A ditch that gives every plot its own inlet is the modern part, and under the GM's ruling of 2026-09-28 that nothing attested only in modern times is drawn, it goes; the older field form it gives way to, water passing from plot to plot, is described in the fields record.
