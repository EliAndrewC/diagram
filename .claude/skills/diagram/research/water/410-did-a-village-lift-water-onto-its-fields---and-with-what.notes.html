• kotobank-fumiguruma - 「Japanese paddy irrigation is generally done easily by channels or by setting up a simple weir, so its degree of dependence on lifting by wheels was low.」 (translated; ) 「the dragon-bone wheel used in the 17th century was hard to make and did not spread, and the treadle wheel that appeared in its place in the 18th century also spread only in the downstream reclaimed lands and new-field districts where making new channels was difficult.」 (translated; ) (the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, older edition, entry 水車, as excerpted on this page; it dates the treadle wheel to the 18th century where the same page's main entry gives the Kanbun era, 1661-73)
  • ryukotsusha-jawiki - 「it is said to have been invented in China」 (translated; ) 「two people facing each other tread the one wheel at its upper end, turning it.」 (translated; ) 「it spread mainly in the Kinki region and was used in the early Edo period」 (translated; ) 「it was driven out by the invention ... and spread of the treadle wheel.」 (translated; )
  • fumiguruma-jawiki - 「According to Okura Nagatsune's Nogu benriron, final volume, of Bunsei 5 (1822), it was made in the Kanbun era (between 1661 and 1672) by Kyoya Shichibei and Kyoya Seibei of Nonin-bashi, Osaka, and had spread through the provinces of Japan by around Horeki to An'ei (between 1751 and 1780)」 (translated; ) 「there were three types of one-person treadle wheel: 4 shaku 5 sun, 5 shaku and 5 shaku 5 sun. There were also large two-person wheels and small hand-turned wheels.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-fumiguruma - 「a human-powered water-lifting machine peculiar to Japan, used almost exclusively for paddy irrigation」 (translated; ) 「It is not installed fixed; it can be carried to wherever it is needed and used. It was generally used for lifts of about 1 m or less from the channel to the paddy surface, but for higher paddies two or three might be used in a row.」 (translated; ) 「whereas most dragon-bone wheels needed the labor of two people, the treadle wheel needed only one.」 (translated; ) (Horio Hisashi, the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, revised edition)
  • settsu-fumiguruma - 「the wheel is 164 cm in diameter, with 17 blades」 (translated; ) 「its lifting capacity is a head of a few tens of cm」 (translated; ) 「light, it can be taken apart and carried to the paddy by one person」 (translated; ) 「I heard it was for two treaders」 (translated; ) 「a wheel trodden by six people in three tiers, upper, middle and lower, survived until after the war」 (translated; ) (the two-person and six-person wheels are given under the heading "for drainage")
  • kotobank-fumiguruma - 「In regions and farms without a treadle wheel, a bucket with two pairs of ropes attached, called torioke or furitsurube, was used; in pairs, two people scooped the channel's water and lifted it as if swinging it up into the paddy.」 (translated; )
  • longgu-shuiche-zhwiki - 「the dragon-bone water wheel began about the Eastern Han」 (translated; ) 「in the Song period of agricultural expansion, the dragon-bone wheel's technique improved and it became more widespread」 (translated; ) 「the dragon-bone wheel is suited to use in plains regions, lifting water about 1 to 2 meters, or lifting water directly from a supply canal onto the fields」 (translated; ) 「powered by human (or animal) strength」 (translated; )
  • tiangong-shuili - 「Wherever by a river a noria is made, a weir blocks the flow and turns it beneath the wheel, driving the wheel round; it draws the water into its tubes and tips each one into the trough, and the water flows into the fields. Day and night without rest, a hundred mu are without worry.」 (translated; ) 「At lakes and ponds whose water does not flow, it is turned on a turntable by an ox, or trodden round by several people gathered. The pump's body is two zhang at its longest, and half that at its shortest. Inside, boards are strung on a dragon-bone chain and carry the water upward against its flow.」 (translated; ) 「In general one person's full day waters five mu of fields, and an ox twice that. On shallow ponds and small ditches that will not take a long pump, a pump of a few chi, turned fast by one person's two hands, waters only two mu in a full day's work.」 (translated; ) 「With the well-sweep and the windlass the result is smaller still.」 (translated; )
