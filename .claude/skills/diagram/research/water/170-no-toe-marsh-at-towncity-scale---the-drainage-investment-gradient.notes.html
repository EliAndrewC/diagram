• shitsuden-kotobank - 「A paddy that drains badly and in which the water never runs out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水はけが悪く、水がきれることのない田。」) (デジタル大辞泉) ; 「It refers to a badly drained paddy on which, even during the period when the paddy is not irrigated, farm work is difficult because of excess moisture and standing water, and on which a second (winter) crop such as wheat cannot be grown.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水田の灌漑を行わない期間も過湿、滞水のために農作業が困難で、また麦などの裏作ができない排水不良田をいう。」) (日本大百科全書) ; 「It is thought that from around the late Yayoi period development from wet paddy to dry paddy began in western Japan, and that an advance in paddy-rice agriculture was seen.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「弥生後期頃から西日本で湿田から乾田への開発が始まり、水稲農業の進展がみられたと考えられる。」) (山川 日本史小辞典) ; 「In Japan, from the Meiji period onward, irrigation and drainage works have been carried out as a national undertaking, and the conversion of wet paddy (shitsuden) into dry paddy has been pushed forward.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「日本では明治以降、国家的事業として灌漑排水の工事が行われ、湿田の乾田化が進められてきている。」) (世界大百科事典) (a paddy that drains badly and never runs dry; a badly drained field, waterlogged even outside the irrigation season, on which work is hard and no winter crop possible; wet-to-dry conversion began in western Japan around the late Yayoi; from Meiji the state has driven irrigation and drainage works converting wet paddy to dry.)
  • yato-jawiki - 「A yato, where spring water is easily obtained in the catchment area and which is moreover not readily damaged by floods, was land on which rice cultivation was easy so long as drainage could be secured.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「集水域で湧水が容易に得られ、しかも洪水による被害を受けにくい谷戸は、排水さえ確保できれば稲作をしやすい土地であった。」) ; 「Where the hours of sunlight are short and the drainage is poor, it often becomes marshland.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「日照時間が短く、水はけが悪い場合には湿地状態となることが多い。」) (A yato, where spring water is easily had in the catchment and flood damage is slight, was land easy to grow rice on provided drainage could be secured; with short sunlight hours and poor drainage it often becomes marshy.)
