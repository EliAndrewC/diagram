Does a moat have a current?

Sources: the GM's catch of 2026-07-25; the junction-angle finding at Canal junction angles; this project's measurements of the two drawn provincial cities

Yes - but not one current. A ring has no upstream end: water enters at one point and leaves at another, and it travels BOTH ways round the circuit to get there. So a moat records an INLET and an OUTLET rather than a bearing, and "downstream of a moated city" is reasoned from those two points. An open, river-cut moat derives them for itself, because its two junction feet ARE the inlet and the outlet; a closed ring declares them, because the ring is dug before its feeder and its outfall exist.

Where a channel meets the ring, the junction is SWEPT with the current (the GM caught it by eye, 2026-07-25). An offtake leaves the moat pointing downstream and a tributary joins it pointing downstream: its heading AT the junction must carry a downstream component. A square tap is read here as shedding sediment into its own mouth (the design standards read warn of a right-angle bend overtopping, not of silt), and on the page it says nothing at all about which way the water runs. The angle finding behind it is Canal junction angles.

What the maps show. Every offtake on Tango and Nagahara leaves the ring at 47 to 48 degrees and every drain arrives between 14 and 60.

ONE direction model (the GM's ruling of 2026-07-25). A moat records an inlet and an outlet, and every rule about its current reads those two points - never a direction worked out again from the geometry alongside them, because two sources of truth for one fact is how drift starts.

Why the current is deliberately SNAPPED TO A CARDINAL, and not measured precisely. Modeling the ring exactly - taking the ring's tangent at each tap, in the direction of travel along that tap's own arc - was built, and it is the physically correct picture of the water. It was rejected because it makes the question meaningless. An irrigation offtake leaves the ring roughly PERPENDICULAR, so its component along a precisely measured tangent is near arbitrary: the exact version flagged one of Tango's taps and two of Nagahara's while sparing their neighbors on nothing more than which side of the tap the field happened to sit. The coarse hemisphere is what makes the test mean "the field is not back upstream". So the coarse form is kept on purpose; the fine version measures the wrong quantity. A map drawing convention in the honest sense - a simplification chosen because the precise measurement answers a different question.

The rule the map follows: Town and city tier, where no generator draws the settlement yet. A ring moat records an inlet and an outlet, never a single direction; an open, river-cut moat takes them from its two junctions with the river. Where a channel meets the moat, the channel's heading AT the junction carries a downstream component - an offtake leaving, a drain arriving - and a tap that meets the ring square is regraded by walking its MOAT-SIDE attachment along the rim by arc length until the throat is swept; the sluice and the field it feeds do not move. Separately and coarsely, an irrigation offtake must not leave the ring from a point back upstream of the ground it waters, judged against the current snapped to a cardinal.
