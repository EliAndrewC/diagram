• chang-jiang-jawiki - 「The foremost headwater (the true source, 本源) is the Tuotuo He (Toto River, 沱沱河), which rises from a glacier of the Tanggula Mountains (唐古拉山脈).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ; 「The Tuotuo He joins the Dangqu to become the Tongtian He, and in the western part of Qumarleb County (曲麻萊県) it also joins the Chumar He (楚瑪爾河).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ; 「The upper course, the Jinsha Jiang (kinsakou, 金沙江) or the Dichu River」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the headmost source is the Tuotuo He rising from Tanggula glaciers; the Tuotuo He joins the Dangqu to become the Tongtian He; the upper course is the Jinsha Jiang (or Dichu); the same sentence, after a Tibetan gloss, calls the lower course the Yangzi Jiang.)
  • chang-jiang-jawiki - 「The upper Chang Jiang, which from Yibin to Yichang City in Hubei Province flows through the Sichuan Basin and the Three Gorges, is popularly called the "Chuan Jiang" [because it flows through Sichuan].」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ; 「The middle Chang Jiang, from Yichang to Jiujiang City in Jiangxi Province, is popularly called the "Jing Jiang" because it flows through ancient Jingzhou (荊州, the Hubei Province area).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (from Yichang to Jiujiang the middle river is popularly called the Jing Jiang because it flows through ancient Jingzhou (Hubei).)
  • chang-jiang-zhwiki - 「Below Yibin is the 'Chang Jiang' in the narrow sense; the reaches of the main stem each have their own alternative names - Yibin to Yichang is also called the Chuan Jiang, Zhijiang to Chenglingji is also called the Jing Jiang, the reach within Jiangxi province from Ruichang to Pengze is also called the Xunyang Jiang, the reach within Anhui province is also called the Wan Jiang, and the reach below Nanjing to the Yangtze mouth is also called the Yangzi Jiang.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • chang-jiang-jawiki - 「Because there was formerly a ferry called the "Yangzijin ferry landing" (揚子津渡口), it was called the "Yangzi Jiang". This has come to be used outside China as a word for the Chang Jiang as well.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ; 「The latter was originally the name of a bridge called Yangzi Bridge (揚子橋), but it was misused by Westerners as the name of the whole Chang Jiang.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (it was called Yangzi Jiang after the former Yangzijin ferry crossing, and that name came to be used outside China for the whole Chang Jiang; the latter was originally the name of a bridge, Yangzi Bridge, misapplied by Westerners to the whole river. The reach below Nanjing is carried by the chang-jiang-zhwiki note.)
  • shinano-gawa-jawiki - 「Of this, the stretch called the Shinano-gawa is the Niigata Prefecture area; going upstream into Nagano Prefecture the name changes to "Chikuma-gawa" (chikumagawa).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (going upstream into Nagano Prefecture the name changes to Chikuma-gawa.)
  • yodogawa-jawiki - 「It flows out of Lake Biwa at Otsu. Here it is called the Seta River. Around the point where it enters Kyoto Prefecture it changes its name to the Uji River, and further on, near the boundary of Kyoto and Osaka prefectures, at Shimamoto, it joins the Katsura River and the Kizu River. From this confluence down to the sea is the Yodo River in the narrow sense」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • sumida-gawa-jawiki - 「Once the Edo period began, the stretch downstream of the area around Azuma Bridge was also called the Okawa (大川).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ; 「It is recorded as "Sumida-gawa" (住田河) in a Daijokan edict of 835 (Jowa 2), and was also called the "Miyato-gawa" (宮戸川) among other names.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ; 「It was also called the Miyato-gawa and the Asakusa-gawa.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ; 「Miyato-gawa (the former name of the Sumida-gawa around Asakusa)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (In the Edo period the stretch below Azuma Bridge was also called the Okawa; an 835 Daijokan edict records it as 住田河 and it was also called the Miyato-gawa; it was also called the Miyato-gawa / Asakusa-gawa; the Miyato-gawa was its name around Asakusa. The last gloss is from the page's list of rakugo stories, under the story named for the river.)
  • shinano-gawa-jawiki - 「In old times it was called the 'Okawa' (great river) as 'a big river', but later the lower reaches came to be called the Shinano River, as the river flowing down from Shinano Province」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (that nobody needed a whole-course name because nobody administered or traveled the whole course is this page's inference from the cases)
