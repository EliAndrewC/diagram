Why does every ditch turn on a curve?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-07-25; meander-enwiki (how far a natural channel's bend swings against its own width, and what a turn does to the two banks), jsslkx-002-2021 (the alignment rule for a dug canal - the large bends followed, the small ones straightened), jawiki-yosuiro

Because water turns on a SWEPT BEND and never on a mitered corner. A hand-dug earthen ditch that changes direction does it on a sweep: a sharp corner scours on the outside and silts on the inside until the water has rounded it anyway,72 and the standing alignment rule for a dug canal says the same thing from the design side: follow the large bends where the ground leads, and straighten the small ones, rather than put in a corner.73 Sharp corners belong to MASONRY - a stone-lined aqueduct, a sluice box, a revetted canal - and nothing at these scales is lined, so nothing on these maps shows one.173

How wide the sweep is. The radius is about 2.5 channel widths, capped at 35% of either adjacent leg. A meandering channel turns on a radius of two to three times its own width,74 which is the geometry the figure is taken from, and a dug farm ditch is no tighter - which is this record's own step from a river's bend to a dug one, not the article's - so the bend SCALES with the ditch instead of being a fixed drawing quirk. A fat head-race sweeps wide and a thin lateral turns tight, both at the same real ratio, at every map scale. The cap stops two neighboring bends from eating the leg between them.

What is deliberately NOT rounded. An OFFTAKE, where a branch leaves a main: a notch cut in the bank is a junction, not a bend, and the main is not turning there. And a turn under about 8 degrees, which has no visible elbow and would only gain points from being rounded.
