• kotobank-shigarami - 「Stakes driven in a row in a river, with brushwood, bamboo and the like twined onto them from both sides, to hold back the flow of water.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水流をせき止めるために、川の中に杭(くい)を打ち並べて、その両側から柴(しば)や竹などをからみつけたもの。」) (the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's entry)
  • kotobank-shigarami - 「[Earliest examples] "The Asuka River - if one laid a shigarami across it and dammed it, would even the flowing water go gently?" (Man'yoshu (late 8th century) 2.197) "at the meeting of the two rivers they cut great trees and laid them as an abatis, and heaped up shigarami in great number" (Heike monogatari (early 13th century) 7)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「[初出の実例]「明日香川四我良美(シガラミ)渡し塞(せ)かませば流るる水ものどにかあらまし」(出典：万葉集（８Ｃ後）二・一九七) 「二の河の落ちあひに大木をきってさかもぎにひき、しがらみを夥しうかきあげたれば」(出典：平家物語（１３Ｃ前）七)」) (the poem's fence is imagined, not one that stood; the Heike one is a work of war)
  • suido-ishizue-manyoshu - 「"In the Asuka River the fine water-weed grows in every shallow, but because of the shigarami it cannot sway together" (book 7). What was laid across the river was a shigarami (fence), stakes driven in with wood or bamboo laid across to dam the water, the same structure as the one that appeared at the Kodera site (Ehime prefecture).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明日香川瀬々に玉藻は生ひたれどしがらみあれば靡きあはなくに(巻7) 川に掛けられたのは「しがらみ(柵)」であり、杭を打ち込み木や竹を横にわたして水を堰き止めており、古照遺跡(愛媛県)で出現したものと同様の構造である。」) (the build is the column's reading; the poem does not describe it)
  • kotobank-seki - 「The oldest example is stakes and sheet piles set up in a channel at the Itazuke site in Fukuoka prefecture (of the Yusu pottery phase), said to be either Final Jomon or the start of the Yayoi period.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「最古の実例は，福岡県板付遺跡の水路に杭や矢板を立てたもの（夜臼（ゆうす）式土器の時期）であって，縄文時代晩期とも弥生時代初期ともいわれる。」); 「A representative example is the weir of the Kodera site in Ehime prefecture (Kofun period). Slanting members are driven in from downstream toward upstream, a crosspiece is laid, and upright members are driven in against it on the downstream side. This is repeated in three tiers (4.2 m wide).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「その代表例が愛媛県古照（こでら）遺跡のせき（古墳時代）である。下流側から上流に向けて斜材を打ち込み，横材を渡し，それに接して下流側に縦材を打ち込む。これを3段繰り返す構造（幅4.2m）になっている。」) (Sahara Makoto's section of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry)
  • kotobank-seki - 「Weirs of the Edo period were almost all for obtaining farm water, and according to the Kenrei Shuchi they can be broadly divided into grass weirs and washing weirs.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸期のせきは農業用水の取得のためのものがほとんどであり，《県令須知》によれば草ぜきと洗いぜきに大別しうる。」) (Kitamura Toshio's section; the section does not say what a grass weir was built of)
  • mizu-shiba-izeki - 「The weir was built here in the early Edo period. It began with the new-field development by the Satsuma domain that started about 380 years ago. It is handed down that at first it was either a stone weir or plants woven onto a foundation of wooden stakes.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「ここに堰がつくられたのは江戸時代初期。380年ほど前に始まった薩摩藩による新田開発に端を発する。当初は石堰だったとも、木杭の基礎に草木を編み込んだものだったとも伝わる。」); 「Built every year in March; in September, when the rice harvest is over, only half is taken down, and the next year the brushwood is laid anew」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「毎年3月に築き、稲刈りが終わる9月に半分だけ撤去して、翌年は新たに柴架けをする」); 「In 1902 (Meiji 35) the foundation became stone, but it was often washed away by typhoons, so in 1950 (Showa 25) a concrete foundation was made.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「1902年（明治35）に基礎は石となったが台風でしばしば流されたため、1950年（昭和25）にコンクリート製の基礎がつくられた。」) (the page is of 2018, so about 380 years before is about 1638)
