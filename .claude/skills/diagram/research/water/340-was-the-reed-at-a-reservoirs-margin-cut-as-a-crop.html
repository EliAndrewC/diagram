Was the reed at a reservoir's margin cut as a crop?

Sources: ohmi-yoshi (the reed harvest, attested for Lake Biwa and river reed beds), tameike-jawiki (a pond's embankment and its inside mown regularly), seitai-kobo-kaibori-2017 (what a pond's draining yielded: mud for the fields, fish for the table)

Not found - so it is drawn as a standing fringe. The reed that fringes a tameike (see A reservoir's shore is reeded) is the same plant that was cut elsewhere as a crop. The reed-harvest economy (yoshizu screens, thatch, an uncut bed going to ruin (荒れる)) is well attested - for Lake Biwa and river reed beds, by an organization named for Lake Biwa. No source, primary or scholarly, was found putting that economy on tameike margins, and extending it there is an inference the sources do not make.

What a pond did give the village. The pond itself was tended: because a tameike serves the farms, its embankment and the inside of the pond are regularly mown - but no page read says what became of the cut. And when a pond was drained out of season for kaibori, the mud dredged from its bottom went onto the fields as fertilizer and its fish were eaten.

What the map draws. The margin's reed as a standing fringe, and nothing of a reed harvest - no cut bed, no drying racks, no stacks - because nothing read puts one there. That is a guess only in the negative sense: the harvest is left out because it is unattested, not because it is known to be absent.
