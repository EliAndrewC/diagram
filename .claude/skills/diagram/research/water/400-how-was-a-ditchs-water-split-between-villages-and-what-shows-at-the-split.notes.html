  • bunsui-jawiki - 「the bansui system was set up, under which the width of channels and the opening times of the weirs and gates leading to them were restricted area by area, and especially in seasons and areas of little water, strict measurement of the flow and adjustment of the intake were carried out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • bunsui-jawiki - 「conflict, especially between the upstream reach, favored in division, and the disadvantaged downstream reach, often arose」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • ishizue-jugo-yosui - 「Water tries to flow toward land lower by even a few millimeters.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「If the cross-section where the channels part differs even slightly, the amount of water that flows differs. Moreover, if even one rock is thrown into that fork, the flow changes sharply. The village upstream has more water and the advantage.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • ishizue-jugo-yosui - 「dug in Ten'ei 1 (1110)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「building the Juugou great weir on the Kuzuryu River to take in water, and digging a channel a full 28 km ... the ten villages of the Kawaguchi estate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「very detailed rules were made - the channel width six shaku one sun [a sun is a tenth of a shaku], the water depth three shaku two sun one bu - and a strict system of management was laid down under supervising officials ※2」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「※2 ... the regulations agreed in Tenbun 6 (1537)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「in the Edo period 118 villages」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • nagdoren-usonokuchi - 「From that time on, between the former Kami village, where the water source lay, and the former Nakahata village, where the intake lay, water disputes arose without end.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「every year at the eighty-eighth night ... the seals were broken on two closely guarded wisteria vines, one long and one short, measuring sticks were made to each length, and at a plunge pool partway along the channel water mouths matching the sticks' lengths were made with sandbags to divide the water」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「it was long continued until the Usonokuchi circular divider was completed in Showa 28 [1953].」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-bunsui - 「A technical means used when dividing water from one channel between two areas or villages: a graduated stake is set at the division point, and division by its height is the bungi.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, older edition, entry 分木, as excerpted on this page)
  • kuji-entobunsui-jawiki - 「Before the cylinder divider was built, the Kuji bunryo-hi, to divide the Nikaryo canal's water in the four directions branching from here according to the cultivated area of each basin, was built in the mid-Edo period」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「but even after that the disputes over the amount of water never ceased」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • tcmap-huoquan - 「Under the pavilion iron pillars separate ten openings; this was the boundary of the water division between the two counties of Hongdong and Zhaocheng, three to the south and seven to the north」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) 「the stele in the stele pavilion records the division, and a diagram of the division is carved on its back.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • cylinder-divider-jawiki - 「from the Taisho era, dividers capable of accurate distribution were devised」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「The first cylinder divider was invented by Kaji Kan'ichi and installed in 1914 (Taisho 3) at Koizumi village (Kani district, Gifu)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
