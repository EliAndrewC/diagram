  • suido-ishizue-iseki - 「Grass weir: where an agreement with downstream users prevents building a permanent weir, a simple weir of driven stakes with kaya woven between them is still used today (Chiyoda, Saga).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「草堰 下流との申し合わせにより、恒久的な堰がつくれない所では、杭を打ち、その間にカヤを編みこんだ簡単な堰が今でも使われている。(佐賀県千代田町)」)
  • suido-ishizue-iseki - 「Kodera site: stakes are driven obliquely and logs laid on the row; stakes are then driven again, obliquely or vertically, to fix the logs. Repeating this produces an A-frame form. Some joints of the timberwork are tied with wisteria vine, the gaps are packed with clay, gravel and the like and held with woven grass, raising the weir's ability to lift the water level.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「古照遣跡 杭を斜めに打ち込んで丸太をこの列上に置いたのち、丸太を固定するために再び杭を斜めや垂直に打ち込む。こうした作業をくり返すことによって合掌形式としている。木組みのいくつかの個所はフジづるでしばり、すき間には粘土や礫などをつめ、草などの編物で押さえて堰き止め、水位を上昇させる機能を高めている。」; 遣跡 is the page's own misprint for 遺跡)
  • hrr-agagawa-dento - 「Weirs of this kind were also built in the Edo period; there were weirs made of ushi-waku and waku, and weirs of stone and of kaya-haguchi.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「このような堰は江戸時代にもつくられており、牛枠や枠でつくられた堰、石や萱羽口の堰などもありました。」; "of this kind" refers to diversion weirs built to work in flood)
  • hrr-agagawa-dento - 「A waku is timber joined with mortise and tenon, not woven, built as a four-sided frame, set in the river and fixed by throwing stones in from above.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「枠というのは、木にほぞを組み、編んだりせず、四方形を組んで川に入れ、上から石を投げ込んで固定します。」; given under groynes)
  • kotobank-jakago - 「A cylindrical basket woven of bamboo, wire or the like, packed with cobbles or rubble. The baskets are mostly about 40-60 cm in diameter and 4-10 m long.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「竹や鉄線などで編んだ円筒形の籠の中に玉石(たまいし)や栗石(くりいし)を詰め込んだもの。籠の大きさは直径40～60センチメートル、長さ4～10メートル程度のものが多い。」; the Nipponica encyclopedia's entry)
