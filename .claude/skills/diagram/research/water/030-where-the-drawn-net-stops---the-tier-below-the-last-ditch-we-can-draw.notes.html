• kaogongji-wikisource - 「The cut of one ou, 1 chi wide and 1 chi deep, is called a quan. At the head of the field it is doubled: 2 chi wide and 2 chi deep is called a sui. These lead directly into the rivers, and each bears its own name.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「一耦之伐，廣尺深尺謂之畎。田首倍之，廣二尺、深二尺謂之遂。九夫為井，井間廣四尺、深四尺謂之溝。方十里為成，成間廣八尺、深八尺謂之洫。方百里為同，同間廣二尋、深二仞謂之澮。專達於川，各載其名。」) (an ou is two spades working together; a fu is one farmer's allotment)

  • akiba-1948-kangai - 「Around Meiji 20 (1887), the need to consolidate the plots of paddy land was urged, and as a result the Land Consolidation Law was promulgated in Meiji 32 (1899). It did not yet sufficiently include agricultural water use; only with the amendment of the Land Consolidation Law in [Meiji] 43 (1910) did it come to be included, and a proper arrangement of paddy plots and irrigation channels was realized. Accordingly, irrigation that until then had mostly been flow-through irrigation changed to ponded-storage irrigation and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明治20年前後から田地の区劃整理の必要が叫ばれた結果，耕地整理法が明治32年に発布せられるに至った。しかしこれにはまだ農業水利は充分包含されていなかったが，43年耕地整理法の改正が行われて初めて包含されるようになって，水田区劃と潅漑水路の正しい配置が実現した。従ってそれ迄の多くは掛流し潅漑であったのが瀦溜潅漑等に変って行った。」) (the essay gives the older practice as a consequence and cites no evidence for it)
