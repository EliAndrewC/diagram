Does the reed marsh at the field's foot run along the drain?

Sources: maff-drain-shape (the interceptor drain laid along the contours), matsuura-2017-minuma (where an Edo-period drain lay among the paddies, and what the ground beside it was)

How wide the wet toe is, and why it follows the fan rather than the valley, is its own question; this one is where its inner edge runs.

The GM, on Inashiro (2026-08-26): "I would have expected that the marshland would run parallel to the drainage ditch, but what we actually have is a drainage ditch which runs at an angle ... and then marshland where the demarcation ... appears to run exactly east to west parallel to the edge of the map. that makes it look like a mistake rather than a feature of the geography." The research pass: MAFF's field-drainage design standard places the field-toe interceptor drain (承水路) "nearly parallel to the contours, with a gentle gradient", and the collectors (集水路/排水路) perpendicular to them at 200-300 m spacing (MAFF 排水路の形状・構造と適用条件); the fan-toe spring line is an arc along the fan's foot (the question on its width). So in reality the toe drain and the wet edge run TOGETHER, and our collector's drawn grade is steeper than a lowland collector's fall (~0.1% in this page's estimate; the upland-field standard read gives far steeper grades) (convention - a map drawing convention, recorded: the drain is drawn steep so its flow reads; the marsh must not inherit that fiction). The decision on the wet edge: the toe band's inner edge is sampled across the slope - at each station the shoulder above the LOCAL lowest crop point, smoothed over three stations - so the reeds begin at the fan's foot everywhere along the collector.

Two ways the wet edge is set, and the rule for choosing between them. Some maps in the pool draw their toe by hand, with literal coordinates, and deliberately claim a generous region that OVERLAPS the crop - the reeds then fill up to the field's true low edge whatever its shape, because the scatter skips any point standing on a paddy. Kikuta's hand-drawn marsh reaches well above its entire collector, so it claims ground at rice height as wet, and that is invisible only because of the skip. A computed toe takes its inner edge from the cultivated extent instead.

Is that alignment older than modern drainage standards? Not on any page read. The standard it rests on is the ministry's modern design standard, and one written for upland fields. The one Edo-period work read that places a drain among paddies, the reclamation of the Minuma reservoir in 1728, put its drain "at the lowest ground of the paddies", and the ground beside it was farmed rather than left to reeds: "at the time these paddies were poorly drained deep (wet) paddies", made dry only by modern drainage works. No source read from before modern times places a reed marsh, or any undrained waste, along the line of a drain. So a reed edge laid along the collector is a form attested only in modern times, and under the GM's ruling of 2026-09-28 that nothing attested only in modern times is drawn, it goes. The wet ground at the fan's foot stays, on its own evidence; that the ground beside an old drain was wet paddy rather than reeds is this record's reading of one reclaimed marsh, not a rule found.
