Anchors: headwater streams cluster near ~0.32 m; Himeji's moats run 12-34 m by circuit, the middle moat about 20 m; Osaka's outer moat reaches ~90 m; tameike reservoirs run ~3-26 ha (~150-600 m across). A field ditch is ~1/300 of the 1-cho (~100 m) paddy it feeds - at true scale a hairline, essentially invisible.

The stroke convention, and what it is not (the GM's ruling of 2026-07-21). Watercourses on these maps are LINEWORK, and the ladder above spans about 250 to 1 in true width - about 1,700 to 1 once a reservoir is counted. That cannot be drawn on one sheet without the finest ditch disappearing: a 1 ft ditch at provincial-city scale is a third of a pixel. So the thin end is drawn true-width-or-floored - the field ditch parks at a minimum-visibility floor rather than at its true hairline - while the honesty anchors on the LARGER features, which draw true: a city moat is drawn at its real 66 ft, the low end of the provincial-seat band in the table above. That floor is a sanctioned minimum visibility and never a size license. What always survives it is the ORDERING and the coarse steps: a hairline ditch at the floor (about 1.5 ft on a hamlet sheet), a pond feeder at roughly twice that, a village creek at roughly three and a half times it, a stream feeding a moat drawn as wide as the moat itself - the water that enters has to be carried - and the city moat heaviest of everything on the sheet. A pond is areal: it is drawn as a polygon at the scale of the paddies beside it and never sits on the line-width ladder at all; what sits on the ladder is its outlet. A map drawing convention, disclosed - and what is held is the ordering and the size of the gaps between tiers rather than any exact width, so the ladder can never collapse back into one undifferentiated blue line.
