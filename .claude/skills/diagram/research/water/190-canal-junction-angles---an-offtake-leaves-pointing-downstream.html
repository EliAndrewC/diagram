Canal junction angles - an offtake leaves pointing downstream

Sources: ignou-silt-control (the angle at which a canal is taken off a river, and the angle at which a distributary leaves a parent canal)

Canal practice: an offtake leaves its parent at an ACUTE angle pointing downstream. A canal taken off at 90 degrees to the main flow is the most objectionable orientation there is, and a diversion angle of 30 to 45 degrees against the river's flow is what is normally recommended, because it curves the flow lines through the regulator instead of shearing across them. That recommendation is for a canal leaving a RIVER; the same manual prescribes 60 to 80 degrees for a distributary leaving a parent CANAL, so a drawn junction has to say which of the two it is. A lower bound below 30 degrees is on no page read for this record.
