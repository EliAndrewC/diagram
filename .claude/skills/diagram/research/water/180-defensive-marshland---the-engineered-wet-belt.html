Defensive marshland - the engineered wet belt

Sources: bitchu-takamatsu-jawiki; the Song Hebei tanglao (塘泺) pond-and-marsh belt is He Chengju's memorial in songshi-273-wikisource

What the research found: the Northern Song built an artificial marsh-and-pond belt across the northern Hebei frontier (He Chengju's lake chain of the late 980s; the year is on no page read) specifically as anti-cavalry terrain; Japanese numajiro "marsh castles" (Bitchu Takamatsu, besieged by water 1582) used surrounding marsh as their primary defense; and flooded paddies around castle towns are read here as a de facto glacis. The constant: peri-urban wetness that survives is PURPOSED - so a defensive belt is military ground, not waste.

The rule the map follows: Town and city tier, where no generator draws the settlement yet; no drawn map carries one. A defensive wet belt is hand-placed OUTSIDE the wall circuit, hugging the perimeter: it begins at the moat's outer bank or the foot of the wall and stays within about 180 ft of the wall or moat line, because a bog detached from the fortification defends nothing, and it may be drawn only on a map that HAS a wall or a moat. It does not follow the valley toe - it follows the circuit wherever the wall runs - so the rule that puts marsh on low ground does not apply to it. An approach road crossing it is a CAUSEWAY and keeps a bare tread, since few and constricted approaches are the belt's whole military purpose. It is unbuildable ground exactly like the valley-toe marsh, sacred and funerary siting included.
