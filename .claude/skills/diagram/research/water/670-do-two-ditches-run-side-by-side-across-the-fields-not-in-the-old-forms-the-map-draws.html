Do two ditches run side by side across the fields? Not in the old forms the map draws

Sources: none - this entry reads the record's own entries on how a field's water was shared out, and records a search that found no figure

The question. In a comb of fields - supply canals along the high edge, delivery ditches running down the slope between strips of plots - may two ditches run down side by side, a stone's throw apart, for a long stretch?

What the record says. The old form waters a paddy from the paddy above it, over the bund, rather than from a ditch of its own, and a ditch beside every plot is a change of the Meiji land readjustment and after - see where a field's water comes from and where the drawn net stops. A delivery ditch serves a strip of plots, and the next one serves the next strip.

What the record does not say. No page read gives how far apart two delivery ditches of the old comb stood. The figures that were found are modern design standards and Meiji schemes, and every one spaces the ditches by the block of plots each serves, tens to hundreds of meters apart; none describes two ditches running side by side for a long stretch.

What this means for the map. A delivery that would run down beside another watercourse - within about 12 to 32 ft of it, running the same way, for more than 60 ft past where it leaves - is not drawn: its plots take their water over the bund from the plot above, which is the old form the record gives. A field's drain that would run beside the brook in that way joins the brook instead. The band and the run are a guess (no page gives one): the band is the spacing measured between side-by-side pairs on drawn maps, the run a sixth of the shortest delivery.
