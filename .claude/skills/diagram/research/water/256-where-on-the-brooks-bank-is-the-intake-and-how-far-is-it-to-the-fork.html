Where on the brook's bank is the intake, and how far is it to the fork?

Sources: shimane-toshuko-lecture (one-bank intake; where the current touches the bank; the intake court), fao-irrigation-manual-m7 (the offtake's reach and angle), maff-toshuko-history (the bend; where the river issues from the mountains), maff-toshuko-sand-basin (the settling basin at the canal's start), tabayashi-1987 (short canals; Toro)

The question. The ditch begins at an intake on the brook's bank (above). Which bank, at what angle does the head race leave it, and how far does the head race run before it forks?

Which bank, and at what angle. The intake is on one bank, the side of the land it serves - the rule on torrent and steep rivers, and the principle on gentle ones - at a point where the current's deep line touches that bank, beside the sand sluice. The offtake should stand in a straight reach of the river; failing that, on the outside of a bend, silt tending to settle on the inside; it may leave the river at an angle when the water is clean, and, when it is silt-laden, at a right angle or with a scour sluice - the same page noting that the outside of a bend erodes and may need its bank protected. A Japanese torrent-river intake sited just downstream of the middle of a bend's concave bank took in little sediment. A mountain brook feeding a hamlet fan is drawn with the clean-water offtake: the head race leaves the bank at an acute angle pointing downstream, the angle the record already gives for an offtake leaving its parent (above, where that angle is itself a guess: no source read gives a figure for it). A 1902 treatise placed the head works "where the river issues from the mountains", which is where a hamlet's brook meets the head of its fan.

How far from the intake to the fork. No source read gives a distance. In modern practice what stands in that first stretch is a settling basin, "immediately following the intake mouth, at the starting point of the canal", reached through an intake court that narrows the flow smoothly from the mouth into the canal. Whether a village dug one is not recorded, and the maps draw none. Tabayashi says only that the small canals run "for short distances"; the third-century Toro fields, 7.5 hectares, were watered by a canal "more than 370 meters long". So the length of the head race is not a number the record holds. On the maps it follows from the geometry: the head race runs from the intake on the brook's bank to the division point at the fan's head, and its length is whatever that distance is, the intake's distance upslope of the fork chosen afresh for each map within the "short distances" the record allows - a guess as to the number, derived as to the shape.

What the map draws. At the fan's head, on the fan's bank, is the intake, and the head race leaves it at an acute angle pointing downstream and runs to the division point.
