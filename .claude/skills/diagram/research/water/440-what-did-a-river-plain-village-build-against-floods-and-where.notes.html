• ishizue-waju - 「The farmers had long made their villages and fields on the slightly raised ground such as natural levees.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農民たちは古くから自然堤防などの微高地に集落と耕地をつくってきた。」)
  • waju-jawiki - 「was what was called a shirinashi-zutsumi, having no levee on the downstream side」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「下流側に堤防を持たない「尻無堤（しりなしづつみ）」と呼ばれるものであった」) 「entering the 17th century, so-kakemawashi levees, enclosing existing villages with strong levees over a wide area, came to be built」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「17世紀に入ると既存の集落を広い範囲の丈夫な堤防で囲う総懸廻堤が築かれるようになる」) 「it is only from the Meiji era on that flood control by continuous levees, as today, became general in Japan; before the Edo period the main method of flood control was to build levees as needed to protect villages and fields」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「日本において現在のように連続堤による治水が一般的となるのは明治時代以降のことであり、江戸時代以前は集落や耕地を守るために必要に応じて堤防で築く治水方法が中心であった」)
  • waju-jawiki - 「levees enclosing villages and fields also survive in the basins of the Shinano, Arakawa, Tone, Yodo and other rivers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「集落や耕地を囲んだ堤防は信濃川・荒川・利根川・淀川などの流域にも現存し」) 「the settlements are concentrated on the highest levee within the waju, and the saying "if the levee breaks, flee to the levee" has also been handed down」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「集落は輪中内で最も高い堤防の上に集中しており、「堤防が切れたら、堤防に逃げろ」という言葉も伝えられてきた」) (the page gives the delta's exposure to storm surge as the reason)
  • ishizue-waju - 「setting an intake in a waju levee was often the cause of a breach, so the waju, on the contrary, had difficulty drawing water, and the use of groundwater by bored wells developed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「輪中堤に取入口を設置することは破堤の原因になることが多く、むしろ輪中は引水に困難であり、掘抜井戸による地下水の利用が発達した。」)
  • maff-waju-mizuya - 「So people devised raising their house plots with fill. Since even this could not prevent it fully, they further piled fill higher than the house plot within the grounds, built a building (the mizuya) on it, and prepared for floods.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「人々は盛土をして宅地を高くする工夫をしました。このようにしても防ぎきれないので、さらに屋敷内で宅地より高く盛り土をして、その上に建物（水屋）を建てて水害に備えました。」) 「In the past every mizuya was used as a storehouse. Mizuya range from hut-like ones to ones like reserve dwellings」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「かつてはどの水屋も貯蔵庫として使われていた。水屋には小屋のようなものから予備住宅的なものまであり」)
  • waju-jawiki - 「a south-facing main house was built on a stone base (plinth) to prevent flooding when the water rose, with the mizuya to the northwest of the main house」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「出水時の浸水を防ぐ石垣（基壇）の上に南向きの母屋が建てられ、母屋の北西側に水屋」) 「the mizuya was set on a stone base one step higher than the main house」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水屋（みずや）は母屋よりも一段高い石垣の上に設けられた」) 「since having a mizuya was limited to fairly wealthy houses, raised earth platforms called jomeidan or inochizuka also existed as facilities for those who could not have one」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水屋を持つのはかなり裕福な家に限られるため、水屋を持てない人々のための施設として助命壇（じょめいだん）あるいは命塚（いのちづか）と呼ばれる土盛りをした高台も存在していた」) 「the jomeidan were made jointly by the people of the area, but sometimes landlords and the like built them at their own expense, and a shrine on ground piled higher than its surroundings sometimes served as a jomeidan」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「助命壇は地域の人々が共同で作ったが、地主などが私財を投じて作った場合もあり、周辺よりも土地が高く盛られた神社が助命壇の役割を果たすこともあった」)
  • mizuka-jawiki - 「a storehouse or other building set on fill piled some tens of centimeters to 3 meters higher than the main house, or that fill itself.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「母屋よりも数十センチメートルないし3メートルほど高く盛土を施した上に設けられた倉などの建物、あるいはその盛土をいう。」) 「in the Kiso three rivers "mizuya", in the middle Yodo "dangura", in the middle Shinano "mizukura"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木曽三川では「水屋」、淀川中流部では「段蔵（段倉）」、信濃川中流部では「水倉」」) 「on the ground floor food such as rice bales, miso barrels and soy sauce; on the upper floor household goods such as bedding, low tables, and chests and wicker trunks holding clothing」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「1階には米俵や味噌樽、醤油などの食料、2階には布団やちゃぶ台、衣類を入れた長持や行李などの生活道具」)
  • goka-mizuka - 「Until farm machinery spread, nearly every house kept horses or cattle for farm work, so in a flood the livestock had to be evacuated too, and some houses set a horse-tethering stone beside the mizuka. Some houses also hung under the eaves a small boat for use in evacuating and carrying things.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農業用の機械が普及するまでは、ほとんどが家で農耕用に馬や牛を飼っていたので、水害の際には家畜も避難させる必要があり、水塚の脇には馬つなぎ石を設けていた家もありました。また、避難や物を運ぶ際に使うための小舟を軒下に吊るしておいた家もありました。」)
  • cssn-jiangnan-weitian - 「In the Five Dynasties and the two Song, the Jiangnan region centered on the Taihu plain gradually formed the tangpu polder system, uniting the dredging of channels, the building of dikes, the building of sluices, water transport and so on in the construction of farmland water works, and the polder pattern of "a transverse channel every five or seven li" and "a longitudinal channel every seven or ten li" appeared」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「五代和两宋时期，以太湖平原为中心的江南地区逐渐形成塘浦圩田体系，把浚河、筑堤、建闸、水上交通等统一于农田水利建设中，出现了“五里七里一横塘”“七里十里一纵浦”的圩田格局」) 「Through the Song, in the Jiangdong region linked polders appeared as a new form of reclamation. They joined several polders by building dikes」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「有宋一代，江东地区出现了联圩这一新的围垦形式。它通过筑堤连接多个圩田」)
