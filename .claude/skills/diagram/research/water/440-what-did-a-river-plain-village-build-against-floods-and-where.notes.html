• ishizue-waju - 「In the southwest of the Nobi plain, the three Kiso rivers (the Kiso, the Nagara and the Ibi) converge on low wetland; drainage was poor and the area often suffered flood damage.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「The farmers had long made their villages and fields on the slightly raised ground such as natural levees.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • waju-jawiki - 「the early levees were levees on the upstream side only, aimed mainly at avoiding the direct blow of the muddy flood, and were what was called a shirinashi-zutsumi, having no levee on the downstream side」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「entering the 17th century, so-kakemawashi levees, enclosing existing villages with strong levees over a wide area, came to be built」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「it is only from the Meiji era on that flood control by continuous levees, as today, became general in Japan; up to and through the Edo period the main method of flood control was to build levees as needed to protect villages and fields」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • waju-jawiki - 「levees enclosing villages and fields also survive in the basins of the Shinano, Arakawa, Tone, Yodo and other rivers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「in the delta region, because of storm surges among other things, natural levees are little developed」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「because this area is exposed to storm surges, the settlements are concentrated on the highest levee within the waju, and the saying "if the levee breaks, flee to the levee" has also been handed down」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • ishizue-waju - 「A waju generally has an intake set at the head of the ring and a drain set at its tail.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「setting an intake in a waju levee was often the cause of a breach, so the waju, on the contrary, had difficulty drawing water, and the use of groundwater by bored wells developed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • maff-waju-mizuya - 「So people devised raising their house plots with fill. Since even this could not prevent it fully, they further piled fill higher than the house plot within the grounds, built a building (the mizuya) on it, and prepared for floods.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「In the past every mizuya was used as a storehouse. Mizuya range from hut-like ones to ones like reserve dwellings」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • waju-jawiki - 「a south-facing main house was built on a stone base (plinth) to prevent flooding when the water rose, with the mizuya to the northwest of the main house」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「the mizuya was set on a stone base one step higher than the main house」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「since having a mizuya was limited to fairly wealthy houses, raised earth platforms called jomeidan or inochizuka also existed as facilities for those who could not have one」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「the jomeidan were made jointly by the people of the area, but sometimes landlords and the like built them at their own expense, and a shrine on ground piled higher than its surroundings sometimes served as a jomeidan」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • mizuka-jawiki - 「those mainly in the Arakawa basin and the Tone basin are called "mizuka".」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「a storehouse or other building set on fill piled some tens of centimeters to 3 meters higher than the main house, or that fill itself.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「in the Kiso three rivers "mizuya", in the middle Yodo "dangura", in the middle Shinano "mizukura"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) 「on the ground floor food such as rice bales, miso barrels and soy sauce; on the upper floor household goods such as bedding, low tables, and chests and wicker trunks holding clothing」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • goka-mizuka - 「Until farm machinery spread, nearly every house kept horses or cattle for farm work, so in a flood the livestock had to be evacuated too, and some houses set a horse-tethering stone beside the mizuka. Some houses also hung under the eaves a small boat for use in evacuating and carrying things.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • cssn-jiangnan-weitian - 「In the Five Dynasties and the two Song, the Jiangnan region centered on the Taihu plain gradually formed the tangpu polder system, uniting the dredging of channels, the building of dikes, the building of sluices, water transport and so on in the construction of farmland water works, and the polder pattern of "a transverse channel every five or seven li" and "a longitudinal channel every seven or ten li" appeared」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) 「Through the Song, in the Jiangdong region linked polders appeared as a new form of reclamation. They joined several polders by building dikes」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
