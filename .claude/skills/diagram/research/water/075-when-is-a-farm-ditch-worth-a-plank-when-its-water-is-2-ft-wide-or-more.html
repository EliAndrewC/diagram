When is a farm ditch worth a plank? When its water is 2 ft wide or more

Sources: none - no page read says at what width a farm ditch was bridged (the searches are in the notes), so the line is the GM's ruling; drawing the net at true size, which put the question, is at How our maps draw channel widths

A plank is laid only over water you cannot stride across. A plank bridge is a real object a household builds and maintains, and nobody builds one over something they step over. Whether that crossing was a plank at all, or a log or earth over logs, is below.

The width line for a plank (ruled below) is judged on the ditch's WIDEST point (a run wide at its head and a thread at its tail is worth a crossing somewhere, and the plank is seated where the water is wide enough for one).

RULED 2026-08-26 (GM): the line is 2.0 ft. The GM asked of the reference hamlet: "I think there should probably be plank bridges over the irrigated channels in a few places. Right? Or are these too narrow for that? They don't look too narrow. Is it intentional that they're missing or an oversight?" The research pass (web, Japanese and English, six queries): the traditional farm-level water channel may have been about 2 shaku, ~60 cm (farm paths 3 shaku, the sanshaku-michi), but that is a GUESS: no page that could be read says so. If it holds, it is about the width at the head of our delivery ditches (2.5 ft, tapering toward 1.2 ft, which the map draws no finer than 1.5). What modern ほ場整備 standard drawings give for the smallest field ditches and their crossings was not found on a page that could be read; and NOTHING found says whether an Edo farmer laid a board over a 2-shaku ditch or stepped it. The GM had already ruled on the map. So the line is 2.0 ft: the standard farm ditch earns a board, the ~1.2-1.5 ft tails do not. On Inashiro: 10 planks, among them one on each delivery branch wide enough for one (each over 2.0 ft of water or a little more, seated just below the branch heads because the head itself is a confluence) and two more down the main (2.8 / 2.4 ft); the two 1.8 ft branches stay unplanked. The three branch planks sit at the TAIL of each branch's qualifying run, 160-250 ft below the head, each on a bund crossing mid-field - accepted, since a head seat would stand ~120 ft from the canal plank at the junction. One parallel pair has one branch planked and its twin 12 ft away not, the price of two deliveries running 5-25 ft apart for 300 ft. And at 2.7 ft of drawn width against 3.0-3.7, the unplanked 1.8 ft branches are indistinguishable from the planked ones on the page: the map shows some branches with a crossing and some without.
