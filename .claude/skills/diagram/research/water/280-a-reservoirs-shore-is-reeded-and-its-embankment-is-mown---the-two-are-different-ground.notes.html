  • mineta-2007-tameike - 「The diversity of aquatic plants seen in tameike is on a downward trend overall, with the decline of floating-leaf and submerged plant communities reported in various places in recent years (Hamashima 2003). Among the causes of the decline that have been named are the abandonment of use and of management of tameike, through the increase in abandoned cultivation, changes in irrigation systems and the like. In particular, relatively small-scale tameike often support endangered species - various floating-leaf and submerged plants whose numbers are in marked nationwide decline, such as subuta (スブタ, Blyxa) and mizu-oobako (ミズオオバコ, Ottelia) - yet it is precisely in such small ponds that the abandonment of management and a fall in management frequency, such as the cessation of water use and reduced mowing of the embankment body, are advancing (Mineta 2006).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )

  • mineta-2007-tameike - 「The aquatic plants growing in a tameike can be divided, according to water depth and slope, into large emergent plants such as yoshi (common reed) and gama (cattail); small emergent plants such as tachimo (Ussuri water milfoil) and harii (spikerush), which become submerged plants depending on the growing season and the setting; submerged plants such as itomo (Potamogeton pondweed) and subuta (Blyxa); floating-leaf plants such as hishi (water chestnut) and asaza (fringed water lily); and free-floating plants such as ukikusa (duckweed) and sanshoumo (water fern) (Hamashima 2001).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
