Why is a reservoir's embankment bare of reeds? It is mown and burned to keep it strong

The question: where a reservoir's reeds stop, what is the bank beyond them? The shore itself is reeded and wet, which is its own question, A reservoir's shore is reeded, and its EMBANKMENT is mown. The embankment is not the shore. The EMBANKMENT itself is a different piece of ground and is kept clear: 「the embankment is managed to keep its strength - among other things, cultivation on it is forbidden and mowing and burning-off are carried out to hold back the growth of grass and trees」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「堤は耕作を禁じたり、草刈りや野焼き を行ったりして草木の生育を阻む等、強度を維持するための管理が行われる」) - to keep the bank strong - and the plants named as having grown well in such places are herbs (ワレモコウ, キキョウ, リンドウ, オミナエシ - great burnet, balloon flower, gentian, golden lace - and many of the seven autumn grasses, the page adds, are seen in such places), not wetland ones. The dam's water-contact slope is armored outright against wave erosion - "riprap, stone pitching, concrete-block facing" (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「捨石、石張り、コンクリートブロック張」). This is an INDEPENDENT confirmation of the rule the GM's own complaint produced on 2026-08-28 ("the hazy blue that denotes the marsh is clearly overlaid on top of the greenery of the earthen mounds"): reeds keep off a raised, maintained, planted bank and root in the shallow water outside it. We drew that rule from the GM's eye; the record says the same thing, and gives the reason - the bank is mown to keep it strong.

The rule this produces:

  1. Reeds and haze keep OFF the embankment and off a pond's raised bank - because it is mown and burned to keep it strong, which is also why nothing is cultivated on it.
  2. This is not a map drawing convention. It is what the record describes.

Sources: tameike-jawiki, kagawa-tameike-structure.
