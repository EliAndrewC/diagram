No toe marsh at town/city scale - the drainage-investment gradient

Sources: yato-jawiki, shitsuden-kotobank, hibiya-irie-jawiki, fukada-kotobank (fukada, 深田, defined), kaya-jawiki, yoshi-jawiki, kotobank-shimogoe, shinden-kaihatsu-jawiki, yusuichi-jawiki, suzhou-enwiki (the old city's network of rivers and canals)

What the research found: the real pattern is a gradient of drainage investment and land value - the gradient itself is this page's reconstruction from the attested pieces below. In that reading, VILLAGES had neither the capital nor the incentive to eliminate every wet margin: badly drained paddy on village land, still being converted to dry paddy by state drainage works from Meiji onward, is well attested (Japanese yato valley bottoms were easy rice land wherever drainage could be secured, and often marshy where it could not; villages cut grass and reed for thatch and screens and burned their grass ground each year; that the reed came from wet margins held as village commons under iriai tenure is this page's inference; poorly drained fukada "deep fields" persisted until Meiji-and-later drainage projects), and crucially, in this page's reading, it was USED wet ground - reed bed, wet meadow, more paddy at the reclamation frontier - which is exactly what our sparse harvestable-reed rendering reads as. TOWNS/CITIES, in the same reading, commanded corvee-scale hydraulic engineering and premium peri-urban land: ditch discharge went into an engineered moat/canal/river network (Suzhou's network of canals; the infill of Edo's Hibiya inlet from 1592), and the outskirts were intensively worked (suburban vegetable belts fed by urban night soil; merchant-led new-field reclamation around Osaka, on the old bed of the rerouted Yamato River and the shallows of Osaka Bay, at the same time as the Yodo river flood works; and, in this page's reading, the same around Edo). Where wetness DID persist near a city it was, in this page's reading, bounded and purposed - a flood basin and, in this page's reading, a moat, a lotus/fish pond or a deliberate defensive inundation - never a diffuse seepage marsh below a ditch. What a town's dry margin carries instead - vegetable plots nearest the houses, and beyond the fields a cut-over wood or a cut grass ground - is Did a town's edge grow vegetables for the town, fed by its night soil? and Why is a town's edge wooded or grassed rather than bare, and with what?.
