What drawing at TRUE SIZE left open

Sources: the true-size decision above, of which these are consequences; jsslkx-002-2021 (the minimum bank top of a lateral or farm canal)

Drawing the net at true size leaves several neighboring rules to be re-set against it. Each of these is measured.

  • The dry-hem stand-off is DERIVED from the canal's bank. It is the stroke's LOCAL half-width at each hem boundary point plus a 5.0 ft berm: a ~1 m embankment top, which is the minimum bank top a Jiangsu design guideline sets for a lateral or farm canal,49 plus room to stand for the annual dredging.175 The guideline declares its own canal designs subject to GB 50288, China's national irrigation and drainage standard, which is where the number comes from; GB 50288 itself is on no page this project has been able to read, so the attribution is second-hand. The dredging half of the berm is this record's own reason - the guideline's stated reason for the bank top is vehicle passage where it doubles as a road, and it gives none where it does not. Measured: bare berm 4.89 ft median, held unbroken over a 1,592 ft chain, with the residue at the fork recorded below.

What remains, measured and ACCEPTED. Three hem corners at the bunsuiguchi keep less than the full berm - 2.00 ft, 2.19 and 2.90 - against a 4.89 ft median held over the whole 1,592 ft chain. Geometric rather than a bug: the stand-off runs along ONE canal's normal and cannot clear a stroke crossing at an angle, and a fork is where three strokes meet. Invisible at fit zoom, and accepted.

The taper is SUB-PERCEPTUAL at true scale - RULED ON, not merely noticed. The GM looked at the finished map and asked the right question: "Is that getting narrower as it goes down into the fields, or along the edge at the top, though? It doesn't visually look like it is." It is not. What matters here is the GRADIENT, not the ratio, and the two had been quietly conflated:

stroke run width ft lost per 100 ft
canal A (the top edge) 1,504 ft 4.5 -> 1.5 0.20
delivery ditch (down into the fields) ~500 ft 2.5 -> 1.5 0.20
canal B (short west arm) 343 ft 4.0 -> 1.5 0.73
a delivery drawn ~3x oversize 540 ft 8.0 -> 3.0 0.93

So a delivery sheds ONE FOOT of width over five hundred, and canal A three over fifteen hundred. The ink is monotone and the head-to-tail ratio is honest, so the two ENDS of a canal differ plainly side by side - but there is no gradient to follow along a stroke, and canal B is the only one that reads, because it is short. Canal A's gradient is identical to a delivery's; only its total drop is larger.

Why this is the honest outcome rather than a defect. A real ditch narrowing from ~1.4 m to ~0.45 m over 460 m is not visually dramatic from above either.170 A taper the eye can follow comes from inflating the net, not from the taper law - so true absolute size and a visible taper are in genuine tension and no amount of engineering dissolves it.

The decision, with the alternatives priced. Both were put to the GM against keeping true size: x1.5 (delivery 3.75 -> 1.8, gradient 0.39) and x2 (5.0 -> 2.4, gradient 0.52), each a single documented multiplier preserving every relative size. The GM chose true size. Recorded here so a future reader meets a deliberate trade rather than an apparent bug.

A plank is laid only over water you cannot stride across. A plank bridge is a real object a household builds and maintains, and nobody builds one over something they step over.169

The line is judged on the ditch's WIDEST point (a run wide at its head and a thread at its tail is worth a crossing somewhere, and the plank is seated where the water is wide enough for one).

RULED 2026-08-26 (GM): the line is 2.0 ft. The GM asked of the reference hamlet: "I think there should probably be plank bridges over the irrigated channels in a few places. Right? Or are these too narrow for that? They don't look too narrow. Is it intentional that they're missing or an oversight?" The research pass (web, Japanese and English, six queries): the traditional farm-level water channel is 2 shaku, ~60 cm (farm paths 3 shaku, the sanshaku-michi), which is precisely the width of our delivery ditches (2.1685 -> 1.2 ft); modern ほ場整備 standard drawings size the smallest field ditches at 30-60 cm and carry a crossing at every plot entrance, but that is machinery-era practice;167 and NOTHING found says whether an Edo farmer laid a board over a 2-shaku ditch or stepped it. The GM had already ruled on the map. So the line is 2.0 ft: the standard farm ditch earns a board, the ~1.2-1.5 ft tails do not. On Inashiro: 10 planks - one per delivery branch (2.0 / 2.0 / 1.95 ft of water, seated just below the branch heads because the head itself is a confluence) and two more down the main (2.8 / 2.4 ft); the two 1.8 ft branches stay unplanked. The three branch planks sit at the TAIL of each branch's qualifying run, 160-250 ft below the head, each on a bund crossing mid-field - accepted, since a head seat would stand ~120 ft from the canal plank at the junction. The parallel pair at x~2225/2240 has one branch planked and its twin 12 ft away not, the price of two deliveries running 5-25 ft apart for 300 ft. And at 2.7 ft of drawn width against 3.0-3.7, the unplanked 1.8 ft branches are indistinguishable from the planked ones on the page: the map shows some branches with a crossing and some without.

ACCEPTED (not fixed) 2026-08-17: the visibility floor lands exactly on the paddy bund's stroke width. The channel floor is 1.5 ft at hamlet scale and the bund is also 1.5, so the finest water tier is drawn at precisely the width of the field boundaries it runs among, separated only by hue. A floor of 1.2 ft was tried and rejected: the floor is not cosmetic - it also sets the clearance the bunds keep from a supply channel, so lowering it moves every bund that hems a supply stroke and re-welds the plot seams. A floor of 1.2 ft broke the paddy seams of two of the twenty-four maps rolled for it that hold at 1.5. A cosmetic ambiguity does not buy two broken maps.

The bund width is 1.5 ft by its own research (a plain aze ran ~1-2 ft),50 so there is room to take the BUND to ~1.3 and leave the water alone - which changes a stroke width and touches no clearance at all.
