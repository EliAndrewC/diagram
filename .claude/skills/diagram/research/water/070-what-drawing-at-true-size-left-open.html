What drawing at TRUE SIZE left open

Sources: the true-size decision above, of which these are consequences; the bank's evidence is at Is the bank left beside a supply canal only a modern rule?

Drawing the net at true size leaves several neighboring rules to be re-set against it. Each of these is measured. The width at which a ditch drawn at true size earns a plank is its own question, When is a farm ditch worth a plank?

  • The dry-hem stand-off is DERIVED from the canal's bank. It is the stroke's LOCAL half-width at each hem boundary point plus a 5.0 ft berm: about the width of the bank bund an early Yayoi paddy kept along its canal (2.6 to 4.9 ft), a strip of walkable earth that is as old as paddy farming (Is the bank left beside a supply canal only a modern rule?), plus room to stand for the annual dredging. The dredging half of the berm is this record's own reason. Measured: bare berm 4.89 ft median, held unbroken over a 1,592 ft chain, with the residue at the fork recorded below.

What remains, measured and ACCEPTED. Three hem corners at the bunsuiguchi keep less than the full berm - 2.00 ft, 2.19 and 2.90 - against a 4.89 ft median held over the whole 1,592 ft chain. Geometric rather than a bug: the stand-off runs along ONE canal's normal and cannot clear a stroke crossing at an angle, and a fork is where three strokes meet. Invisible at fit zoom, and accepted.

The taper is SUB-PERCEPTUAL at true scale - RULED ON, not merely noticed. The GM looked at the finished map and asked the right question: "Is that getting narrower as it goes down into the fields, or along the edge at the top, though? It doesn't visually look like it is." It is not. What matters here is the GRADIENT, not the ratio:

stroke run width ft lost per 100 ft
canal A (the top edge) 1,504 ft 4.5 -> 1.5 0.20
delivery ditch (down into the fields) ~500 ft 2.5 -> 1.5 0.20
canal B (short west arm) 343 ft 4.0 -> 1.5 0.73
a delivery drawn ~3x oversize 540 ft 8.0 -> 3.0 0.93

So a delivery sheds ONE FOOT of width over five hundred, and canal A three over fifteen hundred. The ink narrows steadily and the head-to-tail ratio is honest, so the two ENDS of a canal differ plainly side by side - but there is no gradient to follow along a stroke, and canal B is the only one that reads, because it is short. Canal A's gradient is identical to a delivery's; only its total drop is larger.

Why this is the honest outcome rather than a defect. A real ditch narrowing from ~1.4 m to ~0.45 m over 460 m (canal A's own drawn dimensions, in meters) is not visually dramatic from above either. A taper the eye can follow comes from inflating the net, not from the taper law - so true absolute size and a visible taper are in genuine tension and no amount of engineering dissolves it.

The decision, with the alternatives priced. Both were put to the GM against keeping true size: x1.5 (delivery 3.75 -> 1.8, gradient 0.39, the tail scaled from its true 1.2 ft rather than the 1.5 ft floor it is drawn at) and x2 (5.0 -> 2.4, gradient 0.52), each a single documented multiplier preserving every relative size. The GM chose true size. Recorded here so a future reader meets a deliberate trade rather than an apparent bug.

ACCEPTED (not fixed) 2026-08-17: the visibility floor lands exactly on the paddy bund's stroke width. The channel floor is 1.5 ft at hamlet scale and the bund is also 1.5, so the finest water tier is drawn at precisely the width of the field boundaries it runs among, separated only by hue. A floor of 1.2 ft was tried and rejected: the floor is not cosmetic - it also sets the clearance the bunds keep from a supply channel, so lowering it moves every bund that hems a supply stroke and reshapes the plot seams. A floor of 1.2 ft broke the paddy seams of two of the twenty-four maps rolled for it that hold at 1.5. A cosmetic ambiguity does not buy two broken maps.

The bund width is 1.5 ft by its own research (an early Yayoi paddy's small bunds were about 0.7 to 2 ft, and an Edo survey reckoned a bund at 1 ft; see Is the bank left beside a supply canal only a modern rule?), so there is room to take the BUND to ~1.3 and leave the water alone - which changes a stroke width and touches no clearance at all.
