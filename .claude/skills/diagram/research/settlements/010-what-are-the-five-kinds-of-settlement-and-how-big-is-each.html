What are the five kinds of settlement, and how big is each?

Sources: l7r-median-domain (the domain's settlement ladder and the household of five); the setting's own budget notes (the caste tables each tier is sized from). This entry rests on the setting's canon rather than on historical work, and is stated as canon

The maps draw five sizes of place, and the differences are not only of scale - each tier keeps institutions the one below it does not.

  • A hamlet is 10 to 20 households, 50 to 100 inhabitants, usually working one to three small fields. Below ten the place is an outlying farmstead or two; above twenty it is a small village and starts growing the things a hamlet must not have. A hamlet has no headman of its own - it falls under the headman of the village district it belongs to - no shrine, and no tax-free plot.
  • A village is 40 to 100 households, 200 to 500 inhabitants, around 350 in the ordinary case, and it does have a headman, a shrine and its two or three tax-free plots.
  • A town is a county seat of about 1,200 inhabitants in roughly 238 households: about 156 of them farm, and the other 82 are about 24 merchant, 29 laborer, 13 servant, 12 burakumin and 4 samurai households. A town is the first tier that is not peasant-only, and the state is present in it as the county magistrate in a walled manor rather than as a peasant headman.
  • A provincial city is a provincial governor's seat of about 3,000 inside the walls in about 600 households - and, structurally, no farmers at all (below).
  • A domain capital is the daimyo's castle-town seat, about 12,360 inhabitants, which inherits the whole provincial-city tier and overrides it.

The household itself is about five, over two or three generations under one roof, which is where every population figure on these maps comes from.

Villages and hamlets are peasant-only: no samurai lives in one. The samurai of a county live in its town, and the country estates of a city's senior houses stand outside that city's walls.

The rule the map follows: a hamlet draws 10 to 20 households and no headman's house, no shrine and no tax-free plot; a village draws 40 to 100 households with a headman's house (the largest on the sheet), a shrine and two or three tax-free plots; a town draws its 82 non-farm households at their documented counts - 24 merchant, 29 laborer, 13 servant, 12 burakumin, 4 samurai - a magistrate's walled manor, and as much of its ~156-household farming cohort as the sheet holds; a city draws about 600 urban households and no farmers; a capital draws about 12,360 inhabitants' worth. No samurai house stands in a hamlet or a village.
