What does the page beside a map say about its size?

Sources: the GM's rulings of 2026-08-29; l7r-median-domain (towns, villages and hamlets as separate lines of the domain, and what a town's own figure counts); the setting's own budget notes (the town's household breakdown)

A hamlet and a village state their farmhouse count, exactly, with no tilde. The GM, 2026-08-29: "You should not use a ~ for the number of farmhouses, because that actually IS an exact map feature - the number of farmhouses listed should be whatever is actually displayed on the map itself for hamlets and villages." Every household is drawn, so the count is a statement about the sheet in front of the reader, who could sit and count them. The population that follows it is an inference from that count - five to a household - and carries the tilde.

A town and a city state their non-farm dwelling count instead, also exact, and no farmhouse count at all. The GM's reason is that both "are surrounded by significant farm fields and farmer populations, which are deliberately not all rendered on the map. Therefore, that number should not be included." Their populations are then explained rather than left bare, because the two figures mean different things and a reader would otherwise take them for the same. A city's excludes farmers entirely: its figure is urban dwellings times five, and the farms on its sheet belong to village districts and counties that are counted separately. A town's is the town's own roughly 238 households, some 156 of which farm the fields around it - so the figure is about 1,200 while the sheet draws every one of the 82 non-farm dwellings and only a sample of the farmhouses.

The 1,200 is the town's own, not its county's. The breakdown adds up on its own - 156 farming plus 24 merchant, 29 laborer, 13 servant, 12 burakumin and 4 samurai households - one town map draws exactly those 82 non-farm dwellings, and the domain's own tables list towns, villages and hamlets as separate lines summing to the domain. Reading a town's figure as taking in the farmers of its county's village districts would therefore double-count about 172,000 inhabitants across the domain. The GM, 2026-08-29: "The default population of a town is already 1,200 So you can make that the population for all of the towns which we have already generated and document that. Though, again, that is the total population including farmers, which means that the sum total of that population is not visible on the map, which is intentional."

It is therefore the tier's figure rather than the sheet's, and that is a disclosed departure from what the rest of a map's card does: everywhere else the card describes the drawing, and here it describes the place the drawing is a sample of.

The rule the map follows: a hamlet's or a village's card states the exact number of farmhouses drawn, untilded, and a population of about five per household. A town's or a city's card states the exact number of non-farm dwellings drawn and no farmhouse count; a town states the tier's own ~1,200 and says that it includes farmers who are not all drawn, and a city states a figure that excludes farmers altogether. A card whose drawn houses and declared households differ names both.
