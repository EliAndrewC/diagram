Is every household in a hamlet actually drawn?

Sources: the GM's own wording of the village floor, "~70 households ... at least 50 houses"; this project's measurement of the seventeen shipped settlement maps

Essentially yes, at hamlet and village scale. These are to-scale maps, so a declared household is expected to have a roof on the sheet: the placer is held to between 0.85 and 1.05 occupied farmhouses for every household declared, and a hamlet that declares fifteen and seats twelve is a failure to be fixed rather than a map to be shipped. Measured across the shipped maps, sixteen of seventeen sit at exactly 1.00 - one house per household - and the seventeenth at 0.94.

The GM's figure is a floor, not a ratio: about seventy households want at least fifty houses drawn, which is a minimum on what a sheet may show, not a statement that three households in ten share a roof with another. Extended families genuinely do share a roof - that is why a household averages five over two or three generations - but the sharing is inside the household, not between two of them. Abandoned houses do not count toward the households: they are former homes.

Where the two numbers do differ, the map's own card says so rather than leaving a reader to divide: one sheet drawing 66 farmhouses for 70 households names both, because "66 farmhouses, population ~350" otherwise invites the reader to divide and get 5.3 to a house.

The rule the map follows: a hamlet or village map seats between 0.85 and 1.05 occupied farmhouses per declared household, and aims at one apiece. When the placer runs short it widens the ground it draws candidate seats from rather than re-rolling the map, so the houses already seated stay where they are. Abandoned houses are not counted against the household total, and a map whose drawn houses and declared households differ states both.
