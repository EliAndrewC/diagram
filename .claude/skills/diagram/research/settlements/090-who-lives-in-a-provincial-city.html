Who lives in a provincial city?

Sources: l7r-budgets, the setting's own budget notes (the city caste tables, which list no farmers at all, and the samurai a provincial city houses); l7r-median-domain (the ranks of the provincial government); kotobank-jokamachi (townsmen who farmed beside their trade)

By the setting's own budget tables, a provincial city holds between 2,000 and 4,000 inhabitants inside its walls - about 3,000 inhabitants in 600 households in the ordinary case, and the mix is roughly 40% laborer, 20% servant, 25% merchant, 10% samurai and 5% burakumin households - about 240 laborer, 120 servant, 150 merchant, 60 samurai and 30 burakumin. There are structurally no farmer households in the count: the farmers who work the city's land live in the surrounding villages and hamlets and are counted in those totals. Where a city has fields inside its wall, the ground is worked by the city's own households beside their other work, as some townsmen of small castle towns farmed alongside their trade, and they stay in their own castes (Who works the fields inside a city's wall?).

The samurai tenth is the provincial government and its households: the governor, the six provincial ministers and their deputies, the clerks under them, and rank-and-file bushi on administrative and military duty - together with a small foreign Imperial sub-station of yoriki who audit the tariff collection there and answer to the Imperial Magistrate at the domain capital rather than to the governor.

The mix matters as much as the total, because a city that meets its population with the wrong castes reads as the wrong kind of place. The failure this guards against is a real one and mechanical: when the households are packed one caste after another, the last caste in the list gets whatever ground is left, which can be nothing. The servants live among the households they serve, so they are placed among them rather than after them.

The rule the map follows: a provincial city places about 600 households at roughly 40% laborer, 20% servant, 25% merchant, 10% samurai and 5% burakumin, each caste within about 30% of its share, and no farmer households at all. Servants are placed among the laborers and merchants they serve. A walled samurai estate is a household like any other, so the estates outside the wall and the merchant estates inside it count toward their castes and the ordinary houses need only make up the remainder.
