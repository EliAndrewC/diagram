How far is it across this map? The scale, tier by tier

Sources: the GM's decision of 2026-07 and this project's 2026-07 audit of the drawn maps' real areas; the anchors that fix the compound scale are on the Mode A scale entry

Each tier is drawn at a fixed number of real feet to the pixel, and the numbers are round on purpose, so that a reader can measure a distance off the sheet in their head: one foot per pixel for a hamlet and a town, two for a village, three for a provincial city and a capital, and a third of a foot for a compound plan, which is the finest of the ladder. The reason for that particular ladder, and the real building complexes that bracket the compound figure, are on the Mode A scale entry linked above.

What the ladder bought was consistency in real feet. The audit that drove it found the dwellings already mutually consistent across every map, but town fields running four to eight times under their real area, and fixed-pixel constants - roads, streets, an inn, a stable yard, a storehouse - silently changing their real size from one tier to the next, simply because they were written in pixels. Every such constant is declared in real feet now and converted per map.

The one thing that is not to scale is the width of a thin line, and that is a map drawing convention. A five-foot alley at three feet to the pixel would be 1.7 pixels wide, which is invisible, so thin linework is floored at four pixels on the sheet. The floor only ever raises a line to legibility; nothing is inflated past it, and anything genuinely wider than the floor is drawn at its true width.

The rule the map follows: a hamlet and a town sheet are drawn at 1 ft per pixel, a village at 2, a provincial city and a domain capital at 3, and a compound plan at 1/3. Every size in the engine is declared in real feet and converted at the map's own scale, so a feature that is thirty feet across is thirty feet across on every tier. Thin linework is floored at 4 pixels of sheet width and is otherwise drawn true.
