Presentation: the map's drawing conventions - labels, captions, framing and cropping

The findings behind the labels, captions, titles and frames on these maps, the decisions they drove, and - where no generator draws the feature yet - the rules a map follows.

Almost everything on this page is a map drawing convention: a choice about how the sheet is drawn so a human eye can read it, with no historical finding behind it. A settlement had no title lettered across its fields and no bar showing a hundred feet; those are ours, and so is every rule about where a caption may sit. Where a decision does rest on something about the world - a pond that feeds a field by gravity has to stand above the field's water entry - the entry says so. Everywhere else the honest label on the decision is "convention", and the entries use that word rather than dressing a drawing choice as a finding.
