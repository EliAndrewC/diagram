Why does a caption sometimes break across two lines?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-08-27

Because wrapping is sometimes the only thing that clears the ground, and the GM ruled that clearing the ground is what decides it: "if the label was split across multiple lines, and that caused it to not overlap with anything, then we should do that. If running the label on one line caused it to not overlap with anything, then we should do that. If both things cause the label to overlap with something, then we can just pick one ... this should be a rule for all labels". So a caption is drawn on one line if that clears the sheet; otherwise on the first of two, then three, lines that does; and if nothing clears, on one line again.

"Clears" is measured on the block as it will actually be drawn - its true rotated outline, turned about its own center exactly as the finished text is - against the true outlines of everything built and every caption already placed. Ground cover and land parcels are not obstacles: the scrub commons is a rectangle the size of the whole drawing, and counting it had every probe reporting blocked ground everywhere.

The cutting itself follows a few plain rules, all of them about how the words look. Words are never broken across lines. Among the ways of cutting a caption into a given number of lines, the one whose longest line is shortest wins, with ties going to the most even lengths. A line holding a single short word - "of", "the" - is refused whenever there are more words than lines, so a shrine named for a Fortune is cut as "Shrine of / Benten" or "Shrine / of Benten" and never leaves "of" stranded on a line of its own. A wrapped caption keeps the one-liner's center and its tilt, and the map records the block rather than the line, so what is checked is what is drawn. One hamlet's notice board is the worked example: "notice / board" at about 57 degrees, off the farmhouse the single line crossed.

The rule the map follows: a caption is drawn on as few lines as clear the ground beneath it, preferring one; words are never broken; a wrapped caption's lines are as even as they can be and never leave a two- or three-letter word alone on a line; and the wrapped block keeps the seat and the angle the single line would have had.
