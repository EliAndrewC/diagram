Why does a caption tilt with the thing it names?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-08-27; this project's measurements of the three drawn cities

So that the words read as part of the thing, the way a level caption reads as part of a level building. The GM stated it as a general rule on 2026-08-27: "the notice board is at an angle, therefore, the notice board label should be at exactly the same angle". A caption takes its subject's own rotation - exactly, not approximately - normalized only so far as is needed to keep the text from reading upside down, and it is seated off the subject's rotated edge rather than off an upright box around it. A road's caption takes its bearing from the stretch of roadway it is seated against; a row of shopfronts takes the axis of the street it fronts, read off the street rather than copied by hand, because a copied number goes stale the next time the row is laid.

A subject at a square rotation - a quarter, a half or three quarters of a turn - has a horizontal edge of its own, so its level caption is aligned with it and stays level.

What does not tilt is everything with no axis to follow: a caption over an upright block of packed houses ("laborers' dwellings", "samurai houses", "barns"), a caption on a point fixture such as a boundary stone, and a city district's caption. The fabric under all three drawn cities' district captions was measured and is level, so tilting them would align them with nothing on the map. Tilt follows the subject's axis; it is not decoration.

One ripple is worth knowing when a caption at the map's edge tilts: the block it occupies is taller than its level form, so a frame taken partly on captions can grow by a few feet - one city's grew about 45 ft off its tanning yard's caption, which stranded an off-map channel anchor that had been written against the older frame.

The rule the map follows: a caption naming a rotated feature is drawn at that feature's own angle and seated off its rotated edge; a caption naming a road or a row laid along one runs along that line at its bearing; a caption naming an area, an upright block or a point fixture is level. The text never reads upside down.
