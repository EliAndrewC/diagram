What else was planted on a pond dike besides mulberry?

Sources: isis-dykepond, dili360-2005-sangji, gd-gazetteer-sangji

What the research found. Of the things a silk-and-fish hamlet has that a rice hamlet does not (the rest are in What stands on a dike-pond hamlet that a paddy hamlet lacks?), the mulberry dike is one planting of several: other dike crops: sugar cane, fruit, banana (蔗基 / 果基 / 蕉基, zheji / guoji / jiaoji). The 1980s survey carried by i-sis: sugar-cane dikes 18% of the district against mulberry 12%; the magazine 中国国家地理 (Chinese National Geography) 2005 on the lost landscape: 蕉林蔗海 (jiaolin zhehai, banana groves and a sea of cane); gd-gazetteer-sangji, the gazetteer office, which frames the types as a SUCCESSION - the mulberry dike giving way, as the delta urbanized and industrialized, to fruit (chiefly banana and citrus), cane and vegetable dikes - rather than as three plantings side by side. Attested alternative FORMS of the dike planting. Each is drawn: a hamlet rolls one crop and plants every dike with it, cane, banana or fruit, and Kuwabata is the mulberry case.

Departures and honesty. The gazetteer office frames the dike-pond types as a SUCCESSION (the mulberry dike 桑基 giving way to fruit, cane and vegetable dikes, 果基、蔗基、菜基, the fruit chiefly banana and citrus, as the delta urbanized and industrialized), not one dike carrying all three - so a hamlet would be of one dike type rather than mixing crops on a dike. By the gazetteer's own account that succession is a modern one: the fruit, cane and vegetable dikes are drawn here as attested forms, but none of them is attested before the mulberry dike gave way. The Pearl-delta reads are 1980s surveys and a summary of Ruddle & Zhong's monograph, which itself could not be read.
