• wuhurec-wanchun - 「It was against this background that in the sixth year of Jiayou (1061), Zhang Yong, fiscal commissioner of Jiangdong, and his assistant Xie Jingwen pressed for the restoration of the Wanchun polder in Wuhu county, and sent Shen Pi, magistrate of Ningguo county, to survey it on the ground.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ), 「It is six zhang broad, one zhang and two chi high, and eighty-four li long.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ), 「Within the polder a through road runs twenty-two li long, meeting the dike on the north; its width takes two carts, and willows are planted along it in rows.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ) and 「Five water gates were made, and in another forty days it was finished.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ) (Shen Kuo's record, as the page reproduces it; a zhang is ten chi)
  • wuhurec-wanchun - 「The southeast of the polder lies on a great marsh, where wind and water drive against it; the dike cannot stay firm for long.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ) and 「This ground has no abrupt rise; what comes on slopes gently for a full hundred paces. A hundred rows of willows are set along the dike, and reeds are planted in rows below them, so that where the water bites is a hundred paces off, and the dike never meets the water. The foot of the dike is several zhang broad and its top tapers to only a few chi, so it does not set itself against the water, and the water's force cannot contend with ours.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ) (the rebuilders' answer, before the work, to the objection that waves would wear the dike away)

  • fukui-kuzuryu-edo-dikes - 「The dikes of the Kuzuryu river and others up to the Edo period were chiefly kasumi dikes and waju dikes, Japan's ancient art of dike building, meant to weaken the force of a flood and let the water spread over the low ground.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ), 「Besides these, in the early Edo period the Shigo dike, 5,700 ken (10.36 km) from Jodoji village to Yamagishi village (now Mikuni town), and a dike of 1,200 ken (2.18 km) from Amasugo (now Fukui city) to Egami (now Fukui city) had also been built.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「The dikes of this time, though they differed from place to place, were for the most part about 3 ken (5.45 m) across the base, 1 ken (1.82 m) across the crown and 2 ken (3.64 m) high.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
