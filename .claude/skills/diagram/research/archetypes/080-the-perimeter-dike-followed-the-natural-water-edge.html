The perimeter dike followed the natural water edge

Sources: sdlib-shunde-jitang (the dike as pond spoil, planted), miragenews-polders (fish-scale polders named for their aerial outlines; organic forms replaced by standardized rectangles after 1949)

What the research found: a wei-tian 圩田 / dike-pond dike was dredged pond-mud heaped and packed (the 挖塘培基 dig-and-pile cycle that also formed the ponds),14 trapezoidal in section, PLANTED with mulberry/willow/crops that bind the soil,15 walked and lived on, and constantly breached-and-repaired16 - so it read as a mottled, vegetated green-brown band of VARYING width, not a uniform stroke. Crucially, the plan form splits: the surveyed interior ditch/paddy grid was genuinely rectilinear, but the OUTER perimeter dike followed the natural water edge (lake / creek / marsh) in gentle curves and non-square bends - the "fish-scale polder" (鱼鳞圩, yulin wei) form named for exactly those irregular overlapping outlines.17 The dead-straight right-angled rectangle is a post-1949 industrial shape (heritage scholars: "the organic forms of polders were replaced by standardised rectangular patterns"),18 so a dead-straight rectangular polder would be the one genuinely anachronistic thing on the map.
