Did villages grow lotus before the modern market? Yes - in China from the sixth century, and in Japan by the late Edo period

Sources: qimin-yaoshu-yangyu (lotus root planted in fish-pond mud), qimin-yaoshu-enwiki (the treatise finished in the sixth century),pwsannong-shuisheng-shucai (deep-water lotus before the Song; Qing paddies turned to seed lotus), leng-2020-sunan (Wu county the Ming-Qing lotus district), kadoma-rekishi (lotus growing active at Kadoma in the late Edo period), alic-iwakuni-renkon (Iwakuni lotus from 1796, spread on reclaimed land), iwakuni-kankou-renkon (the domain's enterprise from 1811)

The deep-water lotus field the maps draw in a village's lowest plots (above) is a premodern form: villages in both countries grew lotus before the modern market, and in China the root lotus grown before the Song was the deep-water kind.

In China the Qimin Yaoshu, finished in the sixth century (in 544 by one account), already gives a method for planting lotus root in the mud of a fish pond. The root lotus grown before the Song dynasty was essentially all deep-water lotus, shallow-water lotus first appearing in Song texts. In the Ming and Qing, Wu county was the main lotus-root district among the water-vegetable districts around Suzhou's lakes. And in the Qing the counties of Hunan mostly grew seed lotus (the kind grown for its seeds rather than its root) in paddy fields, farmers from the Daoguang reign (1821-50) on abandoning rice fields for it one after another because lotus seed was dear.

In Japan, on the low, water-rich ground of Kadoma near Osaka, lotus-root growing became active in the late Edo period, one cash crop beside rapeseed and cotton; its peak, and the fame of Kawachi lotus root, came only in the Taisho period, with strains brought in from elsewhere. At Iwakuni it is said to have begun in 1796, the domain's successive lords spreading it on reclaimed land fit for no other crop, and from 1811 it was run by the domain. Kasai, east of Edo, grew only a little lotus until the Meiji market (above), so the Edo-period districts were some places, not everywhere.
