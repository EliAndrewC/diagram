• tanada-jawiki - 「Tanada (rice terraces) are paddies made on sloping ground.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • tanada-jawiki - 「For this reason, when in the Edo period the room for new-field development on the alluvial plains, reclamation included, grew scarce, paddies were next made on steep mountain slopes to raise the kokudaka on which a domain's economy rested, and many of what are now called tanada were made.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the paragraph is about western Japan; of the east the page says 「For this reason, even in the Edo period there were many places that never went so far as to cut tanada into the slopes of steep mountains」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ))
  • suido-ishizue-tanada - 「Unlike the new-field development carried out by lords, and later by great merchants, it was not planned work needing great wealth and advanced technique, but development of small areas that could be done almost with the hoe alone.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Each paddy was not only narrow but irregular in shape; its water depended largely on the favor of the rain, and it lay on a mountainside reached only by climbing narrow, winding, steep paths.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • titian-zhwiki - 「China's terraces are mainly distributed in the mountain country south of the Yangtze, most of them in Guangxi and Yunnan, because these places have more rain and many mountains, and the terraces are built along the mountains; of them the terraces of the Ailao mountains in Yunnan and of Longji in Longsheng, Guangxi, are the better known.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • suido-ishizue-yachida - 「Everywhere in Japan one sees paddies made by opening small valleys among the hills」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「From this there is also a view that in the middle ages the yachida was the most stable farmland, the standard farmland.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • yachida-jawiki - 「The yachida is a long, narrow paddy distributed over the wet ground of a valley.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It is also called yatsuda.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-yatoda - 「Because a yato could use its spring water for irrigation and could be opened in small pieces, the opening of valleys like the yato was pursued vigorously, above all in the middle ages, and made the representative farm landscape of the middle ages.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Such valley paddies are called yatoda and yachida in the Kanto region, tanada and yamada in the Kinki region, and sakoda in the Chugoku region.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
