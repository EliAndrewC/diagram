Does who held or rented a plot show in the field pattern?

Sources: kotobank-bunsan-sakuho (a household's fields in small parcels scattered among its neighbors'), tanada-jawiki (terrace holdings intricately intermixed), kotobank-warichi-seido (the periodic redistribution of fields by lot, and how its shares were made up), l7r-merchant-families (the setting's canon: most farmers are tenants)

No. A household's land was not a block; it was a handful of small parcels scattered over the village's ground and intermixed with the parcels of other households - the long-standing pattern that even the land reform after the Second World War could not break up. A terrace that looks to an outsider like one landscape is, in its holdings, intricately intermixed, with little land to a household. So the bunds a map draws are the bunds of the ground and the water, not of ownership: one household's plots lie among its neighbors' with nothing between them that the others lack.

Warichi: the holdings that moved

In some villages the holdings did not even stay put. Under warichi, a custom of the Edo period, a village re-divided its fields among its landholders by lot to even out the tax each bore against the quality of the ground; it was commonest on unstable land - the new fields of the low ground along great rivers, and landslide country - and each cultivator's land moved at every redistribution. The shares were made to scatter on purpose: the village's land was measured and graded into groups by paddy or dry field, soil, lie of the land and how often it flooded; each group was divided into as many parts as there were lots, and one part from every group made up one lot. The re-division was meant to come every five, ten or twenty years, though in practice the term slipped. What a warichi share looked like on the ground - whether the parts were cut as equal strips inside each group, or marked in any way - no page read says.

Tenancy: a contract, not a mark

In the setting, most farmers are tenants and most landlords are merchants. A tenant worked plots that were already there, under an agreement with whoever held them, and nothing read says a rented plot looked any different from a held one.

What this means for the map

Tenure does not show in the field pattern, and the maps are right to draw none: no household's plots gathered into a block, no line round a holding, nothing to set a tenant's plot apart. The irregular patchwork of small plots is itself what scattered, intermixed holding looks like from above. Warichi changes who works which plot, not the plots, so it gives the map nothing to draw.
