Were most villages clustered or scattered, and how common was each form?

Sources: kotobank-shuson (the clustered village is the general form, dense or loose by region), kotobank-sanson (the dispersed village and where it is found), kotobank-sonraku (the block village the commonest clustered form), suido-ishizue-yachida (the scattered valley settlement of the late Heian period)

The place card asks whether the kind of place on the map is an ordinary one (What a settlement IS). Part of that is the shape of the place: are the houses gathered in one knot, or spread among their fields?

Gathered, as a rule. In Japan the clustered village is the general form: the farmhouses are gathered in a limited area, and the fields lie around them. How tightly they gather differs by region. Around the old capital and the Inland Sea, dense villages of farmhouses behind walls, side by side, are common; in the Kanto and the northeast, loosely clustered villages are common, each house on a wide lot with its own grove, set well apart. Among clustered forms the commonest is the kaison, the block village, rather than a street village strung along a road; that ranking is a general one, not made for Japan alone.

Scattered, in particular places. The dispersed village, its houses standing apart among the fields, is named by region: the Tonami plain, the lower Oi river, the Sanuki plain, the Izumo plain. Further back, in the late Heian period, on the two estates the valley-paddy history draws on, the paddies lay almost without exception in small valleys, and a narrow valley is thought to have held one house or a few beside its own small valley paddy, scattered as small villages or single steadings up each branching valley.

How common each form was: no figure. No page read gives a national count or share of the clustered, block, street or dispersed forms, for any period. So the record can call the clustered village the usual form and the dispersed village a regional one, and can put no number on either.

What it means for the maps. A hamlet drawn as one cluster of houses with its fields around it is the general form, and nothing here changes how one is drawn. A dispersed hamlet would be a regional form; no generator draws one.
