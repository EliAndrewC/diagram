• fusekoshi-jawiki - 「In the case of open channels such as irrigation canals and drainage canals, where the channel crosses a river and the difference in height between the two banks is large, the water is in most cases passed beneath the river by gravity flow from the higher side, as an inverted siphon[1]. The "Shibayama fusekoshi" of the Minumadai canal in Musashi province (Saitama prefecture) [...]」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the bracketed numbers are the page's own citation marks) and 「As an example where a fusekoshi-hi (inverted-siphon culvert) was installed, the "Unomori fusekoshi-hi" of the Ogaki ring-dike [is famous] [...]」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Where an open irrigation or drainage channel crosses a river with a large level difference between the banks, it is usually passed under the river by gravity as an inverted siphon - a method also seen in historically old channels such as the Shibayama fusekoshi of the Minuma canal (Saitama) and the Kyuzaemon culvert of the Nijikka canal (Osaka); the Unomori fusekoshi-hi of the Ogaki ring-dike is a famous example.)
  • suirokyo-jawiki - 「A suirokyo (水路橋, aqueduct bridge) is a bridge for carrying water across a river or a valley. ... A structure that passes underneath a river is called a fusekoshi.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「In Oita prefecture there is the Meisho Iro No. 1 Main Line No. 1 Bridge, a six-span arch bridge of the largest class in Japan. On the Lake Biwa Canal, a Japan Heritage site, there is the Suirokaku, which passes across the grounds of Nanzen-ji on a bridge.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (An aqueduct bridge carries water across a river or valley; a structure passing under a river is a fusekoshi. Oita has one of Japan's largest six-arch aqueducts on the Meisho canal; the Lake Biwa canal's Suirokaku crosses Nanzen-ji's grounds on a bridge.)
