• fusekoshi-jawiki - 「In the case of open channels such as irrigation canals and drainage canals, where the channel crosses a river and the difference in height between the two banks is large, the water is in most cases passed beneath the river by gravity flow from the higher side, as an inverted siphon[1]. The "Shibayama fusekoshi" of the Minumadai canal in Musashi province (Saitama prefecture) [...]」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「用水路・排水路など開水路の場合、河川と交差する部分で、沿岸の双方の高さに高低差が大きい場合、高い側から逆サイフォンで河川を自然流下で通過させることが多い[1]。武蔵国見沼代用水（埼玉県）の「柴山伏越」[2]や二十箇用水（大阪府）の「久左衛門樋」[3]など、歴史的に古い水路・用水にも見られる工法である[1]。」) (the bracketed numbers are the page's own citation marks) and 「As an example where a fusekoshi-hi (inverted-siphon culvert) was installed, the "Unomori fusekoshi-hi" of the Ogaki ring-dike [is famous] [...]」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「伏越樋が設置された例としては大垣輪中の「鵜森伏越樋」が有名で」) (Where an open irrigation or drainage channel crosses a river with a large level difference between the banks, it is usually passed under the river by gravity as an inverted siphon - a method also seen in historically old channels such as the Shibayama fusekoshi of the Minuma canal (Saitama) and the Kyuzaemon culvert of the Nijikka canal (Osaka); the Unomori fusekoshi-hi of the Ogaki ring-dike is a famous example.)
  • suirokyo-jawiki - 「A suirokyo (水路橋, aqueduct bridge) is a bridge for carrying water across a river or a valley. ... A structure that passes underneath a river is called a fusekoshi.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水路橋（すいろきょう）は、川や谷を渡って水を運ぶための橋である。...川の下をくぐる構造物は伏越と呼ばれる。」) and 「In Oita prefecture there is the Meisho Iro No. 1 Main Line No. 1 Bridge, a six-span arch bridge of the largest class in Japan. On the Lake Biwa Canal, a Japan Heritage site, there is the Suirokaku, which passes across the grounds of Nanzen-ji on a bridge.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大分県には日本最大級の6連アーチ橋である明正井路一号幹線一号橋がある。日本遺産の琵琶湖疏水には南禅寺境内を橋で通過する水路閣がある。」) (An aqueduct bridge carries water across a river or valley; a structure passing under a river is a fusekoshi. Oita has one of Japan's largest six-arch aqueducts on the Meisho canal; the Lake Biwa canal's Suirokaku crosses Nanzen-ji's grounds on a bridge.)
