Were fish fry a trade, and which ponds were the nursery ponds?

Sources: cssn-sangyuanwei, fao-ac264e-ch4

What the research found. One of the things a silk-and-fish hamlet has that a rice hamlet does not (the rest are in What stands on a dike-pond hamlet that a paddy hamlet lacks?) is fish fry (鱼花, yuhua) as a trade, and nursery ponds. Jiujiang in Nanhai was a fry-trading place by the seventeenth century (a study of the township's rise from the Ming, Miles 2003, has no publicly readable copy); Qu Dajun's seventeenth-century observation of Jiujiang village in Nanhai, in his Guangdong xinyu, 「the men trade fish fry, the women feed the silkworms」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「男贩鱼花，妇女喂爱蚕」) - men trade fry, women feed the worms. Fry are reared in small ponds, then fingerling ponds, before the grow-out ponds are stocked, so a block's smallest parcels would be its nursery ponds - a modern manual puts them at 25 to 30 percent of pond area.
