Were fish fry a trade, and which ponds were the nursery ponds?

Sources: cssn-sangyuanwei, fao-ac264e-ch4

What the research found. One of the things a silk-and-fish hamlet has that a rice hamlet does not (the rest are in What stands on a dike-pond hamlet that a paddy hamlet lacks?) is fish fry (鱼花, yuhua) as a trade, and nursery ponds. Jiujiang in Nanhai was the fry-trading place: Qu Dajun's seventeenth-century observation of Jiujiang village in Nanhai, in his Guangdong xinyu, 「the men trade fish fry, the women feed the silkworms」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「男贩鱼花，妇女喂爱蚕」) - men trade fry, women feed the worms. How the township came to hold the trade, and why most villages kept no nursery ponds at all, is Did a dike-pond village rear its own fish fry, or buy them? Where fry were reared, they went into small ponds, then fingerling ponds, before the grow-out ponds were stocked; a modern manual sizes a fry pond at one to three mu and a fingerling pond at two to five, so a block's nursery ponds, where it kept any, would be its smallest parcels.
