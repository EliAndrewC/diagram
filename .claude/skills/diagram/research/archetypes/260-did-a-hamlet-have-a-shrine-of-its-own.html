Did a hamlet have a shrine of its own?

Sources: kotobank-ujigami (a tutelary shrine in every village, and the village as its unit since the Edo period), tanba-2019-higashi-ashida (one community with a shrine for the whole and one for each of its sections)

Every village had one. The ujigami, once the god of a clan, came to be identified with the chinju and the god of the birthplace, and a shrine of that kind is found in every village today. The village as its unit is old: once the village system was settled under the Edo order, the idea of a village's own god and the village as its parishioners spread over nearly the whole country.

Below the village, both forms are attested. In Tanba city, in Hyogo, most communities have one shrine each, and some have none at all. One, Higashi-Ashida, of about 180 households, has fourteen: one shrine for the whole community, and the rest each the shrine of one of its sections. The shrine priest puts the number down to the villagers' refusal to merge their shrines under the order of 1906. So a part of a village could keep a shrine of its own beside the village's, and another part could have none and share the village's. These are communities of today, counted by a modern newspaper, and the sections that kept their shrines did so by resisting a merger meant to cut them away; that the Edo-period village held such sections' shrines as well is this page's reading, not something the article states.

The branch hamlet itself: not found. The entries on the edago read here give its founding and its taxes and say nothing of a shrine, and no page read says how many hamlets had one.

What it means for the maps. The research supports two forms: a hamlet with a small shrine of its own, and a hamlet with none that shares its village's shrine. Where the research supports two forms the record makes it a knob, rolled per hamlet, and it cannot yet weight the roll. The hamlet maps draw no shrine, on the GM's rule for what a hamlet has; whether the hamlet should roll between the two forms is put to the GM, and until the GM rules the maps keep drawing none.
