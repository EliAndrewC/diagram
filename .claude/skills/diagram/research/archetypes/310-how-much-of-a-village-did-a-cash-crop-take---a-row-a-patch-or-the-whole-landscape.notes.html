• chaen-jawiki - 「The form called keihan-cha[1] (bund tea) or bund tea garden was also often seen.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「畦畔茶[1]（けいはんちゃ）あるいは畦畔茶園と呼ばれる形態も多く見かけられた。」) and 「Bund tea is a tea garden planted in a single row on the bunds of paddies and dry fields; it also served to mark the boundary with the neighbor, as a windbreak and to stop soil washing away, and the harvest beyond what the farm household used itself was sometimes shipped to wholesalers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「畦畔茶とは田畑の畦畔に一列に殖栽された茶園のことで、隣との境界を示したり、防風、土の流出防止の目的も兼ねており、農家が自家消費する以上の収穫分は問屋に出荷されたりもした。」)

  • tokyo-ja-kasai-renkon - 「The lotus root here seems to have been grown only a little from the mid-Edo period, but after Tanaka Tokuemon succeeded in trial-growing a superior strain in the mid-Meiji, lotus root being far more profitable than rice, its cultivation spread rapidly...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「当地の蓮根は、江戸中期からわずかに作られていたようですが、明治中頃に田中徳右衛門が優良系統の試作に成功して以来、稲作より蓮根のほうが各段に儲かるとあって、栽培が急速に広まり、…」), 「This place had the rich soil carried by the Edo and Ara rivers and many deep paddies, and, being near a great city, night soil from the city of Tokyo was easy to get as fertilizer.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「当地は江戸川や荒川が運んできた肥えた土と、深い田が多く、しかも、大都市に近いため、肥料として東京市中からでる下肥が入手しやすかった。」) and 「Once lotus-root growing in this district was so thriving that, around Obon, it was covered in lotus leaves as far as the eye could see...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「かつてこの地帯は、お盆の頃となると見渡す限りの蓮の葉に覆われるほど、蓮根栽培が盛んで、…」) (the page is headed Kasai lotus root, 葛西蓮根 in the Japanese; its "here" and "this place" are Kasai)
  • katsushika-renkon - 「But lotus root came to be grown in the Katsushika area comparatively late; in the "Tokyo-fu shiryo" [Tokyo Prefecture gazetteer materials] of 1872 (Meiji 5), there is no mention of lotus root among the products even of the settlements later known as lotus-root districts.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「しかし葛飾区域で蓮根が作られるようになったのは比較的遅く、明治５（1872）年の『東京府志料』には、後に蓮根の産地として知られるようになる集落でも生産物の中に蓮根の記載がない。」) and 「The "Okudo village record" published in 1917 (Taisho 6) records that 7 cho 6 tan of lotus root was planted in Kamikomatsu, Shimokomatsu, Okudo and Okudo-shinden together.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大正６（1917）年発行の『奥戸村誌』には、上小松、下小松、奥戸、奥戸新田で合わせて７町６段の蓮根が作付けされていることが記されている。」)
  • kadoma-rekishi - 「In the late Edo period [the area] also developed through the growing of rapeseed and cotton, and lotus-root growing became active too.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代後期には菜種や木綿の栽培でも発展を遂げ、蓮根栽培も活発になってきました。」) and 「In the Taisho period, with the introduction of "Kaga lotus" and "Bitchu lotus", lotus-root growing reached its peak, and the name "Kawachi lotus root" spread across the country.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大正期に「加賀蓮」「備中蓮」の導入によって蓮根栽培はその最盛期を迎え、全国的に「河内蓮根」の名が広まっていきました。」)
  • iwakuni-kankou-renkon - 「In Bunka 8 (1811) it began as a domain-run enterprise.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「文化8（1811）年には藩営として始まりました。」) ("it" is Iwakuni lotus-root growing, the page's subject, which the sentence before starts from a Bitchu strain brought from Okayama)
  • pwsannong-shuisheng-shucai - 「So in the Qing dynasty most counties of Hunan used paddy fields to grow seed lotus.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; original: 「因此，清代湖南各县大都利用水田栽培子莲。」) and 「After Daoguang, because lotus seeds were dear, farmers one after another abandoned rice fields and switched to planting seed lotus.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; original: 「道光以后，由于莲子价昂，农民纷纷废稻田改植子莲。」) (the Daoguang reign's dates, 1821-50, are not on this page)
