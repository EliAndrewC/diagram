• chaen-jawiki - 「The form called keihan-cha[1] (bund tea) or bund tea garden was also often seen.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Bund tea is a tea garden planted in a single row on the bunds of paddies and dry fields; it also served to mark the boundary with the neighbor, as a windbreak and to stop soil washing away, and the harvest beyond what the farm household used itself was sometimes shipped to wholesalers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • wuhurec-wanchun - 「Flanking the ridge of the dike, mulberry was planted in rows - some tens of thousands of mulberry trees.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ) (Shen Kuo's record of the Wanchun polder, 1061, as the page reproduces it)
  • tokyo-ja-kasai-renkon - 「The lotus root here seems to have been grown only a little from the mid-Edo period, but after Tanaka Tokuemon succeeded in trial-growing a superior strain in the mid-Meiji, lotus root being far more profitable than rice, its cultivation spread rapidly...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ), 「This place had the rich soil carried by the Edo and Ara rivers and many deep paddies, and, being near a great city, night soil from the city of Tokyo was easy to get as fertilizer.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Once lotus-root growing in this district was so thriving that, around Obon, it was covered in lotus leaves as far as the eye could see...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the page is headed Kasai lotus root, 葛西蓮根 in the Japanese; its "here" and "this place" are Kasai)
  • katsushika-renkon - 「But lotus root came to be grown in the Katsushika area comparatively late; in the "Tokyo-fu shiryo" [Tokyo Prefecture gazetteer materials] of 1872 (Meiji 5), there is no mention of lotus root among the products even of the settlements later known as lotus-root districts.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「The "Okudo village record" published in 1917 (Taisho 6) records that 7 cho 6 tan of lotus root was planted in Kamikomatsu, Shimokomatsu, Okudo and Okudo-shinden together.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kadoma-rekishi - 「In the late Edo period [the area] also developed through the growing of rapeseed and cotton, and lotus-root growing became active too.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「In the Taisho period, with the introduction of "Kaga lotus" and "Bitchu lotus", lotus-root growing reached its peak, and the name "Kawachi lotus root" spread across the country.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • iwakuni-kankou-renkon - 「In Bunka 8 (1811) it began as a domain-run enterprise.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ("it" is Iwakuni lotus-root growing, the page's subject, which the sentence before starts from a Bitchu strain brought from Okayama)
  • pwsannong-shuisheng-shucai - 「So in the Qing dynasty most counties of Hunan used paddy fields to grow seed lotus.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ) and 「After Daoguang, because lotus seeds were dear, farmers one after another abandoned rice fields and switched to planting seed lotus.」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; ) (the Daoguang reign's dates, 1821-50, are not on this page)
