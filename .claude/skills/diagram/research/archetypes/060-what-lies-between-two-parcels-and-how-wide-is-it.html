What lies between two parcels, and how wide is it?

Sources: kato-1999-ittanbu-kukaku (bunds of two shaku in a field replanned in 1869), shokukaku-suiden-jawiki (the small bunds of the earliest paddies, half a meter or less); the GM's question of 2026-07-24 and this project's decision on it

A polder's parcels are drawn as separate shapes, so the space between two of them IS the bund - and the two directions are not the same thing. Between parcels stacked one below another down the fall there is nothing but the walking bund: a ridge of piled earth with the footpath along its top. The ridge itself is narrow. A paddy replanned in Gifu in 1869 had bunds two shaku wide, about two feet, and the small bunds dividing Japan's earliest paddies, two thousand years before, were half a meter wide or less and rebuilt every season. How much wider a bund ran where it also carried the path, no page read says, so the three feet the walking bund is drawn here, ridge and path together, is a GUESS (the same finding, with the rest of what was read, is at How wide is a bund?). Along the module lines the other way a delivery ditch runs with its spoil banks either side, and that corridor is about eight feet. The distinction matters because a wide gap is a PROMISE: a reader who sees eight feet of ground between two parcels is being told that water runs there. A bay split lengthwise into strips of a single holding would otherwise give the cut between two strips of the same farm the full ditch corridor - wider than any plain walking bund, and a promise of water that is not there. The decision (2026-07-24): a gap is only as wide as what runs in it. A split within a bay gets the same plain walking bund a row edge gets, and only the module lines, where a ditch is actually drawn, keep the corridor width. The dike-pond block is unaffected: its gaps are the same in both directions already.
