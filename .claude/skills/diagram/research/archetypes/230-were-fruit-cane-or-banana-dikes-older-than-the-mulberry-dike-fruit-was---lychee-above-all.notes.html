• zhwiki-guangdong-xinyu - 「completed in the seventeenth year of Kangxi (1678), one of Qu Dajun's Five Books of Qutuo」 (translated; )
  • guangdong-xinyu-22 - 「In the villages of Guangzhou's large counties, people often give up fertile fields to make dikes, planted with fruit trees. Lychee is the most common, tea and mulberry next, mandarin and orange after them; longan is mostly planted beside houses but also on the dikes. Below the dike is a pond for fish; it is drained at year's end and in spring used to sow rice seedlings.」 (translated; )
  • guangdong-xinyu-25 - 「In Guangzhou, all the jiwei embankments are planted with lychee and longan, and some people give up rice fields to plant them.」 (translated; ) (in the entry on the lychee; 磯圍 is not defined on the page and is most plausibly the diked polders' and rivers' embankments)
  • pwsannong-zhusanjiao-nongyeshi - 「Fruit-dike fishponds first arose in Nanhai and Shunde in the mid-Ming. Mulberry-dike fishponds, which replaced them in the late Ming and early Qing, grew with sericulture and in the Qing became the dominant dike-pond type」 (translated; )
  • guangdong-xinyu-25 - 「In a field, each mu can take twenty-odd lychee trees, and twice that many longan. Silt is made into mounds about two chi high so that floodwater does not reach them, and they are covered over with grass so that the fierce sun does not reach them.」 (translated; ) (the field is the rice field given up in the sentence before; a Qing chi is about 32 cm, and a mu of 240 square bu of 5 chi about 6,600 sq ft, so twenty-odd trees is one to about 300 sq ft - this page's arithmetic)
  • pwsannong-zhusanjiao-nongyeshi - 「Besides mulberry-dike fishponds and fruit-dike fishponds, the region also had rice-dike, cane-dike and fan-palm-dike fishponds, among other dike-pond types.」 (translated; ) (the sentence gives no date; it stands in the page's section on the Ming and Qing)
  • guangdong-xinyu-27 - 「In Zengcheng's Xizhou many grow bananas; after three or four years they cut them all down to plant white cane ... and after two years of white cane, bananas again.」 (translated; ); 「cane fields are nearly equal to rice fields」 (translated; )
