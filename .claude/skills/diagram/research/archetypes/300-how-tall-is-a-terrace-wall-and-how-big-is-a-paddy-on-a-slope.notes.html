  • tanada-jawiki - 「Western Japan has much ground where steep mountains drop straight into the sea, its alluvial plains are in many places comparatively narrow, and the land fit for farming had long since been turned to fields to a high degree. ... In doing so, using the traditional skill of building stone walls, the bunds and banks of the terraces (the earth slopes, doha) were built as stone walls able to withstand the steep slope, to raise the yield to the limit.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • tanada-jawiki - 「On the other hand, eastern Japan, from the Tokai and Hokuriku regions eastward, was blessed with comparatively broad alluvial plains, and besides had wide room to open farmland on the little-developed diluvial uplands and river terraces, mainly on the Pacific side, and on alluvial fans, mainly on the Sea of Japan side. For this reason, even into the Edo period there were many places where things never went as far as cutting terraces into the slopes of steep mountains; terraces were seldom built, or where they were built the bunds and banks were earth mounds with gentle slopes, a terrace landscape in contrast to western Japan's.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
