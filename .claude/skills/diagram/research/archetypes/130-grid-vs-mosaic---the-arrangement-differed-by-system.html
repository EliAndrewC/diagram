Grid vs mosaic - the arrangement differed by system

Sources: cssn-jiangnan-weitian (the tangpu lattice at five-to-seven and seven-to-ten li). The Pearl-delta reads as a mosaic of CONSTRUCTED ponds threaded by meandering natural rivers, the made and the natural hard to tell apart. The dikes' erosion from 20 m to under 4 m as the ponds grew is chi-2024-dike-pond-commons's, measured in one Shunde village over the decades since the reform and opening-up

What the research found: the answer is BOTH, and it splits by system. The individual ponds genuinely WERE rectangular oblongs - hand-dug from marsh (挖塘培基: dig the pond, pile the spoil into the dike), the recurring descriptor a "chessboard" of "rectangular-shaped dikes... with water in between" - because you dig a pond in whatever packs the space and straight dikes are easiest to pile, walk, and plant mulberry on. So all-rectangular ponds are correct; this is the rare farm landscape that does NOT grow organically around the terrain, because the farmers MADE the terrain. BUT the arrangement differed by system. The lower-Yangtze 圩田 (wei-tian) was a SURVEYED rectilinear grid - the Song-era 塘浦 tangpu lattice, deliberately engineered - so a clean grid is historically right for it. The Pearl-delta 桑基魚塘 (dike-pond) instead accreted household-by-household into a MOSAIC; the landscape-ecology literature describes the historical delta not as a uniform chessboard but as mosaic-like constructed ponds among meandering natural river systems, the boundary between constructed and natural blurred - rectangles, yes, but of varied sizes at varied local orientations fitted around winding interior creeks (the perfectly-uniform grid is closer to the MODERN consolidated look, which eroded the dikes considerably: as the delta commercialized the water bodies expanded and arable dikes that used to be 20 m wide were eroded to less than 4 m, the fish farming itself narrowing the dike - a study of one Shunde village over the decades since the reform and opening-up, not a measurement across the whole delta).

So the arrangement is ROLLED per settlement, not chosen once. Both readings are supportable and two polders that look different multiply the variety a reader can see, so the arrangement is a knob each map rolls from its own seed: the surveyed grid at one end; at the other, the interior lattice displaced by a smooth correlated drift that fades to nothing at the pinned perimeter, so neighboring nodes move together, the lines stay continuous but BEND, the interior ditches become meandering creeks and the parcels skew to trapezoids while the individual ponds stay four-sided - which is what the record describes, the mosaic being in the ARRANGEMENT and never in the ponds being triangular. The pool's rice polder keeps the surveyed grid it is named for; its dike-pond block runs the mosaic and declares it.

The variety this buys is meant to count structurally: the pond layout is one of the axes that decide whether two settlements read as genuinely distinct places, so a grid polder and a mosaic polder of the same water direction are different by construction.
