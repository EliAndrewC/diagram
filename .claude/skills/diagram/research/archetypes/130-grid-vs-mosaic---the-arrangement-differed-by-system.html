Grid vs mosaic - the arrangement differed by system

Sources: cssn-jiangnan-weitian (the tangpu lattice at five-to-seven and seven-to-ten li), digang-sangji-people (the chessboard of ponds as Digang's ponds look from the air today). The Pearl-delta reads as a mosaic of CONSTRUCTED ponds threaded by meandering natural rivers, the made and the natural hard to tell apart. The dikes' erosion from 20 m to under 4 m as the ponds grew is chi-2024-dike-pond-commons's, as its informants report it for one Shunde village over the decades since the reform and opening-up

What the research found: the answer is BOTH, and it splits by system. The individual ponds genuinely WERE rectangular oblongs - dug out of low-lying fields (挖深田地为塘: dig the fields deep into ponds, the spoil spread around them as the dike), and seen from the air today the ponds look like a huge chessboard - because you dig a pond in whatever packs the space and straight dikes are easiest to pile, walk, and plant mulberry on. So all-rectangular ponds are correct; this is the rare farm landscape that does NOT grow organically around the terrain, because the farmers MADE the terrain. BUT the arrangement differed by system. The lower Yangtze 圩田 (wei-tian) was a SURVEYED rectilinear grid - the Song-dynasty 塘浦 tangpu lattice, deliberately engineered, its channels five to ten li apart - so a clean grid is historically right for its CANALS. At the scale of the ponds inside that lattice, no premodern source was found that draws or describes them laid out in a uniform chessboard: the one pond-scale chessboard found is Digang's ponds on Lake Tai as a reporter sees them from the air today. The Pearl-delta 桑基魚塘 (dike-pond) instead accreted, on this page's reading household by household, into a MOSAIC; a landscape-architecture essay describes the historical delta as mosaic-like constructed ponds among meandering natural river systems, the boundary between constructed and natural blurred - rectangles, yes, but of varied sizes at varied local orientations fitted around winding interior creeks (the perfectly-uniform grid is closer to the MODERN consolidated look, which eroded the dikes considerably: as the delta commercialized the water bodies expanded and arable dikes that used to be 20 m wide were eroded to less than 4 m, the fish farming itself narrowing the dike - a study of one Shunde village over the decades since the reform and opening-up, not a measurement across the whole delta).

So a dike-pond block is drawn as the mosaic, and the uniform grid of ponds is not drawn. The grid is attested for the canals and the mosaic for the ponds; a uniform chessboard of ponds is found only in the present day, and the GM's rule of 2026-09-28 is that the maps draw nothing that is only modern ("We should eliminate anything which is only modern"). So the pond layout is not a choice between two attested forms to roll between: it is the one form, the interior lattice displaced by a smooth correlated drift that fades to nothing at the pinned perimeter, so neighboring nodes move together, the lines stay continuous but BEND, the interior ditches become meandering creeks and the parcels skew to trapezoids while the individual ponds stay four-sided - which is what the record describes, the mosaic being in the ARRANGEMENT and never in the ponds being triangular. How strongly the lattice bends is a degree along a continuum, calibrated on the Kuwabata map, which was drawn with it.
