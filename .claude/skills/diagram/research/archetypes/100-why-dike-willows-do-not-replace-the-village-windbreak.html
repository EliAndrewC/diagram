Why dike willows do NOT replace the village windbreak

Sources: usu-windbreak (shelter up to 30 times the windbreak's height - the entry's 10-20 times is inside it), yashikirin-jawiki (igune on the north and west; species sugi, pine, hinoki and keyaki), pollarding-enwiki, waju-jawiki, wind-gradient-enwiki (open water slows the wind less than land)

Why the dike willows do NOT replace the village windbreak (GM 2026-07-24, asked and answered): a windbreak's sheltered zone runs ~10-20x barrier HEIGHT, and the homestead belt is full-height dense planting for exactly that reason, while dike willows are POLLARDED low because the withies are the harvest - a single porous row at rooftop height shelters almost nothing at the houses' distance. Worse, willow and mulberry are DECIDUOUS: bare sticks during the NW winter monsoon, the one season shelter matters, which is why the homestead windbreak is a different planting (the igune of the flat Sendai plain, on the north and west against the winter wind, are sugi, pine and hinoki with keyaki - the same reason; that they persisted amid abundant field-edge trees is unsourced). And an east-dike village sits downwind of the whole pond sheet - open water drags on the wind far less than land does, so the wind reaches the dike little slowed. Utility rows on the dike and a windbreak grove at the homestead are DIFFERENT plants doing DIFFERENT jobs; the Pearl delta planted its dikes and the waju its homesteads.
