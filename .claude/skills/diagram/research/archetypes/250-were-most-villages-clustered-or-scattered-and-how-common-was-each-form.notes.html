  • kotobank-shuson - 「In the Kinai and the Setouchi, dense villages in which farmhouses enclosed by walls stand close beside one another are common, but in the Kanto and the Tohoku loosely clustered villages are common, in which each house lot is wide, with a homestead grove and the like, and the houses stand at wider intervals.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-sonraku - 「Among the gathered forms there are the block village, the most general, the street village and road village in which houses stand in a line along a street, and the round village seen in Germany, Poland and elsewhere」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-sanson - 「Of irregular dispersed villages, the Tonami plain in Toyama prefecture, the lower Oi river, the Sanuki plain, the Izumo plain and others are given as examples」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • suido-ishizue-yachida - 「For example, in Iriki-in in Satsuma province (Kagoshima prefecture) and the Ota estate in Bingo province (Hiroshima prefecture), it has become clear, from the names (the names of the holders of land whose rights were established) recorded in documents of the time that survive today in place names and the like, that in the late Heian period the paddies were almost without exception valley paddies opened in each small valley, using spring water, rainwater and the like, and that development had not advanced on the somewhat more open alluvial lowland downstream.」 and 「Following this scattering of the paddies, the houses too were scattered, and it is thought that the landscape was one in which small villages or dispersed settlements, one house or a few farmhouses each paired with a small valley paddy, were dotted here and there up each narrow branching valley.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
