In small retiring houses built also upon the brink of roads, and in the neigh- bourhood of villages, reservoirs are constructed of com- pact materials to preventtheabsorptionof whatever they receive, and straw is carefully thrown over the surface from time to time to stop the evaporation.」 (volume 2, pages 474-475, the margin reading "Journey to Canton"; the imprint is London, 1797)
  • pwsannong-gudai-feiliao - 「Wang Zhen's Nongshu, in its chapter on manuring the soil, also points out: "Southern households that farm fields commonly set a brick enclosure at the head of the field and use [the manure] after it has ripened in the pit; their fields are very fine; northern farm households should also copy this, the gain may be tenfold."」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; original: 「王祯《农书》“粪壤”篇也指出：“南方治田之家，常于田头置砖槛，窖熟而用之，其田甚美，北方农家亦宜效此，利可十倍。”」) (the page gives no date for the book)
  • pwsannong-gudai-feiliao - 「The Ming-dynasty Shen shi nongshu also says "cattle manure brought home must go into the tan, with water added to rot it"; the "tan" here means a pit dug in the ground ... The Shen shi nongshu also points out "the tan must be deep and large"」 (translated from the Chinese (simplified) by this project; original: 「明代《沈氏农书》还说“牛壅载归，必须下潭，加水作烂”，这里所说的“潭”是指在地上挖的坑……同时《沈氏农书》还指出“潭要深大”」) (that the tan is dug in the ground is the page's gloss; the manual's own words are "deep and large")
  • shimogoe-jawiki - 「If you put fresh filth on the dry field and make it manure for the crop, then as it rots it soaks into the soil, disease attaches itself to the crop, and damp-natured insects swarm out in numbers. It should be rotted well inside the storage jar (tametsubo) and so made nourishment for the crop.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「不浄の新しきを畠に置き、作毛のこやしとするに、其の腐りて土地にしみつき、作毛に病ひ付き、しつ性の蟲共多くわき出る。溜壺の内にて、能く腐らせて作毛の養ひとすべし」) (the page's own attribution: 「the Hyakusho Denki of Tenna 1 (1681)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「天和元年（1681年）の『百姓伝記』」))
