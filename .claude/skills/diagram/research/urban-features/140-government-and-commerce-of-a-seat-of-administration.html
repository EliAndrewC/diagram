Government and commerce of a seat of administration

Sources: the setting's own budget notes (the Six Ministries, gate tariffs, the tax-rice granary); guanxiang-zdic (the definition; what filled the strip outside a gate is not on this page); kanazawa-jokamachi-study (the offices standing inside the castle with the ruling house's own mansion), imagawa-2010-jokamachi-kaido (the street from the main gate, and the frontages ranged along it)., sekisho-jawiki, shukuba-jawiki, kurayashiki-jawiki

Following imperial Chinese yamen and Japanese jokamachi practice, government offices cluster around the governor's mansion (the grandest compound) and FRONT an official avenue, never floating mid-block. A provincial city carries the Six provincial Ministries (one being Rites, sited in the temple neighborhood). The state taxes trade, and the gate tariff and the tax-rice granary are the setting's own canon rather than a historical finding: inspection and tariff stations at the gates (the historical barrier stations stood on the roads, at passes and river crossings), caravan facilities (inn and stables) on the trade route, a tax-rice granary at a collecting magistracy, and markets springing up just outside the gates. A county-seat town is run by a magistrate from a walled manor.
