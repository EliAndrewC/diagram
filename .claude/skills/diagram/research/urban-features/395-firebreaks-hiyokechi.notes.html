• hiyokechi-jawiki - 「A hiyokechi is an open ground for fire prevention that the Edo shogunate established in Edo in the wake of the Meireki great fire of Meireki 3 (1657).」 (translated; ) (with 「In the broad sense it also includes the hirokoji and the like, streets having the same purpose」 (translated; ); the page says the ground was not simply vacant, and 「a blaze could not jump」 was this page's)
  • hiyokechi-jawiki - 「After that fire, five burned-over sites were assigned as hiyokechi, and thereafter, mainly to keep fire from spreading to Edo Castle, they were added little by little, centered on the castle's northwest side, growing to as many as 13 sites by the Kyoho era.」 (translated; ) (it contains no rule about spacing between houses. "Never spaced individual machiya apart" is therefore an inference from what the page does NOT say, not a statement it makes.)
  • tokyo-archives-hirokoji - 「In Edo there were streets called hirokoji, built wider than an ordinary street. Most were established at places about the city as "fire-clearance open ground" (hiyoke-akichi) to stop a fire spreading.」 and 「Because these open grounds fundamentally had the function of fire-clearance ground, no permanent building could be put up on them. But on the hirokoji established at busy bridgeheads and before the gates of temples and shrines that drew many worshippers, rows of movable and removable stall-shops were put up and goods were traded, markets formed, and besides street performance, entertainments were staged in booths screened with reed matting - so many of them developed into pleasure quarters.」 (translated; ) (the Tokyo Metropolitan Archives' own page for its published study of the Edo hirokoji.)
