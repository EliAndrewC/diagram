Which way out of town does a tanning yard stand - kegare has a direction, not a radius

Sources: kotobank-hisabetsu-buraku (the leather workers housed at the castle town's edge, at chokepoints and on the riverbank), joetsu-hisabetsu-history (Takada's quarter at its northwest edge on the highway), uw-edo-manifold (Edo's outcaste head at the city's northern edge), kozukappara-jawiki (Edo's northern execution ground), yoshiwara-jawiki (the pleasure quarter moved behind Senso-ji), kawaramono-jawiki (the riverbed workers)

Why the quarter stands in town while its yard stands out on the water, and why the walk between them is not a defect, is Does a tanning yard need its quarter close by. This question is the rule that does tie the two together: which way out of town the yard faces.

  • Distance between quarter and yard is not the measure. Across the pool (the project's test maps) it runs from 90 ft (Hirameki) to 2,587 ft (Tango), and the far ones are not defects. The GM's ruling: a walled city keeps its burakumin INSIDE, because the caste's in-town duties require it, while the noxious phase of the trade goes out to the water - so distance between the two is the NORMAL arrangement, not a failure of it. Measuring the same maps by BEARING from the settlement core separates them cleanly where distance cannot: Hirameki 0.6 deg, Minami 17.2, Hoshizora 28.5, Nagahara 56.0, Ubame 100, Tango 127. The rule is a direction test.

  • Why direction, and which one. The history gives an EDGE, not a compass point. When the Sengoku lords built their castle towns and took the leather workers under their protection and control, the ground they housed them on was, as far as the record can tell, the edge of the castle town, a chokepoint on the roads, or the riverbank - and the riverbed was where the hides were worked. Which edge differed from town to town: Takada, laid out in 1614, set the people of its guard and cleaning duties in a block at the town's northwest edge along the Kaga road; Edo's outcaste head lived by the river at the city's northern edge, the side where its northern execution ground lay and where the pleasure quarter was moved, behind Senso-ji, and the riverbed workers took their name from the riverbed. So there is no one compass direction - no page read ties the outcaste side to the unlucky northeast or any other bearing - but within a town the quarter, its works and the execution ground go out the same way, to the edge where the road leaves and the river runs (the shared bearing is this page's reading of Edo and Takada; no page read states it as a rule). The quarter, the execution ground and the tanning yard share a bearing, and the daily carcass haul never crosses the rest of the settlement - real castle towns are read as keeping designated carcass routes off the main streets for precisely that reason (a rule on no page read). The CREMATION ground is the deliberate exclusion: it is monk-run and follows the temple/funerary complex (in the GM's setting monks officiate at a cremation with burakumin assistants, as where the crematory stands records; that the ground therefore follows the temple rather than the outcast side is the map's own convention). Tested across the pool, that association is real where it matters (Hoshizora 130 ft from its monastery, Ubame 360 ft) and absent elsewhere (Tango 2,606 ft), so cremation is excluded from the bearing rule WITHOUT a positive "follows the monastery" rule being asserted in its place - the evidence supports the exclusion, not the converse claim.
  • The 90 deg threshold, and why it is not a fresh number. It is the "same half of the compass" test the execution ground already uses, applied to the other burakumin-run works rather than a fresh tolerance. The pool's loosest accepted map (Nagahara, 83.7 deg when measured from the middle of its laborers' dwellings, where the rule measures it, rather than the 56.0 deg from the whole settlement's core above) sits inside it and the two defects sit outside.

The two maps that waive the direction rule, and why each is allowed to. A rule measured in feet was considered and rejected: Nagahara's yard stands about 1,390 ft from its quarter and is correct, and a distance rule would have condemned it. What the direction rule forbids is the yard facing the opposite way out of town, which sends the tanners' daily carcass haul back through the whole settlement - the traffic real castle towns are read as routing around with designated carcass ways (a rule on no page read). Two maps stand outside it for reasons the map can state. TANGO because the Emperor lies southeast, and that status claim takes the quarter to the opposite side from its own works. UBAME because its land falls southwest while its only tannable water is the valley stream in the east, so the fall of the ground and the water genuinely disagree in that hill valley. A waived map no longer holds the rule honest, which is why both are recorded as waivers rather than quietly accommodated by loosening it. The cremation ground is excluded from the rule on purpose: it is drawn monk-run, following the funerary complex (the map's convention, as above), which need not be the outcast side at all - Hoshizora's stands 130 ft from its monastery and almost exactly opposite the quarter, and that is a correct map. And the rule abstains on a settlement with no dwellings outside the quarter, where the core lands on the quarter itself and no bearing exists.

The rule the map follows: a tanning yard stands on the QUARTER'S SIDE of the settlement, within 90 degrees of the quarter's bearing from the core - the same half-of-the-compass test the execution ground takes. The 90 degrees are this project's own figure, borrowed rather than freshly invented.
