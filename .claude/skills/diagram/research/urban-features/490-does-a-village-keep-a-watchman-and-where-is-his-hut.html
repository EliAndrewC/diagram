Does a village keep a watchman, and where is his hut?

Sources: kotobank-banta (the hired watchman of towns and villages, his duties, status, hut and pay), kotobank-bangoya (a watch hut at a village's entrance)

The hired watchman. Edo-period towns and villages hired a watchman, the banta, for the night watch and to guard against fire and at the sluice gates. In a village his work was guarding the hills and fields and the sluice gates, running errands, keeping the night watch and moving vagrants on; outside Edo most such watchmen were of hinin status. He sat in a watch hut and was paid a watchman's allowance, and in some regions he also dug graves, cremated the dead and dealt with travelers who died on the road. Which caste keeps this post in the setting is the setting's to say; the map draws only the hut.

Where the hut stood. The one placement found is an early attestation, from a work of 1721: in the villages, under the name of the advance hawk watch, a watch hut was built at the village's entrance. That was a watch named for the hawks, not the ordinary village watchman, so putting every village watchman's hut at the entrance is reading one attestation wider than it goes. How many villages kept a watchman, and how big his hut was, no page read says. A town's watch, at its ward gates, is a different arrangement and a different question.

What it means for the map. Whether a village keeps a hired watchman is a knob, rolled per village from the map's seed: one form keeps a watchman and draws his hut, the other keeps none. The hut stands where the lane enters the village, on the one attestation found and because a watchman who guards the approaches and moves vagrants on is best placed there; how often a village keeps one, and the hut's size, are guesses.

The rule the map follows: each village rolls from its seed whether it keeps a watchman (about one village in two, a guess); one that does draws a single small hut, about 9 by 6 ft (a guess), beside the lane where it enters the village. A hamlet keeps none (a guess).
