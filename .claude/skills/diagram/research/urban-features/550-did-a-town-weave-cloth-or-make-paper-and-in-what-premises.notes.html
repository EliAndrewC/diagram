  • kotobank-konya - 「Textiles for clothing and the like were still made at home in the early modern period, but the dyeing of the cloth and thread that were their material was entrusted to specialists such as the kon'ya.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「衣料などの織物は近世でも自家生産であったが、その材料の布や糸の染色は紺屋などの専業者に頼んでいた。」) (the Nipponica entry, by Endo Motoo)
  • kotobank-debata - 「This arrangement was carried on as one of the rural industries, the weaving being done by farm households as a side job in the slack farming season.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この形態は農村工業の一つとして行われ、農家の副業で農閑期に製織されることになる。」); 「Against this, what is done as a household industry under one's own management is uchibata.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「これに対し家内工業として自己の経営のもとで行われるのが「内機(うちばた)」である。」) (the Nipponica entry, by Tsunoyama Yukihiro, which gives no period for either)
  • kotobank-nishijin - 「After the Onin war at the end of the Muromachi period, weaving revived on the site of the camp of Yamana's western army (Nishijin) and flourished; the name refers to the textiles produced there since.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「室町末期の応仁の乱後，山名氏の西軍陣地（西陣）跡に機業が復活，繁栄して以来，この地で産した織物をいう。」); 「formed a guild of draw-loom weavers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「高機織屋仲間を結成」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry)
  • toyota-museum-washi - 「From the Edo period into modern times, papermaking was carried on, on a small scale, as a side job of the farming off-season.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代から近現代にかけて、農閑期の副業として小規模ながら紙漉きが行われてきました。」)
  • kotobank-washi - 「The stack of sheets is left overnight as it is, and the water pressed out by setting weights on it or by a lever or similar method; at the right time the sheets are peeled off one by one, pasted onto drying boards, and dried naturally in the sun.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「紙床はそのまま一晩放置し、上から重石(おもし)を置いたり、天秤(てんびん)式などの方法で水切りをし、適当なころに1枚ずつはがして干し板に貼(は)り、日光に当てて自然乾燥を行う。」); 「on a fine day they dry in about an hour」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「晴天ならば1時間ほどで乾く」) (the Nipponica entry, by Machida Seishi, describing the craft as practiced into modern times)
