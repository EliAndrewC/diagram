What does a kiln works look like on the map - a chambered climbing kiln with a 60 ft fire gap

Sources: noborigama-jawiki, charcoal-kiln-enwiki

Why the works is one kiln rather than a tile kiln, and why its potters live at it - a firing that is stoked round the clock for days among the reasons - is Kiln works - a kiln is not a tile kiln, and the potters live at it. This question is the kiln's drawn shape, its fire gap and its sizes.

  • THE FORM. A low earthen mound is a CHARCOAL kiln's shape, and would be indistinguishable from Ubame's charcoal district. A tile or pottery kiln in this culture is a long chambered tunnel built into a slope: firebox at the bottom, chambers climbing, chimney at the top. The chambered silhouette also carries the siting fact a mound would hide - the works wants rising ground. (No separate slope band is drawn behind the kiln: at city scale it reads as a stray tab off the kiln's shoulder rather than as ground, and the chambered form says it already.)
  • THE 60 FT FIRE GAP, and why it is not a new number. It joins the existing separation ladder at the rung the refining forge already occupies. A firing is a very large fire, but an ATTENDED one - being stoked in shifts is the whole reason it runs the way it does - so it sits with the live worked fire at 60 ft and not with the UNATTENDED self-heating charcoal stack at 30 ft, nor with the 120 ft nuisance figures, which defend against smell carried on air rather than against ignition. Duration does the work here that the forced blast does for the forge. The gap is measured from the KILN BODY rather than from the works' ground, because the works' own cottages stand inside that ground - a bounding-rect test could only ever measure the gap to somebody else's house.
  • Deliberate departure from literal reality. Sizes are true feet at the map's grain with no legibility inflation, pitched against the pool's other bulk works (the charcoal yard's 88 x 58, the brewery's 96 ft hall): a 46 ft kiln, a 32 x 18 ft throwing and drying shed, a 30 x 24 ft clay pit, and 28 x 18 ft cottages on a 140 x 120 ft ground.
