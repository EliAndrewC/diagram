Commercial frontage and wealth-stratified housing

Sources: kyomachiya-jawiki, kyototuu-unagi, qixian-cnr (a Chinese county seat's shops, workshop and lodging behind), machiya-shoka-jawiki (Kyoto's three frontage bands), nagaya-jawiki

In premodern Japanese castle-towns (jokamachi) and Chinese cities the valuable street frontage is COMMERCE: shops and workshops line the high street, each facing the road, while dwellings sit behind them or in the block interior reached by alleys. A merchant family lived over/behind its own shop (so merchant residences sit directly behind the storefronts, sharing their orientation). Housing was stratified by wealth, never one size: Kyoto's townsmen fell into a few with large houses of ten ken of frontage or more, the ordinary shopkeepers with four or five, and the small traders with one and a half to two or renting, though how many households stood in each band is on no page read. The merchant houses stand as independent shops on the main street while the laborer warren packs dense and uniform in the back-street tenements. What each band's frontage is in feet, on the Japanese and the Chinese model, is under how wide a merchant's shophouse is; the walled merchant compound of the richest few is under why a city has only one or two walled merchant compounds.
