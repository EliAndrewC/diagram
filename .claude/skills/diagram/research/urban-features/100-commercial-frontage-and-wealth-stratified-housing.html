Commercial frontage and wealth-stratified housing

Sources: kyomachiya-jawiki, kyototuu-unagi, qixian-cnr (a Chinese county seat's shops, workshop and lodging behind), machiya-shoka-jawiki (Kyoto's three frontage bands), nagaya-jawiki

In premodern Japanese towns (Kyoto the attested case) and at least one Chinese county seat the valuable street frontage is COMMERCE: shops and workshops line the high street, each facing the road (in Kyoto on a narrow, deep lot), while dwellings sit behind them or in back-street tenements. A merchant family lived in the same house as its own shop (so a merchant residence shares its storefront's orientation). Housing was stratified by wealth, never one size: Kyoto's townsmen fell into those holding large houses of ten ken of frontage or more, those holding ordinary houses of four or five, and those holding small houses of one and a half to two or renting, though how many households stood in each band is on no page read. The merchant houses of the middle stratum and above stand as independent shops on the main street while the laborer warren packs dense and uniform in the back-street tenements. What each band's frontage is in feet, on the Japanese and the Chinese model, is under how wide a merchant's shophouse is; the walled merchant compound of the richest few is under why a city has only one or two walled merchant compounds.
