How many bathhouses does a place keep, and does a county town have one?

Sources: sento-jawiki (Edo's count), joetsu-furoya and joetsu-furoya-pdf (a castle town's and a port's counts, and who used them), murakami-castle-population (the castle town's population), yashio-noma-yogyo (one bathhouse among nineteen villages), mengliang-lu-13-wikisource (the Song bathhouse keepers' guild)

Who used them. A bathhouse served people without a bath of their own. In the castle town of Takada and in Naoetsu-imamachi, ordinary townspeople had no bath and went to the bathhouse; in the countryside every house had a bath, and to save firewood and labor the households of a settlement heated theirs in turn and bathed at one another's. The commercial bathhouse is old in China too: in Southern Song Hangzhou its keepers had a guild of their own, the "fragrant water guild".

How many, by the size of the place.

  • A great city. Edo in the Bunka era (1804-1818) is said to have had more than 600 bathhouses for some 1.0 to 1.2 million inhabitants - one for every 1,700 to 2,000.
  • A provincial castle town. Takada's bathhouses formed a guild of 19 in 1769 and stayed at 19 until the end of the shogunate; Naoetsu-imamachi kept 8 to the same end. Takada counted 24,571 inhabitants in the national ranking of 1886, which, against the 19, is one bathhouse per 1,300 or so - a rough figure, since that census came nearly twenty years after the count it is set against, and neither source gives the castle town's population in the Edo period itself.
  • A large village. In 1829 the nineteen villages of what is now Yashio city, 5,672 inhabitants, had a single bathhouse, and it stood in Hachijo, a village of 856 and the largest of them, a hub of traffic.

So a county town keeps one. A county seat of about 1,200 is larger than Hachijo and, like it, the chief place and traffic hub of its district; it keeps one bathhouse for its townspeople, while the farmhouses around it bathe at home.

What it means for the map: a county seat keeps exactly one bathhouse. A city keeps one for every 1,300 to 2,000 inhabitants - the band between the provincial castle town and Edo, a degree rolled per settlement - and never fewer than one; the city maps' present count of one per full 2,000 sits at the sparse end of that band. What a bathhouse draws is the next question.
