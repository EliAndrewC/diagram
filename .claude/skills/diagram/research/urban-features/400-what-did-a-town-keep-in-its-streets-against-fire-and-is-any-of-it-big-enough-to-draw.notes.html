• tensuioke-kotobank - 「Also called simply tensui; in the Edo period, a tub in which rain water (tensui) was led from the gutters and the like and stored for fire prevention.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「単に天水ともよばれ、江戸時代、雨水(あまみず)（天水）を雨樋(あまどい)などから引き、防火用にためておいた桶。」) / 「It is said to have become general from the Kansei era (1789-1801) onward, and it was kept permanently on roofs, at the eaves of houses, at the corners of town crossings and so on. The tubs were larger or smaller according to where they were placed; at town crossings and the like a large tub holding about 6 koku (about 108 liters) of water was placed, and on top of it hand buckets were stacked so that they could be used at once.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一般化したのは寛政(かんせい)（1789～1801）以降といわれ、屋根の上、家の軒先、町の辻角(つじかど)などに常置された。桶の大きさには、その桶の置かれる場所によって大小があり、町辻などには約六石（約108リットル）ほどの水が入る大桶が置かれ、その上には手桶を積んで、すぐ使用できるようにしてあった。」) (Nipponica's entry on the page; the liter figure is the page's own)

  • edo-hikeshi-gakken - 「Edo firefighting, which lacked scientific firefighting technology like today's, was basically "destructive firefighting", which pulled down the houses downwind of the fire's source to prevent its spread and stop the damage.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「現代のような科学的な消防技術がなかった江戸の消防は、火元より風下の家々を壊して延焼を防ぎ、火災の被害をくい止める、「破壊消防」が基本。」) / 「In practice it seems it was mostly used to spray water on the standard-bearers and others on the roofs to protect them from the sparks.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「実際には、屋根上の纏持ち等に水を吹き付けて火の粉から護ることが多かったようだ。」) (of the ryudosui hand pump)
