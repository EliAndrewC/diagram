• tensuioke-kotobank - 「Also called simply tensui; in the Edo period, a tub in which rain water (tensui) was led from the gutters and the like and stored for fire prevention.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) / 「It is said to have become general from the Kansei era (1789-1801) onward, and it was kept permanently on roofs, at the eaves of houses, at the corners of town crossings and so on. The tubs were larger or smaller according to where they were placed; at town crossings and the like a large tub holding about 6 koku (about 108 liters) of water was placed, and on top of it hand buckets were stacked so that they could be used at once.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Nipponica's entry on the page; the liter figure is the page's own)
  • hinomi-yagura-bousaihaku - 「1 koku 5 to to 1 koku 6 to (270 liters to 288 liters)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (a pump's delivery per minute; the page's own conversion, which puts a koku at 180 liters)

  • edo-hikeshi-gakken - 「Edo firefighting, which lacked scientific firefighting technology like today's, was basically "destructive firefighting", which pulled down the houses downwind of the fire's source to prevent its spread and stop the damage.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) / 「In practice it seems it was mostly used to spray water on the standard-bearers and others on the roofs to protect them from the sparks.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (of the ryudosui hand pump)
