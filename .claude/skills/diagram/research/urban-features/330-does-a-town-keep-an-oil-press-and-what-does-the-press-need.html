Does a town keep an oil press, and what does the press need?

Sources: yashio-noma-yogyo (an oil presser in a village of 856), nonoichi-aburaya (oil shops in a village, and the water-wheel mill), tiangong-kaiwu-faju (the Chinese press, pan and mill)

Yes - one, in a place of this size. Hachijo, the largest village in what is now Yashio city and a crossroads, with 856 inhabitants, had one household in 1836 entered for oil pressing, which also took pledges as a pawnbroker. In Ishikawa county, where rapeseed grew well, Nonoichi village had households that kept oil shops in the Edo period, for lamp oil was precious before electricity. So a county seat keeps one oil presser, and it is a trade among others rather than a works standing alone; how many a city kept was not found.

What the press needs. The Chinese technical encyclopedia Tiangong kaiwu describes it. The press is a hollowed log, a large one an arm-span round, camphor being the best wood; where such trees were scarce north of the Yangzi, four logs were bound together with iron hoops. Its slot is some 3 to 4 chi long. The seed is first roasted in a flat pan only 6 cun deep.

The mill comes in three forms, and each draws differently. The plainest is a trough sunk in the ground with an iron wheel on a pole, pushed back and forth by two men; a presser with capital builds a stone mill turned by an ox - an edge-runner mill - one ox doing the work of ten men; and in Japan the seed was also ground by a water wheel before pressing. The water-wheel mill needs a stream, and the ox mill a circular track for its stone; that the hand mill stands indoors and the ox has a stall beside its track is a guess.

What it means for the map: a county seat keeps one oil presser and a city keeps at least one. Its mill is a knob rolled per settlement among the three attested forms: a hand trough inside the press house, an ox mill with its circular track in the yard, or a water-wheel mill on a stream or channel, the last only where one runs by. The press house's size and its place in town are not in any source read: the building of about 40 to 60 by 25 to 30 ft is a guess sized to hold a press log, a roasting hearth and the mill, and standing toward the edge of town is a guess from its roasting fire.
