Is a fire gap kept round a charcoal yard?

Sources: nwcg-safety-zones, fukagawa-kiba-summary (Edo moving its timber stacks out)

What a charcoal yard is - a tallied depot with covered sheds and a weighing floor - is Charcoal yards: a tallied depot, a cooling ground, and a weighing floor. This question is how far it stands from its neighbors.

A modern figure, not drawn. A 30 ft gap can be reasoned from this project's other separations (~6 ft for an attended forge against hay, 120 ft for putrefaction carried on air) and from today's wildfire guidance, whose safety-zone rule, for people rather than timber, is four flame heights; 30 ft is about one flame-height clear of a fully-involved stack of perhaps 10-12 ft (this record's guess), a rule of thumb for the ignition of timber that no page read gives. No premodern figure for a gap round a charcoal store was found. What Edo did with a combustible stock it feared was to move the trade: after the fire of 1641, whose spread was blamed on the high stacks of timber dealers scattered through the town, the dealers were gathered onto Eitaijima. So the maps keep no measured gap round a charcoal yard; how far a yard stands from the houses is this record's guess.
