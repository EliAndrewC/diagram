What stands on the punishment ground in town, and why is the beating not done there?

Sources: cangue-enwiki, five-punishments-enwiki (the bamboo beating as a punishment of the code), yamen-enwiki (the yamen's own runners applying it)

A seat of justice draws two grounds, sited by opposite logics; why, and the execution ground outside the settlement, are The justice works - why a county seat executes, and why the ground is outside. This question is the other one, the punishment ground inside. Its governing variable is foot traffic.

  • The beating is not here. The bamboo (笞 / 杖) was a punishment of the code, and applying minor punishment fell to the yamen's own runners, who stood on both sides of the court at a trial; our reading is that it was a COURT act done inside the yamen, before the magistrate's bench - which in our maps is the magistracy compound - while the 枷 jia (cangue) was the public part, worn for as long as several months in severe cases, under the magistrate's seal, for passers-by to see. So the street feature DISPLAYS; it does not try. It also draws no notice board: the settlement already has a kosatsuba, and the offender in the cangue is the display (that the boards carried the crime in writing is on no readable page).

The rule the map follows: the PUNISHMENT ground - a cangue frame, a flogging post and a kneeling stone on about 30 by 12 ft of tamped earth (the flogging post and the kneeling stone are this project's guess: no page read puts either on a public ground, and the Edo jail page places flogging on the jail's own premises) - stands inside the settlement at the market or the magistracy frontage, within about 60 ft of a street and never in the roadbed, inside the rampart where there is one, and carries no notice board of its own because the settlement's kosatsuba already stands.
