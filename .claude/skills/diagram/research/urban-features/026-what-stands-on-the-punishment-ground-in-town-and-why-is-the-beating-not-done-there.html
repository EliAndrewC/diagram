What stands on the punishment ground in town, and why is the beating not done there?

Sources: cangue-enwiki

A seat of justice draws two grounds, sited by opposite logics; why, and the execution ground outside the settlement, are The justice works - why a county seat executes, and why the ground is outside. This question is the other one, the punishment ground inside. Its governing variable is foot traffic.

  • The beating is not here. The bamboo (笞 / 杖) was a COURT act administered inside the yamen courtyard before the magistrate's bench - which in our maps is the magistracy compound - while the 枷 jia (cangue) was the public part, worn for weeks or months - as long as several months in severe cases - under the magistrate's seal, for passers-by to see. So the street feature DISPLAYS; it does not try. It also draws no notice board, because the crime text rides on the cangue and the settlement already has a kosatsuba.

The rule the map follows: the PUNISHMENT ground - a cangue frame, a flogging post and a kneeling stone on about 30 by 12 ft of tamped earth - stands inside the settlement at the market or the magistracy frontage, within about 60 ft of a street and never in the roadbed, inside the rampart where there is one, and carries no notice board of its own because the crime rides on the cangue.
