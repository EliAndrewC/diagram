• hinomi-yagura-kotobank - 「it was in the year after the Meireki fire (1658), when the shogunate's own standing fire brigade was set up, that one 3 jo (about 9 meters) high was built at a fire-brigade mansion; the fire watch kept lookout day and night, and on sighting an outbreak signaled with a drum.」 (translated; ) (Nipponica's entry on the page)
  • hinomi-yagura-tfd - 「it is said to have begun with the one built at a fire-brigade mansion when the standing fire brigade was organized in Keian 3 (1650). This fire-watch tower was about 9 meters high」 (translated; ) (the page dates the brigade to 1650, where the other two accounts give 1658)
  • hinomi-yagura-bousaihaku - 「The height was about 5 jo (about 15 m)」 (translated; ) (of the tower at the fire-brigade mansion of 1658)
  • hinomi-yagura-bousaihaku - 「by contrast, the fire-watch towers set up at the daimyo mansions and at each kido were made lower than the standing brigade's towers, were all black-lacquered, and the legs of a town tower were not allowed to be enclosed with boards all round」 (translated; )
  • hinomi-yagura-tfd - 「even to build a fire-watch tower, it was not permitted unless one was a daimyo of 80,000 koku or more」 (translated; ) / 「the standing brigade's tower was fitted with a great drum and a hansho bell, and two lookouts always kept watch, but the towers of the daimyo brigades and the town brigades had no lookout posted, and only a board-clapper or a hansho bell hung on the tower」 (translated; )
  • hinomi-yagura-kotobank - 「a tall tower or platform made by combining timber, iron posts and the like to watch for the outbreak of fire. The simple kind is called a hi-no-mi-bashigo.」 (translated; ) (Britannica's entry on the page; hi-no-mi-bashigo, "fire-watch ladder")
  • jishinban-jawiki - 「many jishinban huts were fitted with a ladder on the roof (a small-scale fire-watch tower) and a hansho bell」 (translated; )
  • kidoban-jawiki - 「Because the fire-watch tower (ladder tower) was beside the kido, they also had the part of striking the hansho bell when there was a fire」 (translated; )
