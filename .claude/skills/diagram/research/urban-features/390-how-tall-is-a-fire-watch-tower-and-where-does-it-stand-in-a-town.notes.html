• hinomi-yagura-kotobank - 「it was in the year after the Meireki fire (1658), when the shogunate's own standing fire brigade was set up, that one 3 jo (about 9 meters) high was built at a fire-brigade mansion; the fire watch kept lookout day and night, and on sighting an outbreak signaled with a drum.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明暦(めいれき)の大火の翌年（1658）のことで、幕府直属の定火消(じょうびけし)が設けられた際、火消屋敷に高さ3丈（約9メートル）のものが建てられ、昼夜、火の番が見張り、出火を認めると太鼓で合図した。」) (Nipponica's entry on the page)
  • hinomi-yagura-tfd - 「it is said to have begun with the one built at a fire-brigade mansion when the standing fire brigade was organized in Keian 3 (1650). This fire-watch tower was about 9 meters high」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「慶安３（1650）年に定火消（じょうびけし）が組織されたとき、火消屋敷に建てられたのがはじまりとされています。この火の見櫓の高さは約９メートル」) (the page dates the brigade to 1650, where the other two accounts give 1658)
  • hinomi-yagura-bousaihaku - 「The height was about 5 jo (about 15 m)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「高さは５丈ほど（１５ｍほど）」) (of the tower at the fire-brigade mansion of 1658)
  • hinomi-yagura-bousaihaku - 「by contrast, the fire-watch towers set up at the daimyo mansions and at each kido were made lower than the standing brigade's towers, were all black-lacquered, and the legs of a town tower were not allowed to be enclosed with boards all round」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「これに対し、大名屋敷や各木戸に設けられた火の見櫓は、定火消の櫓よりも低くされており、すべて黒塗りで、町方の櫓の脚は周囲を板で囲うことは許されなかった。」)
  • hinomi-yagura-tfd - 「it was not permitted unless one was a daimyo of 80,000 koku or more」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「八万石以上の大名でなければ許されない」) / 「the standing brigade's tower was fitted with a great drum and a hansho bell, and two lookouts always kept watch, but the towers of the daimyo brigades and the town brigades had no lookout posted, and only a board-clapper or a hansho bell hung on the tower」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「定火消の櫓には大きな太鼓と半鐘が設けられ、常に２人の見張番が監視していましたが、大名火消や町火消の櫓には、見張番を置かず、櫓に板木（ばんぎ）や半鐘が下げられているだけでした。」)
  • hinomi-yagura-kotobank - 「a tall tower or platform made by combining timber, iron posts and the like to watch for the outbreak of fire. The simple kind is called a hi-no-mi-bashigo.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「火災発生を見張るために木材や鉄柱などを組合せてつくった高い塔，または台。簡単なものは火の見ばしごという。」) (Britannica's entry on the page; hi-no-mi-bashigo, "fire-watch ladder")
  • jishinban-jawiki - 「many jishinban huts were fitted with a ladder on the roof (a small-scale fire-watch tower) and a hansho bell」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「自身番屋の多くには、屋根に梯子（小規模な火の見櫓）や半鐘が備えられていた」)
  • kidoban-jawiki - 「Because the fire-watch tower (ladder tower) was beside the kido, they also had the part of striking the hansho bell when there was a fire」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「火の見櫓（梯子櫓）は木戸の側にあるため、火事があった時には半鐘を打つ役割もあり」)
