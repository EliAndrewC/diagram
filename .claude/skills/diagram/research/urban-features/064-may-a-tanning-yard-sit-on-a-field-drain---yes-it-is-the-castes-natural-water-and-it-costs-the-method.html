May a tanning yard sit on a field drain - yes, it is the caste's natural water, and it costs the method

Sources: mizuarasoi-jawiki, suiriken-jawiki, himeji-shironameshi-jawiki, chouara-tannery-enwiki, tanning-leather-enwiki

Why a tanning yard needs water at all, and how many pits it runs, is Tanning yards - hides come from fallen draft stock, and water is the gate; why its discharge must stay below every paddy intake is Why must tannery effluent never reach a paddy intake. This question is the yard with no stream near its quarter.

  • WHY A YARD MAY SIT ON A FIELD DRAIN AT ALL, AND WHY THAT IS THE CASTE'S NATURAL WATER (GM question 2026-08-08, of Hoshizora: "the tanning yard is next to a drainage ditch of a farm field - is that realistic as a source of water for a tannery?"). Hoshizora has no stream near its burakumin quarter (the valley watercourse runs across the far west), so its yard takes the northeast comb's drain. Three separate findings say that is right, and one says what it costs.

  • Water RIGHTS, not water quality, is the strongest argument - and it was the one nobody had written down. In Japanese irrigation communities (the Chinese record has not been read on this) an upstream tap was communal property held by custom, allocated by the village association and fought over in the courts. A kawata quarter would never be granted a headwater offtake (this page's inference from the caste's standing). The water an outcast community could actually hold is the water nobody upstream is fighting over: the tail of the system, below every paddy intake. So a yard on a field drain is not the map settling for second best when no stream is handy - it is the sociologically correct water for this caste, and it is the literal sense of kawaramono, "riverbed people": not people who happened to live by water, but people whose only claim was to the part of the watercourse nobody else wanted. Read that way, a ditch-fed yard is more attested than a yard on a good stream reach would be.
  • It is also the only siting that automatically satisfies the salt rule. A drain is the one course on a map where every intake is upstream by construction, which is exactly what the two intake rules demand. The residual risk is real but off-map: paddy drainage is commonly RECAPTURED by lower fields in both traditions (a "drain" at one level is a "supply" at the next), so a ditch-fed yard is only honest where the drain reaches a genuine sink - off the frame, a marsh, or a drainage tameike - rather than another comb's head. Hoshizora's NE drain leaves the east edge of the map.
  • What it COSTS, which should not be elided: the method changes. A ditch-fed yard is not a cosmetic variant of a stream-fed one. Shironameshi stakes hides in a CURRENT for several days and lets river bacteria do the unhairing; a drain has no current, so a ditch-fed yard ponds its own soak behind a gated intake cut and is running pit-soaking, the standing-vat soak other tanning traditions used (Fez's stone vats are the case read), rather than the Japanese one these maps otherwise commit to. That is a downgrade, not an equivalence - it is why the glyph draws a sluice gate instead of staking frames, and it is the honest thing to say if a PC asks a Hoshizora tanner why his leather is not Ubame's.

  • And it makes the yard SEASONAL in a way a stream-fed yard is not. A paddy drain runs during the irrigation season and is near-dry from harvest to transplanting, with its heaviest flush at the pre-harvest drawdown. Carcasses arrive on the opposite schedule: draft stock dies most in late winter and early spring, when the fodder is gone and the animal is worn from the autumn hauling. The two do not have to be reconciled, because raw hides were salt-cured to keep until they could be worked - a wet-salted pack stands a month - and that curing is how the hide trade bridged the gap - but it means a ditch-fed yard is a summer works whose pits stand empty half the year, while its winter is spent salting hides down and doing the clean in-town leather craft. Good adventure-hook texture, and it is why the small pit count is not the only thing keeping this from reading as an industry.

The rule the map follows: a yard with no stream near its quarter may take a field drain as its water. It is drawn with a sluice gate on a gated intake cut rather than staking frames, and it is drawn only where the drain reaches a genuine sink - off the frame, a marsh or a drainage tameike - which the downstream rule of the salt question enforces on the map.
