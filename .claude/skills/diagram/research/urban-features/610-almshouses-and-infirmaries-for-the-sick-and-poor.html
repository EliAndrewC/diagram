Almshouses and infirmaries for the sick and poor

The Song dynasty ordered a poorhouse and an infirmary for every county, and for every walled town or market of a thousand households or more, to feed and house the old, the sick, orphans and beggars; the infirmaries were run by monks recruited for them. Medicine was sold from the official pharmacies, which a Song account of Hangzhou names among the city's famous shops.

Japan kept fewer. A dispensary and an almshouse were founded at a temple in the eighth century, and in the Edo period the shogun's city kept one infirmary, a single longhouse with beds for forty.

  • The Northern Song capital kept four almshouses, feeding three hundred a day.
    The capital had of old an east and a west almshouse for the old, the sick, orphans, the destitute and beggars. The emperor Yingzong added a south and a north one and enlarged the buildings of the first two, and the four together fed three hundred a day.
  • Early in the Chongning era (1102-1106) the state set up a poorhouse and an infirmary.
    The minister Cai Jing set up the juyangyuan, a poorhouse, and the anjifang, an infirmary run by monks recruited for it; a monk who cured a thousand in three years was rewarded with the purple robe.
  • Every county, and every larger town or market with an official, was to keep a poorhouse, an infirmary and a pauper cemetery.
    Every walled town, stockade, garrison post and market of a thousand households or more that had a supervising official was to add the three, following the counties.
  • Southern Song Hangzhou's official pharmacies are listed among the city's famous shops.
    A list of the city's famous shops names the prepared-medicine bureau at Huimin ward and the Hemin Huimin pharmacy beside a cooked-meat shop and a private medicine shop.

Japan's relief houses were founded in its capitals, a few at a time.

  • Japan's first dispensary and almshouse were set up at a temple in 723.
    They were founded at the Kofukuji temple, and in 730 a dispensary was set up in the empress's household; the dispensary gave medicine and treatment, the almshouse cared for the sick, orphans and the poor.
  • Edo opened an infirmary in 1722 inside the shogunate's herb garden.
    The Koishikawa Yojosho was a shingle-roofed longhouse with two rooms for preparing medicine and meals, and beds for forty, the number later raised as it was needed. No page we read gives its floor area, or that of any other relief house.
