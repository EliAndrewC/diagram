• momisuriki-jawiki - 「Hulling with the clay mill produced many broken grains (imperfect rice).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「土臼による籾すりは、多くの砕け米（不完全米）を発生させていた。」); 「Because broken rice could not be shipped, the farm households made it into rice flour and ate it themselves as flour-rice and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「砕け米は出荷できないため、農家により米粉にされ粉飯などにより自家消費されていた。」) (that the hulling itself was the farm's is this record's reading of the two sentences)
  • kotobank-tsukigomeya - 「Tsukigome is the work of polishing rice; in the early modern period those who made it their specialty arose, were called tsukigomeya, and took charge of the last stage of the rice trade.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「搗米は精米する作業で，近世にはこれを専業とするものが成立し，搗米屋と呼ばれて米穀流通の最終過程を担当した。」); 「In Edo, as of 1744 (Enkyo 1), there were 18 guilds, some 2,100 tsukigomeya, doing business using some 5,500 mortars.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸には1744年（延享1）当時18組，およそ2100人の搗米屋が存在し，臼5500ほどを使って営業していた。」); 「Also, from 1744 on, it was forbidden to polish rice in the villages and sell it in the city of Edo.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また1744年以降は，農村で白米にして江戸市中へ売ることも禁じられていた。」) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, by Ito Yoshikazu)
  • seimai-jawiki - 「the karausu, which pounded rice by treading on it on the principle of a see-saw, came to be used」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「足踏み作業でシーソーの原理により米を搗く「唐臼」が利用されるようになった」) (of the Edo period, after the clay mill of the Genroku era)
  • seimai-jawiki - 「it is said that at the time several waterwheels were set on the Onden river」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「当時、穏田川にはいくつか水車が設置されていたという」); 「The waterwheel's power moves pestles up and down and polishes brown rice.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水車の水力で杵を上下させ、玄米を精白する。」) (the caption to Hokusai's "Waterwheel at Onden" from the Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, of the 1830s)
