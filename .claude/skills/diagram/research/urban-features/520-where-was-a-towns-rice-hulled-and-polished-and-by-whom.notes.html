• momisuriki-jawiki - 「Hulling with the clay mill produced many broken grains (imperfect rice).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Because broken rice could not be shipped, the farm households made it into rice flour and ate it themselves as flour-rice and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the article sets these two sentences just after the modern hullers of early Showa: 「In early Showa modern hulling machines were developed, and from around Showa 10 they began to spread nationwide」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) - so they describe the clay-mill era up to about 1935, not the Edo period in particular)
  • nta-nengumai-genmai - 「The basis was to pay the annual tax in brown rice. Not only for the tax: in the Edo period the word "rice" meant brown rice. Both the rice that commoners paid to their lords as tax and the rice that commoners shipped as merchandise were rice in the state of brown rice, threshed and hulled (momisuri).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • nta-nengu-nonyu - 「The annual tax was the village's responsibility until it was delivered to the storehouse the lord named (tsumemai).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「In Dewa province the tax rice, packed in bales, was put in the village storehouse near the village, and the next spring was carried to the town of Sakata and loaded onto coasting ships.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-tsukigomeya - 「Tsukigome is the work of polishing rice; in the early modern period those who made it their specialty arose, were called tsukigomeya, and took charge of the last stage of the rice trade.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「In Edo, as of 1744 (Enkyo 1), there were 18 guilds, some 2,100 tsukigomeya, doing business using some 5,500 mortars.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Also, from 1744 on, it was forbidden to polish rice in the villages and sell it in the city of Edo.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, by Ito Yoshikazu)
  • seimai-jawiki - 「In the Genroku era of the Edo period the tsuchiusu, which polished rice by turning it by hand, was introduced from Qing, and further the karausu, which pounded rice by treading on it on the principle of a see-saw, came to be used」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (of the Edo period, after the clay mill of the Genroku era)
  • seimai-jawiki - 「it is said that at the time several waterwheels were set on the Onden river」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「The waterwheel's power moves pestles up and down and polishes brown rice.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Katsushika Hokusai, Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, "The Waterwheel at Onden". It depicts a waterwheel that stood on the Onden river in Edo. The Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji is a collection of pictures of famous places of the late Edo period (the 1830s)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the caption to Hokusai's "Waterwheel at Onden" from the Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, of the 1830s)
