• hansho-kotobank - 「A bell struck to announce fire, natural disaster, thieves and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「火事、天災、泥棒などを知らせるために打つ鐘。」) and 「Before it, a thick wooden board (bangi) was used, and also for notices of meetings and the like; the hansho spread in the Edo period as fire brigades came to be organized.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「それ以前は厚板の板木(ばんぎ)を使って寄合などの連絡にも使っていたが、江戸時代に火消（消防組）が組織されるようになって半鐘が普及した。」) and 「It went from being mounted on a ladder to being set on a built tower, and those on iron towers still remain.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「梯子(はしご)の上に取り付けたものから、櫓(やぐら)を組むものになり、鉄製の櫓に取り付けたものはいまも残っている。」) and 「Many had a hut beside them housing a fire pump and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「そばに消防ポンプなどを納めた小屋を設けたものが多い。」) (Nipponica)
  • hinomi-yagura-kotobank - 「In the Kyoho era (1716-36), one fire-watch tower was set up per 10 wards, and wards without a tower had a fire-watch ladder set on top of the self-watch house.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「享保年間（1716-36）には10町に一つずつ火の見櫓が設けられ，櫓のない町には自身番屋の上に火の見梯子が設けられた。」) (Sekai daihyakka jiten)
  • kotobank-hinoban - 「In the villages, young men's associations and five-household groups were engaged in firefighting until modern times.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「村方では近代まで若者組や五人組が消防に従事していた。」)
  • bousaihaku-hinomi - 「Most of the fire-watch towers one can see today are iron ones built from the Showa 20s to the 40s」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「現在目にすることのできる火の見櫓は、多くが昭和20年代から40年代にかけてつくられた鉄製のものだ」) (the Showa 20s to 40s are 1945 to 1974)
