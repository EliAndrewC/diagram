• hansho-kotobank - 「A bell struck to announce fire, natural disaster, thieves and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It is made of bronze, and is called a hansho because it is small compared with a temple's great bell.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Before it, a thick wooden board (bangi) was used, and also for notices of meetings and the like; the hansho spread in the Edo period as fire brigades came to be organized.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It went from being mounted on a ladder to being set on a built tower, and those on iron towers still remain.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Many had a hut beside them housing a fire pump and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Nipponica)
  • hinomi-yagura-kotobank - 「In the Kyoho era (1716-36), one fire-watch tower was set up per 10 wards, and wards without a tower had a fire-watch ladder set on top of the self-watch house.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Sekai daihyakka jiten)
  • kotobank-hinoban - 「In the villages, young men's associations and five-household groups were engaged in firefighting until modern times.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • bousaihaku-hinomi - 「Most of the fire-watch towers one can see today are iron ones built from the Showa 20s to the 40s」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Showa 20s to 40s are 1945 to 1974)
