Does a tanning yard need its quarter close by, and did the tanners sleep at the works?

Sources: jokamachi-wiki-corpus, eta-jawiki, danzaemon-jawiki, himeji-shironameshi-jawiki, shiniuba-jawiki

Why a town has a tanning yard and why it stands on the water is Tanning yards - hides come from fallen draft stock, and water is the gate. This question is where the tanners live relative to their works; which way out of town the works face is Which way out of town does a tanning yard stand.

  • WHERE THE QUARTER SITS RELATIVE TO ITS OWN WORKS (GM question 2026-07-27: "does a tanning yard need burakumin housing close by? Or would the tanners just sleep at the yard?").

  • Why an in-city quarter is right. Japanese castle towns mostly began with no circuit town wall at all (the sogamae that took in a whole town came later, and to some), so "inside or outside the walls" is barely a Japanese question - the kawata quarter sat at the town's EDGE, typically beside a river. Edo is the clearest case: Asakusa Shinmachi, the Danzaemon compound, sat well within the city as an enclosed precinct - fenced and screened by temples and shrines - holding the head's residences, storehouses, a shrine, a courtroom and a prison, with housing for the three or four hundred family members of its officials. China-first has fewer complications in this page's reading: no hereditary outcaste attached to the leather trades (China had debased statuses of its own, but not this one), tanners and butchers as ordinary artisans, and the noxious WATER trades in the extramural suburbs (guanxiang) below the water gate. Either way, quarter in-town and works downstream is the attested arrangement rather than a compromise.
  • The load-bearing detail: one caste, TWO work sites, and only one of them stinks. The soak/unhair/dry phase is riverbank work. Everything else the caste does - execution, constabulary and watch service, the actual leather CRAFT (sandals, and in this page's reading drum heads and armor lacing), lamp wicks - is clean, quiet, and done at home or in town. So the quarter follows the in-town duties and the yard follows the water, and a daily walk between them is not a defect. It is also why no satellite hut stands at the pits: the historical unit is a corporate community under a headman, not scattered outbuildings, and a lone outcast house away from its quarter is not a thing the record shows (this page's inference from the corporate form).
  • No, the tanners did not sleep at the works, and no watchman is needed. Nothing attests tanners bedding at the pits, and the setting does not need it: under shironameshi the exposed phase is several days of hides staked IN the river, which no watchman covers anyway. The security is not a person but kegare plus monopoly - a heimen who lifts a hide has handled death-pollution and has nowhere to sell it, because the burakumin hold the carcass right (danna-ba) and are the only legal channel for leather. Worth keeping straight for adventure hooks: stealing from a tanning yard is easy and nearly pointless, which is a more interesting fact than a guard post would have been.
  • And the smell argument runs the OTHER way. The 120 ft standoff exists to keep the stench off OTHER castes' houses, which is exactly why burakumin dwellings are already exempt from it. Distance-from-nuisance is a privilege of the clean castes; the quarter is inside the polluted zone by definition. Walking half a mile to escape your own trade's smell is a comfort a kawata household did not have. The exemption is deliberate and was paid for: counting their houses as something to stay away from inverts the institution, and it bites - with them counted, Hoshizora has no viable site for its yard at all.

A long walk to work is not a defect (GM ruling 2026-07-24). Tango's quarter is in-wall at the northwest and its yard is outside the southeast rim; Hoshizora's crematory is across town from its quarter. Moving a quarter closer to its work would be the wrong fix: being made to walk is one of the indignities the caste is subject to even in a Rokugan kinder to its underclasses than the societies it draws on. There is deliberately no rule pairing a yard with its quarter by distance.

The rule the map follows: a tanning yard keeps 120 ft from every dwelling, wall to wall, except the burakumin dwellings that are exempt by design; no hut is drawn at the pits, and nothing pairs a yard with its quarter by distance.
