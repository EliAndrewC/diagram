• kotobank-zaimokuya - 「Beginning with the timber merchants who gathered at Horikawa in Kyoto in the twelfth century, and with timber guilds (kiya-za) set up in the thirteenth century at Horikawa, Kizu, and Wakae-tsu in Kamakura in the Kanto, among others, the timber trade arose in places of consumption with good water transport.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「12世紀、京都・堀川に材木商人が集住したのをはじめ、13世紀には堀川、木津、または関東鎌倉和賀江津(わかえつ)に材木座（木屋座）を設けるなど、水運の便のよい消費地で、材木商いはおこった。」); 「The places where timber merchants lived together were called kiba.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「材木商人の集住地は木場(きば)とよばれた。」) (the Nipponica entry, by Endo Motoo)
  • zaimokuuri-jawiki - 「the Horikawa of that time is said to have been 4 jo (about 12.1 meters) wide, and able to serve as a timber pond」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「当時の堀川は、川幅が4丈（約12.1メートル）あり、貯木場として成立しえたという」) (of Heian-period Kyoto, after a history of Saga timber cited on the page)
  • kotobank-zaimokuya - 「The retail shops kept a number of sawyers (kobiki) to supply large and small cuts, and raftsmen (kawanami-tobi) to move the timber they had gathered.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「小売商店は、大割・小割を提供するために、何人かの大鋸引(おがひき)（木挽(こびき)）を、集荷した材木を移動させるために筏(いかだ)師（川並鳶(かわなみとび)）を抱えていた。」); 「Retail timber dealers lived scattered in every region.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「小売りの材木屋は各地に散住していた。」); 「Building was for the most part contract work, and master carpenters and contractors were each entrusted with their work by the timber dealers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「建築は大半が請負仕事であり、大工棟梁(とうりょう)や請負師は材木屋からそれぞれ仕事を委託された。」) (the Nipponica entry, by Endo Motoo; its raftsmen show the retail dealers it describes were ones on water, so the dealer in a town without a river is this record's reading of "in every region")
