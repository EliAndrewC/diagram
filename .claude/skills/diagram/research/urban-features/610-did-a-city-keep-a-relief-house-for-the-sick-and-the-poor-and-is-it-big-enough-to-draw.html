Did a city keep a relief house for the sick and the poor, and is it big enough to draw?

Sources: songshi-178-wikisource (the Song poorhouses and infirmaries), mengliang-lu-13-wikisource (Hangzhou's official pharmacies), seyakuin-jawiki (Japan's ancient dispensaries), koishikawa-yojosho-jawiki (Edo's infirmary)

The Song put one in every seat. The Northern Song capital kept an east and a west almshouse for the old, the sick, orphans and beggars, and a later emperor added a south and a north one and enlarged the buildings of the first two, the four together feeding three hundred a day. Early in the Chongning era (1102-1106) the minister Cai Jing set up the juyangyuan, a poorhouse, and the anjifang, an infirmary run by monks recruited for it, and every walled town, stockade, garrison post and market of a thousand households or more with a supervising official was to add a poorhouse, an infirmary and a pauper cemetery as the counties had. Medicine itself was sold from shop-fronts: the official pharmacies of Southern Song Hangzhou are named among the city's famous shops.

Japan had one in the capital, if any. A dispensary and an almshouse were set up at the Kofukuji temple in 723, and a dispensary in the empress's household in 730; the dispensary gave medicine and treatment, the almshouse cared for the sick, orphans and the poor. In the Edo period the shogun's city opened its infirmary, the Koishikawa Yojosho, in 1722 inside the shogunate's herb garden at Koishikawa: a shingle-roofed longhouse with two rooms for preparing medicine and meals, and beds for forty, the number later raised as it was needed. No page read describes such a house in a castle town below the capital, nor gives the floor area of any of them.

What it means for the map: two forms are attested, so a city rolls between them. In the Song form every provincial city and capital keeps a poorhouse and an infirmary, and a county seat keeps them too; in the Japanese form only a capital keeps one, a single longhouse standing in the ruler's herb garden, and a provincial city has none. Either way the pharmacy is a shop among the shops and is not drawn apart.

The rule the map follows: where a relief house is drawn, it is one longhouse about 25 ft deep and 110 ft long, or in the Song form two of them, the poorhouse and the infirmary, side by side in one walled yard. The size and the shared yard are guesses: forty sleepers at two mats each, doubled for the rooms and passages, and no page read says where the two Song houses stood or whether they shared a wall.
