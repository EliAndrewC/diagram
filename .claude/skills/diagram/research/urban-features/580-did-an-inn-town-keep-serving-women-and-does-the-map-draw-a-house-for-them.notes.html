• meshimori-jawiki - 「Meshimori onna, or meshiuri onna, were unlicensed prostitutes who existed at the post towns of early modern Japan (centered mainly on the Edo period).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「飯盛女（めしもりおんな）または飯売女（めしうりおんな）は、近世（主に江戸時代を中心とする）日本の宿場に存在した私娼である。」); 「In the Edo period prostitutes were allowed to do business only inside the pleasure quarters the rulers designated, the Yoshiwara of Edo and others, but the meshimori alone were half-tolerated under the name of 'servants of the post town'.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、娼婦は江戸の吉原遊廓ほか、為政者が定めた遊廓の中のみで営業が許されていたが、飯盛女に限っては「宿場の奉公人」という名目で半ば黙認されていた。」)
  • meshimori-jawiki - 「In 1772 limits were set of 150 each at Senju-juku and Itabashi-juku, 500 at Shinagawa-juku and 250 at Naito-Shinjuku.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「1772年には千住宿、板橋宿に150人、品川宿に500人、内藤新宿に250人の制限をかけている」)
  • meshimori-jawiki - of the three post towns at the foot of Mount Asama: 「At these inns and the like it was the custom to keep meshimori (also called 'white necks') as employees to entertain the lodgers, and as a result, at Karuizawa-juku and Oiwake-juku in particular, the female population greatly exceeded the male.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「これら旅籠等では、雇人として飯盛女（白首ともいう）を抱え、宿泊者を接待するのが例であり、結果、特に軽井沢宿・追分宿では、男性人口に比して女性人口が大幅に上回っていた」)
  • yukaku-jawiki - 「Besides these, in the Edo period, the places outside the licensed quarters where prostitutes gathered were the meshimori inns of the post towns (see meshimori onna) and the okabasho of the temple-gate towns and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「その他、江戸時代に公許の遊廓以外で遊女（女郎）の集まる場所に宿場町の飯盛旅籠（めしもり はたご）（飯盛女を参照）や門前町などの岡場所（おかばしょ）があった。」)
