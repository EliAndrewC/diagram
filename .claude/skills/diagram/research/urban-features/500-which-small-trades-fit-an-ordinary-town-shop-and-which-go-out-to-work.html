Which small trades fit an ordinary town shop, and which go out to work?

Sources: l7r-castes (the setting's list of the crafts), token-edo-shokunin (the craftsmen who went out to work and those who worked at home), kotobank-okeya (the cooper), kotobank-tatamiya (the tatami maker), kotobank-tofuya (the tofu maker), kotobank-sobaya (the noodle shop), kotobank-yakushuya (the apothecary), kusuriya-jawiki (a street of drug shops in Edo)

The setting's list of castes names these trades: coopers, weavers and every other worker in fiber, noodle makers, tofu makers, and the brewers of sake and soy sauce. The question for a map is whether any of them needs more ground than the front room of its keeper's house. Japanese towns once sorted their craftsmen by just that: the carpenter and the plasterer went out to work, while a cabinetmaker worked in his own workshop at home.

  • Cooper. From the early modern period the cooper both made and sold his tubs and barrels at home, and in the castle towns the coopers lived together in cooper streets; because hoops broke often, some also went out to mend them, walking the round of the towns and villages.
  • Tatami maker. Mostly worked at home, sometimes on the job.
  • Tofu maker. Shop sale began in the seventeenth century, and by the eighteenth there were tofu makers not only in the towns but in the villages around them; the shop sold over its counter and by peddlers carrying tubs.
  • Noodle shop. A shop that makes noodles and serves them or sells them, in the one place.
  • Apothecary. A house that compounded crude drugs and sold Chinese-style medicine. In the great city the drug sellers stood eave to eave in one street, Nihonbashi Honcho, from the Edo period on.
  • Carpenter. Works away from home, on the building he is putting up (above), so his own house needs no yard; how a town's timber reaches him is Does a town keep a timber yard, and does it need a river?
  • Rice dealer. Where was a town's rice hulled and polished, and by whom?

So every one of them fits the generic shop: the work and the sale share the keeper's house, and the trades that go out to work do that work away from it. What the record did not find is any of them with a yard, a kiln or a water supply of its own; none of the pages read gives a workshop's size.

The rule the map follows: the cooper, the tatami maker, the tofu maker, the noodle shop, the apothecary and the carpenter are ordinary shops drawn as the generic shop, never works with a glyph of their own. A castle town or a city may set one trade's shops together in a street, as the castle towns did with their coopers and Edo with its drug sellers.
