Where does a village's communal well stand - by the lane, or among the houses it serves?

Sources: kotobank-tsurube-ido (wells shared by a village or a group within it), kotobank-tsujiido (the shared well at the roadside), seiyo-karihama-ido (a surveyed village's wells, one to a handful of houses, paved round)

Both, and the record attests the two as different forms. A well was often shared - by a whole village or by a group of households within it. One kind stood by the way: the shared well at the roadside had a name of its own, the "wayside well". The other stood among the few houses that dug it. In Karihama, a village on the Ehime coast surveyed house by house, the wells were dug at about one to every five to ten houses, some 150 in all; a dozen of them were used in common, and round many of the common ones the ground was paved with stones, making a space at the wellside where the neighbors met. Karihama was short of water and drank from its wells and springs until piped water came in 1952, so it had more wells than a village on a stream would need (Wells - the research, and the deliberate liberty has the counts).

What was not found: no page read sets a village's well out on open common ground away from the houses, and none says how often a village well had a roof over it - the well house is defined, and one survives, but its frequency was not read. A well on a village's commons, away from its houses, is therefore a guess.

What it means for the map. Two forms are attested, so the siting is a knob rolled per settlement from the map's seed: in one village the common wells stand at the lane side, on the verge where the lane passes the houses; in another they stand inside the clusters of houses that share them. The rule the map follows: each settlement rolls from its seed whether its communal wells take the LANE-SIDE form (the wellhead on a lane verge, clear of the tread, within the reach of the houses) or the CLUSTER form (the wellhead in the open ground between three to ten houses, off every lane); a well still has a dwelling within about 95 ft at the one-foot grain . A communal well of either form carries a paved apron about 4 ft wider than its curb all round. The apron's width is a guess; the two forms are attested; a well out on open common ground is not drawn.
