How big is a town shop, and does it stand apart from its keeper's house?

Sources: machiya-shoka-jawiki (the shop as part of the house, and the Edo front shop's size), yashio-noma-yogyo (country trades kept by farming households)

It does not stand apart. The Japanese town house, the machiya, was a dwelling with a shop attached: the shop and the family's rooms were one building. In the countryside the same holds one step further - the shops of a market village were kept by households that also farmed, in the intervals of farm work, so the shop was a room of a farmhouse. A town's shop is therefore not premises of its own beside a merchant's dwelling; it is that dwelling's street front.

How big. In Edo the street lots were a standard 20 ken (about 120 ft) deep; the shop building on the street, the front shop, was held to about 5 ken (about 30 ft) deep, with back-lane tenements behind it on one side where the shop was about 3 ken (18 ft) wide and on both sides where it was about 5 ken (30 ft) wide. Smaller still, the ordinary Kyoto house at the end of the Muromachi period was about 2 ken by 2 ken. So an ordinary town shop, with its family's rooms, ran about 18 to 30 ft along the street and about 30 ft deep - some 540 to 900 sq ft on the ground. No surveyed frontage for a country town's shop was found.

What it means for the map. The shop glyph the maps draw, about 48 by 32 ft, is wider than most: its 32 ft matches the front shop's depth, but its 48 ft frontage is that of a large house, one and a half to two and a half times an ordinary shop's, and its area is roughly twice one. That is a convention, not a finding - the real ordinary shop is 18 to 30 ft wide and about 30 ft deep.

What the finding supports instead: an ordinary town or city shop is the street front of its keeper's house, one building about 18 to 30 ft wide along the street and about 30 ft deep, its width rolled along that band per building. It houses its keeper's household, so a shop is counted as a merchant or tradesman's house rather than as an empty building added beside one.
