Where does the execution ground stand - by the road, on the outcast side, clear of the graves and past the boundary stone?

Sources: eta-jawiki, suzugamori-jawiki, kozukappara-jawiki, teramachi-jawiki, senju-shuku-jawiki, dosojin-jawiki

Why a county seat has an execution ground at all, and why it stands outside the settlement rather than in the market, is The justice works - why a county seat executes, and why the ground is outside. This question is where outside: which road, which side of town, and how far from the graves and the boundary stone.

  • The governing variables. For the execution ground: the road and the direction pollution runs - NOT population and not the settlement's geometric edge. The ground exists to be seen by travelers arriving, so it hugs the busiest way out; kegare puts it on the outcast side - at the edge and by the water, where the quarter itself stood; downwind and downstream is this page's reading - which is why the ground is measured against the burakumin quarter (in the setting's canon, burakumin perform every execution that is not a samurai's).
  • A deliberate departure from literal reality, disclosed. The 400 ft clearance between the execution ground and the burial ground is a map-legibility floor, not a historical measurement. In reality the executed went into a pit AT the ground (Kozukappara's burials were haphazard enough that a memorial hall was founded beside it in 1667 for that reason) while the community's dead went to temple graveyards at the town's outer edge, in the temple quarter that castle towns gathered there - a different ground from the execution ground. Our maps are a few thousand feet across and cannot hold that, so the separation is compressed and the RELATIVE ordering kept honest instead: the execution ground is always the further-out, more polluted of the two. Analogy to this record's other pollution separations (the cremation ground and tanning yard demand 120 ft clear of dwellings, and two POLLUTED grounds need more separation than polluted-from-clean does) gives about 150 ft, and at 225 ft Nagahara's ground still read, in the rendered map, as part of the burial cluster beside it. 400 ft is derived from the picture rather than by analogy - at the coarsest scale we draw it is the distance at which the two are unmistakably two places.
  • Two rules, and why they are shaped as they are. (1) The ground stands ~120 real ft from a drawn ROAD but ~400 ft from a GATE: a road is a line the ground can hug (Suzugamori sat on the Tokaido), while a gate is a point standing in for the road that leaves it, and Edo's grounds were miles out along their highways. (2) The outcast-side rule tests DIRECTION only: radius is an artifact of where the wall happens to be, while the claim the research actually supports (downwind, downstream, the outcast side) is directional.
  • The boundary stone earns its place. 道祖神 dosojin / sae no kami stand at village boundaries and crossroads, and the etymology is the point: sae means "to block", and the deity's job is to stop intruders and calamity from ENTERING. That converts "outside the settlement" from a vague spatial claim into a stated ritual boundary - the ground is not merely far from the houses, it is on the far side of the stone that keeps pollution out - which is why the stone stands outside the wall. A dosojin inside the rampart bounds nothing.

The stone stands nearer the houses than the ground does, and a radial ordering could not say so (GM 2026-07-27). The ordering is measured to the settlement's EDGE and not to its center: a settlement is not a disc, so a ground tucked in beside a quarter on a different bearing can lie inside the stone's line for most of the town: at Ubame, 86 of 118 dwellings stood nearer the killing ground than the stone that is supposed to bound it.

The rule the map follows: the EXECUTION ground stands outside: past the boundary stone, within about 120 ft of a drawn road but about 400 ft out from a gate (a road is a line the ground can hug; a gate is a point standing in for the road that leaves it), 400 ft clear of the burial ground, off the farmland, and on the same side of town as the outcast quarter - a direction, never a radius. Its road side faces the road. The BOUNDARY stone stands on the road out, between the settlement and the ground: past the rampart where there is one, and 60 ft clear of the nearest dwelling's wall where there is not - not the 120 ft the ground owes, because that is a pollution separation and a boundary stone pollutes nothing; it marks where clean ground ends and all it must not do is stand among the houses. And the stone stands nearer the settlement's edge than the ground does. Every distance here is wall to wall, never center to center. The 60 ft around the stone is this project's own figure.
