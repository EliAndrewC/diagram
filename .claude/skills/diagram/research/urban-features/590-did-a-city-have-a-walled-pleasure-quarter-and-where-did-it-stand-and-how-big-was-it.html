Did a city have a walled pleasure quarter, and where did it stand and how big was it?

Sources: yukaku-jawiki (what a licensed quarter was, how few there were, how most pleasure districts grew), shinmachi-yukaku-jawiki (Osaka's quarter, and the three great quarters compared), yoshiwara-jawiki (Edo's quarter: its wall, its sites and its size), wazi-zhwiki and goulan-zhwiki (the Song entertainment precinct)

The Japanese licensed quarter was rare. A yukaku was a district that gathered the licensed brothels and was closed off by walls or moats. The three great quarters were Osaka's Shinmachi, Kyoto's Shimabara and Edo's Yoshiwara, and besides them there were licensed quarters in only twenty-odd places across the country. Most pleasure districts grew up without any plan; a quarter set apart from the town and closed off by barriers or moats was a minority, a special case. Where there was no licensed quarter, the trade went on at the post-town inns and the okabasho (the question before this one).

Even the three great quarters took three forms. Shinmachi lay inside the town, with seven gates and a board fence on its east side only; Shimabara lay at the town's edge, with two gates and an earthen wall all round; the Yoshiwara had a single gate and was cut off from the town by its wall and the fields about it. The Yoshiwara's one official approach was a road from the embankment to its great gate, and past the gate lay the quarter, shut in by a high wall and a ditch, the ohaguro-dobu.

Where they stood: on new ground at the edge, and pushed outward as the city grew. Osaka's quarter was laid out in 1627 on newly opened ground, gathering brothels that had been scattered through the town, and was called the west quarter because it lay west of the castle town. Edo's first quarter was a block near Nihonbashi on reed ground by the bay, remote for the Edo of the time; as the city spread until daimyo mansions stood beside it, it was ordered in 1656 to move behind the Senso-ji temple, to the embankment at Nihonzutsumi.

How big. The Yoshiwara's first site was 2 cho, about 220 m (720 ft), square; the page gives the second site both as 3 cho square and as a little over 20,000 tsubo (about 16 acres, a square some 840 ft on a side), figures that do not agree; the smaller is taken here. Osaka's quarter held seven blocks and, in the Genroku era (1688-1704), more than 800 courtesans; no page read gives its area.

No page read gives the Song dynasty a walled quarter of this kind. Its cities' entertainment precincts, the washe, rose under the emperors Renzong and Shenzong: within them railings or cloth curtains parted the performers from the audience, and among them were shops for clothes, toys, sundries, tea, wine and haircuts. The word goulan, which named their performance halls, came to mean a brothel from the Ming on. No page read describes a walled brothel quarter in a Chinese city.

What the setting says. The setting's own notes put geisha houses in a city - one tale sets a young clerk's job at "the largest geisha house in the district, where gambling and opium and prostitution are abundant" - and the history of the Crab and Crane speaks of "the illegal prostitution of geisha". In Rokugan, then, the trade is unlicensed and goes on in the geisha houses, and nothing in the notes sets out a licensed quarter.

What it means for the map: no map draws a walled pleasure quarter. A capital's entertainment district stays what it is - teahouses, geisha houses and playhouses among the townspeople's streets - which is the setting's form and also the form most Japanese pleasure districts took. This is a deliberate deviation for a capital, where Edo, Kyoto and Osaka each had a licensed quarter, and it follows the setting's notes. Should the GM rule that a capital licenses a quarter, the history offers three forms to roll between per capital - inside the town with a fence on one side, at the town's edge with an earthen wall, or cut off beyond the town by a wall and a ditch with a single gate - each on new ground, between the Yoshiwara's two sites in size, 720 to 840 ft square.
