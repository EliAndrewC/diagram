Roofed playhouses (shibai goya)

Roofed theaters with seating, built for kabuki, arose late in the Edo period in Edo, Kyoto and Osaka, and reached the other cities only after the Meiji restoration. A great city kept a handful, licensed by the authorities and standing together: Edo had three, Osaka five. The oldest that survives, in the pilgrimage town of Kotohira, is about 80 by 145 ft.

  • The oldest surviving playhouse measures about 80 by 145 ft.
    The Kanamaruza, in the temple-gate town of Kotohira, measures 13 ken 2 shaku across its front by 24 ken 2 shaku deep at ground level, with a revolving stage 2 ken (about 12 ft) across.
  • Was a city playhouse the same size?
    The Kanamaruza was raised in 1835 on the model of the Onishi Shibai, one of the theaters of Dotonbori in Osaka (by one account the Chikugo-za), so its measures are the nearest we have to a city playhouse of its day. No page we read measures one of the city theaters themselves.
  • Before the Kanamaruza, Kotohira put up a temporary hut for each run of plays.
    It did so about three times a year.
  • Roofed playhouses reached the smaller towns only in the 1900s.
    After the Meiji restoration playhouses came to be built in every city, and in small and middling towns from the 1900s.
  • Edo's playhouses were thinned to four by the 1670s and to three from 1714.
    Edo had many playhouses at first; by the 1670s the shogunate permitted only four, and from 1714 three.
  • Osaka's Dotonbori had five theaters, licensed from 1653.
    The five began with five theater licenses recognized in 1653.
  • A city's playhouses stood together on one street.
    Two of Edo's three faced the same street, a stone's throw apart.
  • In 1842 Edo's playhouses were moved far outside the outer moat.
    After the fire of 1841 the shogunate moved them, with the puppet theaters, to a site of over 10,000 tsubo, about 8 acres, at Saruwaka-cho in Asakusa.

Song China's great cities held their performance halls inside the entertainment precincts.

  • A Song performance hall in a great city could hold audiences of several thousand.
    The Chinese Wikipedia article on the halls, the goulan, says so, but no page we read gives a hall's measures.
  • One entertainment precinct could hold several halls, and a large one more than fifty.
    The halls were the chief venues of Song and Yuan drama in the cities.
