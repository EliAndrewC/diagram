• kotobank-gogura - 「A storehouse set up in villages in the Edo period to hold tax rice, or grain stored against bad harvests, among other uses.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Not every village had one: there were also cases where several villages shared one, or where the headman's storehouse was used in its place.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten)
  • kotobank-gogura - 「After harvest, tax rice was put in each village's gogura and then shipped on - on shogunal land to the shogunate's stores in Edo and Osaka, on domain land to the domain granary or market.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「From the mid-Edo period, besides this temporary storage of tax rice, it was also used as a reserve-grain store as famine-relief policy developed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「At first village officials' private storehouses were often used, but after irregular releases of grain came to light, the shogunate in 1789 (Kansei 1) ordered gogura built at the village's expense.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「The storehouse was entrusted to village officials, and deposits and releases were entered in books.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Yamakawa Nihonshi shojiten)
  • kotobank-gogura - 「The gogura is attached within the village's dwellings; mostly it is built on dry-field land; where there is a vacant house site or the like, it is also built on a house plot.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (quoted in the dictionary from Jikata hanreiroku, 1794, book 6)
  • yabu-yamaji-gogura - 「In 1790 (Kansei 2), the Edo shogunate, as a famine measure, ordered gogura and kakoigura built to store grain against bad harvests.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • yabu-yamaji-gogura - 「At Yamaji a single gogura building stands, its door turned to the old former prefectural road.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It is a two-story building with a tiled roof, 340 centimeters across the front and 290 centimeters along the side, and 500 centimeters high to the roof.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • nasushiobara-kazugamuro-gogura - 「This gogura is one of the grain storehouses the Kurobane domain built in its villages in 1769 against famine; it was managed mainly by village officials.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It was graded upper, middle and lower according to the village's population and stored barnyard millet and foxtail millet; it seems about 2 sho (3.6 liters) of barnyard millet per household was laid up each year.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • okayama-u-gogura-list - 「one of the 96 gogura that once stood on Sado」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「on receiving the shogunate's order to store food, one was built per aza (hamlet section) on the intendant's order (holding 10 koku)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「one of 8 gogura built jointly by villagers within the Nikko shrine estate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the second passage is the page's entry for the Hataya gogura, Katashina village, Gunma)
  • kotobank-shaso - 「At harvest farmers delivered grain in proportion to their holdings; it was stored by district against famine in bad years.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「In normal years the stored grain was lent to farmers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It is said to have been first carried out in the Aizu domain in 1655, based on Zhu Xi's shecang method.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten)
  • kotobank-shaso - 「Under Sui, they were set up per she (village), and the charity-granary grain was by law levied according to wealth.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • shecang-zhwiki - 「Shecang were mostly set in the rural she, and were also called xiangcang.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) and 「Shecang were mostly run by the people themselves; their grain was contributed voluntarily by local wealthy or ordinary households, stored in normal times and given out in relief in disaster.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • changpingcang-zhwiki - 「The changping granary, a state-run granary of ancient China, used to steady grain prices and for disaster relief.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) and 「Changping granaries were mostly set up in county seats and managed directly by the county magistrate」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
