• kotobank-gogura - 「A storehouse set up in villages in the Edo period to hold tax rice, or grain stored against bad harvests, among other uses.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、年貢米の保管、または凶作に備えての貯穀のためなどに村々に設置された蔵。」) and 「Not every village had one: several villages shared one, or the headman's storehouse was used instead.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「すべての村にあったわけではなく数か村が共同使用をしたり、名主の土蔵を準用することもあった。」) (Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten)
  • kotobank-gogura - 「After harvest, tax rice was put in each village's gogura and then shipped on - on shogunal land to the shogunate's stores in Edo and Osaka, on domain land to the domain granary or market.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「年貢米は収穫後，各村の郷蔵に納められたのち，幕領では江戸・大坂の幕府蔵へ，藩領では藩庫や市場へ回送された。」) and 「From the mid-Edo period, besides this temporary storage of tax rice, it was also used as a reserve-grain store as famine-relief policy developed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸中期以降は，こうした年貢米の一時保管に加え，備荒政策の展開にともない貯穀蔵としても利用された。」) and 「At first village officials' private storehouses were often used, but after irregular releases of grain came to light, the shogunate in 1789 (Kansei 1) ordered gogura built at the village's expense.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「はじめ村役人の私蔵を利用することが多かったが，不正な出穀が発覚したため，幕府は1789年(寛政元)村負担による郷蔵の建造を命じた。」) and 「The storehouse was entrusted to village officials, and deposits and releases were entered in books.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「蔵の管理は村役人にゆだねられ，貯穀・出穀の状況は帳簿に記帳された。」) (Yamakawa Nihonshi shojiten)
  • kotobank-gogura - 「The gogura is attached within the village's dwellings; mostly it is built on dry-field land; where there is a vacant house site or the like, it is also built on a house plot.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「郷蔵は村居の内に附、多分畑地に立る明屋館等あれば、やしきにも立る事也」) (quoted in the dictionary from Jikata hanreiroku, 1794, book 6)
  • yabu-yamaji-gogura - 「In 1790 (Kansei 2), the Edo shogunate, as a famine measure, ordered gogura and kakoigura built to store grain against bad harvests.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「寛政2年（1790）、江戸幕府は飢饉対策として凶作に備えて穀物を貯蔵する郷蔵や囲蔵を建てるように命じました。」)
  • yabu-yamaji-gogura - 「The Yamaji gogura stands alone, a single building, with its door facing the old prefectural road.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「山路の郷蔵は、昔の旧県道に扉をむけて一棟だけ立っています。」)
  • nasushiobara-kazugamuro-gogura - 「This gogura is one of the grain storehouses the Kurobane domain built in its villages in 1769 against famine; it was managed mainly by village officials.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この郷蔵は明和6年(1769)に黒羽藩が飢饉に備えて村々に建てた穀類貯蔵倉庫の一つで、管理は主に村役人があたっていた。」) and 「It was graded upper, middle and lower according to the village's population and stored barnyard millet and foxtail millet; it seems about 2 sho (3.6 liters) of barnyard millet per household was laid up each year.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「村の人口に応じて上中下に区分し、稗(ひえ)・粟(あわ)を貯蔵した倉庫であり、稗を1戸当たり2升(3.6リットル)程度、毎年貯えたようである。」)
  • okayama-u-gogura-list - 「one of the 96 gogura that once stood on Sado」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「かつて佐渡島内に96ヶ所あった郷倉の1つ」) and 「on receiving the shogunate's order to store food, one was built per aza on the intendant's order (holding 10 koku)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「幕府の食糧貯蔵の命を受け、代官の命令で一字に一棟が建てられた （10石を格納）」) and 「one of 8 gogura built jointly by villagers within the Nikko shrine estate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「日光神領内の村人が協同で建てた8つの郷倉の1つ」)
  • kotobank-shaso - 「At harvest farmers delivered grain in proportion to their holdings; it was stored by district against famine in bad years.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「収穫期に農民がその持高に応じて米穀を納め、それを地域ごとに貯蔵して、凶作年の飢饉などに備えた。」) and 「In normal years the stored grain was lent to farmers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平時には、その蔵穀が農民に貸し付けられた。」) and 「It is said to have been first carried out in the Aizu domain in 1655, based on Zhu Xi's shecang method.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「朱子の社倉法に基づいて明暦元年（一六五五）会津藩で実施されたのがはじめであるといわれる。」) (Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten)
  • kotobank-shaso - 「Under Sui, they were set up per she (village), and the charity-granary grain was by law levied according to wealth.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「隋では社(村落)ごとに設置され義倉米は富に応じて徴収されることが法制化された。」)
  • shecang-zhwiki - 「Shecang were mostly set in the rural she, and were also called xiangcang.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「社仓多设于乡社，也称乡仓。」) and 「Shecang were mostly run by the people themselves; their grain was contributed voluntarily by local wealthy or ordinary households, stored in normal times and given out in relief in disaster.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「社仓大多为民间自营，其谷为地方豪富或一般民家自动输供，平时积贮、灾时赈民。」)
  • changpingcang-zhwiki - 「The changping granary, a state-run granary of ancient China, used to steady grain prices and for disaster relief.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「常平仓，中国古代官办的粮仓，用以平抑粮价与赈灾。」) and 「Changping granaries were mostly set up in county seats and managed directly by the county magistrate」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「常平仓大多在县城中设立，由知县直接管理」)
