How do you get through a packed commoner quarter - and why is the path not a street?

Sources: this project's own measurement of the drawn town and city maps; roji-jawiki (the back alley of an Edo commoner district: where it began, how wide it was, what ran down it and who used it), netease-city-street-widths (a Chinese city's lanes, in a popular account)

A town's warren with no lane, alley or street in it at all is a defect, walled or not - without one it hangs straight off the trunk road with no way into it, and reads as a scatter of identical boxes rather than as fabric. The CITIES carry ways through their quarters - Tango 6 alleys and 8 streets, Minami 9 and 7, Nagahara 3 and 10 - and walled Hirameki 2 streets, against zero lanes, zero alleys and zero streets on each of the two UNWALLED towns, Hoshizora and Ubame.

What such a quarter actually had was not a street but the back alley, the roji. In Edo its mouth opened between the merchant houses fronting the main street, and between one row of back tenements and the next it ran about 3 to 6 shaku wide, 0.9 to 1.8 m, with boards laid down its middle over a small ditch 6 to 7 sun (about 7 to 8 in) wide. It was narrower than a side street, and almost nobody used it but those connected with the buildings along it; a gate with doors stood at its mouth, opened at dawn and shut at dusk. A Ming or Qing Chinese city's lanes, in one popular account, ran under 3 m. That is why the path is not a street: it is a private passage serving the houses on it, not a way anyone passes through, and it is never drawn or labeled as a street. How wide a town's street and its side lanes were is the ways page's question, and where a city's shops and its alley housing stand is the city fabric page's.

The rule the map follows: a packed commoner quarter of any size carries narrow back alleys between its house rows, one every few rows of the district's depth, drawn as a single-file way and captioned as paths if at all - never as streets, because each serves only the houses on it. In life such an alley was 3 to 6 ft wide with a board-covered ditch down its middle; the drawn width is a map drawing convention. A town's warren with no lane, alley or street in it at all is a defect, walled or not. How many rows apart is a map drawing convention chosen so the quarter reads as fabric at map scale, not a figure from a record.
