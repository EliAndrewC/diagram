• jishinban-jawiki - 「In Edo it was under the supervision of the town magistrate, and was set up in each machi of the townsmen's ground in the Kyoho era (depending on the size of the machi, two or three machi set one up jointly; this was called moai), and in September of Kaei 3 (1850), at the end of the shogunate, there are said to have been 994 watch houses.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸においては町奉行の監督下にあり、享保年間に町人地の各町ごとに設けられ（町の規模によっては2、3ヵ町で共同で設置。これを「最合（もあい）」という）、幕末の嘉永3年（1850年）9月には994の番屋があったと伝えられている」)
  • tsujiban-kotobank - 「a facility for keeping order set up mainly in castle towns in the Edo period」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代，おもに城下町に設けられた治安維持のための施設。」) / 「In Edo, by who bore the cost, they were divided into three kinds: the shogunate's public tsujiban, the wards' tsujiban (jishinban), and the samurai houses' tsujiban kept by daimyo and hatamoto.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸では負担者によって，幕府による公儀辻番，町による町の辻番(自身番)，大名・旗本による武家方辻番の3種にわけられる。」) / 「Of these, the samurai houses' tsujiban numbered 936 in the Genbun era.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「このうち武家方辻番は元文年間に936カ所あった。」) / 「Usually, in a tsujiban hut of 2 ken frontage (about 3.6 meters) and 9 shaku depth (about 2.7 meters), built by cutting open the wall of a mansion, the three arresting tools were kept, and four or five men were on duty day and night.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「普通は屋敷の塀を切り開いて建てた間口二間（約3.6メートル）、奥行九尺（約2.7メートル）の辻番所に捕物(とりもの)三つ道具を備え、昼夜4、5名が詰める。」) / 「There were tsujiban in Kyoto too.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「辻番は京都にもあった。」) (several encyclopedias on the page)
  • muramatsu-tsujiban - 「The general structure of a tsujiban hut seems to have been a frontage of 2 ken and a depth of 9 shaku, six mats in size, with a lean-to of 2 ken frontage and 1 ken depth set onto it, about ten mats together, and a sturdy thing with a tiled roof.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「辻番所の一般的な構造は間口が2間、奥行きが9尺で6畳の広さがあり、そこに間口2間、奥行き1間の下屋を設け、合わせて10畳大で、瓦葺のがっしりしたものだったらしい。」) / 「Judging from this map, the building of the Muramatsu domain's tsujiban was about 13.5 tsubo, far larger than the roughly 5 tsubo of other domains'.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この地図から推測すると、村松藩の辻番所の建屋は13、5坪ほどあり、他藩の5坪ていどに比べるとはるかに大きい。」) (a local historian's article on the Edo guard box of the Muramatsu domain; the 5 tsubo is the author's reading of an 1883-84 survey map)
