• kidoban-kotobank - 「these kido were set up one per machi in a large town and one per several machi in middle and small towns; in the case of Edo, their form was double doors hung between posts set about 2 ken apart, with a fence or board wall on each side running to the edge of the road, and beside it the kido keeper's watch hut. At night, from yotsu (10 p.m.) to mutsu (6 a.m.), they were shut, and passage was possible only through the small side gate (or wicket) under the keeper's watch」 (translated; ) (Heibonsha's entry on the page)

  • banta-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, those hired by towns and villages who kept night watch and watched over fires, sluice gates and the like.
