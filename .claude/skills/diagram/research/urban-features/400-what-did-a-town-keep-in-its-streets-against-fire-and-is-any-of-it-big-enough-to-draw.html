What did a town keep in its streets against fire, and is any of it big enough to draw?

Sources: tensuioke-kotobank (the rain-water tubs, where they stood and how big), hinomi-yagura-bousaihaku (how many liters a koku is), jishinban-jawiki (the gear kept in the watch house), edo-hikeshi-gakken (how a fire was fought, and where the firemen gathered)

What a Chinese city kept - Kaifeng's brick lookout, its barracks and its gear - is the city fabric's Chinese question; what an unwalled seat kept instead of a tower is the question beside it.

Water in tubs, at the eaves and the corners. Rain water was kept against fire in tensuioke, tubs fed from the gutters. They became general, it is said, after the Kansei era (1789-1801), and stood on roofs, at the eaves of houses and at the street corners; at a corner stood a large tub holding about 6 koku, with hand buckets stacked on top of it ready for use. The page's own conversion of those 6 koku, "about 108 liters", is out by a factor of ten: a koku is about 180 liters, so the tub held about 1,080 liters, and was, as a guess at its shape, about 3 1/2 ft across.

The gear, in the watch house. The rest was not in the street at all. The ward's watch house kept the fire gear inside it - the company standard, fire hooks, the hand pump, carrying buckets, ladders and well buckets - and when the bell was struck the ward's officers and firemen ran to the watch house, took the gear out, assembled and went to the fire together. The method was demolition: the houses downwind of the fire were pulled down to stop it spreading, and the hand pump mostly wet the men on the roofs against the sparks.

A fire pond. Nothing read describes a pond or cistern kept in a town street against fire.

What it means for the map. The one thing big enough to draw is the watch house, and the night-watch question sizes it. A corner tub some 3 1/2 ft across is about one pixel at the town tier's 3 ft to the pixel, and is not drawn; that is a map drawing convention of omission. No fire pond is drawn, for want of anything read.
