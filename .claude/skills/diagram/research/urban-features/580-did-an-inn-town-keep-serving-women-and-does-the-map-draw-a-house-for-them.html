Did an inn town keep serving women, and does the map draw a house for them?

Sources: meshimori-jawiki (who the serving women were, where they worked, how many were allowed), yukaku-jawiki (the unlicensed places beside the licensed quarters)

Yes - and they worked in the inns, not in a house of their own. The meshimori onna of the Edo period were unlicensed prostitutes of the post towns. Prostitution was otherwise allowed only inside the licensed quarters, but these women were half-tolerated under the name of inn servants, and the inns of the post towns kept them to wait on their lodgers. Outside the licensed quarters, the places where prostitutes gathered were the post towns' meshimori inns and the okabasho, such as the towns before temple gates.

How many. The shogunate tolerated them but capped them per post town: in 1772 it set a limit of 150 each at Senju and Itabashi, 500 at Shinagawa and 250 at Naito-Shinjuku At the three post towns under Mount Asama the women were so many that at Karuizawa and Oiwake the female population greatly exceeded the male. No page read gives the number at an ordinary post town.

What it means for the map: an inn town draws nothing more for its serving women. They lived and worked in the inns already on the map, so the inns are the feature; there is no separate house, quarter or gate to draw. A walled pleasure quarter belongs to a great city, and whether a city has one is the next question.
