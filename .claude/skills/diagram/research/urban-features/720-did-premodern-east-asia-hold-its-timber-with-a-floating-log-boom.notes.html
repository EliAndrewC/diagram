• tsunaba-nikkoku - 「A river harbor at a key point on the middle course of a timber-carrying river, where a strong holding rope was stretched across to stop the floated timber. Nishikori on the Kiso, Shimo-Aso on the Hida, and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木材運搬河川の中流の要所に頑丈な留綱を張り渡して、流送される木材をせきとめた川湊(かわみなと)。木曾川の錦織、飛騨川の下麻生など。」) (the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's definition)
  • nishikori-tsunaba - 「The pass of Toki Mochimasu dated the 16th of the 10th month of Oei 29 (1422), addressed to the magistrates of the Kawakami barrier of Mino province (Engakuji documents), reads "one hundred rafts of timber for the building of Shozoku-in, namely three ropes' worth from Kawakami to the tsunaba ..."; the Kawakami barrier means the barrier at Kawakami of Nishikori.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「応永二九年（一四二二）一〇月一六日の美濃国河上関々奉行人中宛の土岐持益過所（円覚寺文書）に「正続院造営材木筏百乗、但自河上至綱場三綱分也…」とあり、河上関は錦織河上の関をさしている。」) (that the tsunaba of the pass is Nishikori's is the dictionary's identification; the pass names no place for it, and what a "rope" of rafts counted, the dictionary does not say)
  • guazhi-jianggui - 「Above the boundary marker here, the water shall forever be where the upland sellers moor their timber; downstream buyers may not moor their rafts. ... Set up on an auspicious day in the last month of spring, Jiaqing 2.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「此处界牌以上，永为山贩湾泊木植，下河买客不得停簰。……嘉庆二年季春月榖旦立。」); 「The inscription is carved on a great boulder on the riverbank.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「碑文刻在河岸一块巨石上。」); 「... showing the weighty place Guazhi held in the Qingshui river's timber age.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「彰显了卦治在清水江木材时代举足轻重的地位。」) (Jiaqing 2 is 1797)
  • shiratori-chobokujo-jawiki - 「Fukushima Masanori is said to have had a great pond dug in Shiratori along the Horikawa ... and this is thought to have become the first timber yard.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「福島正則が堀川沿いの白鳥地内に大池を掘らせ…と伝えられており、これが初の木材置場となったと考えられている」); 「The timber felled in the Kiso mountains was made up into rafts, went down the Kiso river and out to sea, then up the Horikawa, and was gathered at the Shiratori timber pond.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木曽の山で伐採された材木は筏に編成され木曽川を下って海に出た後、堀川を上って白鳥貯木場へ集められた。」); 「From Keian 4 (1651) enlargement works were carried out again and again, and by the end of the Edo period it had reached 23,900 tsubo.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「慶安4年（1651年）以来繰り返し拡張工事が行われ、幕末には2万3千9百坪にまでなった。」); 「About Keicho 15 (1610)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「慶長15年（1610年）頃」); 「Fukushima Masanori and others set up a materials yard before the Horikawa was cut, which became the origin of the Shiratori timber pond.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「福島正則らが堀川開削前に資材置き場を設置、白鳥貯木場の起源となる。」); 「along the Horikawa in Atsuta ward, Nagoya, Aichi prefecture」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「愛知県名古屋市熱田区の堀川沿い」) (the fourth and fifth are one entry of the article's timeline)
  • nippon-fukagawa-kiba - 「It looks like a row of square islands, but at the center of each lot a large pond (an underwater timber store) was set. The dry land called doba was only round the edge of the lot, and the pond was joined to the waterways; timber made up into rafts, it is said, was brought in and sent out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「四角い島が並んでいるように見えるが、それぞれの区画の中心部には大きな池（水中貯木場）が設けてある。「土場」と呼ばれる陸地は区画の周辺部だけで、池は水路につながり、筏に組んだ材木を入出荷していたという。」); 「The section from Eitai bridge to Kiba, cut from the Ansei kaisei O-Edo oezu (published 1858, held by the National Diet Library).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「『安政改正御江戸大絵図』（1858年刊、国会図書館蔵）の、永代橋から木場までを切り抜いた。」) (the page's caption to the map it reads)
  • aba-jawiki - 「The aba is built with a main rope stretched from the far bank at a sharp angle to the stream, floats tied to the main rope, so that it catches the timber coming down from upstream and draws it to the near bank.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「網場の構造は、対岸から流れに鋭角の角度をつけて親綱を張り、親綱に浮材を括り付けて上流から流れてくる材を捕捉して手前岸へ引き寄せるようになっている。」); 「Until the Showa 20s, when land transport was still undeveloped, the transport of logs felled in the forests was almost all by timber floating on nearby rivers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「陸上の輸送手段が未発達であった昭和20年代まで、山林から伐採された原木の輸送は、近隣河川を利用する木材流送によるものが殆どであった。」); 「In the example of the former imperial Kiso forest (Nagano prefecture), one made of four ropes 20 cm thick of shirakuchizuru (sarunashi) vine and two steel cables of 6 cm was used.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「旧木曽御料林（長野県）の例ではシラクチヅル（サルナシ）で作られた径20cmの綱4本と6cmの鋼索2本で作られたものが使われていた。」) (the article's sources are of 1976, 1984, 1993 and 2020, and it gives the structure no date)
