• tsunaba-nikkoku - 「A river harbor at a key point on the middle course of a timber-carrying river, where a strong holding rope was stretched across to stop the floated timber. Nishikori on the Kiso, Shimo-Aso on the Hida, and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's definition)
  • nishikori-tsunaba - 「The pass of Toki Mochimasu dated the 16th of the 10th month of Oei 29 (1422), addressed to the magistrates of the Kawakami barrier of Mino province (Engakuji documents), reads "one hundred rafts of timber for the building of Shozoku-in, namely three ropes' worth from Kawakami to the tsunaba ..."; the Kawakami barrier means the barrier at Kawakami of Nishikori.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (that the tsunaba of the pass is Nishikori's is the dictionary's identification; the pass names no place for it, and what a "rope" of rafts counted, the dictionary does not say)
  • guazhi-jianggui - 「Above the boundary marker here, the water shall forever be where the upland sellers moor their timber; downstream buyers may not moor their rafts. ... Set up on an auspicious day in the last month of spring, Jiaqing 2.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ); 「The inscription is carved on a great boulder on the riverbank.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ); 「... showing the weighty place Guazhi held in the Qingshui river's timber age.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (Jiaqing 2 is 1797)
  • shiratori-chobokujo-jawiki - 「Fukushima Masanori is said to have had a great pond dug in Shiratori along the Horikawa ... and this is thought to have become the first timber yard.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「The timber felled in the Kiso mountains was made up into rafts, went down the Kiso river and out to sea, then up the Horikawa, and was gathered at the Shiratori timber pond.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「From Keian 4 (1651) enlargement works were carried out again and again, and by the end of the Edo period it had reached 23,900 tsubo.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「About Keicho 15 (1610)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Fukushima Masanori and others set up a materials yard before the Horikawa was cut, which became the origin of the Shiratori timber pond.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「along the Horikawa in Atsuta ward, Nagoya, Aichi prefecture」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the fourth and fifth are one entry of the article's timeline)
  • nippon-fukagawa-kiba - 「It looks like a row of square islands, but at the center of each lot a large pond (an underwater timber store) was set. The dry land called doba was only round the edge of the lot, and the pond was joined to the waterways; timber made up into rafts, it is said, was brought in and sent out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「The section from Eitai bridge to Kiba, cut from the Ansei kaisei O-Edo oezu (published 1858, held by the National Diet Library).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the page's caption to the map it reads)
  • aba-jawiki - 「The aba is built with a main rope stretched from the far bank at a sharp angle to the stream, floats tied to the main rope, so that it catches the timber coming down from upstream and draws it to the near bank.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Until the Showa 20s, when land transport was still undeveloped, the transport of logs felled in the forests was almost all by timber floating on nearby rivers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「In the example of the former imperial Kiso forest (Nagano prefecture), one made of four ropes 20 cm thick of shirakuchizuru (sarunashi) vine and two steel cables of 6 cm was used.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the article's sources are of 1976, 1984, 1993 and 2020, and it gives the structure no date)
