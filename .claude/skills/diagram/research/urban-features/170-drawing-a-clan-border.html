Drawing a clan border

Sources: nanbu-date-mounds-enwiki, kuniezu-enwiki (the inline list below), kotobank-genroku-kuniezu

Linear, demarcated borders were not foreign to early modern Japan: domains were already building a territorial order with agreed boundaries and mutual exclusion, evidenced by boundary disputes, boundary markers and map-making.64 The worked example is the Nanbu-Date border mounds - a series of earth mounds marking the ~130 km boundary between Morioka (Nanbu) and Sendai (Date), agreed between the two domains in 1642 after more than fifty years of dispute, and growing smaller and more closely spaced over time as the zonal frontier became a line; such markers were common under the Tokugawa.19665 Separately, the shogunate ordered every province to produce kuniezu 国絵図, provincial land maps66 with the boundaries drawn on them - the shogunate's own guideline for the Genroku survey had the boundaries drawn clearly and every variance from the older maps settled by inquiry of the intendants and lords before the map was made up - so putting the line on a map is itself the authentic act.170

The Ubame magistracy's boundary pillars are drawn at ~1-1.5 ft of shaft (up to ~3 ft with a plinth) - an estimate, not a read figure; a GUESS.

The decision. Draw the LINE, not the mound. The period's physically large border markers were earthen mounds, which is a different thing and not what is drawn; the Ubame magistracy uses ~3 ft boundary pillars as location markers. A mound is a structure, which everything would then have to stay clear of - the opposite of what a frontier magistracy wants, since its whole design stands its east wall ON the line with a parley room built into that wall. So the border is drawn as a line with no footprint, which nothing need stand clear of.

What determines it in reality: jurisdiction, and the need to have agreed it in advance. A border exists where two authorities have negotiated and recorded it - which is why the magistracy's parley room, with the line across its floor and kneeling mats on each side, is the architectural expression of the same fact this drawn line is the cartographic expression of.
