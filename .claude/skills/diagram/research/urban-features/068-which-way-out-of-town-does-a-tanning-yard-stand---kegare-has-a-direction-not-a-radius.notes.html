• kotobank-hisabetsu-buraku - 「What was specified as their residence was, it seems, generally the periphery of the castle town, a key point of traffic, or riverbank ground.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「その住所として特定されたのは，概して城下町の周縁部，交通上の要衝，河原の地であったらしい。」) (Heibonsha, Sekai Daihyakka Jiten; said of the leather producers under the Sengoku daimyo, called kawata), and of those daimyo 「The Sengoku daimyo who sprang up everywhere, to rule their domains, gathered their retainers and the merchants and artisans to live below the castle and built castle towns, enacted their own domain codes and pushed the separation of warrior and farmer, and, to hold a steady supply of the leather indispensable to securing their armaments, placed special protection and control on the leather producers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「各地に続出した戦国大名は，領国支配のために家臣団と商工業者を城下に集住させて城下町を建設し，独自の〈分国法〉を制定し，兵農分離策を推進するとともに，軍備の確保のために不可欠であった皮革を安定的に保持すべく，皮革生産者たちに対して特別の保護と統制を加えた。」) and 「The kawaramono made their chief living from odd jobs connected with executions at the riverbed execution grounds, and from the slaughter and cutting up of birds and beasts and the production of leather, with the riverbed as their workplace」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「〈河原者〉は河原の刑場での処刑に関する雑役，河原を作業場とする鳥獣の屠割や皮革の生産を主たる生業とした」) (said in the entry's account of the medieval period, before the Sengoku daimyo; the same sentence goes on to the Nanbokuchō-era garden-makers)
