The bell-and-drum tower (zhonggulou)

A walled Chinese seat of government marked the hours from a tower that held a great drum and a great bell - "morning bell, evening drum". The night was divided into five watches, and the tower struck them.

The towers stood out by their height rather than their plan: the one at Pingyao, a county seat, is about 38 ft square and 61 ft tall. Edo had time bells instead, most of them hanging at temples.

  • The City God temple was ranked down to the county from 1369.
    In 1369, early in the Ming dynasty, the founding emperor graded the City Gods of the empire in four ranks - capital, prefecture, department and county - and City God temples went up everywhere. That the drum-and-bell set stood with the magistrate's office (yamen), the Confucian temple and the City God temple as the standard fittings of a walled northern seat is this project's reading, a GUESS.
  • What did the tower signal besides the night watches?
    The five watches are recorded. That the tower also signaled the opening of the gates at dawn and their closing at dusk, which began the street curfew, and sounded alarms and ceremonies, is this project's reading, a GUESS.
  • A drum tower and a bell tower commonly stood as a pair, facing each other.
    The bell tower usually stood on the east and the drum tower on the west; at Beijing the pair stand north and south, the bell tower to the north.
  • A single building could hold both, as at Shanhaiguan and Tianjin.
    The Chinese Wikipedia article on bell-and-drum towers names both as single-building towers.
  • What form did a county seat's tower take?
    A drum tower, a combined bell-and-drum tower, or a market tower straddling the main street - this project's reading, a GUESS, from the forms recorded. Pingyao, a county seat, has one: its Market Tower on South Street, the only multi-storied pavilion in the old city.
  • Pingyao's Market Tower covers 133.4 square meters (~1,436 sq ft), three bays square.
    That is about 38 ft on a side.
  • Pingyao's Market Tower is 18.5 m (~61 ft) tall.
    It stands in the middle of South Main Street at the center of the county town.
  • Kawagoe's time-bell tower rises 16 m (~52 ft) in three stories.
    No page we read gives the size of its base.
  • Xingcheng, a Ming garrison city, set its tower on a brick platform 21 m (~69 ft) square at the exact center of the town.
    The platform, built in 1454, is pierced by a cross-shaped vaulted passage 4.85 m (~16 ft) wide and 3.3 m (~11 ft) high, so the town's two main streets run through it. Xingcheng is the best-preserved Ming garrison city.
  • An official compound kept a drum tower of its own, the qiaolou.
    The qiaolou held drums and horns to strike the hours and to receive guests. The one in Dai county stands at the heart of the town's crossroads, and Yuanzhou's formed part of its prefectural office compound.

Fire watch was a different office from timekeeping, in China and in Japan alike.

  • Kaifeng, the Northern Song capital, kept fire-watch towers apart from its patrol posts.
    Each ward had a patrol post every three hundred paces, and a fire-watch tower (wanghuolou) stood on high ground with a hundred soldiers quartered below it. The towers and their gear are at Fire watch towers and firefighting gear (hinomi yagura).
  • Were Edo's time bells and its fire towers separate institutions?
    Edo's time bell was run at public expense, unlike the bells of temples and shrines. The page says nothing of fire towers, so that the time bell stood apart from the fire towers (hinomi yagura) is this project's reading, a GUESS. In some towns, too, the fire tower's alarm bell (hansho) carried the time signal.
  • Most of Edo's time bells hung at temples.
    The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Kokucho time bell lists Edo's others, at Ueno Kan'ei-ji, Ichigaya Hachiman, Senso-ji, Shiba Zojo-ji and five more places; that most of them are temples is this project's reading of the list.
  • How far could a time bell be heard?
    Edo's Kokucho time bell collected a fee from the roughly four hundred blocks - about three hundred at first - within which it could be heard: 4 mon for each ken of a townsman's street frontage. On the block count that is a radius of about 1.2-1.4 km (~3,937-4,593 ft), this project's arithmetic, a GUESS, and a low one, since only the blocks that paid were counted.
  • Beijing's drum tower and bell tower struck the evening for an inner city about 6.5 km (~21,325 ft) by 5.3 km (~17,388 ft).
    Each evening the drum tower struck its drum eighteen times, then the bell tower its bell eighteen times, three rounds of each, 108 strokes in all. The inner city's walls ran 24 km (~78,740 ft) around; its sides are this project's reading of a map, agreeing with that perimeter.
  • Night watchmen walked the streets striking the watches.
    A watchman on his rounds struck a bamboo clapper, a gong or a wooden block through the streets and lanes to announce the time.
