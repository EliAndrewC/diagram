  • token-edo-shokunin - 「Carpenters and plasterers, who went out to work, were called deshoku; those who worked in a workshop in their own homes, such as cabinetmakers, were called ijoku.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-okeya - 「From the early modern period coopers appeared who worked at home, combining making and selling, and in the castle towns and such places they lived together and formed cooper streets and barrel-maker streets.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Because the hoops of tubs and barrels often broke, some went out to work at repairs, and some walked the round of the towns and villages.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, by Endo Motoo)
  • kotobank-tatamiya - 「They worked mainly at home, but sometimes went out to work.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Nipponica entry, by Endo Motoo)
  • kotobank-tofuya - 「In the seventeenth century shop sale began.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「In the eighteenth century they appeared not only in the towns but in the surrounding villages too.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Shop sale and peddling remain the same today too.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「A shop where a tofu craftsman (tofu-shi) makes and sells tofu.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「There was also peddling, the men carrying it in half-tubs slung from a shoulder pole, the women with a half-tub on their heads.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Nipponica entry, by Endo Motoo)
  • kotobank-sobaya - 「A shop that makes soba, or mainly noodles, and serves them to eat or sells them.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry)
  • kotobank-yakushuya - 「A house that compounds crude drugs and also deals in Chinese medicines.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry)
