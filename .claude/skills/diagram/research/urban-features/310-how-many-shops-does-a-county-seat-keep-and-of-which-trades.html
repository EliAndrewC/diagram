How many shops does a county seat keep, and of which trades?

Sources: yashio-noma-yogyo (the trade census of nineteen villages and of Hachijo, the largest of them), zaigomachi-jawiki (how large an Edo country town was), l7r-budgets (the setting's merchant households in a town)

The one place the record can count shop by shop is Hachijo, a village in what is now Yashio city that was the largest village within the present city's bounds and a key point of traffic. A survey of 1829 counted 856 inhabitants in 166 households there, and 66 of those households - four in ten - lived partly by a trade or a craft. They kept six taverns, three hairdressers, one bathhouse and three pawnshops. A fuller list of 1836 names the rest: a sake brewer, an oil presser who also took pledges, a bathhouse, two dyers, and more than a dozen small shops selling sake, vinegar and soy sauce with sandals, paper, tofu and sundries beside them. The liquor shops were general stores as much as anything, dealing in food and sundries too. Across the nineteen villages of the present city area, some 5,672 inhabitants in 1,034 households, about a quarter of the households lived partly by trade, with 45 taverns, 18 pawnshops and 13 hairdressers between them, and the one bathhouse at Hachijo.

Hachijo is a large village at a crossing of traffic rather than a seat of government, and smaller than the county seat of about 1,200 the setting draws; Edo's country towns - post towns, ports, and other towns that stood in law in the countryside - commonly ran from 200 to 1,000 households and 1,000 to 6,000 inhabitants. So Hachijo sits just below the small end of that range, and a county seat is a little above it. The setting's own tables give a town 120 merchants in 24 households; Hachijo's 66 trading households among 166 show that a place of this size carried a good deal more commerce than its merchant households alone, because households that farmed also traded or worked a craft between their farm work.

For a city the record found no trade census. The figure the city maps use - about one storefront per 25 to 30 inhabitants - stays this project's own estimate, and Hachijo, at one trading household per 13 inhabitants, suggests that estimate is sparse rather than generous.

What it means for the map: a county seat of about 1,200 carries on the order of 50 to 90 households that keep a shop or a trade - one for every 13 inhabitants at Hachijo, one for every 23 across the nineteen villages - a degree along a continuum, rolled per settlement. Most are small general stores for liquor, soy sauce and sundries, with a few taverns and hairdressers; one bathhouse, two or three pawnshops (often a sideline of another shop), one brewer, one oil presser and a dyer or two. Each ordinary shop is the front room of its keeper's house (the next question), so a shop count is a count of houses with a shop front, not of extra buildings.
