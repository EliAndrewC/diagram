Why are two or three troughs enough for several dozen oxen?

Sources: unl-beefwatch-cattle-water (how fast cattle drink), blm-oregon-trail-wagons (how long an ox team rested at a halt), caravanserai-enwiki

Where a stable yard's troughs stand and how many it draws is How does a stable yard water its animals - troughs beside a well, filled by relay. This question is why that is enough.

Capacity - why 2-3 troughs absorb several dozen oxen, and why MORE troughs is the wrong lever (GM asked, 2026-07-23: "there might be several dozen oxen passing through at a time... 2 troughs doesn't feel like enough"). It is enough, and adding troughs - the naive fix - does nothing. Edge capacity: each drawn trough is ~14 ft at ~2 ft of edge per drinking head (a guess), used from one side - so one trough serves ~7 head at once; the caravan ground's 3 troughs are ~20 simultaneous stations, a plain yard's 2 are ~14. Turnover: modern feedlot cattle drink ~1-4 gal/min, so a working ox's 5-8 gal session (this page's estimate) is over in two to seven minutes (a modern rate, taken as the animal's own) - the caravan cluster waters ~20 head every ~6 minutes, and several dozen oxen clear in two or three relays (~20-30 min). That fits how a halt worked: the animals were unhitched to rest - most for an hour or so, several hours in hot weather - so watering was a leisurely relay chore for a couple of teamsters, not a race. The binding constraint is the WELL, not the trough edge: a hand bucket-draw sustains maybe 5-10 gal/min (a GUESS - no source read gives a rate), so a train's 300-600 gallons (the figure is worked out at How does a stable yard water its animals) is half an hour to 2 hours of steady drawing NO MATTER how many troughs stand there. Extra troughs would not water anyone faster; they would just hold more standing water. What the troughs actually do is BUFFER - filled between arrivals so the first relay drinks instantly while the drawing continues - which is exactly why a caravanserai watered from one courtyard well or fountain (that this was INSTEAD of multiplying basins is this page's reading), and why we expect a coaching-inn yard to show a SINGLE trough (our 2-3 is already the generous end).
