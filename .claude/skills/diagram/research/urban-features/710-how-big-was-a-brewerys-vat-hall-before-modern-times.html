How big was a brewery's vat hall before modern times?

Sources: bunka-sugita-shikomigura and itami-okada-sakagura (two Edo-period brewery halls), bunka-hatsuzakura-shikomigura (a Meiji brewery hall), yashio-noma-yogyo (a village brewer), tsukurizakaya-jawiki (brewers as town notables with a shop at the center)

About 50 to 62 ft on its long side. A brewer of sake, miso or soy (sakagura; Chinese zaofang) worked a vat hall of fermentation tanks, a koji room, a rice-steaming area with a masonry stove and chimney, its own well and a rice storehouse, the hall standing behind a normal shopfront. The Edo-period halls measured are a fermenting store of the late Edo period in Tochigi, about 60 by 30 ft, and a brewery hall built about 1715 behind a shop in Itami, one of the great brewing towns, a two-story hall about 52 by 49 ft (15.7 by 14.8 m). A castle town's brewery store at Kanazawa, about 62 by 23 ft, is as long and narrower (the county town's brewery).

The long halls are modern. A hall of about 96-103 by 36-39 ft stands in Wakayama, but it is of the Meiji era (the record gives the plan in ken, and its stated area fits the longer ken), and no Edo hall that long was found. The GM ruled on 2026-09-28 that the maps draw nothing attested only in modern times, so the maps do not draw a hall that size: a vat hall is sized from the Edo halls. How tall the tanks stood, how big the koji room was and how much ground the whole compound took, no page read gives, though surviving small-town compounds are said to reach whole-block scale.

Where it stands, and how many. Such a hall has one to two times a shophouse's floor (this page's arithmetic), and would often have been the largest commercial building in a small town (this page's reading). Expect one or two a seat of about 3,000 (this page's estimate; a village could have one), sited in town with a solid shop at the center, because brewers were town notables.

The rule the map follows: a brewery's vat hall is drawn about 50 to 62 ft on its long side and about 23 to 49 ft across, the span of the Edo-period halls and stores measured, behind the brewer's shopfront; the Meiji hall of about 96 ft is not drawn. Where a city keeps a brewery, and what else stands in its compound, is Trade works.
