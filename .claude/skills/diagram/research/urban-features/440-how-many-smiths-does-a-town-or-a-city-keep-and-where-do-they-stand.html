How many smiths does a town or a city keep, and where do they stand?

Sources: ehime-kenshi-joka-shokunin (castle towns gathering their craftsmen into streets by trade), kotobank-kajimachi (the smiths' street, and Kofu's smith households counted), sharing-japan-gujo-kajiyamachi (Gujo Hachiman's smiths' and craftsmen's streets in 1692, and their house widths), ehime-kenshi-mura-shokunin (a large village's craftsmen in 1701), boso-no-mura-kajiya (one smithy's width)

The smiths' street. A castle town gathered craftsmen from elsewhere, gave them house plots, and set them in craftsmen's streets by trade, to serve the lord's retainers and the townsfolk. The smiths' street, kajimachi, is the one whose name survives in town after town. Kofu is one: in the Kofu domain's time, 28 of the castle town's 33 smith houses owing the lord smith's work stood on its Kajimachi, and a register of 1780 still puts 27 of 32 smith houses there, 26 of them working as smiths. A smaller castle town kept fewer. At Gujo Hachiman, a house register of 1692 lists 50 houses along its craftsmen's street and smiths' street; smiths were the largest trade there, with 8 houses, beside 5 doctors, 3 coopers, 2 horse doctors and a carpenter, a tatami maker, a lacquerer, a tailor, a dyer and a sake shop among others. Even a large village could keep several: the register of Kawanoe village in 1701 counts 6 smith households among its craftsmen.

The county town. The setting's own notes give the county town one smith of standing: among the local notables who send the county magistrate seasonal gifts are "the sake brewer, the master smith, the dye-house owner". So a county town keeps a master smith; whether others work under him or beside him the canon does not say.

Is a smithy bigger than a shop? On Gujo Hachiman's streets the houses ran from craftsmen's houses 2 ken wide, about 12 ft, up to large merchant houses. The one smithy whose plan was found is a village smith's, 3½ ken wide, about 21 ft, with its hearth on an earth floor inside. On this record's reading, then, a smithy is wider than the narrowest craftsman's house and well short of a big merchant's; setting a village workshop's width on a town street is a guess. A town smithy's own plan, and how many of a city's smiths made blades rather than tools, no page read gives.

What it means for the map. A county town draws its master smith as one smithy on the shop rows, about 21 ft wide. A city draws its smiths together on one street of its commoners' quarter, not scattered, since that is what the castle towns did. How many line that street is calibrated liberty, a degree between the two towns counted: about 8 for a small castle town like Gujo Hachiman and about 26 to 28 for one the size of Kofu. How a given city's size maps onto that range is a guess. Swordsmiths are not drawn apart from other smiths: no page read separates them on the ground.

The rule the map follows: a county town draws one smithy on its shop rows, a building about 21 ft wide and 18 ft deep that takes a shop-house's plot (the canon's master smith). A provincial city draws a smiths' street: its smithies stand together along one street of the commoners' quarter, between about 8 and about 28 of them (the two counted towns), the number rising with the city's size (the scaling is a guess). A farrier stands apart from them, where the farrier question puts it.
