• kidoban-jawiki - 「kido were set up in many castle towns, beginning with Edo」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸をはじめ、多くの城下には木戸が設けられており」) / 「and were to be shut at night」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「夜は閉じられることになっていた」) / 「at each of those kido there were two kido keepers, called "bantaro" or "banta"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「その木戸にはそれぞれ「番太郎」または「番太」と呼ばれる木戸番が2人いた」) / 「they were for the most part old men and lived in the watch hut」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「彼らは大抵が老人で、番小屋に居住していた」) / 「machi without a kido were to set up temporary kido」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木戸のない町は仮木戸を設置し」) / 「in the seventh month of Meireki 4 (1658)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明暦4年（1658年）7月」) (of the edict of 1658)
  • kidoban-kotobank - 「these kido were set up one per machi in a large town and one per several machi in middle and small towns; in the case of Edo, their form was double doors hung between posts set about 2 ken apart, with a fence or board wall on each side running to the edge of the road, and beside it the kido keeper's watch hut. At night, from yotsu (10 p.m.) to mutsu (6 a.m.), they were shut, and passage was possible only through the small side gate (or wicket) under the keeper's watch」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この木戸は，大きな町では1町単位，中小の町では数町単位で設置され，江戸の場合，その形態は，2間ほどの間隔で建てた柱の間に，両開きの扉をつり，両脇には道路際までの柵，または板塀がとりつき，そばには木戸番の番小屋があった。夜四つ時(午後10時)から朝六つ時(午前6時)までの夜間は閉じられ，木戸番の監視のもとに，脇の小木戸(またはくぐり戸)からのみしか通行できなかった」) (Heibonsha's entry on the page)
  • kidoban-kotobank - 「At night the kido on both sides of the machi were closed, and the keeper signaled passers-by on to the next machi with clappers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「夜間は町の両側の木戸を閉ざし，通行人は番人が拍子木で合図をして町送りにした。」) / 「The keeper also made night rounds against fire」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「番人は防火の夜回りも行い」)
  • kidoban-jawiki - 「The machi of Kyoto, from the Muromachi period, placed gates called kuginuki facing the crossings at both ends of the machi」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「京都の町々は、室町時代から町の両端にあたる四辻に面して釘貫とよぶ門を置き」) / 「In the Edo period the kuginuki came to be called kido-mon, and in a board-floored watch hut of about one tatami placed beside it, watchmen whom the machi hired from lower-status people served, two at night and one by day.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代になると、釘貫は木戸門とよばれるようになり、かたわらに置かれた畳1枚程度の板敷の番小屋に、町が下位身分から雇用した番人が、夜間は2名、昼間は1名勤務した。」)
  • shukuba-jawiki - 「Kido - set up at the ends of the post station (near the mitsuke), the ground between kido and kido (between mitsuke and mitsuke) being taken as the post town. Besides the kido, a standing night lamp was sometimes set up. And in most cases they were closed at night for crime prevention and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木戸 - 宿場の端（見付付近）に設けられ、木戸と木戸の間（見附と見附の間）が宿場町とされた。木戸の他に常夜灯が設置されている場合もあった。また殆どの場合、夜間は防犯等の目的で閉鎖されていた。」)
  • shukuba-mitsuke-kaido-walking - 「At the entrances of a post town were set up mitsuke, kido and a boubana.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「宿場の出入口には見附（見付とも）や木戸、棒鼻（ぼうばな）が設けられていました。」) / 「the mitsuke of a post town is an earthen bank built on both sides of the entrance with a fence set on top of it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「宿場の見附とは出入口の両脇に土手を築きその上に柵を設けたものです。」)
  • kidoban-jawiki - 「The watch hut was fixed at a beam span of 6 shaku, a length of 9 shaku, and an eave height of 1 jo, with the ridge height to correspond.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「番屋は梁間6尺、桁行9尺、軒高1丈とし、棟高はこれに相応する高さと定められた」)
  • jishinban-jawiki - 「The watch house was usually set at the south corner of a crossroads facing the main street running east and west. The rule was 9 shaku by 2 ken, but in practice they were about 2-3 ken」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「番屋は、たいていは東西の往来の大通りに面した四ツ辻の南側角に設置された。9尺2間というきまりだが、実際には2-3間ぐらいはあり」) / 「Both the kido watch hut and the jishinban were built against the kido set at the two ends of each machi's main street, the kido keeper at one and the jishinban at the other.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木戸番屋・自身番屋ともに、各町内の大通りの両端に設置された木戸に接して建てられており、一方に木戸番が、もう一方に自身番が詰めた。」)
  • jishinban-jawiki - 「In Osaka it was also called kaisho, and was not a separate building as in Edo but was built in line with ordinary townhouses」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大坂においては会所とも呼ばれ、江戸のように独立した建物ではなく、一般の町家と軒並に作られており」)
  • banta-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, those hired by towns and villages who kept night watch and watched over fires, sluice gates and the like. Many were of hinin status.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、町や村に雇われ、夜警や火事・水門などの番に当たった者。非人身分の者が多かった。」) / 「in some villages, guarding the hills, fields and sluice gates, running errands, night watch, controlling vagrants and so on」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「村によっては山野水門の警備や走り使い、夜番、浮浪者の取締りなど」) / 「elsewhere many were of hinin status and were also called ban-hinin」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「その他の所では非人身分の者が多く番非人ともいわれた」) / 「in some regions many were of hinin status, and they took charge of grave-digging, cremation, the disposal of those who died on the road and so on」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「地方によっては非人身分の者も多く，穴掘，火葬，行路死者の始末などにあたった。」) (several encyclopedias on the page; the Edo gate keeper, one says, was of commoner status)
  • jinkennagano-burakushi - 「at the start of the early modern period, the Matsumoto, Matsushiro and Ueda domains and others moved the "kawata" and "kawaya" into their castle towns」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世初頭には、松本・松代・上田藩などが「かわた」「かわや」を城下町へ移住させ」) / 「there are a considerable number of cases in which, in the middle and later periods, they were invited by villages and moved there, mainly as village guards」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「中後期に村から招請されて、主として村の警備役として移住した事例も相当数ある。」)
  • tsujiban-kotobank - 「The tsujiban of Aizu-Wakamatsu were set up by the town, and it seems there were about two men to each.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「会津若松の辻番は町で設けたものであるが，だいたい1ヵ所に2人のようである。」) / 「In Aizu-Wakamatsu, twice every night, at the ninth hour (midnight) and the eighth hour (2 a.m.), they went round the group calling "take care", and if there was a wanderer, they asked his address and then passed him on to the next tsujiban」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「会津若松では，毎夜九つ（0時），八つ（2時）の2回，組中に〈御用心〉を触れ回り，流浪の者があれば，その住所を尋ねたうえで次の辻番所へ継送りするといった職務があった。」) / 「In Aizu-Wakamatsu, the tsujiban keepers' wages, the tsujiban's lamp-oil money, the cost of building the hut and so on were borne by a group formed of several machi.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「会津若松では，辻番人の給分，辻番所の油銭，番所普請の費用などを，数ヵ町で編成した組が負担した。」) (Heibonsha's entry on the page)
