• dobashi-jawiki - 「Until the Edo period the overwhelming majority of the bridges over Japan's rivers were dobashi, and quite long bridges too were built this way. Plank bridges, with boards for the bridge surface, were limited to an important few, and stone bridges were very few indeed.」 (translated; ), and 「When logs are laid side by side with no gaps to make the bridge surface, the surface is uneven. As it is hard to walk on as it is, earth is put over it and trodden firm, and the earth fills the hollows and makes it flat.」 (translated; ; the article carries a notice that it lists too few sources)
  • hlj-traditional-bridges - 「Japan's bridges before the early modern period, except for very short stone bridges, were almost all "wooden bridges," many of them timber girder bridges」, 「bridges came to be built in the main castle towns, but because of the character of the rivers and the conditions of the alluvial plains, they were almost all timber-post and timber-girder bridges」 and 「in the Edo period stone arch bridges came to be built in parts of Kyushu, stone cantilever bridges (hanebashi) were also built in parts of Yamaguchi Prefecture, and stone arch bridges are seen in a very few places on Honshu too, but these may be taken as exceptions」 (translated; )
  • zhouzhuang-zhwiki - 「the simple, old stone arch bridges」, 「Zhouzhuang is an administrative town of the People's Republic of China, under Kunshan, in Suzhou, Jiangsu Province」 and 「ringed by lakes, with waterways crisscrossing the town, a typical Jiangnan water town」 (translated; ; named among what draws visitors to the town's surviving Ming and Qing fabric)
