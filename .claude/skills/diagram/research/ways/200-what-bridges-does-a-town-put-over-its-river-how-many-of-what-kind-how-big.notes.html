• dobashi-jawiki - 「Until the Edo period the overwhelming majority of the bridges over Japan's rivers were dobashi, and quite long bridges too were built this way. Plank bridges, with boards for the bridge surface, were limited to an important few, and stone bridges were very few indeed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代まで日本の川にかかる橋の圧倒的多数は土橋で、かなり長い橋もこの方式で作られた。板を橋面にした板橋は重要な少数に限られ、石橋はごく僅少であった。」), and 「When logs are laid side by side with no gaps to make the bridge surface, the surface is uneven. As it is hard to walk on as it is, earth is put over it and trodden firm, and the earth fills the hollows and makes it flat.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「丸太を隙間なく並べて橋面を作った場合、橋面が凹凸になる。そのままでは歩きにくいので、そこに土をかけて踏み固めると、へこんだ部分に土が詰まって平らになる。」; the article carries a notice that it lists too few sources)
  • hlj-traditional-bridges - 「Japan's bridges before the early modern period, except for very short stone bridges, were almost all "wooden bridges," many of them timber girder bridges」, 「bridges came to be built in the main castle towns, but because of the character of the rivers and the conditions of the alluvial plains, they were almost all timber-post and timber-girder bridges」 and 「in the Edo period stone arch bridges came to be built in parts of Kyushu, stone cantilever bridges (hanebashi) were also built in parts of Yamaguchi Prefecture, and stone arch bridges are seen in a very few places on Honshu too, but these may be taken as exceptions」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世以前の日本の橋は、ごく短い石の橋を除いて、そのほとんどが「木の橋」で、多くが木桁橋」, original: 「主要な城下町で橋が整備されるようになりますが、河川の性格や沖積平野（ちゅうせきへいや）*****という条件から木柱・木桁橋がほとんどでした。」 and original: 「江戸時代には九州の一部で石造アーチ橋が架けられるようになり、山口県の一部地域で石の刎橋（はねばし）*******も架けられましたし、本州のごく一部でも石造アーチ橋が見られますが、例外としてよいでしょう。」)
