• dobashi-jawiki - 「Until the Edo period the overwhelming majority of the bridges over Japan's rivers were dobashi, and quite long bridges too were built this way. Plank bridges, with boards for the bridge surface, were limited to an important few, and stone bridges were very few indeed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代まで日本の川にかかる橋の圧倒的多数は土橋で、かなり長い橋もこの方式で作られた。板を橋面にした板橋は重要な少数に限られ、石橋はごく僅少であった。」; the article carries a notice that it lists too few sources)
  • hlj-traditional-bridges - 「Japan's bridges before the early modern period, except for very short stone bridges, were almost all "wooden bridges," many of them timber girder bridges」, 「bridges came to be built in the main castle towns, but ... they were almost all timber-post and timber-girder bridges」 and 「stone arch bridges came to be built in parts of Kyushu ... but these may be taken as exceptions」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世以前の日本の橋は、ごく短い石の橋を除いて、そのほとんどが「木の橋」で、多くが木桁橋」, original: 「主要な城下町で橋が整備されるようになりますが、河川の性格や沖積平野（ちゅうせきへいや）*****という条件から木柱・木桁橋がほとんどでした。」 and original: 「江戸時代には九州の一部で石造アーチ橋が架けられるようになり、山口県の一部地域で石の刎橋（はねばし）*******も架けられましたし、本州のごく一部でも石造アーチ橋が見られますが、例外としてよいでしょう。」)
