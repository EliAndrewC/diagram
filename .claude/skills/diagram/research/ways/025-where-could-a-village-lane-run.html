Where could a village lane run?

Sources: aze-jawiki (the bund heaped up on the plot boundary, and the path along it), kotobank-azemichi (the narrow path between fields); the GM's rulings of 2026-07-21 and 2026-08-12.

What a village lane carried, and how wide it was drawn, is the question before this one.

Where a lane may run was ruled on the map. A trodden path never crosses row crops: historically it runs on the baulk between plots or along the edge of the hem, and the GM ruled so on 2026-07-21 after looking at a village's west field spur. It may touch a plot's boundary, since the baulk the path walks on is itself heaped up on the boundary between one paddy and the next. A limitation was accepted with it: where a staircase of dry plots blocks the way to the paddy and no clear baulk exists, the field track simply stops at the hem's edge, because past it farmers walk the plot baulks. The alternative - routing a detour around the foot of the staircase - was drawn and measured, and it disturbed the farmstead layout on every generated version of the map it was tried on, while the hem-edge stop left that layout untouched.

The other ruling concerns wet ground. The GM, on 2026-08-12: "there's supposed to be a rule that paths don't pass through marshland". None of these maps draws a causeway, so a track does not cross the reed toe - but it does not have to turn away over the settlement's back either, because the wet toe is only as wide as the ground the alluvial fan waters (see the water page), leaving dry footslope at both of its lateral ends.

Two further questions about lanes are answered on the homesteads page: whether every farmhouse is reached by a lane, and in what form, and how a village lane bends.

The rule the map follows: a lane runs on the baulk between plots or along the hem's edge and never across row crops, and it stops at the flooded paddy, since from there the way into the fields is on foot along the bunds. the track that links the settlement to the wider world continues out of the cluster and runs off the edge of the sheet. It never stops in mid-landscape, which reads as a path to nowhere, and it carries no label, because only an Imperial road is labeled. It keeps off wet ground: it leaves downslope past the end of the wet toe, on the flank the cluster already sits on, and turns along the contour only where the alluvial fan reaches the frame and leaves no dry ground to pass by. The field-ward spur and the road-ward connector are the two ends of one through-route. The settlement's own inner lanes are exempt from the off-the-edge rule: they legitimately end in the cluster or at the paddy.
