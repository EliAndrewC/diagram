Could a cart use the roads here, and where would it go?

Sources: kotobank-daihachiguruma (the Edo hand cart's spread and its size), mlit-kinsei-michi (how goods moved in Edo's streets), l7r-wagons (wagons and carts on the setting's roads)

In Japan, carts kept to the towns. Carts were confined to the cities and barred from the highways to the end of the shogunate; the evidence is at "What vehicle used a village lane?" Within the towns they spread. By an 1815 report from Edo, the hand cart (daihachiguruma) was first made there by ox-cart carpenters after the great fire of 1657 and had come into use in Kyoto, Osaka and the castle towns of other provinces, though the same page's other entries say Osaka used a different cart, with disc wheels and a plank bed; in the late Edo period it spread beyond the castle towns as well. In Edo, goods moved by hand cart, horse and ox cart. The hand cart's bed was 8 shaku long and 2 shaku 5 sun wide (a sun being a tenth of a shaku), about 8 by 2.5 ft.

In this setting, wagons use the roads. The GM's notes put wagons and carts on the roads between towns: a caravan's wagons crossing a bridge, a merchant robbed of a cartload of charcoal on the road, and hay hauled in wagons behind teams of oxen. Carts on the highways are a deviation from Edo Japan, and the setting's own.

What the map draws. A compound that ships or receives bulk goods - rice bales, charcoal - has a cart gate, a cart yard and a lane a cart can use, and the road outside it carries carts. That is a deviation, from the setting's canon. A lane for carts is wider than the cart: its bed is 2.5 ft wide, and how much wider its wheels ran is on no page read, so a cart lane of about 6 ft is a guess. How wide the road at a compound's gate is: "How wide is the road at a compound's gate?"
