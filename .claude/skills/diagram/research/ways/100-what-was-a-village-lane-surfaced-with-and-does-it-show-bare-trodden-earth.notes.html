• jarimichi-jawiki - 「In Japan, until modern times, roads trodden hard by people and horses were the norm, and gravel roads were limited to main roads.」 (translated; )
  • ishidatami-jawiki - 「The simplest state of a road is a strip where the grass has worn away because people and livestock walk it often, or a way made passable by felling trees in a forest or wood; that is, it is basically bare earth, and on a plain earth road rain turns it to mud, and walkers and carriages alike were put to great trouble.」 (translated; ) (the article's general overview, not a statement about Japan in particular)
  • mlit-tokaido-michi - 「Edo-period road policy and road types and grades [are given in] the 'Ieyasu hundred articles', said to be Ieyasu's testament」 (translated; ); its table: 「field road 3 shaku (about 90 cm)」 (translated; )
  • gokaido-jawiki - 「Works were carried out such as spreading gravel and sand to harden the road surface and planting ginkgo trees.」 (translated; )
  • mlit-kinsei-michi-gokaido - 「Hardening the surface with gravel and sand ... could not be carried through; when it rained, people walked the mud roads in high geta」 (translated; )
  • mlit-kinsei-michi-gokaido - 「In 1612 the shogunate issued an order on road maintenance as an 'oboe' [a written memorandum]. Spread and harden sand and stone where there are puddles and mud. Make water flow at the side of the road.」 (translated; )
  • mlit-tokaido-michi - 「Namely: 'hollows made by passing horses are to be filled with sand or stones and well hardened; the sides of the road are to be given a suitable amount of moisture and hardened; the turf of road embankments is not to be stripped', and so on.」 (translated; ) (the same 1612 order as the note above, in this page's wording)
  • gokaido-jawiki - 「In principle the road magistrate assigned the carrying-out of highway maintenance to the post towns and villages along the road and made them responsible for it.」 (translated; ); 「Depending on traffic, the roadside villages were sometimes not enough, and assignments extended to surrounding villages not directly on the road; each such assigned section was called a soji-choba.」 (translated; )
  • mlit-kinsei-michi-gokaido - 「Day-to-day maintenance was borne by roadside and nearby villages. Those bearing it were set as towns and villages within roughly 20 km of the highway.」 (translated; )
  • ishidatami-jawiki - 「Old highways: the Edo shogunate ordered difficult stretches improved with stone paving.」 (translated; ); 「On the old Tokaido, part of the stone paving of that time survives at Hakone and at Kikugawa-juku.」 (translated; )
  • gokaido-jawiki - 「For the 14th shogun Iemochi's journey up to Kyoto in Bunkyu 3 (1863), a record survives that the Hakone mountain road was repaired and paved with round stones.」 (translated; )
  • gokaido-jawiki - 「On the Fushimi-Kyoto and Otsu-Kyoto roads, where haulage was heavy, a carriageway for ox carts was separated from the road for people and horses; thick granite cart-stones were laid in two wheel tracks, and gravel was spread level on the ox path.」 (translated; )
