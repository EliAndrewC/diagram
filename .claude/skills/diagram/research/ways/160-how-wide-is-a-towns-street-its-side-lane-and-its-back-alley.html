How wide is a town's street, its side lane and its back alley?

Sources: shukubamachi-kotobank (the width of the street through a post town), mlit-kinsei-michi-gokaido (the standard width of the highway itself), netease-city-street-widths (a Chinese city's main street and lanes, in a popular account), roji-jawiki (the width of the back alley, and that it is narrower than a side street)

The Japanese town whose street is described in figures is the post town, strung out along its highway, and there the street was the highway: within the town it ran 2 to 4 ken wide, which the account calls relatively wide. At the ken of about 6 ft that is about 12 to 24 ft. The highway's own standard, set in 1605, was about 9 m, cut to about 4 to 7 m in the mountains. China gives the same band: in one popular account an ordinary Ming or Qing city's main street was only about 4 to 8 m wide - about 13 to 26 ft - and its lanes under 3 m; the article cites nothing for the figures.

Below the street, the readable figure is the back alley's. The roji between one row of back tenements and the next was about 3 to 6 shaku wide, 0.9 to 1.8 m, and it was narrower than a side street (yokocho). The side street itself - the lane off the main street - is given a width in ken on no page that was read.

So the town's main street, drawn 24 ft wide, sits at the top of the post town's band and inside the Chinese account's; a street down to about 12 ft is as well attested, and where in the band a town's street falls is a degree, not a choice between forms. The back alley is drawn 10 ft wide, wider than the 3 to 6 ft it measured, so that it reads as a way between the house rows at the town's scale: a map drawing convention. The side lane's width is a guess between the two. The Imperial road keeps the 30 ft it is drawn at on every sheet, which is the capital page's question; a city's street widths are the city fabric page's, its share of the ground in streets and its grid, and a village lane is this page's first question.

The rule the map follows: a town's main street is 24 ft wide, a figure inside the 12 to 24 ft its history gives; a lesser street may be drawn narrower, down to about 12 ft, and its width is a guess. A back alley between house rows is drawn 10 ft wide - a map drawing convention for a passage 3 to 6 ft wide. The Imperial road through the town stays 30 ft.
