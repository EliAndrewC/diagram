It lies far inside the territorial boundary.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「The village boundary is the point where a road coming into the village from outside is about to enter the settlement; one can say there are as many village boundaries as there are roads leading out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-murazakai - 「Because it is where people gather, it also serves as a plaza, the center of the village.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-murazakai - 「The dosojin, too, is usually enshrined here.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • dosojin-jawiki - 「Dosojin were enshrined mainly at spatial and social boundaries such as a village's entrances and exits, passes, crossroads and bridge feet」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • michikiri-jawiki - 「On the entrance roads, to ward off or drive out evil, michikiri was done: enshrining a dosojin, stringing a shimenawa (or a straw serpent), offering sandals.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「ropes shaped as centipedes, snakes or dragons fixed to a boundary tree; similar ropes stretched across the road」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-murazakai - 「Kanjo-tsuri, stringing a thick sacred rope at the village boundary at New Year, is common in the Kinki region and is the typical example; the huge straw figures often seen in Tohoku and the big straw serpents placed at village boundaries across the country carry the same meaning.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kanjonawa-jawiki - 「In farming areas throughout Japan, there remain examples of ropes, figures, sandals and the like made of straw or grass set up at the village boundary, meant to keep calamities such as epidemics from entering or to load calamity onto them and drive it out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Around the Kinki region they are concentrated in Wakasa (Fukui), the Koto and Konan areas (Shiga), Iga (Mie), the eastern mountains of Nara, southern Yamashiro (Kyoto) and elsewhere.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • mlit-tokaido-mitsuke - 「At the entrances of post towns on the Tokaido and other main highways stood structures called mitsuke. Generally the one at the Edo-side entrance was called the Edo mitsuke and the one at the Kyoto (Kamigata) side the Kamigata mitsuke. The stretch between them was, so to speak, the extent of the post town.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Trapezoidal banks stand on both sides of the road at right angles to the Tokaido, with a fence built on top.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「The base is 2 ken 2 shaku (about 4.2 m) wide, the top 2 shaku (about 60 cm), the height 8 shaku 5 sun (about 2.5 m). On top of the bank is a fence 2 shaku 5 sun (about 75 cm) high.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the figures are for Kanagawa post town's Edo-side mitsuke); 「It appears they were originally set up as simple defenses, and they are understood also to have shown the post town's extent visually; but considering that the Tokaido Shukuson Taigaicho has no entry for them, could it be that they were not regarded as so important in the late early-modern period?」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • shukuba-jawiki - 「Masugata - places at both ends of a post town where the highway was bent into a crank.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Kido - set at the ends of a post town (near the mitsuke); the stretch between the gates (between the mitsuke) was the post town.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
