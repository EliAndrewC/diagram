• nrcs-ts14q-abutments - 「Bridge abutments consisting of soil or other erodible material must be protected against the effects of scour. Either the footing should be embedded below the maximum anticipated scour depth or adequate scour protection must be provided. The embedment approach is generally not applicable with the shallow strip footings normally used with NRCS-designed bridges. Therefore, scour protection, such as rock riprap or gabions, should be provided, as necessary, to prevent erosion of the slope and possible undermining of the footings.」 The supplement is about the bridge our maps draw: 「Bridges installed under NRCS programs are generally single-span, single-lane structures and use various simple structural systems including rail cars, steel I-beams, and timber stringers. Timber decking is often used to provide the driving surface. The entire bridge structure is normally supported by simple strip footings of timber or concrete on either abutment.」
  • ritter-timber-bridges - 「Abutments support the bridge ends and contain roadway embankment material. The simplest timber abutment is a sawn lumber or glulam spread footing placed directly on the surface of the embankment (Figure 2-30). This type of abutment is used only when foundation material is of sufficient quality to support loads without excessive settlement, erosion, or scour.」; and of the taller kind, 「Post abutments are used to elevate the superstructure and are provided with a backwall and wingwalls for retaining fill embankment.」

  • saruhashi-jawiki - 「Under such conditions a suspension bridge was the usual choice, but in Edo-period Japan there was one more form, the hanebashi. In a hanebashi, holes are cut in the bank rock and cantilever timbers are set into them at a slant and made to project into the air. On top of it a similar cantilever timber is thrust out and made to rest on the one below. For the support it receives, the upper timber is set to project a little further than the one below. Many of these are stacked, reaching far out into the air.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
