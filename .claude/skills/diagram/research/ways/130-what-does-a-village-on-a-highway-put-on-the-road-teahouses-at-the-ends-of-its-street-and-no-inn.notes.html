• shuson-jawiki - 「A village with dwellings lined along both sides of a road is called a roson. Commerce is not developed as in a gaison, dwelling density is somewhat lower, and dependence on the road is low.」 (translated; ); 「A village more dependent on the road, with houses more densely packed and with shops and lodging, is called a gaison.」 (translated; ); 「Because gaison and roson are very similar in plan, telling them apart on a topographic map is difficult.」 (translated; ) (a geographer's classification, not an Edo one)
  • tateba-jawiki - 「Tateba were rest facilities set up on the Five Highways and similar roads partway along where the next post town was far, or at a difficult spot such as a pass. Teahouses and stalls stood there. ... Horses and palanquins were sometimes changed. Some were set up by domains; others arose naturally at the hands of local people.」 (translated; )
  • mlit-tokaido-tateba - 「There were 18 tateba on the Tokaido in Kanagawa.」 (translated; ); 「The Tokaido in the prefecture counts 9 post towns. The in-between villages between post towns number 56 in all.」 (translated; ); two of the 18 in the page's table stand within post towns: 「Within Hodogaya post town: Sakaigi」 (translated; ) and 「Within Hiratsuka post town: Hachiman」 (translated; ) (「16 of the 56 villages」 is this project's arithmetic on the counts and the table)
  • mlit-tokaido-hatago - 「Between post towns there were also many teahouses, which were rest facilities.」 (translated; )
  • mlit-tokaido-tateba - 「Tateba teahouses were generally concentrated at the corners at both ends of the street's row of houses, and were also set at places with a very wide view, such as Sakaigi at Hodogaya, at the top of the Gonta slope past the town's end; at forks, such as Yotsuya in Hatori village, where the Oyama road splits from the Tokaido; and in in-between villages beside large unbridged rivers such as the Sagami and Sakawa, such as Nango in Chigasaki village and Umezawa in Hatori village.」 (translated; )
  • mlit-tokaido-hatago - 「Partway up the Daimachi slope overlooking the sea, many teahouses stood eave to eave.」 (translated; ) (the caption of a road map of Kanagawa post town's Daimachi)
  • mlit-tokaido-hatago - 「As a rule, a teahouse building had an earth floor and was a very open space, its rooms divided not by walls but entirely by fittings. Because teahouses were defined strictly as rest facilities without lodging, they had to be sharply distinguished, in building structure as well, from inns that kept meshimori women.」 (translated; )
  • ainoshuku-jawiki - 「It was not recognized as a post town; officially it was a village or town, not a shuku, and lodging travelers was in principle forbidden.」 (translated; ); 「As in ordinary post towns, all kinds of shops such as rice dealers and sake dealers stood side by side」 (translated; ); 「A facility smaller than an ai-no-shuku is called a tateba; the so-called 'pass teahouse' is one.」 (translated; ) (the article carries a banner saying it lacks sources)
  • tateba-jawiki - 「A tateba that grew into a large settlement where inns and the like were also set up is called an ai-no-shuku.」 (translated; )
