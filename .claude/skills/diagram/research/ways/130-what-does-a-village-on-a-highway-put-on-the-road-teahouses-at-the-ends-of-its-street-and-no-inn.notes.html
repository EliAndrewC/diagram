• shuson-jawiki - 「A village with dwellings lined along both sides of a road is called a roson. Commerce is not developed as in a gaison, dwelling density is somewhat lower, and dependence on the road is low.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「道路に沿って、道の両側に住居が立ち並ぶ村落を路村…という。街村のように商業が発達しておらず、住居の密度はやや低く、道路への依存は低い」); 「A village more dependent on the road, with houses more densely packed and with shops and lodging, is called a gaison.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「道路への依存度が高く、より家屋の密集度が高く、商店や宿泊施設を持つ村落を街村…という」); 「Because gaison and roson are very similar in plan, telling them apart on a topographic map is difficult.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平面形態は街村も路村も非常に類似しているため、地形図から両者の見分けを付けるのは困難である」) (a geographer's classification, not an Edo one)
  • tateba-jawiki - 「Tateba were rest facilities set up on the Five Highways and similar roads partway along where the next post town was far, or at a difficult spot such as a pass. Teahouses and stalls stood there. ... Horses and palanquins were sometimes changed. Some were set up by domains; others arose naturally at the hands of local people.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「五街道等で次の宿場町が遠い場合はその途中に、また、峠のような難所がある場合はその難所に、休憩施設として設けられたものが立場である。茶屋や売店が設けられていた。…馬や駕籠の交代を行なうこともあった。藩が設置したものや、周辺住民の手で自然発生したものもある。」)
  • mlit-tokaido-tateba - 「There were 18 tateba on the Tokaido in Kanagawa.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「神奈川県下の東海道には18の立場があった。」); 「The Tokaido in the prefecture counts 9 post towns. The in-between villages between post towns number 56 in all.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「県下の東海道は9宿を数える。各宿と各宿の間にある「間の村」は合計56カ村を数える。」) (「about one village in three」 is this page's arithmetic on the two counts)
  • mlit-tokaido-hatago - 「Between post towns there were also many teahouses, which were rest facilities.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「宿場間には休憩施設である茶屋も多くあった。」)
  • mlit-tokaido-tateba - 「Tateba teahouses were generally concentrated at the corners at both ends of the street's row of houses, and were also set at places with a very wide view, such as Sakaigi at Hodogaya, at the top of the Gonta slope past the town's end; at forks, such as Yotsuya in Hatori village, where the Oyama road splits from the Tokaido; and in in-between villages beside large unbridged rivers such as the Sagami and Sakawa, such as Nango in Chigasaki village and Umezawa.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「立場茶屋は一般に街道町並み両端の一角に集中するとともに、保土ヶ谷宿境木のように宿はずれから権太坂を上り切ったきわめて眺望がきく場所、あるいは羽鳥村四ツ谷のように東海道から大山道へと分かれる分岐点、さらには茅ヶ崎村南湖（なんご）や羽鳥村梅沢のように相模川や酒匂（さかわ）川など架橋されない大河川に接する間の村に設けられた。」)
  • mlit-tokaido-hatago - 「Partway up the Daimachi slope overlooking the sea, many teahouses stood eave to eave.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「海を望む台町の坂の途中にはたくさんの茶屋が軒を並べていた。」) (the caption of a road map of Kanagawa post town's Daimachi)
  • mlit-tokaido-hatago - 「As a rule, a teahouse building had an earth floor and was a very open space, its rooms divided not by walls but entirely by fittings. Because teahouses were defined strictly as rest facilities without lodging, they had to be sharply distinguished, in building structure as well, from inns that kept meshimori women.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「茶屋の建物構造は、土間があり、部屋を壁などで仕切らずにすべて建具で仕切るというきわめて開放的な空間であることを原則とした。茶屋は、あくまでも宿泊を伴わない休憩施設との規定から、飯盛女（めしもりおんな）を置く旅籠屋と建物構造上でも峻別する必要があったからである。」)
  • ainoshuku-jawiki - 「It was not recognized as a post town; officially it was a village or town, not a shuku, and lodging travelers was in principle forbidden.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「宿場としては非公認であって、公式には宿ではなく村もしくは町とされ、旅人の宿泊は原則禁じられていた。」); 「As in ordinary post towns, all kinds of shops such as rice dealers and sake dealers stood side by side」 (original: 「一般の宿場同様に米屋や酒屋などの各種商店が櫛比しており」); 「A facility smaller than an ai-no-shuku is called a tateba; the so-called 'pass teahouse' is one.」 (original: 「間の宿より小規模な施設を立場（たてば）と言い、いわゆる“峠の茶屋”等がそれである。」) (all translated from the Japanese by this project; the article carries a banner saying it lacks sources)
  • tateba-jawiki - 「A tateba that grew into a large settlement where inns and the like were also set up is called an ai-no-shuku.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この立場が発展し、大きな集落を形成し、宿屋なども設けられたのは間の宿（あいのしゅく）という。」)
