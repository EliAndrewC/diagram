Why is there a crescent pond in front of some villages, and why is it labeled?

Sources: thcdc-half-moon-pond (water in front and the hand-dug pond, the pond and mound as yin and yang, the half shape's inferred reason, the pond's field-water supply); cssn-citang-panyu (drainage, fire and fish); weilongwu-zhwiki (the drying floor between pond and house); storm-banyuechi (hill behind and water in front); the GM's ruling of 2026-07-21 (the label)

What it is. The half-moon pond (半月池, banyuechi) is geomancy made into a landscape. A well-sited house wants high ground behind it and water in front of it - 背山面水, beishan mianshui - and where no natural river or stream lies in front, a household simply digs a half-moon pond by hand to complete the arrangement. Among the Hakka the pond lies lower than the mound of earth behind the house, and the two are read together as the yin and yang of one whole, the pond yin and the mound yang; the common idea that water brings a household wealth seems to count for less than that. Why the pond is HALF a circle the Taiwan Hakka Museum can only guess, and it offers two hedged reasons: most likely the pond copies the one before a school of the imperial examinations, and, it infers, commoners kept to a half circle so as not to exceed what the regulations allowed their betters. Its water was led in: most farmhouses stood among the fields, and a channel led stream water from the fields into the pond, slowed on purpose, because fast water was held to carry wealth away as fast as it came. The water in front of a Cantonese ancestral hall had practical uses as well as geomantic ones - drainage, fire prevention and raising fish - and in a Hakka enclosed house the grain-drying floor lies between the pond and the house.

Why it carries a label when almost nothing else does. These maps do not label what a reader can see for themselves - no "gate", no "north", no naming of an ordinary road - and this pond is the one deliberate exception, ruled by the GM on 2026-07-21: the half-moon pond is culturally specific and reads as nothing but a pond-shaped pond to a reader who has not met one. "Geomantic pond" is the GM's own wording for the label, chosen over a religious one.

The rule the map follows: where a village digs one, the pond is drawn as a half-disk at the front of the settlement with its flat bank toward the houses, that bank left open as the forecourt, and it labels itself "geomantic pond". No vegetation touches its water: the groves keep clear of the full circle the half-disk is cut from, so the open forecourt stays bare too, and the scrub and reed scatter skips it as it skips any other water. The pond is drawn apart from the field channels, fed by nothing the map shows; that is a DEVIATION from the one page read, where a channel of field water fed the pond, and it stands only until the map draws an inlet or the GM rules on it.
