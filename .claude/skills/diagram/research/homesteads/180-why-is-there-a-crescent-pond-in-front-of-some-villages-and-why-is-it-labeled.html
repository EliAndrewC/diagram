Why is there a crescent pond in front of some villages, and why is it labeled?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-07-21; no work is cited for the pond's own doctrine

What it is. The half-moon pond (半月塘, banyuetang) dug at the front of a village is GEOMANTIC and not religious: no deity, no rites, nobody worships at it. It is cosmological engineering of the same kind as facing a house south. A well-sited village wants the hill behind it and water in front of it - 背山面水, beishan mianshui - and where the back half is the high ground with its grove, the front half is water; where no river obliges, the village DIGS it. Still water is held to gather and keep a lineage's fortune where running water carries it away, and the HALF shape is deliberate: a complete thing can only wane, so the shape leaves the lineage room to grow. The grove arc behind and the water arc in front cradle the settlement as one system. In practical terms it is the rain-fed runoff basin, deliberately NOT connected to the irrigation, and the village's fire water, its fish and duck water and its washing water, with the flat bank serving as the open forecourt where threshing and ceremony happen. The shrine is where religion happens; the pond is how a well-sited village is SHAPED. (This rests on general reading of geomantic village siting; no source is cited for it.)

Why it carries a label when almost nothing else does. These maps do not label what a reader can see for themselves - no "gate", no "north", no naming of an ordinary road - and this pond is the one deliberate exception, ruled by the GM on 2026-07-21: the half-moon pond is culturally specific and reads as nothing but a pond-shaped pond to a reader who has not met one. "Geomantic pond" is the GM's own wording for the label, chosen over a religious one.

The rule the map follows: where a village digs one, the pond is drawn as a half-disk at the front of the settlement with its flat bank toward the houses, that bank left open as the forecourt, and it labels itself "geomantic pond". No vegetation touches its water: the groves keep clear of the whole disk, and the scrub and reed scatter skips it as it skips any other water.
