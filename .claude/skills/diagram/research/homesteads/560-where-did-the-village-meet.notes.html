• kotobank-yoriai - 「The meeting was often held at the house of a village official or office-holder such as the nanushi, the shoya or the ward head, but in the Kinki region facilities called kaisho or kaigisho were set up from early times.」 (translated; )「The kaisho was often within the precinct of the village's tutelary shrine, suggesting a tradition of deciding matters before the gods.」 (translated; ) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, revised edition, by Fukuta Ajio)
  • kotobank-mura-yoriai - 「For the place of the village meeting, in old times the office-house of a village official, or a temple, a shrine or a nenbutsu hall and the like were used, and there were places that held it at the festival lodging after the festival, but gradually meeting halls and the like became general.」 (translated; ) (the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry by Takeuchi Toshimi)
  • kotobank-mura-yoriai - 「The full members of the village meeting were the representatives (the heads) of the households, the basic units that made up the mura; all were required to attend as a duty and to join in the deliberation and decision, and attendance by proxy was permitted only to an adult heir.」 (translated; )「The matters discussed were the year's plan for running the shared life, with its budget and accounts, and urgent matters as they arose; the regular meeting was usually held twice a year, and in an emergency one was held as needed.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-mura-yoriai - 「The early-modern village meeting was convened under the lead of the village officials (the leading men) and valued as the occasion for deliberating, deciding or announcing everything in the shared life - paying the tax, handling the village's charges, the village festival and more - and it was made up mainly of the honbyakusho stratum, but later it seems generally to have come about that the komae, hirakata and mizunomi also took part as their strength grew.」 (translated; )
  • citang-zhwiki - 「The ancestral hall, also called the family temple, the ancestors' hall or the public hall, is a shrine founded on the lineage system for enshrining and sacrificing to the ancestors; it also serves for the family's publicity, for enforcing the clan's rules and household law, and for deliberation and feasting.」 (translated; )
