What else was a half-moon pond for, and what stood around it?

Sources: weilongwu-zhwiki (the pond's name and uses, its widening with the house, the circle it closes with the rear wing, the grove behind, the drying floor and its gatherings, the hall where the gods are worshipped); storm-banyuechi (the pond's other names, washing and laundry, the unsettled origin of its shape); sohu-minjian-fengshui (the folk reading of the half shape); fengshuilin-zhwiki (the grove behind a village); zhwiki-hongcun (a half-moon pond at the heart of a village)

What the pond is, and why the map labels it, is at Why is there a crescent pond in front of some villages? This question asks what else it did, and what stood behind and before it.

Its names, and its everyday water. Before a Hakka enclosed house the pond is called the fengshui pond (风水塘, fengshuitang), and a Taiwan folk dictionary gives it as the eyebrow-moon pond (月眉池), also called the half-moon pond, the fengshui pond or the fish pond. Its water was used every day: it was stored against drought, stocked with fish, let out onto the fields, tempered the air a little and stood ready against fire, it served for washing, and before one ancestral hall in Pingtung the neighbors' household water, laundry and washing all came from the water system that fed its pond. It grew with its house: as the enclosing wing was extended the pond was dug wider by hand, and the Hakka hold that it must be wider than the span between the two dog-holes beside the side gates, so that the fengshui does not flow out.

The half shape, the grove behind, and the ground before it. Seen from above, a weilongwu's half-moon pond in front and its half-moon wing behind close into one circle around the main hall; fengshui reads the two halves as heaven, which is round, and the hall-and-wing house between them as earth, which is square. A folk fengshui page gives the half circle a meaning of its own: a moon only half full, because a full moon can only wane, which the householder takes as a spur to keep striving; but where the shape came from, a survey of the explanations says, there are not yet the sources to settle. Behind a weilongwu stands a fengshui grove of hardy trees and bamboo, holding the soil and breaking the wind, and many south-China villages were sited against a dense wood that screened their rear, which the villagers guarded as the village's luck. That the grove behind and the pond in front were planned as one system is this page's reading of those pages, a GUESS: the weilongwu article closes its circle with the house's own rear wing, not with the grove. The ground a household gathers on is the drying floor between house and pond, the space for the new year and the festivals, for weddings and funerals. Where a village's ancestral hall stands against this pond is taken up in Where did a south-China village's ancestral hall stand, and how big was it?; in Hongcun, a Huizhou village, the half-moon pond lies at the village's middle, formed by its water channel, with the houses laid out around it and the lineage hall at the middle of its north bank.

What no page read says. None says the pond has no god or is no place of worship. The worship the weilongwu article names happens inside the house, in the dragon hall at the middle of the rear wing, and every page read gives the pond only water, fish, wealth and the yin of the pair; that the pond is how a well-sited village is shaped rather than a shrine is this page's reading and a GUESS.
