What makes the headman's house different?

Sources: the GM's rulings of 2026-07-21; no work is cited for what the office kept in store

Its size, and almost nothing else. The GM ruled on 2026-07-21, on a map that had drawn the headman as a special case, that the headman's house is a FARMSTEAD and not an exception: the grandest house in the village gets its work yard and its dooryard garden like every other farm, and both are capped at an ordinary farm's sizes, because a big house does not mean a field-sized kitchen bed. On a scattered map the headman's homestead brings its grove too, where there is room for one. What makes the house read as the headman's is that it is plainly the largest - about 92 by 56 ft against a plain farmhouse's 46 by 28 - and not a different plan, a compound wall or a gate.

What the office does add is a storehouse. The village headman (shoya) is by definition among its most prosperous farmers, and the office needs fireproof storage in a way an ordinary farm does not: the village's tax ledgers, its land registers and the tax rice waiting for collection are exactly what a kura is for (this rests on general reading; no source is cited).96 So the headman always has one, where an ordinary farm carries one only sometimes (the question below). The GM ruled that on 2026-07-21; left on the ordinary roll, every headman on the sheet had come up bare, which is chance reading as doctrine.

A map drawing convention, and the reason for it: the headman's house is drawn as a plain farmhouse enlarged rather than with the large-house glyph, because that glyph carries a storeroom wing that stands outside the ground the homestead reserves and lands on a neighbor's work yard.
