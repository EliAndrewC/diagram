How tightly does a nucleated village pack?

Sources: this project's measurement of the drawn villages

A clustered village is a compact FABRIC and not a wide hollow arc, and one drawn village came out as the arc: a thin string of houses around an empty middle. The cause was not the shape the houses were seeded in - it was its WIDTH. The placer pulls every homestead in to hug the paddy edge and then packs it along that edge, so a wide seed cloud gets strung out by that pull while a narrow deep one packs into a blob. Narrowing the crescent's lateral reach and letting DEPTH carry the frontage fixed it: built coverage went from 0.20 to 0.31 and the cluster's span halved, from about 1,900 ft to about 1,070 ft.

The calibration, and an honest limit. Coverage here means how much of the ground inside the houses' own outline is actually BUILT - houses, gardens, work yards and homestead groves. The pathological arc measures 0.20; the rolled villages that look right measure 0.28 to 0.31; the tightly hand-placed villages measure about 0.40. A floor of 0.25 sits clear below the healthy band and above the pathology, which is exactly what it is for: it catches the pathology, and it does not demand hand-placed density. The rolled placer tops out near 0.31, because hugging and packing along an edge is looser than a person seating houses one at a time, and that is accepted - a few back-row houses still stand about 700 ft from the crops, which reads as the honest back of a compact village rather than as a stranded arc.

The rule the map follows (village tier): a clustered village of a dozen houses or more keeps at least a quarter of the ground inside its own outline built. A hamlet is legitimately loose and is not held to it, and neither is a cluster too small for the measure to mean anything.

What this measure cannot see. The measure counts homestead groves as built ground, and the grove's own footprint is the bounding box of the house cloud - so the numerator and the denominator move together, and a clump refused because a house stands there relocates to the next gap instead of dropping. A cluster that SPREADS therefore grows its grove into the gaps it opened, and the coverage figure can rise while the built fabric thins. Measured on the hamlets of 2026-09-13: one cluster's grove carries 246 clumps against a like cluster's 22, at the same household count, its coverage on this definition rose from 0.294 to 0.339, and its houses-gardens-yards fabric alone fell from 0.279 to 0.130 while its outline more than doubled. The 0.25 floor above was calibrated on villages whose groves had not relocated, No village ships today, and hamlets are exempt by the sentence above, so nothing currently drawn turns on it.
