Does the village's name say where it stands?

Sources: the GM's general rule of 2026-07

A Rokugani place name usually says something about where the settlement sits relative to its fields, its water or its ground, and the GM made it a general rule in 2026-07 that the name is read BEFORE the settlement is seated: "the map should be able to be read back into the name". The worked example is Ueda (上田, "upper paddy"), whose paddies are the upslope ones - so its dwellings stand DOWNSLOPE of them, facing up-valley toward the fields. On that map's northeast-high ground that puts the cluster on the LOWER, southwest flank with the bulk of the paddy rising above it, which is the opposite of the default reading, a village on the high flank.

Other cues worth reading the same way: shimo- (下, lower) says the reverse of Ueda; kawa- or -gawa (川, river) puts the settlement hard by the watercourse; and yama- (山, mountain), saka (坂, slope), hara (原, plain or moor), tani or -dani (谷, valley floor), sawa (沢, a marshy stream), hama (浜, shore) and -guchi (口, a mouth or entrance or pass) each name the ground the place stands on. Where the name says nothing about the ground, the default holds.

The rule the map follows: a settlement whose name names its ground is seated to match the name, and the default - a clustered village on the dry high flank, hugging its paddy - applies only where the name is neutral. No generator reads a name; this is a rule for whoever specifies the map.
