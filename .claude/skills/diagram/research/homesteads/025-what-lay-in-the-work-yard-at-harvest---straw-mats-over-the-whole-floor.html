What lay in the work yard at harvest? Straw mats over the whole floor

Sources: kitamoto-inakoki-niwa (threshing in the yard, mats filling it, how many a large farm kept, husking indoors), kitamoto-mushiro-niwa (the ordinary count of mats), tobunken-mushiro (the size of one mat), seika-mingu-dakkoku (the grain dried on mats in the sun, Kinki), ehime-mushiroboshi (days of mat drying, Shikoku), kasuga-aki-wo-suru (mat drying every day, Kyushu)

How big the yard was is How big was the work yard?; this question is what lay on it.

Threshing was done in the yard, on mats. The usual place to thresh was the house's yard. Mats were laid down and the threshing combs set out on them, and the rice was drawn through the combs a handful at a time.

Then the grain was dried on mats over the whole yard. The threshed grain was dried with the mats spread to fill the yard: about a day for rice that had hung on a rack, about three fine days for rice threshed without much drying first. That the mats covered the whole yard is Kitamoto's record alone; the other regions read say the grain was dried on mats, not how much of the yard they took. The mat drying is recorded in the Kinki region, the grain spread on mats in the sun; in Shikoku, three to about six more days on mats for rice first dried lying on the ground; and in northern Kyushu, on the mats every day so the grain would not rot. The husking came later and indoors, on the earthen floor of the house or in the barn, usually as night work.

How many mats. A yard was commonly measured in the mats it held, ordinarily forty to sixty, a few households past a hundred; a large farm kept a hundred or a hundred and fifty mats ready. One mat was about 3 by 6 feet. So a yard at harvest was not open ground around a mat: it was a floor of mats, as many as it held.

What the sources do not say. How the mats were laid - edge to edge, in rows, with walkways between - no page read says, nor whether anything else stood in the yard while the grain dried.

What this means for the map. The map is not drawn for a season (the GM, 2026-09-28: seasons are future work); the yard is drawn as the harvest leaves it, covered in mats. Each mat is drawn at its real 3 by 6 feet, in rows across the whole floor - the rows are a GUESS, the sources being silent on the arrangement. The map draws fewer mats than covered the yard, a third to a half of them, in rows, every other row set over by half a mat, with a little bare ground around each: at this scale dozens of mats edge to edge would read as a textured floor, not as mats, so the drawing shows a smaller number that gives the impression of many - a map drawing CONVENTION (the GM, 2026-09-28). The real count is the one above, forty to sixty on an ordinary farm.
