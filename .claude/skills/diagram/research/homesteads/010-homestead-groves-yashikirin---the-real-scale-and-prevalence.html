Homestead groves (yashikirin) - the real scale and prevalence

Sources: kashima-kainyo-1987 (the 1987 survey's figures - 1,542 trees over 46 households, 48% sugi1), yashikirin-jawiki (the west-and-south sentence), isa-auf-fukugi-2011 (Okinawa's north-and-east groves, the cross-check that makes the side a knob), visit-toyama-sankyoson (what the Tonami plain is)

  • Historical scale - the real numbers. A homestead grove is a substantial STAND, not a few trees. The best hard data is a 1987 survey of Kashima in the Tonami plain2 (the dispersed-farmstead country whose homestead woodlands are the kainyo,3 46 households): ~33 trees of trunk diameter >= 10 cm per homestead (1,542 trunks over the 46 households - arithmetic on the two footnoted figures, not a number the page states), of which cedar (sugi) was ~48% (~16 cedars per house4), the rest spread over ~83 other species;5 ~6 species per homestead (range 1-14);6 for a large homestead, 200+ trees across 31 species is a GUESS at the top of the band, resting on no source7 - That count is trunks >= 10 cm ONLY - it EXCLUDES the bamboo stand (one stood on every homestead, and a count of trunks 10 cm and over does not reach a culm),8 saplings, and the shrub layers below the canopy (the page sorts the stand into four layers by height and counts 44 / 76 / 80 / 153 species from the tallest down)9 - so the honest figure for a typical grove is ~30-40 mature trees + a bamboo grove + understory, and a big one 100-200+. The grove canopy footprint is therefore the LARGEST homestead appurtenance - bigger than the farmhouse, and far bigger than the garden or threshing yard - wrapping the windward faces as a belt.151 How DEEP the belt is has no firm number in the record - the "2-3 rows of sugi" below is itself unsourced - so the drawn arm depth (13 ft, one to two crowns) is a GUESS. Which faces the belt takes is rolled per map, because the record gives more than one answer: on the Tonami plain the tall sugi stand from the south face round to the west, with hackberry, alder and a little bamboo from the west round to the north, and the general account of the mainland yashikirin puts the tall trees on the west and south;10 the maps put the belt on the northwest by default; a map with a Tonami-like wind takes the southwest arms. The map draws EVERY canopy tree, one crown at its real size on real spacing (Forest density and crown size: a mean crown radius of 8.5 ft on ~13 ft centers, no crown seated under another), on a footprint that is itself to scale - exactly as the farmhouse, the threshing yard and the garden are drawn at their true size. The grove's dominance is therefore not a glyph calibration; it falls out of drawing the real thing: clearly the dominant homestead feature, a dense stand of dozens of trees, never a garden-sized clump of 5-10. (Cross-check on the windward rule: Okinawa's homestead groves sit on the E/N sides, because there the damaging winds come from the north (the monsoon) and the east (typhoons),11 not the mainland's west and south - same logic, different geography, which is exactly why the windward side is a per-map knob.)
