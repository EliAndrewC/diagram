Homestead groves (yashikirin) - the real scale and prevalence

Sources: kashima-kainyo-1987 (the 1987 survey's figures - 1,542 trees over 46 households, 48% sugi), yashikirin-jawiki (the west-and-south sentence, and the Izumo grove's full ring), irie-2020-igune (the Sendai plain's two-sided grove), tonami-yashikirin-haichi (the Tonami grove's open front), isa-auf-fukugi-2011 (Okinawa's north-and-east groves, the cross-check that makes the side a knob), visit-toyama-sankyoson (what the Tonami plain is)

  • Historical scale - the real numbers. A homestead grove is a substantial STAND, not a few trees. The best hard data is a 1987 survey of Kashima in the Tonami plain (the dispersed-farmstead country whose homestead woodlands are the kainyo, 46 households): ~33 trees of trunk diameter >= 10 cm per homestead (1,542 trunks over the 46 households - arithmetic on the two footnoted figures, not a number the page states), of which cedar (sugi) was ~48% (~16 cedars per house), the rest spread over ~83 other species; ~6 species per homestead (range 1-14); for a large homestead, 200+ trees across 31 species is a GUESS at the top of the band, resting on no source. That count is trunks >= 10 cm ONLY - it leaves out saplings and the shrub layers below the canopy (the page sorts the stand into four layers by height and counts 44 / 76 / 80 / 153 species from the tallest down), and the page does not say whether it takes in the bamboo stand that stood on every homestead - so the honest figure for a typical grove is ~30-40 mature trees + a bamboo grove + understory, and a big one 100-200+ (a GUESS, resting on the unsourced figure above). The grove canopy footprint is therefore the LARGEST homestead appurtenance - bigger than the farmhouse, and far bigger than the garden or threshing yard (this page's own arithmetic on tree counts and crown sizes; no page read compares the footprints). Which sides of the house it covers was a regional custom: on the Sendai plain the north or the west, often lacking the south or the east side; on the Tonami plain every side but the east, the front of the house, where the grove is thin; and on the Izumo plain before the Meiji era the whole way round the house. How MANY sides it takes is rolled per settlement - two, three or all four - at Which sides of the house did a homestead grove take? How DEEP the belt is has no firm number in the record, so the drawn depth of the belt on each face (13 ft, one to two crowns) is a GUESS. Which WAY its windward sides face is chosen per map, because the record gives more than one answer: on the Tonami plain the tall sugi stand from the south face round to the west, with hackberry, alder and a little bamboo from the west round to the north, and the general account of the mainland yashikirin puts the tall trees on the west and south; the maps put the belt on the northwest by default; a map with a Tonami-like wind puts it on the south and west faces - that the Tonami plain's damaging wind comes from the south and west is a GUESS read off that planting, since no page read names the wind's quarter. The map draws EVERY canopy tree, one crown at its real size on real spacing (Forest density and crown size: a mean crown radius of 8.5 ft, with ~13 ft from one trunk's center to the next, no crown seated under another), on a footprint that is itself to scale - exactly as the farmhouse, the threshing yard and the garden are drawn at their true size. The grove's dominance therefore falls out of drawing the real thing: clearly the dominant homestead feature, a dense stand of dozens of trees, never a garden-sized clump of 5-10. (Cross-check on the windward rule: Okinawa's homestead groves sit on the E/N sides, because there the damaging winds come from the north (the monsoon) and the east (typhoons), not the mainland's west and south - same logic, different geography, which is exactly why the windward side is a per-map knob.)
  • Before 1868: the grove itself is in the Edo record, but the tree count above is not - it is the 1987 survey's. The grove's shape is dated in two regions, two windward sides from the early Edo period and a full ring until the Meiji era, and no page counts how often each shape occurred. The evidence is at Was the homestead grove there before 1868, and how big was it?, and for the shape, with the sides the maps roll, at Which sides of the house did a homestead grove take?
