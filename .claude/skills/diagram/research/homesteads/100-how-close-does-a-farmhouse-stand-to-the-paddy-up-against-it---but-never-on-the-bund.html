How close does a farmhouse stand to the paddy? Up against it - but never on the bund

Sources: pmc7538448-levee, paddy-field-enwiki, fengshui-zhwiki

The GM: "One of the farmhouses in the reference hamlet appears to actually be touching the edge of the rice paddy fields. Is this realistic? ... I do imagine that they would be pretty much right up against the edge, but actually touching looks wrong to me."

What the record gives, and what it does not. No source states a setback in feet - the question is not one anyone measured - so the floor is DERIVED from three attested parts:

  • The bund (aze) is a thing, not a line. PMC 7538448: rice paddy levees "are constructed and maintained to retain water in the paddies and to allow the passage of people and transportation of tools", and "farmers generally maintain levee grasslands by periodic mowing". Wikipedia "Paddy field": plots "separated by bunds approximately 10 cm in height" (a Korean example). The IRRI Rice Knowledge Bank's "How to construct bunds" tells a farmer to "Construct no wider and taller than 50 cm x 30 cm bunds, around the field". Pulling the other way: FAO's Fish Culture in Rice Fields, ch. 7 gives a permanent "dry bund" a 25 cm minimum height (60 recommended) and a 180 cm base tapering to 60 cm - a different, heavier structure than a field partition. So "about 1.5 ft" is one convention among several, and the 6 ft floor below leans on it lightly.
  • The house's roof reaches past its wall. A thatched farmhouse's eaves overhang the wall by about 3 ft; its drip line is where the wall's rain lands.
  • The plot fronts the paddy. The nucleated cluster stands on the field margin with its face to the water (the seat with the hill behind and water before it - 背山、向陽、面水 in the fengshui phrase (the source's water is a watercourse; the paddy is this page's extension) - beishan mianshui, "back to the hill, facing the water" - research above), so "right up against the edge" is the norm the GM expects.

Therefore a wall nearer than bund + path + eave stands with its roof over the levee path and its drip line in the rice. 1.5 + ~1.5 + 3 ft is 6 ft, the floor a wall may never cross - a MINIMUM below the 10-13 ft the map draws.

The bund's width is a GUESS, since no readable page supports it, so the 6 ft floor is a soft threshold: two of its three parts are not read.
