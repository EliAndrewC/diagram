• kitamoto-inakoki-niwa - 「The place where threshing was done was usually the house's yard.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「脱穀を行う場所は、家の庭が普通だった。」)
  • kitamoto-inakoki-niwa - 「Straw mats (mushiro) were spread and the koki [threshing combs] set out in a row, and the rice was held a handful at a time and drawn through.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「莚（むしろ）を敷いてコキを並べ、ひと握りずつ稲を持って稲を引き扱く。」)
  • kitamoto-inakoki-niwa - 「Mats were spread to fill the yard, and rice that had hung on the rack [hande] was dried about a day, rice threshed without much drying about three days of good weather.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「庭一杯に莚を広げ、ハンデに掛けた稲なら一日程度、あまり干さずに脱穀した稲なら天気の良い日に三日間ほど干した。」)
  • seika-mingu-dakkoku - 「The threshed momi is spread on mats and dried in the light of the sun.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「脱穀したもみはむしろに広げ、太陽の光に当てて乾燥（かんそう）させます。」)
  • ehime-mushiroboshi - 「as the ways of drying the rice called jikiboshi, betabori, jiboshi and so on say, it was dried three days in the laid-flat state and taken in, and once made into momi was mat-dried [mushiroboshi] a further three days or more, up to about six days.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「稲の干し方は、ジキボシ、ベタボリ、ジボシなどというようにベタガリの状態で三日干して取り込み、籾にしてさらに三日以上六日位ムシロボシをする。」)
  • kasuga-aki-wo-suru - 「When the threshing was over, so that the momi would not rot, the work of drying the momi was done every day on the mats」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「稲扱ぎが終わると、籾が腐らないよう、毎日むしろの上で籾干し作業をし」)
  • kitamoto-inakoki-niwa - 「Husking was usually night work.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「籾すりはたいていヨナベ仕事になった。」); 「Husking was done in the earthen-floored room inside the house or in the barn」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「籾すりは家の中の土間や納屋で行い」)
  • kitamoto-mushiro-niwa - 「At harvest time straw mats were spread over the whole yard, and the size of the yard was often expressed in the number of these mats.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「収穫期には庭一面にムシロが敷かれ、しばしば庭の広さはこのムシロの枚数で表現された。」); 「Ordinarily forty to sixty mats; among them there were also houses that went past a hundred mats.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「普通四〇〜六〇枚、中には百枚を越す家もあった。」)
  • kitamoto-inakoki-niwa - 「A large farm kept as many as a hundred or a hundred and fifty mats ready」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大きな農家なら一〇〇枚も一五〇枚も莚を用意しておき」)
  • tobunken-mushiro - 「At Nishidani the number of warp threads is 21 upper threads and 21 lower threads, 42 in all, and with these a mushiro (莚) mat of about 3 shaku in width and roughly 6 shaku in length (90 x 180 cm) is woven.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「西谷では経糸の数は上糸 21 本・下糸 21 本の計 42 本で、これで幅およそ３尺、長さ６尺（90 × 180㌢）程度の莚が織りあがる。」)
