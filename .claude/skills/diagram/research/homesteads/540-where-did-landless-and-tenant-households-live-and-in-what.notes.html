• kotobank-mizunomi - 「In the Edo period, against the honbyakusho who were landholding farmers, the farmers without assessed land who owned no fields were called mizunomi or mizunomi-byakusho.」 (translated; )「In the villages of the Edo period, the mizunomi-byakusho, unlike the honbyakusho, were not seen as full farmers and were not permitted to take part in village government.」 (translated; )「Because they owned no land, they became tenants, or combined this with migrant service and day labor, or with various crafts, and formed the poor stratum of the farming villages.」 (translated; ) (the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry by Uesugi Masahiko)
  • kotobank-nago - 「Where the mizunomi-byakusho was a tenant who rented fields, the nago was distinguished by also renting the house plot and house as well as the fields.」 (translated; ) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, revised edition, on the nago of the early modern to modern period)
  • l7r-merchant-families - 「Most farmers are tenant farmers, and most landlords are merchant landlords, so merchants end up making a sizeable percentage of their revenue through farm rent.」 (the setting's canon, in "Merchant Families")
  • kotobank-kadoya - 「A name for the dependent farmers of the Edo period.」 (translated; )「It comes from their living in the gate-hut within the master household's house plot.」 (translated; ) (the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry by Baba Akira)
  • kotobank-kadoya - 「Of the house plots registered in the survey registers, many had within the plot's enclosure, together with the main house, huts, kadoya, retirement houses and the like; in the huts, kadoya and retirement houses lived weak farmers (small farmers on the way to independence) under the master household's protection and control, and the master household lived in the main house.」 (translated; ) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, older edition, as excerpted on the page from its entry on the yashiki)
  • sukumo-shishi-kenchi - 「Farmers are registered in the survey registers as cultivators, but there were many registered persons without a house plot, not registered against any house plot.」 (translated; )「Most of these registered persons without a house plot are said to have been small farmers - kin, hikan, nago and the like of a powerful farmer who owned a house plot - who lived in a separate building or a hut on the plot of the head of the household or of the master household.」 (translated; )「Looking at Yamana village, against 360 registered persons, the holders of house plots number 120.」 (translated; )
  • takizawa-sonshi-yashiki - 「At the land survey of Genbun 2 (1737)」 (translated; ) 「to nago-byakusho, as a rule, no house plot is given, though according to the fief-holder's assessed yield a house allowance alone may be admitted」 (translated; ) (the Nanbu domain's rule of 1737, as the Takizawa village history gives it)
  • takizawa-sonshi-yashiki - 「In the Nanbu domain too, in Shotoku 5 (1715), an order restricting branch houses was issued, forbidding the taking of a house plot on the takanome (land already cultivated) and permitting branch houses on vacant house plots or at the field edge (nogiwa) and along the roads at the foot of the lord's hills」 (translated; )「In Shiwa district, by the custom for branch houses, giving a branch house one cho of cultivated land had long been held the best area, and giving it three buildings - main house, stable and privy - as one hearth was the minimum.」 (translated; )
