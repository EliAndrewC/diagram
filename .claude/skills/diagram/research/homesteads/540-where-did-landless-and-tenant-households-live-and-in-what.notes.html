• kotobank-mizunomi - 「In the Edo period, against the honbyakusho who were landholding farmers, the farmers without assessed land who owned no fields were called mizunomi or mizunomi-byakusho.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代に、高持(たかもち)百姓である本百姓に対して、田畑を所有しない無高の百姓を水呑とか水呑百姓とよんだ。」)「In the villages of the Edo period, the mizunomi-byakusho, unlike the honbyakusho, were not seen as full farmers and were not permitted to take part in village government.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の村では、水呑百姓は本百姓と異なり、一人前の百姓とみられず、村政への参加が許されなかった。」)「Because they owned no land, they became tenants, or combined this with migrant service and day labor, or with various crafts, and formed the poor stratum of the farming villages.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「彼らは土地を所有しないため、小作人となったり、出稼奉公(でかせぎぼうこう)、日傭取(ひようとり)を兼ねたり、諸職人を兼ねたりして農村の貧農層を形成していた。」) (the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry by Uesugi Masahiko)
  • kotobank-nago - 「Where the mizunomi-byakusho was a tenant who rented fields, the nago was distinguished by also renting the house plot and house as well as the fields.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水呑百姓が耕地を借りる小作人であるのに対して，名子は耕地の外に家屋敷をも借りるという点で区別されたのである。」) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, revised edition, on the nago of the early modern to modern period)
  • l7r-merchant-families - 「Most farmers are tenant farmers, and most landlords are merchant landlords, so merchants end up making a sizeable percentage of their revenue through farm rent.」 (the setting's canon, in "Merchant Families")
  • kotobank-kadoya - 「A name for the dependent farmers of the Edo period.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の隷属農民の呼称。」)「It comes from their living in the gate-hut within the master household's house plot.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「主家の屋敷地内の門小屋に居住していたことに由来する。」) (the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry by Baba Akira)
  • kotobank-kadoya - 「Of the house plots registered in the survey registers, many had within the plot's enclosure, together with the main house, huts, kadoya, retirement houses and the like; in the huts, kadoya and retirement houses lived weak farmers (small farmers on the way to independence) under the master household's protection and control, and the master household lived in the main house.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「検地帳に登録された屋敷は，その多くが屋敷囲いの内部に母屋（おもや）とともに小屋，門屋（かどや），隠居屋などを備え，小屋，門屋，隠居屋には主家の庇護･支配を受ける弱小農民(自立過程の小農)が起居し，母屋には主家が住いした。」) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, older edition, as excerpted on the page from its entry on the yashiki)
  • sukumo-shishi-kenchi - 「Farmers are registered in the survey registers as cultivators, but there were many registered persons without a house plot, not registered against any house plot.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農民は検地帳には耕作者として登録されているが、屋敷のところには登録されていない無屋敷登録人が多くいた。」)「Most of these registered persons without a house plot are said to have been small farmers - kin, hikan, nago and the like of a powerful farmer who owned a house plot - who lived in a separate building or a hut on the plot of the head of the household or of the master household.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この無屋敷登録人の多くは、屋敷を所有する有力農民の血縁者、被官、名子などで、家長ないし主家の屋敷の別棟、小屋などに住んでいた小農民であるという。」)「Looking at Yamana village, against 360 registered persons, the holders of house plots number 120.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「山奈村についてみると登録されている３６０人に対して屋敷持は１２０である。」)
  • takizawa-sonshi-yashiki - 「to nago-byakusho, as a rule, no house plot is given, though according to the land-holder's assessment a house allowance alone may be admitted」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「名子百姓に対しては原則として屋敷を遣わさぬが、地頭の高に応じて家代ばかりは認めることもある」) (the Nanbu domain's rule of 1737, as the Takizawa village history gives it)
  • takizawa-sonshi-yashiki - 「In the Nanbu domain too, in Shotoku 5 (1715), an order restricting branch houses was issued, forbidding the taking of a house plot on the takanome (land already cultivated) and permitting branch houses on vacant house plots or at the field edge (nogiwa) and along the roads at the foot of the lord's hills」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「南部藩においても正徳五年（1715年）に分家制限令を出し、「高の目」（既耕地）に屋敷どりすることを禁じ、「明屋敷」あるいは野際（のぎわ）・御山裾道への分家は許可している」)「In Shiwa district, by the custom for branch houses, giving a branch house one cho of cultivated land had long been held the best area, and giving it three buildings - main house, stable and privy - as one hearth was the minimum.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「紫波郡においては、分家慣行として、古くから分家に対して一町歩の耕地を与えることが最高適正面積とされ、母屋・厩・雪隠三棟一カマドとして、分家に与えるのを最低限としていた。」)
