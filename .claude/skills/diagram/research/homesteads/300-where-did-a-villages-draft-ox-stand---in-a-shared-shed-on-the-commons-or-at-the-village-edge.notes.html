  • agrinews-2023-tajima-maya - 「The maya is a space for keeping cattle set up inside the house. ... Partly because there was little flat land and not enough spare land to build a cattle shed, the dwelling style of the maya, keeping cattle inside the house, came about. ... According to the museum, at the peak in the 1950s there were about 4,000 'houses with a maya' in present Mikata district (excluding former Kasumi town).」 (translated; ) (the second sentence is the page's direct quotation of Noda Masanobu, deputy director of the Tajima Cattle Museum)
  • kotobank-umaya - 「A farmhouse's stable is divided into the inner stable (uchi-umaya) inside the main house and the outer stable (soto-umaya), which stands on its own.」 (translated; ); 「In regions such as southern Kanto inner stables are few, but nationally the inner stable was widely practiced, taking a corner of the doma, the work space inside the main house.」 (translated; ) (Miyazawa Satoshi's article in Heibonsha's Kaitei shinpan Sekai daihyakka jiten, s.v. 厩; the article gives no period for the inner stable's spread)
  • ndl-crd-shakkogyu - 「In Sanuki cattle were mainly used for cultivation. At the June and November cultivation times draft cattle ran short, so there was a custom of borrowing cattle for that period from neighboring Awa. ... The mountain villages of Awa have much grassland, so cattle are easily grazed and they kept more plow cattle than their paddies needed, which is why they lent cattle to Sanuki in the busy farming season. They were called kometori-ushi (rice-taking cattle) because the cattle were lent and one koku of rice was taken in return. ... At times, as many as several hundred cattle came over the passes on the Awa-Sanuki border.」 (translated; )
