• kotobank-yashiki - 「Generally a peasant's yashiki had the main house and the naya inside the yashiki enclosure, and in many a stable for cattle or horses was attached to one of them. The main house was built behind the middle of the enclosure, and the open ground on the south side of the main house was used as the work yard and drying yard, and was called kado or kadoniwa.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一般に百姓の屋敷は，屋敷囲いの内部に母屋と納屋を備え，そのいずれかに牛馬の厩舎を付設しているものが多い。母屋は屋敷囲いの中央より後方に建てられ，母屋の南面の空地は作業場，乾燥場として利用され，それを〈かど〉〈かどにわ〉などと呼んだ。」) (the Heibonsha entry, early modern section)
  • kotobank-yashiki - 「Usually a hedge or an earthen wall runs around it, and there are also some that dug a moat to keep out intruders. The buildings within the yashiki include, besides the main house, the kamaya, the bath, the privy, the storehouse, the work shed, the compost shed, the livestock shed and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「たいてい周囲には垣根や土塀がめぐらしてあり，堀を掘って外からの侵入を防ごうとしたものもある。屋敷内の建築施設としては，母屋のほか，釜屋，風呂，便所，土蔵，仕事小屋，堆肥小屋，家畜小屋などがある。」) (the Britannica entry)
  • kotobank-yashiki - 「Some peasant yashiki of villages on flat land had a homestead grove from the north to the northwest ... and this was customarily called igune (Tohoku), kune (Kanto), tsuijimatsu (Izumo) and so on」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平坦地農村の百姓の屋敷には，その北側から西北にかけて屋敷林を備えるものがあり…これを〈いぐね〉（東北），〈くね〉（関東），〈築地松〉（出雲）などと…呼びならわした。」) (the Heibonsha entry)
  • yashikirin-jawiki - 「"Kune" means a land boundary, and the igune is what divides the inside of the plot from the outside」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「「くね」は地境を意味し、居久根は敷地の内外を分けるものである」)
  • kotobank-kune - 「A fence woven of bamboo or the like. Also, a hedge. Kune-gaki.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「竹などを編んだ垣根。また、いけがき。くね垣。」) - with its example from the kyogen Uri-nusubito, dated 「late Muromachi to early modern」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「室町末‐近世初」) (the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry)
  • kotobank-yashiki - 「From the Tohoku region to the Kanto, the yashiki is relatively wide and its sheds many, but going west the opposite tends to be seen, and the yashiki is more often enclosed by its attached buildings than by a homestead grove.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「東北地方から関東にかけては、屋敷は比較的広く小屋の数も多いが、西に行くにしたがって、これと反対になる傾向がみられ、また屋敷林よりも付属施設で屋敷を囲う場合が多い。」) (the Nipponica entry, by Takeuchi Yoshitaro)
  • yashikirin-jawiki - 「its original function was to strengthen the earth bank enclosing the house, built against floods」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「本来の機能は水防のために築かれた家の周囲を囲む土居を強化するためにあった」); 「Before the Meiji era it enclosed the whole circumference of the house」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明治時代以前には家の全周を囲っていたが」) (of the Izumo tsuijimatsu; the second sentence carries no reference on the page)
