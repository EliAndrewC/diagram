• kotobank-yashiki - 「Generally a peasant's yashiki had the main house and the naya inside the yashiki enclosure, and in many a stable for cattle or horses was attached to one of them. The main house was built behind the middle of the enclosure, and the open ground on the south side of the main house was used as the work yard and drying yard, and was called kado or kadoniwa.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Heibonsha entry, early modern section)
  • kotobank-yashiki - 「Usually a hedge or an earthen wall runs around it, and there are also some that dug a moat to keep out intruders. The buildings within the yashiki include, besides the main house, the kamaya (the cauldron house, where the cooking was done), the bath, the privy, the storehouse, the work shed, the compost shed, the livestock shed and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Britannica entry)
  • kotobank-yashiki - 「Some peasant yashiki of villages on flat land had a homestead grove from the north to the northwest ... and this was customarily called igune (Tohoku), kune (Kanto), tsuijimatsu (Izumo) and so on」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Heibonsha entry)
