• kotobank-minka - 「In the Edo period, the state of farming villages near Utsunomiya in the Kanto region becomes clear from records in which, when the Tokugawa shogun made his pilgrimage to the Toshogu at Nikko, floor plans of every house in the village were drawn up to lodge his retainers.」 (translated; )「The eighteenth-century records among them show that in the villages of the time houses of around 20 tsubo (about 66 square meters) were the most common, there were also houses of 4 to 5 tsubo, and many small houses of under 10 tsubo, while large houses were few」 (translated; ) (the Nipponica entry by Hirai Kiyoshi; the feet are this page's conversion)
  • nihonminkaen-kanto - 「Building type: farmhouse. Construction: gabled, thatched, length 14.5 m, depth 8.9 m. Date: the very end of the 17th century」 (translated; ) (the Hirose house)
  • nihonminkaen-kanagawa - 「Building type: farmhouse (headman's house). Construction: hipped, thatched, length 15.6 m, depth 8.9 m. Date: the fourth year of Jokyo (1687), from an ink inscription」 (translated; ) (the Kitamura house); 「Building type: farmhouse. Construction: hipped, thatched, length 13.6 m, depth 8.2 m. Date: the later 17th century」 (translated; ) (the Kiyomiya house); 「Building type: farmhouse (headman's house). Construction: hip-and-gable, thatched, length 16.4 m, depth 9.1 m. Date: the very end of the 17th century to the early 18th century」 (translated; ) (the Ito house); 「Building type: farmhouse (headman's house). Construction: hip-and-gable, thatched, length 14.5 m, depth 7.3 m. Date: the very end of the 17th century」 (translated; ) (the Iwasawa house)
  • boso-no-mura-kazusa - 「Taking as its models the main house of the Akiba family of Isagoda in Oamishirasato and the gate-house, storehouse, stable, barn and wood shed of the Naito family of Kayahashi in Ichihara, built in the later Edo period, it reproduces a headman-class farmhouse by traditional methods」 (translated; )「Main house: a thatched building 10 ken wide, 6 ken deep, 192.79 square meters in area.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-nihon-no-minka - 「A hereditary shoya's house with a frontage of 30 meters, with five entrances used according to rank, and holding all its work spaces inside: a good example of an upper farmhouse.」 (translated; ) (the former Hirayama house, Goshogawara, Aomori, 1769)
