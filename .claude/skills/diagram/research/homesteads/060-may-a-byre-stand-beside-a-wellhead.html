May a byre stand beside a wellhead?

Sources: magariya-jawiki (the stable projecting on the south face, joined to the house and warmed from its hearth), fao-farm-structures-ch14, ido-jawiki, watering-trough-enwiki, pig-toilet-enwiki

Answer: yes, and the vernacular puts them far closer than our maps do.

On Kashikawa a shared byre stands 38 ft from a communal wellhead.

Japan (the closest analogue, and the strongest signal). In the magariya L-plan farmhouse the draft animal lives under the house's own roof: the stable wing (umaya) meets the dwelling (omoya) at right angles and extends off its SOUTH face (the Tono basin's arrangement - the article records both handed forms, and the magariya as the house of the better-off),31 warmed by the air from the kitchen hearth, a sign of what the horses were worth (the sunlight reason is on no page read).103 And the well (ido), where a house had one, sat in the rear corner of the earthen-floored doma or in a rear projection room - i.e. inside the same building.156 A household's horse and a household's well were therefore separated by the width of a farmhouse, on the order of 20-40 ft, as a matter of course.138 This is also why these maps draw no European multi-stall barn.

China (the setting's model). Vernacular villages co-located the animal facilities with the latrine - pigsties grouped with the privy (cattle sheds and chicken houses too, in this page's reading) - because both ends of that group feed the same manure economy that kept the soil fertile for millennia - the pigsty privy is attested from Han-dynasty models (a saying that night soil is to be treasured like gold is on no page read).137 That is a positive siting rule about the muck cluster, and it is worth knowing; what it is NOT is a rule holding livestock away from drinking water. No separation doctrine turned up in the geomantic or vernacular-layout material.

Elsewhere, corroborating. The public watering trough at a village or town water point - a trough, ideally with a well and a pump beside it (this page's phrasing) - is a widespread and well-documented arrangement.136 Where communities invested in water infrastructure at all, watering the beasts AT it was the norm rather than a transgression.135

What is genuinely true and worth not over-reading: livestock near a shallow, unsealed well IS a real contamination vector, and modern rural-water guidance measures exactly that (the Chinese studies are on no page read).134 But that is a public-health finding about the consequences, not evidence that the builders sited to avoid it. A generator that separated byre from well would be drawing a modern sanitary intuition, not a Rokugani village.

The decision, therefore: the beasts are watered at the well, and a byre near one is correct. A byre never overlaps the wellhead itself.

And a byre does not always stand against its own farmhouse. Both arrangements are attested, and neither is the correction of the other.133 A household that OWNS its team houses it in its own homestead - the magariya's short arm IS the stable, and the north-China courtyard house puts its animals in the EAST wing, the one the summer afternoon sun and the winter northwesterly make unfit to live in, which takes the stable, the kitchen and the grain store together92 - while a team that is SHARED or hired stands where the borrowing household can walk to it, on the common ground among the homesteads.104 Two supportable forms are rolled per settlement rather than settled once, which is also what lets two maps differ honestly: on a courtyard map the byres follow the wealthiest households and each stays within its owner's own yard, and on a commons map, the commoner of the two, they stand out among the homesteads where a borrower can reach one. A flat claim that a byre always abuts its farmhouse is not supported by the record and is not what the map draws.
