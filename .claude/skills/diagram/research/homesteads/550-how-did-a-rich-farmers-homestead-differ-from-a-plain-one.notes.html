• kotobank-gono - 「Gōnō: the village landlords who grew from the middle of the eighteenth century onward with the development of small commodity production.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「18世紀半ば以降，小商品生産の展開に伴って成長していった村方地主をいう。」)「These gōnō had two lineages: those converted from the earlier myoden landlords and sakutoku landlords, and those who grew through commercial activity and commodity production; the latter in most cases won their standing as village landlords through village disputes.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「これらの豪農には，それまでの名田地主・作徳地主が転化したものと，商業活動や商品生産によって成長してきたものとの二つの系譜があり，後者は多くの場合，村方騒動によって村方地主としての地位を獲得していった。」) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, revised edition, by Sasaki Junnosuke)
  • sennan-yamada - 「The Yamada family were wealthy farmers from the Edo period who served as shoya generation after generation, and boasted a village assessment of 1,600 koku.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「山田家は、代々、庄屋職を務めた江戸時代からの豪農で村高1,600石を誇りました。」)

  • yamagata-kashiwakura - 「In Oka, around the Kashiwakura Kyuzaemon house (the head house), the houses of the Kashiwakura kin, the Kashiwakura Soemon house first among them, stand side by side.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「岡地内には、柏倉九左衛門家（本家）の周辺に、柏倉惣右衛門家をはじめとする柏倉一類の各家が建ち並んでいます。」)「Surrounding each house, black board walls run on for a total length of about 1,000 meters.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「各家の周りを囲むように、総延長約1,000mにも及ぶ黒塀が連なっています。」)「A plot of about 1,600 tsubo, adjoining the Kashiwakura house and lying to its northwest, with nine buildings centered on the main house, the front storehouse and rear storehouse among them - 310 tsubo of buildings.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「敷地約1,600坪、柏倉家住宅と地続きで北西に位置し、主屋を中心に前蔵、裏蔵など９棟、310坪の建物があります。」) (the Soemon house)
