• kotobank-nanushi - 「The nanushi was a peasant by status, but belonged to a stratum a step above the ordinary villagers: they could build a gate on their house plot and set a formal entrance step (shikidai) in the main house, and were allowed exceptions in dress and footwear (wearing silk and leather-soled sandals).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「名主は身分としては百姓であるが，一般村民よりは一段高い階層に属し，その屋敷に門を構えたり，母屋（おもや）に式台を設けることができ，着衣や履物にも特例を許されていた（絹物や雪駄の着用）。」) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, revised edition, on the Edo-period nanushi)
  • nagayamon-kotobank - 「In farming villages this nagayamon was permitted only to the upper farmers who served as village officials, such as nanushi and shoya.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この長屋門は農村においては名主，庄屋など村役人を務める上層の農民だけに建てることが許されていた。」) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry); 「These had no guardroom, and the rooms either side of the doorway were used as menservants' rooms, attendants' rooms, a barn, a stable and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「これらには番所はなく、扉口両わきの部屋は男部屋、伴部屋(ともべや)、納屋(なや)、厩(うまや)などに利用された。」) (the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry, on the nagayamon of farmers who served as shoya or nanushi)
  • kotobank-nanushi - 「The nanushi did the daily business at home.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「名主は日常業務を自宅で行っていた。」)「Village officials such as the kumigashira gathered at the nanushi's house to allot the annual tax and the village's expenses, and to draw up the registers the lord ordered and the petitions from the village to the lord.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「名主宅に組頭等の村役人が集まり，年貢・村入用の割当てをしたり，領主から命ぜられる諸帳簿や，村より領主への願書類等の作成に当たった。」) (the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, revised edition)
  • boso-no-mura-kazusa - 「The plot is 2,500 square meters, made up of eight structures: the main house, the storehouse, the nagayamon, the stable, the barn, the woodshed, the work shed and the well.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「敷地は2,500㎡、主屋・土蔵・長屋門・馬小屋・納屋・木小屋・作業小屋・井戸の８棟で構成されています。」) (the square feet are this page's conversion)
  • sennan-yamada - 「The Yamada family were wealthy farmers from the Edo period who served as shoya generation after generation, and boasted a village assessment of 1,600 koku.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「山田家は、代々、庄屋職を務めた江戸時代からの豪農で村高1,600石を誇りました。」)「The eight structures on the plot of about 840 tsubo - the main house, the entrance wing, the kitchen wing, the rice storehouse, the storehouse, the front gate, the nagayamon and the earthen wall - are all buildings of the middle to late Edo period and are nationally registered tangible cultural properties.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「約840坪の敷地内の計８棟、主屋、玄関棟、台所棟、米蔵、土蔵、表門、長屋門、土塀は、いずれも江戸中期から末期の建造物で国の登録有形文化財です。」) (the square meters are this page's conversion)
  • yamagata-kashiwakura - 「became the grand shoya of a group of villages assessed at 6,700 koku.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「組村高6,700石の大庄屋になりました。」)「The Kashiwakura house has a plot, inside and outside the residence, of some 2,300 tsubo and more, with about 360 tsubo of buildings: looking past the east-facing nagayamon, the main house stands in front, to the south two storehouses are joined by a corridor, to the north is the north granary that can hold 4,500 bales of rice, and behind the main house is the house storehouse.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「柏倉家住宅は、邸内・外の敷地約2,300余坪、建物は約360坪を有しており、東向きの長屋門を見越して、正面に主屋（おもや）、南に２棟の土蔵を廊下でつなぎ、北には4,500俵の米が収納できる北倉、そして主屋の後方には家蔵があります。」)
  • takizawa-sonshi-yashiki - 「At the survey of Genbun 2 (1737), where a house plot of five to three se had until then been allowed to those farming ten koku or more, thereafter up to three se was allowed for upland-type plots and up to five se for paddy-type plots, and the rest was to be entered in the survey's assessment」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「元文二年（1737年）の検地に際し、従来は十石以上を耕作するものには五畝乃至三畝の屋敷を認容していたが、以後は畑形の場合は三畝までを認め、田形の場合は五畝までとし、その他は検地高に編入すること」) (the Nanbu domain's rule, as the Takizawa village history gives it; the square meters are this page's conversion, one se being thirty tsubo)
