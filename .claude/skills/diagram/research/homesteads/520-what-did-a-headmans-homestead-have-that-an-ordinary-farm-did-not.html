What did a headman's homestead have that an ordinary farm did not?

Sources: kotobank-nanushi (the gate and formal entrance the office allowed, and the office work done at home), nagayamon-kotobank (the gatehouse reserved to village officials), boso-no-mura-kazusa (a headman-class farm and its plot), sennan-yamada (a hereditary headman's plot and its eight buildings), yamagata-kashiwakura (a grand headman over a group of villages), takizawa-sonshi-yashiki (the house plot a domain allowed a farmer of ten koku or more), l7r-budgets (the setting's canon)

The office brought the right to a gate and a formal entrance. A village headman was a farmer by status but of a higher stratum than the other villagers: they could build a gate on their plot and a formal entrance step on the main house, and wear what others could not. The nagayamon, the gatehouse with rooms either side of the passage, was in farming villages allowed only to the upper farmers who served as village officials, and a farmer's version used its side rooms as servants' rooms, a barn or a stable. The office was a right to these, not a duty to build them.

The house was the village office. The headman did the village's daily business at home, and the other village officials met at the headman's house to share out the year's tax and the village's costs and to draw up the registers and petitions. No page read describes a separate office building; the work happened in the house. The village meeting itself is taken up in Where did the village meet?

What the surviving headmen's farms hold. A reconstructed headman-class farm of the late Edo period in Chiba stands on a plot of 2,500 square meters (about 26,900 square feet, some 164 feet a side if square) with eight structures: the main house, a storehouse, a nagayamon, a stable, a barn, a woodshed, a work shed and a well. The Yamada family of Sennan, hereditary headmen and wealthy farmers, keep eight buildings of the middle to late Edo period on a plot of about 840 tsubo (about 2,780 square meters): the main house, an entrance wing, a kitchen wing, a rice storehouse, a second storehouse, a front gate, a nagayamon and an earthen wall. So a headman's farm carried one or two storehouses. A grand headman over a whole group of villages is on another scale: the Kashiwakura house in Yamagata has about 2,300 tsubo of plot, an east-facing nagayamon, two storehouses to the south joined by a corridor, a north granary for 4,500 bales of rice and a house storehouse behind. The Chiba page names no hedge or wall round its plot; the Yamada plot has its earthen wall.

How much ground that is against an ordinary farm. The Nanbu domain in the far north had until its survey of 1737 allowed a farmer working ten koku or more a house plot of three to five se (about 300 to 500 square meters), and from then on at most three se on an upland plot and five on a paddy plot. The two village headmen above stand on five to nine times that (this page's arithmetic, across two regions and between a rule and two survivors, so a degree, not a measure). That the survivors are the grand end of the range is taken up in How big was an ordinary farmhouse, and how much bigger was a well-off one?, which also measures the headman's house itself.

The setting's own word. The canon gives a village a headman's household only "slightly larger" than the others.

What this means for the map. The gate is a KNOB, not a rule: a headman might build one, and the nagayamon was theirs to build, but the sources give the right and not how many used it. The same holds for a wall round the plot, which one survivor has and one does not name. The maps draw the headman with no gate and no wall, by the GM's ruling of 2026-07-21 in What makes the headman's house different?; against this finding that is a deliberate deviation until the GM rules on the knob. One storehouse is what the maps draw and the lower end of what the survivors carry; a second is attested. A headman's plot drawn from the two survivors would be about 2,500 to 2,800 square meters; for an ordinary village's headman, whose plot no page read measures, that is a GUESS.
