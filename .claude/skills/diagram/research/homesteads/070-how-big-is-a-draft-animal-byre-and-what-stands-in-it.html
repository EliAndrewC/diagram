How big is a draft-animal byre, and what stands in it?

Sources: fao-farm-structures-cattle (shade and stall dimensions per animal) and tnau-cattle-housing (covered and open space per adult beast), both modern cattle standards; this project's drawing decision

Wet-rice plowing and puddling turn on an ox or a water buffalo, and the shed drawn for one is a single-bay open-fronted lean-to of about 16 by 11 ft, some 15 sq m. That is the room one or two draft animals need and no more. Nothing that can be read measures a premodern byre: the only figures available are modern housing standards, and what a standard states is a generous minimum - the room a keeper today is told to provide for a modern beast - rather than the size of anything anyone built. One such standard, a design manual for the tropics, reckons a shade structure at 2.5 to 3 sq m per animal and a single stall at 2.2 m by 1.2 m for a cow of 500 to 600 kg. Checked against a second - an Indian extension table allowing an adult beast 3.5 sq m under cover with 7 sq m of open ground beside it and 60 to 75 cm of manger space - it comes out on the same order of size, which says the figure is a common one rather than one page's idea of it; both are modern recommendations, so their agreement cannot stand in for a measurement neither of them made. A 15 sq m shed holds one or two beasts by either allowance, with fodder and a passage beside them, and it is sized by that allowance rather than by anything attested. The size is doing a second job as well. Anything larger starts to read as a European multi-stall barn, and there are none on these maps, because the draft animal of an East Asian wet-rice farm lives under the farmhouse roof (the question above). A byre on the sheet is a shelter for a beast or two, kept visibly well under the farmhouse's own 120 sq m.

The rule the map follows: a byre is drawn as a small plank-roofed shed of about 16 by 11 ft with a dark stall mouth, distinct from the solid gray storehouse and from the dwellings, and it is kept small on purpose.
