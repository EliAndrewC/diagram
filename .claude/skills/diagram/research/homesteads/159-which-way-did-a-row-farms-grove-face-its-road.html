Which way did a row farm's grove face its road?

Sources: akiyama-1992-tama-yashikirin (row farms' groves beside their roads), miura-2019-yashikiyama (a street village's windbreaks), yamamoto-2016-asuka-yashiki (which way the houses of a clustered village and of a street village faced)

Where the grove stood on the planned rows is at How wide was a farm in a row?. This question asks what a row farm's grove and house did when the road ran on the windward side.

Two forms

Row farms kept windbreaks: a street village on the Toyama plain was described in 1934 as holding them as the plain's scattered farms did. Two forms are attested:

  • The grove kept to the windward, the road on that side. At Ogawa a farm settled in the Kanbun era (1661-1671) had its road on the north; the grove north of its house was mostly zelkova, rather thin, and its land ran from the road southward as house lot, field, then woodland. Its bamboo grew on the south and was sold to basket makers: across the road to the north stood a row of farms with groves, so no bamboo was needed on that side as a windbreak; at Minami-cho, on the old Yanagikubo new fields, another farm south of its road had its bamboo on the south, which the survey ties to the row of grove farms across the road. A third kept a single line of white oak and zelkova along the road north of its house.
  • The house turned to its road. At Yanagikubo a house faced east, its entrance path opening from the road on the east, lined with zelkova. In Asuka, in the Nara basin, every main house of a clustered village had its roof ridge east-west, which the authors read as facing south, although its main street runs north-south, while in two villages strung along a north-south street about eight houses in ten were turned to the street.

Where the gate of a farm with its road on the north stood, and whether it reached the road through its grove or round it, was not found (a GUESS).

What it means for a map

The grove's face is a knob with two attested forms: the grove to the windward north and west with the front open south where the road ran on the north, the bamboo set south where a row of grove farms across the road already breaks the wind; or the house turned to its road (accurate for the Musashino farms and the Asuka villages). The map draws only the first, a full grove on the windward sides, and a farm of the near row, between the paddy and the street, with the street to its north reaches it by a path round its grove (the path, a GUESS).
