What stood on a farmstead - the inventory, with numbers

Sources: sugiura-1973-fuzoku (the one quantified source)

The GM: "What things would exist on a noticeable percentage of farmhouses that we are not currently representing on our maps ... What, if anything, are we missing?"

The one quantified source. Sugiura Tadashi, "農村集落における農家の付属建物について - 宮城県宮崎町の例" (On the ancillary buildings of farmhouses in agricultural settlements: Miyazaki-machi, Miyagi), Tōhoku Chiri 25(3), 1973, pp. 145-152 (JStage tga1948/25/3/25_3_145): a July 1972 survey of all 87 households in three hamlets (Asahi, Kita-Nagashida, Minami-Nagashida) on the terrace south of the Naruse river, every roofed outbuilding counted and classified by function. Averages per household: 4.38 outbuildings (4.57 for farm households) - "surprisingly many" against the Tōhoku norm of a single main building. Table 5 gives the MEAN NUMBER of buildings per household by function - not the share of households owning one: the paper counts every roofed outbuilding, a building serving two functions counts once under each, and a household may hold two of a kind (the non-farm row carries 1.40 storage sheds per household), so each figure is an upper bound on the share; the B1 row is houses built before 1944 - the traditional stock, and the one that speaks for our period: privy outbuilding (便所) 0.87; firewood shed (マキ小屋) 0.76; straw shed (ワラ小屋) 0.68; barn (納屋) 0.58; work shed (作業場) 0.55; livestock shed (畜舎) 0.32; storehouse (くら) 0.24; compost/ manure shed (堆肥舎) 0.24; bath shed (浴室) 0.29 (all-house average); chicken coop (鶏舎) 0.16 (0.28 all houses); general storage (物置) 0.48 (all); well house (井戸小屋) 0.19 (all); pickle shed (つけもの小屋) 0.24; hasa shed 0.13; a bran or bird shed 0.03; household shrine (社) 0.01 over all houses and none among the pre-1944 ones; charcoal shed 0.01. Table 6 gives what was INSIDE the old main house instead: livestock 60.5% of B1 households, bath 52.6%, privy 31.6%. Scope, stated: 1972, Tōhoku (snow country, 5 km from the mountains, 145 cm max snow) - a region the author himself says is unusually many-building; the Edo hamlet this map draws is temperate lowland, so these are the SHAPE of a farmstead's inventory and an upper band on counts, not a transplant.

What else stood on or near a farmstead that this count does not settle - shared and private wells, the harvest drying racks, the household shrine, roadside stones, the gate, the attached stable, a headman's compound, and the guesses with no readable page behind them - is What else stood on or near a farmstead - wells, drying racks, shrines, gates and stables.

Every number above is measured for 1972 Miyagi and is a GUIDE elsewhere, for the scope stated.
