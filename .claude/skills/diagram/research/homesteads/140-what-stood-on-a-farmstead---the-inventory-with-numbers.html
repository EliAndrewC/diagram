What stood on a farmstead - the inventory, with numbers

Sources: sugiura-1973-fuzoku (the one quantified source), boso-no-mura-kazusa, magariya-jawiki

The GM: "What things would exist on a noticeable percentage of farmhouses that we are not currently representing on our maps ... What, if anything, are we missing?"

The one quantified source. Sugiura Tadashi, "農村集落における農家の付属建物について - 宮城県宮崎町の例" (On the ancillary buildings of farmhouses in agricultural settlements: Miyazaki-machi, Miyagi), Tōhoku Chiri 25(3), 1973, pp. 145-152 (JStage tga1948/25/3/25_3_145): a July 1972 survey of all 87 households in three hamlets (Asahi, Kita-Nagashida, Minami-Nagashida) on the terrace south of the Naruse river,43 every roofed outbuilding counted and classified by function. Averages per household: 4.38 outbuildings (4.57 for farm households) - "surprisingly many" against the Tōhoku norm of a single main building.44 Table 5 gives the MEAN NUMBER of buildings per household by function - not the share of households owning one: the paper counts every roofed outbuilding, a building serving two functions counts once under each, and a household may hold two of a kind (the non-farm row carries 1.40 storage sheds per household), so each figure is an upper bound on the share; the B1 row is houses built before 1944 - the traditional stock, and the one that speaks for our period: privy outbuilding (便所) 0.87121; firewood shed (マキ小屋) 0.76; straw shed (ワラ小屋) 0.68; barn (納屋) 0.58; work shed (作業場) 0.55; livestock shed (畜舎) 0.32;45 storehouse (くら) 0.24; compost/ manure shed (堆肥舎) 0.24; bath shed (浴室) 0.29 (all-house average); chicken coop (鶏舎) 0.16 (0.28 all houses); general storage (物置) 0.48 (all); well house (井戸小屋) 0.19 (all); pickle shed (つけもの小屋) 0.24; hasa shed 0.13; a bran or bird shed 0.03; household shrine (社) 0.01 over all houses and none among the pre-1944 ones; charcoal shed 0.01.120 Table 6 gives what was INSIDE the old main house instead: livestock 60.5% of B1 households, bath 52.6%, privy 31.6%46. Scope, stated: 1972, Tōhoku (snow country, 5 km from the mountains, 145 cm max snow)47 - a region the author himself says is unusually many-building; the Edo hamlet this map draws is temperate lowland, so these are the SHAPE of a farmstead's inventory and an upper band on counts, not a transplant.

The rest of the record: shared wells outnumbered private ones, 「because the cost of digging a well was high ... jointly owned wells were numerous」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「井戸の掘削費用が高額で ... 共同所有の井戸が多かった」)48 (ja.wikipedia 井戸) - the map's communal wells are the norm; the harvest drying rack (稲架, hasa) 「They are mainly, and in most cases, built in the paddies and dry fields after the harvest.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「主に収穫後の田畑に作られることが多い」) - stands IN THE FIELDS after harvest, seasonal, some regions leaving the frame up49 (ja.wikipedia 稲架); the household shrine (屋敷神, yashikigami) is a small stone or wood hokora in a plot corner, N/W or N/E50 (ja.wikipedia 屋敷神) - attested, and Sugiura's table puts it at 0.01 per household, so RARE; dosojin and jizo stand on the road and at passes, jizo also "in the villages"51 (nakasendoway.com) - a village-ENTRANCE stone is a guess, with no readable page behind it; a gate (屋敷門) is a WARRIOR house's feature (JAANUS52) - not a commoner farmstead's without a source; the magariya attached stable is a Nanbu/Tōhoku form from horse-breeding country, its stable warmed from the kitchen hearth (that the cold made it is this page's reading)119 - our separate byres are the temperate reading; a headman-class compound (Chiba museum's Boso-no-Mura reconstruction) had eight buildings: 主屋・土蔵・長屋門・馬小屋・納屋・木小屋・作業小屋・井戸 (main house, storehouse (kura), gatehouse (nagayamon), stable, barn (naya), firewood shed, work shed and well) - the top of the ladder, not the typical plot.118 Guesses, with no readable page behind them: persimmon "planted in the gardens of farming families without exception" from the late Edo (the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, MAFF); firewood "stacked behind a shed or along the yashiki's front wall" (Chiba museum); straw ricks (waraguro) built around a pole to ~2 m and kept to spring; the goemon-buro bath as a standalone shed common among farmers; water mills RARE - a 1788 record counts 33 waterwheels on the whole Musashino plateau; kabata a spring-fed Shiga regional form; the communal village graveyard the Edo norm, the farmstead grave (屋敷墓) a Heian-Muromachi elite practice; chickens kept before Meiji mainly for time-telling and fighting, so a coop is uncertain.

Every number above is measured for 1972 Miyagi and is a GUIDE elsewhere, for the scope stated; the persimmon and the firewood placement are guesses, with no readable page behind them.
