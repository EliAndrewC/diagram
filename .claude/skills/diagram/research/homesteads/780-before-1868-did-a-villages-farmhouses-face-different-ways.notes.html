• yamamoto-2014-koshijo-shuraku - 「Over a wide area including the south of Okinawa Island and the Miyako and Yaeyama islands, villages are seen made up of groups of rectangular blocks, called 'grid villages'.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「These 'grid villages' are said to have come to be seen in many places under the Ryukyu kingdom from the eighteenth century onward, in the course of reorganizing existing villages and of building new villages, which involved moving villages and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「According to the Kyuyo and other sources, Maekawa village was permitted to move to its present site in 1736.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「So this report takes as its case the Maekawa village of Tamagusuku in Nanjo city (the former Tamagusuku village), which just barely keeps copies of cadastral maps thought to date from the time of the land survey project」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Checking the cadastral maps and other records based on the land survey project, one can grasp the village's original spatial structure, from before house plots multiplied and were subdivided.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; );「Today the village spreads over a gentle south-facing slope at 45 to 90 m above sea level」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「Even as a grid village, the streets within it curved widely and each block was bow-shaped」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「The grid villages too, however, saw the land-sharing system abolished with the land reorganization project (from 1899), private ownership of land advance, land gathered and split up, and then the damage of the Battle of Okinawa.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • yamamoto-2014-koshijo-shuraku - 「Overall, blocks of the 'single horizontal row' type, one plot north to south by three to seven plots east to west, predominate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「It was supposed that in that process curved streets running along the east-west contour lines came to be laid out, and that groups of house plots kept narrow from north to south, so as to make little difference in height within each plot - the 'single horizontal row' blocks that make up the 'grid village' - were the premise.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
