Where did the manure heap stand, and what went into it?

Sources: jawiki-koedame (the night-soil jar), shimogoe-jawiki (how night soil was fermented; the manuals it quotes), kyuhi-jawiki (what stable manure was made of), sugiura-1973-fuzoku (the compost-shed rate), nogyo-zensho-jawiki (the manual's date)

The manure heap is one of the farmstead's fixtures the GM asked to see drawn; the whole set, the privy it is tied to, and how many of each the map draws, are at The farmstead's fixtures.

The manure heap - the practice attested, the place a GUESS. ja.wikipedia 肥溜め / 下肥: night soil was fermented in 「a jar buried in the ground, or a pit finished with plaster」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「地中に埋めた壺や、漆喰をほどこした穴」), vessels from which the liquid cannot leak, the jar being one of 「about 1 to 1.5 meters in mouth diameter」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「口径1-1.5メートル程度」), 「1-2 weeks in summer, 3-4 weeks in winter」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「夏の場合1～2週間、冬の場合3～4週間」), though the same page also cites a year of fermenting from the Sendai city history; the night-soil pit's two attested places, beside the privy or out by the fields, are at the privy's question. Where the heap of stable manure stood in the yard no readable page says, and it is drawn near the stable or under the eaves - a GUESS. The stable's own litter - straw and fallen leaves mixed with the dung and trodden by the cattle and horses until it rotted - became stable manure (kyuhi, 厩肥). With the Chinese pigsty-privy finding above, the heap is drawn as one 8 x 6 ft mound beyond the privy (size GUESS); drawn on 40-70% of homesteads (Sugiura: 0.24 compost SHEDS per household - the open heap is commoner than its shed; GUESS).
