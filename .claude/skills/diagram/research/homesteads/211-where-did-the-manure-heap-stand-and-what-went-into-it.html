Where did the manure heap stand, and what went into it?

Sources: jawiki-koedame (the night-soil jar), shimogoe-jawiki (how night soil was fermented; the manuals it quotes), kyuhi-jawiki (what stable manure was made of), sugiura-1973-fuzoku (the compost-shed rate), nogyo-zensho-jawiki (the manual's date)

The manure heap is one of the farmstead's fixtures the GM asked to see drawn; the whole set, the privy it is tied to, and how many of each the map draws, are at How our maps draw the farmstead and what stands on it (yashiki).

The manure heap - the practice attested, the place a GUESS. ja.wikipedia 肥溜め (koedame) / 下肥 (night soil): night soil was fermented in 「a jar buried in the ground, or a pit finished with plaster」 (translated; original: 「地中に埋めた壺や、漆喰をほどこした穴」), vessels from which the liquid cannot leak, the jar being one of 「about 1 to 1.5 meters in mouth diameter」 (translated; original: 「口径1 - 1.5メートル程度」); fermenting took 「1-2 weeks in summer, 3-4 weeks in winter」 (translated; original: 「夏の場合1～2週間、冬の場合3～4週間」), though the same page also cites a year of fermenting from the Sendai city history; the night-soil pit's two attested places, beside the privy or out by the fields, are at Farm privies and their night soil (benjo). Where the heap of stable manure stood in the yard no readable page says. The stable's own litter - straw and fallen leaves mixed with the dung and trodden by the cattle and horses until it rotted - became stable manure (kyuhi, 厩肥). With the Chinese pigsty-privy finding at the privy, the heap, or on some farmsteads the pit, is drawn beyond the privy, the heap as one 8 x 6 ft mound (place and size a GUESS); drawn on 40-70% of homesteads (0.24 compost SHEDS per old farmhouse - main house built 1944 or earlier - in Sugiura's survey; that the open heap is commoner than its shed is a GUESS with no count behind it). That the manure house and the heap are premodern, and why no count sets their share, is at Was there a manure heap on a farm before 1868?

What can be read of the manuals is a modern rendering of the Nogyo Zensho's manure house (at Was there a manure heap on a farm before 1868?), and what the ja.wikipedia page on night soil quotes: Hyakusho Denki (1681) on letting night soil rot in the storage jar before it goes on the fields, and Nogyo Zensho (農業全書) (1697) on fresh dung working poorly and ripened dung working well.
