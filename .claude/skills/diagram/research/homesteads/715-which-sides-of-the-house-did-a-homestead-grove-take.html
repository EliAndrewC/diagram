Which sides of the house did a homestead grove take?

Sources: yashikirin-jawiki (the Takada order of 1625, the Izumo grove before and after the Meiji era and its flood bank, and the Tonami front), irie-2020-igune (the Sendai plain's two-sided grove), kashima-kainyo-1987 and tonami-yashikirin-haichi (the Tonami grove's sides and open front), isa-auf-fukugi-2011 (Okinawa's north-and-east groves)

How big the grove was, and that it is older than 1868, are at Was the homestead grove there before 1868, and how big was it?; the size the maps draw, and which way its windward side faces, at Homestead groves (yashikirin) - the real scale and prevalence. This question asks which sides of the house the grove took.

Its shape was a regional custom. The regions in the record disagree:

  • The full ring, on the Izumo plain. There the pine groves went the whole way round the house before the Meiji era, and took a hook shape covering only the north and west afterward, as flood damage lessened and building changed; the pines had been planted in the first place to strengthen an earth bank built round the house against floods.
  • The Takada order of 1625. In the Takada domain an order had farmers plant camellia and bamboo grass on the south side and sugi around the homestead. Read as written, it does not say the sugi closed a ring, and it plants the south side with something else.
  • Two sides, on the Sendai plain, since the early Edo period. Against the frequent, strong north-northwesterly winter wind, the grove there is said to form on the north or the west of the plot and often to lack the south or the east side; and on the homesteads built on the plain's new fields, trees were planted in many layers on the north and west sides at the urging of Date Masamune, the first lord of the Sendai domain - so from the early Edo period - and kept as a windbreak for more than several hundred years.
  • Open at the front, on the Tonami plain. Tall sugi, oak and zelkova stood from the south face round to the west, hackberry and alder with a little bamboo from the west round to the north, and flowering and fruit trees on the east; the east is the front of the house, and there the grove is thin. The east side was the way in, with a garden, and the tall sugi on the west and south stood against wind and snow. Neither survey page dates this planting.
  • North and east, in Okinawa, where most large trees stand north of the house and the next most to the east.

The decision. On the record read, the grove's sides were a regional custom: the two windward sides in the most regions, the front alone left open on the Tonami plain, the full ring on the Izumo plain's flood ground. The GM ruled on 2026-09-29: because it does sound as if groves completely surrounding farmhouses was a thing, then that should indeed be a tunable knob. It seems like two sides is the minimum. Three sides would sometimes be the case, and all four sides would also sometimes be the case. Offered the split and the flood-ground rise below, the GM accepted both: Your suggestions sound great, so yes, please go with all of that. Both the percentage split and the flood ground adjustment. So the maps roll the grove's sides.

The rule the map follows: a settlement's farmstead groves take their sides from one roll per settlement, the same at every farm, because the shape is reported as a regional custom. Two sides half the time, three sides three times in ten, all four sides twice in ten. Two sides are the windward pair - the form reported in the most regions, and the only one with a general statement and an early-Edo date. Three leave open only the front, as on the Tonami plain; the maps put that front on the side away from the wind, where the yard and the way in are, as the Tonami plan does - this project's decision. Four close the ring, as on the Izumo plain. Where the farms stand on flood-prone ground - fields of reclaimed low ground behind dikes, or houses on a dike - four sides rise to four times in ten and the other two keep their 5 : 3 ratio (two sides 37.5%, three sides 22.5%), because the Izumo ring's own stated cause was flood; a map may declare its ground either way. Every one of these shares is a GUESS: no page read counts how many farmsteads took each shape, on any ground or in any period. The windward sides are the deep stand; the other planted sides are a thinner band of lesser trees, as on the Tonami plain. That band is one tree deep: 17 ft, two mean crown radii (Forest density and crown size). Carrying the Tonami pattern round a full ring is a GUESS, and so is the band's depth, since no page read gives it. A grove on all four sides is broken once, at the middle of its front, for a way in about 12 ft wide: the break is a physical necessity, since the farm must be reached, and its place at the middle of the front and its width are a GUESS, since no old page read gives where in a ring its opening stood or how wide it was. Which windward pair a map takes is chosen per map, at the scale question linked above. The village's shelter belt is a different planting and not this roll: it stays on one or two windward sides (Does a shelter belt wrap the settlement?).
