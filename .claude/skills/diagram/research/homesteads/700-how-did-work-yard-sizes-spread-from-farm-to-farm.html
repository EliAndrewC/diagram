How did work-yard sizes spread from farm to farm?

Sources: kamikanai-1771-houses (the skewed house sizes and how they follow the holding), kikoba-kenchi (the spread of homestead lots, and how they follow the holding), santome-shinden-allotment (the colony of equal homesteads), kitamoto-mushiro-niwa (the band and its tail)

How big the median yard is, and what it rests on, is at How big was the work yard? This question is how the sizes spread around it.

The spread is lognormal, and it is right-skewed. No survey gives a histogram of yards, so the shape comes from what the cadastres do tabulate, and every one is right-skewed: Kamikanai (1771) gives a complete main-house histogram whose 31 commoner houses fit a lognormal of median 22.5 tsubo and sigma 0.46 in log space, with the headman detached at 3.1x; Kikoba's Genroku 検地帳 (kenchicho, the land-survey register) puts homestead lots at 20-100 bu, and its table of lots and house footprints together peaks at 30 bu and 50 bu. Kitamoto's own band-and-tail, 40 to 60 mushiro usually and past 100 for a few, implies sigma 0.35-0.45 - that convergence is what the drawn sigma of 0.40 rests on. A floor exists but is not zero: early registers carry a no-homestead class (無屋敷登録人, muyashiki torokunin), but by Genroku, as the record has it on no page it could read, every peasant household, the fieldless included, had come to hold a house plot - the landless simply occupy the small end, so the roll is floored at 8 tsubo rather than allowed to vanish.

The yard follows the household, but not proportionally. The proportional coupling is ADDITIVE - 「if the holding increases by about 5 koku, the tsubo count increases by about 10 tsubo」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「持高が5石ほど増加すれば坪数が10坪ほど増加する」) - so a 20x holder does not get a 20x yard (the sentence is the paper's own on Meiwa-era Kamikanai, and is of the main house's floor area, not the yard; of Kyoho-era Iwahara village the same paper finds no fixed correspondence between holding and floor area, and holdings inside that village span landless to about 30 koku). Kikoba's register ties the lot itself to the holding the same way: the lot averages 67.3 tsubo among holders of 100 se (畝, a tenth of a tan) and more, and 33.3 tsubo among holders of 0-25 se - twice the lot, not four times. The generator gives the yard the household's own deviation in log space, amplified and then perturbed by an independent positional draw: a large household is overwhelmingly likely to have a large yard, a mismatch is possible and rare - the GM's own reading of what the record implies.

One attested form is NOT skewed, and is a knob. A planned shinden colony issued every settler an identical homestead - Santome 1696, "the size of the house lots is an entirely equal allotment" (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷の規模はまったくの均等配分」) - so an "allotted" yard setting draws uniform yards. Two attested forms become a per-settlement knob.
