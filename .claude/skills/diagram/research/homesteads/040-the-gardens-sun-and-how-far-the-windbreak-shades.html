The garden's sun, and how far the windbreak shades

Sources: sendai-igune-list, kurita-2019-igune, minami-2024-igune, minami-2022-igune, yashikirin-jawiki, kashima-kainyo-1987, isa-auf-fukugi-2011, kikanchiiki-igune (how an igune is kept), tonami-yashikirin-haichi; the shadow is computed for 38N

What prompted it (GM 2026-08-25): "there is not enough space for sunlight to hit the gardens and thrashing yards ... The Windbreak Forest ... is so close to the gardens ... that I do not believe that those gardens would get sufficient sunlight."

This question is the shade the village's windbreak belt throws on the yards and gardens. The shade a garden takes from the houses around it and from a neighbor's grove is at How much open ground does a kitchen garden keep to its south and east?; where the record puts the grove, the yard and the garden around one house is at Which side of the house did the windbreak stand on? - and it holds no measured distance from a plot to the treeline, so the number below is DERIVED, as the yard's 39 ft was.

The belt's height, from the record. Searched: igune (Sendai/Osaki), kainyo (Tonami), tsuijimatsu (Izumo), Musashino yashikirin, Okinawa fukugi, Chinese fengshui woods. Found: the Sendai igune planting list classes sugi/keyaki/kuromatsu/hinoki as "tall trees" at >= 15 m, sub-canopy ~10 m, shrubs ~3 m; two surveys of igune measured their height - one at the grove's highest point with a laser rangefinder on the ground, the other putting a grove of tall and low trees at about 9-16 m; a 2022 paper describes an igune 10 m high on the side of the prevailing wind; Tonami's kainyo put tall sturdy trees, chiefly sugi, on the west and south, 11-28 m tall and about 15 m on average; Izumo's tsuijimatsu pines are clipped flat at a fixed height (the 8-12 m every 4-5 years carried for them is on no page read), the one attested topping practice; Okinawa fukugi belts average 7-10 m. Limb-pruning (edauchi) is part of keeping an igune: in the Sawadaue district of Osaki the 23 households whose igune remain formed a preservation society that does the pruning and clears the fallen trees in common. That kainyo and igune were never height-capped is this page's reading: no page read speaks of cutting their tops, and Izumo's level topping is the one trimming practice attested. So the band this page reads from the figures above is ~10 m for a working belt, 15-25 m for an untended mature stand.

The shadow, computed for the season that matters. At 38N in the shoulder month the 3pm sun stands 28 deg high at azimuth ~232, so a belt throws 1.9 x its height to the NORTHEAST - a 10 m (33 ft) belt ~63 ft, of which ~50 ft reaches eastward; a 15 m belt ~95 ft / ~75 ft. In the 7th month the same hour gives ~35 ft / ~50 ft. The shoulder binds.

The decision: 50 ft of clear ground west and southwest of every yard and bed (the 3pm shadow starts from the southwest, so the keep-out runs from a plot's north edge to 50 ft below its south edge), enforced when the belt is planted (a clump that would shade a plot is refused and re-seated deeper, and the belt's near face is moved out by the sun lane where the wind puts the belt west). Windbreak-role groves only: the copse is the dooryard's own persimmon and bamboo, and the Tonami survey puts the persimmon on the open east front of the house.

75 ft was tried first and declined - a ruling, not a taste. 75 is the same geometry at 15 m, the class floor of an untended stand. At 75 the belt must stand so far off the westernmost houses that it falls outside the edge of the drawn map, which is set by the houses, yards, beds and roads, and the map's edge does not open for the belt (GM 2026-07-20: the communal windbreak clips at the view edge). Measured on Inashiro: at 75 the belt fell from 131 clumps to 38 and was no longer continuous. At 50 it stands whole (81 clumps, continuous) inside the map's edge. What is accepted: a belt kept at working height. Two rulings conflicted - a belt that stands off the plots (2026-08-25) and a map edge that does not open for the belt (2026-07-20) - and the GM reconciled them the next day: "treat the innermost edge of the windbreak forest as being something that is preserved". The belt's inner FACE sets the map's edge, standing off the page edge by the same margin every other feature gets; the belt's depth still clips. What was priced and declined: letting the windbreak set the map's edge (this reverses the GM's ruling of 2026-07-20); a sun lane scaled to 15 m without moving the map's edge (measured: the belt is dropped). Who chose: this was settled on the measurement, and the GM on 2026-08-26 chose to open the map's edge at the face rather than shrink or raise the belt - 10 m stays as the record's working height.

Departures taken knowingly: the sun lane is a square, not a solar wedge that swings through the afternoon (the yard's south corridor takes the same shortcut); the small stand-off the belt's face keeps from the plots is an average plot overhang; what guarantees it is the rule that refuses any clump that would shade a plot.
