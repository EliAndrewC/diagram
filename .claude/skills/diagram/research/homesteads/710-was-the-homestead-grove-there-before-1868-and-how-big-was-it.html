Was the homestead grove there before 1868, and how big was it?

Sources: miura-2014-kainyo (the Edo documents on the Tonami grove), tonami-kainyoclub (the 1789 farm manual's account of the grove)

The size of the grove the maps draw, and the survey it comes from, are at Homestead groves (yashikirin) - the real scale and prevalence. This question asks which of that is older than the modern surveys. Which sides of the house the grove took is at Which sides of the house did a homestead grove take?

The grove itself is premodern. The Kaga (Kanazawa) domain, which ruled the Tonami plain, treated the homestead grove - written 垣根 in its documents, spelled kaine in village papers - as a stand of timber and bamboo under its control: a 1658 notice for the Tonami district put the cutting of the domain's reserved trees and bamboo around homesteads and on field ridges under permit; a 1750 document calls a grove kept thick a fire- and windbreak; and in 1782 a villager whose rice tax fell short sold four large sugi from within his own grove to his headman to make it up. A farm manual of 1789, Miyanaga Shoun's Shika nogyo dan, quoted by the Tonami plain's grove club, lists what the grove gave: shelter from wind and cold, a guard against thieves and a neighbor's fire, firewood from its branches and leaves, its core trees thinned and cut for use, and fallen leaves for the hearth and the fields.

Its size is not. No Edo page read counts a grove's trees: the one number is the four large sugi sold in 1782, which is not the grove's total, so the ~30-40 mature trees the maps draw is a 1987 count with no premodern figure to set against it.

The decision. On the record read, the dense stand of large sugi with its bamboo is premodern and the count that sizes it is modern. So the maps keep a dense stand, with its tree count labeled a GUESS set from the 1987 survey.
