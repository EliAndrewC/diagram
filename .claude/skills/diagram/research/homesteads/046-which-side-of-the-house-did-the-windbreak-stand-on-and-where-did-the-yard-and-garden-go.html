This question is where the record puts the grove, the yard and the garden around one house. The distance the map keeps between the village's windbreak belt and the plots, and how it was worked out, are in The garden's sun, and how far the windbreak shades.

Where the plots stood, from the record. On the Tonami plain the east side is the front of the house, and a survey of the plain's groves found little grove there - flowering trees and fruit trees, persimmon and peach among them, were often planted instead; from the south round to the west it found many tall trees - sugi, kashi and keyaki - and from the west round to the north sugi, enoki and alder often mixed with bamboo. The grove held off the strong south wind of early spring and the cold winter monsoon. A model homestead the Tonami research institute drew up in 1996, whose text is on no page read, is carried here for two more things: the front (E) yard as the work yard, kept open, with a persimmon at its center, and the N/W bamboo strip as shady and damp and given to the kitchen drain and service sheds. (ja.wikipedia 屋敷林, in the passage footnoted with the Sources above, itself puts Tonami's bamboo on the SOUTH side with the storehouses - see the vegetation research.) Tohoku: the igune stand on the north and west, which leaves the south side open (this page's inference; that it served as a drying yard is this page's reading too, and the rice itself was rack-dried in the field). So the record's answer, in this page's reading, is that the HOUSE BODY was the spacer - plots on the sunlit lee side, the belt behind - and it holds no measured plot-to-treeline distance.
