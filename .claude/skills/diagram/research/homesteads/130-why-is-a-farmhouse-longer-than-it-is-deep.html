Why is a farmhouse longer than it is deep?

Sources: kotobank-minka, nihonminkaen-kanagawa and nihonminkaen-kanto (measured farmhouses); this project's drawing decision

No two farmsteads were copies of one another, so the houses on a map vary - but they vary along their LENGTH and hardly at all across their DEPTH. The basic farmhouse is a rectangular plan under a hipped or gabled roof. A minka grew by adding bays to its long axis: another bay under the same ridge, an aisle down one side. It got longer, not much wider (the direction of growth, and the rarity of a square or a four-to-one house, are this page's reading of the plan types). So a clustered map varies a house's length by roughly 0.85 to 1.35 of its base and its depth by only 0.90 to 1.10, and the proportions that come out stay inside about 1.3 to 2.5 long to deep. The measured farmhouses under one roof at the Nihon Minka-en museum, built in the late seventeenth and early eighteenth centuries, run from about 1.6 to 2.0 times as long as deep (this page's arithmetic on their figures). A house built as separate blocks falls outside that: the Ota house, a headman's farmhouse of the bunto type, has a main block of 9.6 by 8.3 meters, nearly square, with its doma a second block of 10.0 by 8.3 meters beside it. The band here measures a house under one roof; the drawn band is wider than that on both sides, and its ends - and the 2.7 to 1 past which a house is drawn wrong rather than drawn differently - are a calibration, not a finding. How big a farmhouse was, and how the surviving houses differ from an ordinary one, is at How big was an ordinary farmhouse.

A scattered map varies the same thing from the other end. There the whole homestead takes a wealth standing - poorer, ordinary or better-off - which scales the house and its grove together, so a richer farm reads bigger across the board. That scaling reaches what is DRAWN and not what is reserved, so it never moves a neighbor and never changes how many households fit on the map.

The rule the map follows: farmhouses differ in size and in proportion within a modest range, and a farmhouse is never drawn squarer than about 1.3 to 1 or longer than about 2.7 to 1. The largest house on the map is the headman's (the question above).
