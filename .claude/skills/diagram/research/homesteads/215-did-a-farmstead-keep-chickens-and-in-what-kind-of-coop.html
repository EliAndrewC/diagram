Did a farmstead keep chickens, and in what kind of coop?

Sources: cambridge-animals-china, qimin-yaoshu-yangji, pitt-zhengzhou-coop, zhwiki-liuchu

The coop is one of the farmstead's fixtures, and the GM asked for it as imperial China had it rather than Japan; that ruling, the whole set, and how many of each the map draws, are at The farmstead's fixtures.

The chicken coop - imperial China's. Animals through Chinese History (Cambridge): “it seems that farmers in most regions of China managed to keep a pig and some chickens in their yard, along with a draught animal or two” (the chapter leans on survey data of the late 1920s and early 1930s, its own caveat, and is read here back into the late imperial countryside). The Qimin Yaoshu 養雞第五十九 ("Raising chickens", its 59th chapter; wikisource): 「For the chickens' roost it is fitting to set a cage on the ground, with a perch placed inside ... if they are kept in the trees, at one encounter with wind and cold the large ones waste and grow thin and the small ones may die」 (translated from the Classical Chinese by this project; original: 「雞棲宜㧻地為籠内著棧 ... 若在之樹林一遇風寒大者損瘦小者或死」) - build the roost as a ground-level enclosure with a perch inside; left to roost in trees the birds sicken - so a COOP, not a tree. A late-Ming coop at Zhengzhou (Pitt HAA): "square-shaped structure had six niche-like openings in the west wall, with some eggshell fragments" - square; no size given. The chicken is one of the 六畜 (liuchu, the six domestic animals) (zh.wikipedia). No source gives a household PROPORTION; the 50-80% of homesteads drawn with one is a GUESS bounded by "most regions". 5 x 5 ft, square after Zhengzhou, size a GUESS; seated on the flank by the yard (GUESS - "in their yard").
