Did a farmstead keep chickens, and in what kind of coop?

Sources: cambridge-animals-china, qimin-yaoshu-yangji, pitt-zhengzhou-coop, zhwiki-liuchu, buck-1930-farm-economy (how many farms kept chickens)

The coop is one of the farmstead's fixtures, and the GM asked for it as imperial China had it rather than Japan; that ruling, the whole set, and how many of each the map draws, are at The farmstead's fixtures.

The chicken coop - imperial China's. Animals through Chinese History (Cambridge): “it seems that farmers in most regions of China managed to keep a pig and some chickens in their yard, along with a draught animal or two” (the chapter leans on survey data of the late 1920s and early 1930s, its own caveat, and is read here back into the late imperial countryside). The Qimin Yaoshu 養雞第五十九 ("Raising chickens", its 59th chapter; wikisource): 「For the chickens' roost it is best to set a cage on the ground with a perch placed inside ... If they are left to the trees, then at one encounter with wind and cold the big ones will be harmed and grow thin, and the small ones may die.」 (translated from the Classical Chinese by this project; original: 「雞棲宜㧻地為籠内著棧 ... 若在之樹林一遇風寒大者損瘦小者或死」) - build the roost as a ground-level enclosure with a perch inside; left to roost in trees the birds sicken - so a COOP, not a tree. A late-Ming coop at Zhengzhou (University of Pittsburgh, History of Art and Architecture): "square-shaped structure had six niche-like openings in the west wall, with some eggshell fragments" - square; no size given. The chicken is one of the 六畜 (liuchu, the six domestic animals) (zh.wikipedia). How many farms kept them was counted by Buck's survey of 2,866 farms in seventeen localities of seven provinces in 1921-1925: "In the seventeen localities chickens are found on 82 per cent of all farms and are the most common type of livestock", and they were more common in east-central China than in the north. So the coop band is centered on that count, read back from the 1920s into the late empire as the Cambridge chapter's figures are; a farm keeping chickens is taken to keep them in a coop, after the Qimin Yaoshu. 5 x 5 ft, square after Zhengzhou, size a GUESS; seated beside the house, by the yard (GUESS - "in their yard").

The rule the map follows: a coop on about four farmsteads in five - the band rolled per hamlet around the survey's 82%, higher where the hamlet grows wet rice and lower in a dry-field hamlet - a GUESS that reads Buck's east-central-against-north difference as rice farming against dry fields, which no page read says. How wide the band is, is calibrated liberty.
