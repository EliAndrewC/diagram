Village names that describe the site (chimei)

Many Japanese place names were made from the lie of the land: naming a place from its terrain is one of the common ways of naming, most of all for small places and for old names. Two terrain words, the characters for a valley and for a mountain valley or marsh, each make about four percent of Japan's indexed place names, and what they are read as and what they mean shift between eastern and western Japan.

  • Naming a place from its terrain was common, most of all for small places and old names.
    The Japanese Wikipedia article on place names calls it one of the many common ways of naming, especially for small-scale terrain and for names given long ago: a valley is named X-tani, a hill X-ga-oka.
  • Many names' origins are disputed or wholly unknown.
    The same article says many names have several competing explanations with none settled, or can only be called entirely unknown, so a name is a cue to its ground, not proof of it.
  • The characters for a valley and for a mountain valley or marsh each make about four percent of Japan's indexed place names.
    A geography paper counts 20,422 names in 谷 (tani or ya, "valley"), 3.95% of the names indexed nationwide, and 21,746 in 沢 (sawa, "mountain valley or marsh"), 4.21%.
  • The valley character is read tani in the west and ya in the east.
    The 13,484 names that read it tani or dani are mostly in western Japan. Names that read it ya are commonest on the Pacific side of eastern Japan, where shallow valleys cut into uplands or low mountains, though 22% of them are in western Japan.
  • Sawa names are almost all eastern, and the word means a valley in the east and a marsh in the west.
    Of the names ending in sawa, 95.7% are in eastern Japan, counting as far as the four eastern prefectures of the Chubu region, and 97.5% with Toyama, Aichi and Gifu added. Today sawa means a mountain valley in eastern Japan and a marsh in western Japan.

A river's name did not always follow the land in the same way; how one river could carry several names along its course is at River names: one river, many names.
