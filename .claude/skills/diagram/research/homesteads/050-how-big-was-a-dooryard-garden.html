How big was a dooryard garden?

Sources: hatake-jawiki; the GM's ruling of 2026-07-21 that everything on these maps is drawn at its true size; this project's measurement of the drawn hamlets; kateisaien-jawiki (the name of the bed a household grows its own food on), shakkanho-jawiki (the tsubo and the se in square meters); no work is cited for the area band

The saien is the small intensive bed by the kitchen that feeds a household its daily greens - daikon, onions, beans, herbs - and nothing beyond them; the ground a Japanese household cultivates for its own consumption has its own name, the yashikibatake, the homestead field.89 Bulk vegetables and dry crops grew out in the household's own hatake dry fields, not here, which is why the bed is a matter of a few tsubo rather than of tan.139 The band the map holds a household's beds to is a few tsubo up to about 1.4 se, which is roughly 10 to 140 sq m (one tsubo is about 3.31 sq m; one se is 30 tsubo, about 99 sq m).90 Below the band the bed reads as no garden at all, and above it as a field. The same reading holds on the Chinese side: a rice-south farmhouse's dooryard plot was the same small intensive kitchen bed, with the bulk growing out in the household's fields. (The band and the Chinese comparison are both GUESSES: the article that names the bed gives no area for it, and no readable page was found that gives one or that measures the Chinese plot.)91

The band is a real quantity rather than a drawing habit because the bed is drawn at TRUE size at every tier, exactly as the house and the work yard are - the GM's ruling of 2026-07-21 that these maps draw things at the size they were. Nothing on a homestead is enlarged against the fields to make it read.

The rule the map follows: a household's garden is drawn at true size, and its beds TOGETHER are held to 10-140 sq m - a split plot is still one household's garden, so the beds are summed. The drawn hamlets land at about 35-115 sq m per household, centered near 55 sq m (about half a se), comfortably inside the band.
