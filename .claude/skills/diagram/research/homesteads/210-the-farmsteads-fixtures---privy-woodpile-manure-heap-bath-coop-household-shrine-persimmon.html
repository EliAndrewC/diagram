The farmstead's fixtures - privy, woodpile, manure heap, bath, coop, household shrine, persimmon

Sources: kotobank-benjo, sinyoken-madori, artic-pigsty-latrine, sugiura-1973-fuzoku (the rates)

The GM chose which of the farmstead's fixtures the map draws, from the inventory above: "privies are something that we want ... firewood stacks are actually large enough to render ... Same thing with manure heaps, same thing with baths, same thing with persimmons ... a household shrine being something which is very rare, but which is notable when it does appear ... as for chicken coops ... go with whatever was the case in imperial China." Every drawn size is TRUE feet at the map's scale; the sizes are GUESSES unless a source is named, because the record describes these things and almost never measures them.

This question holds the privy and what the map draws of every fixture. Each of the others is its own question: the manure heap, which the record ties to the privy, at Where did the manure heap stand?, the woodpile at Where was the firewood stacked?, the bath at Did a farmhouse have a bath shed?, the coop at Did a farmstead keep chickens?, the shrine at Which farmsteads had a household shrine? and the persimmon at Why does a persimmon stand beside so many farmhouses?

The privy (便所, benjo). The Nipponica encyclopedia (on kotobank): 「In farm households it was usual to provide one urinal and one privy as a single building independent of the main house.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農家では、小便所一つと大便所一つを、母屋から 独立した一つの建物として設けるのが普通であった」) - on a farm the urinal and the privy were ONE building independent of the main house, and that was the norm. Where it stood (sinyoken floor-plan (間取り) pages): 「it is thought the privy was under the eaves in front of the stable to the left of the entrance, or else a separate outhouse in the yard」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「便所は入口の左横の厩の前の軒下か、あるいは庭に別棟の外便所があったと考えられる」) - under the eaves by the stable beside the entrance, or a separate outhouse in the yard. No page read names a privy by the back door or at the gate (searched 2026-09-14: the three sinyoken pages on the floor plan and the privy's history). The map draws every privy as a separate outhouse in the yard and ROLLS per house where in the yard it stands: behind the house by the back door .60, by the gate .25, by the stable or shed .15, each falling back to the others. Only the stable seat is attested, and there the source's privy is under the eaves rather than a separate outhouse; the back-door and gate seats, and the weights that make the back door the commonest, are a GUESS. On most houses a seat on the house's sunny side is tried first - why is at The outhouse faces the sun. Size: no source gives one - 6 x 6 ft, one ken square (a ken is about 1.8 m, or 6 ft), a GUESS. Share: the source's "普通" (ordinarily) reads as near-universal; Sugiura's 0.87 privy outbuildings a household and 0.32 of farms with a privy inside the house, both for farms whose main house was built by 1944 (a 1972 survey) (overlapping) agree; drawn on 85-95% of homesteads. That the privy stood outside the house on most farms before 1868 is at Before 1868, was a farm's privy a building of its own? In China under the Han dynasty, the Art Institute of Chicago catalog says of its pottery model of a pigsty and latrine: "latrines—or toilets—were customarily built above a pigsty and connected by pipes to a cesspool" - in China the privy and the muck were one cluster; that a Japanese farmstead kept them together too is this page's reading (a GUESS), and it is why the manure heap (below) is seated BEYOND THE PRIVY. How usual an outdoor privy was, two more places it stood - the front yard and inside the barn - and where its night soil was kept are at Where did the privy stand, and where was its night soil kept?

The straw rick - NOT DRAWN. The GM deferred it with every seasonal thing; what it was, where it stood and for how long is at Where did the straw rick stand after the harvest.

What the map does: on the sample hamlet the map is tested on (15 houses), this puts a privy on 14 of the 15 houses, a woodpile on 14, a coop on 12, a manure heap on 9, a bath shed on 1, a persimmon on 10, and one household shrine.
