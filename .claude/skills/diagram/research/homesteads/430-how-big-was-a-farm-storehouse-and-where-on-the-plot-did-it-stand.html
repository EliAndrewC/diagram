How big was a farm storehouse, and where on the plot did it stand?

Sources: sekkei-sya-sayama-kura (the usual size, and where kura stood), bunka-sado-tsuchiya-dozo (a farm grain kura), boso-no-mura-kazusa (the build), kotobank-dozo (the wall)

Size: a small two-story block, about 15 by 18 feet. An architect who surveyed the kura of a new-field village west of Edo found the commonest, standard size there to be 2.5 by 3 ken - about 15 by 18 feet - in two stories. A farm grain kura of 1890 from the plain of Sado island is of the same order: two stories, 34 square meters on the ground (about 365 square feet), which the national register calls an ancillary building a farmhouse could not do without.

Build: thick earth walls under a separate tile roof. A kura's walls are earth laid on a bamboo lattice, coat after coat, 20 to 30 centimeters thick and finished in lime plaster. The reconstructed headman-class farm in Chiba has a two-story kura, entered at its gable end, walled in earth roof and all, with a tiled roof set over the earthen one. Both kura read are tiled, so even where the house is thatched the kura is drawn tiled (this page's reading of two examples).

Where it stood: free of the house, in front or behind, on no fixed side. A kura was built when money had accumulated, an ancillary building of the main house; because the plans changed before it was built, kura ended up in front of the house or behind it, few were built against the house on its downwind side where a seasonal wind blew, and they stood on any side, east, west, north or south. The same architect adds that in his district farmhouses probably came to own kura only in the last years of the shogunate, a sign of how late and how uncommon one was. What this means for the map. The kura is drawn at about 15 by 18 feet, and it stands apart from the farmhouse on its own footprint, in front of the house or behind it, on a side rolled per farm rather than fixed to one compass wall. Drawing it as an annex against a fixed west or north wall is not what the record shows.
