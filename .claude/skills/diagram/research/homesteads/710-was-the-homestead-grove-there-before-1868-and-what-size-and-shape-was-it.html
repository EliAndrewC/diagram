Was the homestead grove there before 1868, and what size and shape was it?

Sources: miura-2014-kainyo (the Edo documents on the Tonami grove), tonami-kainyoclub (the 1789 farm manual's account of the grove), yashikirin-jawiki (the Takada order of 1625, and the Izumo grove before and after the Meiji era)

The size of the grove the maps draw, and the survey it comes from, are at Homestead groves (yashikirin) - the real scale and prevalence. This question asks which of that is older than the modern surveys.

The grove itself is premodern. The Kaga domain, which ruled the Tonami plain, treated the homestead grove - written 垣根 in its documents, spelled kaine in village papers - as a stand of timber and bamboo under its control: a 1658 notice for the Tonami district put the cutting of the domain's reserved trees and bamboo around homesteads and on field ridges under permit; a 1750 document calls a grove kept thick a fire- and windbreak; and in 1782 a villager whose rice tax fell short sold four large sugi from within his own grove to his headman to make it up. A Tonami farm manual of 1789, Miyanaga Shoun's Shika nogyo dan, lists what the grove gave: shelter from wind and cold, a guard against thieves and a neighbor's fire, firewood from its branches and leaves, its core trees thinned and cut for use, and fallen leaves for the hearth and the fields. In the Takada domain an order of 1625 had farmers plant camellia and bamboo grass on the south side and sugi around the homestead.

Its size is not. No Edo page read counts a grove's trees: the one number is the four large sugi sold in 1782, which is not the grove's total, so the ~30-40 mature trees the maps draw is a 1987 count with no premodern figure to set against it.

Nor is a belt on the windward arms only. The premodern record puts the grove round the homestead: the 1625 order's sugi go around it, and on the Izumo plain the pine groves enclosed the whole circuit of the house before the Meiji era and took the hook shape covering only the north and west afterward, as flood damage lessened and building changed.

The decision. On the record read, the dense stand of large sugi with its bamboo is premodern, the count that sizes it is modern, and a grove on its windward arms only is the modern form of one that before 1868 closed round the house. So the maps keep a dense stand, which the Edo record attests, with its tree count labeled a GUESS set from the 1987 survey, and they do not draw the arms-only belt as the premodern grove - before 1868 it went round the homestead.
