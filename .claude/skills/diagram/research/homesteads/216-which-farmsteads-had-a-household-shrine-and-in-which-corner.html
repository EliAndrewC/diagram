Which farmsteads had a household shrine, and in which corner?

Sources: tokushima-yashikigami, jawiki-yashikigami, kameyama-yashikigami, minka-en-yashikigami, satologue-yashikigami, sugiura-1973-fuzoku (the rate)

The household shrine is one of the farmstead's fixtures the GM asked to see drawn; the whole set, and how many of each the map draws, is at The farmstead's fixtures.

The household shrine (屋敷神) - the GM chose between two attested forms. Tokushima prefectural library: 「in some cases every house has one, in others only certain old families do」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「各家にある場合と特定の旧家にだけある場合がある」) - in some places every house has one, in others only certain old families; ja.wikipedia 屋敷神 states the same two patterns. Two forms would ordinarily be a knob; the GM ruled for this map: "very rare, but which is notable when it does appear" - the old-families pattern; Sugiura's table puts the shrine outbuilding at 0.01 per household over all houses and at none among the pre-1944 ones; drawn on 3-8% of homesteads, and the number drawn never exceeds that share, so chance alone cannot make shrines common. The every-house pattern is the DECLINED alternative. Corner: NE (the kimon, 鬼門) - 「in a corner of the house plot, especially at the kimon (the northeast corner), a small shrine of stone or wood, or with a straw roof」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷の一隅、特に鬼門（きもん）（北東隅）に、石や木、わら屋根の祠」) (minka-en; satologue puts it in a corner of the plot); ja.wikipedia puts it most often at the northwest or the northeast; a Kameyama folk survey finds it at the northwest of one house and moved from the northeast to the southwest at another; a count of NW 17 of 37 and NE 11 of 37 from the same survey is a GUESS, since only part of its page decodes and the count is not in the part that does; SW 「It is usual for it to be enshrined at the southwest corner of the homestead; there are some that take a tree or a stone as a mark to show where the god is, and there are also some that enshrine a small hokora.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷の西南隅に祀られているのが普通」) in Tokushima - three corners attested, rolled NW .45 / NE .35 / SW .20. Size: 「small shrines of stone or of wood」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「石造か木造の小祠」); one measured example 「a stone shrine about 40 cm each in width, depth and height」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「幅・奥行き・高さ各40cm位の石の祠」) (Tokushima) - about 1.3 ft. DRAWN at 6 x 6 ft, the small-shed module (at a true 3 x 3 ft the glyph is unreadable - the GM "could not tell what it even is") - a MAP DRAWING CONVENTION above the one measured stone, the GM's ruling: "as a glyph rendering convention, we could make it the same size as one of those small sheds ... but also red and visually distinctive in some other way" - vermilion, a ridge line and a torii standing before the door.
