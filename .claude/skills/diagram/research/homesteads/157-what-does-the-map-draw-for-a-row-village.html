What does the map draw for a row village?

Sources: kotobank-santome-shinden (Santome's roads, its strips and its shared wells), shinden-shuraku-kotobank (the compact holding on reclaimed ground)

What a row follows, and its sides, are at How was a row village laid out?; the frontages, fields and groves the record found are at How wide was a farm in a row?. This question gives what the map draws from them, each value with its label.

The farms and the street

  • The spacing. The farms stand one farmstead frame apart - the farm's grove and ground and the lane's room between two groves - lot against lot, since a grove farm cannot stand on a narrower lot. The frame's width falls at or just past the top of the record's frontages of 9 to 40 ken (about 54 to 240 ft), a GUESS.
  • The street runs about 24 ft off the dry edge, near enough that the field reads as the row's own and clear enough that the street is not on a bund, and is drawn one step wider on the map's scale of road widths than the lanes off it, with a 6 ft tread (both a convention); Santome's real roads were 6 ken wide, about 36 ft. The road the map is entered by carries the street on off the sheet as the road the row stands on (a convention).
  • Where the street goes. It is laid along the stretch of the dry edge, within a row's length of the settlement's center, that holds the most farms - this project's method.
  • More streets. A row the line cannot hold grows further streets parallel to the first, each with its own row, never a rank behind a row, as Santome's colony was laid on roads running lengthwise and crosswise (accurate for Santome); at most six, a GUESS.

The far row's holding

Where the farms stand on both sides of the street, the far row's holding is drawn behind each farm, in the form of the row's line:

  • On a street laid first, a strip behind the farm one lot wide and three lots deep - the planned row's order of house lot, then field, back from the road. Santome's strips ran 375 ken deep, about 2,240 ft; a paddy row borrows the form, not that depth, so three lots is a GUESS. The strip is cut in plots about 150 ft deep (a convention; the real plots' size was not found).
  • On the dry edge, one lot deep (a GUESS), compact and near the house - accurate for a dike row, and drawn the same on a levee or a fan's foot, this project's decision.

The holding is drawn as dry field; which crop it carried is a GUESS.

A row's water

Each row is rolled at even odds between two kinds of water: each farm its own well, or wells shared along the street, one at the middle of each stretch of the row no longer than 1.6 times the 760 ft the map lets a farm lie from its water, so no farm is farther than that from one. The even odds are a GUESS: the record rules only on the dispersed farm, which has its own well (Does a DISPERSED hamlet's outlying farm have its own well?), and Santome's few deep shared wells answered a water-poor upland, so they are not carried to a row on lower, wetter ground (this project's decision).
