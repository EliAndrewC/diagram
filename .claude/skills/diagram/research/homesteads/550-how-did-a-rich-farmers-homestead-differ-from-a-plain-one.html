How did a rich farmer's homestead differ from a plain one?

Sources: kotobank-gono (who the rich farmer-landlords were), sennan-yamada (a rich farmer's eight buildings), yamagata-kashiwakura (a rich family's houses and their walls), l7r-merchant-families (the setting's canon)

Who they were. The gōnō were the village landlords who grew from the middle of the eighteenth century with the spread of small-scale production for market, some out of older landlord families and some out of trade and production. Such a family often held village office too, and the Yamada of Sennan were rich farmers who held the headman's office generation after generation. In the setting most landlords are merchants rather than farmers, so the farming landlord who lives in the village is the exception there.

More buildings, more storehouses, and a wall. The Yamada plot of about 840 tsubo holds eight structures: the main house with its own entrance wing and kitchen wing, a rice storehouse and a second storehouse, a front gate, a nagayamon and an earthen wall. In Oka, in Yamagata, the Kashiwakura families' houses stand side by side around the head house, ringed by black board walls some 1,000 meters long in all; one of those kin houses, on about 1,600 tsubo joining the head house's plot, has nine buildings centered on its main house, front and rear storehouses among them, with 310 tsubo of buildings. Against the one main house and its few sheds of a plain farm (the inventory is in What stood on a farmstead - the inventory, with numbers), both rich farms here are walled or gated compounds with two or more storehouses, and the Yamada house is split into wings. How big its house was against a plain one is measured in How big was an ordinary farmhouse, and how much bigger was a well-off one?

What this means for the map. A rich farmer's homestead is a gated, walled compound with two or more storehouses, and in history its family often held village office, the headman's among them, so where a village has one it is best drawn as the headman's homestead at the top of the range in What did a headman's homestead have that an ordinary farm did not? Because the setting gives most land to merchant landlords, a map that also draws a second rich farm beside the headman's goes beyond the canon's picture; how often a village carries one at all is a GUESS.
