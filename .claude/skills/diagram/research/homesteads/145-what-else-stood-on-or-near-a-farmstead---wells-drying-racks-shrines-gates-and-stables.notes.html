• ido-jawiki - 「Wells may be individually owned, and they may be jointly owned.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「井戸には個人所有の場合と共同所有の場合がある」)「In general, because the cost of digging a well was high, and because there were also many places where obtaining water was difficult from a topographical standpoint as well, jointly owned wells were numerous.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一般には井戸の掘削費用が高額で地形的な観点からも水を得ることが困難な場所も多いことから共同所有の井戸が多かった」) (Wells were either privately or jointly owned; in general, because digging was costly and many places were topographically hard to get water in, jointly owned wells were common.)
  • hasa-jawiki - 「They are mainly, and in most cases, built in the paddies and dry fields after the harvest.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「主に収穫後の田畑に作られることが多い。」)「There are also regions where, rather than being built every year, they are kept installed in place nearby.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「毎年作るのではなく近くに備え付けしてある地方もある。」) (Mostly built in the paddies and fields after harvest; in some regions they are not built each year but kept installed nearby.)
  • jawiki-yashikigami - 「They are often enshrined in one corner of the plot on which the homestead stands, and in that case they are often enshrined to the northwest or northeast of the homestead.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷がある敷地の一角に祀られることが多く、その場合は屋敷の北西・北東に祀られることが多い。」)「Most yashikigami are small shrines (hokora) built of stone or of wood.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷神の多くは石造か木造の小祠である。」) (Usually enshrined in a corner of the house plot, and then usually at the NW or NE; most are small stone or wooden shrines.)
  • nakasendoway-dosojin - "The guardian deities are found most often in the more isolated parts of the highway, where protection was most welcome, and near the mountain passes." / "Jizo-san are found near the passes, but also in the villages where they protect the spirits of dead children." (the source's own English)
  • jaanus-yashikimon - "The front gate of a warrior's manor house." (the source's own English)
  • magariya-jawiki - 「The Nanbu magariya existed over almost the whole of the Iwate-prefecture portion of the Nanbu domain region (the former Nanbu clan territory).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「南部曲り家は、岩手県南部領地方（旧南部藩領）の岩手県部分のほぼ全域に存在した。」); magariya-jawiki - 「It was seen especially in Iwate, Shiwa, Hienuki, Kamihei, Shimohei and Kunohe districts, which were horse-breeding areas」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「特に、馬産地であった岩手郡、紫波郡、稗貫郡、上閉伊郡、下閉伊郡、九戸郡に多く見られ」); magariya-jawiki - 「on the south side the stable (umaya), joined to the lower end of the main house, projects out, and in many cases it is arranged so that the warm air let out from the kitchen's kamado (cooking stove) flows into the stable and warms the horses kept there.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「南側に厩（うまや）が母屋の下手に接続して突出し、台所の竃から排出された暖気が厩に流れ込み、飼育されている馬を温める仕組みになっていることが多い。」)
  • boso-no-mura-kazusa - 「It is composed of eight structures: the main house, the earthen-walled storehouse, the row-house gate, the stable, the barn, the firewood shed, the work shed and the well」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「主屋・土蔵・長屋門・馬小屋・納屋・木小屋・作業小屋・井戸の８棟で構成されています」); boso-no-mura-kazusa - 「It reproduces a farmhouse of the village-headman class by traditional building methods」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「名主クラスの農家を伝統的な工法で再現しています」)
