How thickly was a worked coppice stocked, and how wide were its crowns?

Sources: rinya-satoyama-junkan (thin, low, many-stemmed trees cut every 20-30 years), niigata-konara-coppice (the 15-30 year cycle), katakura-1989-konara-coppice (the shoots a cut stump sends up; the stems a hectare in worked woods), hasegawa-2018-toyama-konara (the timber in a worked wood against an uncut one), migita-chiba-konara-canopy (1,700 stems per hectare at 29 years), hectare-enwiki (the hectare in square feet)

What a worked wood looked like. The village fuel woods were cut and let regrow from the stump every twenty to thirty years or so, and were made of relatively thin, low trees growing several stems to a stump; the konara woods in particular were cut on a short cycle of about fifteen to thirty years. A cut konara stump sends up about thirty-three shoots the next spring, and about fourteen are still standing three years on - so a worked wood stands as clumps of thin stems, not as spaced single trees. It also held much less timber than the same wood left uncut: about 100 cubic meters a hectare at twenty years, against 265 in today's uncut woods of fifty to sixty.

How many stems. Konara woods in Nagano that had been cut and regrown from the stump for generations stood, at 15 to 23 years, at 2,560 to 5,030 konara stems a hectare, the younger stands the thicker. Woods of the same kind at 26 to 31 years, the age they were cut at, held 1,460 to 2,063 stems a hectare of every kind. A planted konara stand at Tsukuba, 29 years old, had 1,700 stems a hectare, 14.3 m tall on average (about 47 ft), trunks 14.8 cm in diameter at breast height - inside the range of the worked woods at cutting age. A hectare is 107,639 sq ft, so 1,700 stems is one to about 63 sq ft, a mean spacing near 8 ft; 5,000 is one to about 22 sq ft, near 4.6 ft. The 500-800 canopy stems a hectare the forest density entry draws its woods at was read from no page, and a worked wood near cutting age held two to three times it.

How wide the crowns were. No page read gives a crown width for a konara, a kunugi or any tree of a worked fuel wood.

What this means for the map. A village's coppice is drawn as a thicket, denser than an old hill wood, with smaller crowns: the density is calibrated on a wood near the age it was cut, and the crown width is a guess made to fit it.

The rule the map follows: a woodland commons is stocked at about 1,700 crowns a hectare - one to about 63 sq ft, on centers near 8 ft - a calibration on a wood near cutting age (the planted konara stand of 29 years, inside the 1,460-2,063 of worked woods at 26-31). A wood cut more recently stood thicker, to about 5,000 stems a hectare at 15 years; the map does not draw that younger stage. Each crown is drawn about 8-9 ft across, so that neighbors just meet: a GUESS, sized from the spacing, because no crown width was found. The windbreak belt and other woods keep the forest density entry's figures until they are researched in their own right.
