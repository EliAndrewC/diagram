How thickly was a worked coppice stocked, and how wide were its crowns?

Sources: rinya-satoyama-junkan (thin, low, many-stemmed trees cut every 20-30 years), niigata-konara-coppice (the 15-30 year cycle), katakura-1989-konara-coppice (the shoots a cut stump sends up), hasegawa-2018-toyama-konara (the timber in a worked wood against an uncut one), migita-chiba-konara-canopy (1,700 stems per hectare at 29 years), hectare-enwiki (the hectare in square feet)

What a worked wood looked like. The village fuel woods were cut and let regrow from the stump every twenty to thirty years or so, and were made of relatively thin, low trees growing several stems to a stump; the konara woods in particular were cut on a short cycle of about fifteen to thirty years. A cut konara stump sends up about thirty-three shoots the next spring, and about fourteen are still standing three years on - so a worked wood stands as clumps of thin stems, not as spaced single trees. It also held much less timber than the same wood left uncut: about 100 cubic meters a hectare at twenty years, against 265 in today's uncut woods of fifty to sixty.

How many stems. The one stems-per-hectare figure read for a konara stand of about coppice age is a planted stand at Tsukuba, 29 years old: 1,700 stems a hectare, 14.3 m tall on average (about 47 ft), trunks 14.8 cm through at breast height. A hectare is 107,639 sq ft, so that is one stem to about 63 sq ft, a mean spacing near 8 ft. It is a planted stand, not one regrown from stumps, and it stands at the long end of a cutting cycle; a wood cut younger has thinner stems and, from the shoot counts above, more of them. The 500-800 canopy stems a hectare the forest density entry draws its woods at was read from no page, and this figure is two to three times it.

How wide the crowns were. No page read gives a crown width for a konara, a kunugi or any tree of a worked fuel wood.

What this means for the map. A village's coppice is drawn as a thicket, denser than an old hill wood, with smaller crowns: the density is calibrated on the one measured stand, and the crown width is a guess made to fit it.

The rule the map follows: a woodland commons is stocked at about 1,700 crowns a hectare - one to about 63 sq ft, on centers near 8 ft - a calibration on one planted konara stand of 29 years. Each crown is drawn about 8-9 ft across, so that neighbors just meet: a GUESS, sized from the spacing, because no crown width was found. The windbreak belt and other woods keep the forest density entry's figures until they are researched in their own right.
