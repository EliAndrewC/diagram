Was the grass beside a field kept cut before modern times? Yes - for green manure, though how often is known only today

Sources: kotobank-karishiki (the grass of hills and bunds cut for green manure, from antiquity through early modern farming), kotobank-azebiki (the Edo land survey's one-shaku strip beside a bund), hiroshima-keihan-manual (how often a bund is mowed today)

The crop margin keeps scrub a strip's width off every field, and the cut bank does the same beside every irrigation channel, both on the reasoning that the grass there was cut. This question asks what is known of that cutting before modern times.

  • The cutting: attested before modern times. A historical dictionary describes karishiki as green grass and young shoots gathered in early spring on hills, fields, paddy bunds and the like and plowed straight into the fields as fertilizer; it says the method spread from antiquity and still held an important place in early modern farming, so that paddies needed wide grass-cutting ground around them. Another dictionary on the same page places karishiki before the Meiji period, when bought fertilizer became general. So the grass of a field's bund was cut, and the ground round the paddies was grass kept for cutting, before modern times. A channel's bank is, in this record, the bund of the paddy beside it (The bund runs along the channel bank), so its grass was cut the same way; no record read speaks of a channel's bank in particular.
  • How wide: one old figure, for the bund's own strip. The shogunate's land survey took out of a field's measured area a bund reckoned at one shaku (about 1 ft) and a one-shaku strip along it, by an order of 1726. So the survey reckoned about 2 ft, from the field's measured edge to the bund's far side, as ground outside the field; whether any crop stood on it the order does not say. How far the grass-cutting ground beyond it ran, no record before modern times that was read gives.
  • How often: known only today. The number of cuttings a year - 3.4 on average in a 2007-08 survey of Hiroshima's collective farms, done with brush cutters - is modern. The one old timing read is the early-spring cutting for green manure; no record before modern times giving how many times a year a bund was cut was found.
  • What it means for the map. The margin itself - cut grass between a crop and the scrub, on a field's edge and along a channel's bund - is historical, and the maps keep it. Its 6 ft is this record's choice, not an old figure: wider than the 2 ft of bund and strip the 1726 survey reckoned, and inside the grass-cutting ground the dictionary places round the paddies, whose width no old record read gives. That the grass was cut as often as today is a guess.
