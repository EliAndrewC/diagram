How big is a water-mouth grove? No Chinese one has been measured

Sources: forests-2020 (where the water-tail grove stands), park-2013-maeulsup (the Korean village-entrance grove's mean area), kweon-youn-2021 (the Mt. Jiri village groves' areas), coggins-minor-2018 (the grove's topographic place), huangshan-daily-jiekou (one very large Chinese water-mouth grove)

The village's back grove, how many groves a village kept and how dense they were, are at The fengshui forest - real scale. This question is the smaller grove at the village's low entrance.

No readable Chinese source measures one. The nearest analogue is the Korean village-entrance grove (동구숲), the analogue of the water-mouth grove (the Chinese grove type at the village's entrance, 「where the stream flows out of the village」 - Fujian's forests 「are mainly located near a villages’ entrances, ancestral halls, and temples」): the most numerous type in the field survey (42 of the 100 groves surveyed), averaging 7,149 m² and standing ~730 m from the village center, while a 24-grove sample around Mt. Jiri averages 0.22 ha (range 0.025-0.63 ha). A Huangshan Daily piece on forest stewardship in Huangshan describes the water-mouth grove of Xin'anyuan village (Hecheng Town, Xiuning County, Anhui) as over a hundred mu - about 7 ha - with nearly a thousand ancient trees, the largest in East China: the one Chinese water-mouth figure found, an extreme rather than a type.

What it means for the map. The ~0.1-0.5 ha water-mouth cluster drawn is a GUESS that sits inside the analogues' spread, smaller than the back grove and positioned by Coggins' topographic rule, where the stream leaves the village.
