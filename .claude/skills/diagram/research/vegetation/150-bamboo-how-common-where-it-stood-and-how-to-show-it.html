Bamboo: how common, where it stood, and how to show it

Sources: satoyama-enwiki, phyllostachys-enwiki, pmc5723622-bamboo-range, bamboo-growers-hardiness, bambubatu-madake; the Shirakawa farmstead grove (Kids Web Japan) has no readable page, chikurin-jawiki, take-jawiki, yashikirin-jawiki

The GM: "is there supposed to be bamboo on the reference hamlet? Why or why not? how common was it for there to be bamboo in settlements such as this?" - and the rendering problem behind it: a culm is only inches across (a figure no page read here gives), so at 1 px = 1 ft a bamboo stand has nothing to draw at true scale. This question holds whether a hamlet has bamboo and where it stood; how a stand is drawn is its own question, How is bamboo drawn, when one culm is too small to see?, and so is a farmstead's own strip, Did every farmstead keep its own bamboo, and on which side?

How common: common below the frost line, absent above it. From Wikipedia "Satoyama": satoyama "contains a mosaic of mixed forests, rice paddy fields, dry rice fields, grasslands, streams, ponds, and reservoirs for irrigation", and by the 1960s were used as "rice fields, plowed fields, shifting cultivation, grasslands, thatch fields, secondary forests for fuel, and giant bamboo forests". From Wikipedia "Phyllostachys bambusoides": madake's "long internodes and equally long fibres ... make it ideal for traditional basket-weaving and the production of fans"; its sheaths wrapped food and covered geta; it made shakuhachi. From PMC 5723622, the moso/madake range study: bamboo "distribution remained south of 41°N, in areas that included plains and hilly regions of central Honshu, as well as the coasts of northern Honshu" (the paper's warming-scenario projection, not its present-day survey result, and not leaned on here); stands "were not found at study sites where ... mean annual minimum temperature was below −16.8°C", and "moso and madake bamboo distribution in northern Japan depends primarily on temperature", agreeing with prior work "that moso bamboo cannot withstand temperatures around or below −18 to −20°C". From the growers: madake "Minimum Temp: 5°F (-15°C), Hardiness Zone: 7" (completebamboo.com), with another grower giving -20 to -23 C - a spread, not a number; and no read source says new SHOOTS take frost, only that "Colder weather will likely cause frost damage to leaves and canes" (bambubatu), so the record says only "frost-tender at the margin". So: a lowland paddy hamlet in a temperate province has bamboo as a matter of course, and a cold upland one may have none - that half is sourced. Two attested forms, so the map varies it from settlement to settlement.

Where it stood. Two places, both already in this record: (1) the homestead's own yard - on the Tonami plain the bamboo stood on the SOUTH side with the storehouses and fruit trees; and, inside the farmstead's grove of tall trees, on its windward west and north side, which is its own question, Did a farmstead's grove carry bamboo?; (2) the take-yabu (bamboo thicket) as its own stand at the village edge, harvested like a coppice. Bamboo was NOT scattered one culm at a time through a mixed wood: a stand is a clonal thicket, in most cases of nearly a single species.
