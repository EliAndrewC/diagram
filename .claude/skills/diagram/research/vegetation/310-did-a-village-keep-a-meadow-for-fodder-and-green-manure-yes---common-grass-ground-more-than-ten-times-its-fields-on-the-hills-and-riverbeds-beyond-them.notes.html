• kotobank-magusaba - 「Grass ground where fodder is cut. Most of it was commons (iriai), used jointly by the inhabitants of the area. Magusayama. Kusakariba. Kayaba. Koegusayama. Magusakariba.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「まぐさを刈る草生地。そのほとんどは入会地で、地域の住民が共同で利用した。秣山。草刈場。茅場(かやば)。肥草山(こえぐさやま)。秣刈場。」) (the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry)
  • kotobank-magusaba - 「In the Edo period, a moor whose purpose was the gathering of fodder and fertilizer.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、飼料・肥料採取を目的とした原野。」); 「the places where the fertilizer grass of the fields (karishiki) was gathered were also called magusaba, by confusion.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「田畑の肥草(こえくさ)（刈敷(かりしき)）を採取する場も秣場と混同して称した。」) (the Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho entry)
  • kotobank-magusaba - 「Magusaba were, as a rule, most often a commons at the level of the village or the hamlet.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「なお、秣場は通常、村・集落単位の入会(いりあい)場であることが多い。」)
  • fuchu-magusaba-jawiki - 「The magusaba was important as a source of livestock fodder, paddy fertilizer (karishiki) and fuel」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「秣場は、家畜の飼料・水田の肥料（刈敷）・燃料の供給地として重要なものであったが」); 「as the paddies increased, the land that could be opened shrank, and at the same time the places for gathering the grass that became fertilizer were disappearing, so a scramble between villages over the little grass there was broke out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水田が増えた一方で開発できる土地が減り、同時に肥料となる草を採取する場が無くなっていったことで、わずかな草をめぐる村同士の争奪戦が起きていた」)
  • aso-kayaba - 「When a settlement comes to 50 to 60 households, it must secure 20 to 30 hectares of kayaba to meet the demand.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「集落が50～60戸ともなると、その需要を満たすだけの茅場も20～30㌶は確保しなければなりません。」); 「The kaya is cut from February into March, when the leaves of the kaya have fallen and only the stems remain.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「茅切りは、茅の葉が落ちて茎だけになる2月から3月にかけて行なわれます。」)
  • biodic-kusayama - 「it is said that, to secure enough karishiki (grass and small branches put into the paddy as fertilizer) and fodder (grass fed to horses and cattle), sources of organic material such as grass mountains (moor, turf hills, brush hills and the like) more than ten times the fields were needed」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「十分な刈敷（水田に入れて肥料とする草や小枝）や秣（馬や牛の飼料とする草）を確保するためには田畑の１０倍以上の草山（原野・芝山・柴山など）等の有機資源の供給源が必要であったとされ」) (under the heading for the middle to late Edo period)
  • takamori-karishiki - 「According to the Man-nikki oboe-cho of Kaei 7, about 12 to 15 da of karishiki were put on 7 tan, which means 2 da (about 70 kan) of karishiki were put on each tan.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「嘉永 7 年の『万日記覚帳』によれば、7 反歩に刈敷 12～15 駄位入れたというから、反当り２駄（70 貫位）の刈敷を入れていたということになる。」); 「There was also a calculation that without mountain and moor exceeding 10 times the area of the fields, enough fertilizer could not be secured (Kusayama no kataru kinsei, by Mizumoto Kunihiko).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「田畑面積の 10 倍を超える山野がないと十分な肥料確保ができないという計算もあった（『草山の語る近世』水本邦彦著）。」) (a da is one horse-load)
  • kotobank-karishiki - 「For that reason wide grass-cutting grounds were needed around the paddies, and it was also gathered from the commons of hill and moor, land used in common.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「そのため水田の周囲には広大な採草地を必要とし，共同利用地である山野の入会地からも採取した。」) (the Yamakawa entry)
  • satoyama-jawiki - 「A hill whose trees have been deliberately clear-felled so that the whole hill is covered only in grass is called a kusayama. In early-modern paddy farming, dead grass was an important fertilizer. Kusayama were set up to supply the dead grass the village needed, and were managed so that no trees grew.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「ある山の樹木を意図的に皆伐し、山全体を草のみで覆ったものを草山と呼ぶ。近世の水田耕作では枯れ草が重要な肥料であった。村に必要な枯れ草を賄うために草山が設定され、樹木を生やさないように管理されていた。」)
  • biodic-kusayama - 「its form appears to have varied, some being grassland and some sparse woodland chiefly of pine up to 3 to 4 m tall」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「その形態は、草原であるものや、樹高３～４ｍまでの松を中心とした疎林であるものなど、多様であった様子」)
  • satoyama-jawiki - 「Sometimes a small kusayama was set up for the sole use of a particular paddy, and it was called a "tazuke kusayama".」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「特定の水田に専用の小規模な草山を設定することもあり「田付草山」と呼ばれた。」)
  • kitamoto-magusaba - 「As the opening of new fields went on in the city's area too, and the magusaba within the villages ran short, it was the natural course of things that dependence on Kaminuma and Shimonuma, the riverbed of the Arakawa, grew.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「市域でも新畑の開発が進み、村内の秣場が不足してくると荒川の河川敷である上沼と下沼へ依存を高めるのは自然の勢いである。」)
  • takamori-karishiki - 「Villages without hills came to ask villages with hills, borrow the hill, and take the grass and wood they needed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「山のない村は山のある村に頼んで、山を借り必要な草木をとるようになった。」)
