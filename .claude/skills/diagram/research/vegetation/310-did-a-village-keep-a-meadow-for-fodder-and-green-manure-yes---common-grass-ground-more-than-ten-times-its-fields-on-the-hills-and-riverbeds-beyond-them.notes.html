• kotobank-magusaba - 「Grass ground where fodder is cut. Most of it was commons (iriai), used jointly by the inhabitants of the area. Magusayama. Kusakariba. Kayaba. Koegusayama. Magusakariba.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry)
  • kotobank-magusaba - 「In the Edo period, a moor whose purpose was the gathering of fodder and fertilizer.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「the places where the fertilizer grass of the fields (karishiki) was gathered were also called magusaba, by confusion.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho entry)
  • kotobank-magusaba - 「Magusaba were, as a rule, most often a commons at the level of the village or the hamlet.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • fuchu-magusaba-jawiki - 「The magusaba was important as a source of livestock fodder, paddy fertilizer (karishiki) and fuel」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「as the paddies increased, the land that could be opened shrank, and at the same time the places for gathering the grass that became fertilizer were disappearing, so a scramble between villages over the little grass there was broke out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • aso-kayaba - 「When a settlement comes to 50 to 60 households, it must secure 20 to 30 hectares of kayaba to meet the demand.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「The kaya is cut from February into March, when the leaves of the kaya have fallen and only the stems remain.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • biodic-kusayama - 「it is said that, to secure enough karishiki (grass and small branches put into the paddy as fertilizer) and fodder (grass fed to horses and cattle), sources of organic material such as grass mountains (moor, turf hills, brush hills and the like) more than ten times the fields were needed」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (under the heading for the middle to late Edo period)
  • takamori-karishiki - 「According to the Man-nikki oboe-cho of Kaei 7, about 12 to 15 da of karishiki were put on 7 tan, which means 2 da (about 70 kan) of karishiki were put on each tan.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「There was also a calculation that without mountain and moor exceeding 10 times the area of the fields, enough fertilizer could not be secured (Kusayama no kataru kinsei, by Mizumoto Kunihiko).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (a da is one horse-load)
  • kotobank-karishiki - 「For that reason wide grass-cutting grounds were needed around the paddies, and it was also gathered from the commons of hill and moor, land used in common.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Yamakawa entry)
  • satoyama-jawiki - 「A hill whose trees have been deliberately clear-felled so that the whole hill is covered only in grass is called a kusayama. In early-modern paddy farming, dead grass was an important fertilizer. Kusayama were set up to supply the dead grass the village needed, and were managed so that no trees grew.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • biodic-kusayama - 「its form appears to have varied, some being grassland and some sparse woodland chiefly of pine up to 3 to 4 m tall」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • satoyama-jawiki - 「Sometimes a small kusayama was set up for the sole use of a particular paddy, and it was called a "tazuke kusayama".」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kitamoto-magusaba - 「As the opening of new fields went on in the city's area too, and the magusaba within the villages ran short, it was the natural course of things that dependence on Kaminuma and Shimonuma, the riverbed of the Arakawa, grew.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • takamori-karishiki - 「Villages without hills came to ask villages with hills, borrow the hill, and take the grass and wood they needed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
