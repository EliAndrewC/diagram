• kotobank-magusaba - 「with the end of the wars that had gone on since the Sengoku period, the demand for war horses fell in the early Edo period」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「戦国時代以来の戦乱の終結とともに、江戸初期には軍馬の需要が減って」); 「the magusaba changed into places for gathering karishiki」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「秣場が刈敷採取の場に変化した」); 「But though the war horses decreased, the pack horses coming and going on the highways carrying travelers and goods never ceased, and their demand grew with the development of the commercial economy, so it goes without saying that where these pack horses and the farm horses and cattle were kept, it was used as fodder ground in the original sense.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「しかし軍馬は減っても、旅客や貨物の運搬に街道を往来する荷馬は絶えることがなく、商品経済の発展とともにその需要は増したから、これらの荷馬および農耕牛馬を飼育する所では、本義の秣場として利用されたことはいうまでもない。」) (the Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho entry)
  • kitamoto-magusaba - 「Magusa is horse grass: literally the grass made into the horse's feed, that is, fodder. This magusa was taken from the undergrowth and young branches of the woods and moors, and was put into the stable as fodder, and also trodden by the horses into stable manure (fertilizer grass)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「秣（まぐさ）とは馬草のことで、文字通り馬の飼料にする草で飼葉（かいば）のことである。この秣は山林や原野の下草や若枝があてられ、厩（うまや）に入れて飼葉にするとともに、馬に踏ませて厩肥（きゅうひ）(肥草（こえぐさ）)とし」)
