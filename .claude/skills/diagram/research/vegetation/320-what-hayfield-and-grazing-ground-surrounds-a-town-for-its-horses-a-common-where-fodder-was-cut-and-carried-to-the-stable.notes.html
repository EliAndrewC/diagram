• kotobank-magusaba - 「with the end of the wars that had gone on since the Sengoku period, the demand for war horses fell in the early Edo period」 (translated; ); 「the magusaba changed into places for gathering karishiki」 (translated; ); 「But though the war horses decreased, the pack horses coming and going on the highways carrying travelers and goods never ceased, and their demand grew with the development of the commercial economy, so it goes without saying that where these pack horses and the farm horses and cattle were kept, it was used as fodder ground in the original sense.」 (translated; ) (the entry in the Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho, a Japanese general encyclopedia)
  • kitamoto-magusaba - 「Magusa is horse grass: literally the grass made into the horse's feed, that is, fodder. This magusa was taken from the undergrowth and young branches of the woods and moors, and was put into the stable as fodder, and also trodden by the horses into stable manure (fertilizer grass)」 (translated; )
