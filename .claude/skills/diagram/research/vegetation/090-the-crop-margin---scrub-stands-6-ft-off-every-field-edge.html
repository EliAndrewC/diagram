The crop margin - scrub stands 6 ft off every field edge

Sources: aze-standard, nougyoudoboku-keihan (the bund), pmc7538448-levee (levees walked and mowed periodically), kotobank-karishiki, conservation-headland-enwiki, meadow-enwiki (cleared land goes to scrub when the cutting stops), nonoichi-keihanritsu (the bund share on flat ground), hiroshima-keihan-manual (the bund and slope-face share on hill ground)

  • What was found. A paddy levee (keihan/aze) is a narrow earthen ridge - about a foot to two feet wide across its top and a foot tall - and wider, this record guesses up to ~3 ft, where it doubles as a footpath (the bund widths that were read are at How wide is a bund) (azemichi). The levee grass was CUT several times a season - for green manure and, this record guesses, for fodder and thatch. Constant cutting is what keeps woody scrub from establishing; this record takes the cut ground to reach about 1-2 m past a field's edge, a width no page read gives. No page read gives a traditional clean-strip width between a crop and the growth beyond it either. What the pages do measure is how much of the ground round a paddy is bund and bank that cannot be cropped: on the flat Kanazawa plain 2.8% of a paddy district, across hilly Hiroshima 9.5% on average, and in mountain paddies the slope faces below the fields can be larger than the flooded area itself; the bunds there are mown some 3.4 times a year today. So on flat ground the cut edge is the narrow bund, while on terraced ground it is the whole slope face below a field, which can be many times wider. The "conservation headlands" of modern British farming, strips along a field's edge where pesticide is sprayed only selectively, are a MODERN wildlife intervention, introduced in the 1980s, not the historical norm, and this record reasons, with no width read for them or for any historical margin, that East Asian land hunger kept margins narrow.
  • The decision it drove. A crop margin of 6 ft (~1.8 m) - the bund plus the cut strip beside it, a figure this record chose, since no page gives one. It is a flat-ground figure: where a field stands above a terrace riser, the kept-cut slope face below it is wider than 6 ft, and this entry does not set that width. Scrub of every kind stands off every paddy and dry plot by this margin, and the tall glyphs (the scraggly pines and the woodland crowns) stand back by as far as their drawn crowns spread as well, so nothing leans over a crop.
  • Disclosed departures. (1) Grass-tuft blade TIPS may lean up to a few real feet past the margin line at coarse tiers - accepted deliberately: this record takes it that the grass of a bund, cut only a few times a season, overhangs its edge, and the overhang is too small to see on the drawn map. (2) The reed marsh gets NO margin at all - this record takes it that wet ground starts at the polder dike, so reeds abutting a field's low bund is drawn as honest.
