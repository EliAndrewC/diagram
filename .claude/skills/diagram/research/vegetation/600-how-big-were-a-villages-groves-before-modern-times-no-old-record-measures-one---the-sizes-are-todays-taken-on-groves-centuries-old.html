How big were a village's groves before modern times? No old record measures one - the sizes are today's, taken on groves centuries old

The sizes the maps draw for the back grove, and every measurement behind them, are at The fengshui forest - real scale. This question asks whether those sizes describe the groves as they stood before modern times, or only as they survive today.

The back grove is old. The wood behind a village is not a modern planting: in Hong Kong the oldest dates back more than 300 years, and many have stood in place for well over 300 years, some more than 600, while much of the forest around them grew back only after the Second World War. Nor are their bounds a modern reserve's: 60 per cent of the known woods lie outside any legally protected zone.

The water-mouth grove is old too. In Huizhou, in the hills of southern Anhui, the village laid out between its stream's source and its water mouth was a fixed feature by the middle of the sixteenth century at the latest, and eighteenth-century verse describes the water mouth under dense shade, screened by bamboo and trees. One such grove has been counted: at Huangling, in Wuyuan county, the water-mouth grove holds over a hundred trees, most of them over five hundred years old. Trees that old stood there before 1912, so that grove already held something like a hundred trees then - a GUESS from their ages, since trees lost or planted since are not recorded.

But no old record measures either grove. No page read gives an area, a width or a tree count for a back grove or a water-mouth grove as it stood before 1912; every figure the maps use - the back grove's one to two hectares and its density, the water-mouth cluster's size - was measured in this century on groves that survive.

What this means for the map. Both groves are old forms, and the maps draw them as accurate. Their sizes are modern measurements of old groves, and there is no earlier figure to draw instead, so the maps keep the measured sizes; that the surviving groves keep the extent they had before modern times is a GUESS. The one count dated by its trees, Huangling's hundred-odd, is more than the few dozen old trees the water-mouth cluster draws.
