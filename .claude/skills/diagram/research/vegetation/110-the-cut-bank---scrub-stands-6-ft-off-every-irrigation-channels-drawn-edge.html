The cut bank - scrub stands 6 ft off every irrigation channel's drawn edge

Sources: reasoning from the crop-margin entry (pmc7538448-levee: levees mown; bund upkeep) applied to the channel bank; no source states a bank margin in feet - the 6 ft is the crop margin's figure reused, riparian-zone-enwiki

  • What prompted it. With only the water's own edge and the 6 ft crop margin keeping scrub off, the berm strips between the supply channels and the dry hem plots would carry tufts mid-strip. The GM read such strips as scrub crowding the channels and ruled that the bank takes a margin too.
  • The decision. Scrub stands 6 real feet off the drawn water edge of every irrigation course. The reasoning is the crop margin's, applied to the bank by analogy with the paddy levee: a levee is maintained ground, walked and kept up, its grass mown periodically, and this record takes a supply channel's bank to be kept the same way, so that woody scrub does not establish close to the water. The 6 ft is the crop margin's figure reused; no page read gives the width a bank's grass was cut to, or says of a bank in particular that mowing keeps woody growth off it - that land kept cut does not go over to scrub is the general point the crop-margin entry cites. Between them the crop margin and the bank margin close any berm strip up to ~12 ft of bare ground, which covers every hem berm the comb builds (3-9 ft on the Inashiro map).
  • Deliberate exclusions. (1) STREAMS take no margin - a natural brook bank is vegetated to the water's edge, and a sterile halo along a brook would be wrong; only the engineered courses are maintained ground. (2) The reed marsh keeps its no-margin rule from the crop-margin entry - reeds ARE the water fringe. (3) The blade tips of a grass tuft may still lean a few feet into the margin, exactly as at crop edges.
