• hanamoku-enoki - 「In the old days, a dosojin or a small shrine was worshipped near the entrance of a village, and enoki were planted there. This is thought to have been because the enoki had since ancient times been believed to be a tree that wards off evil spirits, because it spreads its branches wide and makes much shade, and because it bears fruit that can be eaten, among other reasons.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the page quotes a Chiba prefecture series on its great old trees)
  • ichirizuka-jawiki - 「a mound (a heap of earth) set beside the main highways at every 1 ri (about 3.927 kilometers), with Nihonbashi in Edo as the starting point」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「The milestone mound was made by heaping up earth 5 ken (about 9 meters) square and 1 jo (about 1.7 meters) high, and trees such as enoki were planted on top of it, with care taken that travelers could rest in the shade.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「They began when, on the 4th day of the 2nd month of Keicho 9 (March 4, 1604 in the Gregorian calendar), Tokugawa Ieyasu ordered his son Tokugawa Hidetada to set them up, with the gold-mine magistrate Okubo Nagayasu as general supervisor.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • ichirizuka-jawiki - 「The original form of the milestone mound is one set up as a pair on both sides of the highway.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • ichirizuka-jawiki - 「The tree species on the five highways were enoki 55%, pine 27% and sugi 8%」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-ichirizuka - 「They were built facing each other on both sides of the road, and as a rule the tree was the enoki.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the entry in the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the standard large dictionary of Japanese)
