Where does the scrub stop at a marsh? Grass grades into the reeds; brush and pines stop at the edge

Sources: none - the GM's ruling on the drawn edge and measurements on our own maps; what grows at a marsh's margin, which the edge follows, is at The marsh margin

The form the edge takes, and the two forms it is not (the GM, 2026-08-26: "the marshland is not supposed to overlap with the scrubland rendering"). Dry-ground scrub - brush discs, blades, pines - scatters, with no rule, through the reeds in the same pattern as outside them; under the rule, on one hamlet only four scrub bases stand inside its marshes, all of them on the marsh's own edge. Cutting the scatter dead at the marsh's boundary exposed a ruled line and a bare strip along the straight western edge of the toe, because the reeds themselves feather inset from that boundary. Letting every kind of scrub thin INTO the marsh across that feather closed the line, and left a seam of pines standing in the bog along some 1,600 ft of the northern edge, which the GM read as the overlap still being there: "it looks like it is still overlapping!" The form that holds: grass alone grades into the reeds across that feather, a wild edge with no line in it, while brush and pines stop dead at the marsh's edge - because, as The marsh margin records, a marsh's margin grades through sedge and grass into reed, and no pine or dry brush stands in it. The reverse rule is not needed: the marsh is drawn after the scrub and its own reeds already keep off crops, water and buildings.

The rule the map follows: no dry-ground scrub is drawn inside a marsh. Grass thins into the reed edge and is gone beyond it; brush and pines stop at the marsh's edge, and every patch of rough ground a map lays down after the marsh keeps out of it the same way.
