Did every farmstead keep its own bamboo, and on which side?

Sources: yashikirin-jawiki, tsuijimatsu, visit-toyama-sankyoson

Whether a hamlet has bamboo at all, and the communal thicket at the village edge, are at Bamboo: how common, where it stood; how a stand is drawn is at How is bamboo drawn?

Household bamboo, per farmstead - which side, and how common (the GM's question, 2026-08-27). The GM asked whether a farmstead's own bamboo always stood to the north-west and whether every farmstead had one.

  • WHERE (ja.wikipedia "屋敷林"): on the Tonami plain 「On the south side there were storehouses (kura), barns (naya) and the like, and fruit-bearing plants such as fig, grape and persimmon, and bamboo and so on, were planted」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「南側には蔵や納屋などがあり、無花果や葡萄、柿などの果実がなる植物や竹などが植えられていた」) - storehouses and barns on the SOUTH side, and there the fruit trees and the bamboo; and separately, 「at houses near rivers and in flood-prone areas, alder and root-spreading bamboo are planted as flood protection to keep the soil from washing away」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「河川の近い家や水害の多い地域では、防水用としてハンノキや、根を張る竹を植えて土壌の流出を防いでいる」) - by rivers and in flood-prone ground, bamboo (and alder) planted for its roots. From tsuijimatsu.com: the kainyo 「is centered on cedar, with ate (asunaro), zelkova, oaks, bamboo, persimmon, chestnut and others besides」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「はスギが中心で、ほかにアテ（アスナロ）・ケヤキ・カシ類・竹・柿・栗などで」) - cedar central, bamboo among the secondary species. A GUESS as far as the table goes: the grove as a whole faces the local harmful wind - north and west (Isawa, Iwate; Hikawa, Shimane), west (Iide, Yamagata), south and west (Tonami). The Tonami row, and the north and west of the igune generally, are on a page that was read; the Isawa, Hikawa and Iide rows were seen only in a search snippet, on no page that could be read. Not confirmed: the "N/W strip ... kitchen drain" sentence (the 1996 Tonami model homestead, the homestead research) - no readable source carries it. But the wind side itself is read: on the Tonami plain bamboo was often mixed into the grove from the west round to the north of the house, which is at Did a farmstead's grove carry bamboo? So the SIDE is three-formed at least (with the storehouses; at the wet edge; on the wind side) and is ROLLED per farmstead, weighted toward the back of the house and the shed's side.
  • HOW COMMON (Visit Toyama): on the Tonami plain "each house has been surrounded by homestead woodland ... over 7,000 houses", with "cedar, Japanese zelkova, and bamboo ... used as materials for building new houses as well as materials for various everyday tools"; and tsuijimatsu.com: 「bamboo was an important material for everyday life」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「竹は日常生活の資材として重要なものでした」). Not found: any share of farmsteads keeping bamboo, and any source distinguishing household bamboo from the communal take-yabu by number. So the presence rate is a GUESS: about three farmsteads in five keep a stand, "one of several secondary species" read as common but not universal.
  • As built: a 22 x 16 ft strip stands at a farmstead that has one, most often behind the house or beside the shed; on Inashiro 3 of 15 farmsteads keep one.
