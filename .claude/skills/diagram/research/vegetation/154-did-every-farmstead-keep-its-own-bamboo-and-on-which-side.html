Did every farmstead keep its own bamboo, and on which side?

Sources: yashikirin-jawiki, tsuijimatsu, visit-toyama-sankyoson

Whether a hamlet has bamboo at all, and the communal thicket at the village edge, are at Bamboo: how common, where it stood; how a stand is drawn is at How is bamboo drawn?

Household bamboo, per farmstead - which side, and how common (the GM's question, 2026-08-27). The GM asked whether a farmstead's own bamboo always stood to the north-west and whether every farmstead had one.

  • WHERE (ja.wikipedia "屋敷林"): on the Tonami plain 「On the south side there were storehouses (kura), barns (naya) and the like, and fruit-bearing plants such as fig, grape and persimmon, and bamboo and so on, were planted」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「南側には蔵や納屋などがあり、無花果や葡萄、柿などの果実がなる植物や竹などが植えられていた」) - storehouses and barns on the SOUTH side, and there the fruit trees and the bamboo; and separately, 「at houses near rivers and in flood-prone areas, alder and root-spreading bamboo are planted as flood protection to keep the soil from washing away」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「河川の近い家や水害の多い地域では、防水用としてハンノキや、根を張る竹を植えて土壌の流出を防いでいる」) - by rivers and in flood-prone ground, bamboo (and alder) planted for its roots. From tsuijimatsu.com: the kainyo 「is centered on cedar, with ate (asunaro), zelkova, oaks, bamboo, persimmon, chestnut and others besides」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「はスギが中心で、ほかにアテ（アスナロ）・ケヤキ・カシ類・竹・柿・栗などで」) - cedar central, bamboo among the secondary species. Region by region, the grove as a whole faces the local harmful wind: south and west on the Tonami plain, and the north and west for the igune generally; in Isawa, Iwate, the homestead grove shelters the house from the north-west winter wind. Other regions (Hikawa, Shimane; Iide, Yamagata) were searched for on 2026-08-27 and seen only in a search snippet, on no page that could be read, so their sides are not given here. Not confirmed: that the bamboo stood in a north-west strip by the kitchen drain (the 1996 Tonami model homestead, the homestead research) - no readable source carries it. But the bamboo's own side in the grove is read: on the Tonami plain bamboo was often mixed into the grove from the west round to the north of the house, a side read as windward from the Sendai groves rather than stated, which is at Did a farmstead's grove carry bamboo? So the SIDE is of at least three kinds (with the storehouses; at the wet edge; on the wind side) and is chosen at random per farmstead, weighted toward the back of the house (a seat no page read names, a GUESS), then the wind side, then the shed's side, and least toward the other flank. How much weight each side gets is a GUESS: no page read gives a share per side.
  • HOW COMMON (Visit Toyama): on the Tonami plain "each house has been surrounded by homestead woodland ... over 7,000 houses", with "cedar, Japanese zelkova, and bamboo ... used as materials for building new houses as well as materials for various everyday tools"; and tsuijimatsu.com: 「bamboo was an important material for everyday life」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「竹は日常生活の資材として重要なものでした」). Not found: any share of farmsteads keeping bamboo, and any source distinguishing household bamboo from the communal take-yabu by number. So the presence rate is a GUESS: about three farmsteads in five keep a stand, "one of several secondary species" read as common but not universal.
  • As built: a 22 x 16 ft strip (its size a GUESS) stands at a farmstead that has one. Its side is rolled: most often behind the house, then on the wind side, then beside the shed, least often on the other flank, each weight a GUESS. On Inashiro 8 of 15 farmsteads keep one, 6 at the windward north-west corner and 2 on the far flank: every shed there stands on the house's north side, so behind the house and beside the shed are one place, and no strip found room there: one rolled for either takes the windward corner. A farm drawn with its own grove carries its bamboo inside that grove rather than in a strip of its own, as the Tonami kainyo does, bamboo among its secondary species. There it is a patch of culms the strip's size, 22 x 16 ft, on the house side of each windward band of the grove. The size is a GUESS, taken from the strip, and so is its seat on the house side, and so is a patch in every windward band: the Tonami grove held its bamboo from the west round to the north of the house (at Did a farmstead's grove carry bamboo?), while its tall windbreak stood on the south and west, so the bamboo side and the wind side share only the west.
