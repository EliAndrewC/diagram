Why does the belt run off the edge of the sheet, and why is the bare stretch there not a hole?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-08-29; this project's measurements of the scripted hamlets

A cropped map is read as a window, not as a world. The GM's ruling (2026-08-29): "I do not mind if a lot of the windbreak forest is off the edge of the map. So I am happy to make a ruling that as long as it is clear from the map and where the map is cropped, that there is or could be more windbreak forest that we are not seeing, then that is fine. That is completely in line with our map cropping conventions. And even though it sounds as if this might look a little different in situations in which there is discontinuity in the clumping of trees of a windbreak forest - that is still fine." A reader already understands that a feature meeting the frame continues past it; that is the convention every road, field and watercourse on these sheets relies on. So a belt whose planting runs off the sheet is sound and needs no repair, and a bare stretch beyond the frame is not a hole in anything the reader is shown.

Where the map is judged for holes, it is judged only where a reader can look. The belt's recorded footprint runs off the page as freely as its clumps do. Measured on two maps: on one, the footprint spans 747 ft along its own across-wind axis while the frame holds only the middle 537 ft of that, and the planting covers 596 ft that overruns the visible stretch at both ends; on the other, footprint 917 ft, frame 741 ft, planting 787 ft. On both, the planting covers every column a reader can see. The whole apparent gap was off-page, so the scan for holes is clipped to the framed view - the same rule the planting already follows, where a clump whose crown merely crosses the frame is kept and one with no visible ink is waste.

The other repair was tried first and measured. Extending the PLANTING out to the ends of the belt's footprint, so the end stretches were offered seats like any interior gap, bought one clump on one map, at the page edge. It was reverted, because a belt that appears to stop short is usually a belt whose short end is off the page.

Why an off-page clump still counts, and what that costs. The window is clipped to the page; the PLANTING is not, so an off-page clump still fills an on-page column. The opposite form was priced - credit a clump only where its crown reaches the view. On one hamlet that form leaves a 60 ft bare run on the frame's bottom edge, and the ground underneath settles it - that visible ground is a homestead. One farmhouse, two threshing yards, two kitchen gardens and a persimmon stand within 90 ft of it, and a yard's southern sun corridor and a garden's eastern one are keep-outs the belt's seating is right to respect. The symmetric form therefore demands canopy exactly where the map is right to refuse it. The reader loses nothing: the canopy in that gap is the copse, which is what a dooryard grove is for.

The limitation that was accepted, and the alternative that was priced. The guarantee is weaker than it looks: the bare column at the page edge is excused by the copse that stands under it, and nothing holds the copse there. The cost of closing it was priced against one column at one page edge, and the weaker guarantee was accepted.

Where the ruling and the rule meet. The second half of the 2026-08-29 ruling - "even though it sounds as if this might look a little different in situations in which there is discontinuity in the clumping of trees of a windbreak forest, that is still fine" - read as covering ON-PAGE holes would relax the rule exactly where it has earned its keep: a wellhead seated inside a belt, or a lane crossing it, suppresses the clumps around it, and that is discontinuity in the clumping. The ruling and the rule's purpose meet head-on there, and the rule stands as written.

The rule the map follows: a large area feature near the frame - a shelter belt, a grazing ground, a wood, a forest - CROSSES the frame rather than sitting wholly inside it, so the reader sees that it continues. Nothing beyond the frame is drawn, and nothing beyond the frame is counted against the map: a belt is asked for unbroken planting only across the stretch a reader can see.
