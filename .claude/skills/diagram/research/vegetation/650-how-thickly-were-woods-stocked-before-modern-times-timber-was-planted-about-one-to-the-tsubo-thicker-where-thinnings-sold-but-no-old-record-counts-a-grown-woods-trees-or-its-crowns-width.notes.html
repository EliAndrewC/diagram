• tokugawa-1941-zorin - 「In the Edo period the number of seedlings planted was most often expressed as so many to the tsubo. Sometimes they were planted at a fixed interval, in units such as 'three shaku', 'six shaku', 'nine shaku' or 'two ken', 'three ken', 'ten ken', but this too is seen as an expression grown out of the idea of planting by the tsubo. There are also some cases of so many to the se or to the tan, but stating the count as so many to the chōbu, as is done today, was extremely rare.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (p. 191)
  • tokugawa-1941-zorin - 「As examples of dense planting there are even some set nine to the tsubo, but about three to the tsubo was the most common, and regionally it seems to have been practiced comparatively widely toward Kyushu and the Tohoku. In every case dense planting could not reach the aim of raising the wood unless it was accompanied by thinning.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (p. 204)
  • tokugawa-1941-zorin - 「Seen overall, planting density in the Edo period took one to the tsubo as the usual; but in regions where small thinnings had a value woods were planted thick, and where hill land was still wide and the soil fertile, even on ground where the trees could be expected to grow fast, in districts where thinnings were hard to use because transport was inconvenient, sparse planting was held proper.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (p. 207)

  • tokugawa-1941-zorin - 「In the hill woods of the Owase-Minamiura district of Kita-Muro county in Kii, where planting of sugi, hinoki and pine is said to have begun about the Genna era, a bakumatsu survey records that about three shaku apart was usual, and that at times 8,000 were planted to the chōbu. In neighboring Hikimoto, 10,000 sugi and hinoki to the chōbu and 7,000 pine were thought proper, and in Aiga village 10,000 sugi and hinoki to the chōbu and 8,000 pine ... in part of the Owase district named above, though it was the same district, sugi and hinoki were set at 800 to the chōbu and pine at 700, an extremely sparse planting.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (p. 197)

  • tokugawa-1941-zorin - 「The method of raising sugi woods in coastal districts practiced from the Tenpo era on in Shizukuishi village, Minami-Iwate county, Rikuchū: 'the planting distance is about four or five to seven or eight to a tsubo (six shaku square); but the spacing differs with the site: where wind and spray are fierce, plant thick to hold off wind damage; and where the ground is shut in by cliffs, like a ravine, and warm and close, the trees grow fast, so plant thin and let them grow well'.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (p. 205)
