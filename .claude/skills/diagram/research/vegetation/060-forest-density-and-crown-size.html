  • Density and crown size - the numbers and the why. A closed premodern hill wood (taken here to be the mixed broadleaf/conifer cover of a settled valley's back slope, cut over for fuel and timber on a rotation - that makeup is itself a GUESS on this entry; where a village's own fuel wood stood is answered in where a village kept its fuel wood) carries roughly 500-800 canopy stems per hectare - a GUESS, since the figure is on no page read. A village's worked coppice was stocked more thickly than this, and is answered in its own entry, how thickly a worked coppice was stocked. 1 ha = 107,639 sq ft, so ~600 stems/ha is one canopy tree per ~180 sq ft: a mean spacing near 13 ft. Canopy crowns in such a stand run ~5-8 m across (16-26 ft), also a GUESS with occasional wider emergents, so a mean crown radius of 8.5 ft (a 17 ft crown, at the low end of that band) is drawn, jittered 0.75-1.4x. Crowns of ~17 ft mean diameter on 13 ft centers OVERLAP, and that is the point - some classifications class a wood by its crown cover, and this record reads a closed wood as one whose crowns meet, so the packed look is honest rather than decorative. Same finding as the mulberry rows: drawn to scale, real planted density honestly IS a dense mass of crowns, not sparse symbols spaced for the eye. Nothing is inflated for legibility - at one foot per pixel a crown is drawn 12-24 pixels across and it shrinks with the map at coarser scales, exactly like the buildings.
