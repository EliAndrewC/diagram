  • Density and crown size - the numbers and the why. A closed premodern hill wood (the mixed broadleaf/conifer cover of a settled valley's back slope, cut over for fuel and timber on a rotation) carries roughly 500-800 canopy stems per hectare. 1 ha = 107,639 sq ft, so ~600 stems/ha is one canopy tree per ~180 sq ft: a mean spacing near 13 ft. Canopy crowns in such a stand run ~5-8 m across (16-26 ft) with occasional wider emergents, so a mean crown radius of 8.5 ft is drawn, jittered 0.75-1.4x. Crowns of ~17 ft mean diameter on 13 ft centers OVERLAP, and that is the point - closure is what makes a wood a wood, so the packed look is honest rather than decorative. Same finding as the mulberry rows: at a to-scale grain, drawing real planted density honestly IS a dense mass of crowns, not sparse symbols spaced for the eye. Nothing is inflated for legibility - at one foot per pixel a crown is drawn 12-24 pixels across and it shrinks with the map at coarser grains, exactly like the buildings.
