• tamamuro-2009-terauke - 「not all villages (numbering close to 65,000) in Japan had temples that could accept villagers as parishioners. Thus, when the bakufu demanded these certificates, a link between a temple and a potential parishioner had to be established swiftly.」; 「Many of these new parishioners chose their temple affiliations without doctrinal or faith considerations, but based on geographical proximity to a temple.」; 「It was during this period that all Japanese were compelled to produce certificates of temple registration」; 「the beginnings of the temple-parishioner system in the first half of the seventeenth century」

  • koshoji-jiin-zoka - 「There were dōjō that were the forerunners of temples, and in this period those dōjō came to take temple names.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「寺の前身となる道場があって、その道場がこの時代に寺号を号するようになったということです。」); 「As farming households, now widely established, became their parishioners, the dōjō developed into temples.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「広範に成立した農民の家が檀家となることで、道場は寺に発展していったのです。」) (of the Shin sect's temples in the early and middle Edo period; a dōjō here is a meeting hall of the Shin faithful, not a training hall)
  • onga-choshi-jiin - 「The temple was founded in Keichō 1 by Kiun Seiyō, 6th abbot of Ryūshōji of the Sōtō Zen school in Takakura village (at that time a hall-hermitage); Shōgan, 2nd abbot of Jōrakuji, had his petition granted in Genroku 1 with the whole village as parishioners; the next year, Inoguchi Jirōshichi (the headman was Inoguchi Koshichi) conferred with Shōgan and strove for the temple's founding, giving financial help among other things toward establishing it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「同寺は慶長元年高倉村禅宗洞家龍昌寺第6世機雲盛用が開山(当時堂庵)、常楽寺2世松岩和尚は元禄元戊辰年一村惣旦那として願済、翌2年井野口次郎七(庄屋は井野口小七)は松岩和尚と図り、寺院創建のため財政的援助など開創に努力した。」) (Jōrakuji of Onitsu; Keichō 1 is 1596, Genroku 1 is 1688)
