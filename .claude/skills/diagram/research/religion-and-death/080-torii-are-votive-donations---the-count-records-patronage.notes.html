  • fushimi-inari-senbon-map - 「The devotion by which worshippers seek to express their prayers and their feelings of gratitude by dedicating torii along the approach to the Myobu-sha arose already in the Edo period and has formed today's famous sight, the 'Senbon Torii' (Thousand Torii).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「崇敬者が祈りと感謝の念を命婦社参道に鳥居の奉納をもって表そうとする信仰は、すでに江戸時代に興り今日の名所「千本鳥居」を形作っています。」)
  • fushimi-inari-senbon - 「It is the result of the spread, from the Edo period onward, of donating torii as a token of gratitude, in the sense of thanks that a wish 'passes through' or 'has passed through'.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「願い事が「通る」或いは「通った」御礼の意味から、鳥居を感謝のしるしとして奉納することが江戸時代以降に広がった結果です。」) (on the FAQ page)
  • fushimi-inari-jawiki - 「The custom of dedicating torii began in the Edo period.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「鳥居を奉納する習わしは江戸時代に始まった。」); torii-enwiki - 「Fushimi Inari-taisha in Kyoto has thousands of such torii, each bearing the donor's name.」 (Fushimi's custom and, on the English page beside it, the donor's name on each gate; ujiko subscriptions, merchants and the local lord as the donor classes, and the custom's reach beyond Inari, are this page's own)
  • hokora-jawiki - 「Unlike a shrine it has no torii, or if it has one it is no more than a very small-scale thing.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「神社と異なり鳥居はないか、あっても非常に小規模なものに過ぎない。」); torii-enwiki - 「If the sandō passes under multiple torii, the outer of them is called ichi no torii (一の鳥居; first torii).
