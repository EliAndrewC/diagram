• sodo-jawiki - 「It is a place where practitioners (monks) carry on a communal life while devoting themselves to Buddhist practice.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「修行者（僧侶）が集団生活を行いながら仏道修行に励む場である」) (communal monastic housing as one of the compound's own halls, from a Japanese Zen hall; the 25 to 45 count, the initiates and the lay servants are this page's own arithmetic)
  • ranzan-kinsenji - 「kuri: the building in which the temple's head priest and his family live」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「庫裏(くり)…寺の住職や家族の住んでいる建物。」) (the web editors' gloss on a village temple's register entry, which lists the kuri among the temple's buildings; that the priest was married is the gloss's "family")
  • kamigamo-shake-plan - 「This area is where, from the Muromachi period, the townscape took shape as a quarter of the residences of the Kamigamo shrine's priests.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「このあたりは，室町時代から上賀茂神社の神官の屋敷町として町並みが形成されてきたところである。」); 「the earthen bridges over the Myōjin river, the earthen walls along the river, the gates of the priest families' houses, the gable-entered priest houses」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明神川に架かる土橋，川沿いの土塀，社家の門，妻入りの社家」); 「These traditional buildings can be broadly divided into the priest-house style and the townhouse style.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「これらの伝統的建造物は，社家と町家の様式に大別できる。」) ; and 「Until the Meiji Restoration, the old settlement was a settlement of a special character, in which the Kamigamo shrine's priests (the shrine officials and the ujibito) and farmers lived clustered together.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明治維新までの旧集落は，上賀茂神社の神官（社司と氏人）と農民が集住する特殊な性格を持つ集落であった。」) (a shrine's priest families, not a temple's)
