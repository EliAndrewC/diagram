  • kotobank-bomori - 「Bōmori means the keeper of a temple lodging, but in the Shin sect in particular it means the head priest's wife.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「坊守 (ぼうもり) 寺坊の番人を指すが，とくに真宗では，住職の妻をいう。」); 「In the Ikkō Senju Nenbutsu Myōchō dated the 13th of the first month of Kōkoku 4 (1343) there is "Shaku Ryōshinbō, head of the meeting hall; Enshinbō, bōmori", and it can be confirmed that the wife of a meeting hall's head was already called bōmori.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「興国4年（1343）正月13日付の《一向専修念仏名帳》には，〈釈了心房道場坊主 円心房坊守〉とあり，すでに道場主の妻を坊守と呼んでいたことが確認できる。」); and 「In Rennyo's Gobunshō, in five fascicles, there is "the wives of the priests of the lodging houses"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「蓮如の《御文章（ごぶんしよう）》五帖には〈多屋の坊主達の内方〉とあり」) (Kitanishi Hiroshi's article in the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia)
  • koshoji-yoshizaki-taya - 「It was in Bunmei 3 (1471), at the age of fifty-seven, that Rennyo moved to Yoshizaki.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「蓮如上人が吉崎に移るのは文明三年（1471）、五十七歳の時のことです。」); and 「At Yoshizaki a town grew up, and several lodging houses were built too.   • sakamoto-satobo-otsu - 「In the Edo period, when monks who had continued their training on Mount Hiei grew old, they were granted by the Tendai head a "satobō (retirement residence)" at the foot of the mountain.
