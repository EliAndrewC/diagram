  • ranzan-kinsenji - 「rules approved, Shōwa 17 (1942)」 (translated; ); 「Kuri: frontage six ken three shaku」 (translated; ); and 「kuri: the building in which the temple's head priest and his family live」 (translated; ) (a village temple's entry in the Hiki district temple register, which carries a note of 1942 and lists the kuri among the temple's buildings, and the web editors' gloss on it; that the priest was married is the gloss's "family", and the gloss is modern usage)
  • kotobank-bomori - 「Bōmori means the keeper of a temple lodging, but in the Shin sect in particular it means the head priest's wife.」 (translated; ); 「In the Ikkō Senju Nenbutsu Myōchō dated the 13th of the first month of Kōkoku 4 (1343) there is "Shaku Ryōshinbō, head of the meeting hall; Enshinbō, bōmori", and it can be confirmed that the wife of a meeting hall's head was already called bōmori.」 (translated; ); and 「In Rennyo's Gobunshō, in five fascicles, there is "the wives of the priests of the lodging houses"」 (translated; ) (Kitanishi Hiroshi's article in the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia)
  • koshoji-yoshizaki-taya - 「It was in Bunmei 3 (1471), at the age of fifty-seven, that Rennyo moved to Yoshizaki.」 (translated; ); and 「At Yoshizaki a town grew up, and several lodging houses were built too. A lodging house was the quarters of the priests stationed at Yoshizaki. The lodging houses were also used to lodge the believers who came to worship.」 (translated; ) (that the lodging houses housed the priests' wives is this page's reading of this passage with the encyclopedia article under kotobank-bomori)
  • yoshizaki-gobo-jawiki - 「Moreover, the Yoshizaki area round the temple was lined with priests' quarters, lodgings for the believers and the like, and formed a temple town.」 (translated; ) (an encyclopedia article; it gives no count of the houses)
  • kamigamo-shake-plan - 「This area is where, from the Muromachi period, the townscape took shape as a quarter of the residences of the Kamigamo shrine's priests.」 (translated; ); 「the earthen bridges over the Myōjin river, the earthen walls along the river, the gates of the priest families' houses, the gable-entered priest houses」 (translated; ); 「These traditional buildings can be broadly divided into the priest-house style and the townhouse style.」 (translated; ) ; and 「Until the Meiji Restoration, the old settlement was a settlement of a special character, in which the Kamigamo shrine's priests (the shrine officials and the ujibito) and farmers lived clustered together.」 (translated; ) (a shrine's priest families, not a temple's)
  • takabatake-naragoround - 「the Kasuga shrine's priests' quarter, which survived until Meiji」 (translated; ); and 「01 The lower Negi path (the Whispering Path): one of the paths by which the Kasuga shrine's priests went from Takabatake, which was the priests' quarter, to the Kasuga shrine.」 (translated; )
  • sakamoto-satobo-otsu - 「In the Edo period, when monks who had continued their training on Mount Hiei grew old, they were granted by the Tendai head a "satobō (retirement residence)" at the foot of the mountain. In the Sakamoto district many of these retirement residences were built.」 (translated; )
