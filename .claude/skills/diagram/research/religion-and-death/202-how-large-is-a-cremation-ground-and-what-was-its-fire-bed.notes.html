• tokyo-kasoba-history - 「The Hikawa village cremation ground stood in Hikawa village, Nishitama district, Tokyo (now Hikawa, Okutama town, Nishitama district); its year of construction was 13 December Taisho 11, the site was 10 tsubo 5 go (34.65 m²), and the building was a simple cremation ground of 6 tsubo (19.88 m²).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「氷川村火葬場は東京都西多摩郡氷川村（現西多摩郡奥多摩町氷川）にあったもので、建設年は大正 11 年 12 月 13 日、敷地は 10 坪 5 合（34.65 ㎡）、建物は 6 坪（19.88 ㎡）の簡易な火葬場であった。」); a second site at 「The site was 42 tsubo, and the building was a single-story wood-and-mortar structure. There was one cremation furnace and the fuel used was firewood.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「敷地は 42 坪で、建物は平家木造モルタル造であった。火葬炉は 1 基で燃料は薪を使用していた。」); and a third at 「The site was 600 tsubo (1,980 m²), the cremation room a single-story wooden building of 36 tsubo (118.8 m²), with an attached single-story wooden building of 12 tsubo (39.6 m²).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「敷地は 600 坪（1,980 ㎡）、火葬室は木造平屋建 36 坪（118.8 ㎡）で、木造平屋建 12 坪（39.6 ㎡）の附属建物があった。」) The dated examples are Meiji through Showa municipal facilities rather than Edo ones.
  • tokyohakuzen-yoyogi - 「In the Bunroku era (1593-1596) the crematory (hiya, that is, a cremation ground) at Sennichidani in Yotsuya moved to Sendagaya village, and in Kanbun 4 it moved to Okamidani in Yoyogi village.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「文禄年(1593–1596年) 、四谷千日谷にあった火屋(火葬場)が千駄ヶ谷村に移り、寛文４年に代々木村狼谷に移転。」); 「It is recorded in the local history 'Shinpen Musashi Byobu Fudoki-ko' [sic], compiled by the Shoheizaka Gakumonjo, the Edo shogunate's own institution of learning, that it was 900 tsubo in extent and that there were three houses of those engaged in cremation.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「広さ9百坪で火葬に従事した者の家が3軒あったことが江戸幕府の直轄教学機関である昌平坂学問所が編纂した郷土史「新編武蔵屏風土記稿」に記録されている。」) (a crematory company's own history quoting the gazetteer, whose title it misprints)
  • kasoba-jawiki - 「They burned the dead in open country away from habitation, stacking up firewood and laying the body on top of it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「人里離れた野原で木薪を組み上げてその上に遺体を載せてで焚焼していた。」); 「Beside a burial ground and the like, a permanent fire bed was provided by digging a shallow trench about one size larger than a coffin and framing it with stone, earthenware and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「墓地の傍らなどに、棺桶より一回り大きい程度の浅い溝を掘って石や土器などで枠組みするなどした恒久的な火床を設けて」); and, of the roofed forms that appeared around the middle of the Edo period, 「... a fire bed made on ground leveled and laid with firm good soil, with posts raised at the four corners several shaku away from the fire bed, carrying a simple roof ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「...硬質良土を敷き込んで整地した上に火床を作り、火床より数尺離れた四隅に柱を建てて簡易な屋根を掛けたもの...」) and 「... in snow-country districts, a hut of about 4 to 6 tatami is built with a full tiled roof set on stout walls of stacked cut stone, and a channeled fire bed is dug into its center ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「...積雪地帯では切出石を積み上げた強固な壁の上に本格的な瓦屋根を載せた4 - 6畳ほどの小屋を造り、その中心に溝形火床を掘り込む...」) - the tatami figure belongs to the snow-country hut of stone and tile, not to the four posts and the simple roof
