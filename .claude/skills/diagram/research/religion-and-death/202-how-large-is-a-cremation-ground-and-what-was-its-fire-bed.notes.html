• tokyo-kasoba-history - 「The Hikawa village cremation ground stood in Hikawa village, Nishitama district, Tokyo (now Hikawa, Okutama town, Nishitama district); its year of construction was 13 December Taisho 11, the site was 10 tsubo 5 go (34.65 m²), and the building was a simple cremation ground of 6 tsubo (19.88 m²).」 (translated; ); a second site, the Komiya Kigenso ground of a union of Komiya, Tokura and Hinohara villages, 「It was a cremation ground of a union of Komiya village, Tokura village and Hinohara village. It was established on 5 August Showa 22.」 (translated; ), 「The site was 42 tsubo, and the building was a single-story wood-and-mortar structure. There was one cremation furnace and the fuel used was firewood.」 (translated; ); and a third, Hachioji's, 「According to the cremation-ground register, it was established on 28 September Meiji 43 (1910) (directive no. 1045). It is thought to have been a privately owned simple facility, and on 28 December Taisho 9 (1920) the transfer from Hayashi Gonjiro (transferor) to Hachioji city (transferee) was approved.」 (translated; ), 「The site was 600 tsubo (1,980 m²), the cremation room a single-story wooden building of 36 tsubo (118.8 m²), with an attached single-story wooden building of 12 tsubo (39.6 m²).」 (translated; ) The dated examples run from 1910 to 1947, none of them Edo; Hachioji's was likely private until 1920.
  • tokyohakuzen-yoyogi - 「In the Bunroku era (1593-1596) the crematory (hiya, that is, a cremation ground) at Sennichidani in Yotsuya moved to Sendagaya village, and in Kanbun 4 it moved to Okamidani in Yoyogi village.」 (translated; ); 「It is recorded in the local history 'Shinpen Musashi Byobu Fudoki-ko' [sic], compiled by the Shoheizaka Gakumonjo, the Edo shogunate's own institution of learning, that it was 900 tsubo in extent and that there were three houses of those engaged in cremation.」 (translated; ) (a crematory company's own history quoting the gazetteer, whose title it misprints)
  • kasoba-jawiki - 「They burned the dead in open country away from habitation, stacking up firewood and laying the body on top of it.」 (translated; ); 「Beside a burial ground and the like, a permanent fire bed was provided by digging a shallow trench about one size larger than a coffin and framing it with stone, earthenware and so on.」 (translated; ); and 「Around the middle of the Edo period ...」 (translated; ), the forms that then appeared being 「... a fire bed made on ground leveled and laid with firm good soil, with posts raised at the four corners several shaku away from the fire bed, carrying a simple roof ...」 (translated; ) and 「... in snow-country districts, a hut of about 4 to 6 tatami is built with a full tiled roof set on stout walls of stacked cut stone, and a channeled fire bed is dug into its center ...」 (translated; ) - the tatami figure belongs to the snow-country hut of stone and tile, not to the four posts and the simple roof; the page dates the forms to a period and gives no year
  • kasoba-jawiki - 「Cremation done in this way in the open, without a building, a furnace or other permanent equipment for it, is called 'field burning', and until the end of the Edo period the great majority of cremation grounds were 'field-burning grounds'.」 (translated; )
