• tokyo-kasoba-history - 「The Hikawa village cremation ground stood in Hikawa village, Nishitama district, Tokyo (now Hikawa, Okutama town, Nishitama district); its year of construction was 13 December Taisho 11, the site was 10 tsubo 5 go (34.65 m²), and the building was a simple cremation ground of 6 tsubo (19.88 m²).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「氷川村火葬場は東京都西多摩郡氷川村（現西多摩郡奥多摩町氷川）にあったもので、建設年は大正 11 年 12 月 13 日、敷地は 10 坪 5 合（34.65 ㎡）、建物は 6 坪（19.88 ㎡）の簡易な火葬場であった。」); a second site, the Komiya Kigenso ground of a union of Komiya, Tokura and Hinohara villages, 「It was a cremation ground of a union of Komiya village, Tokura village and Hinohara village. It was established on 5 August Showa 22.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「小宮村、戸倉村、檜原村の組合による火葬場である。昭和 22 年 8 月 5 日に設置されている。」), 「The site was 42 tsubo, and the building was a single-story wood-and-mortar structure. There was one cremation furnace and the fuel used was firewood.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「敷地は 42 坪で、建物は平家木造モルタル造であった。火葬炉は 1 基で燃料は薪を使用していた。」); and a third, Hachioji's, 「According to the cremation-ground register, it was established on 28 September Meiji 43 (1910) (directive no. 1045). It is thought to have been a privately owned simple facility, and on 28 December Taisho 9 (1920) the transfer from Hayashi Gonjiro (transferor) to Hachioji city (transferee) was approved.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「火葬場台帳によると、設置は明治 43 年（1910）9 月 28 日（指令第 1045 号）となっている。個人所有の簡易な施設であったと思われ、大正 9 年（1920）12 月 28 日に林権次郎（譲渡人）から八王子市（譲受人）に於いて譲受の件が許可された。」), 「The site was 600 tsubo (1,980 m²), the cremation room a single-story wooden building of 36 tsubo (118.8 m²), with an attached single-story wooden building of 12 tsubo (39.6 m²).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「敷地は 600 坪（1,980 ㎡）、火葬室は木造平屋建 36 坪（118.8 ㎡）で、木造平屋建 12 坪（39.6 ㎡）の附属建物があった。」) The dated examples run from 1910 to 1947, none of them Edo; Hachioji's was likely private until 1920.
  • tokyohakuzen-yoyogi - 「In the Bunroku era (1593-1596) the crematory (hiya, that is, a cremation ground) at Sennichidani in Yotsuya moved to Sendagaya village, and in Kanbun 4 it moved to Okamidani in Yoyogi village.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「文禄年(1593–1596年) 、四谷千日谷にあった火屋(火葬場)が千駄ヶ谷村に移り、寛文４年に代々木村狼谷に移転。」); 「It is recorded in the local history 'Shinpen Musashi Byobu Fudoki-ko' [sic], compiled by the Shoheizaka Gakumonjo, the Edo shogunate's own institution of learning, that it was 900 tsubo in extent and that there were three houses of those engaged in cremation.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「広さ9百坪で火葬に従事した者の家が3軒あったことが江戸幕府の直轄教学機関である昌平坂学問所が編纂した郷土史「新編武蔵屏風土記稿」に記録されている。」) (a crematory company's own history quoting the gazetteer, whose title it misprints)
  • kasoba-jawiki - 「They burned the dead in open country away from habitation, stacking up firewood and laying the body on top of it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「人里離れた野原で木薪を組み上げてその上に遺体を載せてで焚焼していた。」); 「Beside a burial ground and the like, a permanent fire bed was provided by digging a shallow trench about one size larger than a coffin and framing it with stone, earthenware and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「墓地の傍らなどに、棺桶より一回り大きい程度の浅い溝を掘って石や土器などで枠組みするなどした恒久的な火床を設けて」); and 「Around the middle of the Edo period ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代中期ごろになると」), the forms that then appeared being 「... a fire bed made on ground leveled and laid with firm good soil, with posts raised at the four corners several shaku away from the fire bed, carrying a simple roof ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「...硬質良土を敷き込んで整地した上に火床を作り、火床より数尺離れた四隅に柱を建てて簡易な屋根を掛けたもの...」) and 「... in snow-country districts, a hut of about 4 to 6 tatami is built with a full tiled roof set on stout walls of stacked cut stone, and a channeled fire bed is dug into its center ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「...積雪地帯では切出石を積み上げた強固な壁の上に本格的な瓦屋根を載せた4 - 6畳ほどの小屋を造り、その中心に溝形火床を掘り込む...」) - the tatami figure belongs to the snow-country hut of stone and tile, not to the four posts and the simple roof; the page dates the forms to a period and gives no year
  • kasoba-jawiki - 「Cremation done in this way in the open, without a building, a furnace or other permanent equipment for it, is called 'field burning', and until the end of the Edo period the great majority of cremation grounds were 'field-burning grounds'.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「このように火葬を行うための建物や炉などの恒久的設備を設けず野外で行う火葬は「野焼き」と呼ばれており、江戸時代末までは大多数の火葬場が「野焼き場」だった。」)
