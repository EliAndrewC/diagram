How big is a temple in a temple quarter, and how do the temples line the street?

Sources: kanazawa-teramachidai-plan (a temple quarter's size, its two streets and what stands on a precinct), hirosaki-rekimachi-ch4 (why the temples were gathered, and sorted by sect), zenringai-jawiki (the temples on one street), hirosaki-shinteramachi-matinami (the town houses among a temple street's temples), matsukura-2008-taito (the blocks of Edo's temple quarters)

What the research found - the quarter. Kanazawa's Teramachidai district, about 1,220 by 1,000 m (some 4,000 by 3,300 ft) and 22 ha (54 acres), holds 52 temples, 2 shrines and about 320 town houses, most of the temples on ground the lord granted them, sorted by sect along its two streets by the domain's plan. At Hirosaki the lord gathered the temples in 1610 for the castle town's defense and the control of its population, a street of Soto temples round his own family temple and, after the fire of 1649, a new street for the other sects; 33 temples line that first street's avenue. The quarter was never temples alone: the second street, laid out in 1650, had 45 town houses and one samurai house beside its temples in 1678, and early in Meiji 14 of its 234 houses were temples.

What the research found - two ways to meet the street. Along Kanazawa's wide straight road the temples, with large precincts, stand in an orderly unbroken row, most with an earthen wall and a gate on the street, the main hall, kuri and bell tower seen over the wall. Along its narrower curving road the old gate-land lots survive with town houses lining the street, so the temples are hard to see and have few walls; each gate stands back from the street, and the main hall, kuri and bell tower stand at the end of an approach. The town houses' lots are narrow and deep, facing each other across the street.

What the research found - a precinct. Most main halls face the street; where the kuri stands depends on how big the precinct is; the larger, hip-roofed halls all have a frontage of 8 ken (some 48 ft) or more; and a garden behind the hall is built up against the graveyard or the neighbor's lot at the back. An ordinary Edo temple's precinct was about 1,000 tsubo ("What does a temple precinct hold?"). The Edo-period blocks that survive in Edo's old temple quarters average about 7,300 to 9,000 m² (some 79,000 to 97,000 sq ft), against 2,800 m² for the ward's blocks as a whole, room for two or three such precincts to a block, and Kanazawa's 22 ha come to about 4,100 m² for each of its 54 temples and shrines, streets and town houses included - both this project's arithmetic.

What was not found. No page read gives a single ordinary temple's frontage and depth, or the size of a small temple in a Chinese city's lanes. The quarter's small shrines are "How big is a small shrine in a town?".

The decision it drove. A temple quarter meets its street in two attested forms, so the form is a knob, rolled per quarter from the map's seed: the walled row, precincts side by side along a wide straight street, each with its wall and gate on the street; and the gate-land row, town houses on narrow deep lots lining the street, each temple behind them reached by an approach from a gate set back between them. The counts per block are this project's arithmetic from the attested sizes.

The rule the map follows: the precincts of a temple quarter are ordinary precincts of about 35,000 sq ft, two or three to a block, sorted by sect, their main halls facing the street and their graveyards at the back; the quarter also carries town houses, on the street itself in the gate-land row.
