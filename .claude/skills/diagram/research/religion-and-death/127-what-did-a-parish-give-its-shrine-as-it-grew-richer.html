What did a parish give its shrine as it grew richer? Guardian dogs, lanterns, strength stones and a fence

Sources: komainu-jawiki (the commoners' stone guardian dogs), gltjp-zenkokuji and darumamuseum-bishamonten (Bishamon's tigers), niiza-ishigami-lantern (a village's dated lanterns), nerima-hikawa-chikaraishi (strength stones beside an approach)

FeatureWhat was foundOn the sheet
Stone guardian dogs, the komainuStone pairs given by commoners multiplied from the Edo period on. At a temple of Bishamon the pair is tigers, "unlike the more common guardian lion-dogs", the general form being "Stone tigers like Komainu in front of Bishamon temples".On the wealth knob, none at average; a Bishamon shrine's are tigers (accurate at named temples, its reach to a village a guess).
Stone lanternsVotive gifts at village shrines: a pair given to a village's tutelary shrine is inscribed in 1799 by the village's parishioners.On the wealth knob, none at average.
Strength stones, the chikaraishiAt one shrine, eight oval river stones placed beside the approach, used by the young men of the region for trials of strength from late Edo into Meiji.On the wealth knob, none at average.
A fence around the sanctuaryA later donation (under "Was a village shrine walled or fenced?").On the wealth knob, none at average.

The decision. The guardian dogs and the lanterns are gifts that accrue with a parish's wealth; the strength stones, set beside the approach, and the sanctuary's fence go with them on the sheet's wealth knob, and at average wealth none is drawn. What stands in the precinct at average wealth is under "What else stood in a village shrine's precinct?".
