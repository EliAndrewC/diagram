Why does that swept ground have a ragged edge?

Sources: ndl-crd-nanukabon (the graves and their paths cleaned at the start of Bon), kotobank-ujiko (who cleaned a village shrine); the GM's ruling of 2026-07-23; the equinox festivals, from the campaign notes; this project's own measurement of its drawn maps

The ruling. A swept collar drawn as the feature's own footprint grown outward reads as a crisp bare box in the scrub around every shrine and every graveyard, because the scrub is drawn everywhere except inside it. The GM ruled on 2026-07-23 that "such clearage rarely conforms to precise geometric shapes". The why: swept ground is PRODUCED by tending - brooms, feet, the traffic of the approach - radiating outward from the feature, and its edge sits wherever the tending peters out into the scrub that season; nobody surveys it. That the tending came in bouts at set times of year is attested: the graves (and, in one modern popular account, the paths to them) were cleaned on the day that opens Bon, in this setting at the spring and autumn equinox festivals too, and, in the form folklore records in modern Japan, a village shrine was cleaned by representatives its own households chose, sometimes in rotation. How far out each bout reached is in no source read (Is the ground around a shrine or a grave swept clear of scrub?). Ruled edges belong to what is surveyed or built - a wall, a paddy bund, a house plot - which is the same reasoning that gives the perimeter dike and the common burial ground their shapes.

Notches inward, and no lobes outward. The irregular edge is cut INTO the padded rectangle: shallow notches, deeper along the sides than at the corners. Outward lobes were tried and rejected, for two reasons. A swept collar is a maintenance CLAIM, so making it irregular should mean the sweeping falls SHORT of the surveyed ideal; it should never annex ground the tending does not reach. And a lobe can newly cover scrub that legitimately predates the clearing - a graveyard's lobe reaching a toe marsh that was already there. Notches-only makes every swept collar a subset of the rectangle the ground already had, so nothing else on the map moves.

The rule the map follows: at every tier the swept ground is DRAWN as an irregular patch bitten out of the swept collar, never as a rectangle, and the same feature always draws the same patch. The ground it RESERVES - what placement and clearance rules test against - stays the plain rectangle, so the shape is a drawing decision and never a placement one.
