Torii spacing - two regimes and nothing in between

Sources: meiji-jingu-access (the ~10-minute walk from each entrance to the main hall), nara-nagao-jinja (about 300 m between Nagao Shrine's first and second gates, on an approach a little over 7 m wide); Kasuga's 1.3 km is this page's reading, on no page read; meiji-jingu-jawiki counts 8 torii, so "three" there is the ranked set (一・二・三の鳥居), not the total29, sanuki-kokubunji-jawiki, shimotsuke-kokubunji-jawiki

  • How far apart, and how far from the hall. What the research found: real torii spacing, as this page reads the attested cases, falls into two regimes with nothing between them.141 (1) Donation rows: Fushimi Inari's Senbon Torii section runs ~800 gates104 along ~400 m in two parallel rows - a pitch on the order of 0.5-1 m - and where the row loosens further up the mountain the gates are described as "at times tightly packed and at times irregularly spaced and several yards apart", i.e. ~3-9 m at the loose end. (2) Ranked gates (ichi- / ni- / san-no-torii) are landmarks strung along a whole approach, not a corridor: Nagao Shrine's ichi->ni is about 300 m, on an approach a little over 7 m (4 ken) wide,88 Meiji Jingu's three span a ~10-minute walk from the entrance to the main hall - the first gate at the approach's mouth, the second where the southern and northern approaches meet, the third in front of the worship hall - so the ~250 m pitch beside it is this project's own arithmetic on those ten minutes,22 and Kasuga Taisha's ichi-no-torii is said to stand 1.3 km from the shrine, which no page read states.89 At one to three feet per pixel a ranked pair is off the map at every settlement scale. The decision: since Rokugan's 1/3/7 set is neither regime, the pitch is an explicit house rule (~20 ft, cap 32 ft) rather than a copied fact - a deliberate invention, with no source behind it. The approach GAP, by contrast, is grounded: a village shrine's arch sits ~20 ft off its hall (a small keidai with the hall right there), and a great precinct may legitimately keep a courtyard between its OUTERMOST arch and its main hall, comfortably inside the norm for a proper precinct (Sanuki's Nara-period provincial temple, its kokubunji, had a precinct of 240 by 220 m, and that is the small end - Shimotsuke's ran 457 by 413 m).122 Where the INNERMOST arch stands is a separate question, and the GM settled it below.

A donation row is a designated-site special case (the GM's ruling of 2026-07-25). The Fushimi-style row - dozens to thousands of arches, given one at a time and standing almost shoulder to shoulder - is not how an ordinary approach in this setting works. It belongs to a handful of named great sites: Shinden Togashi, the Temple of Amaterasu, the Ki Rin Shrine and others of that rank. Everywhere else the 1 / 3 / 7 set is the whole vocabulary. A map of one of those sites is therefore drawn as an explicit outlier, exempt from the pitch cap; and drawing its row honestly would want the one-arch-span FLOOR relaxed as well, because a real donation row is packed tighter than a 16 ft glyph allows at these scales. That relaxation has not been taken; the floor is a drawing limit, not a finding about how gates were spaced.

And the avenue STARTS at its hall (the GM's ruling of 2026-07-27): "the distance from the front of the temple should be the same as the distance between each torii arch." An avenue can be perfectly spaced at 20 ft and still stand yards away from the temple it serves - three arches up a street on the far side of a flophouse, or with the caption and two rows of houses between it and its hall - and then it does not read as that temple's approach; it reads as red marks near some other building. So the threshold is set alongside the count and the stride: the whole run is moved bodily, keeping its direction, its curve and its stride and changing only its distance, until the innermost arch stands one pitch off the nearest FACE of the hall (measured to the footprint rather than to the center, so a run drawn off to one side slides onto the flank it stands off rather than diagonally into the middle of the building). The rule is an upper bound rather than an equality, deliberately: a village path seats its arches at 0.6 to 0.9 of its 30 ft stride, and the village rule governs that end, so the two meet without forcing cosmetic change on maps the GM has already approved.

And a caption never stands in the approach (the same conversation: an arch must "never be covered by the 'temple of X' label"). A hall's own caption and its approach both want the ground at the hall's face, which is where the collision came from. The caption tries three baselines in a STRICT ORDER - the side the map's author asked for, that same side pushed past the far end of the avenue, then the opposite side - and takes the first that touches no arch. Stepping PAST the avenue has to exist because a parish graveyard may sit deliberately at the back of a monastery, so flipping the caption off the approach would only bury it in the cemetery. An order rather than a score, for a drawing-order reason: a hall goes down early, so a scorer can see almost nothing at that point - a fire tower, the clergy houses and the graveyard's neighbors are all drawn later - and a scorer working blind can put a caption on a fire tower that does not exist yet. The author of the map knows what is on each side; a scorer at that moment does not.
