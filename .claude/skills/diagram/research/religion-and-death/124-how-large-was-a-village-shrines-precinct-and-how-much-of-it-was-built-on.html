How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?

Sources: ranzan-shrine-register (fourteen village shrines of one Saitama district, from the Meiji register), sano-shrine-gazetteer (fifty village shrines of one Tochigi district, from the 1902 and 1964 prefectural records), kanzaki-hamlet-shrines (nine unranked hamlet shrines of one Saga district, from the 1915 register), rokusha-jinja-fuchu (a precinct and its buildings in one entry), chinju-no-mori-jawiki (the wood as the shrine's setting), cunmiao-baike and gantang-temple-thepaper (China)

What the research found - the area. Japan's shrine registers give each precinct's area in tsubo, and three districts' registers can be read, transcribed shrine by shrine. In Hiki district, Saitama, the fourteen village-rank shrines, the sonsha, run from 55 to 708 tsubo, with a median of 403 tsubo, about 14,300 sq ft, the area of a square some 120 ft on a side. In Sano, Tochigi, fifty village shrines run from 142 to 2,700 tsubo, with a median of 655, about 23,300 sq ft. One step down, the unranked hamlet shrines, the mukakusha, of Kanzaki district, Saga, run from 50 to 662 tsubo with a median of 148, about 5,300 sq ft. A village shrine's ground, then, ran typically from about 150 to 650 tsubo, some 5,000 to 23,000 sq ft, and a hamlet's smaller one lay at the bottom of that band. China's village temples run smaller where they are a single hall - around 100 m² in southern Fujian and over 400 m² in the north-east of the province - and one Zhejiang village temple's compound stood on about 2.5 mu, some 500 tsubo.

How much of it was built on. Very little. Rokusha Shrine in Hiroshima, a village shrine from 1872, lists a worship hall of 9 tsubo and an office of 30 inside a precinct of 285 tsubo: 13.7 per cent built. The Tochigi register's Wakukama Shrine has a worship hall 5 ken by 2 ken and a sanctuary under 1 tsubo on 457 tsubo, about 2 per cent. The buildings of a village shrine covered somewhere between a fiftieth and a seventh of its ground. What covered the rest, no source measures. The registers single out old, dense trees - one unranked shrine's 1,848 tsubo are praised as unmatched in the region for "their luxuriant, dense trees" - and a shrine's setting is by definition the wood kept around its sanctuary, its approach and its place of worship. That the unbuilt ground was mostly that wood is a guess from those two, not a measurement.

The decision. The precinct is drawn as the grove (the GM's ruling of 2026-09-27, choosing to add a grove around the shrine on the village map): the wood surrounds the hall and the sanctuary and runs out along the approach to the outermost arch, its area inside the register band (accurate as a band; the wood's share of it a guess). The swept ground is a small clearing at the hall and the sanctuary, not a yard. Why no fence bounds it is under "Was a village shrine walled or fenced?", and what else stands in it under "What else stood in a village shrine's precinct?".
