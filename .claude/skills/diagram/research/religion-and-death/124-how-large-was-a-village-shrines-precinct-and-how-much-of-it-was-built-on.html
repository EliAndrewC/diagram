How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?

Sources: ranzan-shrine-register (fourteen village shrines of one Saitama district), ranzan-hakusan-jinja (the smallest of the fourteen, its own page), sano-shrine-gazetteer (fifty village shrines of one Tochigi district), kanzaki-hamlet-shrines (hamlet shrines of one Saga district, with Edo site areas), niiza-ishigami-lantern (a village's larger precinct), hasegawa-2021-nagoya-precincts (a city's shrine precincts today), rokusha-jinja-fuchu (a precinct and its buildings in one entry), chinju-no-mori-jawiki (the wood), gantang-temple-thepaper (China)

What the research found - the area. The shrine registers of three districts give each precinct's area in tsubo and can be read, transcribed shrine by shrine. In Hiki district, Saitama, the fourteen village-rank shrines, the sonsha, run from 55 tsubo to 708 tsubo, with a median of 403 tsubo, about 14,300 sq ft, the area of a square some 120 ft on a side. In Sano, Tochigi, fifty village shrines run from 142 to over 2,700 tsubo, with a median of 655, about 23,300 sq ft. One step down, the unranked hamlet shrines, the mukakusha, and a few "miscellaneous" ones of Kanzaki district, Saga, from the registers of about 1880 and 1915, run from 50 to 662 tsubo with a median of 148, about 5,300 sq ft. A village shrine's ground, then, ran typically from about 150 to 650 tsubo, some 5,000 to 23,000 sq ft, and a hamlet's smaller one lay at the bottom of that band. Some ran well above it: the tutelary shrine of Ishigami village, in Saitama, had over 2,000 tsubo with its grove at the end of Meiji. Today's city shrines spread the same way: of 92 Nagoya shrines that answered a survey, 21 have under 1,000 m², about 300 tsubo, and 37 more under 2,500 m², about 760 tsubo, and the other 34 larger, 8 of them over 10,000 m², about 3,000 tsubo. Fujian's village temples are put at around 100 m² in the south of the province and over 400 m² in the north-east, figures that no readable page confirms; one Zhejiang village temple's compound stood on about 2.5 mu, some 500 tsubo.

Why the registers may not give an Edo precinct. Every register figure above is Meiji or later, taken after the government took shrine lands, and a register can err either way - the evidence, and the one Edo set of site areas, a Kyushu plain's hamlet shrines at the bottom of the band, are under "Do the shrine registers give an Edo precinct's size?".

How much of it was built on. Very little. Rokusha Shrine in Hiroshima, a village shrine from 1872, lists a worship hall of 9 tsubo, an office of 30 and a sanctuary of about 3.4 inside a precinct of 285 tsubo: about 15 per cent built. The Tochigi register's Wakukama Shrine has a worship hall 5 ken by 2 ken and a sanctuary under 1 tsubo, with an offering hall of 1 ken by 1 and a half, on 457 tsubo, under 3 per cent. The buildings of a village shrine covered somewhere between a fortieth and about a seventh of its ground. What covered the rest, no source measures. The registers single out old, dense trees - one unranked shrine's 1,848 tsubo are praised as unmatched in the region for their "luxuriant, dense trees" - and a shrine's wood, the chinju no mori, is by definition kept around its sanctuary, its approach and its place of worship. That the unbuilt ground was mostly that wood is a guess from those two, not a measurement.

The decision. The precinct is drawn as the grove (the GM's ruling of 2026-09-27, choosing to add a grove around the shrine on the village map): the wood surrounds the hall and the sanctuary and runs out along the approach to the outermost arch, its area inside the register band. The band is the registers as found, which may understate or overstate an Edo precinct. The Hoshigaoka precinct, about 860 tsubo, drawn as the map's grove from its well to its outermost arch, sits above Saitama's largest and above Tochigi's median of 655 (accurate as a band; its place in the band a guess; the wood's share of it a guess). The swept ground is a small clearing at the hall and the sanctuary, not a yard. Why no fence bounds it is under "Was a village shrine walled or fenced?", and what else stands in it under "What else stood in a village shrine's precinct?".
