A cremation ground's cleared core is drawn at 30 to 80 ft at village and town scale and about 80 to 160 ft at city scale, and the recorded sites fall across that band: a village cremation ground of 10 tsubo 5 go (34.65 m²), a second site of 42 tsubo, and a third at 600 tsubo (1,980 m²), about 145 ft square. The one of them dated in the record was built in Taisho 11 (1922), a modern facility rather than an Edo one. The one real extent is Edo's own: the crematory at Yoyogi is recorded in the Musashi gazetteer at 900 tsubo, some 180 ft square, with three houses of the men who worked it. The pyre itself is not a built platform at all: in open country it was firewood stacked on the ground, the permanent form was a shallow trench about one size larger than a coffin framed in stone and earthenware, and two roofed forms are described: a fire bed on leveled ground with posts at its four corners carrying a simple roof, and, in snow country, a hut of about four to six tatami with a full tiled roof on stacked cut-stone walls and a channeled fire bed dug at its center.

The rule the map follows: a cremation ground's cleared core is 30 to 80 ft at village and town scale and 80 to 160 ft at city scale, with a fire bed about a coffin's length and a little wider, roofed where it is permanent - four posts and a simple roof over the bed, or, in snow country, a walled hut of about four to six tatami, roughly 8 to 10 ft square.
