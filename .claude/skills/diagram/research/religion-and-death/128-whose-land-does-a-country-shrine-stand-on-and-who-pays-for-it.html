Whose land does a country shrine stand on, and who pays for it?

Sources: jochi-jawiki, shuinchi-jawiki, danka-seido-jawiki, lijia-zhwiki and mianning-village-temples (the land, the income and the records)

The plot the monk lives on is exempt land, jochi - "the precincts of temples and shrines and fields and homesteads holding a document of tax exemption" - and the sealed grants, the shuinchi, ran down to "a mere 1 koku"; the income is the parish's, each household paying "fuse" to the temple it "entrusts all of its funerary and memorial rites to". In China the temple's land came "partly bought and partly donated, to serve as a permanent resource for the deity's incense and lamps", while the population was the lijia's business, "allocating corvée labor and collecting tax by head-count", and nothing read puts a village temple in the registration business; whether Ming or Qing temple land was exempt could not be read. None of this is drawn; the precinct's area is under "How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?".
