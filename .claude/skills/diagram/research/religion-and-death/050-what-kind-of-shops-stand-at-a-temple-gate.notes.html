• monzenmachi-jawiki - 「They are formed when tradesmen and craftsmen doing business with the shrine or temple's own people and with worshippers gather in front of a large shrine or temple that draws many pilgrims.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「In response to the lodging demand of worshippers and of pilgrimage confraternities, many inns were sited before the gate; thereafter, as the railways opened and day-trip worship advanced, the composition of trades shifted from inns to eating houses and souvenir shops.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) The shift is at the pilgrimage towns the article describes; the Edo gate towns quoted two notes on had teahouses and souvenir shops from the seventeenth century.
  • kotobank-monzenmachi - 「An economic settlement formed when the festival market became permanent and eating-and-drinking establishments and the like were added to it.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) The page prints several signed reference works side by side; this one names the eating houses at the gate.
  • matsui-2014-edo-religious-space - 「Around the first half of the 17th century, when the new gate towns arose, rest teahouses, souvenir shops and the like were set up, and they had the look of gate towns.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) The paper draws the passage from Nishiyama's 1983 study of Edo's gate towns.
  • nakamise-jawiki - 「Its origin was a privilege to trade on the approach and nearby, granted in the early Edo period to local residents who had been assigned the duty of cleaning Sensoji's precinct and approach.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「In the Edo period the shops from the Denboin toward the Niomon were called yakudana, and 20 water teahouses called the "Twenty-House Teahouses" stood in a row. The shops toward the Kaminarimon were called hiramise, and sold toys, sweets, souvenirs and the like.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • miyagegashi-jawiki - 「Once the Edo period came in, traffic on the highways increased, and the sweets and such that were sold at the teahouses along the way developed in their own directions out of what various travelers said of them. And that travelers spread by word of mouth the existence of the sweets sold at the places they traveled to was the greatest occasion for sweets developing in the provinces.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-miyage - 「At Kumano and elsewhere, in the early modern period the shugenja became half-monk and half-layman and came to sell souvenir goods; later, as demand expanded, specialist souvenir shops also came into being, and formed a gate town.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); and, of the goods, 「For these, talismans and amulets above all were used, and lucky dolls and toys and picture books and sacred medicines.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) The entry is signed, and it dates the spread of the custom to the middle of the early modern period, when the highways were made up and commoner pilgrimage spread through the pilgrimage guides and oshi.
  • soba-noodle-jawiki - 「The general term for soba served at shops operating around Zenkoji's main approach. There is no particular common feature, but it seems to have become tacitly recognized and established from the density of soba shops lining the street and from worshippers seeking a resting place and a meibutsu.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) The passage describes the present street and gives no date for the word's use there.
  • qingming-enwiki - 「Traditionally, a family will burn spirit money (joss paper) and paper replicas of material goods such as cars, homes, phones, and paper servants.」 (the source's own English; the modern goods in the list are the page's, the practice is the old one)

  • lin-2001-miaohui - 「Temple fairs: the temples of every village all hold an annual gathering. When the day comes, traders assemble together and sightseers are as thick as a weave; in a year of good harvest, plays are staged to swell the occasion. The temple fair is the countryside's one great trading ground and pleasure ground. The sort that are physicians, diviners, astrologers and physiognomists, and storytellers, conjurors, martial performers, peep-show men, big-tent men and other variety acts, likewise put up sheds and offer their arts.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) The paper quotes a Qing county gazetteer; the four-character phrase this project renders as physicians, diviners, astrologers and physiognomists is the standard Chinese name for the fortune-telling trades, and they stand at a village temple's annual fair rather than in a permanent shopfront at a great temple's gate.

  • eiheiji-jawiki - 「It is said that Dogen invited carpenters from Song China, whose master builder would come to call himself Kuro Genzaemon, and had them build Eiheiji. They made a carpenters' village near the gate and lived there, were called the Eiheiji carpenters, and for generations have handled Eiheiji's enlargements and repairs.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) The page gives it as a tradition.
