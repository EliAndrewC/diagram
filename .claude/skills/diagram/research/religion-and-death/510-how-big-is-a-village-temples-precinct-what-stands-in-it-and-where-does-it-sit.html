The hall. The main halls of the five village temples of one Fukuoka farming town ran from 4 to 6 ken across and 4 to 6 deep - about 24 to 36 ft a side - for parishes of 24 to 216 households. The town's gazetteer also gives each temple's land, but in two cases that figure is smaller than the temple's own main hall, so it is not the precinct and it is not used here. What stands in it. The small temple's whole plan was, most often, a main hall and a bell tower. The smallest temple measured, a Kōshin hall in the same Fukuoka town that had once belonged to the shugen order, had a hall 4.5 ken wide and 3 deep (about 27 by 18 ft), a kuri 2 ken wide and 2.5 deep (about 12 by 15 ft), and a site of 185 tsubo, about 6,600 sq ft. Beside the hall a village temple might keep lesser halls - one had a Yakushi hall and a Kannon hall in its grounds - and its graveyard: the great flood of 1891 scoured out the graveyard inside the precinct of another temple of the same town.
