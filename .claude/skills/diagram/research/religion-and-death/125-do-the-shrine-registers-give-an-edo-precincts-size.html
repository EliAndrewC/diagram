Do the shrine registers give an Edo precinct's size? Not reliably - they can err either way

Sources: agechi-rei-jawiki (the Meiji land orders), kanzaki-hamlet-shrines (a precinct cut in 1877, and the domain's Edo site areas), ranzan-shrine-register and sano-shrine-gazetteer (precincts later enlarged)

Every register figure under "How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?" is Meiji or later, and each was taken after the government took shrine lands: the domains that temples and shrines had held in the Edo period were confiscated by the orders of 1871 and 1875, and on the Kanzaki page the land-tax reform of 1876 split a shrine's ground into precinct and outside land and put the outside land into state hands, a return the prefect of Nagasaki signed in 1877 marking to be kept 1 tan 13 bu of one shrine's former grounds of 7 tan 5 se 27 bu as its present precinct. So a register can understate an Edo precinct. It can also overstate one: some precincts were later enlarged by land the state had taken - a Hiki shrine was permitted in 1903 to add 1 tan 5 se 1 bu of surrendered forest to its precinct - and by mergers, Sano's Wakukama Shrine growing fivefold in 1911 when another shrine's ground was joined to it. The one Edo set on any page read is Kanzaki's: for several of the same hamlet shrines the page quotes the Saga domain's temple-and-shrine returns of 1663 to 1859, which give each site in se and bu - the entries quoted run from 1 se 15 bu to 1 tan 8 se, about 45 to 540 tsubo, four of the six under 1 tan (300 tsubo), a se being 30 tsubo. That set is the hamlet shrines of one district of Saga, in Kyushu, the small end of the range of precinct sizes the maps draw on. The maps therefore use that range as the registers give it, disclosed as able to err either way.
