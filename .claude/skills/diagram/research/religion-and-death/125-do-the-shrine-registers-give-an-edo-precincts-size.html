Do the shrine registers give an Edo precinct's size? Not reliably - they can err either way

Sources: agechi-rei-jawiki (the Meiji land orders), kanzaki-hamlet-shrines (a precinct cut in 1877, and the domain's Edo site areas), ranzan-shrine-register and sano-shrine-gazetteer (precincts later enlarged)

Every register figure under "How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?" is Meiji or later, and each was taken after the government took shrine lands: the domains that temples and shrines had held in the Edo period were confiscated by the orders of 1871 and 1875, and on the Kanzaki page the land-tax reform of 1876 split a shrine's ground into precinct and outside land and put the outside land into state hands, a return the prefect of Nagasaki signed in 1877 keeping 1 tan 13 bu of one shrine's former grounds of 7 tan 5 se 27 bu as its present precinct. So a register can understate an Edo precinct. It can also overstate one: some precincts were later enlarged by returned land - a Hiki shrine took back 1 tan 5 se 1 bu of forest in 1903 - and by mergers, Sano's Wakukama Shrine growing fivefold in 1911 when another shrine's ground was joined to it. The one Edo set on any page read is Kanzaki's: for several of the same hamlet shrines the page quotes the Saga domain's temple-and-shrine returns of 1663 to 1859, which give each site in se and bu - mostly 2 to 8 se, about 60 to 240 tsubo, one as small as 1 se 15 bu and a few 1 tan (300 tsubo) or more, a se being 30 tsubo. That set is a Kyushu plain's hamlet shrines, the bottom of the band. The band is therefore used as the registers give it, disclosed as able to err either way.
