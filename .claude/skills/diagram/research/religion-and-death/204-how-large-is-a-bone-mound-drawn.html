How large is a bone mound drawn?

Sources: kyototuu-mimizuka (the ear mound's width across and its height), cremation-enwiki (how little cremation leaves to bury), kozukappara-jawiki (the Edo execution ground's dead and its extent)

Why a funerary feature is drawn to its real size is at How large are the gates, walls and funerary features drawn?; the ground a single grave takes is at How much ground does a grave take?

A pauper bone mound is drawn at 10 to 30 ft across with nothing behind the band, while the base of Kyoto's monumental ear mound is measured: 30 ken across (about 55 m at 1.82 m to the ken; the page's own conversion says 49 m), standing 4 ken (about 7.2 m) high under a 5-ken stone pagoda, on a regional encyclopedia site that names no source for the figures. Cremation leaves little to bury - an adult's remains weigh about 2.4 kg on average, roughly 3.5% of the body's mass, and cremation makes very economical use of cemetery space - which is why the great Edo execution ground - more than two hundred thousand put to death between its founding and its abolition, on a plot about 108 m across the front and a little over 54 m deep - is not remembered for a mound; no page read describes one raised there.

The rule the map follows: a pauper bone mound is drawn at about 22 ft, with a small floor under it so that it never vanishes , and anything outside 8 to 32 ft is wrong.
