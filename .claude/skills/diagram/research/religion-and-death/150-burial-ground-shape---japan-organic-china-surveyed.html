Burial-ground shape - Japan organic, China surveyed

Sources: louzeyuan-zhwiki (1104,30 high barren ground, fenced, numbered plots, continued through Ming), toribeno-jawiki (no defined boundary),31 adashino-jawiki (an exposure-burial (風葬) ground, later cremation; Rendaino (蓮台野) has no article;32 the Osaka seven graveyards (大坂七墓) page is gone), osaka-nanabaka-jawiki, fengshui-enwiki

What the research found - the two reference cultures split. Japan (the organic pole): commoner burial grounds were unplotted and terrain-following everywhere - Kyoto's great burial fields (Toribeno, Adashino, Rendaino) were open hillside/riverbank grounds shaped purely by terrain; a village kept an unsurveyed sanmai on waste land at its edge; Osaka's Edo-period dead were gathered out of graveyards scattered through the city into seven grounds on its outer rim, one of which yielded more than 1,500 skeletons and 350 ossuaries to excavation.117 Premodern Japan offers essentially NO surveyed rectangular commoner cemetery.118 China (the split case): ordinary family graves were scattered organically over unfarmable hillsides by feng shui119 - no communal commoner cemetery at all - BUT the institutional "common burial ground" for the poor and unclaimed dead genuinely WAS a surveyed rectangle: the Song state pauper cemeteries (louzeyuan 漏泽园, proposed by Cai Jing in 1104 - 建議, a proposal, not an imperial edict37 - and continued as Ming yizhong 义冢 charity graveyards)26 were fenced compounds (圍欄, a fence, not a wall) on high barren ground23 with plots in ordered rows,105 numbered against the Thousand Character Classic,24 a drainage ditch every three rows,106 and a resident caretaker monk.25
