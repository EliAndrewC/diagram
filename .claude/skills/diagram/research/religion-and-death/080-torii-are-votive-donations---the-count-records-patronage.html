Torii are VOTIVE DONATIONS - the count records patronage

Sources: fushimi-inari-senbon (~10,000 on the mountain,36 ~800 along the main path, donated from Edo28 into Meiji (unsourced)). The 1 / 2-3 / dozens distribution is assembled from normative statements - no census exists, fushimi-inari-jawiki, torii-enwiki, hokora-jawiki

What the research found: torii were VOTIVE DONATIONS, not shrine construction - a shrine's count records its accumulated patronage21 (ujiko subscriptions, grateful merchants, the local lord), a custom that began in the Edo period.123 The historical distribution: 1 torii is the overwhelming mode (~90%+ of true village shrines); 0 is the norm only BELOW the shrine tier (hokora/wayside god-shelves, which vastly outnumbered real shrines - a proper shrine with none was rare enough that each had a story); 2-3 is the ranked-shrine tier (the numbered ichi/ni/san-no-torii on long approaches, 3 the conventional ceiling); and the dozens-plus tail is specifically the Inari donation-row cult (Edo-onward, a handful to a few dozen at a modest rural Inari, hundreds only at famous cult sites, ~10,000 at Fushimi).124 No scholarly per-shrine census exists - the shape is assembled from normative statements plus attested examples.

An alternative, declined. A torii avenue could be sized to the space in front of it, on the reasoning that a temple with a long approach would fill that approach. It is not: the count is PATRONAGE rather than street room, and sizing by space bans a count of three, which is exactly what a town rolls most of the time. What the approach space does govern is the avenue's LINE, which the author of a map draws to fit the ground it crosses.
