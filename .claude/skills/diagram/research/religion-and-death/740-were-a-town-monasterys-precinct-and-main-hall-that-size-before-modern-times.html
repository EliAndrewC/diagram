Were a town monastery's precinct and main hall that size before modern times? Roughly, by an Edo village's own record

Sources: ranzan-senjudo-1843 (an Edo village's record of its two temples and their exempt land), ranzan-senjuin and ranzan-koshoji-senjudo (the same two temples in the later register)

What the research found. The bands under "How big is a town monastery, what stands in its precinct, and where in the town does it stand?" are measured from a district temple register made after 1868. For two of its temples an Edo record survives: the account of itself that Senjudō village drafted in 1843 names its two temples, the Zen temple Senjuin and the Nichiren temple Kōshōji, and the land each held exempt from tax for a hall in its care: 2 tan 4 se 22 bu, partly paddy and mostly upland, for the Senju Kannon hall in Senjuin's care, and 2 tan 3 se 4 bu of upland for Kōshōji's hall of the thirty guardian deities. That is about 742 and 694 tsubo, some 26,400 and 24,700 sq ft. The register gives the same two temples precincts of 813 and 453 tsubo, and says of both that exempt land was surrendered after the Restoration. An exempt holding is not a precinct: it counts fields, and Senjuin's later precinct is larger than its holding, so it bounds an Edo precinct only roughly. But the Edo holdings and the later precincts are of one order, and the register's median of 462 tsubo, about 16,400 sq ft, lies below both holdings and within the two temples' later range. No precinct of an Edo temple measured as a precinct was read.

The halls. The one main hall of known Edo date that was read is the Aichi Zen temple's of 1697, about 54 by 47 ft, under "How big is a town monastery...", inside the band of 24 to 63 ft. The register's halls are those standing after 1868, and not all are Edo buildings: Senjuin's halls burned in 1876 and were rebuilt, so its hall of 7 ken, the register's median width, is a Meiji one, while Kōshōji's hall of 4 ken, the band's narrowest, is most likely the one rebuilt after a fire of the 1740s (this page's inference from the register's history). The 1843 record gives the Kannon hall in Senjuin's care, which it lists apart from the temple, as 3 ken square, about 18 ft.

The decision (the GM's rule of 2026-09-28, that anything attested only in modern times is eliminated). A temple precinct holding a main hall, a kuri, a bell tower and a gate is attested before modern times, so the maps keep the town monastery. Its sizes are mixed. The precinct's default of about 16,400 sq ft stands, calibrated to the Edo holdings of two of the register's temples, about 700 tsubo, some 25,000 sq ft, which it lies below as a precinct should lie below a holding that counts fields; and the main hall's default of about 42 ft stands inside the band that holds the hall of 1697 (a guess in degree, both). The bands' ends, 285 and 1,944 tsubo and 4 and 10 and a half ken, rest only on the register made after 1868, and have no Edo figure behind them (a guess in degree).
