Does a temple's gate have shops selling its amulets and incense?

Sources: the GM's correction; omamori-enwiki (the amulet as a donation to the shrine or temple that issues it); xiangyouqian-zhwiki (incense and lamp oil given to a Chinese temple as a donation, and then money in their place); Chinese temple accounts, traced to no readable page

Amulets and charms pass between the temple and the believer directly, with nobody in between; that they, with the incense and lamp oil offered to it (next sentence), are a major part of what keeps it is a GUESS, on no page read. On the Chinese side that flow is a DONATION rather than a sale: believers gave incense sticks, candles, lamp oil and gold paper to temples and monasteries for use in the offerings, and later gave money in their place, which is why the money itself is called incense-oil money. What no page read carries is a temple's own accounts booking an annual oil-lamp and incense income. The Japanese side is the same relation: an omamori is issued by a shrine or a temple and is viewed mainly as a donation to the place that issues it, so what the visitor pays is taken mainly as a donation to the institution rather than as the price of a line of stock. Manufacture away from the precinct, and charms issued by no shrine at all, belong to the modern trade and to no part of this setting.
