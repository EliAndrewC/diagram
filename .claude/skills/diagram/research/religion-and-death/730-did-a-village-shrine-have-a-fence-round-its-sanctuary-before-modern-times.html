Did a village shrine have a fence round its sanctuary before modern times? Only a lord's or a great shrine is known to

Sources: kotobank-tamagaki (the shrine fence in writings of 712 and 1283), iwashimizu-mizugaki and iwakiyama-mizugaki (fences round the sanctuaries of a shrine the shogunate rebuilt and a shrine a lord built, 1634 and 1686-1694)

What the research found. A fence round a sanctuary is old at the great shrines. The historical dictionary quotes the word from the Kojiki of 712, and a shrine's "tamagaki, mizugaki, aragaki and so on, layer upon layer" from a collection of Buddhist tales of 1283. The shogunate's rebuilding of Iwashimizu Hachimangu in 1634 enclosed its sanctuary with a fence, and at Iwakiyama Shrine the lord of the Hirosaki domain built, between 1686 and 1694, a fence that runs from the inner gate "once round the main sanctuary". At an ordinary shrine, the dated fences round a sanctuary are of 1918 and 1928, under "Was a village shrine walled or fenced?", and no fence round a village shrine's sanctuary dated before 1868 was found.

The decision (the GM's rule of 2026-09-28, that anything attested only in modern times is eliminated). A fence round the sanctuary alone is attested before modern times only where a lord or the shogunate built the shrine; at a village shrine it is attested only in modern times, so the maps do not draw one there, however rich the parish (accurate). What else a richer parish gave its shrine is under "Inside a village shrine's precinct: halls, basin, sacred tree and offerings (keidai)".
