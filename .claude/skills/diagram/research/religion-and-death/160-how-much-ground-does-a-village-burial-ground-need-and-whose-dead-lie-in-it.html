How much ground does a village burial ground need, and whose dead lie in it?

Sources: sumida-miyakodori-33-2 (ten graves in a 40 m² excavation at a former temple graveyard in Sumida), tanigawa-1992 (the sizes of graves excavated at an Edo temple graveyard)

Whose ground it is. Why the maps draw a village one communal burial ground at all is above. This question is how large that ground is, and which households it serves.

What a hamlet's own ground would be. The setting cremates and then inters, so what a hamlet carries to a distant ground is an urn rather than a body. A hamlet's own patch would be small - roughly 750 to 2,450 sq ft for full-body burial, less for urns (a GUESS: this project's own rough reckoning of a hamlet's deaths over a 30-year reuse at the grave sizes below, which no page read gives), a ground thirty to fifty ft across, which is drawable at the hamlet tier's scale. A hamlet's dead lie either in a burial ground of its own at its edge or in the village's ground, rolled per hamlet; the evidence for each is at Where do a hamlet's dead lie?

The decision. The drawn village ground is the marked, ritually active ground of the village, and it is sized to the population it serves: the village's own households and those of the hamlets whose dead lie in it, so between the village's own ~350 inhabitants and the whole district's ~800. At roughly 25 to 30 deaths per 1,000 a year (a rate this project chose below the levels of up to about 40 that the demographic transition's first two stages typically reach; no page read gives a premodern East Asian rate), with a plot reused after about 30 years (the reuse period is this project's choice too; no page read gives one), a population of P keeps 0.75 to 0.9 P active urn plots; at 10 to 20 sq ft each that is P × 7.5 to 18 sq ft - some 260 to 320 plots and about 0.06 to 0.15 acre for the village's own ~350, some 600 to 720 plots and about 0.14 to 0.33 acre for the district's ~800. The plot figure is of the order the graves excavated at an Edo temple graveyard reach: jar burials in wooden burial chambers run 85 cm to 1.4 m along the major axis, and the simple jar burials and wooden coffins, the kind that match the graves of farmers found in villages, 65 cm to 2.3 m, most of them under 1.4 m. One excavation does give a density: a 40 m² excavation at a former temple graveyard in Sumida's Higashi-Komagata held the traces of ten graves, one box coffin and nine barrel coffins - about 4 m², some 43 sq ft, a grave. That is the trench rather than the whole ground, and those are body burials in coffins rather than the packed urn plots drawn here, so it sits above the 10 to 20 sq ft band and corroborates instead the Chinese full-body figure of about 50 sq ft a grave in a Qing charity ground, given at the grave-size question below; the step from a grave's size to an urn plot's footing is still this project's own. A planner's reckoning of 1934 by households rather than by urns - one family plot of about 36 sq ft a household, at about five inhabitants a household, times 1.5 to 2 for the paths, or P × 10.8 to 14.4 sq ft - lands inside the same band at both ends (about 0.09 to 0.12 acre at 350, 0.2 to 0.26 at 800); it is at How much ground does a grave take, and how large is a burial ground drawn?, and how far from the houses the ground lies is at How far from its houses does a village bury its dead, and on which side?

The rule the map follows: a village draws ONE burial ground, sized to the population P it serves - its own inhabitants and those of the hamlets whose dead lie in it - at P × 7.5 to 18 sq ft, with glyph rounding allowed both ways: about 0.06 to 0.15 acre when it serves the village's own ~350, about 0.14 to 0.33 acre when it serves the whole district's ~800. A hamlet's dead lie either in a burial ground of its own at its edge, about 750 to 2,450 sq ft (a guess), or in the village's ground, rolled per hamlet (Where do a hamlet's dead lie? ). The ladder rises steadily with the population served - the district's ~800 sits below a town's ~1,200, and the village ground's band nests under a town's 0.25 to 0.75 acre.
