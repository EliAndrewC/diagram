How big is a small shrine in a town, and what stands on it?

Sources: kanazawa-teramachidai-plan (the two shrines of a temple quarter), kanzaki-shrines-kushida (small shrines' ground and halls)

What the research found. Of the two shrines in Kanazawa's temple quarter ("How big is a temple in a temple quarter?"), one stands on a lot with a single joined worship hall and sanctuary, a shrine office and a small Inari shrine; the other stood inside a temple and, the year after a fire in 1871, took the west side of that temple's precinct for its ground. In the town of Kanzaki a ward's shrine once held about 100 tsubo (some 3,600 sq ft) and has lost all but 36 m²; the district's small shrines stand on some 250 to 500 m² (2,600 to 5,600 sq ft), a register of 1459 giving one 2 se 11 bu (some 71 tsubo), and their halls are small, a sanctuary 1 ken square before a worship hall 3 by 2 ken (some 18 by 12 ft), or a single hall of 1 tsubo.

What was not found. No page read gives the ground of a castle town ward's own shrine in the Edo period, or the size of a small shrine in a Chinese city's lanes.

The decision it drove. The shrine's buildings are accurate; its plot is a guess from one district's register, most of whose shrines are a hamlet's rather than a town's.

The rule the map follows: a small shrine in a town or a temple quarter stands on about 3,500 sq ft: a sanctuary some 6 ft square behind a worship hall some 18 by 12 ft, with at most a shrine office and a lesser shrine beside it. Or it stands on a slice of a temple's precinct.
