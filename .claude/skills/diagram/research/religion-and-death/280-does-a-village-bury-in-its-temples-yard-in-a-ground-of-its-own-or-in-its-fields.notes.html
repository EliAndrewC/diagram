• takeuchi-2017-bochi-hosei - 「In Japan, even at the end of the Edo period and in early Meiji, graveyards of various forms - individual, lineage, settlement and temple graveyards and others - existed across the country, but the idea of 'private ownership' in the modern legal sense was extremely weak in them, and like the customary rules of funerals, they were left to the customary rules of each locality.」 (translated; )
  • kaf2-kinsei-bo - 「In the Edo period, supported by the temple-certification system, many temples were built, temple graves attached to temples spread, and the dead came more often to be buried at temples.」 (translated; ); 「a grave area that was reburied when its temple moved was investigated」 (translated; ); 「The grave area was limited in size and burials were made in the same place, so many overlapping cases were found.」 (translated; ) (of an excavated grave area at a site in Sagamihara, reburied when its temple moved)
  • bochi-jawiki - 「a graveyard held in common and managed by the inhabitants of a village settlement」 (translated; ) (the page's definition of 村落墓地, the village-settlement graveyard)
  • ryobosei-jawiki - 「The two-grave custom is one of Japan's grave customs, one that separates the ground where the body is buried from the ground for visiting the grave.」 (translated; ); 「It is often a communal graveyard belonging to each settlement, and its burial ground is in some cases divided into plots by household, by age and the like, while in others there is no rule at all and burial is made in whatever ground is free, or an old grave is dug up and a further burial added, among other ways.」 (translated; ) (of the burial ground in the two-grave custom)
  • meiji-1884-bochi-saimoku - 「Art. 3: Anyone registered in the town or village, or dying there, may be buried in its graveyard, whatever their lineage or sect; this does not apply where there has hitherto been a special custom.」 (translated; ); 「This regulation is the first systematic graveyard law in our country.」 (translated; ); 「Graveyard and Burial Control Regulations, standard detailed rules for enforcement (excerpt), Meiji 17 [1884], November 18, Home Ministry instruction Otsu No. 40」 (translated; ) (the page reads the exception as still allowing graveyards kept for one sect's parishioners, such as a temple's)
