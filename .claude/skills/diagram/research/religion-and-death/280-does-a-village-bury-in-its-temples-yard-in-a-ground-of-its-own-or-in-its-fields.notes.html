• takeuchi-2017-bochi-hosei - 「In Japan, even at the end of the Edo period and in early Meiji, graveyards of various forms - individual, lineage, settlement and temple graveyards - existed across the country, but the idea of 'private ownership' in the modern legal sense was extremely weak in them, and like the customary rules of funerals, they were left to the customary rules of each locality.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「わが国では、幕末から明治初期の時点においても個人墓地、同族墓地、集落墓地、寺院墓地などの様々な形態の墓地が各地に存在していたが、これらの墓地には、近代法的な意味での「私有」という考え方が極めて希薄で、葬祭の習俗的規制と同様、その地方の慣習的規制に委ねられていた。」)
  • kaf2-kinsei-bo - 「In the Edo period, supported by the temple-certification system, many temples were built, temple graves attached to temples spread, and the dead came more often to be buried at temples.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代になると寺請制度に支えられて多数の寺院が建立され、寺院に付属する寺院墓が普及し、遺体はお寺に埋葬されることが多くなりました。」); 「a grave area that was reburied when its temple moved was investigated」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「寺院の移転に伴って改葬された墓域の調査が行われ」); 「The grave area was limited in size and burials were made in the same place, so many overlapping cases were found.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「墓域の広さには限りがあり同じ場所へ埋葬したため、重複する例が多く認められました。」) (of an excavated grave area at a site in Sagamihara, reburied when its temple moved)
  • bochi-jawiki - 「a graveyard held in common and managed by the inhabitants of a village settlement」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「村落の住民が共有して管理する墓地」) (the page's definition of 村落墓地, the village-settlement graveyard)
  • ryobosei-jawiki - 「It is most often a communal graveyard belonging to each settlement, and its burial ground is in some cases divided into plots by household or by age, while in others there is no rule at all and burial is made in whatever ground is free, or an old grave is dug up and a further burial added.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「集落ごとの共同墓地であることが多く、その埋葬地は家や年齢などによって区画分けされる場合もあれば、まったく決まりが無く空いた土地に埋めたり、古い墓地を掘り起こして追葬する場合など様々である。」) (of the burial ground in the two-grave custom)
  • meiji-1884-bochi-saimoku - 「Art. 3: Anyone registered in the town or village, or dying there, may be buried in its graveyard, whatever their lineage or sect; this does not apply where there has been a special custom.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「第3条 墓地ハ種族宗旨ヲ別タス其町村ニ本籍ヲ有シ若クハ其町村ニ於テ死シタルモノハ何人ニテモ之ニ葬ルコトヲ得其従前別段ノ慣習アルモノハ此限ニアラス」); 「This regulation is the first systematic graveyard law in our country.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この規則は、わが国における最初の体系的な墓地法である。」); 「Graveyard and Burial Control Regulations, standard detailed rules for enforcement (excerpt), Meiji 17 [1884], November 18, Home Ministry instruction Otsu No. 40」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「墓地及埋葬取締規則施行細目標準（抄） 明治17年11月18日内務省乙第40号達」) (the page reads the exception as still allowing graveyards kept for one sect's parishioners, such as a temple's)
  • buck-1930-farm-economy - 「in most regions having no hill land they are located in every conceivable position in a field without regard to resulting inconvenience of cultivation.」; 「6.
