Was a village shrine's precinct that size before modern times? Yes - Edo returns give shrine grounds across the whole band

Sources: kanzaki-hamlet-shrines (the Saga domain's shrine sites), nerima-kushi-meisaicho (two Musashi villages' returns, with their shrines' exempt lands), jochi-jawiki (what exempt land was)

The maps draw a village shrine's precinct at about 150 to 650 tsubo, and the Hoshigaoka shrine's at about 860, a band read from shrine registers of 1880 and later (under "How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?"). This question asks whether shrine grounds of that size are found before 1868.

What the research found. They are, in two kinds of Edo record. In Saga, in Kyushu, the domain's temple-and-shrine returns of 1663 to 1859 give the sites of several hamlet shrines of Kanzaki district, from 1 se 15 bu to 1 tan 8 se, about 45 to 540 tsubo. In Musashi, near Edo, a village's return listed each shrine with its exempt land, the jochi, the ground the lord took no tax on. Kami-Nerima's return of 1821 gives the village's tutelary shrine, a Hachiman shrine, 3 tan 8 se 15 bu, about 1,155 tsubo; the same return gives its lesser shrines grounds of "about 10 tsubo" or "about 20 tsubo", outside the survey, and one Inari shrine 1 chō 1 tan 1 se 18 bu, about 3,350. Doshida's return of 1804, and its return of 1831 again, give its shrines exempt lands from 7 se 6 bu to 6 tan 6 se 20 bu, about 216 to 2,000 tsubo, beside halls as small as 4 shaku square.

What an exempt-land figure measures. Exempt land was a shrine's or a temple's precinct, but also fields and house plots held under a document of exemption, and a shrine's could be fields: Doshida's return of 1814 lists 5 chō 6 tan 6 se 20 bu of exempt land as a "Myōjin exemption", land exempted for the shrine of a kami. So an Edo exempt-land figure is the shrine's precinct or more: a ceiling on it, not its measure. The Saga sites are the nearer measure of a precinct.

The decision (the GM's rule of 2026-09-28, that anything attested only in modern times is eliminated). The band of 150 to 650 tsubo lies inside the Edo figures: Saga's hamlet-shrine sites cover its lower part, and Musashi's village shrines' exempt lands its upper part and beyond. It is not a modern-only figure, and the maps keep it (accurate as a range). Hoshigaoka's 860 tsubo lies above every Edo site figure found and inside the exempt-land figures, which may include fields - within the range a tutelary shrine near Edo held, its place in it a guess. Why the Meiji registers can err either way is under "Do the shrine registers give an Edo precinct's size?".
