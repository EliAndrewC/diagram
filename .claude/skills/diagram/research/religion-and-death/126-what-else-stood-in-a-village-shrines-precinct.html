What the research found. Beyond the hall, the sanctuary, the arch and the wood (the wood is under "How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?"), a village shrine's ground held little, and most of what it did hold was given to it later, one piece at a time. The table sets out each feature, what was found, and what the country-shrine sheet does with it at average wealth.

The decision. At average wealth the sheet draws the hall, the sanctuary, the arches, the sacred tree and the basin in their clearing inside the grove, and nothing else: the guardian dogs and the lanterns are gifts that accrue with a parish's wealth; the strength stones, set beside the approach, go with them on the sheet's wealth knob, and the stage and the ring, real but not general, are knobs whose default is absent. The dedication is named at the sanctuary.
