The graveyard ceiling does not scale with temple count

Sources: ryobosei-jawiki and kotobank-kyodo-bochi (one communal ground to a settlement, outside its dwellings); osaka-nanabaka-jawiki (Osaka's scattered graveyards merged into seven on the city's rim); teramachi-jawiki (temples and graveyards gathered at a castle town's edge); reasoning from this project's own funerary doctrine and the Fox precincts' canon - a decision to REJECT a change

What determines it in reality: parish structure and burial LAND, not the number of foundations. A burial ground needs suitable ground outside the built area and serves a catchment: a settlement's graveyard is most often its communal one, formed apart from the dwellings. A city's count of grounds was a land decision. After the siege of Osaka the graveyards scattered through the city were merged, for military reasons, into a handful on its outer rim - four districts' into one, a plateau's several into another, one quarter's divided among three - and with two added later they became the city's seven graveyards. Temples followed the ground rather than the other way about: at the largest of the seven, laid out in 1621, two temples were founded in 1637 and 1648. Castle towns did the same with their temples and graveyards together, gathering them on the town's edge as a line of defense. In this setting a graveyard is a parish ground inside a temple's or a shrine's precinct, as Does a graveyard stand beside its temple or its shrine? sets out; what is shared is the ground's catchment, not a rule that each foundation has its own.

What was reasoned. The tempting change is to scale the 2-4 ceiling with the eight precincts. That is wrong twice over: the Fox precincts are economic institutions holding forest usufruct, not eight parishes, so they have no separate congregations of dead; and the existing city funerary doctrine already sites burial grounds by ground suitability (off the fields, downstream, across the water where the bank allows), which constrains LAND rather than counting temples. A whole city burying in seven grounds is the pattern; eight small precincts sharing three burial grounds is this page's own arrangement of it, and no page read gives a ratio of foundations to grounds.

The decision it drove. The ceiling stands unchanged. This is a case where the research led to REJECTING a change.
