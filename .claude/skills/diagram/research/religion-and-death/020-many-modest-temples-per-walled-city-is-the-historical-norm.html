Many modest temples per walled city is the historical norm

What determines it in reality: patronage and institutional history. Each temple is a separate FOUNDATION supported by its own patron households, so - on this page's reasoning - temple count tracks how many distinct patrons a place has accumulated - which is why it does not scale smoothly with population.

What the research found. Eight temples in Minami, the Fox seat of 2,600 inhabitants, need no disclosed liberty. Japanese castle towns concentrated their temples into designated temple districts (teramachi), often at the outer rim of the jokamachi, where the spacious precincts formed part of the city defenses: Kanazawa ~70, Sakai ~60 and ~50 in its two wards, Akita 43 in 1663, Takada ~25 relocated in one 1614 wave, and, the lowest count in the band, Takayama, a castle town whose size is on no page read, "over 10 temples and shrines". On the China side the standard county-seat kit carried the Confucian school-temple (wenmiao, decreed for every county school under the Tang in 630) and the City God temple (chenghuangmiao, at every seat of local government, its gods given noble ranks by the Ming founder at four administrative levels) as separate state foundations - that they came before any Buddhist or Daoist house was counted is this page's framing, and Pingyao's City God temple is itself a complex of THREE distinct temples on one site. Where those state foundations stood in a seat, how big they were, and what the setting has in their place is "Does a county seat or a city carry the state cult's altars and temples?".

The decision it drove. Eight modest precincts is inside the attested band, not a liberty. The existing "two major complexes" default is where the real liberty sits - see "City temple size", which records the deliberate 2-4x clergy over-density. We keep the two-complex default for non-Fox cities anyway, because it is a game-legibility choice (a few named, visitable temples beat a dozen anonymous ones), not a historical claim.

What a gazetteer shows. The Song gazetteer of Suzhou, completed in 1192, files the shrines of the cults under a heading of their own, the Daoist abbeys and then the Buddhist temples of the prefectural city under theirs, and the temples outside the wall under a third. It enters thirty Buddhist houses inside the city, two of them Tang foundations whose site was no longer known - a count this project made on the page, and a count for one of the empire's greatest cities rather than a county seat. No count of a county seat's temples was found. The band therefore rests on the Japanese teramachi figures, with China supplying the mandatory-foundations STRUCTURE and one great city's count rather than a county seat's number.
