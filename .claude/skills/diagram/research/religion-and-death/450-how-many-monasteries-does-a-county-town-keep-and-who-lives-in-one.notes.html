• ranzan-temple-register - 「From the Hiki district temple register, the Hiki district shrine register and the Hiki district hall and hermitage register made by the Saitama prefectural school affairs section in Shōwa 38 (1963), the main parts concerning the temples, shrines, halls and hermitages of the Ranzan town area are extracted and posted here.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the page then lists the nineteen temples, each with its village or its village's section, 大字: two in Furusato village (古里村), Oppata (越畑), Senjudō (千手堂), Kamagata (鎌形) and Ōkura (大蔵) each, one in each of nine more; the count of fourteen villages is this project's)
  • amagasaki-teramachi - 「On the castle-town map of the early Edo period (1635), twenty temples can be made out」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • miyazu-teramachi - 「In the southwest part of the former Miyazu castle town, as many as twelve temples are concentrated.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • hongwu-emperor-enwiki - 「The government abolished roughly 3,000 Buddhist and Taoist monasteries, and limited each county to one monastery with a maximum of two monks.」 (a legal cap; the page says nothing of how far it was enforced)
  • ranzan-kinsenji - 「Item: kuri, frontage 6 ken 3 shaku, depth 3 ken 3 shaku. Item: dormitory, frontage 4 ken, depth 3 ken 3 shaku」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); and the editors' glosses, 「kuri: the building in which the temple's head priest and his family live」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「dormitory: the lodging of monks in training」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Kinsenji, a Sōtō temple of Oppata village; the glosses are the web editors', not the register's)
  • ranzan-koshoji - 「Item: precinct, 1,944 tsubo, government land class 4. Item: front dormitory ... Item: back dormitory」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Kōshōji, a Sōtō temple of Hirono village, the largest precinct of the nineteen)
  • eijuji-shinshiro - 「Centered on the forecourt, the kuri and study are placed on the east side, and on the west side the dormitory, the meditation hall and the tutelary shrine hall, in order from the north」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); and 「at temples of such ranks as the jōgōe-chi, the kata-hōdōe-chi and the zuiie-chi, it was laid down that the training retreat be held in that period, and such temples had to have a full set of buildings - main hall, kuri, meditation hall, dormitory, gate and the like」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); and 「Eijuji's temple buildings, though they lack the east and west corridors on the south enclosing the forecourt, complete the traditional temple layout of a core temple among early-modern Sōtō temples」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Eijuji, Shinshiro, Aichi; the requirement is for the ranked temples that held the retreat, not for every temple)
