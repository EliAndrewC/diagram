How much ground does a grave take, and how large is a burial ground drawn?

Sources: tanigawa-1992 (excavated grave sizes), feng-yizhong-jiangnan (mu and coffin counts for Qing charity graveyards)

Why a funerary feature is drawn to its real size is at How large are the gates, walls and funerary features drawn?; how little cremation leaves to bury is at How large is a bone mound drawn?

Burial grounds size to the population served at about 10 to 20 sq ft per packed urn grave, with about one generation of active plots before reuse. The excavated graves the paper measures are of that order - jar burials in wooden chambers 85 cm to 1.4 m along the major axis, jar burials and wooden coffins of another category 65 cm to 2.3 m, most under 1.4 m - and the four-to-six-times multiple for Chinese full-body burial is measured: a Kangxi endowment of 4 mu of Jiangnan field took 573 coffins, about 4.7 m² - some 50 sq ft - a grave, inside the 40 to 120 sq ft the multiple implies, in charity graveyards that ranged from 3 fen to 10 mu. That one is a ground for the drowned, packed - by this project's comparison - more tightly than a family grave sited by fengshui. The acreage ladder those plots build to (a village district 0.15 to 0.30 acre, a town 0.25 to 0.75, a city 0.75 to 2 split across its yards) is this project's own arithmetic, on no source. This setting follows the Japanese cremate-then-inter mode.

The rule the map follows: burial grounds are sized to the population they serve, and the ladder stays monotone: a village district ground below a town's, a town's below a city's.
