• kotobank-haka - 「Formerly, many households each had a graveyard, or a branch house set its grave beside the main house and so formed a lineage grave. Because each household setting up graveyards here and there as it pleased, and all of them being designated tax-exempt land, ran against the common interest, from the Meiji period on graveyards were often consolidated. There were also yashiki-baka, graves set within a household's own house land.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「もとは家ごとに墓地をもち、あるいは分家が本家のそばに墓を設けて同族墓を形成しているものが多かった。各戸がかってにあちこちに墓地を設け、すべてを免租地に指定することは共同の利益に反するので、明治時代以後しばしば墓地の共同化が進められた。自家の宅地内に墓を設ける屋敷墓もあった。」) (Inokuchi Shoji's article in the Nipponica encyclopedia)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「One sometimes sees houses with a graveyard within the house plot (on the grounds of the home) or on land adjoining it. According to Yanagita Kunio, they are many in the Kanto, in Ou, and in the mountain villages of southern Kyushu, among other places」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷内（自宅の敷地内）またはその隣接の地に、墓地のある家を、ときどき見かける。 柳田國男によると、関東・奥羽、そして南九州の山村などに多く」) (Yanagita's finding as the blog reports it)
  • ikikata-yashiki-bochi - 「House-plot graveyards that survive today are not many across the country, and among regions they are few in the Kansai and are also said to be many in the Kanto.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「現存する屋敷墓地は全国的に多くはありませんが、中でも関西には少なく、関東に多いとも言われています。」)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「According to Mr. Takatori, in the Meiji period there was advice from the administrative authorities that graveyards be gathered into one communal graveyard to each oaza.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「高取氏によると、明治時代に、墓地は大字に１つの共同墓地にまとめるようにと、行政当局からの勧告があったという。」); 「Many oaza followed the administration and gathered them into a communal graveyard. But there were also oaza that did not.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「行政に従って共同墓地にまとめた大字も多かった。しかしそうならなかった大字もある。」) (Takatori Masao, as the blog reports him; an oaza is a district of a village)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「The second is the graveyard from before Genroku, which was at the southeast corner of the house plot.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「２つめは、元禄以前の墓地で、屋敷の東南の角にあった。」); 「The third is the graveyard at the northwest corner of the house plot; it appears on a survey map of the end of Meiji, but its land category is woodland, as in substance it is, and it was originally a burial place.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「３つめは、屋敷の北西の角の墓地で、明治末の測量図にも載るが、地目は山林であり、実質もそうであるが、元は墓所だった。」); 「At that time I think they raised no gravestones and buried in the woods behind the house plot's tutelary shrine. That place lies to the northwest of the main house's plot and is part of the house grove」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「当時は、石塔は建てず、屋敷の氏神の背後の山林に埋葬したものと思う。そこは本家の屋敷の西北に当たり、屋敷林の一部であるので」) (the writer's own family; the burial behind the shrine is the writer's surmise, marked as one)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「Here in the former Hatara district too, many houses have a graveyard adjoining or close by, though not within the grounds.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「旧幡羅郡の当地でも、敷地内ではないが、墓地が近接あるいは接近している家は多い。」) (the writer's own district)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「But no house moved it very far. Many used part of their own fields or woods within the hamlet of the koaza.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「しかしどの家もそれほど遠くへは移していない。小字の集落内の自分の畑や山林の一部を利用したものが多い。」) ; and the sentence before it, which gives the move itself as the writer's surmise: 「They probably moved the graveyards out of the house plots.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「墓地を屋敷内から移したのであろう。」)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「In mountain districts with little flat land and the like, the graveyard seems often to be set part-way up the mountain behind.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平地の少ない山間の地などでは、墓地は背後の山の中幅に設ける例が多いらしい。」)
  • eisi-yashikibaka - 「It is a form in which each household's grave is built using part of the land that household owns.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「それぞれの家で所有している土地の一部を利用して、各家のお墓が建てられているという形です。」); 「graves suddenly appear in the middle of a field along the road, or in a house's front garden」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「道路沿いの畑地の真中や、家の庭先などにいきなりお墓が現れたりする」) (a stonemason's account of a visit to Fukaya in northern Saitama)
