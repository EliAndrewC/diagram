• kotobank-haka - 「Formerly, many households each had a graveyard, or a branch house set its grave beside the main house and so formed a lineage grave. Because each household setting up graveyards here and there as it pleased, and all of them being designated tax-exempt land, ran against the common interest, from the Meiji period on graveyards were often consolidated. There were also yashiki-baka, graves set within a household's own house land.」 (translated; ) (Inokuchi Shoji's article in the Nipponica encyclopedia)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「One sometimes sees houses with a graveyard within the house plot (on the grounds of the home) or on land adjoining it. According to Yanagita Kunio, they are many in the Kanto, in Ou, and in the mountain villages of southern Kyushu, among other places」 (translated; ) (Yanagita's finding as the blog reports it)
  • ikikata-yashiki-bochi - 「House-plot graveyards that survive today are not many across the country, and it is also said that they are especially few in the Kansai and many in the Kanto.」 (translated; )
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「According to Mr. Takatori, in the Meiji period there was advice from the administrative authorities that graveyards be gathered into one communal graveyard to each oaza.」 (translated; ); 「Many oaza followed the administration and gathered them into a communal graveyard. But there were also oaza that did not.」 (translated; ) (Takatori Masao, as the blog reports him; an oaza is a district of a village)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「The second is the graveyard from before Genroku, which was at the southeast corner of the house plot.」 (translated; ); 「The third is the graveyard at the northwest corner of the house plot; it appears on a survey map of the end of Meiji, but its land category is woodland, as in substance it is, and it was originally a burial place.」 (translated; ); 「At that time I think they raised no gravestones and buried in the woods behind the house plot's guardian god. That place lies to the northwest of the main house's plot and is part of the house grove」 (translated; ) (the writer's own family; the burial behind the shrine is the writer's surmise, marked as one)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「I have heard that in one area of Kodama district in Saitama Prefecture too, many houses have a graveyard beside the house plot; here in the former Hatara district too, many houses have a graveyard adjoining or close by, though not within the grounds.」 (translated; ) (the writer's own district, set beside one in Saitama)
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「But no house moved it very far. Many used part of their own fields or woods within the hamlet of the koaza.」 (translated; ) ; and the sentence before it, which gives the move itself as the writer's surmise: 「They probably moved the graveyards out of the house plots.」 (translated; )
  • nireyama-yashiki-bochi - 「In mountain districts with little flat land and the like, the graveyard seems often to be set part-way up the mountain behind.」 (translated; )
  • eisi-yashikibaka - 「It is a place called Fukaya, in the north of Saitama Prefecture」 (translated; ); 「It is a form in which each household's grave is built using part of the land that household owns.」 (translated; ); 「graves suddenly appear in the middle of a field along the road, or in a house's front garden and the like」 (translated; ) (a stonemason's account of a visit to Fukaya in northern Saitama)
