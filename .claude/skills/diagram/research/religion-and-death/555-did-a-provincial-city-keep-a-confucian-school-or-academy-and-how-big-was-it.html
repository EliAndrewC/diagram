Did a provincial city keep a Confucian school or academy, and how big was it?

Sources: hanko-jawiki (the domain schools, their number and beginnings), kodokan-jawiki (the largest domain school and where it stood), taku-seibyo-jawiki (a small lord's school and its Confucian hall), shizutani-taiseiden-bunka (a domain-built Confucian hall), shizutani-gakko-jawiki (what a domain-built school held), yushima-seido-jawiki (the shogunate's own school), shuyuan-zhwiki (what a Chinese academy was), yuelu-shuyuan-zhwiki (a provincial capital's academy), l7r-rites-and-temples (where the setting schools its samurai children)

What the research found - Japan. A Japanese domain kept one school for its retainers' sons, the hanko; the first was founded at Okayama in 1669, and in their period of growth they reached 255, in almost every domain. Some began as a Confucian shrine and a lecture hall at the house of the domain's own Confucian scholar. The largest, Mito's Kodokan, stood inside the castle's third bailey on about 105,000 m² (some 26 acres). The Confucian hall itself was small: the lord of Taku founded a school in 1699, set an image of Confucius in its lecture hall, and in 1708 finished a hall three bays by three, with a skirt roof about it; the Confucian hall the Okayama domain built at Shizutani in 1684 is likewise three bays by three, and that school, opened for commoners, held a lecture hall and two dozen other structures - the Confucian hall among them, with a study, a drinking room, a library, a shrine and a stone wall. Above every domain stood the shogunate's own school at Yushima in Edo, whose ground grew in 1799 from 12,000 to over 16,000 tsubo (about 10 to 13 acres), its hall rebuilt at two and a half times its first size, after the Confucian shrine of Mito.

What the research found - China. Every Chinese prefecture and county had its official school with its Confucian temple, which "Does a county seat or a city carry the state cult's altars and temples?" sizes. Beside those stood the academies: a separate system from the official schools, founded privately, though sometimes under official direction. The Yuelu Academy at Changsha, a provincial capital, covers 21,000 m² (some 226,000 sq ft), laid out as a run of courtyards along one axis - gate, second gate, lecture hall, library and Confucian temple on it, dormitories and shrines to either side - though the same page elsewhere puts its Confucian temple to one side of the axis.

The setting. The GM's canon sends a county's samurai children to the provincial city or the domain capital for schooling. It names no Confucian temple, so a school in the setting carries no Confucian hall.

What was not found. No readable page gives the site of a typical domain school, between Taku's small one and Mito's great one, nor sizes for a school's lecture hall.

The decision it drove. None yet: no map draws a school. The history gives two forms - Japan's one school per domain, at the castle, with a lecture hall and a small hall three bays square; China's official school at every seat and an academy in the larger cities - and the canon puts schooling in both the provincial city and the domain capital. Whether a provincial city's map draws a school, and on how much ground, is left for the GM; until then it is a guess that a provincial city holds one school compound for samurai children.
