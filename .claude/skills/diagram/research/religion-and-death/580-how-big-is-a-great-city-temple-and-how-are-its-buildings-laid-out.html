How big is a great city temple, and how are its buildings laid out?

Sources: zojoji-history (an Edo great temple's precinct, sub-temples and monks), zojoji-keidai (the distances along its approach), kaneiji-jawiki (a precinct, its main hall and cloister), chion-in-jawiki (a great temple in three terraces), zuiryuji-jawiki (a provincial lord's temple, building by building), daxiangguo-zhwiki (a capital's great temple), kaiyuansi-quanzhou-zhwiki (a prefectural city's great temple on its axis)

What the research found - Japan's great temples. At its Edo-period height Zojoji, a shogun's family temple in Edo, held a precinct of 250,000 tsubo (some 200 acres) with forty-eight sub-temples and a hundred-odd dormitories, and is said to have kept three thousand monks in training; today its halls, bell tower and sutra house stand on 16,000 tsubo (some 570,000 sq ft). Its approach runs about 108 ken (some 640 ft) from the outer gate to the main gate and about 48 ken (some 290 ft) on to the main hall, numbers the temple ties to the 108 passions and the 48 vows; its sutra house is 9.5 ken (about 56 ft) square. Kan'ei-ji at Ueno held over 305,000 tsubo with 36 sub-temples at its height; its main hall of 1698 was 45.5 by 42 m (149 by 138 ft) and 32 m (105 ft) tall, with a square cloister before it entered by a gate on the front, and the abbot's quarters behind it. Chion-in in Kyoto climbs its hillside in three terraces: the main gate and the sub-temples below, the central halls in the middle, Honen's mausoleum above. Its main hall is 44.8 by 34.5 m (147 by 113 ft), eleven bays by nine, and its gate, 24 m (79 ft) tall, is the largest two-story main gate of any temple standing in Japan.

What the research found - a lord's temple in a provincial castle town. Zuiryuji in Takaoka, built to mourn the lord who built Takaoka's castle, sets its main gate, Buddha hall and dharma hall in one straight line with cloisters to left and right and the other halls placed symmetrically, a plan said to follow a great Chinese monastery; its plan names an outer gate, the main gate, the Buddha hall, the dharma hall, a bath, a privy, the great kuri, the monks' hall and a tea hall. Its bell tower stands on the north cloister between the tea hall and the kuri, five stone mausolea of the Maeda and Oda houses stand outside the southwest cloister, and its dharma hall is a six-room plan of 186 tsubo of floor (some 6,600 sq ft).

What the research found - China. At its Northern Song height the Daxiangguo temple of Kaifeng, the capital, ran 64 sub-temples on 540 mu (some 90 acres, if the mu is the modern one) with several thousand monks, and a Song writer says its court and side galleries held ten thousand people trading. The Kaiyuan temple of Quanzhou, a prefectural city, stands on 78,000 m² (some 19 acres); on its axis come a screen wall, the main gate that is also the hall of the Heavenly Kings, a worship pavilion, the main hall, an ordination platform and the sutra library, with a pagoda, a shrine to its patrons and a chapel to the east and a second pagoda and further halls to the west. Its main hall is nine bays by six, 1,338 m² of floor (some 14,400 sq ft) and 20 m (66 ft) tall. What was not found. No page read gives the precinct of a great temple in a provincial Japanese castle town, or the size of an abbot's quarters or of a sub-temple's lot.

The decision it drove. Every great temple read, Japanese or Chinese, of any sect, sets its gates and principal halls on one line and its other buildings to the sides of it, so the plan below is accurate. Its size is not: the great temples read stand on 19 to 250 acres, and the two whose monks are counted kept thousands, while the setting's city complexes, with their 15 to 30 monks, are drawn at about 73,000 sq ft ("What does a temple precinct hold?"), less than a tenth of Quanzhou's temple. Keeping the great temple's plan at a provincial city's size is a deviation.

The rule the map follows: a great city temple is a walled precinct on one axis: an outer gate, the main gate, then the main hall (and in a Zen house a dharma hall behind it) in a line, with a cloister or corridors round the central court; the bell tower and the sutra house stand to the sides of that court, the kuri and the monks' quarters to one side, the abbot's quarters behind the main hall; sub-temples are separate walled lots below or around the core; the lords' mausolea or the graveyard lie outside the cloister at the back. Where the ground slopes the three parts climb it as terraces. The main hall is drawn between an ordinary temple's 60 by 40 ft and the great halls' 150 by 115 ft, about 90 by 70 ft - a guess.
