Where do the first and the innermost arches stand, and where does the hall's label go?

Sources: torii-enwiki, sando-enwiki and kokugakuin-eos-torii (the first arch where the approach enters the grounds); the GM's rulings of 2026-07-27 on the avenue's threshold and on its caption. The pitch the arches are laid at is under "Torii spacing - two regimes and nothing in between".

Where the first arch stands. The approach "is almost always straddled by one or more torii", "its point of origin" being "usually straddled" by one, and the first, the largest, "stands at the approachway to the grounds of the shrine", further arches standing within the precinct. The first arch is therefore where the approach enters the shrine's ground, at the edge of its wood (under "How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?"). No page gives a village approach's length, or the distance from its innermost arch to the hall.

The avenue STARTS at its hall (the GM's ruling of 2026-07-27): "the distance from the front of the temple should be the same as the distance between each torii arch." An avenue can be perfectly spaced and still stand yards away from the temple it serves - three arches up a street on the far side of a flophouse, or with the caption and two rows of houses between it and its hall - and then it does not read as that temple's approach; it reads as red marks near some other building. So the threshold is set alongside the count and the stride: the whole run is moved bodily, keeping its direction, its curve and its stride and changing only its distance, until the innermost arch stands one pitch off the nearest FACE of the hall (measured to the footprint rather than to the center, so a run drawn off to one side slides onto the flank it stands off rather than diagonally into the middle of the building). A village's run is laid through the same pitch and seated by the same rule.

And a caption never stands in the approach (the same conversation: an arch must "never be covered by the 'temple of X' label"). A hall's own caption and its approach both want the ground at the hall's face, which is where the collision came from. The caption tries three baselines in a STRICT ORDER - the side the map's author asked for, that same side pushed past the far end of the avenue, then the opposite side - and takes the first that touches no arch. Stepping PAST the avenue has to exist because a parish graveyard may sit deliberately at the back of a monastery, so flipping the caption off the approach would only bury it in the cemetery.
