  • bochi-jawiki - 「a graveyard that the inhabitants of a settlement own in common and manage」 (translated; ) (under the page's heading 村落墓地, "village graveyard"); 「and at present, village graveyards and private graveyards are not permitted」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-ryobosei - 「Whereas the burial grave is set on riverbanks, in the hills, by the shore and the like, the visited grave is set in the precinct of a temple, a hall or the like within the village. The burial grave is most often on common land, the dead being buried in the order of their deaths, and when there is no more room to bury, returning to the first place. And at the burial place no more than a stone or the like is set; no permanent structure is made.」 (translated; ) (the Heibonsha encyclopedia's entry); 「This form of burial is distributed centered on the Kansai region, and is hardly seen in Kyūshū or the Tōhoku region.」 (translated; ) (the Yamakawa dictionary of Japanese history's entry; the Heibonsha entry has it reaching Kantō and Tōhoku, and hardly seen in Kyūshū)
  • haka-jawiki - 「Until the Second World War there were also many cases of building a grave on a corner of one's own land, or through a neighborhood group and the like」 (translated; )
  • danka-jawiki - 「Making one's grave at one's danna-dera was also half made an obligation, and it was around this time that ordinary people too came to set up stone monuments at their graves.」 (translated; ) (the Edo date is the surrounding passage's, not this sentence's)
  • oikawa-2008-kikaijima - 「From the Meiji Restoration on, wind burial (exposure burial) was banned by the political authorities as unhygienic ... and at this time "where to make the burial place (the place of first burial)" varied from settlement to settlement.」 (translated; ); in the paper's table, 「outside the cliff caves too, settlement graveyards begin to form」 (translated; ); and 「Kikaijima's settlements are highly independent, and partly because marriage within the village has been the rule, differences in folk culture between settlements are assumed to have been large even at the early-modern stage」 (translated; ) (the paper credits this to Gamō 1959)
