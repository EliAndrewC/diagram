  • kotobank-ryobosei - 「Whereas the burial grave is set on riverbanks, in the hills, by the shore and the like, the visited grave is set in the precinct of a temple, a hall or the like within the village. The burial grave is most often on common land, the dead being buried in the order of their deaths, and when there is no more room to bury, returning to the first place. And at the burial place no more than a stone or the like is set; no permanent structure is made.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「埋墓が河川敷・山中・海浜などに設置されるのに対し，詣墓は村内の寺・堂などの境内に設けられる。埋墓は共有地の場合が多く，死亡順に埋葬していって，埋葬する場所がなくなれば，またもとの場所へもどる。また，埋葬地には石などを置くくらいで永久的な施設を設けることがない。」) (the Heibonsha encyclopedia's entry); 「This form of burial is distributed centered on the Kansai region, and is hardly seen in Kyūshū or the Tōhoku region.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この墓制は関西地方を中心に分布し，九州や東北地方ではほとんどみられない。」) (the Yamakawa dictionary of Japanese history's entry; the Heibonsha entry has it reaching Kantō and Tōhoku, and hardly seen in Kyūshū)

  • oikawa-2008-kikaijima - 「From the Meiji Restoration on, wind burial (exposure burial) was banned by the political authorities as unhygienic ... and at this time "where to make the burial place (the place of first burial)" varied from settlement to settlement.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明治維新以降、風葬（曝葬）は不衛生を理由として政治権力によって禁止される…このとき『どこを埋葬地（一次葬地）にするのか』は集落によって様々となった。」); in the paper's table, 「outside the cliff caves too, settlement graveyards begin to form」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「崖穴外にも、集落墓地の形成始まる。」); and 「Kikaijima's settlements are highly independent, and partly because marriage within the village has been the rule, differences in folk culture between settlements are assumed to have been large even at the early-modern stage」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「喜界島は各集落の独立性が高く、村内婚を主としてきたこともあって、近世段階においてさえ、民俗文化の集落間差異は大きかったものと想定される」) (the paper credits this to Gamō 1959)
