Where did the straw rick stand after the harvest, and for how long?

Sources: kotobank-warazuka (where the rick was built, and its season), kigosai-warazuka (its shape, and what its straw went to), hirogawa-genryu-noka (the straw kept in the paddy into the next year)

Once the rice was threshed its straw was stacked into a rick (藁塚, warazuka). The rick is a thing of one season, and the GM set the seasonal features aside for maps of their own, so no map draws one yet. This question asks where it stood and how long it stayed. Whether the paddy under it carried a winter crop is at Did a paddy grow a second crop over the winter?; the farmstead's other fixtures, at The farmstead's fixtures.

On the reaped paddy or on its bunds. The rick was the threshed straw, in bundles, piled into a cylinder on the reaped paddy or on the bunds; it is a season word of autumn. Its form differed from region to region; one was a cylinder built round a pole at its center.

Kept into the next year, and used up through it. Its straw was chopped for compost, or made into straw goods such as bales and rope. In Hirogawa, in Wakayama, the threshed straw was stacked in the paddy and kept until the next year, as fodder for cattle and horses, as manure, and sometimes as fuel. That account is of farm life in the Meiji, Taisho and early Showa periods; that an older village kept its straw the same way is this page's inference. How far into the next year a rick lasted, no page read says.

What it means for the map. A summer map draws no rick. That a rick on the paddy floor was gone before the paddy was flooded and planted is this page's reasoning, and whether one on a bund still stood at midsummer no page read says; so leaving it off the summer map rests on the GM's deferral and on that silence, not on a finding that it was gone. A seasonal map from late autumn through the winter would draw ricks on the reaped paddies and their bunds. On a paddy sown to winter barley, a rick on the paddy floor would stand in the barley's way, so there the bund is the likelier place; that is a GUESS.
