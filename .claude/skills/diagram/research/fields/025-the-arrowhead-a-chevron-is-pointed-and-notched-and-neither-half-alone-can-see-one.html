The arrowhead: a chevron is pointed AND notched, and neither half alone can see one

This follows the minimum basin size rule: an independent review of Mizuguchi found an arrowhead-shaped basin that the size rule could not reach.

What separates it is the conjunction. A basin may taper honestly (the hem strips do). A basin may be concave honestly (one wrapped round a neighbor is). One that does BOTH has a point at one end and a bite out of its side, and that is an arrowhead. Over the 2,777 carved basins of the four scripted hamlets: apex < 40 deg alone is 71 (2.6%), solidity < 0.90 alone is 49 (1.8%), the conjunction is 13 (0.47%) - small enough to read one by one, which is how the thresholds were chosen rather than fitted. Both are round on purpose: 40 deg is "comes to a point", 0.90 is "has a real notch". Fitting tightly to the motivating ring (39 / 0.88) would have caught 11 and been overfitting to one example.
