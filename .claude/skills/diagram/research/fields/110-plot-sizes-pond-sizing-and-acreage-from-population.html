Plot sizes, pond sizing and acreage from population

Sources: kokudaka-jawiki (one koku a year; real stipends ~1.8), gokogomin-kotobank (40% then 50% - the "~45%" is the midpoint), kokumori-jawiki, zakkoku-jawiki and zakkoku-kotobank (coarse grain as the farmers' own food), ndl-crd-katemeshi and katemeshi-jawiki (the share of rice in the everyday pot), kochi-seiri-jawiki, king-forty-centuries

  • Plots: fragmented holdings were normal, each household's paddy lying in scattered plots; the size of a pre-modern plot, taken here as 0.02-0.25 acre, and the count of about 9 plots per household are this page's own figures, and the plots are irregular (see Paddy plots). STRAIGHT rectangular channels/plots are post-1900 consolidation (the ditches on the Tedori River's alluvial fan were only straightened in the early 1900s) - the organic waver is period-correct.

    Which figure is which, because two different things get called a plot. A leveled, diked CELL is one basin that has to hold water at an even depth, and that is physically small: the maps target about 0.05 acre for it (roughly 200 m2, a 47 ft square), derived from real feet at whatever scale the sheet is drawn to, so a basin is the same real size on a hamlet map and on a city map. The older drawing, still used on some maps, divides a field again and again down to a PARCEL grain instead - about 0.10 acre on the village maps, about 0.19 acre on the rest. All three figures sit inside the 0.02-0.25 acre band above, and neither grain is enlarged to make the map easier to read: the coarser one is a parcel, one of the scattered fields a holding was made of, and the finer one is the leveled cell. On this page's reading, a parcel on any slope is split into several cells, because each cell must be level to hold its water at an even depth - which is why the cell sits below the parcel figures.

  • Pond sizing (the rule): sole-storage tameike run ~2,000-2,500 m3 of storage per irrigated ha (typical depth 2-4 m); a STREAM-FED pond refilling 1-2x a season is comfortable at ~1,200-1,500 m3/ha. Hoshigaoka: 31.8 ha of paddy gives about 1.5 ha of pond surface, roughly 47,000 m3 at about 3 m deep, or about 1,470 m3/ha, plus a feeder stream. Pond area is kept proportional to command area.

  • Acreage from population (the sizing rule): a person eats ~1 koku/yr; the assessed rate for middling paddy ~1.3 koku/tan (the middle-grade kokumori, a tax rate rather than a measured yield); coarse grain fills part of the diet while ~45% of rice goes to tax (the midpoint of the 40% and 50% rates) -> ~0.8-1.0 tan gross paddy per person, which leaves coarse grain roughly a third of the diet (28-43%; rice alone would need ~1.4 tan). No source read gives that share of a whole diet, but the mix in the everyday pot brackets it: a farm village's rice was cooked with coarse grain, and around 1830 a village of Ina district in Shinano ate a winter midday rice of 40% rice; in living memory a paddy district cooked about four parts rice to one of barley, while dry-field districts ate millet and barley with a little rice, in the Kanto dry-field belt rice that was 70-80% barley. The third sits between the paddy district's one part in five and the Ina village's three parts in five that were not rice, well short of the dry-field districts, as it should for a village drawn among its paddy; the exact point is this page's own -> 350 inhabitants ~ 280-350 tan = 69-86 acres (+ dry margins later). Kikuta lands at ~79 acres / ~600 plots.
