Plot sizes, pond sizing and acreage from population

Sources: kokudaka-jawiki (one koku a year; real stipends ~1.8), gokogomin-kotobank (40% then 50% - the "~45%" is the midpoint), kokumori-jawiki, zakkoku-jawiki and zakkoku-kotobank (coarse grain as the farmers' own food), ndl-crd-katemeshi and katemeshi-jawiki (the share of rice in the everyday pot)

  • Plots: what a paddy plot was and how big, and how the maps draw it, are at Rice paddies and their plots (suiden) and How our maps draw rice paddies and their plots (suiden).

  • Pond sizing (the rule): a pond is sized by the area of the field it waters, as a Song-dynasty farming manual of 1149 sized a high field's pond at two or three mu in every ten; no storage per hectare is on any page read, in any period (at How big a pond did a field need before modern times?). Hoshigaoka: 31.8 ha of paddy gives about 1.5 ha of pond surface, about a twentieth of the land, plus a feeder stream - a stream-fed pond held below the Song measure, a guess. Pond area is kept proportional to command area.

  • Acreage from population (the sizing rule): a person eats ~1 koku/yr; the assessed rate for middling paddy ~1.3 koku/tan (the middle-grade kokumori, a statutory assessed yield rather than a measured one); coarse grain fills part of the diet while ~45% of rice goes to tax (the midpoint of the 40% and 50% rates) -> ~0.8-1.0 tan gross paddy per person, which leaves coarse grain roughly a third of the diet (28-43%; rice alone would need ~1.4 tan). No source read gives that share of a whole diet, but the mix in the everyday pot brackets it: a farm village's rice was cooked with coarse grain, and around 1830 a village of Ina district in Shinano ate a winter midday rice of 40% rice; in one undated account a paddy district in the Katsushika delta cooked about four parts rice to one of barley, while dry-field districts ate millet and barley with a little rice, in the Kanto dry-field belt rice that was 70-80% barley. The third sits between the paddy district's one part in five and the Ina village's three parts in five that were not rice, well short of the dry-field districts, as it should for a village drawn among its paddy; the exact point is this page's own -> 350 inhabitants ~ 280-350 tan = 69-86 acres (+ dry margins later). Kikuta lands at ~79 acres / ~600 plots.
