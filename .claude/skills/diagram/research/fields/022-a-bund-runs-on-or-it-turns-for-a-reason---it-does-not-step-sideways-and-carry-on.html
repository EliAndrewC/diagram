A bund runs on, or it turns for a reason - it does not step sideways and carry on

Sources: aze-jawiki (a bund on the owners' boundary, no fixed form outside modern works, re-plastered every year), fukui-kenshi-noji (the Edo-period bund work in the farm calendar), suido-ishizue-kochi-seiri (the narrow, irregular early-modern plot, and the jori grid kept in western Japan), jori-jawiki (the jori unit divided into ten). Nothing read speaks to a bund that steps sideways; the footnotes that say so record searches that found nothing.

What the research found. This is mostly the shared-bund finding read one step further, and it is worth writing down separately because the two rules pull in opposite directions and a reader who has only one of them will break the other. Bunds are shared finds real paddy fabric odd-shaped and piecemeal, and Paddy plots - irregular patchwork finds the ruled rectangle chiefly the work of modern land consolidation, which remade the narrow, irregular plots handed down from the early modern period. A bund commonly stands where two owners' land meets, and outside modern works it has no fixed shape or size. So the organic waver is period-correct and must not be cleaned up. One exception is real, and it is a second form rather than a degree of the first: in western Japan regular plots laid out up to the Meiji period, the ancient jori grid among them, were widely distributed, each square unit of the grid divided into ten equal parts, so a plain whose fields descend from it is ruled, not wavering. The maps draw only the wavering fabric; the jori-ruled plain is an attested second form they do not yet draw. What the wavering fabric permits is a bund that BENDS, a basin that is a trapezoid, and a wall that runs out at a T-junction. What it does not permit, and what nothing in the record produces, is a wall that runs, hops a few feet SIDEWAYS, and carries on parallel to itself. Both halves are this page's reasoning from the shared, irregular fabric above; no source states either.

Three reasons, all of them the aze's own economics. The bill for a bund is its LENGTH, since azenuri is re-plastering along it every year before the water goes in, in the Edo-period calendar a job of the weeks before transplanting; a jog buys the extra run between two corners for nothing. Corners are the part of a puddled ridge that slumps and so the part that is re-plastered hardest, and a jog buys two of them - a guess, which nothing read confirms. And the ground the jog trades is worth nothing to either party: it sits at the same level, floods from the same offtake and is reachable from either basin, so no farmer pays a wall's maintenance to move a boundary a few feet sideways along its own line - this page's reasoning, which no source states. Where a real parcel boundary DOES step there is a thing at the corner - a ditch, a path, a rock, an inherited holding - and at the scale these maps are drawn we draw those things, so a step with nothing at its corner is not a boundary at all - a guess, which nothing read confirms.

The decision it drove (GM 2026-08-18, on Inashiro: "the earthen wall is kind of going in a southward direction, and then instead of just continuing on and meeting at the four way intersection between the north south earthen walls and the east west earthen walls, it just goes sharply to the left before going down, thus making these extremely irregular shapes. This really, really looks like a rendering error"). It was a rendering error; the fabric carries only single, small, isolated corners.

Disclosed departure. A long sideways offset is not judged a step at all: past a certain length the offset is a LIMB and the parcel is an L, which is exactly the honest odd shape Paddy plots - irregular patchwork describes.
