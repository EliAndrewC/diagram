Where does a field's water come from, and how is it shared out?

Sources: tabayashi-1987, kagawa-tameike, jsidre-minumadai, japanese-wiki-corpus, beitang-studies, jsslkx-002-2021 (the canal-layout doctrine as a design standard states it), jori-jawiki

  • Pond: a valley-head tameike behind an earthen dike, sitting ABOVE its fields ("located at a valley head and constructed by dividing off the valley mouth with an earthen dike... at elevations higher than the surface of the paddy fields they serve"18 - Tabayashi 1987 ,78 Geographical Review of Japan 60(1)). ONE outlet: an inclined intake (shahi) feeding a bottom conduit (sokohi) through the dam; the spillway is flood-safety, never distribution (Kagawa pref. tameike docs).19 Parent/child pond linkage (oyaike/koike, Kagawa);77 "melon-on-the-vine" in China is reported flavor for larger systems.92
  • Distribution: sluice -> head-race -> division point (bunsuiguchi) -> a branching TREE. "Main canals gradually decrease in size as they are tapped by branch canals" (Tabayashi)20 - hence the drawn taper. The smallest ditches "are often considered parts of the paddy fields they serve"21 - hence ditch-as-plot-boundary. The network is sparse: a village digs the minimum; a ditch beside every paddy (yohaisui bunri) is a Meiji land-readjustment (1899/1905) anachronism.22
  • Layout modes (terrain-driven):
    • COMB (the default): supply canals along the HIGH margins, delivery ditches perpendicular down-slope, one drain along the low line. Grounding: the Edo Kishu-school layout (Minuma-dai 1728 - the LAYOUT is sourced, the NAME less so: 紀州流 is attested as Izawa Yasobei's river-channelization method rather than as a field layout;24 the layout is supply on the elevated margins, drainage channel on the lowest line, water reused downstream)23 AND codified Chinese canal doctrine (mains along contours/ridges on high ground, field channels perpendicular to contours).25 Chinese beitang pond systems - the direct tameike analogue - were THE dominant village-scale mode in rice China (8.3M ponds serving ~39% of irrigated area into the 1950s,26 ~71% in hilly regions93); the GM chose the Chinese default deliberately (Rokugan demographics anchor to Song/Ming China).
    • FAN: gently-descending canals radiating from a valley-mouth apex - the Dujiangyan / Tedori-alluvial-fan geometry.27 Correct where the land fans out below the pond.
    • JORI GRID: from the 8th century (743 CE, the mid Nara period, is the first appearance of jori land notation; the page does not single out the plains - it says remains exist "throughout Japan except Hokaido and Okinawa") much of Japan's long-settled PLAINS carried an astronomically-oriented 109 m grid (jori-sei: 1-cho squares in 6x6-ri blocks, cut into ~12 x 109 m tan strips).28 A plains village in an ancient core province shows semi-regular GRIDDED paddies, not organic patchwork115 - Rokugan analog: ancient heartland provinces (e.g. Crane/Phoenix cores). The organic patchwork is correct for terrain-following villages like Kikuta.
