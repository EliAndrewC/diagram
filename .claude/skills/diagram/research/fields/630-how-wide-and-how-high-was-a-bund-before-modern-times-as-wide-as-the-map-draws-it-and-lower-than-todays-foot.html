How wide and how high was a bund before modern times? As wide as the map draws it, and lower than today's foot

Sources: kotobank-azebiki (the Edo land survey's one-shaku bund), hattori-site-yayoiken (the widths and heights of the bunds of an excavated early Yayoi paddy)

The question. The map draws the bund between two paddies about a foot and a half wide, and a bund today stands about a foot high, the height of the standard bund, built for consolidated paddy (at How wide is a bund?). Under the GM's ruling of 2026-09-28 that nothing attested only in modern times is drawn, was a bund that size before modern times?

The width: yes. The Edo land survey reckoned the bund at one shaku wide, about 30 cm, and took a further shaku of field off each side of it; the dictionary's earliest example of the word is an order of the shogunate of 1726. It is a reckoning for tax, not a measured bund. Far older, the paddies of the early Yayoi period dug up at the Hattori site were divided by small bunds of the tilled soil 20-60 cm wide, made to hold standing water and not to be walked on, and had large bunds 80-150 cm wide with flat tops, which seem also to have served as paths for farm work. A bund a foot and a half wide sits inside both.

The height: lower than today's. The same site's small bunds stood 5-20 cm high, about two to eight inches, and its large bunds 15-30 cm. That is the one height read from before modern times, from a single site far older than the setting's centuries; no height for a bund of the Edo period was found, in a farm manual, a survey or a dig. A foot of height is today's standard, attested only in modern times.

What it means for the map. The bund drawn about a foot and a half wide is accurate and stays. Nothing drawn depends on the bund's height, so nothing on the map changes; where the record or the bund's explanation gives a height, it is a few inches, the height of the early bunds read, and not a foot.
