In-field features - flat flooded paddy hosts obstacles least

Sources: ryobosei-jawiki, henriot-shanghai-graves, fengshui-enwiki, tameike-jawiki

  • What the research found. Flat, flooded valley-bottom paddy is the archetype that hosts non-rice obstacles LEAST - it is the most valuable, most intensively worked, and wet. Grave mounds and feng-shui knolls are often MARGIN/slope features in the rice south (feng-shui wants a backing hill + downslope water view, so the dead go on the slope, not the wet center); rock outcrops are a TERRACE feature (bedrock the risers wrap around) and are absent on alluvial valley/polder and delta dike-pond; small OPEN-WATER ponds (a low pocket / header tameike / half-moon by a hall) are the one thing that genuinely belongs in the wet middle. The maps follow a per-archetype table of which features each field type may host.

  • The in-field grave island (GM 2026-07-20: both placements acceptable). The hillside grave is not the only southern form: villagers in the Yangzi delta also buried their dead one by one out in the open fields, while Japanese burials on a family's own land stood beside the bunds rather than in the plot. The evidence, and what the maps make of it, is under Are there really graves out in the middle of the fields? How often a map draws the island is a choice made for the maps, not a figure from any source.
