Why the bund shows at all in high summer, and why it is drawn dark red-brown

Sources: the bund's own dimensions, from the cross-section in Paddy plots - irregular patchwork; the GM's color ruling of 2026-07-24, made on a rendered ladder of five candidates; satoyama-levee-grassland (the grass carried on a paddy levee, and how often it is cut), nabunken-azemame (the beans sown along the bund after transplanting).

The bund is drawn at near-true width - about 1.5 ft of ridge at whatever scale the map uses, with a floor beneath which the line would thin away to nothing when the sheet is rendered. The stroke it is drawn in was chosen deliberately. A pale tan would depict 4-5 ft - an azemichi, a walking bund, on EVERY boundary - and reads as an orange lattice dominating the green. The GM picked a red-leaning dark mud brown from a five-color ladder rendered on one village, over a lighter chestnut that stays more nameably brown at hairline width: a line that dark blends toward black against the rice green at map view, and the GM preferred that weight. The pale tan serves instead as the exposed-earth AREA fill for true-width earthworks - perimeter dikes, dike-top house pads, dike-pond banks - where the ground really is bare earth several feet across. A terrace lip takes the dark color but keeps a wider stroke, because a terrace's near-vertical retaining face has real projected width where a flat bund does not.

The disclosed departure - a map drawing convention. That color is honest for SPRING, when the ridge is fresh wet azenuri plaster. By high summer, which is the growth stage the paddy surfaces depict, a real bund carries grass and bund beans: a paddy levee carries a grassland that farmers keep down by mowing two to five times a year, and beans are sown along the bund after the rice is transplanted and cut with it. That the green bund then very nearly disappears into the green field is this record's own reading; no page read states it. So a visible bund network at that season is a stylization, kept because it is what carries the plot structure to the eye; the honest summer alternative is green on green, which erases the field's whole fabric. It is a rendering choice.
