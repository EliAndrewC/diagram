When would a field's canals fan out, or run on a grid, instead of a comb?

Sources: tabayashi-1987, japanese-wiki-corpus, jori-jawiki

Where a field's water comes from, how it is shared out, and the comb the map draws by default are Where does a field's water come from, and how is it shared out?. This question is the two other layouts the ground can call for.

  • FAN: gently-descending canals radiating from a valley-mouth apex, the geometry this page draws for an alluvial fan such as the Tedori fan, whose paddy irrigation system is attested; the radiating layout, and the likeness to China's Dujiangyan (Sichuan's ancient river-diversion works), are this page's own reading. Correct where the land fans out below the pond.
  • JORI GRID: from the 8th century (jori land notation first appears in 743 CE, the mid Nara period) much of Japan's long-settled PLAINS carried a north-south / east-west 109 m grid, tilted in many regions (jori-sei: 1-cho squares in 6x6-ri blocks, each square cut into ten equal tan). Regular jori parcels were widespread in western Japan, so a long-settled plains village there shows semi-regular GRIDDED paddies, not organic patchwork - Rokugan analog: ancient heartland provinces (e.g. Crane/Phoenix cores). The organic patchwork is correct for terrain-following villages like Kikuta.
