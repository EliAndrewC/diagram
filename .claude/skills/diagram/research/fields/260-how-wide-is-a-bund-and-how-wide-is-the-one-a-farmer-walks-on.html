How wide is a bund, and how wide is the one a farmer walks on?

Sources: kato-1999-ittanbu-kukaku (bunds of 2 shaku and farm roads of 1 ken in a field replanned in 1869), smtrc-2019-nawanobi (the land survey's one-shaku bund), nougyoudoboku-keihan (the modern standard bund), kotobank-aze-sekai-daihyakka (the bund as path and boundary, and what azenuri does), kigosai-azenuri (azenuri a late-spring task in haiku), kubota-azenuri-kuwa (azenuri done with the hoe)

The bund's cross-section today, and why the grid of rectangles is modern, are at Paddy plots. This question asks how wide the bund was before modern consolidation, and how much wider the bund a farmer walked along ran.

The dividing bund: one to two shaku, about one to two feet. The modern standard bund is a trapezoid 30 cm high and 30 cm across its top. The earliest measured figure read for a replanned paddy is two shaku. Nishikimi Chodayu replanned some 43 cho at Inazato, in Hozumi village, Gifu, from the autumn of 1869 to the next spring. There each paddy was 10 by 30 ken, the farm roads and the ditches 1 ken wide, and the bunds 2 shaku. That was a new layout on a new grid, made at the very end of the old order, so its bunds are the nearest figure read and not a measurement of an old field. A second, weaker figure comes from the Edo-period land survey that set each plot's tax. An allowance there called azekiwa-biki reportedly took the bund to be one shaku wide and moved the boundary a shaku on each side, shrinking the taxed area. A present-day column reports this, hedged and without a source. So the dividing bund of one to two feet the map draws is within what was read.

The walking bund: wider, by how much no page read says. Every bund was walked. It is the path of farm work (azemichi) and the boundary of a holding. Its grass has long been cut for manure or fodder, and beans were often grown along it. No page read gives a width for a bund built wider to carry a path. The one wider way read is the replanned field's farm road of one ken, about six feet. So the two to five feet the map allows a walking bund is a GUESS, held between the two-shaku bund and the one-ken road.

Re-plastering the bund each spring: azenuri. Before transplanting, the weeds were stripped from the bund's face and kneaded mud plastered on, so that less water seeped through it. In haiku it is a word of late spring: firming the bunds with plaster before rice planting, so water and fertilizer do not seep away. One verse filed under it runs 「Plastering the bunds - / in the running stream / a straw raincoat shaken and rinsed」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「畦ぬりや蓑ふりすすぐ流れ川」). It was hand work with the hoe, and the hoes differed from province to province.

What it changes on the map. The dividing bund is drawn about a foot and a half wide, within the one to two shaku read, and it stays. The walking bund keeps its two to five feet, still a guess but now held below the six-foot farm road. The farmhouse set-back built on the bund, at How close does a farmhouse stand to the paddy?, now rests on a bund width that was read. Its path and eave parts are still unread.
