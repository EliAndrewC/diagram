Where do the samurai estates sit when the paddy has the near ring?

Sources: the GM's setting notes on land tenure and the samurai country estate; the GM's ruling on approach lanes; heino-bunri-jawiki and jikata-chigyo-jawiki (where Edo-period samurai lived), kadota-kotobank (the medieval warrior's house among its own paddy)

The fans claim the near ring BEFORE any country estate is placed, and the gentry keep the pockets left over - most of their estates stand farther out in the rural district, off the sheet altogether. This rests on the GM's setting notes rather than on the historical record: in the setting a samurai family may keep a country estate close to some farmland, never part of a village, and the wealthiest place theirs on the side facing the capital. It means a city map showing as few as ONE estate, even one whose grounds run off the frame, is the honest signal rather than an omission; a ring of estates crowding the wall would be the error.

How this compares with Japan. The country estate is a DEVIATION from the Edo period, whose samurai were moved off the land to live gathered in the castle town, and even those who held villages as their fief were bound to live in the castle town, one domain's retainers moving between the two as the exception; some domains never made the move, and there many retainers lived in the farming country to the end of the period. The setting's estate is closer to the medieval form, a local lord's residence set among its own paddy, typically spread before its gate, mostly well-established fields worked directly for the lord. So a country estate is rightly drawn with farmland at its door; how far from the city it stands is the setting's, not the history's.

The decision: estate DRIVEWAYS are not drawn. The GM ruled them out because the long worn approach lanes read on the sheet as fat roads laid across the countryside, and the drawing already held that an approach lane is not a thing drawn at this scale. The manors therefore stand as clean walled compounds with their approaches implied off the map.

The rule the map follows: a CITY map shows at least one samurai estate; one of them cut by the frame edge is legitimate and says its land continues beyond. Estates cluster on the side of the map that faces the capital, stand at least about 600 ft apart so they never read as a row, and vary in size. No estate is drawn with a driveway.
