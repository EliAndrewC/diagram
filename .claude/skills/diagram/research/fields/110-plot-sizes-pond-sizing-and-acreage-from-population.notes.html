  • kokumori-jawiki - 「The method adopted was to set upper paddy at 1 koku 5 to, middle paddy at 1 koku 3 to and lower paddy at 1 koku 1 to, dropping 2 to for each rank lower (the two-to descent), with house plots taken as 1 koku 2 to.」 (translated; )「Kokumori is the statutory estimated yield per tan (todai) of paddy, dry field and house plot in the land surveys of early modern Japan.」 (translated; ) (a to, 斗, is a tenth of a koku)
  • zakkoku-jawiki - 「In Japan it was once an important staple grain, and outside Edo, where rice gathered from every part of the country, coarse grain tended to be eaten as the staple, but its consumption and cultivation died out as rice production rose in the Showa era.」 (translated; ) (the page gives no share of the diet; outside Edo it makes coarse grain the staple itself, more than "part")
  • zakkoku-kotobank - 「Historically it was treated as outside the tax levies, and was grown as the farmers' own subsistence food or as a famine-relief crop.」 (translated; ) (山川 日本史小辞典 改訂新版, the Yamakawa dictionary of Japanese history)
  • ndl-crd-katemeshi - 「In farm villages doing heavy labor, three meals a day spread early, but they were plain meals centered on coarse grain, and the rice too was katemeshi mixed with coarse grain. For example, in a village of Ina district, Shinano province, around 1830, on winter mornings they ate roasted barley flour and buckwheat cakes; at midday, katemeshi of 40 percent rice with dried radish and radish leaves and barnyard millet;」 (translated; ) (the library's answer quoting ビジュアル・ワイド江戸時代館 (an illustrated reference on the Edo period), p. 172; one village, one season's meal)
  • katemeshi-jawiki - 「In farming villages, even in paddy districts, people ate rice cooked with large amounts of coarse grains such as barley and foxtail millet, tubers, radish, greens and so on. In dry-field districts a 'three-grain rice' of foxtail millet, barnyard millet and barley mixed with a little rice was the everyday food, and in places such as the Kanto dry-field districts the everyday food was rice that was 70 to 80 percent barley.」 (translated; )「In Mizumoto, in Tokyo's water-country of Katsushika, where paddy fields spread over the delta formed by the lower Edo River, Naka River and others, except in the hot summer season, they eat barley rice nearly every day in a proportion of about 2 sho of non-glutinous rice to 5 go of whole barley.」 (translated; ) (2 sho is 20 go, so four parts rice to one of barley; the page gives no date for the account, so it is not an Edo-period count)
  • gokogomin-kotobank - 「in the early Edo period it was shiko-rokumin (four parts to the lord, six to the farmer), and from the Kyoho era (1716-36) onward it is said to have become gokogomin (five to the lord, five to the farmer)」 (translated; ) (ブリタニカ国際大百科事典, the Britannica International Encyclopedia)「until the Kyoho era (1716-36) it was four parts to the lord and six to the farmer, and thereafter, through the implementation of the kemi (検見, crop-inspection) method, it became five and five - so it is said, though this is not certain」 (translated; ) (日本大百科全書, the Encyclopedia Nipponica) (early Edo 40% to the lord / 60% to the farmer; from the Kyoho era (1716-36) 50/50, which the second entry calls uncertain; the ~45% is the midpoint of the two, on no page)
