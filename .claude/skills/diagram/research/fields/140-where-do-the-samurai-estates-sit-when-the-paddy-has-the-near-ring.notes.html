• heino-bunri-jawiki - 「the shift from warriors living on the land to their gathering in the castle town and the lord's direct rule of the farmers」 (translated; )
  • jikata-chigyo-jawiki - 「They were called jito, but unlike medieval jito they were obliged, except where their duties required otherwise, to live in the castle town (Edo, for the shogunate's direct bannermen and housemen). As an exception, in Sendai domain each retainer alternated his residence between the Sendai castle town and his fief.」 (translated; ) ("they" are the retainers who held villages as their fief)
  • heino-bunri-jawiki - 「In areas that kept a half-farming system as policy, such as the goshi of Kochi domain and the Yonezawa, Soma, Choshu, Saga, Kumamoto and Kagoshima domains, and in regions short of food self-sufficiency, the separation of warrior and farmer (or merchant and artisan) was not made until the end of the Edo period, and there are many cases where many retainers lived in the farming country or in townspeople's quarters.」 (translated; )
  • kadota-kotobank - 「Rice land around the residence of a medieval local lord; the name is thought to come from its typical form of spreading before the residence's gate. ... and because mostly well-established paddy was chosen ... In general, ... it was land the lord managed directly and had worked by dependents, the farmland over which the local lord's control was strongest.」 (translated; ) (日本大百科全書, the Encyclopedia Nipponica)
