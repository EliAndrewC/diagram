• heino-bunri-jawiki - 「the shift from warriors living on the land to their gathering in the castle town and the lord's direct rule of the farmers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「在地武士制から城下集住と領主による農民の直接支配への転換」)
  • jikata-chigyo-jawiki - 「They were called jito, but unlike medieval jito they were obliged, except where their duties required otherwise, to live in the castle town (Edo, for the shogunate's direct bannermen and housemen). As an exception, in Sendai domain each retainer alternated his residence between the Sendai castle town and his fief.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「彼らは地頭と呼ばれたが、中世の地頭とは異なり、職務上の必要による例外を除いては、城下町（幕府直属の旗本・御家人の場合は江戸）に在住する義務を負っていた。例外として、仙台藩では各家臣が仙台城の城下町と知行地との間を参勤交代していた。」) ("they" are the retainers who held villages as their fief)
  • heino-bunri-jawiki - 「In areas that kept a half-farming system as policy, such as the goshi of Kochi domain and the Yonezawa, Soma, Choshu, Saga, Kumamoto and Kagoshima domains, and in regions short of food self-sufficiency, the separation of warrior and farmer (or merchant and artisan) was not made until the end of the Edo period, and there are many cases where many retainers lived in the farming country or in townspeople's quarters.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「高知藩の郷士、米沢藩、相馬藩、長州藩、佐賀藩、熊本藩、鹿児島藩などのように政策的に半農制を残した地区や食糧生産量における自給率が厳しい地域では江戸時代末期まで兵農（商工）分離がされず、家臣の多くが農村地帯や町人地に居住したという事例も多い。」)
  • kadota-kotobank - 「Rice land around the residence of a medieval local lord; the name is thought to come from its typical form of spreading before the residence's gate. ... and because mostly well-established paddy was chosen ... In general, ... it was land the lord managed directly and had worked by dependents, the farmland over which the local lord's control was strongest.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「中世の在地領主の居館の周辺部に所在した田地で、居館の門前に広がる形態が典型であったためこの名がついたと考えられる。…かつ多くは熟田が選ばれたため…一般的には、…などの隷属民に耕作させた直営地で、在地領主の支配権がいちばん強く及ぶ耕地であり」) (日本大百科全書, the Encyclopedia Nipponica)
