Where dry (hatake) crops go - the topographic catena

Sources: satoyama-enwiki (the mosaic and the foothill-to-flat border zone), satoyama-jawiki (the woodland's slopes, its cutting cycle and what it had become). The Takeuchi-school catena sentence ("large middle river terraces... large areas of crop fields and small areas of paddy") is this page's reading, on no page read

WHERE dry crops go: wet-rice villages sort by a topographic CATENA - irrigated paddy holds the flat valley bottom / plain; DRY fields (hatake) take the HIGHER, well-drained ground the water cannot command (river terraces, natural levees / micro-highs threading the plain, alluvial-fan edges, lower slopes, AND the slightly-raised ground the homesteads sit on); the woodland of the foothills (satoyama) lies on the slopes around the village, in some places sharing them with dry plots - broadleaf coppice cut back every ten to twenty years for firewood and charcoal, though by the early modern period most satoyama had become red pine, grass or bare hill. So dry fields are NOT one neat strip - historically they sit in SEVERAL positions, above all AROUND the houses (each family has some paddy and some hatake - the household's dry plots near its home).
