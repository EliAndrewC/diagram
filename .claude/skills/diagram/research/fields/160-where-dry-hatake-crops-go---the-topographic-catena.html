Where dry (hatake) crops go - the topographic catena

Sources: satoyama-enwiki (the mosaic and the foothill-to-flat border zone), satoyama-jawiki (the woodland's slopes, its cutting cycle and what it had become). The catena itself - which ground each kind of field takes - is this page's reading; no source read places them.

WHERE dry crops go: the satoyama, the border zone between the mountain foothills and the flat land, holds paddy, dry fields, woods, grassland and ponds together in one mosaic. This page reads that mosaic as a topographic CATENA - irrigated paddy on the flat valley bottom / plain, DRY fields (hatake) on the HIGHER, well-drained ground the water cannot command (river terraces, natural levees / micro-highs threading the plain, alluvial-fan edges, lower slopes, AND the slightly-raised ground the homesteads sit on) - an order no source read gives. In the Yoshino mountains the satoyama is the dry fields and woods on the slopes around the village; its broadleaf woods were coppice cut back every ten to twenty years for firewood and charcoal, though by the early modern period most satoyama had become red pine, grass or bare hill. So dry fields are NOT one neat strip: on this page's reading they sit in SEVERAL positions, above all AROUND the houses (each family has some paddy and some hatake - the household's dry plots near its home), though one source read puts paddy round houses built on slightly higher ground instead.
