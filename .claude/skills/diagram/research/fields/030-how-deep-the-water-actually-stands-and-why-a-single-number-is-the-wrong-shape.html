How deep the water actually stands, and why a single number is the wrong shape

Sources: maff-suitou-mizu, zennoh-mizukanri

  • The depth is an inch, not four to six, and there is no single number. The maintained figures are 2-3 cm, about an inch - 「after rooting, a slightly shallow water depth of 2-3 cm」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「活着後は水深2~3cmのやや浅水とし」),53 and for the twenty days from heading, 「it is important to keep the field flooded to a depth of about 2-3 cm」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「２～３ｃｍ程度の湛水状態を保つことが重要です」).9 And a single depth is the wrong SHAPE, which is the larger point: there is no season-long depth to state.
  • The season, as the sources give it. 3-4 cm at transplanting, held slightly deep against cold and wind until the plant roots;54 2-3 cm once rooted, deliberately shallow so the water warms and tillering starts early;55 then 中干し (nakaboshi), the mid-season drain (a modern extension calendar's stage; that a premodern paddy was drained mid-season is a GUESS until a period source carries it) - the field is taken down to nothing and dried until 「small cracks appear in the field surface and a light footprint shows」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「田面に小さなヒビが入り、軽く足跡がつく程度」);56 then intermittent wet-and-dry;10 standing water again at heading, 2-3 cm for twenty days;57 intermittent through ripening;11 and a final drain 5-7 days before harvest.58 A paddy is not under water all season, and for part of midsummer it is dry enough to crack.
  • Where a four-to-six-inch figure DOES appear, and why it misleads. 10 cm and 20 cm are real numbers in the record - but as a cold contingency,59 not a norm: MAFF (Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries) gives them under 「when there is a risk of the temperature falling」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「気温が下がる恐れがある場合は」), 10 cm at panicle formation and 20 cm at booting, to protect the young panicle. A figure read out of that context becomes a maintained depth that nobody maintains.
  • What the irrigation survey covers. The irrigation survey this record leans on elsewhere (tabayashi-1987) classifies irrigation systems by their water source and says nothing about water depth.60
  • What we could NOT get, and what that costs. No pre-modern depth figure, in Japanese or Chinese material. The searches returned Edo irrigation infrastructure and water-dispute histories and no number. So the figures above are modern extension guidance applied backward, and the map's explanation says so to the reader rather than presenting them as a historical finding. What is plausible about applying them backward - that the agronomy of a puddled basin has not changed, and that shallow warm water for tillering is a constraint of the plant rather than of the century - is reasoning, not evidence, and is not asserted.
  • The bund is NOT re-derived from this. The aze runs roughly 1-2 ft wide and about a foot high (sourced separately, above).61 A foot of ridge over an inch of water is not a contradiction to fix: the ridge has to hold the 10-20 cm cold-protection state, keep freeboard in rain, and be walked. That is inference rather than a finding, and is labeled one rather than presented as a sourced claim; nothing drawn depends on the depth - the drawn bund is a WIDTH.
  • The map shows one moment. The staging is a real seasonal axis: a map showing nakaboshi would draw cracked mud where this one draws water. The map depicts one moment.
