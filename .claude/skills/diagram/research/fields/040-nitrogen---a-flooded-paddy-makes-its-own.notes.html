• azolla-enwiki - 「They form a symbiotic relationship with the cyanobacterium Anabaena azollae, which lives outside the cells of its host and which fixes atmospheric nitrogen.」「The rotting plant material resulting from the die-off of this Azolla releases nitrogen into the water for the rice plants, providing up to nine tonnes of protein per hectare per year.」 (English; Ecology section)
  • azolla-enwiki - 「Azolla has been used for at least one thousand years in rice paddies as a companion plant, to fix nitrogen and to block out light to prevent competition from other plants.」「The earliest known written record of this practice is in a book written by Jia Sixie in 554 CE in Qimin Yaoshu.」 (English)
  • genge-jawiki - 「As a paddy off-season crop the seed was sown among the rice around September; it grew after the rice harvest, overwintered, and flowered the following spring.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水田裏作で9月ごろにイネの間に種をまき、稲刈り後に生育して冬を越し、翌春に花を咲かせていた」) (the winter green manure grown in the paddy, with its nitrogen mechanism on the same page; the plowing under before flooding and the night soil, ash and cake are on no page read)
  • nabunken-azemame - 「They are soybeans, also called "azemame (畦豆, bund beans)"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「『畦豆（あぜまめ）』とも呼ばれる大豆です」)「Azemame are sown as seed on the bund after the rice is transplanted, and are harvested at the same time as the rice cutting」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「畦豆は田植え後に畦に種がまかれ、稲刈りと同時に収穫されます」) (soybeans also called azemame; sown on the bund after transplanting, harvested with the rice)
