  • dankyu-kotobank - 「Because they are flat, high and dry, they were used early for settlements, dry fields, roads and the like. Where water was hard to get, however, they were sometimes left out of clearing and development, and in many places irrigation works turned them into paddy.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平たんで高燥な場所であるため，早くから集落，畑地，道路などに利用されてきた。しかし，水利の便が悪い場合には，開拓・開発から取り残されたこともあり，灌漑施設の設置によって水田化が進行した場所も多い。」) (改訂新版 世界大百科事典, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia)
  • senjochi-jawiki - 「Fan middle: because the river sinks underground, the water table is low and the land is water-poor, so it is hard to use as paddy. It was therefore often undeveloped coppice,」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「扇央部 河川の伏流により地下水位が低く乏水地となるため、水田には利用しにくい。このため未開発の雑木林になっている例が多かった」)
  • senjochi-kotobank - 「Because a fan's underground water rises as springs at the fan toe, settlements form there, and nearby paddy has often developed early. ... The fan middle, however, has deep groundwater; its development came late and it was long left as wilderness. In Japan it was at last developed from the end of the Edo period into Meiji, and has been used as cotton, mulberry, tea and wheat fields and orchards.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「扇状地の伏流水は扇端に泉となって湧出するので、ここに集落ができ、付近には水田が早くから発達していることが多い。…しかし、扇央部は地下水が深く開発が遅れて長く原野のまま放置されてきた。日本では、江戸時代末期から明治にかけてようやく開発が進められ、ワタ畑、クワ畑、チャ畑、ムギ畑、果樹園などに利用されてきた。」) (日本大百科全書, the Encyclopedia Nipponica)
  • satoyama-jawiki - 「In the Yoshino mountains of Nara prefecture, the mountains are divided, in order from nearest the village and lowest in elevation, into 'satoyama', 'uchiyama', 'okuyama' and 'dake', and what counts as 'satoyama' is the dry fields and coppice woods on the slopes around the settlement.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「奈良県の吉野山地では、山を村落から近く標高が低い順に「サトヤマ」「ウチヤマ」「オクヤマ」「ダケ」と区分しており、「サトヤマ」に該当するのは集落の周囲の斜面にある畑や雑木林である」)「Broadleaf woods: every 10 to 20 years they were cut, leaving the roots, and used for firewood and charcoal.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「広葉樹林の場合：10年から20年ごとに根を残して伐採され、薪や木炭に利用された。」)「Looking back through history, by the early modern period most of Japan's satoyama had become red pine forest, or grass-covered or bare hills.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「歴史を遡ると、近世までに日本の里山の大半はアカマツ林、あるいは草山、禿げ山となっていた。」)
