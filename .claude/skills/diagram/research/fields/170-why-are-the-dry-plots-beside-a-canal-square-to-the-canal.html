Why are the dry plots beside a canal square to the canal?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-07; Buck's surveys for the size of a dry parcel; this project's measurement of its own maps; jsslkx-002-2021 (the orthogonal field grid, and the watering direction that follows the fall on irrigated dry land as on paddy), mu-land-enwiki

A dry field bordering a supply canal is surveyed TO the canal: one edge runs along it and the other stands perpendicular to it, running upslope, so the plots come out as clean rectangles. The parts of that are design law: on flat ground the canals of successive grades run perpendicular to one another, a field canal's length and command area are taken from the length of the cultivated block beside it, and the direction a field canal waters in follows the field surface's own fall - which the rule states for irrigated dry land as well as for paddy.88 What no page read says is which grid is the parent, and that is what this rule turns on; that the parcel takes its frame from the canal rather than from the rice around it is this record's own. Built in the paddy's own frame instead - columns down the fall, rows on the contour - the plots would come out sheared into parallelograms, since a head-race commonly runs diagonally to the fall.

A softened lean, REJECTED. Merely softening that lean while STILL orienting the plots to the paddy was priced and turned down by the GM: the plots take their orientation from the CANAL, not from the rice. The plots are tiled by arc length along the canal's own line and each is built as a rectangle offset perpendicular to the local tangent, so neighbors abut continuously along the canal, nothing is sheared or stepped, and only the outer upslope edge is ragged. That the strips therefore run near-horizontal along one map's head-race and near-vertical along another's is the point, not an inconsistency. They stay on the dry side throughout, both edges upslope of the canal behind a thin berm, and where a map carries more than one fan a hem plot that would lap onto a neighboring fan's rice is dropped: dry crops on flooded ground contradict the whole reason the hem is where it is.

The rule the map follows: a dry parcel is a smallholder's strip, and the map-wide MEAN dry parcel stays under 0.25 acre. The mean Chinese dry parcel this page works from is near one mu, about 0.15 acre at the 614 m2 mu (0.16 at the 1930 metric mu), and the drawn village strips land at 0.16-0.17 with the hamlet's garden-scale strips at about 0.04.99 The tiling is in real feet at every scale - roughly 92 ft across a parcel and 72 ft down a row at the village grain - so a dry cell is never drawn dwarfing the rice basins beside it, which is what happens when a pixel figure is carried unscaled onto a coarser sheet.
