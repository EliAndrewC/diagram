• kotobank-nimosaku - 「Land use in which two different kinds of crop are planted on the same cultivated field twice in one year」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「In Japan the technique of rice-barley double cropping of the paddy was established: the paddy is flooded in summer to grow wet-tropical paddy rice, and in winter the water is let off and it is made into dry field to grow barley/wheat, a cool-temperate field crop.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry)
  • kotobank-nimosaku - 「As back crops of paddy rice, Chinese milk vetch, rapeseed, potato, broad bean, pea, onion and others were also grown.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the entry gives no dates for the list; the potato and the onion are left out of the prose above as crops of later centuries, which is this page's reading, not the source's)
  • kotobank-nimosaku - 「There are historical records showing double cropping in the mid-Heian period, and in the Kamakura period it spread fairly widely.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「is clear also from the law of the fourth month of 1264 (Bun'ei 1), by which the Kamakura shogunate ordered the provinces to forbid collecting annual tax on back-crop barley (Shinpen Tsuika).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-nimosaku - 「In the Edo period, as the small holding of the independent farmer became general and farming grew more intensive, the range of paddy double cropping widened further, centered especially on Kinki and San'yo, which were favored with conditions such as improved varieties, purchased fertilizer and irrigation works」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Kinki is the Kyoto-Osaka region, San'yo the Inland Sea coast of western Honshu)
  • kotobank-nimosaku - 「Before World War II, the line joining northern Kanto to Toyama Prefecture was the limit line of rice-barley double cropping; north of it, low temperature, snow cover and wet paddies were the limiting factors on double cropping.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • nimosaku-jawiki - 「Double cropping, which grows two kinds of crop in a year, needs twice the fertilizer of a single crop a year. So from the medieval period on, when double cropping spread, the use of stable manure and night soil spread to make up the soil's strength.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ); 「But enough fertilizer could be secured only near densely populated areas, and in remote rural areas barley could not be grown as the back crop. Moreover, barley, being weak to damp, cannot be grown in wet paddies.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the passage is set in the medieval period, when double cropping spread)
  • kotobank-kanden - 「a paddy that drains well and, when water is not let in, dries into the state of a field.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Digital Daijisen); 「mechanization and back crops such as barley are also easy」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Nipponica)
