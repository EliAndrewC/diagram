• kotobank-nimosaku - 「Land use in which two different kinds of crop are planted on the same cultivated field twice in one year」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「同じ耕地に、1年に2回、異なった種類の作物を作付けする土地利用」); 「In Japan the technique of rice-barley double cropping of the paddy was established: the paddy is flooded in summer to grow wet-tropical paddy rice, and in winter the water is let off and it is made into dry field to grow barley/wheat, a cool-temperate field crop.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「日本においては，水田に夏は湛水（たんすい）して湿潤熱帯性の水稲を栽培し，冬は水を落として畑地化して冷温帯性畑作物のムギを栽培する，水田の米麦二毛作技術が確立した。」) (the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry)
  • kotobank-nimosaku - 「As back crops of paddy rice, Chinese milk vetch, rapeseed, potato, broad bean, pea, onion and others were also grown.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水稲の裏作にはレンゲソウ，ナタネ，ジャガイモ，ソラマメ，エンドウ，タマネギなども栽培された。」) (the entry gives no dates for the list; the potato and the onion are left out of the prose above as crops of later centuries, which is this page's reading, not the source's)
  • kotobank-nimosaku - 「There are historical records showing double cropping in the mid-Heian period, and in the Kamakura period it spread fairly widely.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平安時代中期に二毛作の存在を示す史料があり，鎌倉時代には二毛作がかなり普及した。」); 「is clear also from the law of the fourth month of 1264 (Bun'ei 1), by which the Kamakura shogunate ordered the provinces to forbid collecting annual tax on back-crop barley (Shinpen Tsuika).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「1264年（文永1）4月に鎌倉幕府が諸国に令して裏作麦の年貢徴収を禁じた法令（《新編追加》）からも明らかである。」)
  • kotobank-nimosaku - 「In the Edo period, as the small holding of the independent farmer became general and farming grew more intensive, the range of paddy double cropping widened further, centered especially on Kinki and San'yo, which were favored with conditions such as improved varieties, purchased fertilizer and irrigation works」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代に入り，本百姓の小規模経営が一般的に成立し，農業経営の集約化が進むと，とくに品種改良，購入肥料，灌漑用水施設などの諸条件に恵まれた近畿や山陽方面を中心として，水田二毛作の範囲がいっそう拡大され」) (Kinki is the Kyoto-Osaka region, San'yo the Inland Sea coast of western Honshu)
  • kotobank-nimosaku - 「Before World War II, the line joining northern Kanto to Toyama Prefecture was the limit line of rice-barley double cropping; north of it, low temperature, snow cover and wet paddies were the limiting factors on double cropping.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「第2次大戦前は北関東から富山県を結ぶ線が米麦二毛作限界線であり，これ以北では低温，積雪および湿田が二毛作の限定要因となっていた。」)
  • nimosaku-jawiki - 「Double cropping, which grows two kinds of crop in a year, needs twice the fertilizer of a single crop a year. So from the medieval period on, when double cropping spread, the use of stable manure and night soil spread to make up the soil's strength.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「年に2種の作物を栽培する二毛作では、一年単作の2倍の量の肥料が必要となる。そのため二毛作が普及した中世以降には地力を補うために厩肥や下肥の使用が広まった。」); 「But enough fertilizer could be secured only near densely populated areas, and in remote rural areas barley could not be grown as the back crop. Moreover, barley, being weak to damp, cannot be grown in wet paddies.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「だが十分な肥料を確保できるのは人口密集地域の近郊に限られ、鄙びた地域では裏作に麦を作ることはできなかった[3]。さらに湿気に弱い麦は湿田では栽培できない。」) (the passage is set in the medieval period, when double cropping spread)
  • kotobank-kanden - 「a paddy that drains well and, when water is not let in, dries into the state of a field.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「水はけのぐあいがよく、水を入れないときには乾いて畑の状態になる田。」) (the Digital Daijisen); 「mechanization and back crops such as barley are also easy」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「機械化や麦などの裏作もしやすく」) (Nipponica)
