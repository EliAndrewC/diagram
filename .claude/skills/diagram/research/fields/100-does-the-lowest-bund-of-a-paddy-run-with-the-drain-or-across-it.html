Does the lowest bund of a paddy run with the drain or across it?

Sources: the GM's report of 2026-08-08 on a town map; aze-jawiki (the bund between a paddy and the ditch beside it, and where the boundary stands), bungotakada-tagoshi (paddy-to-paddy water against canal and drain), suido-ishizue-kochi-seiri (the consolidated plot that touches its channel).

The GM, reading a town sheet on 2026-08-08: "the dark brown earthen bunds appear to overlap with the drainage ditch instead of aligning with it. I would expect the earthen bunds bordering the ditch to be at the same angle as it."

What the research found. Where an open ditch runs beside a paddy, the bund between the two is a kind with its own name, the ditch-side bund, and since the ditch is often public land the boundary is sometimes set at the paddy-side foot of that bund or at the water line. The bund and the ditch lie side by side, so where that ditch is the collector a paddy's lowest bund stands on its bank - this page's reading, which the source does not state. The two cannot interpenetrate: the bund is what holds the water in and the ditch is what takes it away, so a bund drawn across the collector is a basin with its wall standing in the drain. The field's drain-side edge is therefore a single line running WITH the ditch, with the bunds that run down the slope between plots meeting it end-on.

Which paddies have a ditch beside them at all. A modern paddy draws from a supply canal and lets its water out into a drain, while the old way, still kept at Tashibu-no-sho Osaki, passes the water from paddy to paddy; the consolidated paddy of about 1900 was laid out so that every plot touched its channel and a road. On a pre-modern field, then, the collector runs along the field's lower edge rather than past every plot, and the hem this question is about is the row of plots on that edge - this page's reading of the two, which neither states outright.

Two things the drawing has to get right. A hem plot laid on the CONTOUR while the collector runs at up to about nineteen degrees to it would drift some 9 ft relative to the ditch across a 26 ft plot, beginning above the collector and ending below it, and where the hem holds most of the plots along that edge - on one map's western spread of paddy, 14 of the 31 plots along the edge came from the hem, against 4 from the last ordinary row of plots - the field's edge reads as a sawtooth with brown ticks poking out into bare ground. So the hem samples the drain at both of its own bounds and runs both of its edges parallel to what the ditch does between them. Separately, a flat set-back from the drain is blind to the collector WIDENING downstream, and a correctly angled bund would still sit inside the ditch's stroke over the lower half of its run; the set-back is half the ditch's drawn width at that point plus half a bund, so the two abut instead of overlapping.

And one residue accepted. The collector's line wanders on purpose - about 6 ft of node jitter at 120-170 ft spacing - so successive stretches differ by a few degrees. A paddy basin as this project draws it has STRAIGHT sides and cannot chase that, and a bund pinned only at its two ends cuts the corner at every bend and lands in the water. So a bund is lifted to clear the highest point of the bank across its own span, which is also what a farmer laying a straight bund against a crooked ditch would do - this page's reasoning, which no source states. The visible consequence, accepted: a bund can sit a few degrees off its LOCAL stretch of drain - about nine on one map - while clearing it everywhere.
