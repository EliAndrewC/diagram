Does the lowest bund of a paddy run with the drain or across it?

Sources: the GM's report of 2026-08-08 on a town map; the bund's construction, from 'Bunds are SHARED' above.

The GM, reading a town sheet on 2026-08-08: "the dark brown earthen bunds appear to overlap with the drainage ditch instead of aligning with it. I would expect the earthen bunds bordering the ditch to be at the same angle as it." That was a real construction error being read correctly, not a stray plot.

The physical fact the drawing has to honor. A paddy's lowest bund IS the collector's top-of-bank. The two abut and cannot interpenetrate: the bund is what holds the water in and the ditch is what takes it away, so a bund drawn across the collector is a basin with its wall standing in the drain (this rests on general reading; no source is cited).11385 The field's drain-side edge is therefore a single line running WITH the ditch, with the column bunds meeting it end-on at the fall angle.

Two things the drawing has to get right. A hem plot laid on the CONTOUR while the collector runs at up to about nineteen degrees to it would drift some 9 ft relative to the ditch across a 26 ft plot, beginning above the collector and ending below it, and where the hem holds most of the plots along that edge - on one map's west fan, 14 of the 31 against 4 from the closing rank - the field's edge reads as a sawtooth with brown ticks poking out into bare ground. So the hem samples the drain at both of its own bounds and runs both of its edges parallel to what the ditch does between them. Separately, a flat set-back from the drain is blind to the collector WIDENING downstream, and a correctly angled bund would still sit inside the ditch's stroke over the lower half of its run; the set-back is half the ditch's drawn width at that point plus half a bund, so the two abut instead of overlapping.

And one residue accepted. The collector's line wanders on purpose - about 6 ft of node jitter at 120-170 ft spacing- so successive stretches differ by a few degrees. A paddy basin has STRAIGHT sides and cannot chase that, and a bund pinned only at its two ends cuts the corner at every bend and lands in the water. So a bund is lifted to clear the highest point of the bank across its own span, which is also what a farmer laying a straight bund against a crooked ditch would do.112 The visible consequence, accepted: a bund can sit a few degrees off its LOCAL stretch of drain - about nine on one pocket - while clearing it everywhere. It is also why the rule is written about POSITION rather than angle. A correct straight bund and a wrongly contour-laid one occupy the same band of angles on a gently fitted drain, so an angle threshold would have to be either toothless or wrong.
