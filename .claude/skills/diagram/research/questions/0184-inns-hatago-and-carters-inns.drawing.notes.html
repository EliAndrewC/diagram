• machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「The main facility of a post town was the hatago, basically a building of the same plan as the machiya. Past the big door the doma ran to the back, beside it the board-floored mise and daidokoro, and upstairs or at the back several rooms for lodging were fitted out. In the Tokaido post towns of the late Edo period, a projecting lattice on the road-side second floor was a mark of the hatago」 (translated; )
  • toyokawa-akasaka-juku - 「A hatago consisted of an earth floor, a board-floored room, rooms, zashiki, the kitchen quarters, a bath and a privy, and where it faced the highway two stories was usual.」 (translated; ); 「The Ohashiya was a large hatago, with a frontage of about 9 ken and a depth of about 23 ken.」 (translated; ); 「By 1624 the Fifty-three Stations of the Tokaido were almost fully set up, and Akasaka-juku became the thirty-sixth station from Edo.」 (translated; )
  • netease-dachedian - 「On the land of the Northeast in the late Qing and the Republic, along the muddy main roads, one could always see courtyards with thatched roofs and earth walls」 (translated; ); 「The siting of a cart inn was particular: mostly beside a main road, on high ground near water」 (translated; ); 「The yard was fenced round with tall wooden stakes; inside, besides the mud-brick houses where people lived, there was always a spacious stable, and bundles of hay were piled in the corners of the walls.」 (translated; ); 「the three or five rooms were all great kang running north and south, one kang sleeping more than twenty」 (translated; )
