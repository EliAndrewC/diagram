How our maps draw theater stages on shrine ground (miyaji shibai)

Every town on our maps draws one theater stage, unless the town is set up otherwise: a roofed stage facing an open viewing ground, beside a temple's or shrine's hall. That follows the shrine-ground plays of Edo-period Japan, put on in a precinct with nothing roofed but the stage. No town draws a permanent roofed playhouse; how our maps draw those is at How our maps draw roofed playhouses.

  • The stage stands within about 260 ft of the nearest temple or shrine hall.
    This project chose the distance to fit the towns our maps draw; no source gives one.
  • The stage's open viewing side faces the nearest temple or shrine hall.
    Where the audience stood is not recorded; this project's GUESS is between the stage and the hall, inside the precinct. A stage turned away from the hall would put its audience outside the precinct, with its back to the hall that owns the ground.
  • A walled town keeps its stage inside the walls, unless the town is set up otherwise.
    This is the project's choice, not taken from a source; a town set up otherwise may have its stage outside the walls, or no stage at all.
  • The stage is drawn as a roofed stage facing open ground, never as a seating bowl.
    Only the stage of a shrine-ground play was roofed, so the ground before it is drawn open.
  • A Chinese-style town's stage is drawn the same way as a Japanese one.
    This is a GUESS: no page we read describes the stage at a Chinese temple.
