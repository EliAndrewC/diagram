Time bells in castle towns and capitals (toki no kane)

A Japanese castle town told the time by one great bell. The domain's lord set it up, a few low-ranking men rang it, and it was struck about every two hours, day and night, so that retainers and townspeople alike knew the time by it. It also gave the alarm for fire. Edo, the shogun's own city, kept a relay of many bells, and Xi'an in China a drum tower and a bell tower standing apart.

  • At Iwatsuki the time bell was the castle's own, set up by the domain's lord.
    At Iwatsuki the fourth lord set up a bronze bell in 1671 "as Iwatsuki castle's bell", at one spot in the town. In 1687 four low-ranking men rang it, and it was struck twelve times a day.
  • At Matsushiro the domain's bell rang about every two hours, day and night, and gave the fire alarm.
    The domain's rule was a great bell struck every toki, the traditional hour of about two of ours, by which retainers and townspeople alike told the time, and which also gave the alarm when fire broke out in the town. The first lord set it up in 1624, together with a fire lookout.
  • Did a castle town keep only one time bell?
    The list of Japan's surviving time bells has one each for Morioka, Hanamaki, Iwatsuki, Kawagoe and Osaka's town bell, and more than one only for Tokyo, the old Edo. It says itself that it does not list every time bell. No page we read gives a second bell to any domain's castle town.
  • Edo's first time bell hung inside the castle, and a drum took its place there.
    In 1626 the bell was moved out into the town because it disturbed the shogun's quarters, and from then on a drum gave the time signal within the castle.
  • Edo rang its hours through a relay of many bells.
    Edo was the shogun's own city, not a domain's seat, and far larger than any castle town (a comparison this project makes; no page here states it). Its town bell rang every toki by the sound of the castle's drum, and the city's other bells rang on hearing it.

Chinese cities told the hours from towers, by drum and by bell: sometimes one building housing both, the bell-and-drum tower, and at Xi'an two separate towers.

  • Xi'an built a drum tower and a bell tower, one reporting the hours by drum and the other by bell.
    The drum tower was built in 1380, early in the Ming dynasty, west of the bell tower, and the bell tower in 1384, when first built standing opposite the drum tower.
