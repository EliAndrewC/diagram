Doorways and doors (to)

The sources give the widths of Japanese doorways in ken and shaku: a ken is about 6 ft, half a ken about 3 ft, and a shaku about a foot. A farmhouse's main way in was a big sliding door, commonly about 6 ft wide, that opened onto its earth floor, and the household came and went through a low wicket set into it. An ordinary door was no wider than about 3 ft, by this project's reading. A storehouse was closed by a pair of heavy plastered leaves.

  • A farmhouse's main entrance was a big board door onto the earth floor.
    It filled a large opening at the front of the house that led to the earth-floored side, and slid to one side or parted to both.
  • At a farm or fishing-village house, the everyday entrance was usually one ken wide, about 6 ft.
    It was a sliding door onto the earth floor at the front of the house, one ken wide.
  • The household normally came and went through a low wicket set in a farmhouse's big front door.
    That door usually had a wicket in it, and the wicket was what was ordinarily used; it was so low and narrow that no one could pass it without lowering their head.
  • Farm and fishing-village houses had a second entrance at the back, for cooking.
    The entrance at the front of the earth floor was for daily use, the one at the back for cooking.
  • How wide was an ordinary door?
    Up to half a ken, about 3 ft. The big door is described as wider than an ordinary door, and the word for it originally meant a door wider than half a ken; read the other way, that puts an ordinary door at up to half a ken. That is this project's reading of the definition, not a width the page states.
  • Storehouse doorways were closed by two plastered leaves by the end of the Edo period.
    One survives as it was built at the end of the Edo period, its leaves turning on pivots rather than hinges, and a storehouse built between 1830 and 1867 has massive paired doors. Neither page gives a width.
  • How wide was each of a storehouse's leaves?
    The one width found is a present-day plasterer's standard size for each hinged leaf, 3.5 shaku wide and 6 high, about 3.5 by 6 ft. No width for a leaf from before the Meiji Restoration was found.
  • No width is recorded for a kitchen door, a guest entrance, or the opening a storehouse's leaves close over.
    No page we read gives any of them.
