How our maps draw the Imperial Magistrate's compound

A capital map draws the Imperial Magistrate's compound as one walled compound of about 90,000 square feet (~2 acres), holding the magistrate's court, office and residence, in an ink of its own so that it reads as the Emperor's ground and not as another of the domain's offices. Where its staff live is rolled per map.

  • How big is the compound?
    About 90,000 square feet, the size of an Edo town magistracy. It is sized against two offices: about 89,000 to 92,000 square feet for a magistracy, and about 105,600 square feet for the intendant's office at Takayama, which held its rice storehouse too. Those offices differ only in size, not in kind, so every capital's compound gets the one size instead of a size rolled per map.
  • Where the Emperor's granaries stand beside it, the compound and granaries together take a little more ground than the intendant's office at Takayama, which held office and storehouse in one.
    Where the granaries stand beside the compound - one value of their siting knob - the two take about 117,000 square feet, a little over the Takayama office. That the two figures agree is the check on both.
  • The map gives the compound a dozen staff households, fewer than the magistrate's roughly 25 assistants.
    The Imperial Magistrate keeps roughly 25 assistants; the map allots the compound 12 households, on the reading that not every assistant keeps a household of their own there. The count is a GUESS.
  • Where do the magistrate's staff live?
    Either inside the compound or beside it. The record supports both forms, so where they live is a knob, rolled per map from its seed, with neither value favored.
  • On some maps the staff households live inside the compound's own walls.
    They take row housing inside its walls, as a domain's estate in Edo housed its lower-ranking samurai; the map sets the rows along the perimeter.
  • On the other maps they live in a walled group quarter against the compound's wall.
    The compound then holds the magistrate's household and office only, and the staff households take walled plots of their own in the quarter, as the Edo magistracy's officers held granted estates of their own and its constables lived on plots of their own in group housing at Hatchobori.
  • Where the staff have a quarter of their own, it is drawn in the compound's ink.
    It is the Emperor's ground too, so the reader sees one foreign presence rather than a magistracy and an unrelated samurai block. This is a drawing convention.

The rule the map follows: the Imperial Magistrate's compound is one walled compound of about 90,000 square feet, drawn in its own ink as the Emperor's ground, holding the magistrate's court, office and residence. Whether its dozen staff households live inside its walls or in a walled group quarter against them is rolled per map; in the second case the quarter's households each take a samurai plot of the capital's own sizing, and the quarter is drawn in the compound's ink.
