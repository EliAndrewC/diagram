How our maps draw the chief retainer's house (karō yashiki)

Every magistracy on our building plans houses its karō inside the compound's walls. A county town's karō is one of its fifteen or so samurai, so the plans follow the scale of a shogunal intendancy, the kind of office our magistracies are modeled on, whose staff lived inside its compound in small houses and long-houses (nagaya). A mansion of the karō's own near the lord's residence or in the castle is the form at a domain lord's court, and belongs to a castle town, not to a county magistracy. Our plans come in two kinds, the county-town plan a program draws and the magistracy plans drawn by hand, and they use the two forms an intendancy's staff lived in: the first puts the karō in a long-house, the second in a small house.

  • On the program-drawn county-town plan, the karō lodges in a bay of the senior retainers' long-house.
    The long-house, 50 by 36 ft, stands in the outer court, and the karō's quarters are its north 14 ft, with a door of their own. A long-house is where an intendancy's staff lived. The sizes are a GUESS.
  • On the hand-drawn magistracy plans, the karō has a small house of their own in the residence court.
    At Ochiba, Hayakawa and Ubame it stands apart from the magistrate's house, never in the magistrate's own wing. That the karō's quarters are a small house of their own rather than a bay of the staff long-house is a GUESS: no source we read sets the head of the staff apart from the rest, and no page we read places a chief retainer's own house inside the lord's compound. Its size and where it stands in the court are a GUESS too.
  • Every plan labels the karō's quarters with the gloss "the house elder".
    It is a map drawing convention, the same on every plan, so a reader meets the word karō with its meaning.
