How our maps place and draw the city jail (roya)

Two forms of city jail are attested, and they differ in where the jail stands: inside the governor's compound, as in China, or in a compound of its own, as in a Japanese castle town. Each city's map takes one of the two, rolled from its seed - a knob. Either way the jail belongs to the city's Ministry of Justice, which runs the setting's jails.

  • Where the jail stands inside the governor's compound, it is a walled court at the compound's southwest, outside its ceremonial gate.
    That is where a Chinese yamen kept its jail. The court holds an inner ward and an outer ward, with a walled women's ward beside the outer, as a Qing department or county jail did. How the rest of the compound is drawn is at How our maps place and draw the governor's compound (yamen).
  • The jail court's size inside the governor's compound is our own GUESS.
    No page we read gives the area of a prefectural jail. Our maps set no figure for it yet; whatever it is, it stays far below Tenmachō's, as the last point below says.
  • Where the jail has a compound of its own, it stands on a residence plot near the Ministry of Justice.
    It takes the plot's size, as the Hagi jails were built on the sites of two confiscated residences. Placing it near the ministry that runs it is our own choice. The ministry's office is drawn as How our maps draw the offices of the six ministries (liubu) describes.
  • Where the jail has a compound of its own, a holding cell is kept in the governor's compound for the day a case is heard.
    Edo's North town magistracy kept such a temporary cell beside its court.
  • A provincial city's jail is never drawn at the scale of Edo's Tenmachō.
    Tenmachō, Edo's main jail, covered about 8,850 square meters (~95,000 sq ft), a size for the shogun's own capital, not a provincial city.
