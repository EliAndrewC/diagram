How our maps draw a capital's rice storehouses and brokers' row (kura, fudasashi)

A capital map draws its rice as a chain along the river: the wharf, the domain granary's storehouses at the quay, the brokers' row facing the granary on its town side, and the entertainment district beside the row. The Emperor's granaries are drawn apart, as a small walled compound of their own outside the castle, and which of two sites they take is chosen at random for each map. The domain granary and the brokers' row by its wharf take about 108,000 square feet (~2.5 acres) of the capital's ground, the Emperor's granaries about 27,000 square feet (~0.6 acre). Our maps never caption a capital's waterfront a kurayashiki district, since that word names a domain lord's warehouse in Osaka, where the domains sold their rice, as the research tells.

  • The granary's storehouses stand at the quay, a quay's width off the water.
    At Edo's Asakusa granary the storehouses stood in rows facing the boat channels, and the boats unloaded into them, so on our maps a porter never carries a sack further than the storehouse door. The quays, piers and landings of the wharf itself are at How our maps draw wharves and landings (kashi, gangi).
  • The storehouses are not set back from the river against floods.
    Whether such storehouses were set back or built up against floods is on no page we read, so the map keeps them at the quay.
  • How far does a landing stage run into the river?
    About a boat-length, 39 ft, a third of the map's 120 ft river channel. Keeping the fairway clear for boats is this project's reading, carried from the log boom's research; no page we read gives a Japanese rule of the period that kept a river's fairway clear.
  • The wharf keeps one landing stage at each end of the granary.
    Two stages rather than a row of piers leave the river in front of the storehouses clear for passing boats. That is this project's reading, and no page we read gives a period rule for it.
  • The brokers' row is one street of large merchant houses facing the granary across the street.
    In Edo the brokers and rice wholesalers stood eaves to eaves on the granary's town side, so the row is drawn as merchant frontage, never as state ground, its houses among the richest in the town.
  • Where in the granary's block does the firebreak lie?
    Edo's block before the granary held a firebreak as well as the brokers' houses. The source does not say where in the block it lay, so drawing it in front of the storehouses is a GUESS.
  • How long is the brokers' row?
    Six to twelve frontages, a GUESS. Edo's guild had 109 houses, serving the shogun's own retainers, while a capital in the setting pays about 800 samurai, and no page we read counts the brokers of a domain seat.
  • Why is the entertainment district drawn beside the brokers' row?
    Edo's brokers were frequent patrons of the kabuki theater and the Yoshiwara pleasure district, but the page does not place the district beside them, so setting it next door is a GUESS. Where a licensed pleasure quarter really stood, and why a capital map draws an entertainment district rather than one, is at its own section.
  • The Emperor's granaries are a modest walled compound outside the castle.
    They are a working store that faces brigands rather than a siege, so a stout wall and a watch suffice, and no castle ground is spent on them. The map draws no store of the Emperor's rice inside the daimyo's castle.
  • Where do the Emperor's granaries stand?
    Beside the Imperial Magistrate's compound or on the water, chosen at random for each map. Both sitings are attested for such a store, and neither is the stronger default. Beside the magistrate's compound, the official who oversees them is at hand, as the shogun's intendant's office at Takayama kept its rice storehouse in its own grounds. On the water, the grain arrives by boat, as it did at the Asakusa granary and at the domains' warehouses in Osaka. It is the same kind of either-or as the castle at the city's center or its edge: two capitals get different layouts for a documented reason.
