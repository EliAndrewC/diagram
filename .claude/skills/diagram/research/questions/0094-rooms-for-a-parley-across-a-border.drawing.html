How our maps draw a parley room on a border

A border post on our maps has a parley room built into the border wall. This project makes the door that receives a delegation from across the border the border itself, so the room stands astride the line, the border running across its floor: the two sides meet in one room, each kneeling on its own ground. We found no record of such a room: it is a deliberate deviation, this setting's own.

It departs from the two forms we found on record.

  • Russia and Qing China dealt across the line at Kyakhta, each from a post on its own side.
    Each side built a post on its own side of the border, and the two faced each other across it.
  • Tsushima's officers dealt with Korea from a compound on Korean ground, the Japan House.
    Officers sent from Tsushima conducted diplomacy and trade there, and the Korean side's banquet hall stood beside it.
  • The parley room's kneeling mats, two a side across the border line, are a deliberate deviation of this setting's own.
    No page we read seats two parties across a border line in one room. The count of two a side is this project's own choice; nothing in the record sets it.
