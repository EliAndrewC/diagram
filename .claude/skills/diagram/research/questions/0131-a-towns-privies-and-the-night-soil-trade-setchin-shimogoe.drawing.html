How our maps draw a town's privies (setchin)

The only privies a town map draws are the back tenements' shared ones: one or two small sheds to a block, at the far end of the block's lane. A street-front house's privy is at the back of the house, where its through-passage reaches the back yard, and nothing on the map marks the carrying-off of the night soil.

The rule the map follows:
  • A block of back tenements draws one shared privy shed, or two in a long block, at the far end of its lane beside the shared well.
    A back tenement had no privy for each unit but one or two shared ones within the block, and in Edo the shared well and privy stood at the far end of the lane. Our maps follow Edo's arrangement in every town, and which blocks get a second shed (the long ones) is a choice of our own.
  • Each shared privy shed is drawn about 6 by 9 ft, by our own guess.
    That is one ken by a ken and a half, and it is a GUESS: no page we read measures a shared privy.
  • A street-front house draws no privy outside its own walls.
    Its privy stood at the back, where the through-passage reached the back yard.
  • A town map draws nothing for the carrying-off of the night soil.
    The night soil went to the farms as fertilizer, and no page we read says where a night-soil yard stood.
  • The setting's owaiya change nothing on the map.
    The setting gives the night soil to the owaiya, a burakumin trade, but does not say whose privies they empty in a town; whoever empties them, the privies and the lanes are drawn the same.
