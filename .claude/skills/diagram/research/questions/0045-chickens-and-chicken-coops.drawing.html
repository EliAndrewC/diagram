How our maps draw chicken coops

On our maps a chicken coop stands on most farmsteads, about four in five, as a small square enclosure beside the house, where we GUESS it stood. The coop is drawn as imperial China kept it, this project's choice for the farmstead's small buildings, and so is historically accurate to China. The farmstead's other small buildings, and how many of each our maps draw, are at The farmstead's fixtures.

  • How many farmsteads have a coop?
    Between about seven and nine in ten, rolled once for each settlement around the 82 percent of farms with chickens that a survey of the 1920s counted. No count from before the empire fell in 1912 was found; the accounts from its last years say only that nearly every family kept a few chickens, so carrying the survey's share back to the empire is probable, not counted. How wide the range is around 82 percent is calibrated liberty.
  • Does the share change from region to region?
    Not on our maps. A farm survey of the 1920s found chickens more often in east-central China than in the north, but no page we read gives the cause, so reading it as wet rice against dry fields would be a GUESS, and every settlement rolls in the same range.
  • Does every farm that keeps chickens have a coop?
    On our maps, yes. The Qimin Yaoshu tells the farmer to roost chickens in a cage on the ground, not in the trees, and our maps take every farm with chickens to keep them in one. That every such farm did is a GUESS: the accounts from the empire's last years, before 1912, do not say whether the yard's chickens slept in a coop.
  • How big is a coop?
    5 by 5 ft, square after the late-Ming coop excavated at Zhengzhou, whose page gives no size. The size is a GUESS.
  • Where does a coop stand?
    Beside the house, by the yard. The one house type whose coops are placed, the Hakka enclosed house, kept them in its rear ring of rooms, with the kitchens and the pigsties; for an ordinary farm the sources say only that chickens were kept "in their yard", so placing its coop beside the house is a GUESS.
