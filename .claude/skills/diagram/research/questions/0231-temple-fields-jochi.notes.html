• jochi-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, the precincts, fields and house land of temples and shrines that the lord exempted from tax and levies.」 (translated; ) (the Yamakawa dictionary of Japanese history)「There were cases where at the land survey it was left unmeasured and never included in the village assessment, and cases where its yield was entered in the survey register and it was then separately exempted as jochi.」 (translated; ) (the Encyclopedia Nipponica)
  • warichi-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, dividing a village's farmland by the number of cultivators and allotting it, having each farm it for a fixed period, and re-dividing it when the term ended. A system carried out to even out the tax burden and the quality of the soil. It was practiced where production was unstable, such as land damaged by rivers.」 (translated; ) (the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the great dictionary of the Japanese language)
  • warichi-seido-kotobank - 「It is divided into several groups of land (danchi, wari) by paddy or dry field, soil quality, terrain, the expected frequency of floods and so on. Next it is gathered into the number of shares (kuji) the village has set. In doing so each group of land is split into that number of shares, and one from each is combined to make one share. Lots are then drawn by the landholding farmers only」 (translated; ) (the Encyclopedia Nipponica)
