How our maps size kitchen gardens (yashikibatake)

Our maps draw each farm's kitchen garden at its true size and keep it between 10 and 140 sq m (~108-1,507 sq ft), from a few tsubo up to about 1.4 se. That range is the research's own guess at how large these gardens were.

  • Nothing on a farm is enlarged to make it easier to see.
    The house and the work yard are drawn the same way at every scale, so the garden's size on the map is a claim about the past, not a drawing habit.
  • The size range applies to all of a household's beds together, not to each bed.
    A garden split into several beds was still one household's garden, so the beds are measured as one.
  • Below the range a plot would read as no garden at all, and above it as a field.
    The range's two ends are where the research set its guess: a plot of under a few tsubo would hardly be a bed, and one of more than a se or so would be a field in its own right. That reading is this project's, not a figure from a source.
  • How big are the gardens on our maps?
    The hamlets our maps have generated so far land at about 35-115 sq m (~377-1,238 sq ft) per household, centered near 55 sq m (~592 sq ft), about half a se - inside the range our maps allow.
