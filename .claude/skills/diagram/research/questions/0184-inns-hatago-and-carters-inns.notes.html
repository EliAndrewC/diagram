• machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「The main facility of a post town was the hatago, basically a building of the same plan as the machiya. Past the big door the doma ran to the back, beside it the board-floored mise and daidokoro, and upstairs or at the back several rooms for lodging were fitted out. In the Tokaido post towns of the late Edo period, a projecting lattice on the road-side second floor was a mark of the hatago」 (translated; )
  • toyokawa-akasaka-juku - 「A hatago consisted of an earth floor, a board-floored room, rooms, zashiki, the kitchen quarters, a bath and a privy, and where it faced the highway two stories was usual.」 (translated; ); 「The Ohashiya was a large hatago, with a frontage of about 9 ken and a depth of about 23 ken.」 (translated; )
  • jiemian-ancient-inns - 「The Ming had inns of the 'drink in front, lodge guests behind' kind, eating and lodging two in one」 (translated; ); 「But what existed in great numbers in the Ming were still the ordinary lodging inns spread through town and country, or, called together 'mule-and-horse', the 'great inns'. The mule-and-horse great inns mostly stood in the open country, and their lodgers were mostly laborers who came driving carts, who often cooked their own meals, so though the service was simple the price was cheap」 (translated; )
  • michie-siberian-overland - "we arrived at the inn at Nankow, and created a scene of no small confusion by our entry into the courtyard.
