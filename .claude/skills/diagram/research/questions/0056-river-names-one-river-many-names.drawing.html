How our maps name rivers

A river on our maps keeps one name along its whole course, across every province and clan border it passes. That is a deliberate deviation from the history: before the modern era a river changed its name from reach to reach, as the Chikuma still becomes the Shinano where it leaves Nagano and the river through Edo was the Sumida, the Miyato and the Okawa in different places. We keep one name so that players have fewer names to keep track of.

  • One name per river keeps down the names players must remember.
    It is the same choice the setting makes when it names a province's city after the province: the extra names would be historically accurate, but keeping track of them would cost the players more than it would give them. Naming each reach as the history does was weighed and declined for the names it would add.
  • One name per river costs our maps the local names of each reach.
    A map loses the local texture of a river named by the people who live on that reach of it, and a river that crosses a border keeps a name its downstream neighbors would not have used.
  • The same river keeps its name from one map to the next.
    The Hayakawa, which runs south out of the Fox Clan's province of Minami past its provincial city, is still the Hayakawa where it passes Nagahara, downstream in Crab Clan lands, and no map invents a local name for it.
  • A river keeps its one name in the text around a map, too.
    It holds wherever a river is named - in a map's notes, in its titles and in this research's own pages.
