Black pines in Japanese gardens (kuromatsu)

The Japanese black pine (kuromatsu) is the lead tree of the Japanese garden, and a garden pine is a tended one: its new shoots are pinched and further worked twice a year, and a pine left untended loses its worth as a garden tree. Left to grow, it is a tall tree, some 50 to 130 ft. A pine had a set place at a garden's edge too, planted at the edge of the wall so that it could be seen from outside. The garden itself is at Ornamental gardens, ponds and stone lanterns (teien).

  • How tall does the black pine grow when left alone?
    A Japanese guide to garden trees gives the species a height of 15 to 40 m (~49-131 ft).
  • The black pine is the lead tree of the Japanese garden.
    The same guide says it is used as the lead tree of a Japanese-style garden and to decorate celebrations.
  • A garden pine is tended twice a year.
    Its care needs midori-tsumi, the pinching of the new shoots that stick up in early spring, and momiage, twice a year; a pine whose care is neglected loses its worth as a garden tree.
  • One way of shaping a garden pine is called "gate-covering".
    The same guide names a training called mon-kaburi, "gate-covering".
  • A pine was planted by the garden wall to be seen from outside.
    The mikoshi no matsu is a pine planted at the edge of a wall so that it can be seen from outside. The phrase is already in a Japanese-Portuguese dictionary of 1603-04, at the start of the Edo period.
  • How big was a pruned garden pine?
    No page we read gives how wide the crown of a pruned garden pine was, or how tall it was kept. Pruning likely held a garden pine well below the height the species reaches in the wild, by an amount the record cannot put a number on; this is a GUESS. A garden kept up for generations would also hold old trees, whose crowns, even pruned, were likely wider than a young tree's; this too is a GUESS.
