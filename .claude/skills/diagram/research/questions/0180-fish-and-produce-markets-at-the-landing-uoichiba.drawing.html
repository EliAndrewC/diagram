How our maps draw fish and produce markets at the landing (uoichiba)

Our maps draw a river or harbor city's fish market at its landing, laid out on the parts of Edo's, with its produce market apart.

The rule the map follows for a city on navigable water:

  • Our maps give every city on navigable water a fish market at its landing.
    It is drawn as a short row of piers at the bank, a line of storage sheds along the bank behind them, a row of selling sheds and open stalls facing the street behind those, and a well among them - the parts of Edo's fish market in its plan of 1842.
  • Our maps put a city's produce market on its own site, at another stretch of the bank.
    Osaka kept its fish and its produce at separate markets.
  • A provincial city's market is drawn smaller than Edo's, at a size not yet set.
    No generator draws the market yet, so no size has been chosen. When one is, it will be a GUESS, scaled down from Edo's twelve piers and fifty sheds: no page we read says how a smaller city's market was laid out.
