How our maps draw straw bales of rice and charcoal (tawara)

A bale on our maps stands for the straw bales that carried a domain's tax rice and a town's charcoal. No bale from before modern times was found measured, so the maps take their real sizes from twentieth-century bales, and draw them larger than that so that they read.

  • How big was a rice bale before modern times?
    Today's 60 kg (~132 lb) bale is 75 cm (~30 in) long and 47 cm (~19 in) across. Scaled from what the Edo bales on record held - the shogunate's 3 to and 5 sho, Kaga domain's 5 to - an Edo bale would be within a tenth of that size either way - a GUESS. Our maps take a rice bale to be about 2.5 ft long and 1.5 ft across.
  • How big was a charcoal bale?
    A mid-twentieth-century charcoal bale measured 63 cm (~25 in) by 39 cm (~15 in) across its two larger sides. No older one was measured. Our maps take a charcoal bale to be about 2 by 1.3 ft.
  • A bale is drawn about 4 ft long, by convention.
    At their real lengths, about 2.5 ft for a rice bale and 2 ft for a charcoal bale, the bales are too small to read at the map's scale, so those aboard a tax barge and stacked on a charcoal yard's weighing floor are drawn larger.
