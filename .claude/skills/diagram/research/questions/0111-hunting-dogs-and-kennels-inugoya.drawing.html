How our maps draw kennels (inugoya)

A household on our maps that hunts keeps its dogs in a small kennel of its own, beside the stables in the service yard. That such a household keeps dogs is accurate: hawking, in which dogs drove the game out of the thickets for the hawk, spread widely among the daimyo, and boar was hunted with three or four dogs to a hunter, so a lord who hunts boar the more surely keeps them. The kennel itself - its form, its size and its place - is our own reconstruction.

  • The kennel is a small building about 11 ft square.
    No page we read gives a household's kennel a form or a size, so both are a GUESS.
  • The kennel stands beside the stables.
    Stables and kennel both keep the household's animals, and share the service yard, its well and the service gate, away from the ceremonial ground of the forecourt. No source we read puts the two together, so the seat is a GUESS.
  • The one compound recorded as housing dogs gives the kennel no pattern.
    At Kitami in 1693, in the Edo period, an official's compound kept about forty dogs in a care house, a nursing house and sleeping quarters for sick dogs and pups. That was a shelter for dogs in care, not a hunting kennel, so our maps take neither its buildings nor their sizes.
