How our maps draw an inn

The one inn a town draws is its caravan inn, and its form is not the form of the settlement's houses: it is rolled for each town between a single-story wagon inn and a two-story post-station inn (How our maps place travelers' inns and cheap lodging houses), because the record holds the two models and nothing says how common either was. Whichever form it takes, the inn is drawn as one building on the road.

The rule the map follows:

  • Our maps draw every inn as one building about 66 by 48 ft, a GUESS.
    The footprint is ours, taken from no source. It is wider than the largest Japanese inn the record measures, the Ohashiya at Akasaka, a post town on the Tokaido, about 54 ft wide and 137 ft deep, and far wider than the smaller inns whose frontages are at Travelers' inns and cheap lodging houses (hatago, dian, kichin-yado), a known gap.
  • A post-station inn is drawn two stories, as a Japanese inn was on the road front.
    The sources describe it as a townhouse with rooms to let: a passage with an earth floor to the back, its guest rooms upstairs and behind, and two stories where it faced the road. Our maps draw its height, not that plan.
  • Our maps draw no bath, privy, yard or stable belonging to a post-station inn.
    An inn had a bath and a privy, but where they stood, and the want of a yard or a stable, are a GUESS.
  • A wagon inn is drawn as one single-story building, not as the carters' fenced yard the sources describe, a known gap.
    The Chinese carters' inn was a fenced yard on the road with its lodging houses inside, a large stable and hay stacked in its corners. The stable and the hay rest on one account alone, a self-published one of the cart inns of Northeast China, so they are a GUESS. Our maps draw the wagon inn's stable as a building of its own beside the inn (How our maps place travelers' inns and cheap lodging houses), and no fence or lodging houses around a yard.
  • No size for a Chinese inn comes from a source.
    No page we read gives one, so the wagon inn takes the same guessed footprint as the post-station inn.
