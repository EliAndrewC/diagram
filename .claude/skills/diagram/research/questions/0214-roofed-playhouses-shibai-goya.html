Roofed playhouses (shibai goya)

Roofed theaters with seating, built for kabuki, arose late in the Edo period, centered on Edo, Kyoto and Osaka, and spread to every city after the Meiji restoration. A great city kept a handful, licensed by the authorities and standing together: Edo had three, Osaka five. The oldest that survives, in the temple-gate town of Kotohira, is about 80 by 145 ft.

  • The oldest surviving playhouse measures about 80 by 145 ft.
    The Kanamaruza, in the temple-gate town of Kotohira, measures 13 ken 2 shaku across its front by 24 ken 2 shaku deep at ground level, with a revolving stage 2 ken (about 12 ft) across.
  • Were the great cities' playhouses as large as the one at Kotohira?
    Kotohira's playhouse, the Kanamaruza, was raised in 1835 on the model of the Onishi Shibai, one of the theaters of Dotonbori in Osaka (by one account the Chikugo-za), so its measures are the nearest we have to a city playhouse of its day. No page we read measures one of the city theaters themselves.
  • Before its playhouse was raised in 1835, Kotohira staged its plays in a temporary hut put up for each run.
    It put one up about three times a year.
  • Roofed playhouses spread through the smaller towns in the 1900s.
    After the Meiji restoration playhouses came to be built in every city, and from the 1900s one after another in small and middling towns too.
  • Edo's playhouses were thinned to four by the 1670s and to three from 1714.
    Edo had many playhouses at first. By the 1670s the shogunate permitted only four, and from 1714 three.
  • Osaka's theater district, Dotonbori, had five theaters, licensed from 1653.
    The five began with five theater licenses recognized in 1653.
  • Did a city's playhouses stand together?
    In Edo two of the three faced the same street, a stone's throw apart, and Osaka's five stood together east of the south end of Ebisu bridge.
  • After a fire in 1841, Edo's two burned playhouses and its puppet theaters were moved far outside the outer moat.
    The fire burned the Nakamura-za and the Ichimura-za. In 1842 the shogunate moved them, with the puppet theaters, to a site of over 10,000 tsubo, about 8 acres, at Saruwaka-cho in Asakusa.

In China under the Song dynasty, plays in the great cities were staged in performance halls, the goulan, the chief venues of Song and Yuan drama in the cities. The halls stood inside the entertainment precincts, the wazi.

  • How large was a performance hall in a great city of Song-dynasty China?
    Large enough for audiences of several thousand, says the Chinese Wikipedia article on the halls, but no page we read gives a hall's measures.
  • One entertainment precinct could hold several halls, and a large one more than fifty.
    The Chinese Wikipedia article on the halls counts them by precinct.
