How our maps draw a town's and a city's shrines

Our maps draw no district shrine of a town's own and no principal shrine for a city, though Japan's rules of 1871 gave each household-register district of some twenty villages a district shrine and every Chinese seat of government a City God temple. The game's setting gives a town its monasteries and a city its temples and wayside shrines, and names neither a town's shrine nor a city's principal shrine, and it has no City God (How our maps place shrines, temples and graves in each size of settlement). So none is drawn: a deviation the setting itself makes.

Were the setting to add one, the history suggests it would look like this. No size is given here for a City God temple: the four in the research section ranged too widely to set one.

  • A town's district shrine would stand on 500 tsubo (~17,800 sq ft) or more.
    That is the floor the rules of 1897, in the Meiji period, set for a district shrine.
  • How large would a district shrine's halls be?
    A worship hall some 21 by 18 ft and a sanctuary some 9 by 15 ft, with a gate and subsidiary shrines, as at the district shrine of the town of Sano.
  • Where would a city's principal shrine stand?
    On raised ground near the city's seat of government, as Edo's did: it kept to slightly raised ground each time it was moved outward as the castle grew.

The small shrines drawn among a city's temples, or in a town's temple quarter, follow one rule.

  • How much ground does a small shrine in a town stand on?
    About 3,500 sq ft. The plot is a GUESS from the shrine register of one district, Kanzaki. Most of its shrines are a hamlet's rather than a town's, and its areas are today's.
  • How large are a small shrine's halls?
    A sanctuary about 6 ft square and a worship hall about 18 by 12 ft. These sizes are accurate: they are the halls of the Kanzaki district's shrines.
  • What else stands beside a small shrine's hall?
    At most a shrine office and a subsidiary shrine, as at the Suwa shrine in the temple quarter (teramachi) of Kanazawa, a castle town.
  • Some small shrines stand on a slice of a temple's precinct.
    Another shrine of Kanazawa's temple quarter did.
