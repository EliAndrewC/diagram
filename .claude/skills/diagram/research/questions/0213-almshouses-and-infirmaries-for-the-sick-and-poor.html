Almshouses and infirmaries for the sick and poor

The Song dynasty ordered a poorhouse and an infirmary, the state's relief houses, for every county, and for every walled town or market of a thousand households or more; the infirmaries were run by monks recruited for them. Medicine was sold from official pharmacies.

Fewer are recorded in Japan. A dispensary to give medicine and treatment, and an almshouse to care for the sick, orphans and the poor, were founded at a temple in the eighth century, and in the Edo period the shogun's city opened an infirmary, a single longhouse with beds for forty.

In China, the Song record runs from the capital's almshouses to a law for every county.

  • The Northern Song capital kept four almshouses, feeding three hundred a day.
    The capital had of old an east and a west almshouse for the old, the sick, orphans, the destitute and beggars. The emperor Yingzong added a south and a north one and enlarged the buildings of the first two, and the four together fed three hundred a day.
  • In the early 1100s the Song government set up a poorhouse and an infirmary.
    Early in the Chongning era (1102-1106) the minister Cai Jing set up the juyangyuan, a poorhouse, and the anjifang, an infirmary run by monks recruited for it. A monk who cured a thousand in three years was rewarded with the purple robe.
  • Song law required every county, and every larger town or market with an official, to keep a poorhouse, an infirmary and a pauper cemetery.
    Every walled town, stockade, garrison post and market of a thousand households or more that had a supervising official was to add the three, following the counties.
  • Southern Song Hangzhou's official pharmacies are listed among the city's famous shops.
    A list of the city's famous shops names the prepared-medicine bureau at Huimin ward and the Hemin Huimin pharmacy beside a cooked-meat shop and a private medicine shop.

In Japan the relief houses on record stood in the seats of government.

  • When did Japan first set up a dispensary and an almshouse?
    One chronicle, the Fuso Ryakki, records both set up at the Kofukuji temple in 723, in the Nara period. Another, the Shoku Nihongi, records a dispensary "first set up" in the empress's household in 730.
  • Edo opened an infirmary in 1722 inside the shogunate's herb garden.
    The Koishikawa Yojosho was a shingle-roofed longhouse with two rooms for preparing medicine and meals, and beds for forty, the number later raised as it was needed.
  • How large was a relief house's building?
    No page we read gives a floor area for any of them, in China or in Japan; the most we know is the Edo infirmary's longhouse and its forty beds.
  • Did a provincial castle town keep a relief house?
    No page we read describes one.
