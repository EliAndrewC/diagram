How our maps draw a charcoal kiln

In Japan's hill villages charcoal was burned both for a living and as a side job in the farming slack season, and a kiln excavated in Fukushima was dug into the hillside, its chimney rising up the slope behind it. Our village and hamlet maps with hill wood beyond their fields carry one or two of these kilns, a number that is our GUESS. Each is drawn dug into the slope inside the wood, chimney end uphill, and chamber and chimney together run about 15 ft along the slope.

  • A kiln on our maps belongs to a charcoal burner, not to the village's farmers.
    The setting's notes list charcoal burners among the hinin, so a kiln in a village's wood belongs to them, not to the farming households that burned charcoal in the slack season in Japan's hill villages. That is a DEVIATION from the historical record.
  • A kiln's chamber is drawn 10 by 7 ft, the small end of the recorded range.
    The measured chambers run from about 10 by 7 ft at Date City, in Fukushima, to about 13 by 9 ft elsewhere in Fukushima. The Date kiln is dated to the late 16th or early 17th century, so our maps take its size.
  • The chamber is drawn as a low earth hump over the slope.
    The kiln was dug into the hillside, and the hump is a map drawing convention for a chamber under the ground.
  • A kiln's chimney trench is drawn about 8 ft long, running up the slope from the chamber's back.
    The measured trench, 2.36 m (~8 ft) long, rose from the chamber's back at 40 to 60 degrees, so the kiln's long axis lies down the fall line.
  • How many kilns does a map show?
    None on a map with no hill wood; otherwise one, and a second on about one map in three, chosen at random for each settlement, more likely the more hill wood it has. The count and the rate are a GUESS. The district counts of 1936 are for whole administrative villages, so they set no number for one map.

The rule the map follows: a charcoal kiln is a chamber about 10 ft by 7 ft dug into the slope and drawn as a low earth hump over it (a map drawing convention), with a chimney trench about 8 ft long running up the slope from its back, set inside hill woodland with its long axis down the fall line and a path to it from the nearest lane. Zero kilns on a map with no hill wood; otherwise one, and a second on about one map in three - the count and the rate are a GUESS.
