Studies and detached studies (shosai)

A study (shosai) was a room in a private house for reading and writing, and it could be a building of its own. A scholar's detached study survives in Kyoto, and in China the study drew apart from the living rooms into a quiet corner of the compound, beside its rear garden where there was one.

  • A study was whichever room or building held the built-in writing desk.
    That room or building, the one with the tsuke-shoin, was the study, and was called a shoin or a gakumonsho, "place of learning". Either could be a room or a building made for the purpose.
  • Rai San'yō's detached study, built late in the Edo period, survives in Kyoto.
    Rai San'yō was a Confucian scholar, poet and historian of the late Edo period. In 1828 he built a new study-cum-tea room, Sanshi Suimei-sho, on his Kyoto estate. The estate passed out of the family after his death, and by about the middle of the Meiji period its other buildings were lost, so the study alone stands. Its garden is on its west side, and there is none to its east.

The study had a history in China too.

  • In China the study drew apart from the living rooms, into a quiet part of the compound, beside the rear garden where there was one.
    Over time it separated from the living rooms and took on a function and a layout of its own. Its usual place was a quiet part of the house compound, and where there was a rear garden it always stood next to it.

Both of these examples are private homes: a scholar's estate in Kyoto, and a Chinese house compound.
