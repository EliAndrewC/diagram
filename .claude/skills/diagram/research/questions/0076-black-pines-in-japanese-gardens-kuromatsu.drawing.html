How our maps draw black pines in Japanese gardens

A residence's inner garden on our building plans holds a cluster of old pines, drawn as three crowns and labeled. Pines in such a garden are accurate: the black pine was the Japanese garden's lead tree, and a pine was planted by the wall to be seen from outside. The size of their crowns is a GUESS.

  • Our maps draw each pine's crown 4 to 6 ft wide, a GUESS.
    That reads as young or closely pruned trees. No page we read gives the crown of a pruned garden pine, so the size is our own.
  • An old garden's pines were probably wider than we draw them, a GUESS.
    A residence garden kept up for generations would hold older trees whose crowns, even pruned, would be wider. Our own guess for a mature garden pine is a crown of 15 to 30 ft; no page we read gives one.
