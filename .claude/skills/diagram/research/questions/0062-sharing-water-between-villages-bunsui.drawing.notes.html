• nagdoren-usonokuchi - 「From that time on, between the former Kami village, where the water source lay, and the former Nakahata village, where the intake lay, water disputes arose without end.」 (translated; ) 「every year at the eighty-eighth night ... the seals were broken on two closely guarded wisteria vines, one long and one short, measuring sticks were made to each length, and at a plunge pool partway along the channel water mouths matching the sticks' lengths were made with sandbags to divide the water」 (translated; ) 「it was long continued until the Usonokuchi circular divider was completed in Showa 28 [1953].」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-bunsui - 「A technical means used when dividing water from one channel between two areas or villages: a graduated stake is set at the division point, and division by its height is the bungi.」 (translated; ) (the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, older edition, entry 分木 (bungi, "dividing timber"), as excerpted on this page)
  • kuji-entobunsui-jawiki - 「Before the cylinder divider was built, the Kuji bunryo-hi, to divide the Nikaryo canal's water in the four directions branching from here according to the cultivated area of each basin, was built in the mid-Edo period」 (translated; ) 「but even after that the disputes over the amount of water never ceased」 (translated; )
  • cylinder-divider-jawiki - 「from the Taisho era, dividers capable of accurate distribution were devised」 (translated; ) 「The first cylinder divider was invented by Kaji Kan'ichi and installed in 1914 (Taisho 3) at Koizumi village (Kani district, Gifu)」 (translated; )
  • tabayashi-1987 - 「The smallest ditches bringing water to the inlets of paddy fields are controlled by small groups of farmers, and the ditches are often considered parts of the paddy fields they serve.」 「With rotation irrigation, the head of the village directs water delivery to all paddy fields.」 「In the large systems, a hierarchical structure of water management cooperatives and water delivery methods that accords with the branchlike network of canals was established in the Edo period (1600 to 1867 A.D.).」
