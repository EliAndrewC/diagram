How our maps draw the river watch post (funabansho)

At an official's landing, as at Hayakawa, our maps draw a small watch hut at the head of the landing, manned from the magistrate's office to inspect and record the boats, people and goods that come in. Keeping a watch at an office's own landing is a GUESS: no page we read describes one. The hut is the one at the river's edge of the Nakagawa post, which guarded the river way into the shogun's city, not the whole fenced post.

  • Our maps keep the fenced compound for a checkpoint on the way into a capital.
    The Nakagawa post's compound of about 155 by 100 ft guarded the people and goods entering and leaving the shogun's city, so a local office's landing keeps only the hut.
  • The hut's size is our own guess.
    No page we read gives the size of a river watch hut; our maps draw it about 12 by 10 ft.
  • The watch takes no toll.
    The domains' boat watch posts collected taxes as well as inspecting boats. This setting taxes goods where they are sold, not where they pass through, so a river watch on our maps takes no toll from boats passing by - a deliberate deviation from the domains' posts.
