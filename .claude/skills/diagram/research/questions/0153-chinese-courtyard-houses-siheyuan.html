Chinese courtyard houses (siheyuan)

The Chinese courtyard house (siheyuan) closes a yard with ranges - buildings each one row of rooms - on all four sides: the main range on the north, wings east and west, and a range on the south facing into the yard. It was usually home to a large family, and its owner built as many courts as the ground and the household called for, from one to three or four; in Beijing the deepest ran from one lane to the next, some 250 ft.

In the old town of Qixian the same form served the merchants, the shop being the building on the street. The Japanese townhouse is a different building, not the same house in another style: it is at Merchants' townhouses (machiya).

  • Technically, a courtyard house is a three-sided house closed on its fourth side by a row of rooms holding the gate.
    The Chinese Wikipedia article on the courtyard house calls it, technically, a variant of the three-sided house (sanheyuan), closed by a further range holding the gate. The three-sided house of a Chinese farm is at Farmhouses (minka)
  • The smallest courtyard house has one court.
    Through the street gate you are straight in the yard, and single-story ranges stand around it on a central axis: the main range on the north, the wings east and west, and the south range, whose doors open north into the yard.
  • The complete courtyard house has three courts.
    The Chinese Wikipedia article on the courtyard house gives three courts as the complete form.
  • A courtyard house was usually home to a large family.
    The Chinese Wikipedia article on the courtyard house says so of the form as a whole.
  • A courtyard house had as many courts as its owner's ground and household called for.
    A house could be as small as a single court, as large as three or four, or two houses wide with side courts.
  • In Beijing the deepest courtyard house ran from one lane to the next, about 77 m (~253 ft).
    That is the distance between two of the city's lanes (hutong).
  • No page we read says which households lived in a one-court house, or how wide and deep one was.
    The Chinese Wikipedia articles on the courtyard house, the three-sided house and the old town of Pingyao give neither.
  • On the old street of Qixian, an old town in China, most shops are courtyard houses of one court or several.
    The shops on both sides of the old street mostly follow the northern plan of the courtyard house.
  • In Qixian a shop's building on the street is mostly two stories, the shop below and a storeroom above.
    The ground floor held the shop and its workshops and the upper floor a storeroom: the shop in front and the workshop behind, with lodging.
