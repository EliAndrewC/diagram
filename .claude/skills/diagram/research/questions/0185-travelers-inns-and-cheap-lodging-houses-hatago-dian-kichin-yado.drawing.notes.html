  • kotobank-umayado - the Japanese dictionary Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten defines the sense, 「when a person traveling on their own horse lodges at an inn, the inn's taking the horse into its keeping; also, an inn having such facilities」 (translated; ), and cites as the word's earliest attested example, from a novel of 1913, 「the front is a travelers' inn, and at the back there is a large stable, and it also does umayado」 (translated; ). The definition bears the institution; only the 1913 sentence bears the stable-behind arrangement, and the earliest-example label means the compilers found no older written use of the sense. The older senses on the same page are 「a place that keeps horses ready for use as post horses and relay horses」 (translated; ), earliest example from a haikai collection of 1677, and 「laborers working at an umayado; packhorse drivers」 (translated; ), earliest example 1682.
