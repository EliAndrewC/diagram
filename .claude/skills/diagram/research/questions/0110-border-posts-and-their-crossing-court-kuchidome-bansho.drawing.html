How our maps draw a border crossing court

A border post on our maps keeps a court behind its gate, and in this setting that court is also where a delegation from across the clan border is received. The court itself follows the history; that use of it is this project's own.

  • Drawing the court as the ground travelers crossed, between the gate and the inspection hall, is accurate to the surviving post at Nuruyu.
    At the surviving Nuruyu post the road entered by the gate and ran on in front of the inspection hall that faced it, so the ground between gate and hall was the crossing ground.
  • Giving the post officer's house a formal reception room is accurate: the surviving post at Nuruyu, plain as it is, has one.
    Nuruyu's officer's house is a farmhouse in form, but it has a reception room in the shoin style.
  • Keeping the court for delegations of rank from across the border is a deliberate deviation.
    Receiving a neighboring clan's delegations there with ceremony is this setting's own use of the court, built on the crossing ground: no page we read describes a border post that kept a court for receiving a neighboring domain's officials.
