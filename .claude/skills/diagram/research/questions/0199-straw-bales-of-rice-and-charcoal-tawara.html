Straw bales of rice and charcoal (tawara)

A tawara is a straw bale: straw woven into a cylinder, used to carry and store rice and other grains, salt, fish, charcoal and coal. It was also a measure, and from the Sengoku period to the end of the Edo period in 1868 a bale of rice held anything from 2 to 5 to - a to being a tenth of a koku - by time and place. A rice bale of today's size is about 2.5 ft long and 1.5 ft across; no bale from before 1868 was found measured.

The same bales carried a domain's tax rice to its granary and its barges (Storehouses for the tax rice) and a town's charcoal to its yards, where it was weighed (Charcoal yards and charcoal stores).

  • The bale was also a measure for goods other than rice.
    The bale as a unit was used for charcoal, salt, cotton and other goods traded in bales as well as for rice.
  • Not every bale was round.
    In the Iburi district of Hokkaido, a great charcoal district, the charcoal was shipped in square bales.
  • A bale of rice held no standard amount before 1868.
    From the Sengoku to the Edo period its measure differed by time and place: the shogunate's bale held 3 to and 5 sho, Kaga domain's 5 to. The bales themselves came in 4-to, 6-to and other sizes. A history of units lists the Edo bales of 3 to 3 sho 3 go, of 4 to and of 5 to, which one a domain used differing by domain.
  • The 60 kg (~132 lb) bale of rice became the standard after 1868.
    The prefectural rice-inspection rules of the Meiji period made the 4-to bale the rule, and since a to of rice weighs about 15 kg (~33 lb), that bale weighs about 60 kg. It is still the bale of today's rice trade.
  • How heavy was a bale of charcoal?
    It depended on the grade, in the one district we found. In the Iburi district the best charcoal was packed at 4 kan (about 15 kg, ~33 lb), good charcoal at 10 kan (37.5 kg, ~83 lb), and a lower grade at 8 kan (30 kg, ~66 lb), rolled in straw mats. The article gives no date for it.

What a bale held is on record from the Edo period; what one measured is known only from modern bales.

  • How big is today's 60 kg rice bale?
    About 2.5 ft long and 1.5 ft across, by the one we found measured: a rice shop's hollow display bale of that size is 75 cm (~30 in) long and 47 cm (~19 in) across.
  • The one charcoal bale we found measured, from the mid-twentieth century, is about 2 by 1.3 by 1 ft.
    A charcoal bale of the mid-twentieth century in the Edo-Tokyo Museum measures 63 cm (~25 in) by 39 cm (~15 in) by 32 cm (~13 in).
  • No bale from before 1868, of rice or of charcoal, was found measured.
    The Edo-period bale is on record by what it held, not by its size.
  • How big was a rice bale of the Edo period?
    For the bales on record by name, within a tenth of today's, on this project's GUESS. A bale woven to the modern bale's proportions changes its length with the cube root of what it holds: the shogunate's 3 to 5 sho bale would be about 2.4 ft long and 1.5 ft across, a 5-to bale about 2.7 by 1.7 ft.
