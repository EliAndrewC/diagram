Border posts and their crossing court (kuchidome bansho)

A border post on our maps keeps a court behind its gate, and in Rokugan that court is also where a delegation from across the clan border is received with ceremony. The court itself follows the history; receiving a neighboring clan there is this setting's own, and the research below shows what real border posts held.

In the Edo period each domain kept guard posts, the kuchidome bansho, where its roads crossed into a neighboring domain or the shogun's land. They watched the people and goods going in and out - above all to stop the domain's produce leaving - and sometimes taxed the goods at a fixed rate.

The post at Nuruyu, one of the few whose buildings survive, was laid out around the road itself: whoever crossed came in through its gate and passed before the hall where travelers and goods were examined.

  • Few border posts survive; the Nuruyu post of Sendai domain is one.
    Its buildings of the time still stand, which is rare for such a post anywhere in the country.
  • At the Nuruyu border post, the road ran through the gate and past the front of the inspection hall.
    The post commanded a mountain gorge where the road left Sendai for Akita domain, and its front gate stood across the road itself. Facing it was the inspection hall (kendansho, where travelers and goods were examined), and the officer's residence stood to the right, behind the hall. The road entered by the gate and ran on in front of the hall.
  • The officer's house at the Nuruyu border post is a farmhouse in form, but with a formal reception room.
    It measures 23.19 m (~76 ft) by 11.86 m (~39 ft), with an earth-floored side like any farmhouse, but it is furnished with a reception room in the shoin style.
  • Did a border post have a court for receiving officials from the neighboring domain?
    The Nuruyu officer's house had a formal reception room, but no page we read describes a court or hall where officials from across the border were received. The only open ground the records describe is the road inside the gate, where those crossing passed.
  • The shogunate's own checkpoint at Hakone held far more than the Nuruyu post.
    Standing on the Tokaido from 1619 to 1869, it held within its fence a front post with an upper guardroom, a guards' room, a rest room and a bath; an opposite post with a lockup; stables, a street watch and a notice board.
