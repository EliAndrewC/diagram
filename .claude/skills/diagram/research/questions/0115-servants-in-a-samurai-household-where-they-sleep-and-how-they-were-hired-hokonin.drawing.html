How our maps draw servants' quarters in the samurai ward

On our maps a samurai household's servants never get a house of their own. A large household's servants are drawn in a range - a long, narrow building under one roof - on its street edge, as servants historically lived in the ranges along a residence's street front. A small household's are not drawn at all: a smaller house had a wall in place of a range, and its servants are taken to sleep in the house the map already draws.

  • A servant dwelling in a samurai ward is never a freestanding house.
    It is lodging bound to the samurai household it serves, drawn on that household's street edge as a long, thin range in the proportion of a long-house gate, which was a range with a gate cut through it.
  • Our maps draw the range about 15 ft deep, a guess of our own.
    That is the depth this project takes for an ordinary retainer's range, a GUESS: no page we read measures one.
  • Our maps draw no servant building for the smallest households.
    Their few servants are taken to sleep under the main roof, a GUESS, so they are inside the house the map draws.
  • The setting's provincial city attaches 72 servant families to its 60 samurai families.
    By the setting's budget for a provincial city, 72 of its 120 servant families are attached to samurai households - 30 to wealthier samurai (the setting's property rating 2) and 42 indentured to poorer ones (property rating 1) - against 60 samurai families. They are property-holding families in the setting's economy, so each is housed, counted and taxed.
  • Servants' houses standing apart would read as commoners' houses inside the samurai ward.
    A row of servant houses unattached to any samurai household reads as more commoners' houses in the samurai neighborhood - exactly what the fence around a samurai ward exists to keep out. So a servants' range is drawn only against the household it serves.
  • The street-facing range is the house's wall.
    The street-facing walls of a large house were the ranges its servants lived in, so where a samurai house inside a city's wall is enclosed by rank (How our maps draw the samurai quarter and count its households (bukechi)), its servants' range is drawn as part of that enclosure, along the street line.
