How our maps draw a clan border

A clan border is drawn on our maps as a line with no width: it takes up no ground, and nothing on the map need stand clear of it. A border was a line the lords of two neighboring domains had agreed, and Edo-period provincial maps drew their district boundaries as lines; our maps follow that convention.

  • Our maps draw a clan border as a line, leaving out the mounds that marked it.
    In the Edo period, large border markers were lines of earth mounds, large and sparse at first, smaller and closer together later. Leaving them out is a deliberate deviation. A mound is a structure, which everything else on the map would then have to stay clear of - the opposite of what a frontier magistracy wants, since its whole design stands its east wall on the line.
  • Our maps give the boundary pillars at the Ubame magistracy about 1 to 1.5 ft of shaft, up to about 3 ft with a plinth.
    No source we read gives a pillar's size. This is our own estimate: a GUESS. The pillars mark where the line runs and are not a structure to keep clear of.
  • A frontier magistracy's parley room stands on the border line.
    The room has the line across its floor and kneeling mats on each side. It says in a building what the drawn line says on the map: a border exists where two authorities have agreed it. How the room is drawn is at How our maps draw a parley room on a border.
