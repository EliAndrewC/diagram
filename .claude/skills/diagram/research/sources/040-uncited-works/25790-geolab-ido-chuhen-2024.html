Fujiwara Yasushi, "井戸の話 あれこれ(中編)" ("all about wells, middle part", title translated), in the "やわらかサイエンス" ("soft science") column of the Geoscience Research Laboratory (地層科学研究所), March 2024 (in Japanese; https://www.geolab.jp/documents/science/science-158/)

What it is: A short column by a geotechnical software firm on how wells are dug and on castle wells. It sets out three modern drilling methods, the Kazusa-bori of Chiba, the spiral-ramp maimai-zu wells of the Musashino plateau, and why Japanese castles always had a well.

Why it applies, and its limits: It adds to the record that in the mid-Edo period iron-rod drilled flowing wells reached about 30 m and that in Kazusa an oak-rod method, which became Kazusa-bori with its jointed bamboo strips, let a few workers drill to 100 m, and that the maimai-zu well of the Musashino plateau was dug through thick volcanic ash down to the water layer. It dates only the 30 m iron-rod wells (to mid-Edo) and gives no date for the oak-rod method or Kazusa-bori, so the 100 m depth cannot be taken as an Edo-period figure; it gives no site figures; the drilling methods it opens with are modern and do not bear on the setting.
