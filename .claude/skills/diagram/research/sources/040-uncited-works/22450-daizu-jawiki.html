daizu-jawiki

ja.wikipedia ダイズ ("daizu", the soybean, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大豆)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the soybean, covering the crop's growth and rotation needs, its origin in East Asia and the early finds in Japan, its spread to the world, world production and trade, its uses and the foods made from it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the crop's habits: it is injured by growing it in the same ground year after year and should be rotated, it takes more nitrogen from the soil than it returns (the page says one soybean crop uses as much as six rice crops when only straw is plowed in), and it is poorly suited to wet ground, so rice-field rotations raise the ridges. It dates Japanese soybeans to the Middle Jomon period and gives the processed foods (tofu, miso, soy sauce, natto), but its production and trade tables are 1961 to 2019 and say nothing about premodern field sizes or yields.
