Wenxiu Chi, Tobias Plieninger, Guangsi Lin and Koushik Chowdhury, "Governing Landscape Simplification in Rapid Commercialization Contexts - the Case of the Dike-Pond System in the Pearl River Delta, China", International Journal of the Commons 18(1), 260-275, 2024 (https://storage.googleapis.com/jnl-up-j-ijc-files/journals/1/articles/1300/66151cd3a6d55.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a peer-reviewed article on how the cooperatives of Ruxi village in the Pearl River Delta govern their dike-pond system, from 17 interviews, and how that governance relates to the loss of cropped dikes in favor of intensive fish ponds since the 1980s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the dike-pond system in the delta has a history of more than 600 years, with ponds for fish and dikes for feed grass or crops, and it gives the dikes' former width as 20 m eroded to under 4 m, ponds averaging 0.3 ha, and mulberry and sugar cane as the original dike crops. Its measurements are of Ruxi village in 2020 and its comparison of the 1980s and 2020s, a market economy of high-value fish, so the old form is shown only by what interviewees and the 1980s photograph record, and its governance findings are those of modern cooperatives.
