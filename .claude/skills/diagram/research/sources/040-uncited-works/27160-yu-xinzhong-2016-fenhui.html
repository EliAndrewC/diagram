Yu Xinzhong (余新忠), "南方为什么比北方更卫生" ("Why the south was cleaner than the north", title translated), an excerpt from 清代卫生防疫机制及其近代演变 (Beijing Normal University Press, April 2016), on 澎湃新闻 (The Paper), 私家历史, 2016-06-03 (in Chinese; https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1473300)

What it is: An excerpt from a historian's book on Qing sanitation, setting out how Ming and Qing cities handled human waste: Beijing's night-soil depots and ditch-clearing in spring, and the Jiangnan towns' private latrines, night-soil boats and sold collecting rights.

Why it applies, and its limits: It documents that waste in Ming and Qing China was handled by the market and not by officials, that Jiangnan homes had their own latrines and shed-covered manure houses while northern homes often had none, that collectors held assigned street sections, and that Beijing's ditches were cleared once a year in the second month. Its evidence runs from the late Ming through the late Qing and includes the author's own 1980s memory of one small town, it is about cities and their country trade rather than villages, and the author says existing studies give no clear account of how the system worked.
