uno-2018-niwaki-no-hoi

Uno Shinichi, "庭木の方位" ("niwaki no hoi", garden trees and compass direction, title translated), garden-culture study group of Shimane, on peshimane.net (in Japanese; https://peshimane.net/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/2018-04_庭木の方位（宇野-真一）.pdf)

What it is: A short study that collects what three Japanese garden books (Sakuteiki, Sansui narabi ni yagyo no zu, Tsukiyama teizoden maihen of 1735) and house-direction (kaso) practice say about which trees to plant on which side of a garden or house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the Sakuteiki's four-direction tree rules (willow east, catalpa west, katsura (桂) south, cypress north where river, road, pond or hill is missing) and the mid-Muromachi book's line that bamboo is planted to the north, and lists kaso verdicts by direction, so it is a source for the direction lore behind garden planting. The author finds the 1735 book largely free of geomantic taboo, says he cannot sort the kaso rules into sound and superstitious, the Sakuteiki describes the gardens of shinden-style mansions and the second book gardens from the Kamakura period on, not farm homesteads; and the kaso verdicts are kaso as practised now, which the author thinks is mostly derived from the Book of Changes, with no date for any of them.
