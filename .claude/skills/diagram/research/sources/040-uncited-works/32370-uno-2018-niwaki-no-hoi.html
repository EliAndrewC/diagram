uno-2018-niwaki-no-hoi

Uno Shinichi, "庭木の方位" ("niwaki no hoi", garden trees and compass direction, title translated), garden-culture study group of Shimane, on peshimane.net (in Japanese; https://peshimane.net/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/2018-04_庭木の方位（宇野-真一）.pdf)

What it is: A short study that collects what three Japanese garden books (Sakuteiki, Sansui narabi ni yagyo no zu, Tsukiyama teizoden maihen of 1735) and house-direction (kaso) practice say about which trees to plant on which side of a garden or house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the Sakuteiki's four-direction tree rules (willow east, catalpa west, cassia south, cypress north where river, road, pond or hill is missing) and the late-Muromachi book's line that bamboo is planted to the north, and lists kaso verdicts by direction, so it is a source for the direction lore behind garden planting. The author finds the 1735 book largely free of geomantic taboo, says he cannot sort the kaso rules into sound and superstitious, and the first two books describe aristocratic gardens, not farm homesteads.
