toyo-keiki-scales

Toyo Keiki Co., "『東洋計量史資料館』 秤" ("Toyo Museum of Measurement History: balances", title translated), collection page on the company's site (in Japanese; https://www.toyo-keiki.co.jp/toyokeiryoushi/collection/scale/scale.html)

What it is: A company museum's catalogue of balances, each entry giving name, date, maker, use and a short note, from Chinese portable scales (one dated to the Tang) and Edo-period silver and rod scales by the Jin and Shuzui houses to Meiji and twentieth-century scales and foreign examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates Edo-period scales by maker (1611 to 1818; the page's 1857 is how long one Jin scale made in 1663 stayed in use) and names their uses: silver scales and rod scales for gold, silver, tobacco, face powder and medicines, and simple rod scales for grain and livestock. Most entries give no size, many give no year, and the catalogue spans Pompeii to 1982 England, so only its Japanese and Chinese entries bear on the setting.
