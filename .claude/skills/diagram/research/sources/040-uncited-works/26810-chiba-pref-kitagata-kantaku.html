Chiba Prefecture, Kaiso Agricultural Office, "干潟干拓の歴史" ("History of the Higata reclamation", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.pref.chiba.lg.jp/ap-kaisou/kaisou/kibanseibi/higata.html)

What it is: A prefectural page on the drained lakebed of Higata (干潟) in Chiba, the about 5,100 ha of farmland across Asahi, Sosa and Tonosho called "Higata 80,000 koku" (干潟八万石), reclaimed from a lake in 1670, with the new channel, the drainage ditches and the encircling combined ditch, the water shortages that followed, and the Taisho and 1935 irrigation works.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a case of a large lake drained in the 1670s in the Boso area: the area (about 5,100 ha), the date (1670), the new river cut by two undertakers, and the ring ditch and ponds, and it says that the new fields did not get enough water, and that cutting the new river lowered the groundwater of paddies standing higher than the reclaimed land (the page ties this to a water problem downstream). It is a short summary, with no yields or settlement sizes. It does not say whether its 5,100 ha is the area reclaimed in 1670 or today's farmland after land consolidation, and the later works it describes (the widened river and weirs from 1923, the Tone River pumping and tunnel works from 1935) are modern.
