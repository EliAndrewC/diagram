kotobank-daikan-2

"代官" ("daikan", the deputy or intendant, title translated), entries in the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Suzuki Kunihiro, Murakami Sunao and Ono Mizuo), Nipponica (by Murakami Sunao), Yamakawa's Japanese History Dictionary and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BB%A3%E5%AE%98-90927)

What it is: A long set of encyclopedia entries on the daikan, from a lord's deputy in the Heian estates and Kamakura and Muromachi eras to the Edo shogunate's intendants. The Heibonsha entry on the early modern daikan gives their numbers (about 70 to 80 at first, 40 to 50 later), their usual domain of 50,000 to 100,000 koku, their stipend of 150 bales, their staff, and the records of an intendant's office.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports who ran a lord's directly held land and what the office did: collect the land tax, relay orders, keep household registers, hear suits and arrest criminals, from a headquarters (jinya) in the district, with a staff of a dozen or so clerks. But it also says that from the 1680s many shogunal intendants lived in Edo and changed posts every year or two until the Tenpo reform ordered them back to their jinya, that those near Edo kept their office in the city, and that an intendant seldom toured his villages, ruling them by documents through the village officials. Most of it describes the shogunate's own intendants and their reforms of 1725 and later, and says only briefly that domain daikan were smaller, worked under county magistrates, and had a narrower reach.
