ja.wikipedia 彦根城 ("Hikone-jo", Hikone Castle, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/彦根城)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Hikone Castle in Omi, seat of the Ii domain, giving its building history from 1603 to 1622, its plan and surviving towers, gates and stable, its designation as a national treasure and special historic site, and a long account of the campaign for World Heritage listing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a large lord's castle as built: a hill castle of keep, inner and outer baileys with the residence in the second bailey, and two-storey and three-storey turrets, with stones and buildings said to be moved from other Omi castles. It says the Ii domain rose to 350,000 koku, and that in peacetime the castle became a place of domain administration and tax-rice storage, with the chief retainer, who held 10,000 koku and had no separate compound, working in the west-bailey turret about twenty days a month. It is the seat of a top-ranking fudai domain, so it is not typical of a small domain, and its present buildings are in part reconstructed. Much of the page is the heritage campaign, tourism and film use, which bear on nothing in the setting.
