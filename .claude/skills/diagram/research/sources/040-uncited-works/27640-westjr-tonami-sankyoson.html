westjr-tonami-sankyoson

West Japan Railway Company, "暮らしに息づく伝統文化を探る 富山県砺波市 散居村" (Exploring traditional culture alive in daily life: the scattered settlements of Tonami, Toyama, title translated), B-Signal no. 162 (in Japanese; https://www.westjr.co.jp/company/info/issue/bsignal/15_vol_162/issue/01.html)

What it is: A railway company's travel-magazine feature on the dispersed farmsteads of the Tonami plain in Toyama, each ringed by a 'kainyo' windbreak wood, with an interview of a member of the volunteer Tonami Kainyo Club.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the form was in place by 1619 according to the Kaga domain's household and tax-assessment book for the district, that farms were first sited on slightly higher ground within the alluvial fan once the Sho river was embanked, with fields lying around each house for easy work and water control, and that the wood served as a windbreak, shade and a source of fuel and building timber. It reports about 7,000 farms now against over 10,000 before, and a farm-front ritual stand in the paddy; it is a visitor feature quoting one interviewee, gives no wood sizes or spacing, and it says the plain was opened further through the Meiji era and that by the mid-Showa period its fields and canals had been modernized into a geometric grid, so the present field pattern is not the Edo-period one.
