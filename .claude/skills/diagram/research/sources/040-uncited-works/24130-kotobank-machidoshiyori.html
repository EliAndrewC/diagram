kotobank-machidoshiyori

Yoshihara Kenichiro, "町年寄" ("machidoshiyori", the town elder), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典), with the Shogakukan, Yamakawa, Britannica and Obunsha entries on the same page, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/町年寄)

What it is: A page of Japanese encyclopedia entries on the machidoshiyori, the Edo-period town officials: the three hereditary houses over Edo's ward headmen, Osaka's equivalent offices, and the Kyoto town elder of a single block.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the title and rank differed by city: in Edo three houses (Taruya, Nara-ya, Kitamura) ranked above the ward headmen and under the town magistrate, while in Osaka the soudoshiyori was the equivalent and the machidoshiyori sat at the level of Edo's headmen, and in Kyoto each block had its own elder, who changed every few months to three years. It describes duties and income, not a settlement's layout, and for other cities gives only the names the post went by (machidoshiyori in Kofu, Fukui and Nagasaki, machidai in Nagoya, kimoiri in Kanazawa, shoya in Okazaki, among others), not their duties.
