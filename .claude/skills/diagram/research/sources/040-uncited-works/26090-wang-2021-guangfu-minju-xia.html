Wang He (王河), "湾区视野下的岭南民居之二：广府民居（下）" ("Lingnan dwellings in the Greater Bay Area, part 2: Guangfu dwellings, second half", title translated), 5 March 2021, in the series Lingnan Architecture in the Greater Bay Area, on the Nanyue Ancient Post Road Network (in Chinese; http://www.infonht.cn/ViewMessage.aspx?MessageId=10786&ColumnId=21)

What it is: A magazine-style essay by an architect on the layout of Guangfu (Cantonese-speaking Guangzhou-area) houses and ancestral halls: comb-pattern villages, lanes, three-room two-gallery houses, garden houses with fruit trees, and the 'routes', 'courts' and gate bays of ancestral halls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Guangfu villages were laid out in a comb pattern with lanes along the wind and slope, lanes of 1.2 to 2 m for shade, houses with side doors, a drying yard at the pond edge, and that most rural families lived in single three-room two-gallery houses; for halls it says one or three routes were usual, five were very rare (two known), and a three-bay gate was commonest in Guangzhou prefecture. It draws on a 2010 thesis and the author's own work, and describes surviving Qing-period buildings, so it speaks for the Pearl River delta, not northern China.
