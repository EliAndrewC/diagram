kinsei-izen-ido

"井戸" ("Wells", title translated), the wells ranked B or higher in the prefectural data lists, on Kinsei-izen (近世以前の遺跡・遺構) (in Japanese; https://www.kinsei-izen.com/ranking/73_ido.html)

What it is: A long table of surviving historic wells across Japan from Akita to Okinawa, ordered by prefecture. Each row gives the name, location, form, size where known, date (often a tradition), designation, condition, notes on the legend or construction, and the site's own ratings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows the range of well forms a premodern place could hold: stone-lined and hollowed-stone curbs, the spiral pit wells (mai-mai-zu) of the Musashino gravel plateau, and the Ryukyuan spring wells; it also gives sizes for many, such as the Omeshinmachi pit well at 33 by 22 m and 7 m deep. Many dates are legends of a founding saint or hero, which the table itself marks as tradition, and a number of the listed curbs are later renewals or restorations, so a date is not the age of the stone.
