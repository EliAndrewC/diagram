suido-ishizue-kazusabori

Agriculture and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), "井戸を掘る" ("Digging wells", title translated), section "上総掘りの特徴" ("Features of Kazusa drilling"), in the site Suido no Ishizue (水土の礎, "Foundations of Water and Soil") (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part3/01/08.html)

What it is: A short page on Kazusa-bori, a method of drilling flowing wells that arose in the Kazusa region of Chiba in the middle of the Meiji era, drilling with a rope of split moso bamboo (hige) instead of iron or oak rods.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows what the earlier method cost - rods of iron or oak that took great effort to haul up - and what the bamboo rope changed, a crew of 2 to 3 reaching depths of 400 to 500 m or more, and that finished wells were lined with madake bamboo pipe. The method is dated to mid-Meiji, after the 1868 cut-off, and the page does not say how wells were dug in Edo times, so it does not show that this rig was available to a premodern village.
