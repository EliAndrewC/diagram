nagoya-registered-cultural-properties

Nagoya City, "国登録文化財" ("Nationally registered cultural properties", title translated), Nagoya City official website, Cultural Properties Protection Division (in Japanese; https://www.city.nagoya.jp/kyoiku/page/0000008473.html)

What it is: A city list, current to 15 August 2024, of the buildings and structures in Nagoya on the national register of tangible cultural properties, with each entry's date, address, owner and registration date, and one registered monument, Tsuruma Park, opened in 1909.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the surviving registered buildings of a castle-town city: most are Meiji to Showa, with a few from the Edo period, such as a Choboji temple kitchen hall of 1828, Shunkoin's main hall and gate of 1830, a brewery's main house and storehouses of the late Edo period, and the main hall of the temple 善行寺 of 1706 (altered in 1804 and 1976, enlarged in 1915). It is a list, with no plans, sizes or descriptions, and covers only what survives and was registered, so it shows when buildings date and which kinds survive, not what the Edo city held. Several entries were moved from elsewhere or rebuilt (the Matabei, the former Sakagami house, came from the Hida region), and many of its Edo-dated buildings stand in outlying wards such as Midori-ku Odaka-cho and Meito-ku Takabari, not the old castle-town centre.
