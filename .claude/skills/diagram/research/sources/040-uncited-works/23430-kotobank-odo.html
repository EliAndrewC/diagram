kotobank-odo

"大戸" ("oodo", the large door, title translated), from the Daijisen and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A4%A7%E6%88%B8-450323)

What it is: A short Kotobank page of dictionary glosses on the word 大戸. For the house it means the large door at a house's front entrance; the other senses are a heavy drinker and the top class of household under the ritsuryo law.

Why it applies, and its limits: It confirms that a large door on the front of a house was a recognized item with its own name, and its one dated example, from the 1540 diary of the Ishiyama Hongan-ji, uses it for a gate-like door in a fence on a temple courtyard. It gives no size, no construction and no place, and most of the page is about the other meanings (the ritsuryo class of a household with eight or more adult men, and a hard drinker).
