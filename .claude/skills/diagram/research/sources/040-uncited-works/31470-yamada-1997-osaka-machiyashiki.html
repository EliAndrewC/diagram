yamada-1997-osaka-machiyashiki

Yamada Shinobu, "近世大坂における商人の町屋敷所持 - 加嶋屋長田家を事例として" ("Merchants' ownership of town house lots in early modern Osaka: the case of the Kashimaya Nagata house", title translated), Ochanomizu Chiri (お茶の水地理) no. 38, 1997 (in Japanese; https://teapot.lib.ocha.ac.jp/record/35823/files/003806.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a journal paper on the town house lots (machiyashiki) held by the Kashimaya Nagata family, a finance merchant house based only in Osaka, drawn from the family's papers (its deeds of sale, its ward-agent bonds and extracts of its accounts) and the wards' religious-census registers of 1864-1870. It tabulates more than 50 lots bought from 1729 on, by ward, by period and by use (rented houses, storehouses, riverside barn lots), and the few pledged lots it kept when loans on them failed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows what a rich merchant house of a large Edo-period city did with its lots: most were bought and kept as rented houses, storehouses and barn lots near the river, and the main house's lots near its residence were used largely to house kin, not to earn rent. Its uses, except the residence, are those recorded for each lot's former holder, which the paper assumes carried on. The paper's own rent figure, which it suggests covers only the lots the main house managed, is about 2 percent of one year's total income (1856), and it states that rent was not central to the house; it studies one house in Osaka between the 1720s and the 1860s, and says its background for buying lots is still unexplained. The saved text is a scanned page layout, so the tables are scrambled and only the running prose can be read reliably.
