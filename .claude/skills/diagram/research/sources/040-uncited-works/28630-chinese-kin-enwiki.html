en.wikipedia "Chinese kin" (also titled "Chinese clan"; lineage, zongzu 宗族), on English Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_clan)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the Chinese kin or lineage, a patrilineal and patrilocal group with a common surname and ancestor, covering southern versus northern lineage strength, genealogy books (zupu), lineage associations and ancestral shrines. Most of its length is about Han patrilineal identity and non-Han groups, such as the Hui families of Quanzhou and the Zhuang, who forged Han genealogies.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its short passages say that kinship is strong in southern China, tied to an ancestral village and common property, and weaker in the north, where clan members do not usually live in one village or share property; that lineages own shrines and property and may be concentrated in one place or dispersed among several lineages; and that the Qing government encouraged kins to take on some welfare and schooling functions (marked citation needed). It gives no date for the north-south contrast, no village plan or size for an ancestral shrine, and most of the page concerns ethnic identity and genealogy forgery, which does not bear on how a village was laid out.
