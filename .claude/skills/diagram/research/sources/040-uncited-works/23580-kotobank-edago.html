kotobank-edago

"枝郷" ("edagō", a branch village split from a parent village, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (concise edition), the Heibonsha Mypedia, the Nipponica and the Britannica Japan small-item encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9E%9D%E9%83%B7-36659)

What it is: A Kotobank page with five dictionary and encyclopedia entries defining edago, a new village that split off from a parent village (oyago, hongo) when new fields were opened or when a village's assessed yield was divided.

Why it applies, and its limits: The entries say the branch village generally stayed within the parent's organization, and that under the Edo-period system it paid land tax together with the parent under the parent's village head; Maypedia adds that a branch grown rich could try to break away, and that the same parent-and-branch pairing is seen in castle-town wards. They date the term from the late Muromachi or the Sengoku period through the Edo period but give no village size, distance or layout, so they bear on how hamlets attached to a village and not on how one was drawn.
