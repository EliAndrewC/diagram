jaanus-tomobeya

Mary Neighbour Parent (compiler), "tomobeya 伴部屋" (the attendants' room, title translated), in JAANUS, the on-line Dictionary of Japanese Architectural and Art Historical Terminology, Media Center for Art History, Columbia University (https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/record/tomobeya)

What it is: A dictionary entry on the tomobeya, a room for the servants or attendants of high-ranking visitors, found in large village-headman houses and warrior houses of the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the room served as a waiting area and lodging, was especially common around Nara and Osaka, might sit in the main house or in a gatehouse (nagayamon), and describes a Nara magistrate's house of 1632 where it is a plain room at the front corner of the earthen floor with a projecting lattice window. It speaks for the larger houses of headmen and officials, not an ordinary farmhouse, and its regional claim is one sentence.
