sendai-wakabayashi-chomei

Wakabayashi Ward Office, Sendai City, "町名に見る城下町" ("The Castle Town Seen Through Its Street Names", title translated), in "Discovering Wakabayashi Ward", a project for the 400th anniversary of Sendai's founding (2002), on the Sendai city website (in Japanese; https://www.city.sendai.jp/waka-katsudo/wakabayashiku/machizukuri/miryoku/jokamachi.html)

What it is: A ward-office page explaining about thirty street names in the southeast of Sendai's castle town, in the Date domain, one by one. For each it says who was settled there (foot-soldier companies, bow, gun, and boatmen, stone-cutters, dyers, tatami makers, falconers, retainers and temples), when the street was laid out (mostly 1627 to 1645), and what survives.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a dated, named picture of how a castle town's streets were allotted by occupation: foot-soldier streets named for the headcount of the company (50, 60, 300 men) with the home shrine each company had moved in and a practice range beside it, craftsmen's lots fixed at 6 ken of frontage (6.5 for the dyers, who needed drying ground), narrow deep lots, samurai streets with trees and a spring, and the poverty of foot soldiers who grew vegetables and made brushes for income. Many name origins are given as tradition ("it is said"), it covers one quarter of one domain's capital in the first half of the seventeenth century, and it carries no measurements beyond the dyers' frontage.
