kong-2009-beijing-dangpu

Kong Xiangji, "晚清的北京当铺" ("Beijing's pawnshops in the late Qing", title translated), in 博览群书 (Bolan Qunshu, a book-review magazine), 7 July 2009, on Guangming Online (in Chinese; https://www.gmw.cn/02blqs/2009-07/07/content_1023242.htm)

What it is: A historian's essay on pawnshops in Beijing in the late Qing, drawn from the diary of the official Na Tong, archived memorials and memoirs. It covers who owned them, their interest, the tax each paid, the 190 or so shops of the 1890s, and the looting of nearly all of them in 1900.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives figures for the capital's pawn trade: about 190 shops in 1895, a tax of 5 taels a year per shop (raised to 50 in 1898), monthly interest of 2 percent or more (a memoir's figure; one pledge in Na Tong's diary ran at 0.6 percent a month), and a claim that over 200 shops were looted in the summer of 1900. Its evidence is from 1895 to 1902 in Beijing alone (with a Republican-era account of a shop's New Year opening), and its counts of the city's shops disagree (about 190 in the 1895 memorial, over 100 in a memoir, about 110 in an official's account of 1900, over 200 in a diary of the looting), plus a retold report of 75 shops owned by Heshen under Qianlong that the author himself thinks too many, and of pawnshops outside the capital it says only that the Grand Councillor Lu Chuanlin opened one in his home county, Dingxing in Hebei, with no detail of it.
