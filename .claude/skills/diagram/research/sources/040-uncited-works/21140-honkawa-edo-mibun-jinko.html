honkawa-edo-mibun-jinko

Honkawa Hiroshi, "図録▽江戸時代の身分別人口" ("Chart: population by status in the Edo period", title translated), on the data site Honkawa Zuroku (in Japanese; https://honkawa2.sakura.ne.jp/7860.html)

What it is: A statistics page giving the share of Japan's population by status in the early Meiji domain surveys (samurai class 6.4 percent, clergy 1.2 percent, commoners 90.6 percent, outcaste groups 1.7 percent), the occupations of commoners in 1873, samurai shares in several domains, and a 1622 breakdown of 14,313 people in Buzen's rural districts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies status proportions the record can compare with a setting's caste mix, such as 79.2 percent of employed commoners in farming in 1873 and samurai shares of 6.9 to 9.8 percent in the Nanbu, Kubota and Tsuwano domains. The national figures come from the 1870s surveys, the domain figures range from 1681 to 1849, the samurai share runs as high as 26 percent where retainers farmed, and the Buzen table mixes status, trade and disability classes.
