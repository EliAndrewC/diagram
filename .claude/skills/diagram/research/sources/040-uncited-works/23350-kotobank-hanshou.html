kotobank-hanshou

"半鐘" ("hansho", the small alarm bell, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Nipponica and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8D%8A%E9%90%98-606566)

What it is: A Kotobank dictionary page on the hansho, a small bronze bell used as a signal in temples and camps and, from the Edo period, hung in a fire-watch tower to sound fire, flood and thieves. The Nipponica entry says how the strokes signalled the distance of a fire.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports an alarm bell on a ladder or a tower, tied to the organized fire companies of the Edo period, with a wooden board sounded for village gatherings before it; a near fire was struck as a rapid run, and fewer strokes meant a farther one. It does not date the first tower bell beyond "from the Edo period", and names no size, height or number of towers for a town.
