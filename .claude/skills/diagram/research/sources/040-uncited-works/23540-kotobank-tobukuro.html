kotobank-tobukuro

Hirai Kiyoshi, "戸袋" ("tobukuro", the rain-shutter pocket, title translated), in Nipponica (日本大百科全書), with entries from the Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and a home-renovation glossary, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%88%B8%E8%A2%8B-584659)

What it is: A Kotobank page of definitions of the tobukuro, the boxlike housing at the end of a veranda or window where sliding rain shutters are kept when open, with a short account of when it came into use.

Why it applies, and its limits: The Nipponica entry says the pocket came with a single-track groove along which shutters slid out, not before, and dates that to about 1600, with early examples at the great hall of the Ninomaru Palace of Nijo Castle and in an old plan of the main hall of Nagoya Castle. All the datable cases are grand buildings of the shogun's and lords' circle, so the page does not show when a farmhouse or town house had one.
