ja-nagano-hasakake

JA Nagano Prefectural Union of Agricultural Cooperatives (長野県農業協同組合中央会), "稲刈り後の「はざかけ」ってどうやってるの？" ("ine-kari-ato no hazakake tte dou yatteru no?", How is hazakake done after the rice cutting?, title translated), on its Oitabe Fan Club site, September 23, 2014 (in Japanese; https://oishii.iijan.or.jp/products/post-2349)

What it is: A promotional article on the cooperative's food site describing a family in Matsumoto, Nagano, cutting rice on a plot of about 12 ares and drying it on a hase, the pole rack, in the field.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes how the drying rack is built: legs driven with a mallet, poles laid on them and the legs lashed with rope, the pole height set by the length of the rice so the ears do not touch the soil, the rice hung in two tiers, and a sheet put over it against rain for about two weeks. It is one 2014 account of one small plot cut with a binder machine and covered with a sheet made for the rack, and it gives no rack dimensions, so it shows how a rack is used but not what old racks measured.
