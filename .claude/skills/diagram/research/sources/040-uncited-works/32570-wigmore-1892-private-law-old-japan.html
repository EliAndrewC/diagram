John Henry Wigmore (ed.), Materials for the Study of Private Law in Old Japan, Part V, "Property: Civil Customs" (translated local-custom reports with the editor's notes), Transactions of the Asiatic Society of Japan, Tokyo, July 1892, archive.org full text of a Google-scanned copy (https://archive.org/stream/materialsforstu00japagoog/materialsforstu00japagoog_djvu.txt)

What it is: The OCR full text of Part V of Wigmore's 1892 English edition of old Japanese property customs, reported province by province and district by district across the old circuits (Kinai to Saikaido). It covers land registers and title, boundary fences and drains, house and tree spacing, village and town commons, forests, grass lands, ponds, roads, irrigation, rivers, succession to the headship and the setting up of branch households, with an appendix on the ban on land sale in the Sendai fief.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records, in many named districts, how neighbors kept houses back from boundary lines (for example about 3 feet in an Omi village, or a gap through which oxen and horses could pass in Idzu), how villages held and opened forests and grass lands, and how a village's number of houses was held fixed so a new family waited for a vacant house plot. It also shows land periodically redistributed among villagers in Hyuga, Iki and Echigo districts, and water shared by watch-keepers and rotation. The reports describe Edo-period custom as it stood at the Restoration (the text often notes what changed "since the Restoration") and most entries carry no date, in English translation with doubtful and obscure renderings; they give no plans, plot areas or layout of a whole settlement, and the OCR text is noisy.
