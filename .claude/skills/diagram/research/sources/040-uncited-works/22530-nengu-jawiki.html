ja.wikipedia 年貢 ("nengu", the annual land tax paid in rice, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/年貢)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on nengu, the land tax paid mostly in rice, following it from the Ritsuryo tax system of the 7th and 8th centuries through the Heian, Kamakura, Muromachi and Edo periods to its replacement by the land tax of 1873.

Why it applies, and its limits: For the Edo period it says that after Hideyoshi's land survey the village was taxed as a body on its recorded rice yield in koku (the murauke system), that only registered holders of fields and house plots owed it, and that the yearly inspection of the crop gave way to a fixed rate in the mid period. It adds that places without rice paid by a special rate on dry fields or by buying rice with the sale of cash crops, which spread near cities. It gives no tax rate or percentage for any period, and the article marks itself as lacking sources, so it explains the system and not the burden.
