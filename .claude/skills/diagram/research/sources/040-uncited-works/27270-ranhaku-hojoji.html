ranhaku-hojoji

Ranzan Town (嵐山町), "曹洞宗 宝城寺 菅谷村大字志賀" ("Soto sect, Hojo-ji, Shiga in Sugaya Village", title translated), from the Hiki County temple register (比企郡寺院明細帳), in the Ranzan Town Web Museum, vol. 6 (嵐山町web博物誌), on the Ranzan town site (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/09ter10.html)

What it is: A town-hosted transcription of the Meiji-era Hiki County temple register entry for Hojo-ji, a Soto Zen temple in Shiga village, Musashi, with the town's footnotes. It lists the main hall, the priest's residence, two subsidiary halls, the precinct and the temple's outlying land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows what a larger village temple owned beside its hall: a main hall of 10 ken by 7 ken, a precinct of 850 tsubo of state land (revised to 763 tsubo in 1948-49), an Enma hall and a Fudo hall of their own stated sizes, 92 parishioner households, paddy, field and forest plots, and a small irrigation reservoir (tameike) of 5 se 17 bu. The temple's origin is given only as unknown, and the page records the Meiji-era holdings of this one temple, not an earlier century's.
