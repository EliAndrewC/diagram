Fuyutsuki Ritsu, "過疎地神社の研究 : 人口減少社会と神社神道" ("A study of shrines in depopulated areas: shrine Shinto in a society of falling population", title translated), summary of a doctoral dissertation, on the K-RAIN repository of Kokugakuin University (in Japanese; https://k-rain.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2480/files/bunotsu_298.pdf)

What it is: The author's own summary of a doctoral dissertation on how depopulation has changed village shrines in Japan since the war, built on surveys of shrines and their parishioners (ujiko) in the former Kubokawa town of Takaoka county, Kochi prefecture (now Shimanto town), with a 2012 priests' survey and a follow-up on a 1977 Association of Shinto Shrines survey.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives present-day anchors for how a village shrine is kept up: it reports that seven in ten shrines in the town are maintained by 11 to 50 parishioner households, that there are 102 shrines (not counting the head shrines) in seven districts, and that of the dozen or more shrines that once held a flower-picking dance at the autumn festival only two now do. Its evidence is surveys of a depopulated mountain district of Kochi from the 1970s to 2012, so the counts describe shrines already shrunken by emigration, and it does not describe any shrine before the Meiji era.
