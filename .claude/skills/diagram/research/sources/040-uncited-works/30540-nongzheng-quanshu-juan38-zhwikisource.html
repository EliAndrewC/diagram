"農政全書/卷38 種植" (Nongzheng quanshu, volume 38, "Planting", title translated), the "Complete Treatise on Agricultural Administration", on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/農政全書/卷38)

What it is: The Wikisource transcription of volume 38 of the Nongzheng quanshu, the agricultural compendium its Wikisource index page attributes to Xu Guangqi of the Ming (明徐光啓撰), its section on trees (the "wood division"): elm, catalpa, pine and cypress, tung, pepper, paper mulberry, willow, wax privet, tallow tree, lacquer and others. Each entry strings together quotations from earlier works (the Qimin yaoshu, the Nongsang tongjue, the Bencao) with notes signed Xuanhu xiansheng (玄扈先生), which speak of the writer's own county and his own planting of several hundred wax trees.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the planting methods and tract sizes the old agricultural books gave for tree lots, for example elm planted in a separate cut square of land because "under its shade the five grains do not grow", and willow rows laid out by the mu with a stated yield, which a map can use for where woodlots and tree rows stood. Most of the planting recipes are quoted from the Qimin yaoshu, which the notes name as a Northern Wei work by Jia Sixie (its chinaberry entry speaks of northern households), while the notes speak of Jiangnan and Zhejiang, so each entry's era and region must be read from the work it quotes. Several are paper calculations of income (a tract of 30 mu cut one-third a year). The notes on the wax-insect tree say the insects were first raised within the last five to twenty years in Wuzhou, Wuxing and the writer's own county, and they ask whether the tallow tree's use is likewise recent, so the wax entries describe a practice new in the Ming and not an old one.
