"農政全書/卷38 種植" (Nongzheng quanshu, volume 38, "Planting", title translated), the "Complete Treatise on Agricultural Administration", on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/農政全書/卷38)

What it is: The Wikisource transcription of volume 38 of the Nongzheng quanshu, its section on trees (the "wood division"): elm, catalpa, pine and cypress, tung, pepper, paper mulberry, willow, wax privet, tallow tree, lacquer and others. Each entry strings together quotations from earlier works (the Qimin yaoshu, the Nongsang tongjue, the Bencao) with the notes of a commentator who signs as Xuanhu xiansheng.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the planting methods and tract sizes the old agricultural books gave for tree lots, for example elm planted in a separate cut square of land because "under its shade the five grains do not grow", and willow rows laid out by the mu with a stated yield, which a map can use for where woodlots and tree rows stood. The recipes are quoted from earlier centuries and several are paper calculations of income (a tract of 30 mu cut one-third a year), and the commentator's own notes on the tallow tree and the wax-insect tree say these trades were new within living memory in Zhejiang and Jiangnan, so those entries describe a late-Ming practice and not an old one.
