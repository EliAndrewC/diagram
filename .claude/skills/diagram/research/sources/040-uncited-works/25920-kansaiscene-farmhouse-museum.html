Alan Wiren, "Discovering Edo Japan in 12 Houses", Kansai Scene magazine, 1 September 2020 (https://www.kansaiscene.com/2020/09/discovering-edo-japan-in-12-houses/)

What it is: A magazine feature on the Open-Air Museum of Old Japanese Farmhouses in Toyonaka, Osaka, which holds 12 Edo-period buildings moved there from Iwate to Kagoshima, seven of them farmhouses. It describes several of them: a gassho house from Hida-Shirakawa, an L-shaped Iwate house with stable and living wing, a Shodoshima kabuki theater, an Osaka rice granary and a stilted storehouse from Amami Oshima.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows by example that Edo-period farmhouses differed by region and climate, as the L-shaped Iwate house (people in one wing, horses in the other, a hearth at the corner) and the stilted Amami storehouse show. It gives one number, the museum's 36,000 square-meter grounds, and no dimensions or plans of any building; the buildings are dismantled and rebuilt on a museum site, so their setting is not the village they stood in, and the article says nothing of how any of them was laid out among fields or neighbors.
