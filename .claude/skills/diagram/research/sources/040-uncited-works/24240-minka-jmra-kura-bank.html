minka-jmra-kura-bank

Japan Minka Revival Association (日本民家再生協会), "蔵" ("kura", storehouse; title translated), in its Minka Bank (民家バンク) listings (in Japanese; https://minka.or.jp/minkabankcat/type2)

What it is: A listing page of the Japan Minka Revival Association's Minka Bank, which matches owners of old houses with people who will move and restore them, here showing storehouses (kura) on offer in Akita and Iwate. Each entry gives the age, floors, roof, floor area in tsubo and a short description of the frame.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives floor areas for surviving Edo-period farm earthen storehouses (for example a two-story Akita kura of 15.7 tsubo per floor, 30.14 tsubo in all, built in the Edo period, and others of 10.42 to 18 tsubo per floor), and notes that snowy Akita kura were wrapped in a second outer wall and roof. The dates are estimates (one is given as 'over 150 years, Edo period' for want of a ridge tablet), the buildings are a few offered in the northeast, and the page says nothing of how common storehouses were or what a farm kept in them beyond household goods.
