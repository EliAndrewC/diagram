He Liuqin (何流琴), "史海寻踪——古代养猪趣谈" ("Tracing history: notes on pig keeping in ancient times", title translated), 23 January 2017, a science-popularization essay on the site of the Institute of Subtropical Agriculture, Chinese Academy of Sciences (in Chinese; https://www.isa.cas.cn/kxcb/kpzw/202012/t20201221_5831491.html)

What it is: A popular-science essay by an institute staff member on the history of pig keeping in China, from Neolithic domestication through Han pigsties, the Qimin Yaoshu, Song Kaifeng, the Yuan, the Ming ban and the Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries quotations on how pigs were kept: the Qimin Yaoshu rule that pens be small and fouled, with pasturing in spring and summer; Han pottery pigsty models joined to latrines or workshops to gather manure; the Dongjing Menghualu line that herds of ten thousand pigs came into Kaifeng each evening; and the Ming ban on pigs under the Zhengde emperor. It cites no editions or pages and spans seven thousand years, so a given claim must be dated by its quoted source, and it names no numbers for a village's pens.
