Boso no Mura (Chiba Prefectural Museum of the Boso Villages), "様々な建物" ("samazama na tatemono", various buildings, title translated), in "衣・食・住・技" (Clothing, food, housing, craft, title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.chiba-muse.or.jp/MURA/kikaku-mura/juu/jyuu.htm)

What it is: A page of a prefectural open-air museum's digital museum on the kinds of house in Chiba Prefecture from the late Edo period to the Meiji era: a samurai residence, a farmhouse, fishers' houses and a merchant house, each shown by a house rebuilt or moved to the museum.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what separated the houses by occupation: a samurai house of shoin style with a raised site and hedge screening it from the street, a thatched farmhouse with a wide earth-floored doma on a plot wider than a merchant's, a fisher's house with a low roof against wind, and a merchant house with its shop at the front and its home and storehouse behind on a long narrow plot because frontage was taxed. It adds that in Chiba about 30 percent of merchant houses had a storehouse (dozo) by the Meiji era, and that Kazusa farmhouses faced a road and fields with a hill behind. The examples are the museum's reconstructions of Chiba houses (a mid-rank Sakura samurai house, Shimosa and Kazusa nanushi-class farmhouses), so they speak for the Kanto coast, not for every region, and the page gives no dimensions.
