Boso no Mura (Chiba Prefectural Museum of the Boso Villages), "様々な建物" ("samazama na tatemono", various buildings, title translated), in "衣・食・住・技" (Clothing, food, housing, craft, title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.chiba-muse.or.jp/MURA/kikaku-mura/juu/jyuu.htm)

What it is: A page of a prefectural open-air museum's digital museum on the kinds of house in Chiba Prefecture from the late Edo period to the Meiji era: a samurai residence, a farmhouse, fishers' houses and a merchant house, each shown by a house rebuilt or moved to the museum.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what separated the houses by occupation: a samurai house of shoin style with a raised site and hedge screening it from the street, a thatched farmhouse with a wide earth-floored doma on a plot wider than a merchant's, a fisher's house with a low roof against wind on the open Awa coast (the page's Urayasu house inside Tokyo Bay has an ordinary roof and a low ceiling with a loft against floods), and a merchant house with its shop at the front and its home and storehouse behind on a long narrow plot because frontage was taxed. It adds that in Chiba, varying by area, about 30 percent of merchant houses were built as dozo (thick clay fireproof walls) by the Meiji era (the passage is about fireproofing shops on the street, so it may not mean a separate storehouse), and that Kazusa farmhouses faced a road and fields with a hill behind. The examples are the museum's reconstructions of Chiba houses (a mid-rank Sakura samurai house, Shimosa and Kazusa nanushi-class farmhouses, so its wide doma and plot are a village headman's, not an ordinary farmer's), so they speak for the Kanto coast, not for every region, and the page gives no dimensions.
