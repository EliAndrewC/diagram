kikuoka-edo-shokunin-hattatsu

Kikuoka Tomoya (head of the Kensetsu Bunka Kenkyujo, the Construction Culture Institute), "江戸の職人【１】江戸の職人発展史" ("Edo's craftsmen, part 1: the history of Edo's craftsmen", title translated), on the token.or.jp magazine site (in Japanese; https://token.or.jp/magazine/g200310/g200310_1.htm)

What it is: The first page (of six) of a magazine series on the craftsmen of Edo. It tells how carpenters, plasterers and other trades were called to Edo from other provinces and from Kyoto and Fushimi, settled in craft quarters named for their trades, and how those quarters broke up in the later 17th century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a planned craft quarter in a newly built castle town: craftsmen were summoned to build the castle, housed street by street by trade (carpenters' town, sawyers' town, plasterers' town, mat makers' town) and exempt from land tax in return for unpaid labor, and the quarters dissolved by the middle Edo period as work moved into private building and the Great Fire of 1657 scattered them. It speaks only of Edo, the shogun's capital, built in the early 1600s, so it shows the largest and fastest case; it gives no count of craftsmen or size of a quarter, and the remaining five pages of the series are not on this page.
