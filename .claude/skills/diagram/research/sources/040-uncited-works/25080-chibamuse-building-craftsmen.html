chibamuse-building-craftsmen

Boso no Mura (Chiba Prefectural Museum of the Boso Villages), "建物に関わる職人たち" ("tatemono ni kakawaru shokunin-tachi", the craftsmen who build buildings, title translated), in "衣・食・住・技" (Clothing, food, housing, craft, title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.chiba-muse.or.jp/MURA/kikaku-mura/juu/jyuu2.htm)

What it is: A short page of the Boso no Mura museum's digital museum on the trades that built a timber-framed house in Japan from the late Edo period to the Meiji era: the tobi (foundation), carpenter, plasterer, thatcher, tiler and joiner, and which roof or wall each did.

Why it applies, and its limits: It assigns the work by trade: foundations and stone bases to the tobi, frame, siding and nail-less joinery to the carpenter, clay walls, floors, hearths and tile roofs to the plasterer, and thatch, board or tile roofing to separate roofers, with miscanthus (kaya) thatch lasting longer than rice straw because it sheds water. It adds that old tile roofs were laid in clay (dobuki) and that the Kanto switched to hooked tiles after the 1923 earthquake. It is a brief outline of a Chiba museum's reconstructions, gives no costs, numbers or crew sizes, and its late-Edo to Meiji date and the Kanto setting limit how far its trade lines can be applied elsewhere.
