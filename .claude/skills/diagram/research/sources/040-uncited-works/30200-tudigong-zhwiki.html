tudigong-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 土地公 ("Tudigong", the Earth God, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/土地公)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the local earth god Tudigong, covering his origin in the ancient cult of the she (earth altar), the forms of his images and shrines, his festival days, his place in fiction, and tables of the officials and worthies worshipped as local earth gods.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a small, plain village earth-god shrine in a rice country: it says earth-god temples are mostly small buildings put up by villagers, spread through the countryside, and that a farmer with none could pile three stones under a tree or by a road, or tie paper money to a bamboo pole at the field's edge. It also says the spring and autumn sacrifices on the 2nd of the 2nd month and mid-autumn descend from the old she days, quoting Qing county gazetteers of Taiwan. Most of its customs are from Taiwan, Fujian and Guangdong, some of it modern (shrines poured in cement from factory molds). Its three-stone and field-pole customs are footnoted only to two Taiwanese news stories of September 2018, and it names no dimensions.
