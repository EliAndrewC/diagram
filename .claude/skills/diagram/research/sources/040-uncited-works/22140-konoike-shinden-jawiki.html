ja.wikipedia 鴻池新田 ("Konoike shinden", the Konoike new-field settlement, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%B4%BB%E6%B1%A0%E6%96%B0%E7%94%B0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Konoike Shinden, a reclaimed rice settlement in Kawachi Province (now Higashiosaka) developed from 1705 by the Osaka merchant house Konoike on the lake bed left when the Yamato River was rerouted. It covers the settlers, the site's management office (kaisho) and its waterway.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a dated example of a merchant-financed new-field village: about 119 hectares, a village assessment of 1,706 koku, an initial 8 or so settler households followed by 10 or more, settlers brought from Ise and Hirakata, and a management office with a moat and boat landing, where houses along the canals kept boats under their eaves to carry rice to it. The article is a short summary with no sources, the household figures are loose, and its later sections describe the present-day museum and the rebuilt waterway, not the Edo village.
