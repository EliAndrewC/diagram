Li Xuepu (originally in Beijing Evening News), "古代那些独具特色的“旅馆”" ("The distinctive inns of ancient times", title translated), reposted by the 大道知行知行堂 account on NetEase Subscription, 2024-12-29 (in Chinese; https://www.163.com/dy/article/JKDCD7960521BRHI.html)

What it is: A newspaper-column essay, reposted on NetEase, on inns in China from the Zhou courier stations to the Ming and Qing guild and native-place halls (huiguan): the Tang distinction between a guan and a dian, the dizhan that lodged and stored merchants' goods, Song small inns, and Ming relay stations.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies what an urban inn was called and who ran it in each period: Tang guan (larger hostels, public and private) against dian (privately run), the dizhan combining lodging, restaurant, storage and money-changing, Song inns as small as one room, Ming stations 60 li apart, and Beijing's hundreds of huiguan (Suzhou's over ninety) for examination candidates and merchants. It is a sweep across two thousand years with no sources named inside the text, gives no plans or sizes, and says nothing about Japan or about inns in a market town or village.
