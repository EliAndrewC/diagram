Li Xuepu (originally in Beijing Evening News), "古代那些独具特色的“旅馆”" ("The distinctive inns of ancient times", title translated), reposted by the 大道知行知行堂 account on NetEase Subscription, 2024-12-29 (in Chinese; https://www.163.com/dy/article/JKDCD7960521BRHI.html)

What it is: A newspaper-column essay, reposted on NetEase, on inns in China from the Zhou courier stations to the Ming and Qing guild and native-place halls (huiguan): the Tang distinction between a guan and a dian, the dizhan that lodged and stored merchants' goods, Song small inns, and Ming relay stations.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies what an urban inn was called and who ran it in each period: Tang guan (larger hostels, public and private) against dian (privately run), the dizhan combining lodging, restaurant, storage and money-changing, Song inns as small as one room, Ming stations 60 li apart, and Beijing's hundreds of huiguan (Suzhou's over ninety) for examination candidates and merchants. It is a sweep across two thousand years that names only one period text (Hong Mai's Yijian jia zhi) and quotes others without naming them, gives no plans or sizes, says nothing about Japan, and of lodging outside the cities gives only passing mentions: Zhou roadside lodges every ten li, Southern Dynasties inns spread through town and country, and a Song official's one-room village inn.
