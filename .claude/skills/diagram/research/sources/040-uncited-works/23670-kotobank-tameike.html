kotobank-tameike

"溜池" ("tameike", the irrigation pond, title translated), entries from the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (Shirai Yoshihiko and Kitamura Toshio), Shogakukan's Nipponica, the Historical Place Names of Japan series and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%BA%9C%E6%B1%A0-94442)

What it is: A Kotobank page of dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the tameike, the reservoir pond that stores rain for rice irrigation, and on Tameike, the pond at Akasaka in Edo that served as a moat of Edo Castle and as a water supply. The long encyclopedia entry gives the types of pond, the history from antiquity to the Edo period, management, and construction advice drawn from Edo-period writers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports ponds as the water source for paddies where rivers are scarce, by type (valley ponds and flat dish ponds), with a pond keeper and turn-taking for water, and names the Sanuki plain, Nara basin, Osaka plain and Harima plain as the districts with the most today. It says that ponds fed about 20 percent of Japan's paddies, over 70 percent in Kagawa, a present-day count, and that the great age of pond building ran from the late Sengoku period to the early Edo period; its construction rules come from named Edo writers, such as Sato Nobuhiro's embankment slopes of 7 sun 5 bu on the inside face and 5 sun on the outside, not from a survey. It is a general account that gives no size for the common village pond.
