kotobank-katemeshi

"糅飯" ("katemeshi", rice stretched with other foods, title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, a cookery dictionary and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%B3%85%E9%A3%AF-45816)

What it is: A page of dictionary entries on katemeshi, rice cooked with barley, beans, radish or seaweed to make up a shortfall in quantity, with early and Edo-period quotations.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports that the grain ration was routinely stretched, quoting a Heian text and Edo texts, one of which, a satirical sharebon of 1784, has peasants eating two parts barley to eight parts bran, and the encyclopedia says many regions did so until after World War II except on festival days. It gives no proportions for a typical household and no region, and its quotes are tales, fiction and satire, not household accounts: the Heian one comes from a story in the Konjaku collection's book on India.
