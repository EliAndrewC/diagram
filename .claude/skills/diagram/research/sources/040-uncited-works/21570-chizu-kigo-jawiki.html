chizu-kigo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 日本の地図記号の一覧 ("nihon no chizu kigo no ichiran", list of Japanese map symbols, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E3%81%AE%E5%9C%B0%E5%9B%B3%E8%A8%98%E5%8F%B7%E3%81%AE%E4%B8%80%E8%A6%A7)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia list of the map symbols set by the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan, giving each symbol's name, meaning and the thing it was drawn from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how a national survey chose its marks, for example the torii for a shrine, a swastika for a temple, rice stubble for paddy, mulberry and tea bushes for their groves, and bamboo with a shadow for a bamboo grove, which bears on how a reader expects such things to be drawn on a map. These are modern surveying conventions, several taken from Meiji-era bodies (the Teishin-sho postal ministry, the army's medical corps), and the page also lists symbols for modern things such as wind turbines and power plants, so it records a drawing convention and not any premodern landscape.
