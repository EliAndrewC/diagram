Dali Rongmei (大理融媒), "大理这个村庄竟有11口古井" ("This Dali village has as many as 11 old wells", title translated), on the village of Mingzhuang in Haidong town, posted 2026-05-18 on NetEase Subscription (in Chinese; https://www.163.com/dy/article/KT7SSDMI0550AN9P.html)

What it is: A local-media news item on the old public wells of Mingzhuang, a Bai village in Haidong town, Dali, Yunnan: it counts 11 surviving public wells, describes their stone rims and washing troughs, and says how they are spread through the village's lineage quarters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports that every lane and family quarter of the old village has a public well, that nearly all are still used, with round-inside, square-outside stone rims, wash troughs and clothes-beating slabs, and that rope-wear grooves and non-local marble point to a Ming-Qing building date. It is a present-day local-media feature on one Yunnan Bai village, says no elder could date the wells, so the Ming-Qing date is the writer's inference from materials, and gives no depths, spacing or the number of households each well served.
