zh.wikipedia 廟會 ("miaohui", temple fairs, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/庙会)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the temple fair, the festival held at and around a temple, with a history by dynasty from the Qin to the Qing, a section on how fairs are funded and where they are held, and regional sections from Taiwan and mainland China to Japan, Korea and overseas Chinese quarters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the temple fair began as a sacrifice and by the Ming had turned toward a market, quoting the Dijing jingwu lue on the Beijing City God temple fair, where of those present six in ten came to look, three to trade and one to worship, and it says that in earlier times the cost was met from temple oil money and a levy on each household's men. The Ming count is of the capital's City God fair, held on the first, fifteenth and twenty-fifth of the month with stalls stretching three li, not of a village fair; the funding passage cites no source and names no place or period beyond "in ancient times" (its collectors are called luzhu and toujia); most of its content on the modern fairs is Beijing, Taiwan and overseas, and the history sections give no figures beyond that one Ming count and the thousand-odd Buddha images Luoyang's temples sent to a Northern Wei procession.
