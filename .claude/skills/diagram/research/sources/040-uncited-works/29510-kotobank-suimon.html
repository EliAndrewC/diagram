kotobank-suimon

"水門" ("suimon, the sluice gate", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/水門)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for suimon and mito, the sluice gate, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Okuma Takashi), Nipponica, a Kyodo News glossary entry and others, mostly on river-engineering floodgates.

Why it applies, and its limits: The Nihon Kokugo Daijiten gives the premodern sense, a gate on a reservoir, canal or dam intake that regulates the flow, with a 12th-century dictionary (1177-81) and a 1807-11 citation, and an older 10th-century dictionary for the same sense under the reading mito, and Daijisen adds uses for intake, drainage and boat traffic. The Heibonsha, Nipponica and Kyodo entries describe modern flood and tide-barrier gates (the Thames Barrier of 1984; about 27,000 sluice gates and land gates together nationwide, by a government survey), which carry nothing of premodern village practice.
