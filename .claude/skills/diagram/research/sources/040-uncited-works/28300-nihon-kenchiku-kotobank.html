nihon-kenchiku-kotobank

Kudo Keisho (工藤圭章), Omi Sakae (近江栄), Ota Hirotaro (太田博太郎) and others, "日本建築" ("Japanese architecture", title translated), in Nipponica (日本大百科全書), the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典) and the Britannica small-item encyclopedia, on Kotobank (the page saved under this address is the Nihon kenchiku entry) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/並木-110146)

What it is: Four encyclopedia entries on Japanese architecture reproduced on Kotobank, running from prehistoric pit dwellings and raised granaries through temples, shrines, residences and castles of each period to modern architecture. The address given here ends in the word for a row of trees, but the page saved from it is the architecture entry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says for the premodern buildings that timber was the only material until the Meiji era, that cypress was favored and pine, cedar and zelkova came into use later, that houses had raised wood floors with verandas, and that a torii is only two posts and two crossbars. It says that farmhouses kept regional features and very conservative plans, and that Edo-period castles declined after the one-province-one-castle order of 1615. It is a survey of high architecture, with little on farmhouses or village buildings, and it gives no sizes for them.
