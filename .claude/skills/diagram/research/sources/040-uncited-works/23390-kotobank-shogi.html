kotobank-shogi

"将棋" ("shogi", Japanese chess, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Nipponica, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Masukawa Koichi), the Mypedia, the Britannica and the Yamakawa Japanese History Dictionary, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9F%8E%E4%B8%8B%E7%94%BA-78976)

What it is: A long Kotobank page on shogi: its origin in India, its history in Japan from the Heian period, the Edo shogi houses and the castle games, the rules, and the equipment. The address in the URL names the castle town, but the page that loads is the shogi entry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the game as a pastime of nobles, monks and the upper warrior class that spread to townsmen and craftsmen only in the later Edo period, and gives the board of the Edo period (1 shaku 2 sun by 1 shaku 1 sun, about 36 by 33 cm). It says nothing about castle towns or the settlement the URL names, and most of its length is on modern professional titles and tournaments.
