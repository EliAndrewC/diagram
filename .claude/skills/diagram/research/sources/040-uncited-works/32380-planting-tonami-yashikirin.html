planting-tonami-yashikirin

"砺波散居の屋敷林について" ("Tonami sankyo no yashikirin ni tsuite", on the homestead woods of the scattered settlements of Tonami, title translated), PDF on planting.co.jp (in Japanese; https://planting.co.jp/common/pdf/03-tonami.pdf)

What it is: An illustrated essay by Nogami Ryokka (株式会社野上緑化), a landscaping company in Toyama City, on the cedar homestead woods (kainyo) of the Tonami plain, Toyama: how they arose, their species, their wind and heat effects, and their present condition and upkeep.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the houses stand about 100 m apart with the woods mostly on three sides and thickest to the west and south against the prevailing wind, that the Kaga domain banned unauthorized felling of pine, cedar, zelkova, cypress, hemlock, oak and large bamboo, and that the word kainyo comes from "kakine" (hedge) and names the strip between the house and its fields. Its measurements and storm counts are from 1996-2012 and its pruning advice is modern, so those parts say nothing of Edo practice. The 100 m spacing and the count of about 8,000 houses describe the plain today, after felling that began in the late Meiji period, and the Kaga felling ban is given with no date or source. The PDF names no author; it is a landscaping firm's essay.
