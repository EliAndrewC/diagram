komonjyo-murameisaicho-3

Uno Aiko, "江戸時代の村の面積、用水、家数など_村明細帳3" ("Edo jidai no mura no menseki, yosui, kazu nado", an Edo village's area, water supply and house count, from the village detail register part 3, title translated), in Komonjyo Net (古文書ネット) (in Japanese; https://komonjyo.net/murameisaichyou02.html)

What it is: A page that transcribes and translates into modern Japanese the opening of a village detail register of Bunka 13 (1816) from Tama County, Musashi: a total area of 123 cho 9 tan 5 se 3 bu (8 tan 9 bu of paddy, the rest dry fields), yield ratings by land class, water drawn from the Yotsuya (Tamagawa) aqueduct, no minor taxes, and 45 houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives one real village's land area, paddy to dry-field split (dry fields about 99 percent by the register's figures; the page says over 80), house count and water source, a concrete Kanto upland case against which a drawn village's mix can be compared. The page's remarks that the average village had about 50 cho and 40 to 50 households rest on two other Musashi villages and no stated survey, and the one village is a dry-field Musashi plateau settlement, not a rice-paddy one: Onumata Shinden (named in part 1 of the series from the same register), a village reclaimed from the Musashino upland in the Edo period rather than an old one.
