heshang-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 和尚 ("heshang", the Buddhist term for a monk, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/和尚)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the word heshang, a transliteration of the Sanskrit upadhyaya: its meaning as a monk's personal teacher, the quoted Tang and Song glosses on its origin, and its everyday use in Chinese society for a male monk.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the vocabulary of a Chinese monastery - that heshang first meant the teacher who ordained a monk, later came to mean any male monk in common speech and the abbot of a temple, and that nuns were called nigu - and quotes Tang and Song dictionaries for the word's history. It is about terms, not numbers or buildings, and its note on monks who marry is about Japan after the Meiji Restoration and a Song-era record of 'wives of monks', with no count of how common that was.
