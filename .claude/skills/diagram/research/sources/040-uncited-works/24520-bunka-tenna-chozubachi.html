bunka-tenna-chozubachi

Agency for Cultural Affairs, "天和二年銘手水鉢" ("tenna ninen mei chozubachi", the stone hand-washing basin inscribed Tenna 2, title translated), on Cultural Heritage Online (文化遺産オンライン) (in Japanese; https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/249273)

What it is: A Cultural Heritage Online record of a stone hand-washing basin of 1682 designated by Suginami Ward, standing in the Komyoin temple grounds in Ogikubo, Tokyo, with its dimensions, its inscription and a commentary.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its commentary reads the inscription as showing that it names eleven people (the petitioners, a stonemason, a carpenter and the abbot), that the petitioners are inferred to be independent landholding farmers (honbyakusho) and the central farmers of Ogikubo village, and that the carpenter's name means a basin shed was built with it. That is a dated, village-level instance of farmers paying for a temple's water basin in the Edo suburbs; it describes one temple in one village near Edo, and the page does not say how common such gifts were. Its commentary calls the basin a first-rate piece by a mason from Edo itself, costly and paid for by the village's wealthiest farmers, so it shows a rich suburban village's gift rather than an ordinary one.
