bernstein-cremation-meiji

Andrew Bernstein, "Fire and Earth: The Forging of Modern Cremation in Meiji Japan," Japanese Journal of Religious Studies, vol. 27, no. 3-4, abstract page on the site of the Nanzan Institute for Religion and Culture (https://nirc.nanzan-u.ac.jp/nfile/2722)

What it is: The abstract page of a journal article on Japan's 1873 nationwide ban on cremation and its repeal two years later, by Buddhist priests, educated citizens and some officials who argued cremation was sanitary and saved grave space.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says cremation, a Buddhist practice, had long been called barbaric and unfilial by Confucian and nativist scholars, and was a minority practice before the ban and a majority one afterward, so it is evidence that burial was the usual form before Meiji. Only the abstract is on the page, so the evidence for the claims is not shown, and its subject is the Meiji change itself, not a count of earlier practice by region or class.
