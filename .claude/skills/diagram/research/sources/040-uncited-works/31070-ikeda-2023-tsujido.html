ikeda-2023-tsujido

Ikeda Kaori (池田 佳織), "日常と信仰の歴史的建造物 備後地方に点在する辻堂" ("Historic buildings of daily life and faith: the roadside halls scattered across the Bingo region", title translated), Kyoto University of the Arts correspondence course, Department of Arts and Liberal Studies, online graduation research exhibition, September 2023 (in Japanese; https://g.kyoto-art.ac.jp/reports/6415)

What it is: An undergraduate graduation report on the wall-less four-post roadside halls (tsujido, yotsudo) of the Bingo region of Hiroshima and Okayama. It describes their size, siting, uses and history from field visits in 2021 to 2023, and ends with a list of problems and proposals for preservation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a regional roadside form: a roofed, wall-less, four-post hall of about two tatami beside a field or old road, holding a Jizo, with a raised floor, used for rest, worship and meetings; it reports about 600 in the Edo period and fewer than half now, and a 1700 land survey register entry for yotsudo. It admits there is no clear record of the halls' history, it takes the Mizuno Katsunari legend and the medieval origin from other writers' works, and it describes the present halls, many rebuilt in concrete.
