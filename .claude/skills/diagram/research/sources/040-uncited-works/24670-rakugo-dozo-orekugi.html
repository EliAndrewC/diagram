rakugo-dozo-orekugi

"土蔵・オレクギ" ("dozo / orekugi", the earthen storehouse and its hooked nails, title translated), a rakugo explainer page citing the Fukagawa Edo Museum's guide booklet (in Japanese; https://rakugo.or.jp/dozou-orekugi.html)

What it is: A short explainer page on the earthen-walled storehouse (dozo) of Edo, drawn from rakugo stories and credited to the Fukagawa Edo Museum's guide booklet. It describes the three-part door of a dozo, the sealing of its gaps with clay during a fire, and the hooked nails (orekugi) and plaster knobs on a Kanto storehouse wall.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says why Edo's merchant houses had so many storehouses (frequent fires) and how the door was built and sealed, and it names the orekugi as a mark of Kanto storehouses, with a purpose it admits has no settled account. It is about Edo's big merchant houses, gives no sizes, and says nothing of storehouses in a village or a smaller town.
