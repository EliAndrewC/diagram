saito-fukuoka-2011-ara-fan-levees

Saito Shigeru and Fukuoka Shoji (齊藤 滋, 福岡 捷二), "荒川扇状地における集落の展開と自然堤防の役割に関する研究" ("Arakawa senjochi ni okeru shuraku no tenkai to shizen teibo no yakuwari ni kansuru kenkyu", settlements on the Ara River alluvial fan and the role of natural levees, title translated), Journal of Japan Society of Civil Engineers, Ser. B1 (Hydraulic Engineering) 67(4): I_673-I_678, 2011, on J-STAGE (in Japanese; https://jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jscejhe/67/4/67_4_i_673/_pdf)

What it is: The full text of a six-page hydraulic-engineering paper on the Ara River alluvial fan in northwestern Saitama (Kumagaya and Fukaya). It plots settlement sites from the Paleolithic to the Nara-Heian period, shrines and temples, and the Meiji-era (about 1884) Jinsoku survey maps against natural levees, then overlays flood-hazard maps.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports that settlement sites of every period from the Jomon on, shrines and temples, Meiji villages and the roads joining them sit mostly on natural levees, that the 1884 map shows paddy and dry field mottled to match the levees, and that the levees are about 0.5 m high. It is one fan in the Kanto plain, its sites are mapped but not dated closely, and its flood-protection finding rests on a modern hazard map and the paper itself says the Meiji-era houses off the levees cannot be shown to have been lived in.
