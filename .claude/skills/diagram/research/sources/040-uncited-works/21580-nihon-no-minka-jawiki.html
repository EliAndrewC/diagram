ja.wikipedia 日本の民家 ("nihon no minka", the Japanese common house, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E3%81%AE%E6%B0%91%E5%AE%B6)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article defining the minka, the dwelling of ordinary people (farmhouse, fisher's house, townhouse and the houses of middle and lower samurai), with a long table of the minka designated Important Cultural Properties and two named for National Treasure status.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the scholarly definition of the term and a dated list of surviving houses, from the late Muromachi (Hakogi and Furui houses, 1467-1572) through the Edo period, with each house's prefecture and building date, which shows what survives and where. It says a survey begun in 1966 systematized plans, structure and change over time. The list is of the best surviving houses, so it speaks for the substantial end of the housing stock, and it gives no plans or dimensions.
