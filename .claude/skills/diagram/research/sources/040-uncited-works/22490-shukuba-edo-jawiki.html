shukuba-edo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 宿場 ("shukuba", the post station, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宿場町)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the post station (shukuba) and the post town built around it, covering the ancient relay-station system, the Edo-period stations on the five great highways, the buildings found in a station, and a regional list of surviving station towns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists what a station held: the toiyaba relay office, the honjin and waki-honjin lodgings for samurai and court nobles, inns, cheap self-catering inns, teahouses, shops and the notice board, plus the masugata crank at each end and the gates between which the town lay, closed at night. It also says Tokugawa Ieyasu began the Edo system after Sekigahara and fixed 53 stations on the Tokaido in 1601, though the page dates the last of them, Shono, to Kanbun 1 and writes 1624, a year that does not match that era, so its dates need care. It gives no station size or layout figures, and much of its length is a list of present-day preserved towns.
