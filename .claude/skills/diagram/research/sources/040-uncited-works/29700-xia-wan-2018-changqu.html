xia-wan-2018-changqu

Xia Jing and Wan Yongchun, "长渠：“长藤结瓜”式灌溉工程典范" ("the Long Canal: a model of the 'long vine with melons' irrigation works", title translated), Guangming Daily (光明日报), 19 August 2018, on Guangming Online (in Chinese; https://news.gmw.cn/2018-08/19/content_30623515.htm)

What it is: A short newspaper report on the Changqu canal near Xiangyang, Hubei, a 49.3 km channel begun in 279 BC and newly listed as a World Heritage Irrigation Structure. It describes the canal's linked-pond layout, its water-sharing rules and its modern expansion.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a premodern Chinese irrigation form in which a diversion weir feeds a canal and the canal fills ponds along it that are drawn on in the dry season, with the ponds topped up a few times each irrigation season. It says rotation of water by turns was practiced by the Northern Song, and that Zeng Gong described the management in the 11th century. The report gives the pond top-ups (one to four a season) in the present tense with no date, so they may describe today's system, and the area, flow and reservoir figures it gives are those of the 20th-century rebuilt system, not the old one; it gives no pond sizes or spacing.
