yoneji-1971-ujigami-chinju

Yoneji Minoru, "氏神鎮守と社会構造の関連に関する一考察(一)" ("ujigami chinju to shakai kōzō no kanren ni kansuru ichikōsatsu (1)", a study of the guardian shrine of the community and social structure, part 1, title translated; the page's own English subtitle is "A study on Ujigami-Chinju (guardian god of community) and social structure (1)"), 法學研究 (Journal of law, politics, and sociology) Vol. 44, No. 5 (May 1971), pp. 57-80, Keio University Law Research Association, on Keio's KOARA repository (in Japanese; https://koara.lib.keio.ac.jp/xoonips/modules/xoonips/download.php/an00224504-19710515-0057.pdf?file_id=136613)

What it is: The full text of the first part of a two-section journal article in sociology on the village's ujigami-chinju (the guardian shrine of the whole community): section one states the aim and section two covers the shrine's worship organization as part of village life. The remaining five sections are announced in its table of contents but are not in this part.

Why it applies, and its limits: It argues, with the Meiji 1882 Home Ministry notice and quotations from Ariga Kizaemon and Harada Toshiaki, that the ujigami-chinju was normally the shrine of the whole village, that its worship group was ordinarily the village itself, and that a village might hold other small shrines (household, kin-group, confraternity) under it. Its stated object is the modern shrine and village, mostly after the Meiji Restoration and the state's shrine policy, with the earlier period brought in only as continuity; it gives no sizes, plans or dates for any shrine's ground, and its scanned text carries some character errors.
