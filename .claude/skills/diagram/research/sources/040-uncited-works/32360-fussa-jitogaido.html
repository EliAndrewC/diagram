fussa-jitogaido

Fussa City Local History Room, "もっと知りたい 福生市の史跡と文化財（４）" ("Want to know more: Fussa City's historic sites and cultural properties 4", title translated), municipal leaflet (in Japanese; https://museum.fussa.tokyo.jp/cms/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/1-4_ido.pdf)

What it is: A two-page municipal leaflet on the "Den Jito Ido" well in Kumagawa, Fussa (Tokyo), a city-registered cultural property, with how the area was ruled in the Edo period and pictures of the well in the Meiji and Showa eras.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the well was a village's shared well until the 1960s, that a bannerman lord tradition says the Naga-shio lord dug it for a village short of water, and that Kumagawa village was split among the shogunate and two bannerman holdings, so it shows a shared well and divided lordship in one Kanto village. The Meiji picture shows a bucket well with a barrel curb, and the Showa one a hand pump; the dig is a tradition the page does not date, and only one of four such wells survives.
