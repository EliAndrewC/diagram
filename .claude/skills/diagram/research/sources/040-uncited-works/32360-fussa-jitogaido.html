fussa-jitogaido

Fussa City Local History Room, "もっと知りたい 福生市の史跡と文化財（４）" ("Want to know more: Fussa City's historic sites and cultural properties 4", title translated), municipal leaflet (in Japanese; https://museum.fussa.tokyo.jp/cms/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/1-4_ido.pdf)

What it is: A two-page municipal leaflet on the "Den Jito Ido" well in Kumagawa, Fussa (Tokyo), a city-registered cultural property, with how the area was ruled in the Edo period and two reference drawings of local wells, one of 1906 and one of the Showa 30s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the well was the district's shared well until the Showa 30s (1955-1964), that by tradition the bannerman Nagashio, lord of the village's Nabegayato district, dug it for his subjects short of water, and that Kumagawa village was split among the shogunate and two bannerman holdings, so it shows a shared well and divided lordship in one Kanto village. The Meiji picture shows a bucket well with a barrel curb, and the Showa one a hand pump; the dig is a tradition the page does not date, and only one of four such wells survives.
