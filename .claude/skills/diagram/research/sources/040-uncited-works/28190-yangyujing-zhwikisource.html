"養魚經" ("Yangyu Jing", the Fish-Raising Classic, title translated), attributed to Tao Zhu Gong, in the Yuhan Shanfang Congshu collection, edited by Ma Guohan, on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%A4%8A%E9%AD%9A%E7%B6%93)

What it is: A very short text on Chinese Wikisource giving a recipe for fish-pond farming that the editor's preface attributes to Tao Zhu Gong (Fan Li), with the editor's note on its sources, and a second paragraph on how to get large fish into a pond. It is the Qing-era collector's reconstruction of a lost book from passages quoted in the Qimin Yaoshu, and the page gives no other source for the text.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies a concrete early layout for a carp pond: six mu with nine islets, twenty gravid carp and four males each three chi long set in at the second month, and soft-shelled turtles added in the fourth, sixth and eighth months to keep the fish from leaving, with stated sale counts by size and year. The preface says the book is lost and the attribution to Fan Li is the editor's inference from the Shiji, so the text cannot be dated from the page itself, and the yields and prices read as a promoter's round figures, not a measured harvest. The pond is for profit on a large estate, not a village's food pond.
