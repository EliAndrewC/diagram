ndl-kaleido-soybean-ch2

National Diet Library, "第2章 食文化にみる大豆こばなし" ("Chapter 2: Soybean tales in food culture", title translated), in "本の万華鏡 第21回 大豆 -粒よりマメ知識-" ("Kaleidoscope of Books no. 21: Soybeans", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.ndl.go.jp/kaleido/entry/21/2.html)

What it is: A chapter of the National Diet Library's online exhibition on soybeans, built from books in its collection. It covers tofu, natto, soy sauce and miso, edamame and black beans, from the Nara period to the Meiji era.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the spread of soybean foods, saying tofu came from China in the Nara period and reached ordinary people in the Edo period, and it quotes a miso mix from a Kofukuji monastery diary of 1550 (soybeans, wheat, salt and water in equal measures) and the Engishiki's soy-sauce recipes. It also notes that edamame were sold on the branch in Edo and off the branch in Kyoto and Osaka. It is a short exhibition essay of anecdotes, so it gives no yields, farm layout or production scale.
