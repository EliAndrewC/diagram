daidokoro-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 台所 ("daidokoro", the kitchen, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B0%E6%89%80)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the kitchen, covering the word's origin, the history of cooking space from Paleolithic hearths through the Kofun-period kamado to the Edo-period and Meiji house, and modern layouts and fittings.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Japanese-house passages say the kamado reached the archipelago in the fifth century and moved cooking to a wall of the pit dwelling, that in the Edo period food and dishes were washed at the well or riverside and brought back to a wooden sink indoors, and that a commoner's kitchen in Meiji still split into a raised kamado floor and an earthen-floored sink area. Most of the page is about modern kitchen types, fittings and manufacturers, which say nothing of the setting, and the Japan history is a short summary whose Edo and Meiji statements are cited only to a kitchen-industry trade association's history page (キッチン・バス工業会), illustrated by one late-Edo kitchen in a relatively large commoner's house (minka) and a Showa-era reconstruction.
