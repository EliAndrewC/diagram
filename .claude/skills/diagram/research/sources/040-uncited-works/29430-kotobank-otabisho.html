kotobank-otabisho

"御旅所" ("otabisho, the festival resting place", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/御旅所-40405)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for the otabisho, the place where a shrine's portable palanquin (mikoshi) rests during a festival procession, gathering seven dictionary and encyclopedia entries, among them the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Hagiwara Tatsuo), Nipponica and the Britannica small-item entry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the otabisho in records from the late Heian and early Kamakura periods (a Hyohanki entry of 1153; the Hyakurensho record of 1159 for Gion), and says it could be a temporary spot or a grand building, in the parishioners' district or between the main hall and first torii. The entries disagree on distance (Nipponica: from near the shrine to tens of kilometers; Britannica: normally 1 to 2 km, within the village), so none fixes a typical distance; the entries name no particular village.
