kotobank-hijiriushi

Shogakukan Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (Selected Edition) and Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (old edition), "聖牛" ("hijiriushi", a river-training frame, title translated), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/聖牛)

What it is: Dictionary entries on the hijiriushi, a river-training structure of logs in the shape of a pyramid lying on its side, weighted with stone-filled gabion baskets (jakago). The page also quotes the World Encyclopedia's article on the ox-frame family of such works.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the build of the hijiriushi and says that this family of frames was set mainly in gravel-bed alluvial-fan rivers where piles could not be driven, held down by gabions of bamboo or wire. It gives no size, no date, no place and no spacing, so it supports the form and its use but not how many were set or how large.
