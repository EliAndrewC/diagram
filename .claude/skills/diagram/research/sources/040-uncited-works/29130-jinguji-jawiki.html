ja.wikipedia 神宮寺 ("jinguji", a Buddhist temple built to serve a shrine, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/神宮寺)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the jinguji, a Buddhist temple or hall attached to a Shinto shrine under the combination of the two faiths, with its alternative names, its origin and history, and its contrast with the chinju-sha, a shrine serving a temple.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says such temples arose in the Heian period when the gods were taken as protectors of Buddhism, that shrine-temple complexes such as Iwashimizu and Tsurugaoka Hachiman were run mainly by Buddhist monks, that they prospered under the shogunate and local lords in the Kamakura to Edo periods, and that nearly all were converted or lost at the Meiji separation of the two faiths since they had no parishioner households. It gives no figures, no named village example and no layout, and the page carries notices that it lacks sources and may contain original research.
