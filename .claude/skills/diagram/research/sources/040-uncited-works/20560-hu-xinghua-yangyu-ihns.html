hu-xinghua-yangyu-ihns

Hu Xinghua (head of Taiwan's Fisheries Agency), "中国养鱼简史" ("Zhongguo yangyu jianshi", a short history of fish culture in China, title translated), reposted from the agency's site on the Agricultural History of China site (in Chinese; http://agri-history.ihns.ac.cn/scholars/huxinhua1.htm)

What it is: A signed overview of fish farming in China from the Shang oracle bones through the Qing, including the Spring and Autumn carp-pond text attributed to Fan Li, Tang and Song fry trade, and the Ming and Qing mulberry-dike and fruit-dike fish ponds. It quotes many premodern passages and closes with seawater oyster and fish-pond culture on the Guangdong and Fujian coasts and in Taiwan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the pieces of premodern pond culture the setting could draw on: the four domestic carp species mixed in one pond from the late Tang and Song, fry gathered from rivers and sold, and the Pearl River delta and Taihu mulberry-dike ponds in which silkworm waste fed the fish. It is a short survey by an administrator, not a study; its quotations of the Fan Li text and of local gazetteers are given without citations of edition or page, and it is stronger on the Yangzi and Guangdong than elsewhere.
