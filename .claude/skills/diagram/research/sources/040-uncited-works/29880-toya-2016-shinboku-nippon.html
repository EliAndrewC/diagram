Toya Manabu, "神社空間を読み解く⑩神木" ("Reading the shrine space, 10: shinboku, the sacred tree," title translated), in the series "神社に行こう！" ("Let's go to a shrine!"), Nippon.com, 28 Jun 2016 (in Japanese; https://nippon.com/ja/views/b05210)

What it is: A magazine article by a Shinto priest on a shrine's object of worship and its sacred tree, naming the himorogi (an untouched wood worshipped whole as the chinju no mori, within which one outstanding giant tree is the shinboku) and giving named examples such as the great camphor trees of Gamo Hachiman and Chichibu Imamiya shrines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the whole chinju no mori is revered and that its most striking giant tree is taken as the kami's dwelling, so a shrine grove with one marked tree is a Japanese form. It lists examples only by shrine name, gives no tree sizes, ages or counts, and describes present-day shrines without saying how old the usage is.
