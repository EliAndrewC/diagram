ja.wikipedia 汲み取り式便所 ("kumitori-shiki benjo", the dip-out latrine, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/汲み取り式便所)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the collect-and-empty latrine, in which waste drops into a tank under the seat to be dipped out. It covers its history in Japan, its parts, the improved designs of the 1920s and after, its hygiene problems and its use as manure.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the dip-out method became the main one in Japan around the Edo period, replacing earlier latrines over a stream or a pit, that the contents were ladled out with a long-handled dipper and used as farm fertilizer, and that farmers and cities fell out over collection in 1912. Its detailed measures, such as the five-chamber tank of 1927 for ten users, and the later designs are from the 20th century, not the setting's era, and the section on structure carries a notice of missing sources.
