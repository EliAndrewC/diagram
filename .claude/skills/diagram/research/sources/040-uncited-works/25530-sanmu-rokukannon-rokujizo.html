sanmu-rokukannon-rokujizo

Sanmu City Board of Education, "六観音と六地蔵" ("The Six Kannon and the Six Jizo", title translated), in its pages on city-designated cultural properties, on the Sanmu city website (in Japanese; https://www.city.sammu.lg.jp/kyouiku/bunkazai-rekishi/bunkazai/shi-shitei/page001513.html)

What it is: A municipal cultural-property entry on two stone monuments in Sanmu, Chiba: a six-sided Rokukannon pillar of 1811 at Tomiguchi, and a Rokukannon and Rokujizo pair of 1662 in the front courtyard of the main hall of Shinko-ji, each face carved in relief. It transcribes the 1811 inscription, which names the village's parishioners as donors, and gives the designation dates.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that Edo-period villages in Kazusa raised stone images of the six Kannon and six Jizo, dates two of them, and puts the Shinko-ji pair in the temple's front courtyard. It does not say how many such monuments a village had, who beyond the named donors paid, or where the Tomiguchi pillar stood in its village; the 1662 pair is described as among the oldest of its kind in the prefecture, so the 1811 one is the later and more usual date.
