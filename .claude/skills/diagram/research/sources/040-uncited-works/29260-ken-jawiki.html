ja.wikipedia 間 ("ken", the span, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/間)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the ken, the traditional Japanese length of six shaku, covering its origin as the spacing between a building's posts, its use in land surveying, and its fixing at 10/33 m by the 1891 law.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports reading ken as a house-bay unit that became a survey unit, and gives the lengths by ruler: 6.5 shaku under Nobunaga, 6.3 under Hideyoshi's survey, 6.01 in the Edo period, with the page noting actual values varied by period and region. It also gives the Edo-ma (6 shaku, tatami 5.8 by 2.9 shaku) and Kyo-ma (6.5 shaku, tatami 6.3 by 3.15 shaku) house modules. The exact metric figure comes from the 1891 definition, and the page carries only a general note for the earlier ken, so a premodern distance in ken has no single metric value from it.
