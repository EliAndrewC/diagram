Kagawa Prefecture, Rural Development Division, "[農業技術と伝統文化] ため池王国「さぬき」" ("Agricultural technique and tradition: Sanuki, kingdom of reservoir ponds", title translated), in Furusato Bunka Takarabako 2016 (in Japanese; https://www.pref.kagawa.lg.jp/nosonseibi/green/takarabako/6.html)

What it is: A prefectural culture-and-heritage page on the irrigation ponds (tameike) of Sanuki: the network of ponds over the plain, Mannoike, the pond plugs and the June pulling of the plug, the old water-sharing customs (including incense-stick watering), and the hand-dug Miroku tunnel and the Hoonen dam.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the sharing of water in a dry province in three steps, filling the ponds, dividing between parent and child ponds, and giving each field its share, and names the incense-stick watering, where a field took water only while a stick of a length set by its size burned. It also says rice farming in Sanuki is thought to have covered about 22,000 ha by the Nara period. It gives no date for the water-sharing customs, saying only that Kagawa once had them, and its newer parts (the Kagawa canal of 1981, the Hoanko reservoir of 2009, the Hoonen concrete dam) are modern.
