mlit-chugen-kanazawa

Kanazawa City Tourism Association, "Chugen (Household Servant): Status and Role" (仲間（奉公人）の身分と役割), interpretive-sign text prepared in fiscal 2018, in the Japan Tourism Agency's Sightseeing Database (in English and Japanese; https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/H30-00650.html)

What it is: A bilingual explanatory text on the chugen, the household servants of Edo-period samurai houses, who were quartered in the gate longhouse or commuted: their origins, pay, clothing and duties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives what a samurai household's lowest servants did and were paid: an average of 360 kg of rice or three ryo a year from 1830 to 1868, plus five cups of brown rice a day, with duties of watching the gate, keeping the grounds, bringing water, drawing baths, caring for horses and accompanying the master. It says poorer households made one chugen do several jobs but gives no number of servants per household or size of their quarters, and it is a sign text from Kanazawa's Nagamachi that speaks of Edo-period samurai households in general without saying whether its figures are Kaga's, and its pay figure is a market average over 1830-1868.
