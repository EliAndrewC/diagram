Feng Dongqing, "山西平遥古城历史、现状、传说、文物古迹" ("Shanxi Pingyao gucheng lishi, xianzhuang, chuanshuo, wenwu guji", the history, present, legends and monuments of the old city of Pingyao, Shanxi, title translated), Sina blog, 2012 (in Chinese; https://blog.sina.com.cn/s/blog_a490495801013itf.html)

What it is: A blog post by a writer on historic architecture about the walled county town of Pingyao, Shanxi. It covers its history, its streets and lanes, its courtyard houses, its city god temple and a detailed account of the Ming wall, gates, towers and moat.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the form of a walled county seat as rebuilt in 1370: a square wall 6,162.7 m around and 10 m high, six gates each with an outer gate court, 72 watch towers spaced 60 to 100 m apart, four corner towers, and a moat 3 m wide, with a city god temple paired with the yamen. Its figures are not sourced, the founding in the Zhou dynasty and the legends of the turtle shape and the 3,000 battlements are tradition, and its Qing details come from repairs of 1850 to 1856, so it speaks for the late Ming and Qing town.
