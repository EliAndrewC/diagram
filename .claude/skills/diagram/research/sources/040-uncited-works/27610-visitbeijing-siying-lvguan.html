Sun Zhen, "私营旅馆在中国的流变过程:准不准开曾是个问题" (How private inns changed in China: whether they were permitted was once a question, title translated), Beijing Daily, 19 April 2017, on Beijing Tourism Network (in Chinese; https://www.visitbeijing.com.cn/article/47QraNqMfGe)

What it is: A newspaper feature reposted on Beijing's tourism site, surveying private inns in China from the Warring States through the Ming and Qing: the Shang Yang ban on inns, the Han and Jin inn types, Tang and Song market rules, Yuan Hangzhou, and the Ming-Qing native-place guildhalls in Beijing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the setting a chronological account of what kinds of inns existed and who ran them: Han 'private lodgings', 'ni lü' inns and country 'ke lu', Pan Yue's Jin-era memorial on keeping inns, Tang-Song market-ward rules, a Yuan report of Hangzhou inns of ten or twelve rooms, and Beijing guildhalls of one to several dozen courtyards, 66 of them Jiangxi. It names three reference books as its basis but cites no passage, gives no inn sizes except those few, and covers the whole empire across two millennia rather than one place or period.
