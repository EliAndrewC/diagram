zh.wikipedia 李坑村 ("Likeng village", title translated), Chinese Wikipedia (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/李坑村)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Likeng, a historic-and-cultural-name village in Wuyuan County, Shangrao, Jiangxi, in the Huizhou cultural area. It gives the village's lineage history, its Qing-era merchant population, the public buildings it once held and the buildings that survive.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in the late Qing, Likeng (then called Litian) was one of the larger and richer villages of eastern Wuyuan, with many men trading away from home, mostly in tea, and that until 1961 it still had twelve ancestral halls, seventeen temples and public buildings such as a pavilion and a pagoda; this supports a lineage village of Huizhou carrying a dense set of shared halls and temples. Its picture is the late Qing of one wealthy tea-trade village, not an ordinary farming one, and it records the later losses and the surviving buildings (four Ming houses, dozens of Qing ones) without giving dates for when the halls and temples were built or sizes for any of them.
