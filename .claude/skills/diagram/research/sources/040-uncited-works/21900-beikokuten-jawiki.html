beikokuten-jawiki

ja.wikipedia "米穀店" ("beikokuten", the rice shop, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%B1%B3%E7%A9%80%E5%BA%97)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the beikokuten or komeya, the rice retailer, with the shop's goods and its history from Edo rice pounders to the postwar rationing system and the fall in shop numbers to about 40,000 by 2022.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Edo passage says itinerant rice pounders (komeitsuki) walked the streets with a mortar and pestle and were replaced by shops by the end of the shogunate, and that those shops, run by rice wholesalers and fudasashi (the brokers who sold retainers' stipend rice), polished rice with a foot-driven mortar and displayed rice from several regions in tubs. The Meiji, Taisho and Showa sections and the numbers are about the modern trade, so the page speaks to a premodern town only in those few sentences, and those rest on a single web page ("米屋の歴史") that it cites with no author or publisher.
