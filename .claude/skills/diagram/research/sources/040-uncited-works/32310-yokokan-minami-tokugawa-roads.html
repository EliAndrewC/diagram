yokokan-minami-tokugawa-roads

Yokokan Minami site (yokokan-minami.com), "徳川家康が造った道" ("The roads Tokugawa Ieyasu built", title translated), in its history section on highway travelers, with a bibliography (in Japanese; https://www.yokokan-minami.com/site/rekishi/kaido_toku.html)

What it is: A history page on the Edo-period Five Highways and their post stations, giving road widths, surfacing, station spacing, the rules for official stations, and the cost of daimyo processions, with three books listed as references.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states Tokaido road widths of 3-4 ken (5.4-7.2 m) in general, 5 ken (9 m) or more in the main castle towns and 2 ken (3.6 m) in steep mountain stretches; an excavated Nikko highway about 9 m wide, later 9.4 m, narrowed to 5.7 m after Meiji; gravel surfaces with stone paving on slopes; and Tokaido station spacings of 1.7 km shortest, 9.2 km average and 16.6 km longest. These are the Tokaido and a few other trunk roads under shogunal rule, not a village lane, and the figures are given without a note of which book each comes from.
