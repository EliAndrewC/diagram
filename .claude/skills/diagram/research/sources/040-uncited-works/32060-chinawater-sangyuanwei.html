chinawater-sangyuanwei

Chen Sijie (reporter), "桑园围：岭南水乡的水利传奇" ("Sangyuanwei: a water-engineering legend of the Lingnan water country", title translated), 中国水利报 (China Water Resources News), 12 June 2025, on chinawater.com.cn (in Chinese; https://chinawater.com.cn/wh/202506/t20250612_1065496.html)

What it is: A newspaper feature on the Sangyuanwei dike-pond polder in Foshan, Guangdong, a World Heritage irrigation structure, giving its history from the Northern Song dikes to the Ming closed polder, its sluices, its management and its mulberry-dike fishpond farming.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the first dikes along the West and North Rivers to 1102-1110 and the closed polder to 1396 under the local gentry Chen Bomin, counts 24 surviving sluices, gives irrigation of up to 200,000 mu at the peak against 62,000 mu now, and describes the mulberry-dike fishpond cycle (silkworm waste to fish, pond mud to mulberry). The figures are an expert's quoted summary in a 2025 news piece, with no source for any date or area, and it does not date the mulberry-dike fishpond cycle, saying only that it arose once the polder was built and rice land had spread within it; the polder is a Pearl River delta system of dikes, sluices and fishponds, which it sets beside the Yangtze's yuan and wei polders as China's three premodern polder forms.
