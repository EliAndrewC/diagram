Nanjing City Culture and Tourism Bureau (from the Nanjing City Wall Research Society), "【遗产大观】寿县城墙" ("Shouxian city wall", title translated), World Heritage nomination research page, on wlj.nanjing.gov.cn (in Chinese; https://wlj.nanjing.gov.cn/ztzl/mcq/syyj/202309/t20230905_4003568.html)

What it is: A heritage page on the walls of Shou County (Shouxian), Anhui, a Ming and Qing department-seat (zhou) walled town, which the page sets apart from the prefecture-seat walls of the joint nomination, with its dimensions, gates and flood-resisting features.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a roughly square wall of 7.147 km circuit, 8.33 m high, 18-22 m at the base and 4-10 m at the top, rammed earth faced with brick, four gates each with a semicircular barbican yard, three of them entered off axis (the south gate runs straight through), a moat and a street grid from the four gates, so it is a measured example of a provincial walled town. The form is Ming and Qing, mostly reshaped for floods, and the page is a nomination write-up that names its source only as the book 中国明清城墙 (China's Ming and Qing city walls). It says only the east and north gates keep their original form, and its 3.65 square km is the enclosure of a department (zhou) seat, not a county seat.
