ja.wikipedia 倭館 ("Waegwan", the Japanese trading settlement in Joseon Korea, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%80%AD%E9%A4%A8)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the wakan, the Japanese residential and trading settlements in southern Korea under Joseon, from the medieval three ports to the Edo-period Tsushima compounds at Busan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives sizes for a walled foreign trading quarter: the Duomopo compound of 1607 at about 10,000 tsubo, the Choryang compound of 1678 at about 100,000 tsubo (stated as 25 times Dejima), with 400 to 500 residents, and a bounded set of buildings inside, among them a trade hall, a shrine, a temple and a guard post. It also says Japanese residents were forbidden to leave the compound and women were barred from entering. Its figures are the article's own and the page does not cite where each came from, and it describes a Japanese enclave in Korea rather than a settlement within either country's ordinary towns.
