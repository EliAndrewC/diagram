Beijing Municipal Cultural Heritage Bureau (北京市文物局), Beijing Dajue Temple and Tuancheng Administration Office, "大觉寺寺庙建筑布局和艺术（上）" ("The layout and art of Dajue Temple's buildings, part 1", title translated), posted 27 December 2018 (in Chinese; https://wwj.beijing.gov.cn/bjww/wwjzzcslm/1731063/1731066/djs/1731072/1731295/index.html)

What it is: A heritage-bureau page on the Dajue Temple at the foot of Yangtai Mountain in Haidian, Beijing: its axial layout, five building zones, the halls on the axis, the bell and drum towers and the white stupa.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the plan of a large Liao-origin Chinese monastery set on a slope: an east-west axis with the gate, bell and drum towers, Hall of Heavenly Kings, Mahavira Hall, Amitayus Hall and Compassion Altar, a site of nearly 40,000 square meters, about 400 m by 100 m, facing east. It adds that the main halls are Ming and Qing and went five hundred years without major damage, but the mountain gate was rebuilt in 1996, the page says the white stupa's date and purpose are unknown, and the temple has imperial-lodge courtyards, so it is a hillside royal-patronage temple and not a county or village one; this is part one of the article.
