fukuoka-museum-chikuzen-nosho

Fukuoka City Museum (福岡市博物館), "No. 066 筑前の農書展" ("No. 066, exhibition of Chikuzen's agricultural treatises", title translated), Archives, special exhibition leaflet, July 19 to September 25, 1994 (in Japanese; https://museum.city.fukuoka.jp/archives/leaflet/066/index.html)

What it is: The text of a 1994 exhibition leaflet from Fukuoka City Museum on the Edo-period agricultural treatises (nosho) connected with Chikuzen Province, the Kuroda domain of 520,000 koku around Fukuoka. It introduces the treatises and their authors, among them Miyazaki Yasusada's Nogyo Zensho (first printed 1696), Okura Nagatsune's works and the Meiji writer Hayashi Onri.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the farming of one western domain: new-field reclamation along the shore and hills until the Genroku era, the Chikuzen rice called Tsukushi-mai, rapeseed begun as a local product in that same period, wax-tree (haze) growing as a sideline after the 1732 Kyoho famine, whale-oil spreading against rice pests (begun in Chikuzen in the later 1600s), and a warrior writing about growing vegetables in the sand of his own plot in a castle-town district of Fukuoka. It is a brief exhibition summary of the books, not the books themselves, and gives few figures (it states about 100,000 deaths in Chikuzen in the 1732 famine).
