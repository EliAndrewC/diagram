shinjuku-rekihaku-kurazukuri

Shinjuku Historical Museum, "蔵造り建築" ("kurazukuri kenchiku", earthen-walled storehouse-style building, title translated), permanent-exhibition explanation sheet 5, on regasu-shinjuku.or.jp (in Japanese; https://regasu-shinjuku.or.jp/rekihaku/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/b9b0543df6ac4e52462f567545223b92.pdf)

What it is: A museum explanation sheet on kurazukuri, the plaster-walled fireproof merchant shop (mise-gura) of Edo and Tokyo: its history from the 1720 shogunate notice allowing it, its wall-building steps and its design features.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Edo plaster walls could reach 30 cm, that Kawagoe storehouse walls took about ten coats over about three years while a shop's walls were 12-18 cm and took about half that time, and quotes the 1720 notice allowing earth-walled and tile-roofed houses, so it gives a dated, sized account of the fireproof town house. It is about Edo and Kawagoe merchant shops, not a rural or provincial building, the 1720 date is Edo-period middle, and the Kawagoe figures are cited from a 1976 booklet.
