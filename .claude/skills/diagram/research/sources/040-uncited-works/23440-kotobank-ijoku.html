kotobank-ijoku

"居職" ("ijoku", work done at home, title translated), from the Daijisen and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B1%85%E8%81%B7)

What it is: A short Kotobank page of dictionary entries for 居職, the trade done at home, set against deshoku, the trade done at a site away from home.

Why it applies, and its limits: It splits craftsmen into those who work where they live (it names tailors and seal-cutters) and those who go to the job (plasterers, roofers and gardeners), a split that bears on which trades kept a workshop in the house. Its two dated examples are from the 1810s-20s and 1871-72 and are literary, it names only those few trades, and it says nothing of where such workers lived or how the workshop sat in the house.
