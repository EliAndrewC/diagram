Kato Masafumi (加藤理文), "【理文先生のお城がっこう】城歩き編 第54回 国宝天守に行こう③彦根城" ("Masafumi-sensei's castle school, castle-walking edition no. 54: let's visit a National Treasure keep, part 3, Hikone Castle", title translated), 16 December 2022, on Shirobito, a castle site recognized by the Japan Castle Association (in Japanese; https://shirobito.jp/article/1670)

What it is: A children's-level explainer by a Japan Castle Association director, dated 2022-12-16, on Hikone Castle in Shiga: its founding by the Ii house after 1601, its construction from 1603, its escape from the 1878 demolition order, and a floor-by-floor description of the three-tier keep.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a dated construction record for one daimyo castle: work began in 1603, the main parts were nearly done in 1606, a second phase of enlargement and castle-town widening ran 1616-22, and the whole was finished in 1642, forty years in all. It also describes the keep's formal entrance by a ground-level hall and a second entrance through attached turrets. The castle was built as a shogunate stronghold with help-labor from 12 daimyo of seven provinces in its first phase (later work was the domain's alone), and that phase was fast because stone and old timber were reused from abandoned nearby castles (Sawayama, Otsu, Nagahama, Azuchi) and the keep was moved from Otsu, so its scale and its pace are exceptional, and the page says nothing of the castle town's streets or its lower-ranking households.
