"春秋左氏傳/成公" ("Chunqiu Zuozhuan, Duke Cheng", title translated), the Zuo Commentary's chapters for Duke Cheng's eighteen years, on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E6%98%A5%E7%A7%8B%E5%B7%A6%E6%B0%8F%E5%82%B3/%E6%88%90%E5%85%AC)

What it is: The full classical text of the Zuo Commentary on the Spring and Autumn Annals for the reign of Duke Cheng of Lu, year by year from his first year to his eighteenth, each year as the terse annal followed by the commentary, on the Chinese Wikisource transcription site. The page carries the text only, with no modern commentary or translation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a period account of how states of the Zhou world fought, allied and ruled, and it carries a few remarks on land and cities: the Jin lords weigh a capital site by its soil and water (the thin, shallow ground of Xunxia against the thick, deep ground of Xintian), the Qi envoy argues that the ancient kings laid out fields "to suit the soil" rather than by the chariot's convenience, and the commentator says the Ju people trusted their rough country, did not repair their city walls, and lost three cities within days. It is the speech and politics of the ruling houses of the Spring and Autumn period, centuries before any form the setting models, so it speaks to a state's reasoning and not to how a village or its fields looked, and these remarks are scattered asides in long narratives of war and court.
