aric-tameike-kensetsu

Agriculture and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), "溜池の建設" ("Building irrigation reservoirs", title translated), in 国土づくりを支えた技術, 水土の礎 (in Japanese; https://www.suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part3/01/04.html)

What it is: A short institutional page on how Japanese irrigation reservoirs were built: dam height and length, the intake pipes and spillways, how embankments were rammed, and the difficulty of handling floods during construction, with the Mannou reservoir as its examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the early form of a village reservoir as a bank under 2 m high and 100 to 250 m long holding 1,000 to 40,000 cubic meters, often bent into two, three or four sides, with wooden intake pipes and a spillway cut at one end of the bank. The page says no ancient reservoir survives in its original form, so these figures are its reasoned reconstruction, and its dated examples (853, and the 1849-1853 repair with nearly 400,000 laborers) are of one very large reservoir in Kagawa.
