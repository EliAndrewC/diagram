takao-2005-edo-streets

Takao Yoshiki (高尾善希), "（江戸形成史）泥だらけだった江戸の町 - 江戸の街路と浅草砂 -" ("History of Edo's formation: Edo's muddy town, its streets and Asakusa sand", title translated), on the blog "江戸時代研究の休み時間" (Edo-period research break), 27 August 2005 (in Japanese; http://takaoyoshiki.cocolog-nifty.com/edojidai/2005/08/post_cd16.html)

What it is: A blog post by a historian of the Tokugawa period on how Edo's streets were surfaced in the early 1600s, quoting Miura Joshin's account of sand laid on the roads and an Edo town ordinance of 1648, followed by reader comments.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports early Edo main roads surfaced with sand and gravel, and an ordinance of 1648 telling each town to lay Asakusa sand mixed with sea sand on bad stretches of road, crowned in the middle so they would drain, and to keep the street sewers and side ditches clear. It also says roads stayed so muddy that people wore high clogs. The author states that what the sand was is unknown and that his reading of Asakusa sand as gravel is his own guess, he does not know how far the order was carried out, and the ordinance concerns main roads (kaido), not back lanes. It covers Edo before about 1650 only.
