aric-shoen-development

ARIC (一般社団法人農業農村整備情報総合センター), "開発の花ひらく、荘園" ("The flowering of development: the shoen", title translated), in "土地に応じた開発" (Development suited to the land), "国土づくりの歴史", in Suido no Ishizue (水土の礎) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/03/03.html)

What it is: A page on medieval estate (shoen) land clearing in Japan from the late Heian to early Kamakura peak, with case notes on Todaiji's clearings, the Azumino fan, Todaiji estates in the Fukui plain and the Tomita estate map of Owari (1327).

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows where medieval reclamation went and why: ditches first, then fields on thick-soiled gentle fans and the back marshes behind natural levees, with Todaiji's clearings often near 100 ha, and settlements placed on natural levees in Owari. It says the estate system lasted from the 8th to the 16th century, with about a quarter of estates in the Kinai. It is a broad summary in general terms, and its sizes belong to a great temple's clearings, not a village's: to Todaiji's early estates in the Hokuriku, which the page calls early (初期の) clearings rather than ones of the late-Heian peak.
