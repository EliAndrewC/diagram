kotobank-gisou

"義倉" ("gisou", the famine-relief granary, title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, Nipponica, Yamakawa and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%BE%A9%E5%80%89-50697)

What it is: A long page of dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the gisou, a granary system for famine relief, tracing it through Sui, Tang, Song, Ming and Qing China, Korea and Japan from the Ritsuryo period to the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives figures for the system as written in the codes and in surviving ledgers: Tang 2 sho of millet per mu, Japan's nine household grades with only 10 to 20 percent of households paying in the Echizen and Awa records, and about 80 percent of Awa households in 750 below the lowest grade. It is Japan's Nara-period state scheme and China's tax and charity granaries, so it does not describe how a village stocked its own store, and the Edo revival is only sketched.
