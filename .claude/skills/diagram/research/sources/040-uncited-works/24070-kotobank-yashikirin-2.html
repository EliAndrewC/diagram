kotobank-yashikirin-2

"屋敷林" ("yashikirin", the wood around a farmstead, title translated), dictionary entries from Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/屋敷林-648240)

What it is: A Kotobank page on the yashikirin, a wood planted around a farm house plot. Its main text is a signed Heibonsha entry by Tsutsumi Toshio on what the trees were for and how they varied, with a note on the related woods that belonged to a village's households.

Why it applies, and its limits: The Heibonsha entry says the outer belt marked the boundary and blocked wind, fire and sand, as with the tsuijimatsu pines of the Hikawa plain in Shimane and the zelkova and oaks of the Kanto plain, and that inside it were cedar, cypress, zelkova and oak for timber and fuel, bamboo for shoots and poles, and fruit trees in the garden. The old Heibonsha entry on the yashiki adds that a cadastral survey left out the four-sided strip and the grove of a plot. It describes the form over Japan generally and says its decline is recent; the entry gives no size or share of the plot a grove took.
