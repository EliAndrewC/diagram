kotobank-uranagaya

"裏長屋" ("uranagaya", the back-alley row house, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (abridged edition) and the older Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/裏長屋)

What it is: A Kotobank dictionary page defining the uranagaya, a row house on a back street or alley, with quoted passages from the older Heibonsha encyclopedia on the Edo and Osaka rental houses of that name.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the uranagaya in the townsman districts of Edo and Osaka, rented to craftsmen, peddlers and day laborers, and quotes the basic Edo town-block plan behind them: 60 ken square in kyō-ma measure, with a 20 ken square open lot (kaishochi) in the middle and the street side cut into lots 20 ken deep and 5 to 6 ken wide, the row houses standing on the back of such lots, away from the street. It is about big-city lower-class rentals only, gives no size for a row house or its rooms (the block plan is Edo's general layout, not the row house's own), and says nothing of a provincial town or a village.
