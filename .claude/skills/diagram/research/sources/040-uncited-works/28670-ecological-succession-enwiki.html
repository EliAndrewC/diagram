What it is: The English Wikipedia article on ecological succession, the change in an ecological community's species over time, covering primary and secondary succession, seral stages and the climax concept, the factors that steer it, and its history from Buffon to Odum.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what happens on cleared or abandoned ground: secondary succession after logging, agriculture or fire runs from grasses and herbs to small bushes and trees, with fast-growing pioneer species giving way to shade-tolerant ones. It adds that grasslands are held by fire and grazing and tilled grassland needs a minimum of 100 years to recover, and that ponds filling to dry land is called into question for lack of evidence. Its examples are North American (Kansas, Acadia, Indiana Dunes) and northern European (Blekinge) and it gives no rate or species list for East Asia, so it supports the general sequence only.
