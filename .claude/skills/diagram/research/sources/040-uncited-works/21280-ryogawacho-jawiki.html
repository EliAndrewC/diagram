ryogawacho-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 両側町 ("ryogawacho", a town on both sides of a street, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%A1%E5%81%B4%E7%94%BA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the ryogawacho, the town form in which houses face each other across a street, tracing it from Heian-kyo blocks through medieval Kyoto to Edo and Osaka.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a sequence for how Kyoto's townsmen turned from walled blocks facing inward to shopfronts facing the street, and says the paired street-sides became one town unit in the fifteenth century in answer to wartime need for self-defense. It names Edo's Nihonbashi and Kyobashi and Osaka's Senba as castle-town examples. The article carries notices for missing citations and possible original research, and rests almost entirely on one 2011 policy-studies paper about Kyoto, so the Edo and Osaka claims are thinly sourced.
