ja.wikipedia 有備館 ("Yubikan", the Yubi school, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9C%89%E5%82%99%E9%A4%A8)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Yubikan, the study hall of the Iwadeyama Date family, a branch retainer house of Sendai domain, in present Osaki, Miyagi, with its history, its garden and its restoration after the 2011 earthquake.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places a small domain-retainer school at a retainer lord's castle: the thatched shoin-style building was put up in 1633 as a temporary residence, became a study hall about 1691 under the name Shungakukan, was moved that same year to its present site at the castle's north foot and renamed Yubikan, and was given a stroll garden about 1715, so its present site is not the one it was built on. It is the school of a retainer house at its castle town, which the page calls a terakoya-like study hall and the forerunner of later primary schooling, so it is no model for a domain's main academy; the page gives no dimensions and notes that the main hall collapsed in the 2011 earthquake and was repaired.
