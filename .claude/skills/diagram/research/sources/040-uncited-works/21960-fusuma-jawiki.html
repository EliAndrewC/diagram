ja.wikipedia 襖 ("fusuma", the sliding paper-covered door, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%A5%96)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the fusuma, the paper- or cloth-covered sliding panel that divides Japanese rooms: its name, its frame, pull handles, its descent from the partition screens of Heian shinden halls, gold-ground wall paintings, how it is layered with paper, and its association with white.

Why it applies, and its limits: It traces the fusuma from the standing screens and fixed panels of the Heian shinden to the sliding two-groove form seen in the Tale of Genji, and gives the oldest surviving example (a 1197 door at the Kongobuji Fudodo on Koyasan, whose frame is cypress, with only the frame original). It also says the Momoyama period put cut-in metal pull handles on them and that the gold-ground painting (Kano Eitoku) was for shoin halls of the powerful, so it describes rank rooms rather than a farmhouse, and the page itself carries a notice that it lacks sources.
