kyoto-nishimachi-bugyosho-ato

Kyoto City Tourism Association, "西町奉行所跡" ("Nishi-machi bugyosho ato", site of the West Town Magistrate's Office, title translated), a historical marker text on the Kyoto Kanko Navi site (in Japanese; https://ja.kyoto.travel/tourism/single02.php?category_id=9&tourism_id=192)

What it is: A tourism-association page reproducing the text of the marker at the site of the Kyoto West Town Magistrate's Office near Senbon-Oshikoji, describing the Edo-period office, its staff and its duties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the office's staffing in figures (20 yoriki and 50 doshin for each of the two offices), its duties (city administration, justice, police, tax collection on shogunal land in the home provinces, suits over temple and shrine land) and that the East and West offices served in alternating months and relied on the towns' own self-governing bodies. It also dates the post: first set up in 1600 and permanent from 1668. It describes Kyoto only, which had two magistrates and a special remit, so it does not show how a typical castle town's magistracy was staffed.
