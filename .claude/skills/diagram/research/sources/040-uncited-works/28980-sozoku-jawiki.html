ja.wikipedia 宗族 ("sozoku", the Chinese patrilineal lineage, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宗族)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the zongzu, the patrilineal lineage of Han Chinese, covering its genealogy books, its ancestral halls and shared property, its spread in central and south China, and the strong-in-the-south, weaker-in-the-north contrast.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a lineage kept a genealogy, an ancestral hall and common land, that it grew among new landowners after the late Tang, and that north Chinese lineages were weaker and less often lived in one village than southern ones. Its only footnoted source, James L. Watson's 1982 review "Chinese Kinship Reconsidered: Anthropological Perspectives on Historical Research", backs only its sections on lineage associations and ancestral halls; the overview that gives the late-Tang growth and the north-south contrast cites nothing, the page is marked a stub, and its dates are loose, so it gives the form of the institution, not a count or a date for a given village.
