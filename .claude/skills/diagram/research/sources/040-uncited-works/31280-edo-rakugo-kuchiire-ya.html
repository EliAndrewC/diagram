edo-rakugo-kuchiire-ya

"落語の中の言葉104 口入屋" ("Words in rakugo no. 104: the kuchiire-ya", the employment broker, title translated), 20 September 2013, in the blog 落語と江戸 ("Rakugo and Edo", title translated), on Seesaa (in Japanese; https://9rakugo-fan.seesaa.net/article/201309article_1.html)

What it is: A blog post explaining the Edo employment broker (kuchiire-ya, also jinjuku) that rakugo mentions, largely by quoting Ishii Ryosuke, the Morisada manko, Minami Kazuo's study of Edo society and a sample guarantor's contract, with a broker's handbill.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated Edo facts through its quotations: the shogunate counted over 390 brokers in Edo in 1710 and grouped them into 13 guilds of 30, limited them to 202 in 1730, and listed 408 brokers in 1864; it also says half-year service for maids and menservants went through brokers while apprenticeships usually went through relatives, and that Edo yearly pay for a manservant was about 3 ryo. The facts are Edo's and a samurai household's labor market, secondhand through a blogger's quotations, and the author says the broker's details are not well known; it is about city servants, not village farm labor.
