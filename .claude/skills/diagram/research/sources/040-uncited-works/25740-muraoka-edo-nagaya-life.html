muraoka-edo-nagaya-life

Muraoka Shoji (pen name), "江戸庶民の暮らし(1)" ("the lives of Edo commoners, part 1", title translated), in the Edo dining-culture section of the site "日本食文化の醤油を知る" ("learning about soy sauce in Japanese food culture", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-8.html)

What it is: A long personal-site article on how Edo's commoners lived in tenements: the front row of shops against the back-alley rows, the landlord's duties and income, the alley gates, the shared well and privy, rents and daily earnings, and the rental and secondhand-clothing trades. It ends with a list of the books and museum pages it draws on.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the layout of an Edo townsman block in concrete terms: a block of 60 ken (about 118 m) a side, 20-ken house lots, back tenements of 9 shaku by 2 ken reached by a lane of 3 to 6 shaku, gates shut at dusk, and rents of 300 to 500 mon a month in the Bunka-Bunsei years (1804-1830). It is about the capital's townsman districts only, mostly the late Edo period, and it does not tie its figures to a named source on the page, only to a general reference list at the foot. It also contradicts itself once on the standard tenement, giving 9 shaku by 2 ken as a 3.6 m frontage and 2.7 m depth where elsewhere the 9 shaku (about 2.7 m) is the frontage.
