Lei Guojun, "南京记忆|老行当：钉马掌" ("Nanjing memories: the old trade of shoeing horses", title translated), Nanjing Local Chronicles Office (南京市地方志工作办公室), 2024 (in Chinese; https://dfz.nanjing.gov.cn/gzdt/202404/t20240425_4216465.html)

What it is: A 2024 recollection on a city local-gazetteer office's site of how horses and mules were shod in Nanjing in the 1950s and 1960s: the shops by the city gates, the wooden frame the animal was tied to, and the steps of trimming the hoof and nailing on an iron shoe.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the tools and steps of shoeing as a smith practiced it with iron U-shaped shoes of four to six nail holes, in a frame of two posts, and where the shops stood, near city gates and markets, serving cart drivers. Its evidence is the author's memory of mid-twentieth-century Nanjing, when carts of mules and horses still ran, and it ends when trucks replaced them in the 1960s and 1970s, so it does not date shoeing in earlier centuries.
