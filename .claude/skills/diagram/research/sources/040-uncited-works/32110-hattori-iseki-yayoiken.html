hattori-iseki-yayoiken

NPO Moriyama Yayoi Iseki Kenkyukai (守山弥生遺跡研究会), "服部遺跡" ("the Hattori site", title translated), home page of the Hattori site website, Yayoiken (in Japanese; https://hattori-iseki.yayoiken.jp)

What it is: The home page of a research society's website on the Hattori site in Moriyama, Shiga, a stacked site of remains from the Jomon to the Heian periods in the Yasu River delta, with news items, the history of its discovery and excavation, and the local archaeology center.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the remains lie in separate layers 1.5 to 3 m down because the Yasu River's floods buried a rice-paddy and village landscape repeatedly at intervals of 200 to 300 years, and that the site was found in 1974 during the diversion-channel works and mostly destroyed by them by 1979. The layers it describes are mostly of Yayoi date, long before the period the setting is modeled on, and the page gives no counts or sizes of fields or houses; it supports flooding of a lake-edge delta as a cause of burial and abandonment, not a layout.
