hattori-iseki-yayoiken

NPO Moriyama Yayoi Iseki Kenkyukai (守山弥生遺跡研究会), "服部遺跡" ("the Hattori site", title translated), home page of the Hattori site website, Yayoiken (in Japanese; https://hattori-iseki.yayoiken.jp)

What it is: The home page of a research society's website on the Hattori site in Moriyama, Shiga, a stacked site of remains from the Jomon to the Heian periods in the Yasu River delta, with news items, the history of its discovery and excavation, and the local archaeology center.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the remains lie in separate layers 1.5 to 3 m down because the Yasu River's floods buried a rice-paddy and village landscape repeatedly at intervals of 200 to 300 years, and that the site was found in 1974 during the diversion-channel works and that its excavated part was destroyed when the works resumed in 1979, though the page estimates that more than twice that area survives under the embankments and the paddies beside them. The layers it describes run from the Jomon to the Heian period, all centuries before the Edo period (though the page notes the river still flooded in the early modern period), and the page gives no counts or sizes of fields or houses; it supports flooding of a lake-edge delta as a cause of burial and abandonment, not a layout.
