akishima-hanai-no-ido

Akishima City, "花井の井戸" ("Hanai no ido", the Hanai well, title translated), in "あきしま 水と記憶の物語" ("Akishima: a tale of water and memory", site title translated), section "空間でたどる" (follow it by place) (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/akishima-arch/top/space/spaceA/A13.html)

What it is: A short city-archive page on the Hanai well in the old Haijima village area of Akishima, Tokyo, a site the city designated as a historic remain in 1961, giving the well's legend, its present state and the origin of its name.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records a village well tied to a local water-god belief: the legend says it kept running clear through a great drought, and a stone cut with "water god" (水神) lines its shaft. It gives no date for the well, no depth or size, and the drought story is a tradition, not a recorded event, so it supports only that a village well could carry a water-god marker.
