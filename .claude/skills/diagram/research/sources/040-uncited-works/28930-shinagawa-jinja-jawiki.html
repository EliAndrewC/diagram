ja.wikipedia 品川神社 ("Shinagawa jinja", Shinagawa Shrine, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/品川神社)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Shinagawa Shrine in Kita-Shinagawa, Tokyo, founded in 1187, with its history, gate, subsidiary shrines, festival and designated cultural properties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows one shrine's chain of patrons from 1187 through the Tokugawa, who granted it a five-koku vermilion-seal estate and paid for its repairs, and that a Fuji mound of about 15 m and a stone torii carved with dragons stand in its grounds, neither of them dated by the article. Its dated claims on the Tokugawa grant contradict each other (1600 and 1591 appear together), it describes a shrine under the Tokugawa's special patronage, whose rebuilding and repairs the shogunate paid for, not an ordinary village shrine, and it gives no precinct size.
