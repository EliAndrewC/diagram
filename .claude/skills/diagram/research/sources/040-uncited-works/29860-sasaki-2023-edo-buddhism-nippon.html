Sasaki Shizuka, "Edo-Period Buddhism as Part of the Apparatus of Shogunal Control," in the series "Buddhism in Japan," Nippon.com, 2 Aug 2023 (https://nippon.com/en/japan-topics/b09408)

What it is: An essay by a Buddhist-studies professor on how the Edo shogunate used Buddhism to rule, covering the main temple-branch temple ranking and the danka parishioner system under which every family was registered at a local temple that kept its births, deaths, marriages and moves.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that temples recorded household births, deaths, marriages, travel and moves, served in tax collection and were used to find hidden Christians, which bears on how a samurai-era magistrate could use temples for household records. It is a general essay on the Edo period as a whole, gives no figures, no dates within the period and no region, and does not describe any one village temple's duties.
