miyada-village-ootagiri-iriyama-ron

Miyada Village (宮田村) Board of Education, lifelong learning section, "江戸時代のできごと(2)大田切入山論" ("Edo jidai no dekigoto (2): Otagiri iriyama-ron", Events of the Edo period (2): the Otagiri mountain dispute, title translated), in the village's history pages, on the Miyada Village site (in Japanese; https://www.vill.miyada.nagano.jp/government-pages/c-11039)

What it is: A village-office history page on the 1727 shogunate verdict in the Otagiri dispute between Miyada, Nakagoshi and Suwagata villages and Kamiho and Akasu villages over mountain land in Ina County, Shinano, giving the verdict's original text, a modern rendering and a summary.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows commons practice in a mountain district of Shinano around 1724-1728: the verdict names the mountains as iriai (common-use) land for all five villages to take fodder and firewood, with wood-cutting contracts limited to three years and drawn by lot. It also says that the right to use the commons was traded together with farmland and that the depth of commons secured, not the flat land or irrigation water, set how much land could be farmed (the area under cultivation, not the size of any one field). This is one dispute in one upland district. The page's general account of the commons, including the claim about the area under cultivation, is the village's own summary and is not tied to the verdict: it describes the practice as lasting until "only a few decades ago", so it speaks for the mountain commons of later times as much as for 1727.
