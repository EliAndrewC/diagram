ja.wikipedia 町割 ("machiwari", the laying out of a town's blocks, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/石橋)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on machiwari, the division of a premodern town into streets and blocks, from the Heian capital's grid to castle towns, with a note on its use in modern Kyoto land readjustment. The page was opened from the address 石橋 but is the machiwari article.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Heian-kyo block as a 40 jo (about 120 m) square, traces its change to the two-sided street town (ryogawacho) in the Middle Ages and to rectangular blocks after Toyotomi's Tensho land division, and sorts castle towns by where they put samurai, townspeople and the outer defense line. It is an outline of terms and types with no block sizes for any castle town, and part of its later text concerns twentieth-century Kyoto readjustment projects rather than the premodern layouts.
