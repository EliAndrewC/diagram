Sir George Staunton, An Authentic Account of an Embassy from the King of Great Britain to the Emperor of China (London, 1797), volume 1, full text of the archive.org scan (https://archive.org/stream/authenticaccount01stau/authenticaccount01stau_djvu.txt)

What it is: The OCR text of volume 1 of Staunton's account of Lord Macartney's British embassy of 1792-94, written by a member of the party from the embassy's papers: the preparations, the sea voyage to China, and the first stretch of the Chinese coast and the Pei-ho. Most of it is the voyage out; the Atlantic and Indian Ocean stops (Madeira, Teneriffe, Rio, Tristan d'Acunha) fill nearly the first half of the volume, and China proper begins only about page 400.

Why it applies, and its limits: The East Asian share is small and scattered: front-matter plate captions with general remarks on Chinese military posts, city gates, pagodas, canal sluices and scoop-wheels; Batavia's walled Dutch town and its Chinese community and burial vaults, and the Cochin-China villages of Turon and Callao; and, in roughly the last fifth, a walk through the walled town of Ting-hai on Chu-san, its houses, shops and canal-cut fields, a reclaimed rice polder and farmhouse on Lowang, the walled city of Ten-choo-foo with unbuilt ground inside its wall, and the mud-and-thatch villages at the mouth of the Pei-ho. All of it is 1793 evidence from a foreign party on ships and a few short landings, on one coast and one river mouth in the Qing and in Cochin-China, with no inland Chinese settlement and no Japan; the measures (wall heights, tower spacing, acreages) are British estimates, and the Batavia and Cochin-China passages describe overseas or tropical communities, not China.
