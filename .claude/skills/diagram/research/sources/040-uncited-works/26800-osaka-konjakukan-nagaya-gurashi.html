osaka-konjakukan-nagaya-gurashi

Tani Naoki, "長屋ぐらしの町 ―路地・つきあい・地蔵盆―" ("A town of tenement life: alleys, neighbors, the Jizo festival", title translated), in Anju no. 80, the housing guidebook of the Osaka Museum of Housing and Living (大阪くらしの今昔館) (in Japanese; https://www.osaka-angenet.jp/ange/ange80/2008005)

What it is: An essay by the museum's director on Osaka's rowhouses (nagaya): the household counts of 1689, the street-front rowhouse of ordinary merchants against the alley rowhouse behind, the reconstructed rooms in the museum, and a model of the Karahori alley district set in August 1938.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a household count for Osaka in 1689, 12,977 house-owning and 68,315 renting households (84 percent renting), and describes the plan of both kinds: shop, middle room and parlor with a dirt-floor kitchen and well on the street front, one room and a dirt floor in an alley rowhouse, with a shared well and privy at its side. It describes Osaka, a merchant city, not a village or a samurai town; the alley rowhouse and the Jizo festival scenes of the model are of 1938, and the essay does not date the reconstructed premodern rooms.
