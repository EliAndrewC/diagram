daikansho-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 代官 ("Daikan", the deputy who governed a territory for its lord, title translated), with its section on the daikan's office (daikansho) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/代官所)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the daikan, the official who administered a lord's territory in his place, from the ancient provinces through the Edo shogunate and the domains, with a section on the daikan's office and a list of known offices by region.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the staffing of a shogunal territory's office in concrete terms: a daikan had about ten attached retainers (tetsuke) and a few locally hired clerks (tedai), and the Gojo office governing 70,000 koku of southern Yamato had one daikan, three tetsuke and ten tedai, besides the footmen. It also says a daikan who was a local notable often used his own house as the office. It carries a notice that it lacks sources, several statements are tagged as needing citation, and it is Edo-period shogunate practice first, with domains only sketched.
