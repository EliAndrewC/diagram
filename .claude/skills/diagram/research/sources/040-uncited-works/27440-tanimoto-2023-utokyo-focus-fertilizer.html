Tanimoto Masayuki (谷本雅之), "江戸時代みたいに動植物資源だけ使えばいいの?" ("Edo jidai mitai ni doshokubutsu shigen dake tsukaeba ii no?", Should we use only animal and plant resources like the Edo period?, title translated), Q.9 of "身近な疑問vs東大" (Everyday questions vs. the University of Tokyo), UTokyo FOCUS, 16 May 2023 (in Japanese; https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/focus/ja/features/z1304_00228.html)

What it is: A short interview answer by a University of Tokyo professor of Japanese economic history, published on the university's public news site as one of 21 questions on the green transformation. It traces how Japanese farmers fertilized their fields from the seventeenth century into the Meiji era.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a compact sequence for the setting's fertilizer economy: cut-grass green manure (kariki) from mountain commons in the seventeenth century, with the shogunate and villages limiting the cutting; then, once land and population reached their limit in the early eighteenth century, purchased fish fertilizer (boiled-sardine and herring cake) and traded night soil; then imported Manchurian soybean cake in the Meiji era. It is a modern professor's summary written to answer a question about the present, gives no figures, quantities, prices or place-by-place dates, and the later stages (Meiji soybean cake) fall after the setting's period.
