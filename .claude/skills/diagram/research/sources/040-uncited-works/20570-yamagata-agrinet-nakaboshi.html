yamagata-agrinet-nakaboshi

Yamagata Prefecture Agricultural Technology and Environment Division, "どようぼし（土用干し）ってなんですか？" ("doyo-boshi tte nan desu ka", what is the "doyo drying"?, title translated), in the rice-growing Q&A of Yamagata Agrinet (in Japanese; https://agrin.jp/crop/suito/q_and_a/doyoubosi.html)

What it is: A prefectural farm-information site's answer, dated 2022, to what "doyo-boshi" means. It says the term is the same as nakaboshi, the mid-season drying of a rice paddy by draining it, and gives three reasons for it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the draining is and why it is done: stopping useless stem growth, encouraging healthy roots, and letting oxygen reach the roots so harmful gases do not build up in the soil. It describes present-day Yamagata rice-growing advice and gives no date for the practice's origin; mid-season drainage is old in Japan but spread widely only after the Second World War, and it needs a paddy that can be drained, which many premodern wet paddies could not, so in the setting it belongs to well-drained fields, not to every paddy. It does not say when in the season it is done beyond that it overlaps the summer doyo period.
