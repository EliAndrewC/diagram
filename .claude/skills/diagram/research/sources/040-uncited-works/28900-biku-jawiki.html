ja.wikipedia 比丘 ("biku", the bhikkhu or Buddhist monk, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/僧侶)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the bhikkhu, the fully ordained male Buddhist renunciant, reached from the redirect for sōryo (monk). It gives the term's origin as 'one who begs for food', the Pali and Sanskrit forms, the Zen name unsui for a wandering or training monk, and the note that Japan's traditional sects marry and pass temples down.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in Japan's traditional Buddhist sects, as in Newar and some Tibetan Buddhism, monks' marriage and hereditary succession are the norm and the precepts are not kept, which bears on temple households and succession. It is a short definitional article with no counts, dates or Japanese period, so it does not say when this became the rule or how many monks a temple housed.
