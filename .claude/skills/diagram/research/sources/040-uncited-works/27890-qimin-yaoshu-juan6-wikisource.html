"齐民要术/卷第六" (Qimin yaoshu, scroll 6, title translated), in the Chinese Wikisource transcription of the Qimin yaoshu, simplified-character version (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E5%85%AD)

What it is: The full text of scroll 6 of the Qimin yaoshu, an agricultural treatise, transcribed on Chinese Wikisource, with its interlinear notes in angle brackets. It has six chapters: raising cattle, horses, donkeys and mules; sheep (with felt, curds and butter); pigs; chickens; geese and ducks; and fish ponds with water plants.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives working husbandry for the farmyard in a Chinese text: sheep pens built against the dwelling with a window opening onto them, pigs in small dirty pens with a little roof against rain and snow, chickens in walled yards with a roofed shed, and a fish pond of six mu with nine islets, which the scroll quotes from the Tao Zhu Gong Yangyu Jing, a tale of Tao Zhu Gong advising King Wei that promises yields of tens of thousands of carp, and which is not the scroll's own practice. The page does not date the text or name an author, and the transcription is from 2011; its practice is that of the dry, millet-and-wheat northern plains it was written for, so the figures (a thousand sheep, flock sizes, pond size) are advice and a model, not a survey of any real farm.
