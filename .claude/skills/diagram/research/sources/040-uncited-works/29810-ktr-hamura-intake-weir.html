ktr-hamura-intake-weir

Kanto Regional Development Bureau, Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, "多摩川の見どころ: 25. 羽村取水堰" (Highlights of the Tama River: the Hamura intake weir, title translated), (in Japanese; https://ktr.mlit.go.jp/ktr_content/content/000099135.pdf)

What it is: A government leaflet on the Hamura intake weir on the Tama River, which feeds the Tamagawa Josui aqueduct, covering its parts, its sluice sizes in old units and a raft-passage dispute of the 1710s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records the weir from the Josui's cutting in 1654 and gives the 1790 survey sizes: a first sluice 6 ken long and 2 ken 3 shaku 5 sun wide, a second twice as wide, 35 and 42 flash boards, and wicker stone cylinders (jakago) and timber frames steering the water. It also gives a raft toll of 16 mon per raft and about 3,000 rafts a year in the Meiji records. The weir was rebuilt in concrete from 1911 and the page describes it as it stands, and it is one large aqueduct intake, not a village ditch intake.
