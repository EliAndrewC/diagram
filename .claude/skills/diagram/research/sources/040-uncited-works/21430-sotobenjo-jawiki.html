sotobenjo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia "外便所" ("soto benjo", outhouse, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A4%96%E4%BE%BF%E6%89%80)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the outdoor privy, a rough translation of the English Wikipedia article, covering its building, its pit and pail kinds, public-health problems and its history in Australia, Denmark and the United States.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its opening paragraph says that outhouses were widely seen in farming villages and on farms before chemical fertilizer and flush toilets, for three reasons: it made night-soil manuring convenient, it collected waste from visitors, and a worker in the fields did not have to go into the house. That paragraph names no country or period and cites nothing. Everything after it is about Europe, North America, Australia and India - the Rochdale pail system, Australian dunny carts, American two-story privies - and the page itself says it is a loose machine-aided translation and flags its own lack of sources, so it says nothing of a Japanese or Chinese privy's size, siting or date.
