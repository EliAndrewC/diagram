ichirizuka-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 一里塚 ("ichirizuka", the one-ri mound, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/一里塚)

What it is: The Traditional-Chinese Wikipedia article on the ichirizuka, the earth mound that marked each ri along a road. It covers the Chinese origin of the mound and the national roadside system the shogunate ordered in 1604.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Edo specification: a mound on each side of the highway every ri (about 4 km) from Nihonbashi, five ken on a side (it writes "about 9 square meters", which reads as a side of about 9 m) and one jo (about 3 m) high, and finished in ten years. It also gives the planting from the Tenpo-era Shukuson Taigaicho: hackberry (enoki) over 50 percent, pine about 25, cedar about 10, with chestnut, cherry, cypress and oak for the rest. It speaks of the five great highways and not of a village lane, and the planting count is from the 1830s-1840s, not from 1604.
