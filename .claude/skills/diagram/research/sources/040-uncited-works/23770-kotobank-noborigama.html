kotobank-noborigama

"登り窯" ("noborigama", the climbing kiln, title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen and the Britannica small-item encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%99%BB%E3%82%8A%E7%AA%AF-112447)

What it is: A page of two short entries defining the noborigama, a pottery kiln of several to a dozen or more chambers built in steps up a slope and sharing the heat of the first chamber.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the form was developed in China and Korea and reached Japan from Korea, first built by the Karatsu potters, and was spread over the country in the Sengoku period. It gives no chamber sizes, no site and no output, and the Daijisen and Britannica differ in wording on how it arrived, so it supports the kiln's origin and layout only.
