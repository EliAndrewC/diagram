kotobank-chozubachi

"手水鉢" ("chozubachi, the hand-washing basin", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/手水鉢-568649)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for the chozubachi, the basin that holds water for washing the hands and rinsing the mouth at shrines, temples and tea gardens, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Hyuga Susumu and Miyazawa Satoshi), Nipponica and others.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the stone basin: worked stone became common from the Kamakura period, a surviving example is inscribed 1331 (Taima-dera), a late-15th-century scroll shows one at Seiryo-ji in Kyoto, and tea gardens took them from Takeno Joo's time (1502-55). It gives forms (natural stone, shell, boat and gourd shapes, reused pagoda parts) but no sizes, and says it is unclear when shrines and temples first used them.
