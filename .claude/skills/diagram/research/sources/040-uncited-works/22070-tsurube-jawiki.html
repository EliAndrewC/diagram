tsurube-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 釣瓶 ("tsurube", well bucket, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%87%A3%E7%93%B6)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the tsurube, the bucket and rope or pole gear for drawing water from a well, with its four kinds and its figures of speech.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the rope-and-pulley bucket was the usual Japanese way of drawing well water until the hand pump spread, because Japan's water table is comparatively shallow, and names four kinds: a pair of buckets on a pulley, a bucket on a bamboo pole, a counterweighted sweep and a thrown bucket. It cites an article on Japanese wells from 1951 and a Nara city page for its history of the bucket, and half of the text is idiom, theater and folklore, so it gives no dimensions or depths.
