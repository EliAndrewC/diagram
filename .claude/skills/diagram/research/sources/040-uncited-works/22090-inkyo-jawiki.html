inkyo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 隠居 ("inkyo", retirement, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9A%A0%E5%B1%85)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on inkyo, retirement from office or the headship of a house, covering its folklore studies, its place in the Meiji civil code until 1947, later examples, and the practice in the Nordic countries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the folklorists' three forms of a retired parent's living: in the same house as the heir, in a separate house, or split between the main house and a branch, and says farm and merchant families could retire when the heir was ready to take over the trade. It also says the Meiji code allowed retirement from age 60; but its examples are mostly modern people, most of its text is the scholarly argument over how to define the term, and the Nordic section is another continent's family history.
