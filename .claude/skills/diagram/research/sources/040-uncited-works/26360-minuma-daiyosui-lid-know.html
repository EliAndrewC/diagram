Minuma Daiyosui Land Improvement District, "見沼代用水を知る" ("Getting to know the Minuma Daiyosui", title translated), on the district's own site (in Japanese; https://www.minuma-daiyosui-lid.or.jp/know/)

What it is: The explanatory page of the land improvement district that runs the Minuma Daiyosui, a canal drawn from the Tone River in Saitama in 1727, written with reading aids for children and covering its history, works and founder.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the sequence for one large Edo-period water scheme: a pond (tamei) of about 1,200 ha made by the 1629 Hatcho embankment and serving about 5,000 ha downstream; a canal of about 60 km dug in six months in 1727-1728 with the pond drained for about 1,200 ha of new fields; about 14,000 ha irrigated in all; and boat traffic carrying rice and vegetables to Edo and manure back until Showa 6. The page is the district's own pride in the work, its figures are unreferenced, and it gives the start of digging as both September and August of 1727, so it fits a large state-backed scheme, not a village's own ditch.
