chaya-teahouse-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 茶屋 ("chaya", teahouse, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8C%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the chaya, the shop that serves tea, food and rest: its growth from the itinerant tea sellers of the Muromachi period to the roadside and post-town teahouses of the Edo period, its named kinds, and the place names that keep the word.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the first tea sellers to Oei-era Kyoto (1403, outside the Toji gate) as stall-less peddlers with a carrying pole, and lists the Edo kinds found at post stations and passes (mizu-jaya, kake-jaya, tateba-jaya, ryori-jaya). It gives no sizes or plans of a teahouse, and its Edo material is from the Tokaido and Kiso road prints and an Osaka study, so it says nothing of how one was laid out on a village's edge.
