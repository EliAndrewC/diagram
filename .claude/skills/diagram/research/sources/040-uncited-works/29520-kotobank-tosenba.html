kotobank-tosenba

"渡船場" ("tosenba, the ferry landing", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/渡船場)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for tosenba, the place where a ferry boat lands and departs, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (with a 1631 ordinance citation) and the old Heibonsha encyclopedia's passage on ferries and ferrymen.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a 1631 shogunal ordinance on ferry landings and says that in Japan's flood-prone rivers ferries and ferrymen paid for by fares continued into the early modern and modern periods where bridges were few, with an ancient story of a ferryman at Takase-no-watari. It gives no size, layout or number of ferries, and is a definition and a short historical note, not a description of a landing.
