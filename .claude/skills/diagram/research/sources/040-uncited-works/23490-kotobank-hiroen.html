kotobank-hiroen

"広縁" ("hiroen", the wide veranda, title translated), from the Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and a home-renovation glossary, with a mention in the older Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry on corridors (廊下), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BA%83%E7%B8%81-614334)

What it is: A short Kotobank page defining 広縁 as a wide engawa along the outside of a reception room, with an excerpt from the older Heibonsha entry on corridors.

Why it applies, and its limits: The excerpt places the wide veranda or tatami corridor around the guest hall in the Momoyama and Edo-period houses of shoguns and daimyo, and says that Edo farmhouses, townhouses and the houses of middle and lower samurai had almost no corridors, until a late-Edo veranda on two or three sides came to serve as a way to the toilet and bath. That is a rank marker for a samurai house, from a short excerpt and not a study; the page gives no widths.
