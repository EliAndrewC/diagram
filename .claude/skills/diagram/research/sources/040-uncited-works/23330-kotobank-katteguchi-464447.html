"勝手口" ("katteguchi", the kitchen door, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Nipponica and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8B%9D%E6%89%8B%E5%8F%A3-464447)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering dictionary entries on the katteguchi, the door to a house's kitchen apart from its front entrance, and the host's door of a tea room. The Nipponica entry adds the tea-room door's usual size.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a separate kitchen door as an old form, with the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten quoting a haikai collection of 1666, and says the door lets refuse and laundry out, tradesmen in and neighbors through, which keeps the front entrance clean. Its advice on meters, awnings and glass doors is for modern houses, and the sizes it gives (about 5.1 to 5.2 shaku high, 2 to 2.1 shaku wide) are for the tea-room door only.
