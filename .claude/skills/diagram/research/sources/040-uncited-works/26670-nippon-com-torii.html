nippon-com-torii

nippon.com, "神社空間を読み解く②鳥居" ("Reading the shrine space, 2: the torii", title translated), in the series "神社に行こう！" ("Let's go to a shrine!", title translated), 31 May 2016 (in Japanese; https://www.nippon.com/ja/views/b05202/)

What it is: A short magazine article on the torii, the gate at the entrance to a shrine's sacred ground. It explains the basic form, the numbering of successive torii and the usual colors and materials.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that where a shrine has several torii the largest is the first, at the entrance to the whole precinct, and each further one raises the sanctity; that the basic form is two posts with a lintel and a tie beam; and that torii are unpainted wood in principle, with vermilion mainly at Inari and Hachiman shrines. It describes shrines as they stand and gives no sizes, dates or evidence for how old these customs are, and it notes that concrete, metal and resin torii are now common.
