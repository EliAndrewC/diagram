en.wikipedia "Inari shrine" (the shrine type dedicated to the kami Inari) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inari_shrine)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Inari shrines, covering the deity's origins, the shrine's red torii and paired fox statues, its offerings, and a list of notable shrines and temples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Inari spread partly by being adopted as a yashikigami, an estate deity enshrined on family land, and that almost every Inari shrine however small has a pair of fox statues, which supports a small household or field shrine drawn with foxes and a torii. The fox pair and the torii are described from present-day shrines (the fox claims rest on Smyers's 1999 study of contemporary Inari worship), so they show the form as it survives, not dated Edo practice. Its only count, 2,970 registered shrines, is from a 2007 report and excludes roadside, field and household shrines, so it says nothing of how many stood in a premodern village.
