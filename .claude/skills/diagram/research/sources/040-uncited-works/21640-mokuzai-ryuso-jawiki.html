ja.wikipedia 木材流送 ("mokuzai ryuso", log floating, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9C%A8%E6%9D%90%E6%B5%81%E9%80%81)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on moving timber by river current, in its two forms (single logs floated loose, and logs lashed into rafts), with the season, the water depth needed, the raft unit and its crew, and when the practice ended.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says logs were floated in spring flood where snow lay and in autumn and winter elsewhere, that 0.5 to 1.0 m of water was enough, that a raft was poled by two people (four for a large one), and that a raft on the Kitayama river was built from floors 4 shaku wide and 2 ken long, eight joined to make a standard raft. Its raft dimensions come from one Kii river and a Taisho-era change to iron clamps, the end dates (Tenryu 1956, Yoneshiro 1965) are twentieth-century, and its lead photograph is of Canada.
