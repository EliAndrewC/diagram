en.wikipedia "Siheyuan" (四合院, the courtyard house, title translated) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siheyuan)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the siheyuan, the Chinese courtyard house enclosed by buildings on all four sides, mainly the Beijing form. It covers the history, the placement and use of the main, side, opposite and rear houses, the gate at the southeast corner, and regional variants.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the room-by-room order of a Beijing siheyuan under Confucian ranking (the north main house for the head of the family, the side houses for sons, the rear building for unmarried daughters) and says courtyards run longer north-south in the northwest and broader in the northeast. It carries a banner that it may contain original research, rests on a few cited works, gives no dimensions, and describes the Ming and Qing northern city form rather than a rural or southern house.
