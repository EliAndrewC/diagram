kusanomido-danzaemon

Kitamori Shino, "江戸の裏社会を支配した異例の支配者「弾左衛門」とは何者だったのか?" ("Who was Danzaemon, the unusual ruler of Edo's underworld?", title translated), Kusanomido, 2025-08-04 (in Japanese; https://kusanomido.com/study/history/japan/edo/110180/)

What it is: A web article on Danzaemon, the hereditary head of the outcaste groups of the Kanto under the Edo shogunate: how he gained the post, the trades he controlled, his residence in Imado, Asakusa, and the last holder, Dan Naoki, who lost the post after 1871.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the Imado compound was enclosed by shrines, temples and walls so none could see in, held an office, a private house, storehouses, a shrine, a jail and a white-sand yard for trying his people, and housing for more than 300 officials' family members. It also says the family's genealogy is mostly later authority-building with little reliable primary evidence; the page gives no dimensions, names Shiomi Senichiro's Danzaemon to sono jidai "and others" as its references, and is by a general-interest writer.
