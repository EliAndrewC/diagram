komonjyo-village-basic-data

Uno Aiko, "江戸時代の村数、高、面積、人口など村の基礎データ" ("Edo jidai no sonsū, daka, menseki, jinkō nado mura no kiso dēta", the number, yield, area and population of Edo-period villages: basic village data, title translated), part 7 of "江戸幕府の政治" on 古文書ネット (Komonjyo.net, "Enjoying the Edo period through cursive script") (in Japanese; https://komonjyo.net/muranoshikumi.html)

What it is: A short hobbyist reference page on Komonjyo.net, a site for learning to read Edo-period cursive documents, giving average figures for Edo villages (their number, yield, farmland, households and population), the three village officers and a worked example from a shogunal village in Musashi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that Japan had about 63,000 villages in 1834 (Tenpo 5) and that an average village held 500 koku, 50 cho of fields, 40 to 50 households and about 400 people, with the example of Onumata shinden in Tama County (about 320 koku, 123 cho of fields, 45 households, survey of 1816). It cites one reference work (a 1991 illustrated dictionary of peasant life) for these figures, shows no derivation of the averages, does not say how much villages varied by region, and its example is a newly opened field village near Edo.
