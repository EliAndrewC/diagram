ja.wikipedia 淀屋 ("Yodoya", the Osaka merchant house, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B7%80%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Yodoya, a great Osaka merchant house of the Edo period, covering its founder's development of Nakanoshima, its rice market, its confiscation in 1705 and its Kurayoshi branch.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Yodoya opened a rice market on Nakanoshima that set the national rice price, that 135 storehouses of the domains and rice merchants stood on the islet, that in the 1620s about 2 million of the 5 million koku sold on the market were said to trade at Osaka, and that the market later moved to Dojima in 1697. Its figures of wealth (confiscated assets, loans of 100 trillion yen in modern terms) are given without sources, as are the 135 storehouses, which it does not date, and the 2 million koku traded at Osaka, the latter reported only as said, and the page carries a 2022 banner saying it lacks references.
