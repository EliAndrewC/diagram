"明史/卷75 志第51 職官四" (Mingshi, volume 75, treatise 51, "Officials, part 4", title translated), the "History of Ming", on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/明史/卷75)

What it is: The Wikisource text of the Mingshi treatise on officials, part 4: the Nanjing offices, the princely households, the provincial administration and surveillance commissions, and then the prefecture, subprefecture and county offices with their schools, inspectorates, post stations, tax bureaus, granaries and other minor posts. Each office gets its staff, rank and duties, followed by a history of how the post changed from the first Hongwu years.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists what a Ming county seat held below the magistrate - deputy, registrar, jailer, inspection office, post station, tax bureau, granary, county school with a stated number of stipend students, medical and yin-yang schools, Buddhist and Daoist registries - and counts the empire's 159 prefectures, 234 subprefectures and 1,171 counties. It gives the rule as the Ming court fixed it, not a particular county's roster (it says deputies and registrars were added and cut unevenly), and it was compiled from the dynasty's records after its fall, so it gives no sizes or plans of any of these offices' buildings.
