kotobank-machigumi

"町組" ("machigumi" or "chogumi", the town association, title translated), entries from the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (Nakamura Ken and Tomii Yasuo), Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Yamakawa Japanese History Dictionary and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%94%BA%E7%B5%84-634606)

What it is: A Kotobank page of dictionary entries on the machigumi, the association of neighborhood wards (cho) that governed Kyoto and other towns from the end of the Muromachi period. It lists the Kyoto groups of 1571, their later numbers, and their officers, with a short place-name entry on a samurai district of Sakura castle town of the same name.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a self-governing town built of wards grouped into associations, each run by officers drawn from the ward elders, in Kyoto from about the 1530s to the early Meiji years. It says the early associations could punish some offenses, that this faded as the shogunate's governor of Kyoto took control, and that Sakai, Omi-Hachiman and Otsu had them too. Its figures come from Kyoto, such as 13 groups with 847 wards in Kamigyo by the end of the shogunate, so it is not a model for a small castle town's wards.
