taihi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia "堆肥" ("taihi", compost, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A0%86%E8%82%A5)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on compost, defining it as fully decomposed organic matter used as fertilizer or soil conditioner, and covering its effects on soil, how it is made, its quality standards and its kinds (livestock dung, rice straw, rice husk, bark, kitchen waste).

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies Japanese farm terms and the rice-straw and livestock-dung kinds, and it says that in medieval Japan warriors used the dung of their war horses as manure on their own and their dependents' fields (under 厩肥, stable manure). Most of the page is modern agronomy - a 1994 recommended standard for livestock-dung compost, a 1960s quick compost made with purchased nitrogen fertilizer, rules under modern Japanese fertilizer and livestock-waste law - so its figures do not describe a premodern village's compost heap, and the medieval line cites no source and names no region or date within the medieval period.
