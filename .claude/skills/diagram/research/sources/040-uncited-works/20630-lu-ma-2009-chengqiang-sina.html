Lu Xiqi and Ma Jian, "城墙内的城市?--中国古代治所城市形态的再认识" ("Chengqiang nei de chengshi? Zhongguo gudai zhisuo chengshi xingtai de zai renshi", cities within walls? a re-examination of the form of China's ancient seat-of-government cities, title translated), in Zhongguo shehui jingjishi yanjiu, no. 2, 2009, reposted on a Sina blog (in Chinese; https://blog.sina.com.cn/s/blog_4903e9ef0100h1he.html)

What it is: A blog repost of a journal article arguing that China's county and prefecture seats were not always walled cities. It sorts the Han to Qing record into four periods of wall building and then turns to the suburbs that grew outside the gates.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a period-by-period test of whether a seat had a wall: most Han to Southern Dynasties seats were walled, perhaps half or more of Sui and early Tang seats were not, the Song and Yuan left most inland county seats walled poorly or not at all, and most county seats were walled only from the mid-Ming. It rests largely on the Han River valley and says its case studies may not represent the whole country; the repost breaks off in its third section, after the Tang and Song examples of street quarters outside the walls, so its conclusion is missing.
