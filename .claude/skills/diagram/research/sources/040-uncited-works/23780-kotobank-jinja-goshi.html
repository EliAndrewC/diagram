kotobank-jinja-goshi

"神社合祀" ("jinja goshi", the merger of shrines, title translated), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%A5%9E%E7%A4%BE%E5%90%88%E7%A5%80-298629)

What it is: A signed encyclopedia entry on the government's consolidation of shrines in the early and late Meiji period, with a second signed section on regional differences.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives national counts (195,000 shrines in 1905, 141,000 by 1910, about one in four closed, 35 percent of unranked shrines) and says the policy aimed at one shrine per town or village, and that Mie and Wakayama merged heavily while Niigata did little. All of this is Meiji evidence, a state policy after 1906 that reshaped shrines, so its counts show the shrine landscape after the mergers and cannot be read as the premodern number or placement of village shrines.
