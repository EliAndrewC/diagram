jinjahoncho-shaden

Association of Shinto Shrines (神社本庁), "社殿について" ("shaden ni tsuite", about the shrine buildings, title translated), in "Omairi suru" (visiting a shrine), official site (in Japanese; https://www.jinjahoncho.or.jp/omairi/shaden/)

What it is: A short explainer on the official site of the national association of Shinto shrines, setting out the two main shrine-building styles, shinmei-zukuri (Ise) and taisha-zukuri (Izumo), with the styles derived from each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the styles by their roofs and entrances: gabled, entered on the long side for shinmei-zukuri, which it says grew from the raised grain store, and entered at the gable end for taisha-zukuri, which it says grew from the ancient dwelling. It also names nagare-zukuri, hachiman-zukuri, gongen-zukuri and sengen-zukuri with example shrines. It gives no dates, sizes or plans, so it identifies forms and the origin claims, not how large or how placed a village shrine hall was.
