ja.wikipedia 浅草寺 ("Sensoji", the Asakusa Kannon temple in Tokyo, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/浅草寺)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Sensoji, Tokyo's oldest temple, in Asakusa, Taito ward: its founding legend of 628, its history through the Kamakura, Edo and modern periods, its gates, halls, pagoda and subsidiary halls, its festivals, its designated cultural properties and its archaeology.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the layout of one great Edo-period temple precinct: a gate at the head of an approach road about 250 m long to the inner gate, shops along that road dating from about 1685, a pagoda, a main hall, a shrine to its east and a ferry-landing hall on the Sumida River. Nearly all of the present buildings are reinforced concrete rebuilt after the 1945 air raid, the page dates the oldest extant building to the Muromachi period and the gates to 1960 and 1964, and its founding account is a legend traced to the temple's own chronicle; a metropolitan temple of the shogun's capital is not a village or provincial temple.
