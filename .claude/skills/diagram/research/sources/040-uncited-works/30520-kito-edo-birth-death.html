kito-edo-birth-death

Kito Hiroshi (鬼頭 宏, Sophia University Faculty of Economics), "人生40年の世界：江戸時代の出生と死亡" ("Jinsei 40-nen no sekai: Edo jidai no shussei to shibo", A world of forty-year lives: births and deaths in the Edo period, title translated), a lecture handout in the Anthropology 2000 lecture series, on minato.sip21c.org (in Japanese; https://minato.sip21c.org/humeco/anthro2000/kito.pdf)

What it is: A lecture handout by a historical demographer on Edo-period population: how it is counted (the religious registers and the shogunate's census), population growth and stagnation, fertility, age at marriage, causes of death and the family life cycle, with the author's biography and book list at its head.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the national population as the author estimates it (about 15 million in 1600, chosen from published estimates of 12 to 18 million, 26 million counted in 1721 and about 31 million in his corrected figure), a total fertility of 6 to 7 at the end of the 17th century falling to about 4 by the mid-18th, five births as the common completed family, and a spring-to-early-summer death peak in Shimosa in 1394-1592 that eased once summer wheat was grown as a second crop. It is a lecture summary, so it states these figures without the calculations behind them (the text says it omits them), and it speaks for Japan as a whole, with one village, Yokouchi in Shinano, and one temple's death register in Shimosa as its only local cases.
