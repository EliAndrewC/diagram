National Office for Philosophy and Social Sciences (全国哲学社会科学工作办公室), "明清时期华北的商业城镇与市场层级" ("Commercial towns and market tiers of North China in the Ming and Qing", title translated), a summary of the National Social Science Fund project led by Xu Tan of Nankai University, in its collection of finished results (最新成果集萃) (in Chinese; http://www.nopss.gov.cn/n1/2021/0812/c373410-32191342.html)

What it is: A 2021 summary, on the national social science fund office's site, of a finished project and its monograph on the commercial towns and market tiers of the North China plain (Hebei, Shandong, Henan) in the Ming and Qing. It describes the project's three parts, from the provincial and prefectural capitals to the 30-odd commercial towns studied one by one.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in this region the walls and planned layout of administrative cities mattered little for the many lower county seats and for market towns, and that towns such as Zhoukou, Sheqi, Zhuxian and Zhoucun exceeded most prefectural capitals in volume and reach of trade (Zhuxian and Zhoucun even the provincial capitals); it also gives a three-tier frame (transit hubs, regional commercial centers, base markets, the last including ordinary market towns and county seats). It is a summary of the project's conclusions with no figures of its own, the work is about the northern plain only, and its evidence is mostly customs-station records and merchant-guild stele donations from the mid-Qing, so it speaks of commercial towns of the later empire, not of a county seat's own layout.
