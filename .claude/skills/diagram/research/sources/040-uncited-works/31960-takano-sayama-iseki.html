takano-sayama-iseki

Takano Yoko (高野陽子), a two-page summary of the Sayama site moated residence, Kumiyama, Kyoto Prefecture, pp. 114-115, on the site kyotofu-maibun.or.jp (in Japanese; https://kyotofu-maibun.or.jp/data/mukashi/pdf/m-p114-115.pdf)

What it is: A short illustrated summary of an excavation at the Sayama site in Kumiyama, in the Yamashiro basin south of Kyoto: a square moated residence of a local lord from the late Heian to the Kamakura period, set in a gridded (jori) field system.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the residence as one cho (about 120 m) square inside a moat 7-8 m wide dug in the late eleventh century and kept to the mid-thirteenth, with four raised-floor buildings, two wells and two graves inside, and reads the moat as chiefly flood control with a boat landing, because the site lies at the basin's lowest point. It is one site of an estate administrator in a flood plain, in the Heian and Kamakura periods and not the setting's, and it does not give a floor plan or the moat's depth.
