Kamiyu Rekishi Henshubu (かみゆ歴史編集部), "城下町ってどんな街だったの？" ("What kind of town was a castle town?", title translated), Super Introduction! Castle Seminar no. 44, 26 November 2018, on Shirobito, a castle site recognized by the Japan Castle Association (in Japanese; https://shirobito.jp/article/598)

What it is: A beginner's article explaining castle towns: how they grew from valley towns below mountain castles in the Sengoku period into larger towns on plains, and the three jobs the page gives them - defense, administration and commerce.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states how a castle town was laid out for defense: higher-ranking samurai housed near the castle and lower ranks outside them, then merchant and artisan quarters, then temples and shrines on the outer edge as strongpoints, with moats, gates, crooked streets, T-junctions and dead ends, and in some towns an earthwork or wall around the whole (the sogamae). It also names the notice board as the way a lord's orders reached townspeople. It gives no measurements and no plan of any one town beyond photo captions, and it says Nobunaga and Hideyoshi only "seem" to have favored straight streets.
