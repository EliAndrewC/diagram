ja.wikipedia 水茶屋 ("mizu chaya", the roadside tea stall, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B0%B4%E8%8C%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the mizu chaya, the Edo-period tea stall on roadsides and shrine and temple grounds, with where they stood in Edo, what they served and the waitresses who made some famous.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names where such stalls stood in Edo (below Ueno, Ryogoku, near much-visited shrines and temples, the twenty stalls near Asakusa's Kannon) and quotes a source saying that for one serving the usual tea money was 24 to 50 mon. It is mostly a quotation from Edo-period writings, with no layout of a stall, and it is about Edo and other large temple sites, not a village.
