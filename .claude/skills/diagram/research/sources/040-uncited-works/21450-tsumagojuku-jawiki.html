ja.wikipedia "妻籠宿" ("Tsumago-juku", the Tsumago post station, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A6%BB%E7%B1%A0%E5%AE%BF)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Tsumago, the 42nd post station of the Nakasendo in the Kiso valley (Nagano), covering its position at a road junction, its 1843 head count, its preservation since 1968 and its sights.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the 1843 Nakasendo post-station register for Tsumago: 418 people within the station, one honjin and one waki-honjin, with a masugata (the zigzag crank in the road) and a checkpoint site still marked. The same sentence gives 31 houses and 31 inns, which reads as an error in the page, so its counts want checking against the register itself; the page's wider coverage (a 1,245.4 ha preservation district, 1968 resident charter, 1976 designation) is about the modern preserved town, not the station as it stood.
