imidas-setchin

Yamamoto Hirofumi, "雪隠（せっちん）" ("Setchin, the privy", title translated), in the Jidaigeki yogo shinan, on imidas, 1 October 2009 (in Japanese; https://imidas.jp/jidaigeki/detail/L-57-181-08-04-G252.html)

What it is: A signed dictionary entry by a historian on the Edo privy: where it stood, why a tenement block had a shared one, and how night soil was sold to farmers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says early Edo privies were often small huts over a river bank or drain, which the city repeatedly banned, that tenement privies were shared with the proceeds going to the landlord, that night soil sold for 32 mon a load in the mid-eighteenth century, and that pay-to-use privies stood in entertainment districts at about 5 mon. It describes Edo's townsmen and its feudal houses, and its prices are of one city and one period, so it does not show how a village handled waste.
