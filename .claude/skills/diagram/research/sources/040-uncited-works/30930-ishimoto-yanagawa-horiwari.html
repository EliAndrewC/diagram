ishimoto-yanagawa-horiwari

Ishimoto Daiho (石本大歩), "水郷柳川における生活の場・遊び場としての掘割に関する考察" ("A study of the moats as places of daily life and play in the water town of Yanagawa", title translated), Kyushu University School of Engineering, Architecture (in Japanese; https://arch.kyushu-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/02.pdf)

What it is: A four-page Kyushu University study of the moat-and-canal system (horiwari) of Yanagawa, Fukuoka, based on interviews with 14 residents in their seventies to nineties and old photographs, comparing daily use and children's play in the castle, Yanagawa district and Okinohata districts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how the former castle town's moats were used for drawing water, washing and swimming, with shared washing steps (kumizuba) on the main channel, a water source that decided where drinking water was taken (in Okinohata, the fast, clean water drawn from the castle moat was drunk, while water that had run through the fields first was not, and wells were used there), and moat clearing (hori-hoshi) to take fish and mud. It records life up to the early postwar years as old residents remembered it, and says drinking use faded in the Yanagawa district after its waterworks of 1927, though in the castle district moat water was still sometimes drunk in the early postwar years and piped water reached each household only in the high-growth era, so it reaches back to the Edo layout only through the districts' surviving street and moat plan.
