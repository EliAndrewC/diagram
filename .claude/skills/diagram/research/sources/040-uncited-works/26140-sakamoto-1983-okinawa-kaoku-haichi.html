Sakamoto Iwao, "民家の家屋配置について : 沖縄の集落景観における平面形態の構成に関する研究・その 1" ("Minka no kaoku haichi ni tsuite", On the arrangement of buildings in farmhouse compounds: a study of plan composition in Okinawan village landscapes, part 1, title translated), Transactions of the Architectural Institute of Japan 327, 1983 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijsaxx/327/0/327_KJ00003750793/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: The J-STAGE record, with a long Japanese abstract, of a 1983 survey of how outbuildings are placed around the main house in Okinawan farmhouse compounds and what that does to the village's look.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states rules a compound follows: outbuildings stand on the lower (shimote) side of the main house, the side opposite its first room (ichibanza), and horse and cattle sheds sit at its front on the lower side. The open ground at the upper side settles at about 5 m only once the lot is wider than a certain frontage, and the outbuildings shift to the side on wide lots and to the back on deep ones, most of all on small lots. It describes Okinawan compounds of the late twentieth century, with a note on reinforced-concrete houses, so the rule is a regional custom whose age the page does not give.
