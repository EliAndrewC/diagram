taiko-kenchi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 太閤検地 ("Taiko kenchi", Hideyoshi's land survey, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/太閤検地)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the land survey Toyotomi Hideyoshi ran across Japan from 1582, covering what it changed in land rights and in the end of the estate system, the standard units it used, how much was actually measured, and the number of provinces surveyed in each year to 1598.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the survey's units (6 shaku 3 sun to the ken, about 191 cm; a ken square is a bu, 30 bu a se, 10 se a tan, 10 tan a cho), the four grades of field and the one-cultivator-per-plot rule, which fix how the late-16th-century village and its fields were counted. It says the standard was first used in Mino in 1589, that many surveys departed from it, that some domains did not measure at all, and that whether the koku figure meant yield or tax is disputed; the article also carries a notice that it lacks verifiable sources.
