hasuda-minggu-nogyo

Hasuda City Board of Education, Cultural Properties section (蓮田市), "江戸時代から昭和時代へと続く農業の変革－市内に残る民具－" ("Changes in farming from the Edo period to the Showa period: the folk tools that remain in the city", title translated), an exhibition panel from the city's exhibition hall (in Japanese; https://city.hasuda.saitama.jp/bunkazai/kyoiku/bunkazai/tenjikan/documents/108fb2696d306e5909976930685e744e2d884c4e8b306e59095316300188709000.pdf)

What it is: A museum panel on rice farming in Hasuda, Saitama, walking through the farm year from seed selection to threshing, with the folk tools used at each step, then the village pilgrimage associations and the seasonal rites. It adds sidebars on the night-soil trade with Edo and on planting and harvest dates across Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places in one Kanto lowland village the pole-and-stake drying of cut rice (hasekake and honiokake), a barley second crop that was once shipped out with sweet potatoes, and the Edo trade in which food was carried to the city and night soil brought back on the return trip (the page does not say by cart or by boat, though it names boat traffic on the Motoarakawa, the Ayase and the Minumadai canal). It mixes present-day practice with the old (herbicides, combines) and hedges much of the past with "probably", it borrows its rice-farming outline from Wikipedia, and it dates none of the customs more closely than Edo to early postwar.
