tokyo-sento-1010-history

Tokyo Sento Association (東京都浴場組合), "銭湯の歴史" ("The history of the public bath", title translated), on its official site 1010.or.jp, copyright 2015 (in Japanese; https://www.1010.or.jp/guide/history/)

What it is: A trade association's illustrated history of the Japanese public bath in six parts: temple bathing as charity, the bath-giving of the Kamakura and Muromachi periods, the Edo bathhouse and its steam rooms, the yuna bathhouses, mixed bathing, and the Meiji-era reform bath.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Edo's first bathhouse as built in 1591 near Zenigame bridge and says by the end of the Keicho years (early 1600s) there was a bath "in every street"; it describes the steam bath and the low-doored zakuro-guchi, the 6 p.m. closing, the second-floor sitting room, and mixed bathing running to the late Edo and mostly ending in mid-Meiji. It speaks for the city of Edo and for bathing in general, says nothing of villages beyond group baths gathered at a Yakushi or Kannon hall, and its Edo bath facts come from one early 1614 chronicle it quotes at second hand.
