ja.wikipedia 防風林 ("bofurin", windbreak forest, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/防風林)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on windbreak forests, defining the field, coastal, railway and house-ground kinds, naming the tree species used in Japan, and giving examples from Toyama, Hokkaido, the United States and England.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the house-ground grove (yashikirin) as a windbreak in Japan, naming the Tonami Plain's kainyo, a grove kept from the original forest around each scattered farmstead and valued first against snow. It lists cedar for house surroundings and black pine for coasts. The page cites no study for the tree choices, its dated cases (the 1934 Great Plains belt, the Konsen grid) are modern, and its Japanese examples are of kinds still standing today, so it says nothing of how old the Tonami groves are or how wide a premodern grove ran.
