kanto-nosei-why-paddies

Kanto Regional Agricultural Administration Office, MAFF, "3．なぜ日本は水田を求めたのか" ("3. Why did Japan seek paddy fields", title translated), chapter 3 of "The land that farming built", in "Let's study farming scientifically" (in Japanese; https://www.maff.go.jp/kanto/nouson/sekkei/kagaku/kokudo/03.html)

What it is: A regional agricultural bureau's explainer on why paddy rice became Japan's farmland, comparing the Edo-period food supply with Europe's wheat-and-fallow farming.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, without a cited source for each, an Edo population of about 30 million fed from about 3 million ha in the mid-period (about 10 a a head), against Europe's need of 1 ha or more a head, and 15th-century seed returns of about 5 times for wheat and over 20 for rice. Only Yamane Ichiro's book is credited, beside the yields and the seed-return table; its yields per hectare (about 3.5 t for wheat, about 5 t for rice) are given with no era; its modern seed-return figures (130 times for rice, 24 for wheat) are present-day, and the page's own claims about culture and temperament are asserted, not shown.
