zh.wikipedia 天童寺 ("Tiantong Temple", title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/天童寺)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Tiantong Temple, a Chan Buddhist monastery at the foot of Mount Taibai in Yinzhou, Ningbo, Zhejiang, with its history from the traditional founding date of 300 to the present, its hall layout, its pagoda courts and its Japanese ties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a worked plan of one large Chan monastery whose present layout was fixed in the Ming rebuilding of 1631-1640: a central axis of two ponds, a screen wall and halls rising up the slope, three separate mountain gates three li apart, and a site of 76,400 square meters with over thirty buildings. Many of the halls and pagodas named are 1930s-1980s rebuilds, and the monastery was one of the very largest of its school (ranked third of the Five Mountains in the Southern Song), so its dimensions are an upper bound, not a typical temple's; the page gives dates and sizes with no stated method beyond its cited gazetteers.
