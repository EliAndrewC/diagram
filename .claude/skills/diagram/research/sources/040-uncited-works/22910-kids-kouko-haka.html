Zenkoku Kodomo Koukogaku Kyoushitsu (National Children's Archaeology Classroom), "2021 調べてみよう ～生活文化～「墓」" ("2021 Let's find out: daily life and culture, graves", title translated) (in Japanese; https://kids-kouko.com/prayer/)

What it is: A children's archaeology page surveying Japanese graves from the Paleolithic through the Kofun period, with pit graves, jar burials, coffin types, burial goods and the great tombs.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that adult Jomon burials at Sannai Maruyama lay in rows on both sides of a road through the village, that the ring-shaped villages of the Kanto put them in the central plaza, and that children were buried in jars at or beside the house entrances; in the Yayoi period the graves of the powerful were marked by ditches and mounds, while ordinary graves were unmarked pits. The whole page ends before the Heian period, so it says nothing of how a medieval or Edo village placed its graves.
