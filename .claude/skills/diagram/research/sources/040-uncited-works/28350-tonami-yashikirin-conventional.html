tonami-yashikirin-conventional

Tonami City Board of Education, "3(1) 砺波平野の様々な屋敷林" ("3(1) the various house groves of the Tonami plain", title translated), from 『砺波平野の屋敷林』 ("House Groves of the Tonami Plain", Tonami Dispersed-Settlement Research Institute, 1996, excerpted), on the Tonami Shoso site (in Japanese; https://1073shoso.jp/www/sankyo/detail.jsp?id=18667)

What it is: A city web page excerpting a 1996 research-institute book, describing three house groves of the dispersed-farmstead country of the Tonami plain in Toyama: a forest-like one at Seimei, a cedar windbreak at Inami, and a zelkova grove at Horinouchi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives surveyed counts for individual farmsteads (99 trees of which 62 were cedar in a 1984 survey; 45 species and 234 trees in 1983), shows cedar planted thickest on the windward south and west side, a stone wall higher on the wind side (2 m against 1.5 m), and fruit trees and bamboo placed on the other sides. These are three old family houses chosen as examples, and the surveys are from the 1980s: the page says most of the first house's trees were replanted after wartime requisitioning and that wartime felling removed the largest cedars, so the counts describe a twentieth-century grove, not an Edo one.
