youxiuling-rice-fertilizing-history

You Xiuling (游修龄), "施肥 - 《中国稻作史》第四章(六)" ("Shifei", Fertilizing, from History of Chinese Rice Cultivation, chapter 4, section 6, title translated), posted on 中国经济史论坛 (China Economic History Forum), 7 May 2010 (in Chinese; http://economy.guoxue.com/?p=431)

What it is: A web copy of one section of a Chinese history of rice growing, tracing the words for manure (fen, yong), the growth of fertilizing theory from the Warring States to the Qing, the kinds of fertilizer (human and animal waste, ash, green manure, oil cake, river mud) and the base and top dressing of paddy, and the cycle of waste between town and field.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports Chinese paddy fertilizing as an old, widely practiced and well-theorized part of rice growing, with quoted passages from the Song agricultural treatise of Chen Fu (1149), Wang Zhen (1313), the Shen family's farm book and the Zhixiang Tigang (1747), and it ties the heavy use of river mud and purchased night soil to the dense Hangzhou-Jiaxing-Huzhou region. Most of its detail is from the Ming and Qing in the lower Yangzi, it says no fertilizing is recorded for rice in the north, and it gives no rates or yields; it is a forum copy of a book chapter, with footnotes naming only short titles.
