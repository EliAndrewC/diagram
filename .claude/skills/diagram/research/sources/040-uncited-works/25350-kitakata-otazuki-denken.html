kitakata-otazuki-denken

Kitakata City, "喜多方市小田付伝統的建造物群保存地区について" ("About the Otazuki preservation district for groups of traditional buildings, Kitakata City", title translated), Urban Development Division page on the Kitakata City website (in Japanese; https://www.city.kitakata.fukushima.jp/soshiki/toshiseibi/19478.html)

What it is: A city page on the Otazuki district of Kitakata, Fukushima, selected as a nationally important preservation district for groups of traditional buildings in 2018, giving its area (about 15.5 hectares), its counts of listed buildings, structures and garden or waterway features, and a short account of its history. Most of the rest is the city's preservation rules, subsidy rates and a timeline of the designation work.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Otazuki was laid out in plots in 1582, when a periodic market was moved there from nearby villages, grew as a brewing town (sake, miso, soy sauce) and trading center of northern Aizu, and that its roads, waterways and plot divisions from the end of the Edo period are well preserved with storehouses of many kinds along them; that supports the form of a market and brewing town. The page does not give plot sizes, street widths or the dates of the surviving buildings, and its figures count a restored district of today.
