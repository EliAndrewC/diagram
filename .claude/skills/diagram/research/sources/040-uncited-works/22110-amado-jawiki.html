ja.wikipedia 雨戸 ("amado", rain shutter, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9B%A8%E6%88%B8)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the amado, the sliding wooden storm shutter set outside a Japanese house's openings, with its history, its track and storage-box structure and its latch.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the name appears in building drawings from the late sixteenth century, the Jurakudai great hall of 1587 being the earliest, and that earlier houses had hinged lattice shutters and paneled doors for the weather. It says the shutter slides on a single groove and is drawn one leaf at a time from a box; it gives no sizes, and says nothing about how common the shutter was in farmhouses before the modern period.
