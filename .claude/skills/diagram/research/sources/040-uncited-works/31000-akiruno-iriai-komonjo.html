akiruno-iriai-komonjo

Akiruno City (あきる野市), "郷土の古文書 その5 入会山争論(1)" ("Old documents of our homeland, no. 5: the commons-mountain dispute, part 1", title translated), an explanatory sheet with transcriptions and modern-Japanese renderings (in Japanese; https://city.akiruno.tokyo.jp/cmsfiles/contents/0000001/1225/sono05.pdf)

What it is: A municipal explanatory sheet on iriai commons, with two transcribed documents from Keian 2 (1649): a magistrate's ruling in a dispute between Okuno village and Ina village over a fodder mountain, and the Ina villagers' pledge afterward. It lists what villagers took from commons and says leaf-and-grass litter spread on fields was the main fertilizer.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows what a village commons supplied (fodder, field fertilizer, wild foods, thatching reed, fuel, road and bridge timber) and how two villages of the Akiruno plateau in Musashi quarrelled over one, ending in a ruling that kept existing private stands out of the commons and a pledge not to cut other timber or damage crops. It treats a single seventeenth-century case on one plateau, and says nothing about commons size or how many villages shared one.
