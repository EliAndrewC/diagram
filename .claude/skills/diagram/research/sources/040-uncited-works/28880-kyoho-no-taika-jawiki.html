ja.wikipedia 享保の大火 ("Kyoho no taika", the great fire of Kyoho, the Osaka fire of 1724, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/享保の大火)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article on the Kyoho fire that broke out in Osaka on 14 April 1724 (Kyoho 9), also called Myochi-yake. It gives the course of the fire over two days, the damage, and the shogunate's relief.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the losses of one great Edo-period urban fire: about two thirds of the city, 11,765 houses, 407 wards, 32 storehouses of domains and 44 temples and shrines burned, 293 dead, and ten thousand koku of rice released for relief. It also says the fire spread with the wind shifting through the day and that the castle moat stopped it at the castle. It is a stub drawing on two cited books, so it is evidence of how far a fire could run in a dense merchant city of the early 1700s, and not of the fire-safety rules of a smaller town.
