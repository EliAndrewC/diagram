tonamino-sankyomura

Tonamino.jp, "散居村とカイニョ、アズマダチ" ("The scattered-farmstead villages, the kainyo groves and the azumadachi houses", title translated), in "となみ野を知る" ("Getting to know the Tonami plain", title translated), on the Tonamino.jp site (in Japanese; https://tonamino.jp/s/shiru/post_7.html)

What it is: A page on a local information site describing the scattered farmstead settlement (sankyomura) of the Tonami plain in Toyama, where each farmhouse stands alone among its own fields. It covers the theories of how the pattern arose, the house form with its gable to the east, and the layout of the barn, ash hut, trees and water supply around each house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a dispersed-farmstead form for a plain of braided rivers and gives a farmstead's layout: barn and ash hut east of the main house, a belt of mainly cedar around it, fruit trees to the southwest, bamboo at the kitchen's north side, and river water led in through a settling tank. It says the origin of the pattern is not settled (the Kaga domain's order for defense and protection against foehn fires are named as proposals the page does not confirm) and that the plain was developed mainly for paddy; it describes the surviving houses of today and gives no date for the layout or any dimension.
