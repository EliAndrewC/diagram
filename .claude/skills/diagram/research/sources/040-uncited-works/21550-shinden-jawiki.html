ja.wikipedia 新田 ("shinden", newly opened fields, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%96%B0%E7%94%B0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on shinden, farmland newly opened from marsh, lake, tidal flat or upland, covering Edo-period reclamation, its kinds by who led it, and its regional examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sorts new fields by sponsor (a magistrate's survey, a domain, a local magnate, a village together, or a city merchant hiring tenants) and states a rise in the national rice yield from 18 million koku early in the Edo period to 25 million in the middle and 30 million at the end. It adds that such land was often left unopened because of drought and flood risk and so was more hazard-prone than old fields, and that some projects failed, such as Inba and Teganuma. It gives no field layout or plot size, the page flags that its text lacks sources, and its flood example is the Kumamoto storm surge of 1927.
