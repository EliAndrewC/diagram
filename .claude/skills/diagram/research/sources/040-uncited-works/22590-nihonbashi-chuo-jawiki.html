ja.wikipedia 日本橋 (東京都中央区) ("Nihonbashi (Chuo ward, Tokyo)", the district named for the bridge, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本橋_(東京都中央区))

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Nihonbashi, the district and the town name in Chuo ward, Tokyo, named for the bridge over the Nihonbashi river; the town name itself covers the three blocks at the bridge's south end. It covers the district's Edo-period role as the start of the five highways and as a merchant and money center, its history through the 1923 earthquake, and its present population, businesses and transport.

Why it applies, and its limits: For Edo it says the area was among the first laid out as town ground, that Edo Honmachi was the first street plan, along the Oshu road, that the gold mint, money changers, drug wholesalers and great shops such as Echigoya gathered there, that the fish market stood by the bridge until the 1923 earthquake moved it to Tsukiji, and that the five highways began at the bridge, from which road distance is still measured. Most of the page is the modern business district, with its 2023 population and 2021 business counts, and the Edo account is a few paragraphs about the shogun's capital, which is far larger than any provincial town.
