okada-kitchen-bath-minka

Okada Kenji (housing journalist), "今と昔の台所と浴室考" ("imatomukashi no daidokoro yokushitsu kō", thoughts on kitchens and baths, then and now, title translated), in "にっぽん民家紀行" (Nippon minka kikō), chapter 2 of a Kitchen and Bath Industry Association publication, revised from its bulletin Nos. 38-45 (January 1995 to October 1996) (in Japanese; https://kitchen-bath.jp/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/24imatomukasinodaidokoroyokusitukou.pdf)

What it is: A magazine-style essay by a housing journalist, reprinted as a PDF chapter, touring the kitchens, hearths, water jars, sinks and baths of old farmhouses, merchant houses and inns, mostly buildings moved to open-air museums (the Kawasaki Japan Open-Air Folk House Museum, the Hokkaido Historical Village) plus the Ito estate in Niigata. It gives the stated sizes of several of them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated, measured examples of Japanese house kitchens: a detached kitchen wing joined to the main house in a T (the Mochizuki house, built mid-18th century, main house 4.5 by 5 ken and kitchen wing 5 by 3 ken), hearths 30 to 100 cm high with the fire bed at floor level so the cook squatted, and the Ito landlord's 70 tsubo kitchen serving about 60 residents and cooking a bale of rice a day. The houses are surviving, relocated buildings from the 17th to 19th centuries with some Meiji and Taisho examples (Hokkaido settlers' houses, the gas-fired brick range of the Ito house), the Ito landlord's holding of 1,000 cho was reached only in the 1890s, and the author gives no survey method or count of how common any form was.
