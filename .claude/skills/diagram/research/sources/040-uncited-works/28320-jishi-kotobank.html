jishi-kotobank

Suma Chikai (須磨千穎), Yoshida Nobuyuki (吉田伸之), Shimada Jiro (島田次郎) and others, "地子" ("jishi", land rent, title translated), in the Daijisen, Nihon kokugo daijiten, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典), Nipponica and other works, on Kotobank (the page saved under this address is the jishi entry) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/柵-73184)

What it is: A set of dictionary and encyclopedia entries on jishi, the rent on land, from the Ritsuryo state to the Meiji land-tax reform of 1873. The address given here ends in the word for a fence, but the page saved from it is the jishi entry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the meaning of jishi by age: a rent of one fifth of the harvest on state land in the Heian period, a levy on dry fields and house plots in the medieval period, and in the Edo period a money levy on the house lots of towns, which many towns were excused from, owing labor service instead. It says that in most villages the house lot was too small a part of the land for its tax to be felt separately, which is a point on how village homesteads were taxed. The entries give the term's changing meaning, not the amount of the Edo rate or a village's practice.
