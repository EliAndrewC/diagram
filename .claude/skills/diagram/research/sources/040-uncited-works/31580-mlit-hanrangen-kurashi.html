mlit-hanrangen-kurashi

Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (国土交通省), "氾濫原と人々の暮らし" ("hanrangen to hitobito no kurashi", Floodplains and people's lives, title translated), teaching-material slides of the land classification survey, on nlftp.mlit.go.jp (in Japanese; https://nlftp.mlit.go.jp/kokjo/inspect/landclassification/land/teaching_material_2.pdf)

What it is: A 28-slide teaching deck on river floodplains in Japan, setting terrain classification maps beside Meiji-era land-use maps (mainly around Kawaguchi, Saitama) to show where villages, paddies, fields and woods lay. It then turns to floods, liquefaction and flood works.

Why it applies, and its limits: From the Meiji maps it shows settlements strung in lines along natural levees that stand 1 to 2 m above the floodplain, paddies on the floodplain and marsh, and fields and woods on the dry terraces, which have little water; it also shows the mizuka, a raised mound with a storehouse and a hung boat, and the Kizu canal of 1650 that made paddies possible on the Komaki terrace. Its maps are of the 1880s and later, in the Kanto and Aichi, and its flood-control and liquefaction slides are about the present day, so it supports where the forms stand on the terrain and not any earlier layout.
