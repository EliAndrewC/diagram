karuchibe-kariki-kachiki

Matsuyama Makoto (松山誠), "第72回 「かっちき」「刈敷」とは何か～草を肥料にする農耕の知恵" ("Episode 72: What are 'kacchiki' and 'kariki'? The farming wisdom of fertilizing with grass", title translated), in the column "園藝探偵の本棚" ("The horticultural detective's bookshelf"), Karuchibe (karuchibe.jp), published June 26, 2020 (in Japanese; https://karuchibe.jp/read/10528)

What it is: A magazine-site column on kariki, the practice of working cut green twigs, leaves and grass into a flooded paddy as fertilizer before transplanting, built on two books (Mizumoto Kunihiko's study of Edo grass-hills and Yamaguchi Susumu's account of a Yamanashi town). It gives the Yamanashi steps for kacchiki, the 2 to 4 meter pollard height of the oak stumps that supply the twigs, a rule of thumb of four packhorse loads of green grass, and a quoted passage from Furushima Toshio on the regions where kariki was the main paddy fertilizer.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports kariki as a main self-supplied paddy fertilizer of Edo agriculture, drawn from commons hills and grass-hills near the village, with a worked sequence (cut in spring, lay in the flooded paddy before transplanting, transplant between the twigs, gather the remaining large branches the next spring) and a pollard height of 2 to 4 meters, which the book's author guesses was chosen so the shoots get sun in time for planting. The step sequence and the height come from one farmer's account in one Yamanashi town, said in the page to have been practiced across Japan until the mid-Showa period; the regional spread and the old farm-book lists of plants are the columnist's secondhand summary of the books and carry no figures of area or yield. The saying that four packhorse loads of green grass need no other manure names no area of paddy it was for, and the page's 135 kg a load is the columnist's own conversion.
