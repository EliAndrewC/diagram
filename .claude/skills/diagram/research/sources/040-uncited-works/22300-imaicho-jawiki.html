ja.wikipedia 今井町 ("Imai-cho", a moated temple town in Kashihara, Nara, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/今井町)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Imai-cho in Kashihara, Nara, a Sengoku-period temple town (jinai-machi) ringed by moats, with its street plan, gates, history to the Meiji era and its present-day preservation district.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes how a self-governing merchant town was laid out: a grid in a rectangle (the present district is about 600 m by 310 m; a note puts the 16th-century moated town at about 450 m by 250 m), streets bent or ended in T-junctions to block sight lines, nine gates with four open at night, and three rings of moat with an earth bank planted with brush. Its figures for moat width and depth differ between passages and its notes, and it records the town in the 16th to 18th centuries as a wealthy, armed, self-governing exception (about 1,082 houses and 4,400 people in 1679), not an ordinary village or county town.
