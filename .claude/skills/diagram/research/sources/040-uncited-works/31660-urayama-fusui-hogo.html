urayama-fusui-hogo

Urayama Ryuichi (浦山隆一), Toyama International University, "「風水と抱護」 ―私の環境観―" ("fusui to hogo: watashi no kankyokan", Fengshui and hogo: my view of the environment, title translated), lecture slides (in Japanese; https://petoyama.net/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/080705H4.pdf)

What it is: A five-slide set by a Toyama International University lecturer on fengshui in East Asia and on the hogo, the forest planted around a village in the Ryukyu Islands, with cases from Taramajima and Ishigaki. It closes with a modern spa built on fengshui ideas and a note on present-day uses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the hogo was a belt of trees round a village, shows one about 1.6 km long on Taramajima planted with fukugi and the parcel outlines of a vanished one on Ishigaki that the late-Meiji cadastral survey still recorded, and quotes the Ryukyuan statesman Sai On and Tei Ryosa on planting trees to hold the land's energy. It argues the belt was also a windbreak, a ritual place and a boundary and may have been planned with the village well, but as a hypothesis, in slide fragments; the cases are Ryukyuan, not mainland Japan or China, and the surviving belts are modern.
