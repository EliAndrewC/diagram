goossaert-2000-2

Vincent Goossaert, "Counting the Monks. The 1736-1739 Census of the Chinese Clergy", Late Imperial China 21 (2), December 2000, pp. 40-85, deposited in the HAL open archive (https://shs.hal.science/halshs-00106193v1/file/countingthemonks.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a peer-reviewed article analyzing the Qing census of 1736-1739, in which the Board of Rites registered about 340,000 Buddhist and Taoist clerics county by county, with tables of the extant figures and maps of where Buddhist monks and nuns and the Taoist orders lived.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the only nation-wide count of clergy the author knows of for the Chinese empire, with per-province extrapolations of 0.65 to 3.87 clerics per thousand people and a national mean of 1.92, and it finds from gazetteer lists that registered clerics did not live mainly in large monasteries (a Welch estimate cited there puts the average temple at 5 clerics in residence). It is one count from one reign (1736-1739), not a series across dynasties, and the author himself says the registration was uneven: nuns and married Taoists were badly under-registered, a third of the registers is missing, and he judges the true clergy at about 0.4 percent of the population, well above the registered figure.
