ranhaku-soshinji

Ranzan Town (嵐山町), "日蓮宗 宗信寺 菅谷村大字鎌形" ("Nichiren sect, Soshin-ji, Kamagata in Sugaya Village", title translated), from the Hiki County temple register (比企郡寺院明細帳), in the Ranzan Town Web Museum, vol. 6 (嵐山町web博物誌), on the Ranzan town site (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/09ter16.html)

What it is: A town-hosted transcription of the Meiji-era Hiki County temple register entry for Soshin-ji, a Nichiren temple in Kamagata village, Musashi, with the town's footnotes on its head temple and sect branch. It gives the founding, buildings, precinct, outlying land and parishioner count.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records a temple founded in 1624 (Kan'ei 1) with its founder named as a lay developer, Sugita Hyobu Munenobu, and its first priest named as Nippo; this is a dated example of a lay patron founding a village temple in the seventeenth century. The hall is 7 ken by 5 ken 3 shaku, the priest's residence 6 by 4 ken, the precinct 426 tsubo, and the temple holds 140 parishioner households and 2 cho 5 tan of forest in addition to paddy and fields. The founding date and names are the register's own statement with no supporting evidence on the page, and it covers one temple.
