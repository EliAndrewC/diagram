"東京夢華錄/卷三" ("Dongjing Menghua Lu, scroll 3", title translated), on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E6%9D%B1%E4%BA%AC%E5%A4%A2%E8%8F%AF%E9%8C%84/%E5%8D%B7%E4%B8%89)

What it is: The full text of the third scroll of the Dongjing Menghua Lu, a memoir-guide to the capital Dongjing (Kaifeng), as transcribed on Chinese Wikisource. Its thirteen sections walk the streets around the palace, the Xiangguo Temple market, the list of temples and Daoist halls, the night markets, the carts that carry goods, money-string counts, hiring, fire watch, the morning market and the trades that serve households.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a capital's street life in concrete terms a premodern East Asian city could draw on: a patrol post with five soldiers in every lane of about three hundred paces, brick watchtowers with a hundred or more soldiers and fire gear beside them, night markets running to the third watch and reopening at the fifth, cart types by animal and load, and a different number of coins to the string by trade (77 for the government, 75 on the street, 72 for fish, meat and vegetables). It describes one very large imperial capital, not a provincial city or town, and the page names no date for the scenes and no writer, so its figures cannot be read as typical of smaller places or dated more closely than the text itself allows.
