sougamae-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 総構え ("sogamae", a castle's outer enclosure taking in its town, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/惣構)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the sogamae, the outer line of moat and earthwork that enclosed a castle together with its castle town, with a list of castles that had one. It gives lengths for Odawara, Osaka, Kyoto's Odoi and Sakai, and says when the form was built and dropped.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a source for how a castle town's ring was drawn and how long it ran: Odawara's about 9 km (2.5 ri) of dry moat and earthwork, the Kyoto Odoi's 22.4 km with moat and bank each about 20 m wide, and Sakai's moat about 3 m deep and 10 m wide on three sides. It says the form went up in the late Azuchi-Momoyama period and fell out of use after Sekigahara because it hindered the town's growth, so it speaks for the largest daimyo seats around 1580-1600, not for a modest castle town, and it gives no figure for a typical one; the article is marked a stub.
