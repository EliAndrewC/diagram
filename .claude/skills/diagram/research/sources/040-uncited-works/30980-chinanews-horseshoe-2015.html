chinanews-horseshoe-2015

"秦汉铁匠不会打马掌 元朝建立后马掌才在中原流行" ("Qin-Han blacksmiths could not shoe horses; horseshoes became common in the Central Plains only after the Yuan was founded", title translated), Shanxi Evening News (山西晚报), 30 April 2015, on China News Service (中新网) (in Chinese; https://chinanews.com.cn/cul/2015/04-30/7244333.shtml)

What it is: A newspaper culture piece, reposted on China News Service, on when horseshoes reached China, prompted by a blacksmith shown making one in a Qin-Han television drama. It runs from the Later Jin envoy's 938 account of wooden hoof guards at Ganzhou through the Song and Yuan to the 1980s, and lists where excavated horseshoes have turned up.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a late, northern and frontier arrival of shod hooves in China: it says the technique stayed a foreign novelty through the Tang and Song and spread in the north only after the Yuan, and that excavated shoes cluster in Jilin, Liaoning and Heilongjiang and are rare in the North China Plain and Jiangnan. The piece names no study for its excavation claim, and its one quoted source is the 938 envoy's report from the Hexi corridor, so it cannot say when a given county's draft animals were first shod.
