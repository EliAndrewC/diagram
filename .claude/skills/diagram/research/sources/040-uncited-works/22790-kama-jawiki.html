ja.wikipedia 鎌 ("kama", the sickle or scythe, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/鎌)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the sickle (kama), the hand tool for cutting grass and grain, covering its history from prehistory, its types in Japan (grass, brushwood, tree and serrated-blade sickles), its symbolism and its use as a weapon.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that Japanese sickles have no scythe-and-sickle split, that rice-country blades are straight and wheat-country blades curved, and that the serrated sickle is called the rice-reaping sickle, and it names brushwood and tree-cutting types by use. It gives no dates or sizes for the Japanese types, much of it is about Europe and weapons, and it carries a notice that it lacks sources and may contain original research.
