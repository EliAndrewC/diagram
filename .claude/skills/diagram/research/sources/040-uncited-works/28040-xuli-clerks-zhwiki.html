xuli-clerks-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 胥吏 ("xuli, the yamen clerks", title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E8%83%A5%E5%90%8F)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the xuli, the clerks who handled case files and documents under Chinese officials, tracing them from the Wei-Jin period through the Tang, Song, Ming and Qing and quoting what officials and scholars said of them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the point that a magistrate's office ran on a large body of unranked, hereditary clerks who knew the precedents better than the rotating official did - the article gives about 350,000 clerks in the Tang, about 440,000 in the Northern Song, and a Qing remark that a large county's clerks could number a thousand. The figures come from quoted officials and critics who were hostile to the clerks, the picture is mostly late imperial and its examples are central and prefectural, and it says nothing about how many clerks one county yamen held in practice or where they sat.
