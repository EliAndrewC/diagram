ranhaku-toshoji

Ranzan Town (嵐山町), "曹洞宗 東昌寺 菅谷村大字菅谷" ("Soto sect, Tosho-ji, Sugaya in Sugaya Village", title translated), from the Hiki County temple register (比企郡寺院明細帳), in the Ranzan Town Web Museum, vol. 6 (嵐山町web博物誌), on the Ranzan town site (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/09ter09.html)

What it is: A town-hosted transcription of the Meiji-era Hiki County temple register entry for Tosho-ji, a Soto Zen temple in Sugaya village, Musashi, with the town's footnotes. It records the temple's moves, its hall size, precinct, outlying land and parishioner count.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its founding account says the temple, first called Chokei-ji, stood southeast of the old castle ruins (the town's note names these as the Sugaya Yakata site), was moved after the castle was abandoned, and was moved again to its present ground; that is a documented case of a village temple relocating beside a former fortified site. The precinct is 312 tsubo and is classed as private land of the first class held in common in the temple's name, unlike the state-land fourth class of most neighboring entries; there are 29 parishioner households and the hall burned in 1910. The account of the moves is undated and unexplained beyond the abandonment of the castle, and the page covers one temple.
