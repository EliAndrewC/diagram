ishigaki-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 石垣 ("ishigaki", stone walls, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/石垣)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on stone walls, with short sections on the West and South America and a long one on Japan from the Kofun period to the Edo period, covering castle walls, the three ways of laying them, corner stones and the bulging (haramu) that damages old walls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Japan's slopes made stone foundations necessary for farmland and house sites, that terrace edges of paddies are held by stone walls, and that Ryukyu villages ringed houses with walls against typhoons, which supports stone retaining and boundary walls as an ordinary form. Its detail is on castles: it names the three castle-wall styles (nozurazumi, uchikomihagi, kirikomihagi), dates their naming to Ogyu Sorai's 1727 Kenroku, and says large walls spread from the Oda and Toyotomi era mostly in the west, but it gives no heights or dimensions for farm or house walls.
