sanuki-rekimin-tameike-fushin

Sanuki City Museum of History and Folklore, "ため池の普請 維持管理 編" ("Building and maintaining irrigation ponds", title translated), in its guide "Sanuki no tameike to kurashi" (Sanuki's ponds and life), on the museum's site (in Japanese; https://www.sanuki.ne.jp/rekimin/ikekouzi.html)

What it is: A museum guide page on how irrigation ponds were built and kept up in the Sanuki area of Kagawa in the Edo period. It covers embankment work, intake structures, embankment failures and a village headman's well, and gives its reference as a 2000 Kagawa prefectural book on Sanuki's ponds.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the labor of building a pond embankment by hand: digging soil with hoes, carrying it in baskets, spreading it in thin layers and tamping it with pestles, with the work done in the dry winter. It says wooden bottom conduits had to be replaced every 20 to 30 years, and that the lords of Takamatsu set embankment widths in a design standard of 1798. It is about Kagawa's dry-country ponds and names no pond dimensions or labor counts, so it supports the method and the upkeep burden, not a size.
