hagi-daihachiguruma

Hagi City (萩市), "大八車（だいはちぐるま）・シャリキ 萩の民具（13）" ("The daihachi cart and the shariki, Hagi's folk tools no. 13", title translated), in the city bulletin Shihou Hagi, 1 May 1991, on the Hagi Museum's pages (in Japanese; https://www.city.hagi.lg.jp/hagihaku/hikidashi/mingu/html/013.htm)

What it is: A short municipal museum note on the daihachi cart, a large hand-drawn goods cart called shariki in the Hagi district, with a word on its name, its spread and its lashing and towing fittings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the cart is said to have begun in Edo in the early Edo period, that its names are given by two stories, that it came into wide use across Japan after roads were improved in the Meiji period, and that Hagi kept using it into the 1950s and 60s. The page's origin and name accounts are marked as hearsay and the page gives no size, load or date for Hagi's carts, so it is about a cart of the Meiji era and later more than of the Edo period.
