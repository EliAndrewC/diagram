en.wikipedia "Hongcun" (the village of Hongcun in Anhui, China) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongcun)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Hongcun, a UNESCO-listed village in Yi County, Huizhou, southern Anhui, with its founding, layout and house form.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the village to 1131 and says it grew from 1400 to about 1900, with about 150 Ming and Qing residences, a water channel said to be 400 years old running to every household, and streets laid alongside it, so it can show a Huizhou lineage village's water-led layout. It is the preserved showpiece of a lineage the article says grew rich in trade, so its houses and waterway show what merchant wealth built, not an ordinary farming village. The ox-shaped plan and the courtyard houses are what survives from the Ming and Qing, not the Song village, and several of its claims (the quality of the carvings, the granite paving) carry no citation.
