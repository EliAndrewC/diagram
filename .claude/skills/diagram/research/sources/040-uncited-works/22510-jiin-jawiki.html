ja.wikipedia 寺院 ("jiin", the Buddhist temple, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寺院)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the temple, giving its origin in Indian monasteries and the Chinese use of the word for an office, the split into worship halls and monks' quarters, Japanese temples with their danka parishioners, graveyards and architecture styles, and the halls of a Zen temple.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Japan section says that small local temples hold a parish of danka households and keep a graveyard, and are generally closed to non-parishioners, unlike shrines, while famous temples in Osaka, Nara and Kyoto draw wide pilgrim crowds. It lists the Zen seven-hall set (gate, main hall, lecture hall, meditation hall, kitchen-residence, latrine, bath) and a longer list of optional buildings, with the head priest's quarters, the hojo. It states that temple layouts and building names vary by sect and period and are not standardized as shrines are, so one list cannot stand for every temple, and it gives no sizes or counts.
