kotobank-higata-hachimangoku

"干潟八万石" ("Higata hachimangoku", the Higata eighty-thousand koku, title translated), a mention in the entry on the Tsubaki Sea reclamation in the older Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (世界大百科事典 旧版), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B9%B2%E6%BD%9F%E5%85%AB%E4%B8%87%E7%9F%B3-1397855)

What it is: A very short Kotobank page that holds one excerpt, from the old Heibonsha entry on the Tsubaki Sea reclamation in Shimosa (now Chiba), about the drained lake bed later called Higata hachimangoku.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the contractor Tsujiuchi, with the help of the monk Tetsugyu, finished a new channel in 1670 and began draining the lake, which flooded and damaged villages along the river, and that the dried lakebed was turned to paddy. A full survey in 1695 gave the 18 villages of Tsubaki new field 20,441 koku, 22,072 koku in 1743, and an area of 4,445 cho (survey of 1876). It is only an excerpt of a longer entry, it does not explain the later name's 80,000 koku against these figures, and it describes no layout.
