Yokohama National Highway Office, Kanto Regional Development Bureau, Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, "橋のない川はどうやって渡っていたのですか？" ("How did people cross rivers that had no bridge?", title translated), Tokaido Q&A, Q10 (in Japanese; https://www.ktr.mlit.go.jp/yokohama/tokaido/02_tokaido/04_qa/index3/answer10.htm)

What it is: A government question-and-answer page on how Edo-period travelers crossed unbridged rivers on the Tokaido: carried on porters' shoulders or litters, or by ferry; a bridge of boats was laid only for special processions such as the shogun's journey to Kyoto or a Korean embassy. It names the Tama, Sagami and Sakawa rivers in Kanagawa.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the two ordinary crossing methods, that the Tama and Sagami were crossed by ferry and the Sakawa on foot, in a litter or by horse, and that a flood closed the crossing so travelers were held at the post town before the river. It speaks of the Tokaido's large rivers only, and says nothing of the fords, fees or ferries of an ordinary village road.
