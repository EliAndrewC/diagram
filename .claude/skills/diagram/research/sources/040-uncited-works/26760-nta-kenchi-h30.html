nta-kenchi-h30

National Tax Agency, National Tax College, "1 検地" ("1. Land survey", title translated), in its 2018 special exhibition of tax-history documents (in Japanese; https://www.nta.go.jp/about/organization/ntc/sozei/tokubetsu/h30shiryoukan/01.htm)

What it is: A long page of the National Tax College's 2018 exhibition on the Edo-period land survey (kenchi): how fields were surveyed, graded and given a koku value, with the survey tools and methods, a village map and survey register of a small mountain village in Echigo, a surveyor's field notebook from Dewa, the arithmetic and writing textbooks villagers learned from, and a Sendai foot soldier's 1869 return of his lands.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the survey as a village-prepared report checked by the lord's sampling, done in spring or autumn when fields were bare, with a pace of one village or more a day in short cases and 60 villages in about 40 days in Date Masamune's domain during Hideyoshi's Taiko survey (a late-16th-century pace, not an Edo one), and it argues the survey was an outline inspection resting on the village's own declaration. The Echigo village it shows had nine households and 65 people in the 18th century, with no irrigation works and rain-fed paddy, which is one small hill village. The page gives its argument without notes, so it is the exhibition's reading, not a count.
