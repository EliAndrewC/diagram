lowder-1874-ieyasu-legacy

Toshogu (Tokugawa Ieyasu), "The Legacy of Iyeyasu", translated by J. F. Lowder (Yokohama, 1874), reprinted as Appendix V (pp. 796-814) of J. H. Longford, The Story of Old Japan, with the translator's notes and the reprinter's notes, on gwern.net (in English; https://gwern.net/doc/japan/history/1874-lowder-thelegacyofieyasu.pdf)

What it is: An English translation of the hundred-chapter "Legacy of Iyeyasu", a set of maxims and rules on government attributed to Tokugawa Ieyasu, with six chapters on administrative detail left out and explanatory footnotes. It covers rule over daimyo and samurai, punishments, religion, roads, and the villages' land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries rules a premodern Japanese ruler is made to lay down for the countryside: road widths from a 36-foot great sea road to a 3-foot field path, and an open space of about 360 feet at each ferry landing (chapter 66), a ban on building dwellings on cultivated ground because of the bamboo and trees around the walls, with a three-foot height test for an old enclosure (68), and the cutting back of large trees that shade grain drying near villages (69). The page attributes the text to Ieyasu but gives no date or manuscript for it, the translator admits he could not explain several terms and the reprinter says some explanations are guesswork, and the measures are given in the translator's feet, not in ken or shaku.
