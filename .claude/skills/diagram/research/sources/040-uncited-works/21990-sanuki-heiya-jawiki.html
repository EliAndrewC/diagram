ja.wikipedia 讃岐平野 ("Sanuki heiya", the Sanuki plain, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%AE%83%E5%B2%90%E5%B9%B3%E9%87%8E)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article on the Sanuki plain of Kagawa, in northeastern Shikoku: its area, its four sub-plains, the rivers that build its fans, its geology, and how people have used it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the plain is dry because of the Seto Inland Sea climate, so many irrigation ponds were built, among them Mannoike, said to have been repaired in the ninth century by Kukai and Japan's largest. It also says the ancient jori field grid survives, that salt pans lined the coast and that inland settlements are dispersed; its areas (692.90 km2 in all) and the rivers' habit of running underground are modern measurements, and it gives no pond counts, sizes or dates for the dispersed settlement.
