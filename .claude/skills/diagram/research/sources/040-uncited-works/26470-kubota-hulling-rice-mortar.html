kubota-hulling-rice-mortar

Kubota Corporation, "臼を使った「籾摺り(もみすり)」" ("Hulling rice with a mortar", title translated), from 稲作の歴史とそれを支えた伝統農具, on Kubota no Tanbo (in Japanese; https://www.kubota.co.jp/kubotatanbo/history/tools/hulling.html)

What it is: A short company educational page on hulling rice, from the Yayoi vertical mortar and pestle to the wooden-rimmed and earthen hulling mills, with their sizes, and a note on a water-driven automatic pounder.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the earthen mill (dosu) came from China in the Genroku era, took two or three people to turn, and broke more grain than the wooden-rimmed mill, so it dates the hulling tools a farm owned. The Genroku date is its own statement, uncited, and it gives no counts of how many farms owned a mill.
