kawasaki-hara-inari

Kawasaki City Board of Education, "旧原家住宅稲荷社" ("The Inari shrine of the former Hara house", title translated), in the city's guide to cultural properties of Nakahara Ward (in Japanese; https://www.city.kawasaki.jp/880/page/0000112947.html)

What it is: A city cultural-properties entry for a small house-shrine (yashikigami) to Inari on the grounds of the old Hara family, wealthy farmers and large landowners in Kosugi Jinyacho, Kawasaki, built in the late Meiji era and registered as a national tangible cultural property in 2019.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a village landowner's family keeping a small Inari shrine inside house grounds that faced the Nakahara road, and gives its form: a one-bay nagare-zukuri hall of zelkova wood with a copper roof and an elaborately carved front, 1.0 square meter in building area, kept under a cover building. The shrine is a Meiji one of an unusually rich family, was moved a little in 2015, and no longer stands in its farmstead: the main house of 1911 was moved to the Nihon Minka-en in 1991 and the grounds are now an apartment block and a public plaza. The page gives no earlier date for the practice or any village-wide count.
