macau-pawnshops-macaudata

Huang Kaixin, Ou Wenhao, Deng Kaixin and Huang Minchong, "澳門典當業" ("The pawnbroking trade of Macau", title translated), in 澳門行業史 (A history of Macau's trades), on Macau Data (in Chinese; https://www.macaudata.mo/macaubook/book108/html/002301.htm)

What it is: A chapter-length history of pawnshops in Macau, with a short account of their Chinese origins from the Han to the Qing. Most of it describes Macau's shops of the 1940s to the 1980s: the four grades of shop, their buildings, fittings, staff, tickets, interest and customs.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the building of a large pawnshop in useful detail: a separate multi-story goods warehouse (huolou) set well apart from neighbors against fire, a street wall of granite below and brick above with narrow barred windows, a rooftop for airing furs and quilts, a screen inside the door so customers are not seen, and, for some large shops on the mainland, a wall like a city wall and even a moat. Its figures, such as 3 percent a month and a three-year term for the largest grade, are Macau's of the twentieth century, and it says the "nine out, thirteen back" rate was only a mainland small-shop practice; its account of the earlier dynasties is brief and gives no sources for the buildings of those times.
