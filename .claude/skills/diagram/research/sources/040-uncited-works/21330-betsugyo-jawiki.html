betsugyo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 別業 ("betsugyo", a nobleman's country estate of the ancient period, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%88%A5%E6%A5%AD)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia stub on the betsugyo (also read nari-dokoro), the country houses of ancient Japanese aristocrats, with a note on their uses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the Nihon Shoki records powerful men's villas and farm estates, that the same reading for the two words suggests a betsugyo had fields and woodland attached as an economic base, and that after the founding of Fujiwara-kyo nobles lived in the capital so the estates became bases for hunting and blossom viewing. It covers only the Asuka to Heian nobility and offers no plans, sizes or buildings, so it does not apply to a warrior's or a farmer's holding.
