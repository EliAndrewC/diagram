ja.wikipedia 酒井伴四郎 ("Sakai Hanshiro", title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%85%92%E4%BA%95%E4%BC%B4%E5%9B%9B%E9%83%8E)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Sakai Hanshiro, a 30-koku low samurai of the Kishu Wakayama domain whose diary of his 1860 service in Edo survives, with a summary of the diary's discovery and its later books and adaptations.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports that a low samurai on duty in an Edo domain compound worked only 47 days in the half-year of the surviving diary, mostly two to four hours in the morning, and spent the rest on sightseeing, temple visits, music practice and eating out. It is a biography and publication history, not the diary itself, and it speaks for one retainer of one branch house on rotation duty in Edo in 1860, not for samurai living on their home domain's land.
