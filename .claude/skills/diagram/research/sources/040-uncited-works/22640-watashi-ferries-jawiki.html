watashi-ferries-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 渡し船 ("watashibune", ferries, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/渡し)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on river and strait ferries, mostly a prefecture-by-prefecture list of ferries still running or lately closed, with a short history section and brief notes on ferries in China, Vietnam and elsewhere. The page was opened from the address 渡し, which redirects to 渡し船.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Edo-period material is a few lines: the Tokaido's Umaire river kept boats for up to 20 people, for horses and for freight, the Rokugo bridge was given up after 1688 in favor of a ferry, ferries stood where bridges were hard to build or barred for military reasons, and the Sumida River carried over 20 ferry routes in early Meiji because bridging was restricted for defense. The Kawaguchi ferry on the Arakawa is given three boats and a river about 60 ken (about 109 m) wide at normal water. The rest of the page describes ferries of the 2000s, so it speaks to the premodern setting only through those few passages, and it gives no fares, capacities or landing layouts for the period.
