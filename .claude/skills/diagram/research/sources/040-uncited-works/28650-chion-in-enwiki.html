en.wikipedia "Chion-in" (知恩院, Monastery of Gratitude), on English Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chion-in)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Chion-in, the Kyoto headquarters of the Jodo-shu sect, with its founding in 1234, its rebuilding by the shogun Iemitsu after the fire of 1633, its gate, guest houses, bell and nightingale floor, and the legends of its seven wonders.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dates and superlatives for one great temple, a gate of 1619 the article calls the largest surviving of its kind in Japan, guest houses of 1641 and a 74-ton bell of 1633, and says it was rebuilt under the Tokugawa patronage. It is one head temple of a major sect, in Kyoto and under shogunal patronage, so it is no guide to a provincial or village temple, and it gives no plan, dimensions of the compound or layout of its halls; the seven wonders are legends.
