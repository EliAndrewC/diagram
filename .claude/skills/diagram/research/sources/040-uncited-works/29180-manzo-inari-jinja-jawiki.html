ja.wikipedia 萬蔵稲荷神社 ("Manzo Inari Jinja", the Manzo Inari shrine, title translated), Japanese Wikipedia (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/萬蔵稲荷神社)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on a small Inari shrine at Ohara in Shiroishi, Miyagi, giving its founding about 1785 by a former packhorse driver turned mountain-ascetic priest, its later history, a founding legend, and photographs of its approach.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates a single shrine to the late Edo period, records that it took the old rank of village shrine in 1909 when sixteen neighboring shrines were merged into it, and lists what stands there (torii along the approach, a purification basin, a worship hall, a main hall) by photograph captions only. It gives no precinct size or layout, and it describes one shrine in Tohoku founded by a lone priest, with a legend, so it does not show how a village's own tutelary shrine was set out.
