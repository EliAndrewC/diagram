hall-2013-uzumimon

Chris Hall, "Japanese Gate Typology (8)", The Carpentry Way (blog), 10 January 2013 (https://thecarpentryway.blog/2013/01/japanese-gate-typology-8/)

What it is: A carpenter's blog post in a series on Japanese gate types, on the uzumimon, a gate sunk into the lower stone wall or embankment of a castle, with photographs from Himeji, Sasayama, Nijo, Sekiyado, Tatsuno, Nagoya and Odawara castles.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the uzumimon as a back gate between two ground levels inside a castle, from a full-height gate in the wall seen from below to a half-buried opening seen from above, whose smaller form was meant to be filled with soil and gravel under siege, and notes a version fitted with a small hatch in an earthen wall (tsuiji). It gives no dates or sizes, the author says the term's origin is unclear and that he is unsure whether one pictured roof is original, and several pictured castles are reconstructions, one gate (Sekiyado's) standing moved away from its site. It treats castle gates only, not village or town gates.
