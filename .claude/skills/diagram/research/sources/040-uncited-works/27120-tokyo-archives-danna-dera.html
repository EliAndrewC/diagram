Tokyo Metropolitan Archives, "江戸東京寺院探訪-東京のお寺を調べる-旦那寺への道" ("Exploring the temples of Edo and Tokyo: researching Tokyo's temples, the road to one's family temple", title translated), in 江戸東京を知る (in Japanese; https://www.soumu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/01soumu-archives/07edo_tokyo/0713jiin)

What it is: An archives guide to three bodies of records for researching a temple of Edo or Tokyo: the Gofunai bikou zokuhen (late Edo), the temple description registers of Meiji 5 (1872) and the temple registers of Meiji 10 (1877), with what each records and how to search them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that about seven tenths of Edo was warrior land and the rest was split about equally between townsman land and temple and shrine land, that Tokyo prefecture had 2,486 temples in 1872, and that the Meiji 10 registers carry precinct plans showing hall, residence, graveyard and tree species. The land shares are given as what 'is said' with no source, the page is a finding aid and does not reproduce the records, and the temple count is of 1872, after the end of the shogunate.
