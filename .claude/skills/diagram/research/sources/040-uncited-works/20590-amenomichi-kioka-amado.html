amenomichi-kioka-amado

Kioka Takao, "聚楽第大広間の雨戸 - 雨戸の出現とその時代背景" ("Jurakudai ohiroma no amado: amado no shutsugen to sono jidai haikei", the rain shutters of the great hall of the Jurakudai: the appearance of the amado and its period, title translated), vol. 5 of "雨が育てた日本建築" (Japanese architecture raised by rain), in the web magazine Ame no michi design, 2019 (in Japanese; https://amenomichi.com/nihon/kioka05.html)

What it is: A web-magazine column by a Japanese architect who restores historic buildings, tracing when the amado, the sliding wooden rain shutter, appeared. It dates its first record to the great hall of Hideyoshi's Jurakudai, 1586 to 1587, and reviews the earlier hinged, hanging and sliding door types.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says rain shutters were new in the late sixteenth century, so a setting modeled on the period before that has no amado, and that a shutter running in a single groove from a storage box appeared about the Keicho era (1596 to 1615) and became common by the Kanei era (1624 to 1644). It argues from plans and a few buildings of the warrior elite in Kyoto, Osaka and Sendai, and gives no evidence on how quickly the shutter reached ordinary farmhouses or towns, or what a village house used before it.
