Chao Mingsheng (巢鳴盛), "老圃良言" (Good Words from an Old Gardener, title translated), Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/老圃良言)

What it is: The short full text of a Ming treatise by Chao Mingsheng, in ten headings, on how to raise garden plants (the preface names fine vegetables and fruit, and one heading treats potted flowers): sowing, layering, grafting, transplanting, pruning, protection, forcing, pest control, preparing soil and watering.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries Ming horticultural practice for a village vegetable and fruit garden and potted plants: grafting methods, transplanting with a bamboo knife and stones over the soil, pruning for an old look, and watering by hand in the morning at the root and in the evening on the leaves. It is a single author's account of his neighbors' garden craft in a remote village, about garden vegetables, fruit, trees pruned for an old look and pot flowers, not field crops or a farm's layout. It mixes folk charms (striking trees with an axe at New Year, pouring festival wine on them) in with the craft, and it names no sizes, places or dates.
