Morohashi Kumiko, interview with Matsumura Hiroshi, "A History of Traditional Japanese Bridges, with Several Remarkable Examples", in Highlighting Japan, September 2023, Cabinet Office, Government of Japan (https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/202309/202309_00_en.html)

What it is: An English-language interview in a Cabinet Office magazine with a bridge engineer and author, on the history of Japan's traditional bridges. It covers wooden beam bridges, plank and earthen bridges, the rarity of stone arches before the modern period, famous bridges, and the arched drum bridges before shrine gates.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that until the early modern period almost all Japanese bridges were wooden beam bridges, that stone arches were built only in parts of Kyushu and Yamaguchi, and that bridges stood on alluvial plains and needed frequent repair after floods and fires. It also says that, apart from a few stone-arch bridges and one in Okinawa, the pre-Edo bridges designated as Important Cultural Properties all stand on shrine and temple grounds. It is an interview aimed at visitors and gives no spans, widths or counts.
