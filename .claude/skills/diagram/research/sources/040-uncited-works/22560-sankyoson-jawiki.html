ja.wikipedia 散居村 ("sankyoson", the dispersed village, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/散居村)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the dispersed settlement, a village form in which houses stand scattered among their fields, with the Tonami plain of Toyama Prefecture as its main example. It covers where the form occurs in Japan and abroad, how the Tonami farmsteads grew windbreak groves (kainyo), and the words sankyoson and sonson.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the dispersed form as a real Japanese village type and gives the Tonami case: a landscape dated to the late 16th and 17th centuries, farmsteads set on the slightly higher ground of a flood-prone plain, each ringed by a grove of cedar, chestnut, persimmon and plum, and about 7,000 households over some 220 square kilometers today. The count is the present one, and the page says the origins of most Japanese dispersed settlement are unclear and that many were made by modern land clearing; it carries a notice that its claims lack footnotes.
