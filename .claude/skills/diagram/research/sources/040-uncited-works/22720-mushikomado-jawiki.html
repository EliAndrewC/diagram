mushikomado-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 虫籠窓 ("mushikomado", the insect-cage window, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/虫籠窓)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article on the mushikomado, a window of fine lattice set in the plastered wall of a machiya's low upper floor, with its history and where it is found, and a gallery of surviving houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the window spread in the Edo period as fire protection in crowded townhouse districts, later served only as light and air, began as a small oval and became a large rectangle in Meiji, and is found most in plastered merchant houses in the Kyoto sphere, the Kinki, the Seto Inland Sea coasts, Hokuriku and Tokai. It gives no sizes and no dates for particular houses, and its fire-protection reason is given only as what is said to be the cause, with no citation: its two dictionary references support the definition alone.
