piras-2022-igune

Francesco Piras, Beatrice Fiore and Antonio Santoro, "Small Cultural Forests: Landscape Role and Ecosystem Services in a Japanese Cultural Landscape", Land 11(9), 1494, 2022 (https://mdpi.com/2073-445x/11/9/1494)

What it is: The full text of an open-access paper in the journal Land that maps and counts the igune (farmhouse windbreak woods) of the Osaki Kodo paddy plain in Miyagi Prefecture from 2002 and 2021 satellite images, and reviews the studies of the same area for what the woods supply. It reports their number, sizes, spacing and the trees they hold.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the counts a settlement's farmhouse groves can be set against: 1737 igune and small woods in 2021, 82% under 1 ha and 72% under 0.5 ha, with 19% of paddy and built-up ground within 20 m of one. It also says the woods stand mainly on the north-west side of a farmhouse, with low shrubs on the south so the sun reaches the house and garden, and that Sendai Domain required permits to cut trees and replanting from the early 1600s. The measurements are of the present day, in a landscape with solar panels and mechanized farming; the paper traces the 17th-century rules and an 1858 map only in a sentence and measures nothing before 2002, and it covers one plain in northern Honshu.
