Zhang Huaiyu, "大官道上的车马大店" ("The big cart-and-horse inn on the main official road", title translated), posted on Sohu by a Yuanping local-history account (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/773207664_121124387)

What it is: A retired teacher's memoir of his family's cart-and-horse inn in Daniudian village in Yuanping, Shanxi, in the 1950s. It describes the outer courtyard used for the inn and the inner courtyard where the family lived, with the numbers of carts, animals and guests it held.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the layout and capacity of one inn on a main road: an outer yard of about one mu with eight bays of rooms on the west side (two kitchens, three bays of kang bedrooms and three single guest rooms), three front rooms for the managers, and a stable of five large bays, holding nine carts and 27 draft animals at the most, with camels bedded down in the family yard when the guests were many. It is a single family's memory of the 1950s, about a village inn near a railway and a coal and pear trade, so it shows the form of an old road inn but is not evidence for the premodern period or for any other region.
