Tarama Village Agricultural Heritage Promotion Council (多良間村農業遺産推進協議会), "琉球王国時代の「抱護（ポーグ）」が育む多良間島の持続的島嶼農業システム" ("Ryukyu odai no 'poge' ga hagukumu Taramajima no jizokuteki tosho nogyo shisutemu", the Tarama island's sustainable island farming system nurtured by the 'poge' windbreak forests of the Ryukyu Kingdom era, title translated), application document for Japan Important Agricultural Heritage Systems (GIAHS), Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (in Japanese; https://www.maff.go.jp/j/nousin/kantai/attach/pdf/giahs_7_tarama-1.pdf)

What it is: The 133-page application document of Tarama Island, Okinawa (a flat limestone island of 19.75 square km between Miyako and Ishigaki), for Japan Important Agricultural Heritage status, recognized 24 January 2025, hosted by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries. It describes the island's poge (windbreak forest) system said to be laid out in 1742 on the order of Sai On, its sugarcane and cattle farming, wells, cave springs and reservoirs, shrine-grove (utaki) festivals, village organization and tree and plant lore.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a premodern island village's layout from a case where it survives: the village ringed on its west, south and east by a village poge about 1.8 km long (16 m wide when made, 12 m now), a shore poge around the island, a wooded hill behind the village, fukugi-ringed house plots, hedged fields and utaki groves, with the 1741-1742 survey and planting cited to the Shirakawa genealogy. It is a modern application written to win heritage status, so it stresses the poge's uniqueness; it describes a windswept coral-limestone island of the Ryukyu Kingdom, not a Japanese mainland or inland rice village, and most of its farm figures (sugarcane from the 1890s, cattle, 2020s yields and areas) are modern.
