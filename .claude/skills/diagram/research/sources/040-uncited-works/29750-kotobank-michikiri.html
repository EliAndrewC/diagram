kotobank-michikiri

Tarumi Minoru (the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry), "道切り" ("michikiri", cutting the road, title translated), with the Digital Daijisen and Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/道切り)

What it is: A Kotobank page on michikiri, the village custom of symbolically cutting the roads at the village boundary to keep out disease and impurity, with a signed Heibonsha encyclopedia entry and two dictionary definitions.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the custom is done at the village entrance, most often by stretching a sacred rope (shimenawa) across the road, sometimes hung with a single straw sandal or a fertility charm, and either on a regular schedule or after a calamity. The older Heibonsha excerpt adds that village boundaries are rarely felt as the administrative boundary and that the ritual boundary falls between settlement and fields. It names no region or period, and the earliest quoted use is 1661.
