huiguan-gongsuo-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 會館 ("Huiguan, the guildhall", title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/會館)

What it is: A short Chinese Wikipedia article on the huiguan (also called gongsuo), the Ming and Qing guildhall of merchants and sojourners from one native place, covering its origin, its early purposes and the later split into native-place and merchant-trade guildhalls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the earliest known huiguan to the Yongle era of the Ming (the Wuhu guildhall in Beijing) and says these halls began as places for fellow townsmen to meet, hold entertainments and lodge, and later took on trade-regulating functions; a quoted Chaozhou gazetteer lists four reasons for founding one, including the worship of a shared god. It is two paragraphs resting on one 1989 study of Suzhou and one gazetteer quotation, and says nothing of a hall's buildings, size or siting.
