Liu Jin, "读书 | 兰溪鱼鳞图册——堪称中国现存最系统的古代地籍档案" ("Reading: the Lanxi fish-scale registers, the most systematic surviving ancient cadastral archive in China", title translated), Wenhui (文汇网), 2022-11-17 (in Chinese; https://wenhui.whb.cn/third/zaker/202211/17/495940.html)

What it is: A newspaper book notice on the publication of the Lanxi fish-scale register collection, the land registers of Lanxi county, Zhejiang, compiled in the Qing Tongzhi years after the Taiping war: what fish-scale registers were, what each plot entry records, what survives in Lanxi and what the edition holds.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says each plot entry records the plot's drawn shape, name, four boundaries, land class, area in mu, grade and the owner and his residence, and that the Lanxi set covers the county's walled city (10 wards) and countryside (35 du, 149 tu), including temples, lineage halls and guild halls as landholders. It is a news notice on a scholarly edition, not the registers themselves, quotes a historian's praise without testing it, and gives two counts for the full set (820 and 889 volumes), so its volume counts are not to be relied on; the registers were compiled in the 1860s-70s, within China's premodern period, but the page says they carry land changes recorded up to 1949, with later supplements, so an entry may show a later state than the survey, and it says nothing of Lanxi's Song and Ming registers except that they were made.
