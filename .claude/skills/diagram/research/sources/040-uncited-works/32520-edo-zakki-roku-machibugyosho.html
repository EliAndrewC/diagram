edo-zakki-roku-machibugyosho

Ryujiro (琉次郎), "江戸雑記録" ("Edo zakkiroku", Edo miscellany, title translated), blog, category "町奉行所の組織と人事" (the Edo town magistrates' offices: organization and personnel), first of three pages, on JUGEM (in Japanese; http://edo.ashigaru.net/?cid=7)

What it is: A personal history blog on Edo officials, whose first page of the category on the town magistrates' offices carries five posts (2021-2024) on the staff hierarchy of the Edo town magistracy, the short-lived third (middle) magistrate of 1702-1719, promotion for senior yoriki and doshin, the duty rota and days off, and the standing at-home duty of the teichuyaku.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives numbers for how a magistrate's office was staffed and housed in Edo: 25 yoriki and 100 doshin to each of the two offices after 1719, about 120 doshin each after the Enkyo era, the five duty companies of 4-6 yoriki and about 24 doshin each, and Hacchobori lots averaging 342 tsubo (south) and 275 tsubo (north) for a yoriki and 94 and 79 tsubo for a doshin. It is about the shogun's capital in the Edo period, roughly 1700-1860, so it fits a large city's magistracy and not a provincial county seat, and its figures are the blogger's reading of historians and archives, with some marked as the author's guesses ("it seems", "probably").
