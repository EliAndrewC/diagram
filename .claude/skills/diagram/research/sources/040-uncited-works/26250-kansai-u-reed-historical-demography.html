Kansai University, "庶民の一生を浮き彫りにする“歴史人口学”" ("Shomin no issho wo ukibori ni suru 'rekishi jinko-gaku'", Historical demography, which brings out the lives of commoners, title translated), interview with Prof. Hamano Kiyoshi in the university newsletter Reed (in Japanese; https://www.kansai-u.ac.jp/reed_rfl/archive/08_1.php)

What it is: A university-newsletter interview with a historical demographer on how Edo-period religious inspection registers (shumon aratamecho) are used to reconstruct population, marriage and household life.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the registers were required yearly nationwide from 1671 and list each house's names, ages and relations, sometimes holdings and moves; that Tohoku villages married at about 15 for girls while western Japan married in the twenties; and that Kyoto's Nishijin Hanaguruma-cho had 36 of 50 years of registers, an average household of three to four, and falls of 32 percent in 1836-37 and 31 percent in 1858-61. These are a few findings from a few villages and one town quarter, told as an interview, and the page gives no source tables.
