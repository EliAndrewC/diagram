tonami-kainyo-status

Tonami City Board of Education, "屋敷林" ("yashikirin", house grove, title translated), from the "Sumitsugu" (living on, generation to generation) pages on the dispersed villages of Tonami, on the Tonami Shosou site (in Japanese; https://1073shoso.jp/www/sankyo/detail.jsp?id=7216)

What it is: A short city education board page on the kainyo, the grove that wraps a farmhouse in the dispersed villages of the Tonami plain, Toyama. It gives the grove's likely origin, a local saying about it, and its loss in the Second World War.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the grove as a mark of standing, citing the old Tonami saying "sell the land but never the kainyo" and the page's hearsay ("it is said") that the size of the grove once showed a family's rank, and it gives the grove's purpose as shelter from wind, rain, snow, cold and summer sun. The page gives no date for the saying or the custom, calls the origin in remnant primeval forest only a belief ("considered to be"), and its only dated event is the wartime requisition of cedar and zelkova.
