Ehime Prefectural Lifelong Learning Center, "えひめ、その食とくらし（平成15年度）第１章 第１節 ２（２）江戸時代の食生活" ("Ehime, its food and life (2003), chapter 1, section 1, part 2: eating in the Edo period", title translated), in the database Memories of Ehime (えひめの記憶) (in Japanese; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:1/13/view/2143)

What it is: A chapter of a prefectural survey report on what villagers, townspeople and samurai of Iyo (Ehime) ate in the Edo period, drawn from the farm book Seiryoki, domain decrees, household papers and travelers' diaries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Iyo's rules and cases: the Seiryoki's roughly 110 crops and 80 wild foods, domain orders that farmers eat millet and barley rather than rice, a headman's household eating rice and barley 7 to 3 while hired hands got barley and sweet potato, and a 70-koku Uwajima samurai household eating 2.5 go of rice a day yet running a deficit. It speaks for one Shikoku province, quotes older sources at second hand, and gives no field sizes or house plans.
