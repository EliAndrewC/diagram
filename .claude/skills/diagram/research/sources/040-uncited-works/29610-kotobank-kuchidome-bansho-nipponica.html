Maruyama Yosei, "口留番所" ("kuchidome bansho", the domain border checkpoint), in the Shogakukan Nipponica (日本大百科全書), with the Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten (山川 日本史小辞典), the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (精選版 日本国語大辞典) and the older Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entries for related words, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/口留番所-250699)

What it is: Dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the kuchidome bansho, a checkpoint that Edo-period domains set at their borders and on land and water routes to inspect travelers and goods, with its shift from military guard to the control of trade and the collection of duties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that domains kept their own border checkpoints that were not the shogunate's official barriers, that they inspected people and goods and could levy fees, and that they were common in outer-lord domains of the northeast, Hokuriku and Kyushu. It gives no building plan, size, staffing or place-by-place detail, and the entries explain the institution rather than any one site.
