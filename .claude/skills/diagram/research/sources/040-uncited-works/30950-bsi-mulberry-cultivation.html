bsi-mulberry-cultivation

BSI Biological Science Institute (BSI 生物科学研究所), "桑" ("Mulberry"), in "実用作物栽培学" ("Practical crop cultivation", title translated) (in Japanese; https://bsikagaku.jp/cultivation/mulberry.pdf)

What it is: A 14-page cultivation guide to mulberry in modern Japan, written mainly for fruit varieties but covering silkworm mulberry too: propagation, planting, pruning styles, manuring, pests and harvest.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its table gives row and plant spacing by training style for silkworm mulberry, for example rows 150-250 cm and plants 60-90 cm apart for low-cut trees and rows 300-400 cm and plants 200-300 cm apart for high-cut trees, and it says orchards are renewed every 15-20 years. It also gives mulberry acreage of 700,000 ha in 1930 against 2,011 ha in 2008. Its methods use modern fertilizer, plastic mulch and rooting hormone, and its main subject is fruit mulberry, and it prefers the low-cut form partly because its short trunk suits orchard machinery and lays rows out partly for machine work (high-cut trees it calls a practice of a few snowy cold districts), so its tree forms carry over to a premodern grove but its spacings are a modern bound, not a period measurement.
