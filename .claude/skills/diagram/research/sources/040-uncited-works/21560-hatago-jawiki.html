hatago-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 旅籠 ("hatago", the travelers' inn, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%97%85%E7%B1%A0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the hatago, the inn serving lodging and meals, chiefly in the post-station towns of the Edo-period Five Highways, with its classes, its meals, its guilds and the old inns that survive.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the post-town inn as a graded set: honjin and waki-honjin at the top, then hatago sorted by frontage into large, middle and small, with plain inns apart from those keeping serving-women. It says the Tokaido's 53 stations held about 3,000 hatago and that a night cost about 200 to 300 mon, and it gives sample dinners from an 1813 diary. It covers only highway stations, not village or market-town lodging, and the yen equivalents it gives are modern conversions.
