yakuimon-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 薬医門 ("yakuimon", the yakui gate, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%96%AC%E5%8C%BB%E9%96%80)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the yakuimon, a gate type with two main posts and two rear brace posts under a gabled roof, with its history, structure, rank among gate types and a list of surviving examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the form to the late Kamakura or early Muromachi period, says it first served warrior and court-noble residences and later castles and temples, and ranks it below the munamon, karamon and ueomon and above the hiramon and kabukimon. It gives no dimensions, and its list of surviving examples is of designated cultural properties, mostly temples and larger houses, so it does not show how common the gate was at a village headman's or a lesser samurai's house.
