iwakuni-kintaikyo-pier

Iwakuni City, "橋脚の構造" ("The structure of the piers", title translated), in the technology section of the official Kintaikyo site (in Japanese; https://kintaikyo.iwakuni-city.net/tech/tech3.html)

What it is: A city page on the piers of the Kintai Bridge in Iwakuni, describing how the Edo-period dry-stone piers were built on pine piles and log cribs and how the piers were rebuilt in concrete after the 1950 flood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the old piers were sunk 2 to 2.7 meters below the riverbed on pine piles with a log grid, then faced with stacked stone over a fill of river stones and earth, with the beam ends buried in earth so they rotted and needed replacing about every twenty years. It describes one bridge, a showpiece of its domain, and the post-1950 concrete piers it also describes are not premodern.
