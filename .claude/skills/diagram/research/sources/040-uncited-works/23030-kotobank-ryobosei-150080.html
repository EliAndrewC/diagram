kotobank-ryobosei-150080

"両墓制" ("ryobosei", the double-grave system, title translated), entries in the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nipponica (by Kojima Yoshi), the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Tanaka Hisao), Yamakawa's Japanese History Dictionary and the Britannica Japan dictionary, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%A1%E5%A2%93%E5%88%B6-150080)

What it is: A set of dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the double-grave system, in which a body is buried in one place (the burial grave) and the dead are honored at a stone-marked grave elsewhere (the visiting grave). They cover its distribution, the usual positions of the two graves, and several theories of its origin.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the form for a village map: the burial ground lay away from houses, on a riverbank, in hills or by the shore, often common land, while the visiting grave stood in a temple or hall precinct, and the entries say the system is found chiefly in the Kinki and Kanto areas and rarely in Kyushu or on Shinshu temple lands. The entries disagree on when it began (early Edo with the spread of gravestones, or Kamakura), and they say it was breaking down once stones were set at burial grounds, so none gives a date or a measured size for a burial ground.
