kominkai-nouka-madori

Yuruko (ゆるこ), "古民家が多い農家住宅の広間型と田の字型の間取り" ("The hiroma and da-no-ji floor plans of the farmhouses that make up most old houses", title translated), on the blog Kominka Tanbo (古民家探訪) (in Japanese; https://kominkai.net/nouka-madori/)

What it is: A blog article on the two main floor plans of thatched farmhouses in Japan, the hall type (hiroma-gata) and the four-room "rice-field character" type (da-no-ji), with a table of the room names and uses (niwa, omote, dei, daidoko, nando). It also notes the sacred and everyday (hare and ke) split of the rooms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the hall type with a wide room beside the earth-floored niwa is seen from the late Muromachi period and the four-room type grew out of it, naming what each room was for and that cold regions kept horses in the niwa. It gives no survey, dates or counts of its own, lists a handful of general books as its basis, and says room names and uses vary by house, region, era and class, so it describes the typical plan and does not fit one place or time.
