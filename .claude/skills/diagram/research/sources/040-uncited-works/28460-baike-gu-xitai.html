Baidu Baike, "古戏台" ("ancient opera stages", title translated), online encyclopedia article (in Chinese; https://baike.baidu.com/item/古戏台/8796741)

What it is: A Baidu Baike encyclopedia article on China's old opera stages: their development from the Song to the Qing, their structure and acoustics, regional survivals in Shanxi, Jiangxi, Zhejiang and Guangxi, and their present protection.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the earliest surviving stage to 1183 (the Jin stage at Erlang temple, Gaoping, Shanxi), says Yuan stages stood in temples facing the main hall on a square base 1 to 2 m high, and that Ming stages grew a raised-rear plan and sometimes sat over a temple's gate, so it shows where and in what form a temple stage stood in each dynasty. It is a compiled article whose many figures (3,719 stages in Shanxi, 458 in Leping) are modern counts, whose page text has stray garbled characters, and whose early-period claims lean on numbered references the page lists only in part.
