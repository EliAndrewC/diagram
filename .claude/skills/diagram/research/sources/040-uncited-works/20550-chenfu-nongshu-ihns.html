chenfu-nongshu-ihns

"陈旉农书" ("Chen Fu nongshu", Chen Fu's Treatise on Agriculture, title translated), in the treatise records section of the Agricultural History of China site (in Chinese; http://agri-history.ihns.ac.cn/books/treatise%20records/cfns.htm)

What it is: A modern introduction to the Southern Song farming treatise written by Chen Fu in 1149, on a Chinese agricultural-history site. It walks through the book's twelve "fitnesses" (scale, terrain, tilling, season, crops, dwelling, manure and others), its chapters on oxen and on mulberry and silkworms, and the text's printing history.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains what a premodern southern Chinese rice-region farming manual held and how it was organized, including the sixth chapter's rule that a farmstead should stand near its fields so the farmer can tend them, and the book's advice that a farm's scale should match its labor and capital. The page is a modern summary, not the treatise's text, and it gives that chapter only a one-line description; it treats the Lower Yangzi rice region of the twelfth century and says the dry-crop chapter is brief.
