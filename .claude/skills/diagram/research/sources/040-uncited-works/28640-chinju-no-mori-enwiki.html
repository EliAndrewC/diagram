en.wikipedia "Chinju no mori" (鎮守の森, the tutelary shrine's wood), on English Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinju_no_mori)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on chinju no mori, the forests kept in or around Shinto shrines in Japan, covering the meaning of the term and of mori, planted shrine forests such as Meiji Shrine, the vegetation these groves keep, and their decline after the Meiji shrine-merging policy.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that many Japanese villages had a shrine with a forest before the Meiji era, that the groves are thought to keep the area's original vegetation (with camphor, keyaki and similar trees noted as later introductions or out of place in some districts), and that the Jinja gōshi merging policy cut many shrines and their groves down. It gives no grove size, layout or species counts for a village, and much of it concerns modern ecology, conservation and the twentieth-century loss of groves. It also says gaps were often replanted with cedar or cypress and non-wild plants set in precincts. Its English reads as a rough translation (shrine groves called "township forests", sentences repeated), and it cites mostly web pages and a newspaper advertisement.
