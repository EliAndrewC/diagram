Yamamoto Masakazu (Kyoto City Archaeological Research Institute), "江戸時代の京都の武家屋敷" (Samurai residences of Edo-period Kyoto, title translated), handout for the 310th Kyoto City Archaeological Museum lecture, 7 December 2019 (in Japanese; https://kyoto-arc.or.jp/news/s-kouza/kouza310.pdf)

What it is: The text of a lecture handout outlining what excavation found of samurai residences in Kyoto, from the Jurakudai and Fushimi castle districts through Edo-period lord and shogunate sites to the late Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists site by site what was found inside a residence compound: moat and stone wall, earth wall or fence enclosures, paths from the street, wells and refuse pits fewer than the area would suggest, tiled and post-set buildings, and fine ceramics. It also notes lots mixed among townsmen's houses and a simplified enclosure in the later period. The saved text is an outline with its maps and tables lost, so it gives no sizes, counts or plans, and Kyoto lodgings of out-of-town lords are not a provincial domain's seat.
