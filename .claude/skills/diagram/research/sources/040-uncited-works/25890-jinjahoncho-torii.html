jinjahoncho-torii

Association of Shinto Shrines (神社本庁), "鳥居について" ("torii ni tsuite", about torii, title translated), in "Omairi suru" (visiting a shrine), official site (in Japanese; https://www.jinjahoncho.or.jp/omairi/torii/)

What it is: A short explainer on the official site of the national association of Shinto shrines on what a torii is, its disputed origin and its main forms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a torii stands at the boundary between a shrine's inner and outer worlds and that the ground within is the god's sacred precinct, and that shrines with no main hall but a mountain or other natural object as the god's seat put one in front of it. It lists the plain-lintel shinmei type, the upturned-lintel myojin type, the gabled sanno type and the vermilion Inari type, and says one view counts over sixty forms. It gives no sizes, dates or counts per shrine, and its two origin accounts (the rooster perch of the myth, or foreign import) are stated without evidence.
