nikkenkyo-roots-furo-kitchen

"ルーツを探れ！" ("Seek the roots!", title translated), a page on baths, toilets and kitchens, on the nikkenkyo.jp site (in Japanese; http://nikkenkyo.jp/before/4joho/roots/roots.htm)

What it is: A web page tracing the history of the Japanese bath, toilet and kitchen from the Jomon period to the postwar housing corporation, drawn from three books of the series 物語 ものの建築史 (on the bath, the toilet and the kitchen), published by Kajima.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a samurai kitchen ranged from a living room and kitchen only for the lowest ranks to many rooms for the highest, that the farmhouse hiroma plan centered on a hearth with the kitchen beside it, that snowy regions kept the kitchen within the house while southern islands often put it in a separate building, that night-soil toilets appear after the Kamakura period for fertilizer, and that Edo tenement toilets were shared. It is a general outline with no sizes, no region for most claims, and no citation for any single statement.
