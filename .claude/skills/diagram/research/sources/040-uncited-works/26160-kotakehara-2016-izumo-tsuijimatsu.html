kotakehara-2016-izumo-tsuijimatsu

Kotakehara Noriko, "出雲平野の形成と屋敷林（築地松）の形成" ("Izumo heiya no keisei to yashikirin (tsuijimatsu) no keisei", The formation of the Izumo plain and of its homestead woods, title translated), abstract for the Association of Japanese Geographers spring meeting, 2016 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ajg/2016s/0/2016s_100342/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A conference abstract, in full on J-STAGE, on how the Izumo plain in Shimane formed and how its tsuijimatsu, the black-pine homestead windbreaks, came about.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives four purposes for the pine screen (winter wind off the Japan Sea, holding the ground in a flood, fuel and compost, and showing a house's standing by the size of its compound and the height of its screen), three types by origin, and the count of houses with one falling from 4,117 in fiscal 1994 to 1,516 in 2012. It is a short summary whose origin story (a mythic figure bringing iron and tree planting) is a local tradition, not a documented history, and it gives no sizes for the screens.
