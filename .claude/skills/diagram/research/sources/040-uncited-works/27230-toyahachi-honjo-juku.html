toyahachi-honjo-juku

Toyahachi Shoten, "中山道「本庄宿」について" ("About the Nakasendo post station Honjo-juku", title translated), on the shop's site (in Japanese; https://www.toyahachi.com/%E4%B8%AD%E5%B1%B1%E9%81%93%E6%9C%80%E5%A4%A7%E3%81%AE%E6%9C%AC%E5%BA%84%E5%AE%BF/)

What it is: A ceramics shop's page on Honjo post station in Musashi, tenth station from Edo on the Nakasendo, with its 1843 census figures, its relay system and its merchant Toya Hanbei.

Why it applies, and its limits: It cites the 1843 Nakasendo shukuson taigaicho for Honjo at 4,554 people, 1,212 houses, 70 inns, two honjin and two secondary honjin along a street of about 1.92 km with six relay offices, and says each Nakasendo station had to keep 50 men and 50 horses. These are the largest on the road, so they are an upper bound for a station, and the figures are the shop's quotation of the register and not the register itself.
