kotobank-toi

"樋" ("hi / toi, the water channel, flume and gutter", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/樋)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for the character hi or toi, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, Nipponica, Britannica and others on two uses: a conduit or sluice door that carries water, and a roof gutter. The Heibonsha entry includes a section by Kitamura Toshio on pond sluices (the uchi-hi and the shakuhachi-hi) with examples such as Mannoike and Sayama-ike.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the pond sluice in detail: buried and shakuhachi types, a vertical pipe with plugged holes about 10 cm across, and the premodern advice (Jikata no Kikigaki, Sato Nobuhiro) that most pond failures began at the sluice, so it needs firm ground; it also dates the old Mannoike and Sayama-ike sluices (1608 and 1631 to 1854). Its examples are large irrigation reservoirs, not a village's small pond, and the roof-gutter material (copper, zinc-plated iron, vinyl) is modern, with only a note that wood and bamboo were used before.
