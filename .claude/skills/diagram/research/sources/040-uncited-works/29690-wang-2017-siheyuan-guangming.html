Wang Bin, "北京四合院的建筑布局与居住文化" ("the building layout and living culture of Beijing's siheyuan courtyard houses", title translated), Guangming Daily (光明日报), 10 March 2017, on Guangming Online (in Chinese; https://news.gmw.cn/2017-03/10/content_23932758.htm)

What it is: A newspaper essay by a researcher at the Lu Xun Literary Institute on the Beijing siheyuan: its history from Bronze Age courtyard remains, the parts and proportions of the plan, the design idea it draws from the well-field system, and how each room was used by the family.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the plan a Chinese-model compound follows: a main hall of three bays on the north, side wings, a south-facing range, a gate at the south-east corner, a courtyard of about 1:1 proportions in Beijing against narrower ones in Shanxi and Shaanxi, three-court houses as the typical form, and no more than five courts deep or three ranges wide. It also gives room use by rank and age. It is an essay without notes for its measurements, it describes Beijing houses of the Ming and Qing as they stood into modern times, and it speaks of no other region of China except the narrower northwest courtyards.
