Name-writer column (名家专栏), "中国古代县衙的分布与级别" ("The distribution and grades of ancient Chinese county yamens", title translated), from Wenshi Tiandi issue 274, posted on Sohu (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/438471887_612398)

What it is: A magazine column, written by a law professor and reposted on Sohu, that opens a series on Ming and Qing county yamens. It covers the origin of the county, the ideal of a county a hundred li square, the grading of counties, and county wall sizes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a county was ideally a hundred li square so that a yamen in the middle could be reached in a day, and that walls were allowed only at the seat, with places below the county barred from walling except by special leave. It reports a study that a Ming-Qing county wall ran about four to eight li round, and gives the Qing grading of counties by four marks (on a main road, busy, in arrears, hard to govern). It is a general column for readers, with some figures, such as the Qing shares of each grade, given without a source, so it supports the outline of county administration and not any one county's layout.
