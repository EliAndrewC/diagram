aric-farmland-forest

ARIC (一般社団法人農業農村整備情報総合センター), "農地に結びついた林野" ("Woods and fields tied to farmland", title translated), in "土地を拓く" (Clearing the land), "国土づくりの歴史", in Suido no Ishizue (水土の礎) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/01/10.html)

What it is: A page on the hills and woods that Japanese rice villages used for green manure, hay, fuel and charcoal, and on the commons rules and disputes that grew around them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the main traditional fertilizer was fresh grass and brush cut from the hills and plowed in before transplanting, so the hills needed to be "considerably wider" than the paddy, and that use rose sharply from about 1700. It lists village cutting rules: the hill opened on days fixed by the village officials' notice, first in early May, again in late May for pre-planting green manure, in late June, and for stable hay until early September. It gives no ratio of hill to paddy, and its cutting calendar names no village or region and does not say whether its months are of the old lunar calendar. It describes the Edo-period system, saying only that the rules eased after the Meiji spread of purchased fertilizer.
