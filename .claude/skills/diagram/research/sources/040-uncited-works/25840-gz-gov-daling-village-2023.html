Guangzhou Daily, "900多年大岭村是大湾区内历史文化要素最齐备的国家级历史文化名村之一" ("the 900-year-old village of Daling is one of the most complete national historic villages in the Greater Bay Area", title translated), 8 February 2023, reproduced on the Guangzhou municipal government portal (in Chinese; https://www.gz.gov.cn/zlgz/whgz/content/post_8791801.html)

What it is: A newspaper feature, reposted on a city government site, on Daling village in Panyu, Guangzhou: its founding in 1119, its fishbone lanes running at right angles off a main street along a tidal creek, the water-mouth grove of bridge, pagoda and pond, the clan halls and the oyster-shell walls. It draws on a village elder and on scholars of Cantonese settlements.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a surviving Pearl River delta village in detail: a main stone street 3 to 5 m wide with lanes mostly 1.5 to 3 m and some under 1 m wide, 42 lane gates, a drainage channel along one side of each lane, and a tide-gated bridge keeping the creek's water level. Most of what it describes was built in the Ming and Qing and is seen as restored and lived in today, and the founding date and clan origins are village tradition and a local elder's account, not documents quoted on the page. It is also a showcase village, which the article calls the most complete national historic village in the Greater Bay Area, whose Chen lineage produced 5 jinshi and 14 juren in the Ming and Qing, so its granite main street and its many halls are those of a prosperous gentry village rather than an ordinary one.
