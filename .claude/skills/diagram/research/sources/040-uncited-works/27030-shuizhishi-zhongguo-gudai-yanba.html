shuizhishi-zhongguo-gudai-yanba

Knowledge Service Assistant (知识服务助手) of the China Water & Power Press, "中国古代堰坝" ("Ancient Chinese weirs and dams", title translated), a water-conservancy encyclopedia entry (in Chinese; https://www.shuizhishi.cn/c/2020-04-09/524099.shtml)

What it is: An entry in a Chinese water-conservancy encyclopedia on the weirs and dams of ancient China, tracing them from early flood banks to Han, Tang and later works. It sorts them by material (earth, wood, grass-and-earth, lime, stone-filled wooden cage) and by method of construction.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the main types of old weirs and dams and gives examples of each, and says earth dams were checked with an iron-awl probe more than a thousand years ago and, under the Ming, by filling a dug hole with water to see that it did not leak. It says grass-and-earth dams were temporary, built cheaply to give way in a flood. It spans some 5,000 years in a few pages, names no dimensions for ordinary village weirs, and its examples are large state works, so it speaks for the kinds of structure, not for what a farm village built.
