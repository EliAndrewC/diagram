ja.wikipedia 集村 ("shuson", the clustered or nucleated village, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%A1%97%E6%9D%91)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the clustered village (shuson), the opposite of the dispersed village, with its causes and its forms: the irregular cluster, the linear village (road and street villages), the forest-strip village, the green village, and the marsh village.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the irregular cluster (kaison) is the most common natural form, that it is found nationwide in Japan and especially in Kinki and Hokuriku, and that road villages are common among Edo-period new-field settlements such as Mitomi Shinden. It also dates a shift toward clustering in Japan to the Nanbokucho and Muromachi periods and says there is no rule for how dense a village must be to count as clustered; most of its named forms are German or eastern European and its figures (a 10-20 ha holding) are for those.
