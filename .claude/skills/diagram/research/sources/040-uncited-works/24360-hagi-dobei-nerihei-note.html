hagi-dobei-nerihei-note

Kozo no Rojikku (構造のロジック), "萩の土塀・練塀（子リヘイ）と渡り塀、板塀についての考察" ("A study of Hagi's earthen walls, nerihei, watari walls and board fences", title translated), on note.com, 10 February 2025 (in Japanese; https://note.com/hara3281/n/nc15a3f11f293)

What it is: A blog essay summarizing the 2004 review survey of the Horiuchi and Heiankoji preservation district of Hagi, on how the walls of the domain's samurai houses were built and which survive from the domain era.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports that the survey judged only four earthen walls to date from the domain period, that most are Meiji or later, that Hagi's nerihei was a wall without posts and without roof tile layers, and that the street edge of many house lots was taken by long houses, with the wall only where the stone base rose higher. It is one writer's secondhand summary of one survey of one castle town, and the walls it names are almost all later rebuilds, so it speaks for the form and for Hagi, not for how common each wall was.
