en.wikipedia "Roughness length" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roughness_length)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the roughness length used in wind profile equations, with its definition, its use and a table of values for terrain types from open sea to city centers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the rule that the roughness length is about one-tenth of the height of the roughness elements and a table running from 0.03 m for open grass and 0.10 m for low crops to 0.5 m for parkland and 1.0 m for suburb or forest, which can set how rough a wide stretch of open field, crops, woodland or built-up ground is to the wind; it describes a uniform surface over a long fetch, not the sheltered strip just behind a single tree belt or wall. The values are the WMO observing guide's approximate classes and the one-tenth rule comes from a 1971 wind-tunnel study, the table gives none for paddy, bamboo or a village, and the article's section on related measures has no citations.
