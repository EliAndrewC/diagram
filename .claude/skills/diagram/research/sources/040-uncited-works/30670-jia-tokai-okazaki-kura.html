Yamamoto Hisahito (Sankyo Construction, head of the Dozo Preservation Research Office), "蔵飾り。妻壁に彩色を施したコテ絵 泥遊び精神② 蔵探訪 岡崎の蔵の特性" ("Storehouse decoration, trowel pictures painted on gable walls; the mud-play spirit part 2; visiting storehouses: the character of Okazaki's storehouses", title translated), in the archive of the Tokai branch of the Japan Institute of Architects, 2003 (in Japanese; https://jia-tokai.org/archive/sibu/architect/2003/0308/doro.htm)

What it is: A column by a builder who restores earthen storehouses (kura), on the storehouses of Okazaki and the Mikawa hills: the usual plan, where tile roofs sit directly on the clay or are set on a separate frame, what was stored, how few households owned one, and the decoration of the walls, gables and doors.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the common form (gable-end entrance, 2 by 3 ken, about 12 mats, under 4 meters high) and says that in Okazaki most storehouses held grain below and ceremonial goods and clothes above, that only one or two percent of a settlement owned one, and that one hill hamlet of about 40 houses had more than ten. The one or two percent is the author's own memory, the figures are not from a survey, and the survey area is Okazaki and the hills to its north, so the page gives no date for any storehouse and the ownership share is a twentieth-century recollection.
