Touken World (刀剣ワールド), "刀剣だけじゃない！鍛冶屋の世界" ("Not only swords: the world of the blacksmith", title translated), in the sword-column section (日本刀コラム), on Touken World, the sword site of the Nagoya Token Museum (in Japanese; https://touken-world.jp/tips/71884)

What it is: A popular history of Japanese blacksmiths from the Edo period to the Taisho period: the shift from weapon smiths to general smiths, the specialized tool smith, the 1876 sword ban, the move from traditional to imported steel, and portraits of famous smiths.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in the Edo period the farm or field smith (nokaji) made hoes, spades, sickles, fishing gear, forest blades, kitchen knives and carpenter's tools, and that smiths specialized further in the late Edo and Meiji periods. Most of the page is about the Meiji and Taisho craft, with one smith's plane priced at 5 yen against 80 sen to 1.50 yen for an ordinary one in the mid Taisho, and its legend of a banned smith is marked by the page itself as perhaps not fact; it gives no number of smiths per village or the shop's size.
