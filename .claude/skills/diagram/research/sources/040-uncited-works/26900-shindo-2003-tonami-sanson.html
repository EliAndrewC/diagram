Shindo Masao (secretary general, Tonami Dispersed Settlement Research Institute), "砺波平野の散村の変容と景観保全" ("The transformation of the dispersed villages of the Tonami Plain and the preservation of their landscape", title translated), in the Toyama Prefecture feature of February 2003 (in Japanese; https://www.pref.toyama.jp/sections/1015/ecm/back/2003feb/tokushu/index1.html)

What it is: A prefectural web feature by a researcher on the dispersed farmsteads of the Tonami Plain in Toyama, each inside its own house grove among the paddies. It covers how they formed, their roads, the field consolidation from 1962, factories moving in, and the loss of the groves.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the farmsteads stand 50 to 150 m apart, formed mostly from the end of the medieval period to the early Edo period on a river fan, and that the house grove was mainly cedar, a windbreak and a source of fuel and timber. Its road widths (house lanes about 1 m, village roads about 1.8 m) are for about 1945, and the page says nothing of how a house grove was laid out or sized.
