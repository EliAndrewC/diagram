tarama-fukugi-fengshui

Tarama Village, "フクギと風水に守られた集落" (The village protected by fukugi trees and fengshui, title translated), in "Tarama no miryoku" (The charms of Tarama), Tarama Village official website, updated 18 February 2021 (in Japanese; https://www.vill.tarama.okinawa.jp/kankou/taramano_miryoku/syuraku/)

What it is: A village-office tourism page on the single settlement of Tarama Island in Okinawa, describing the belt of fukugi (Garcinia) trees around it, its grid of roads and its Ryukyuan fengshui layout.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the island's scattered settlements were merged into one at the present site by a sixteenth-century local chieftain, that the village is ringed by a protective wood and laid out on a north-south, east-west grid, and that fukugi are planted around the houses as tide and wind breaks; it says the village is said to have been formed on the Ryukyuan fengshui established by the statesman Sai On (not laid out by him), and that most of the island's woods are planted rather than natural. These are stated as tradition ("is said to") and give no dimensions, tree spacing or dates beyond the century, and the island is a flat, typhoon-struck Ryukyuan village, not a Japanese or Chinese mainland one.
