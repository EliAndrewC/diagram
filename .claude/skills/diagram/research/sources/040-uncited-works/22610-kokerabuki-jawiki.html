kokerabuki-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 杮葺 ("kokerabuki", roofing with thin wood shingles, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/杮葺)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on kokerabuki, roofing in thin split-wood shingles, and its thicker kin tokusabuki and tochibuki. It covers the three grades by board thickness, their woods and how the boards are split, how a roof is laid, service life, fire law, and a list of buildings roofed this way.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the thickness of each grade (2-3 mm, 4-7 mm and 1-3 cm), the woods used (hinoki, sawara, sugi, enoki, and in places chestnut or maki), courses laid about 3 cm apart (the exposure), bamboo pegs, and a service life it states variously as 20-25, about 25, 30-50 and, for split boards, about 40 years. It says the roof was used chiefly on shoin, guest halls and high-ranking samurai houses, and its examples are temples, palaces and the Takayama magistrate's office, so it does not show how common shingles were on ordinary houses. The page carries a notice of missing sources and marks several statements as needing a citation.
