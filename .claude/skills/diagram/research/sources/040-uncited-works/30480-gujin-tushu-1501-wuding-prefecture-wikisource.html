Gujin tushu jicheng (欽定古今圖書集成), Fangyu huibian, Zhifang dian, juan 1501, "武定府部彙考一" (Wuding prefecture, survey part 1, title translated), Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/方輿彙編/職方典/第1501卷)

What it is: Scroll 1501 of the Qing imperial encyclopedia, on Wikisource: the first scroll on Wuding prefecture in Yunnan, covering its history, boundaries, mountains and rivers with irrigation works, city walls, bridges and passes, offices and schools, for the prefecture seat and its two departments and one county.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the wall of a seat of a newly regular prefecture (a stone wall 2 zhang high with no moat, built 1570-71 after native rule ended, which the provincial gazetteer gives as 6 li round with four gates and the prefectural gazetteer as 790 zhang 8 chi, 3 li 3 fen round, with four gate towers but three gates opened), a brick county wall of 1 li 3 fen and an earth department wall of 209 zhang 3 chi with three gates and no moat, plus the layout of a yamen (hall, offices, jail, shrine to the earth god outside the inner gate) and the weirs and ditches that watered fields. It is a Yunnan frontier district under native chieftains until 1569 and written up to the early 1700s, so its numbers belong to that southwestern, partly non-Han setting and not to a Han county of the interior. Its punctuation was added by a program and corrected by volunteers, and it breaks some figures wrongly (the Yuanmou gate-tower heights of 1 zhang 5 chi and 1 zhang 6 chi run across a misplaced break), so a figure must be read from the sense of the unpunctuated text.
