kotobank-watanabe-mura

"渡辺村" ("Watanabe-mura", Watanabe village, title translated), in the Historical Place Names of Japan series (日本歴史地名大系), Heibonsha, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B8%A1%E8%BE%BA%E6%9D%91-3076303)

What it is: A short, partial extract of a place-name entry on Watanabe village, a settlement within Kizu village in Osaka from 1701. It says that most of its land was exempt from the land levy, that its residents were counted as members of the Osaka Sango, and that its people, living in five places in the Tensho years (1573-92), probably had (by the compilers' inference) an earlier house site on Shimonanba village land that was taken in 1619 for the Nagahori canal when the city expanded.

Why it applies, and its limits: It documents how one Edo-period settlement was placed and administered within a large city: its people lived at five scattered sites in 1573-92, some of them probably moved (by the compilers' inference) to one house site, and that site was then taken by the city's growth in 1619, and because they were counted as members of the Osaka Sango the village itself does not appear in the village rolls, which list only its farmland, Nanase shinden. It is only the opening paragraph (the page says the rest is behind a subscription), and it does not name the caste of its residents or the trade they followed, so it cannot be used for either.
