kotobank-watanabe-mura

"渡辺村" ("Watanabe-mura", Watanabe village, title translated), in the Historical Place Names of Japan series (日本歴史地名大系), Heibonsha, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B8%A1%E8%BE%BA%E6%9D%91-3076303)

What it is: A short, partial extract of a place-name entry on Watanabe village, a settlement within Kizu village in Osaka from 1701. It says that most of its land was exempt from the land levy, that its residents were counted as members of the Osaka Sango, and that its people had earlier lived in five places before settling on land taken from Shimonanba village for the Nagahori canal.

Why it applies, and its limits: It documents how one Edo-period settlement was placed and administered within a large city: its people moved there from five scattered sites, and a village in the rolls was kept apart from the city's own wards. It is only the opening paragraph (the page says the rest is behind a subscription), and it does not name the caste of its residents or the trade they followed, so it cannot be used for either.
