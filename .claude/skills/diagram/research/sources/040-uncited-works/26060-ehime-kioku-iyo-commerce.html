ehime-kioku-iyo-commerce

Ehime Prefectural Lifelong Learning Center, "愛媛県史 近世 下 第六章 第二節 二 商品流通の進展" ("History of Ehime Prefecture, early modern, part 2, chapter 6, section 2: the growth of commodity circulation", title translated), in the database Memories of Ehime (in Japanese; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/63/view/7972)

What it is: A long section of the prefectural history (1987) on Iyo's Edo-period commerce: rice shipped to Osaka and sold at Matsuyama, rice prices, paper, wax and cotton, village shops, peddlers and trade through the port of Mitsuhama.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives figures for a domain economy: Matsuyama shipped 30,000 to 40,000 koku to Osaka, about a fifth of its nominal yield; its rice office sold about 63,000 bales in 1707-08 against about 16,000 townspeople; 238 cargo ships entered Mitsuhama in 1864; and domains restricted village shops, with one granting thirteen goods in 1848. It describes Iyo on the Inland Sea, an area of unusually early market growth, and its tables are named but not shown on the page.
