en.wikipedia "Windbreak" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windbreak)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on windbreaks (shelterbelts): rows of trees or shrubs planted to shelter fields, homes and roads, with a section on how wind slows behind a porous barrier.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the physical rule that wind speed falls to a minimum about 3 to 5 times the windbreak's height downwind and recovers beyond that, which holds for a hedge or grove anywhere. Its examples are modern (the American Great Plains Shelterbelt, Canadian prairie programs, an East German poster), it names no East Asian practice (its one Asian example is alley cropping in India), it gives the 3-to-5-heights minimum for a porous belt without saying how a denser or thinner one moves it, the sentence giving that figure carries no footnote of its own (the paragraph's one reference is a 1985 numerical study of flow through a windbreak), and the article itself flags its windbreak-organizations section as unsourced.
