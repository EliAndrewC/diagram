chen-2021-foshan-jitang

Chen Caixia, Huang Guangqing, Ye Yuyao, Zhao Lingling, Jin Lixia and Liu Xulong, "珠江三角洲基塘系统演化及生态修复策略——以佛山4村为例" ("Zhujiang sanjiaozhou jitang xitong yanhua ji shengtai xiufu celue: yi Foshan 4 cun wei li", Change and ecological restoration of the dike-pond system in the Pearl River Delta: a case study of four villages in Foshan City, title translated), Resources Science (资源科学) 2021, no. 2, pp. 328-340, DOI 10.18402/resci.2021.02.11 (in Chinese; https://www.resci.cn/CN/lexeme/showArticleByLexeme.do?articleID=49240)

What it is: The full text of a peer-reviewed journal article on the dike-pond (jitang) farming system of the Pearl River Delta, studying four villages in Nanhai and Shunde districts of Foshan from satellite imagery and semi-structured interviews with seven pond contractors and village figures in 2019. It traces each village's pond land, water-to-land ratio and crop and fish mix from the 1980s to the 2010s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the traditional dike-pond water-to-land ratio as generally 6:4 or 5.5:4.5 with the dike top kept wide enough to farm, and records an unregulated village (Xiqiao) whose ponds still vary from about 0.1 to 0.6 ha at about 2 m deep, with vegetables and fruit on the dikes beside houses and weeds farther off. Its evidence is the 1980s to 2019, after mulberry gave way to sugarcane, grass and then fish-only intensive culture, and its regraded ponds (about 0.45 ha squares, 7:3 to 8:2, 3 m deep) are explicitly modern engineering, so only its account of the traditional ratio and the still-organic Xiqiao ponds bear on a premodern setting; the traditional ratio is stated without a source in the text, and the page dates the delta's large-scale dike-ponds only to the middle and late Ming by citing other works. Even Xiqiao's ponds are a present-day remnant, about 25 ha left among an industrial park and housing. Their 2 m depth is greater than the 1.0-1.5 m the paper gives for the other villages' ponds before regrading. The weeds on the farther dikes reflect the general abandonment of dike cropping since 2000, not the traditional layout.
