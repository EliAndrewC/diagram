kotobank-kusawake

Fukuda Ajio (the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry), "草分け" ("kusawake", the first house to clear a village's land, title translated), with the Digital Daijisen, Heibonsha Mypedia and Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten entries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/草分け-483223)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering four dictionary and encyclopedia entries on kusawake, the founding house of a village; the longest is the signed entry in the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the founding houses of Edo-period reclaimed villages often held the headman offices by inheritance and held special standing in common-land use and shrine rites, and that the usual count is seven houses. The encyclopedia entry itself holds that most founding stories are tradition rather than documented fact, usually paired with refugee-warrior legends, and that such houses are scarce in the old-settled Kinai and common in Kanto, Tohoku and Kyushu.
