Kurosu Shigeru (黒須茂), former director of the Saitama Prefectural Museum, "古文書にみる宿場と村の生活 1" ("Post towns and village life seen in old documents, part 1", title translated), p. 36 of Koho Ageo (広報あげお), the city of Ageo's public newsletter, March 2012 issue (in Japanese; https://city.ageo.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/9862.pdf)

What it is: A one-column article in a Saitama city's newsletter on how Edo-period villages and the Ageo post town were run. It names the village officers (nanushi, kumigashira, hyakushodai, toshiyori), the post-town toiya and the honjin and wakihonjin of late-Edo Ageo, and explains kokudaka, mochidaka, takamochi hyakusho and mizunomi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a concrete cast of village and post-town officers for Musashi province, with the number of office-holders at Ageo post town cited from its own documents, and says the villages of the Ageo area ranged from Haraichi at 235 households to Sugawara Shinden at two. The saved text is a jumbled layout of vertical columns with a quiz and a map mixed in, so the wording of a passage is not reliable, and the post-town office-holders it names come from two dates: the nanushi, three toshiyori and six hyakushodai from a 1737 report of Ageo post town (the Ogawa family documents), the single toiya and the wakihonjin from the late Edo period. Its household counts for Haraichi and Sugawara Shinden carry no date.
