Japanese Wikipedia, "上総掘り" (Kazusa-bori, the Kazusa method of boring wells, title translated), Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/上総掘り)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Kazusa-bori, the hand-powered percussion method of boring a drilled well with an iron pipe hung from a split-bamboo cord, devised in the Kazusa region (now central and western Chiba), with its tools, steps, history and its use for hot-spring drilling at Beppu.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that until the Edo period Japanese wells were dug by a person going down the hole, to a limit of about 30 m and with danger, that the Osaka-bori iron-rod method existed in Edo times, and that the Kazusa region's plateau villages lacked water because the rivers ran in deep valleys; it names Ikeda Kyuzo of Kazusa as drilling a well about 1817 to 1818. The bamboo-cord rig itself it dates to about 1887 (Meiji), so it is evidence for how a plateau village got water, not a tool to draw before then; the page carries a notice that it lacks sources.
