kotobank-jishi

"地子" ("jishi", land rent, the ground rent), entry in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典) by Suma Chiei (ancient and medieval) and Yoshida Nobuyuki (early modern), with the Shogakukan, Yamakawa, Britannica and Obunsha entries on the same page, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/地子-73184)

What it is: A page of Japanese encyclopedia and dictionary entries on jishi, the rent or levy on land, fields and house plots, from the Ritsuryo state's one-fifth of the crop to the Edo-period money rent on town house lots.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in the Edo period jishi meant the money levy on house plots in towns, relatively light, so that from the start of the early modern period many towns (the three great cities, castle towns, temple-gate towns, post towns, ports and mining towns) were exempted from it in whole or in part and their householders instead owed labor service, which is the form a town map would carry. Its ancient and medieval sections are mostly about rent on rice and dry fields, though they note a rent on town house plots (yajishi) from the fourteenth century, with one Kyoto plot owing 550 mon in 1395; it gives no plot sizes or rates for the Edo town levy.
