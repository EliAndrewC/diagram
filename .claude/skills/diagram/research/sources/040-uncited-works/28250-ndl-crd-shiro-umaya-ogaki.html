Ogaki City Library (大垣市立図書館), "江戸時代のお城にある馬屋について知りたい。" ("I want to know about the stables in Edo-period castles", title translated), reference case R1000238, in the Reference Collaborative Database (レファレンス協同データベース) of the National Diet Library (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000296966&page=ref_view)

What it is: A library reference answer, registered 2021, on castle stables, prompted by the one at Hikone Castle. It quotes the Nihon joukaku jiten entry on umaya and notes that Hikone's stable is a designated Important Cultural Property, designated 1 July 1963.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes a castle dictionary that a stable was set in two places, the area of the third bailey and the main bailey: the first an everyday stable with a riding ground, the second a mostly temporary one for keeping war horses. It also quotes the Shin-shitei juyo bunkazai that Hikone's stable is the only surviving example of a stable as a castle's subsidiary building; it gives no dimensions, stall counts or dates of building.
