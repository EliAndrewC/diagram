"江戸時代、領主に納める年貢米は、籾、あるいは籾殻を取り除いた玄米の、いずれで納めていたのか?" ("In the Edo period, was the tribute rice paid to the lord paid as unhusked rice or as husked brown rice?", title translated), Reference Collaboration Database (レファレンス協同データベース), National Diet Library; answer by the Amagasaki City History Museum's local-history archive (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/detail?page=ref_view&id=1000220332)

What it is: An answer by the Amagasaki City History Museum's local-history archive, dated 2017, in the National Diet Library's reference database to whether Edo tribute rice was paid as unhusked paddy or husked rice, built from cited sources: tribute was basically paid as brown rice, with a 1698 village order and an 1801 domain notice from the Amagasaki area as examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports husked rice as the form of tribute in the shogunal domains and in western Settsu, with the exception that rice stored against famine could be kept as paddy, and it records that Kai Province long paid in paddy. Its evidence is a handful of Kinki-region documents and the Jikata hanreiroku, a late-Edo manual for local administrators; finding no reference that answered the question outright, the answer reasons first from the husking done before the rice was baled and then from those documents, and it says practice differed from region to region in Japan; a tax-office quiz quoted in the notes is given with its source unknown.
