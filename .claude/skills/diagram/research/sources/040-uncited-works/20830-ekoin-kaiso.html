ekoin-kaiso

Ekoin (Ekoin temple), "開創" ("Kaiso", the founding, title translated), in the temple's history pages (in Japanese; https://ekoin.or.jp/history/)

What it is: The temple's own history page for Ekoin, a Pure Land (Jodo-shu) temple at Ryogoku in Tokyo, telling how it was founded in 1657 after the Great Fire of Meireki. It also covers its later role as a pilgrimage and exhibition site and its sumo grounds.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where a mass-burial temple for unclaimed dead was placed in Edo (the east bank of the Sumida River, on land granted by the shogun Ietsuna) and that the fire killed more than 100,000, with over 60 percent of the city burned. It also says the temple later hosted public displays of hidden images from famous temples and shrines, and that its sumo grounds were fixed there for 76 years to the end of Meiji. Its figures are the temple's own round statements with no source given, and it is the history of one Edo temple, not a statement about temples generally.
