touken-world-jinya

Touken World (刀剣ワールド), "陣屋" ("Jinya", the office-residence of a lord without a castle, title translated), in the Web Japanese-history dictionary, important terms of the Edo period (Web日本史辞典 江戸時代の重要用語), on Touken World, the sword site of the Nagoya Token Museum (in Japanese; https://touken-world.jp/history/history-important-word/jinya)

What it is: A short encyclopedia-style article on the jinya, which by the Edo period meant the seat from which a lord governed and held a military post, with its buildings and a list of well-known surviving examples. It covers who held one, what it contained, and three large and three surviving ones.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that a jinya generally belonged to a daimyo of under 30,000 koku without a castle, or to a high-ranking bannerman, that it was not built for siege defense, and that it consisted of a residence, storehouses, stable, garden, gate and walls, with retainers' houses sometimes inside or outside the grounds. It gives no dimensions, and it says scale varied with the lord's rank and domain, so it supports what a jinya held but not how large one was; its examples (Iino, Tsuruga, Tokuyama, Takayama) are named without plans.
