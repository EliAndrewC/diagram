kotobank-takafuda-kosatsu

"高札" ("kosatsu" or "takafuda", edict board), in the Digital Daijisen, the Concise Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, Mypedia, the Nipponica, the Yamakawa Dictionary of Japanese History, the Britannica Japan encyclopedia and the Obunsha Dictionary of Japanese History, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%AB%98%E6%9C%AD-62093)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering nine reference entries on the kosatsu, the board on which laws, prohibitions and criminals' offenses were posted to the public, from the late Nara period to its abolition in 1873.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where the boards stood (crossroads, entrances, bridge ends, ferry landings, barriers), that the shogunate's five basic boards of 1711 stayed up to the end of the Edo period and that domain lords could raise their own, and that Edo had six main sites and thirty-five others kept under strict control. The entries stress the shogunate's boards and agree in general terms, but they do not give a board's size or how a site looked in a village.
