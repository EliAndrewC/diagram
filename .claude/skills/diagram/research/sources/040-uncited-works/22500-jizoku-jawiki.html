ja.wikipedia 寺族 ("jizoku", the family of a temple's head priest, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寺族)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article defining the jizoku, the family of the head priest of a Japanese Buddhist temple, with notes on who is counted, the titles for the priest's wife, and the Soto and Jodo Shinshu usages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that married priests are particular to Japan, that temple management and succession in ordinary temples rest on the head priest and the family, and that the Jodo Shinshu temple's wife is traditionally called bomori. It also says that in the Genroku era (1688-1704) the line that supplies a temple's head was called teramoto, iemoto or satomoto, the only dated point in it. The article gives no period for the rest, no counts and no household detail, and it is a stub that marks itself as lacking sources, so its sect usages may reflect present-day practice more than the premodern one. Outside Jodo Shinshu, whose priests married from its founding, a priest's marriage was made lawful only in 1872, so a married priest's family running a temple of another sect is a modern arrangement, not an Edo one.
