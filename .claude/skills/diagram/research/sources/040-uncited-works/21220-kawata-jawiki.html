kawata-jawiki

ja.wikipedia かわた ("kawata", an Edo-period outcaste group whose name means hide-worker, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%8B%E3%82%8F%E3%81%9F)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on "kawata", the name for the outcaste group later called eta, tracing the word and the group from the 15th century through the Toyotomi and early Tokugawa periods.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated records of kawata as leather workers owing hides to a lord (Imagawa, Later Hojo, Kaga domain) in the 16th and early 17th centuries, and says that Hideyoshi-era land surveys show them holding farmland (some near 20 koku), living in separate hamlets, and being assigned jobs in execution, policing and cleaning. It follows the term only to about the 1640s and reports a scholarly dispute over whether early kawata were an occupation or a status; it says little about the later Edo period.
