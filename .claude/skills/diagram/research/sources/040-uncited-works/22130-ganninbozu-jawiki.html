ja.wikipedia 願人坊主 ("ganninbozu", the proxy-petitioner monks, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A1%98%E4%BA%BA%E5%9D%8A%E4%B8%BB)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the ganninbozu, Edo-period street performers who began as monks praying and doing cold-water austerities as clients' proxies and became begging entertainers under the temple and shrine magistrate. It lists their performing types and the Edo districts where they lived.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a lodging quarter of outcaste street performers in Edo and names four districts from an 1842 report to the town magistrate (Hashimoto-cho, Shiba Shinami-cho, Shitaya Yamazaki-cho, and the gate-front district of Tenryuji temple in Yotsuya), and notes that some ran cheap inns for drifters. It is an Edo-only account, mostly of the last decades of the shogunate, and says nothing about performers in a provincial town or village; the Meiji ban and slum clearances it describes come after the setting's period.
