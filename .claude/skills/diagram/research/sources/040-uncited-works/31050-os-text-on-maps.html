os-text-on-maps

Ordnance Survey, "Text on maps", in the Guide to cartography, Geographic Data Visualisation, More than Maps, OS Docs (in English; https://docs.os.uk/more-than-maps/geographic-data-visualisation/guide-to-cartography/text-on-maps)

What it is: A web page from Britain's national mapping agency's cartography guide on lettering a map: the purpose of labels, how type changes with feature class, the placement of text on point, line and area features, and legibility aids such as halos and leader lines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a rule set for the map's own lettering, not evidence about a place: a point label goes above and to the right, then bottom right, top left, bottom left, top centre and bottom centre in turn, a coastal town's name goes in the water and an inland town's wholly on land, and text reads horizontally and from the bottom. It is written for modern digital topographic mapping and tells its reader to place labels by hand where an automatic engine fails; the area and line rules sit in tabs that the saved page does not show.
