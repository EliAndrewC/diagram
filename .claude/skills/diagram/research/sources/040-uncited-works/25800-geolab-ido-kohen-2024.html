Fujiwara Yasushi, "井戸の話 あれこれ(後編)" ("all about wells, last part", title translated), in the "やわらかサイエンス" ("soft science") column of the Geoscience Research Laboratory (地層科学研究所), April 2024 (in Japanese; https://www.geolab.jp/documents/science/science-159/)

What it is: A short column by a geotechnical software firm on the wells of Edo's lower town: wells fed by piped water from the town's water mains rather than groundwater, built of stacked bottomless Hiba-wood tubs bound with bamboo hoops, and cleaned by the tenement's residents on the seventh of the seventh month. It closes with two well ghost stories.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Edo's lower-town wells drew on piped Kanda and Tamagawa water because the reclaimed ground gave salty well water, that the Kanda system's pipes ran about 63 km and the Tamagawa channel about 43 km with a 92 m drop, and that the well-cleaning was done under the landlord's direction. It is a general-audience column that cites its figures to other websites, and it covers Edo only, not castle towns or the countryside, where wells were dug in groundwater.
