kotobank-ryobosei-burial

"両墓制" ("ryobosei", the double-grave system, title translated), dictionary entries from Digital Daijisen, Nippon Daihyakka Zensho, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/両墓制)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering several dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the double-grave system, in which a body is buried in one place (the burial grave) and a second grave is kept for visits and memorial rites. It covers where the custom was found, where each grave lay and why it arose.

Why it applies, and its limits: The Heibonsha entry says the burial grave lay on a riverbed, in the hills or on a beach, often on common land and reused in the order of death, while the visiting grave stood in a village temple or hall precinct. It dates the custom's rise to the early Edo period with the spread of gravestones, though one view puts it in the Kamakura period, and it says the custom is found around Kinki, the Kanto and Tohoku but hardly in Kyushu. Its scholars disagree on the origin, and the page says the custom is breaking down in the present, so it describes surviving practice rather than a documented map of graves in any one village.
