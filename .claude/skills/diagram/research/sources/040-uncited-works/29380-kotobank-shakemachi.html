kotobank-shakemachi

Heibonsha, "社家町" ("shakemachi", the shrine-families' quarter, title translated), in the Historical Place-Name Dictionary of Japan (日本歴史地名大系), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/社家町-521613)

What it is: Three place-name entries of the Heibonsha Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei on towns called Shakemachi, in Hakata (Fukuoka), Nishinomiya (Hyogo) and Tsuruga (Fukui), each a street where a great shrine's priestly families lived, with household counts from the Edo-period gazetteers. The page also carries a quoted line from the older Heibonsha World Encyclopedia that groups shake-machi with temple-gate and oshi towns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a priests' street lying beside a major shrine in three Edo-period towns and gives counts: 11 households in 1690 and 15 in 1766 at Hakata, eight shrine-family names in the 1661 Nishinomiya survey, and 15 households in 1726 at Tsuruga, the fewest of its 36 towns, exempt from the domain's land tax and corvee. All three belong to large shrines (Kushida, Nishinomiya, Kehi) of ports or castle-adjacent towns, and the page is a truncated excerpt (Kotobank prints only part of each entry), so it says nothing about the priests of a village shrine.
