tanigawa-1992-edo-cemeteries

Tanigawa Akio, "Excavating Edo's Cemeteries: Graves as Indicators of Status and Class", Japanese Journal of Religious Studies 19/2-3 (1992), pp. 271-295, translated from the Japanese of 1991 (https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/f1ac/013ceefc6737d3f693e07f0d8a9908b6cab3.pdf)

What it is: A journal article, translated from Japanese, by an archaeologist on excavated Edo-period graves in Tokyo (the Jisho-in site in Shinjuku, shogunal and daimyo tombs, early Edo temple graveyards) and on how grave form, gravestones and Buddhist death names marked social rank. It dates a shift in burial forms to the end of the seventeenth and the start of the eighteenth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the size of a temple precinct (Jisho-in, over 10,600 tsubo, about 35,000 square meters, with 300 tsubo of gate housing and 200 koku of income), the sizes of graves by rank (stone chambers with a long axis of 1.7-2.4 m, jar burials and wooden coffins of 65 cm to 2.3 m, most under 1.4 m), and the finding that the temples were pushed to Edo's edge as the city grew. Its excavated graves are from Edo's temple graveyards. For villages it gives only gravestone forms and death names, from villages around Edo and from Takataki and Yoro in Chiba, and nothing on where a village's graves lay. The author calls the data sparse and his conclusions tentative; the merchant class is left unstudied.
