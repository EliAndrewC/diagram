suzuki-chinjugami-eos

Suzuki Kentaro, "Chinjugami", in the Encyclopedia of Shinto, Kokugakuin University Digital Museum (in English; https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9944)

What it is: A signed entry in Kokugakuin University's English-language Encyclopedia of Shinto defining the chinjugami, the tutelary kami of a bounded area, and tracing how the term spread from temple compounds to provinces, castles, estates, homes and villages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains why a village's shrine came to be called its tutelary: from the medieval and especially the early modern period shrines of differing origins were all called village chinjugami, helped by the Edo-period temple registration that placed shrines under temples, and it names this as the sense of chinju no mori, the grove at a shrine. It describes the idea and its history, not where in a village a shrine stood, how large its grove was, or how any region varied.
