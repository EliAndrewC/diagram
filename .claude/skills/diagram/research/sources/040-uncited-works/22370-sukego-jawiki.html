ja.wikipedia 助郷 ("Sukego", the post-station levy on nearby villages, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/助郷)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on sukego, the corvee the Tokugawa shogunate laid on villages near the post stations of the highways to supply porters and horses, with its forms, its burdens and its abolition in 1872.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the levy began as an occasional call-up and became permanent as travel grew with the alternate attendance system, that the villages' range grew from the neighborhood of a station to places over ten ri (about 40 km) away, and that a village which could not send men and horses paid money instead. It also quotes a historian that the late Edo burden was the main cause of village finances failing; it gives no count of men, horses or days for any one station.
