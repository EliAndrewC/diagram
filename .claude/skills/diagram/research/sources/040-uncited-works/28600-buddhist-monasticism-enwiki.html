en.wikipedia "Buddhist monasticism" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_monasticism)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Buddhist monasticism across Asia: the origin of the monastic order, the Vinaya traditions, ordination stages, celibacy, diet, hierarchy, and regional sections on Tibet, East Asia, Japan and Southeast Asia.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its East Asia section says monks in China, Korea, Vietnam and much of Japan generally did not beg, so monasteries took bulk donations of rice and funds and many monks farmed their own food after Baizhang Huaihai, which bears on how a temple was supplied; it also says East Asian ordination is under the Dharmaguptaka Vinaya, with 250 rules for monks, and that Confucian views discouraged begging. It gives no dates for these practices, no counts of monks or temple grounds, and does not describe a monastery's size or layout. For Japan it says clergy were ordained under bodhisattva vows rather than the Vinaya, which still required celibacy, and that clerical marriage was never widespread outside Jodo Shinshu until the Meiji government's Nikujiki Saitai law, so its non-celibate Japanese clergy is the post-Meiji picture; three of its sections carry the article's own banner that they lack citations.
