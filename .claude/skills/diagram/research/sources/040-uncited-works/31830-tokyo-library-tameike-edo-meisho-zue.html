Tokyo Metropolitan Library (東京都立図書館), "『江戸名所図会 3巻』より「溜池（ためいけ）」" ("Tameike, from Edo Meisho Zue vol. 3", title translated), Saito Chosyu (斎藤長秋) ed., Hasegawa Settan (長谷川雪旦) illus., published 1834-1836, Kaga Bunko 256, on the library's Edo digital pages (in Japanese; https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/modal/index.html?d=5672)

What it is: A library's note on a plate from the illustrated guide to Edo's famous places, describing the Tameike pond in Akasaka: the reservoir made in 1606 at the foot of Sanno, its extent, its use as Edo's early water supply and its later infill.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Asano Yukinaga made the pond in 1606 as an artificial lake reaching from Toranomon, Shinbashi and Shiodome to Akasaka Mitsuke, that it supplied water before the Kanda and Tamagawa aqueducts, and that infilling began in 1652-1655 and ended in 1910. It is the water supply of a lord's castle town, built by a daimyo, not a village irrigation pond, and it gives no depth, area or dam figure.
