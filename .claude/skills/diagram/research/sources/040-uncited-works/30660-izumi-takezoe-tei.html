izumi-takezoe-tei

Izumi Fumoto Machinami Hozonkai (Izumi Fumoto Townscape Preservation Society), "竹添邸" ("The Takezoe residence", title translated), on the society's site (in Japanese; https://izumi-buke.net/takezoetei.html)

What it is: The preservation society's page on the Takezoe residence, an opened samurai house in the Fumoto warrior quarter of Izumi, Kagoshima. It gives the family's history, the date it moved to the quarter (1637), its stipend (140 to 170 koku), and a tour of its rooms, stable, bathhouse and storehouse.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where the house stood against the quarter (on a wide street near the lord's official lodging, the Okariya) and what the rooms were for: an elder's room, a hearth room where the head of the house ate, an earth-floored kitchen where farm work was also done, and a room for a guest's attendants. The house was rebuilt after a fire in the last years of the shogunate or the Meiji period, its roof is now tile where Edo-period warrior houses were thatched, and the stable, bathhouse and storehouse were rebuilt when it opened in 1996, so the page describes a house of one Satsuma gentry family at a late date and not the original plan.
