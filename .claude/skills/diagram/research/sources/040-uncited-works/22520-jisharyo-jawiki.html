jisharyo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 寺社領 ("jisharyo", temple and shrine lands, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寺領)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on temple and shrine lands, the holdings that supported religious institutions, tracing them from the ancient land grants through the estates of the Heian period to their loss under the Edo order and the Meiji confiscations, and listing the number of villages held as temple or shrine land in each province at the end of the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the chain of support for temples and shrines: their own fields and household grants under the codes, then estates, then the few red-seal grants that the Tokugawa order kept, with all land beyond the precinct confiscated in 1871 and 1875. Its province list, drawn from the national museum's database of old villages and domains, counts villages with temple or shrine land at the end of the Edo period, for example 420 in Kubiki district of Echigo and 163 in Tama district of Musashi. The counts are of villages holding some such land, not of acres or of institutions, so they show spread but not the size of a holding.
