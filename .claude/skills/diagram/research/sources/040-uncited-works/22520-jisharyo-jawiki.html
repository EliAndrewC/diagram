jisharyo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 寺社領 ("jisharyo", temple and shrine lands, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寺領)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on temple and shrine lands, the holdings that supported religious institutions, tracing them from the ancient land grants through the estates of the Heian period, their loss in the wars of the Nanboku-cho and Sengoku periods with only some red-seal holdings protected under the Edo order, the Meiji confiscations and the postwar land reform, and listing, district by district within each province, the number of villages at the end of the Edo period that held some temple or shrine land (exempt land included), a village shared among several lords counted once.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the chain of support for temples and shrines: their own fields and household grants under the codes, then estates, then the few red-seal grants that the Tokugawa order kept, with all land beyond the precinct confiscated in 1871 and 1875. Its province list, drawn from the national museum's database of old villages and domains, counts villages with temple or shrine land at the end of the Edo period, for example 420 in Kubiki district of Echigo and 163 in Tama district of Musashi. The counts are of villages holding some such land, not of acres or of institutions, so they show spread but not the size of a holding. The list also leaves out many provinces (Mutsu, Kaga, Noto, Etchu, Nagato, Aki, Iyo, Tosa, Chikuzen, Satsuma among them), so a province missing from it is a gap in the list, not evidence of no temple land.
