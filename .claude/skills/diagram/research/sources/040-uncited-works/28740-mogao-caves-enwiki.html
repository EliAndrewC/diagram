en.wikipedia "Mogao Caves" (the Buddhist cave temples near Dunhuang, Gansu, title translated) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogao_Caves)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the Mogao Caves, the cliff-cut Buddhist cave-temple complex southeast of Dunhuang in Gansu, covering its founding from 366 CE, the Library Cave and its manuscripts found in 1900, the murals, sculptures and architecture, and a count of caves by dynasty.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows who paid for and used a Buddhist site over a thousand years, from the 4th to the 14th century: donors from clergy and local elites to merchants and women's groups, family caves of rich clans, wooden porches built out from the cliff, and silk banners hung on the cliff face at festivals. It is a desert oasis on the Silk Road cut into a soft conglomerate, and the page itself says this is unlike Longmen and Yungang, so its forms are those of rock-cut caves, not of a built monastery in a rice-growing plain. Much of its detail concerns art and the 1900 manuscript find, not the layout of a settlement.
