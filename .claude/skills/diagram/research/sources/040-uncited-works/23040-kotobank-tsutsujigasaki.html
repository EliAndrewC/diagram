kotobank-tsutsujigasaki

"躑躅ヶ崎" ("Tsutsujigasaki", the Takeda lords' residence site in Kai, title translated), in the Britannica International Encyclopedia small-item edition and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%AD%E9%96%80-99316)

What it is: A short encyclopedia entry on Tsutsujigasaki in Kofu, the residence of three generations of the Takeda lords of Kai from 1519, when Nobutora moved there from Isawa, until Katsuyori left for Shinpu castle in 1581. It adds that the residence had a single moat, and places it where the Aikawa fan opens to the Kofu basin.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a warlord's seat that was a small, plainly defended residence with one moat rather than a large castle, and it gives the site's setting on an alluvial fan with hills to the north, east and west. The words about the single moat and Shingen's verse that people are the castle are a general statement with no dimensions, and the setting is a Sengoku domain of the 1500s, earlier than the Edo castle towns the maps mostly resemble.
