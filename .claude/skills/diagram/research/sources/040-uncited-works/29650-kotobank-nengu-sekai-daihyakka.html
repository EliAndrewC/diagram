kotobank-nengu-sekai-daihyakka

Izumiya Yasuo, Oguchi Yujiro, Iinuma Kenji and Saito Yoshiyuki, "年貢" ("nengu", the annual land tax), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典) and the Shogakukan Nipponica (日本大百科全書), with the Yamakawa, Britannica, Obunsha, Digital Daijisen and Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/年貢-111662)

What it is: A set of encyclopedia and dictionary entries on nengu, the annual tax paid by cultivators to their lord, covering its medieval form in the estates, its early modern assessment from the land survey, how it was set each year, collected through the village and delivered, and its end with the land tax reform of the Meiji period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives rates and procedure for the early modern tax: the rate on the assessed yield in shogunate lands as 34 percent in 1716, a peak of 39 percent in 1752 and 34 percent again in 1841 (the Heibonsha entry adds that private domains were generally higher), the estimate of 30 to 50 percent of the crop for the medieval estates, rice from paddy and goods or money from dry fields, and village-wide liability for arrears. The Britannica entry gives 50 percent for the shogunate and 60 percent for domains as usual after the mid-Edo period, which the page does not reconcile with the Heibonsha figures, and Nipponica calls the two-thirds rate set under Hideyoshi a notional figure rather than the actual rate.
