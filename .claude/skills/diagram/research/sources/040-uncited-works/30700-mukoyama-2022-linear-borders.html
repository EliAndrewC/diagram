mukoyama-2022-linear-borders

Naosuke Mukoyama, "The Eastern cousins of European sovereign states? The development of linear borders in early modern Japan", European Journal of International Relations 29(2), first published online November 1, 2022 (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/13540661221133206)

What it is: The open-access full text of a journal article in international relations arguing that Edo-period domains already had demarcated linear borders. It draws on boundary disputes, boundary markers, guardhouses and the shogunate's provincial map projects, with cases from the Sengoku and Edo periods, mostly in northern Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies, for a domain-and-village setting, how borders between lords' lands were fixed: by negotiation or lawsuit, then an exchange of maps and a line of stones, mounds or trees (more than 100 mounds along 130 km between Morioka and Sendai, agreed in 1642), and how guardhouses were posted on the line. Its cases are tozama domains in the north and on the Seto Inland Sea, the author says outright that not all domain boundaries were linear (the Kinai, around Kyoto and Nara, stayed tangled), and it is written to test a theory of state formation, not to describe village or field layout.
