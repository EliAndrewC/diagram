shiboku-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 四木 ("Shiboku", the four cash-crop trees, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/四木)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article on the shiboku, the four trees treated in the Edo period as high-value crops: tea, mulberry, lacquer and paper mulberry (kozo), with the three herbs added to make the shiboku sanso.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the four trees and what each was grown for (tea, silkworm leaves, lacquer ware and candles, paper) and says domains encouraged their planting as a source of revenue and made them monopoly goods. It gives no yields, acreage or regional spread beyond naming Yamashiro tea and Aizu lacquer as famous, and cites only dictionary and encyclopedia entries, a textbook publisher's history page and one town page.
