Feng Jin, "平遥古城为何得以幸存" ("Why Pingyao's ancient city survived", title translated), Economic Information Daily (经济参考报), 2008-06-13, from the People's Daily Overseas Edition (in Chinese; http://www.jjckb.cn/wzyw/2008-06/13/content_102426.htm)

What it is: A 2008 newspaper feature on how Pingyao in Shanxi kept its Ming and Qing county-town walls and streets when other old cities were demolished in the 1980s. It describes the city and recounts how Professor Ruan Yisan stopped the 1981 plan to cut roads through it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives figures for the walled county town as it stands: rebuilt in brick in 1370 on an older rampart, 6,163 m around, walls 10 to 12 m high with battlements, watchtowers, corner towers and three barbican gates, laid out around a market tower with four main streets, eight lesser streets and seventy-two alleys, in courtyard houses. These are the surviving Ming-Qing city as restored after 1988, and the founding claim (the Western Zhou, 2,700 years ago) is tradition, so the page fits the form of a northern county seat, not a measured Ming plan. Most of the text is preservation history, not description of the town.
