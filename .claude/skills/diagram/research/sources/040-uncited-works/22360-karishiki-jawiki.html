ja.wikipedia 刈敷 ("Karishiki", green manure of cut grass and leaves, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/刈敷)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article defining karishiki, the manure made by cutting stems and leaves of plants from hills and paddy banks and working them into the field to rot, with a note on how it was done and when it faded.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the cuttings were taken in early spring mostly from common land, trodden into the paddy by oxen, horses or people before transplanting, and sometimes burned to ash first, and that it was a self-supplied fertilizer from the beginning of paddy farming. It names the labor of cutting and treading and the lack of plants on the plains as the reasons it gave way to bought fertilizer in the early modern period, but it gives no quantities, such as the area of hillside needed per paddy, and does not say how widely it was used.
