zh.wikipedia 會昌滅佛 ("the Huichang suppression of Buddhism", title translated; the page is reached by redirect from 唐武宗灭佛) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/唐武宗灭佛)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the suppression of Buddhism ordered by the Tang emperor Wuzong in the Huichang years (840-846), with the edicts of 842, 844 and 845 and the tallies of temples destroyed and monks and nuns returned to lay life.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the court's own tallies for 845, quoted from the Old Tang History: over 4,600 temples torn down, 260,500 monks and nuns returned to the tax rolls, over 40,000 smaller shrines and halls destroyed, and 150,000 bonded people taxed; it also records the rule that a temple of under 200 rooms was razed, and that Chang'an kept 4 temples of 10 monks each. These are one emperor's decrees in Tang China and were reversed the next year, so they show how large Tang Buddhism was on the books, not how many monks a temple of the setting would hold.
