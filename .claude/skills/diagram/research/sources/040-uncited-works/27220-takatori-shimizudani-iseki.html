takatori-shimizudani-iseki

Takatori Town Board of Education, "清水谷遺跡5世紀中頃に造られた最古級の人工池を検出！" ("Shimizudani site: one of the oldest artificial ponds, built in the mid-fifth century, found", title translated), on the Takatori town site, 2022-02-16 (in Japanese; https://www.town.takatori.nara.jp/contents_detail.php?co=kak&frmId=1278)

What it is: A town press page on the 2021 excavations at the Shimizudani site in Takatori, Nara (1,036 square meters in all), reporting a stone-lined rectangular pond of the mid-fifth century and a large later building built over it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the pond as 26 m by 13 m and 0.5 m deep as it survives (not its original depth) with river-stone walls laid three or four courses high and a stone-filled drain 0.9 m wide running about 4.6 m, and quotes two experts who doubt it was for irrigation or a royal garden, so the page reads it as a ritual pond made by immigrants. It is a Kofun-period find about a thousand years before the setting, in an early report, and it speaks to a ritual pond and not to a village reservoir.
