kotobank-kawajima-machi

"川島町" ("Kawajima-machi", Kawajima town, title translated), in the Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei (日本歴史地名大系, Heibonsha's historical place-name encyclopedia) and the Britannica International Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B7%9D%E5%B3%B6%E7%94%BA-48244)

What it is: A Kotobank page with the Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei entries for four places called Kawajima, an excerpt of each, and a Britannica entry. The first is the Hiki county town in Saitama, in the Arakawa lowland.

Why it applies, and its limits: For the Saitama Kawajima it says that the land has well-developed natural levees and a broad back marsh, that villages and the ancient tombs stand on the levees, that the town is ringed by rivers and yet had poor water supply, and that a water dispute arose between two of its medieval villages in 1452-55; the Britannica entry adds that it is a rice-growing area that often flooded. The Tokushima entry describes a castle-town and river port on the Yoshino, and the Gifu entry a river island of the Kiso. The page prints only part of each entry (it says so and points to JapanKnowledge), and nothing in it measures a village or a field.
