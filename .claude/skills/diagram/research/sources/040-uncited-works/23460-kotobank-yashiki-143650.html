kotobank-yashiki-143650

Yoshie Akio, Hayama Teisaku, Takeuchi Yoshitaro and others, "屋敷" ("yashiki", a house plot and its compound, title translated), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典), in Nipponica (日本大百科全書) and in the Britannica International Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B1%8B%E6%95%B7-143650)

What it is: A Kotobank page whose main entries are a long signed Heibonsha article on the yashiki, the house plot, from its first use in 1071 through the Edo period, and Nipponica and Britannica entries on how a plot was laid out. It covers the plot's size, its rank in the land survey, the buildings and the groves around it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries a good deal on the farm plot: a peasant's plot of one to two tan with house, yard and a little garden, the lord's of over one cho behind an earthwork and moat, the Edo survey measuring a plot with a one-ken margin left out and assessing it near the upper-field rate, the main house set toward the back with the south yard used for work and drying, and the names for the grove on the north and northwest (ignu, kuine, tsuijimatsu). It adds the storehouse at the northwest corner, a small shrine at the northeast, and a note that plots grow smaller going west. The Heibonsha article says the 1-2 tan size was for peasants without giving a region or a date within the medieval period, the layout customs come from Nipponica as general Japanese practice and vary by region, and the page's claims about household law are not a measure of any one village.
