mizu-no-bunka-66-kangai

Watanabe Tsugihiro, interviewed in "使いながら守りつづける灌漑施設から学ぶこと" ("What we learn from irrigation works protected by use", title translated), no. 66, "Water heritage handed down by communities", of the magazine Mizu no Bunka, Mizkan Water Culture Center (in Japanese; https://www.mizu.gr.jp/kikanshi/no66/03.html)

What it is: A 2020 magazine interview with an irrigation-engineering professor and national chair of the International Commission on Irrigation and Drainage, on what irrigation is, how Japan's differs and what the World Heritage Irrigation Structures program protects.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that almost all of Japan's paddies are irrigated but only about 2 tenths of its dry fields, that irrigation needs shared building and upkeep, and that in the Edo-period surge of new fields many rivers ran dry in summer as water went to paddies (a condition called se-giri). It is a present-day overview by a scholar, with one Edo sentence and no figures for a village's water use, so its irrigation percentages describe the 2020 landscape.
