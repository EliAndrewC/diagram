kato-shirobito-gokenin-yakata

Kato Masafumi (加藤理文), "理文先生のお城がっこう 歴史編 第10回 御家人（ごけにん）の館" ("Professor Masafumi's castle school, history, part 10: the gokenin's residence", title translated), 2019/02/22, on Shirobito (城びと), a site certified by the Japan Castle Foundation (in Japanese; https://shirobito.jp/article/677)

What it is: A lesson written for elementary and middle school readers by a director of the Japan Castle Foundation, on the Kamakura shogunate's appointment of protectors and stewards and on the moated residence of a gokenin (a shogun's vassal warrior). It describes the residence's moat, earthwork, gate and buildings and adds a column on Banna-ji in Ashikaga.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the parts of a provincial warrior's compound: a square water moat fed by irrigation water, an earthwork thrown up from the moat's spoil, a gate with watchmen, a main house and a secondary house, kitchen, firewood shed, storehouse, stable and fields worked by corvee labor, and gives a model's usual plot as one cho (about 100 m square) and Banna-ji as about 200 m square. The page says this layout is inferred from documents and picture scrolls, covers the Kamakura period only, and gives its figures for a warrior's seat, so it is no evidence for a commoner's house or for any other century.
