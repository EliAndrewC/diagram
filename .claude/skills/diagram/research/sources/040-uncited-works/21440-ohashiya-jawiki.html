ja.wikipedia "大橋屋" ("Ohashiya", the Ohashiya inn, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A7%E6%A9%8B%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Ohashiya, a two-story hatago (post-station inn) at Akasaka station on the Tokaido, in what is now Toyokawa, Aichi, which stayed open in its Edo-period building until March 2015 and is now owned by the city.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the building to a 1715 rebuilding, with a founding given as 1649 and a one-third loss to a neighbor's fire in 1921, and describes a two-story inn with an open stairwell and no handrail on the stair - a surviving example of a Tokaido hatago, with the 1977 city designation. It gives no floor plan, room count or dimensions, and its founding is given as 1649 in the history list but as "mid-Edo" in the overview, so the age of the fabric rests on the 1715 rebuilding; and about a third of the building burned in a neighbor's fire in 1921, it ran as an inn until 2015, and the city plans seismic reinforcement, so not all of what stands dates from 1715.
