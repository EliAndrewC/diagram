bunka-hosobuchi-nagayamon

Agency for Cultural Affairs, "細淵家住宅長屋門" ("Hosobuchi house, nagaya gate", title translated), in Bunka Isan Online (文化遺産オンライン) (in Japanese; https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/185163)

What it is: A Bunka Isan Online record of a registered nagaya gate in Minami ward, Saitama: a single-story, 30-square-meter gate on a lot's east edge, dated 1751-1829 and moved in 1868-1911, with a gate half and a room half.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a small gate-house plan, with the south half the gateway and the north half a room, and the tradition that it was moved from Iwatsuki Castle, which the page judges credible from its form. It is a surviving single gate, moved from its first site in the Meiji period, and its date is a range of eight decades. The page says it has the heavy look of a warrior's gate, of zelkova with heavy iron fittings and by tradition from Iwatsuki Castle, so its form and finish are a samurai house's, not a village headman's. Its place on the lot's east edge is where it was set after the move.
