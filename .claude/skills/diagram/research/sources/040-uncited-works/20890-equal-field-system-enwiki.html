en.wikipedia "Equal-field system" (均田制度, the Chinese land-distribution system, title as written) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal-field_system)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the equal-field system, the state's assignment of land to households in China from Northern Wei (485) through Tang (abolished 780), with its allotments in mu, its tax obligations, its decline, and a note on its Japanese and Korean counterparts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the allotment figures a premodern Chinese setting could draw on, such as Northern Wei's 40 mu of open field for an adult male and 20 mu of permanent mulberry land (the Tang decree of 624 gave an adult male 100 mu), and shows the state granting and reclaiming land by age and household. It describes a rule on paper, not what households actually held: the article itself says the Tang system failed in practice and that few households had their full public fields, and the page carries a 'needs more citations' banner. These are rules of 485 to 780, centuries before the Ming, Qing and Edo forms the setting is modeled on, and the article converts 40 mu once as about 1.1 hectares and once as about 2.6 hectares, so no area can be taken from it. Its Japan section is of the Nara period (the Taika reforms to about 800) and says that how far the system was carried out there is debatable.
