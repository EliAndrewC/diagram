en.wikipedia "Equal-field system" (均田制度, the Chinese land-distribution system, title as written) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal-field_system)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the equal-field system, the state's assignment of land to households in China from Northern Wei (485) through Tang (abolished 780), with its allotments in mu, its tax obligations, its decline, and a note on its Japanese and Korean counterparts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the allotment figures a premodern Chinese setting could draw on, such as 40 mu for an adult male and 20 mu of permanent mulberry land, and shows the state granting and reclaiming land by age and household. It describes a rule on paper, not what households actually held: the article itself says the Tang system failed in practice and that few households had their full public fields, and the page carries an 'needs more citations' banner. Its Japan section says only that the system's real implementation there is debatable.
