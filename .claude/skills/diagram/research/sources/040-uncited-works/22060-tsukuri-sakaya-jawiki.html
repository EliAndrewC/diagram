ja.wikipedia 造り酒屋 ("tsukuri-zakaya", sake brewery-shop, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%85%92%E8%94%B5)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the tsukuri-zakaya, the brewer who makes sake in a storehouse and sells it from a shop, with its medieval origin, the cedar-ball sign and a list of notable people born into such houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the brewer-merchant arose in the Kamakura period and flourished in the Muromachi period, that the large ones also lent money and shipped goods and kept apprentices and guards in a big household, and that small brewers stood in farm and mountain country and along quiet roads. It draws the line between brewer and plain liquor shop loosely by its own admission, its history of sake's origin is a set of rival claims, and half the page is lists of famous people.
