senjuji-shoro

Takada Honzan Senjuji (真宗高田派 Shinshu Takada-ha), "鐘楼" ("Shoro", The bell tower, title translated), in the temple's guide to its buildings, quoting a panel of the Tsu City Board of Education (in Japanese; https://senjuji.or.jp/sisetsu/shoro.php)

What it is: The head temple's page on its bell tower at Isshinden, Tsu, Mie: its size and form, the date of its rebuilding, and the bronze bell hung in it with its donor and founder.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the tower as one ken on a side with a hip-and-gable roof and splayed corner posts, rebuilt about 1713 as a roof-tile inscription shows after the repair that began in 1993, and a bell cast in 1652 by a Tsu founder for the widow of the fifteenth abbot. It is one tower of a large sectarian head temple, though the panel says its one-ken-square plan and hip-and-gable roof are those of bell towers in general; its Daibutsu-style bracket ends, likened to the Todai-ji bell tower, are grander than a village temple's, and it gives no height or floor measure beyond one ken.
