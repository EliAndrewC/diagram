reinet-yanaka-machizukuri

Kawamura Shigeru (河村 茂), "まちづくりの沿革 (江戸の防衛拠点、寺町から下町、都会の隠れ里へ)" ("History of the town's making: from Edo's defensive outpost and temple town to the downtown and a hidden village in the city", title translated), in the Japan Real Estate Institute's "Land and Humans" series, vol. 8.2, on the Japan Real Estate Institute site (一般財団法人 日本不動産研究所) (in Japanese; https://www.reinet.or.jp/?page_id=13724)

What it is: A web article by an architecture lecturer at Tokyo University of the Arts, published by a Japanese real-estate research foundation, on the history of Yanaka, a temple district north of Edo castle. It covers the siting of Kan'ei-ji, the move of temples to the district, its street layout and its later change into a district of tenement shops.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Kan'ei-ji was placed to the northeast (the unlucky quarter) of Edo castle, with subsidiary temples behind it, and that in the Keian years (1648-1651) fifteen temples were moved there from Kanda; after the great fire of 1657 more arrived, over 70 at last (98 at the peak, 60 now). It adds that the streets on the plateau-valley ground were made narrow, bent, stepped or dead-ended on purpose to slow an enemy, and that temple precinct land came to be leased to townspeople who built shops and tenements. The defense purpose and the street-design claim are the author's account with no source named on the page, and it covers one district of Edo.
