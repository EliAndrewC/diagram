kotobank-uradana-edo

"裏店" ("uradana", the back-lane rented house, title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A3%8F%E5%BA%97-441843)

What it is: A page of dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the uradana, the rented row house in a back lane of an Edo or Osaka block, with an encyclopedia account of the Edo block layout and a standard unit.

Why it applies, and its limits: The encyclopedia gives a 60-ken block with a 20-ken common yard, a standard unit of 9 shaku by 2 ken with 4.5 or 6 mats, and a shared well, privy and refuse heap, and says the form spread after the Kanbun-Genroku years. It describes Edo and Osaka, the largest cities, in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, so it does not say what a provincial town's back lanes held.
