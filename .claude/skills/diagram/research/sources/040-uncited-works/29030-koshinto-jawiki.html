koshinto-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 庚申塔 ("koshinto", the Koshin stone tower, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/庚申塔)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the koshinto, the stone monuments raised under the Koshin belief and its all-night vigils (koshinmachi), with their forms, carvings, distribution across Japan and history. It includes the count of 77 stone koshinto found in Tanto town, Toyooka, Hyogo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says koshinto were set along highways with directions carved on them, at village boundaries as guardian-of-the-road deities, and at crossroads and village entrances, and that they became widespread from the early Edo period, so it can support a roadside or boundary stone at a village edge. Its oldest dated examples are 1471 and its main spread is from the 1600s on, and it says the government of the Meiji era removed many, so the surviving stones it describes stand where they were later moved. Its only local count is from one town in Hyogo, and it notes that the Kanto region has many and Kyoto and Osaka relatively few.
