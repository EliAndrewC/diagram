chinaknowledge-diannong

Ulrich Theobald, "diannong 佃農" ("diannong", tenant farmers), in ChinaKnowledge.de, an encyclopaedia on Chinese history, literature and art (http://www.chinaknowledge.de/History/Terms/diannong.html)

What it is: A signed encyclopedia article (dated 7 March 2021) on tenant farmers in China from the Zhou to the Republic: tenancy and rent forms by dynasty, serf status, the rights of tenants and the later landlord rent-collecting stations, with a source list of Chinese encyclopedias.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives figures for how tenancy worked across dynasties: rents of about half the harvest in the Han and Tang, tenant households of about one third of registered households in the Song (about half the peasantry counting small owners who also rented), a 1027 Song rule requiring a written contract, and a shift from harvest-share to fixed rent by the Qing. It also gives 1930s shares of tenants with eternal-use rights (44 percent in Anhui, 40 in Jiangsu, 30 in Zhejiang), the rent stations of Jiangsu and Zhejiang in the later 19th century, and notes that the fixed rent was most widespread in the lower Yangtze region under the Song. It is a survey of some 3,000 years in which the figures are national or provincial, drawn from other encyclopedias, so a number is not tied to a place or year of the setting.
