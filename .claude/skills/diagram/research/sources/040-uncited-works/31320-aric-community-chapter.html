aric-community-chapter

Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), "Community chapter - History of Community Development in Japan (Primitive-Ancient Period)", in 水土里の軌跡 ("The trajectory of water, land and community", title translated), English edition (in English; https://aric.or.jp/kiseki/en/sato/index.html)

What it is: The community chapter of a web series on Japan's water, land and community, a re-edited magazine article that runs from the Jomon settlement and rice cultivation through the manorial period, the Muromachi self-governing villages and the Edo-period village to the postwar land reform and rural policy since 1965. It is a short narrative history with a list of eight references and no notes at the assertion.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Edo and Muromachi sections state, for the village the setting is modeled on, that the shogunate fixed village boundaries by irrigation water and common land, that Japan had about 70 thousand villages with 80% of the population in them, that the village was run by three officers with five-family groups, that cultivated area doubled from about 1.5 to about 3 million ha in the first 100 years of Edo, and that the village shrine wood (chinju no mori) was a place of gathering. Those figures carry no note at the point they are made and the chapter is a summary of the whole country, so its generalities hold for no particular region, and most of its length is on Meiji and postwar change.
