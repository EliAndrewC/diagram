kotobank-mihono-matsubara

"三保の松原" ("Miho no Matsubara", the pine grove of Miho, title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (selected edition), the World Encyclopedia (revised edition, signed Kitagawa Mitsuo), the Britannica International Encyclopedia and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/道切り-139424)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the Miho pine grove, a scenic site on the Miho sand spit in Shimizu ward, Shizuoka. It describes the spit, its pines, the Miho Shrine and the Hagoromo pine, with its listing as a national place of scenic beauty and a World Heritage component.

Why it applies, and its limits: The World Encyclopedia entry says the spit was built from gravel carried by the coastal current, that the pines were planted along the beach, and that the Miho Shrine and the Hagoromo pine stand within the grove, and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry calls the pines a windbreak forest, the Britannica entry a windbreak and sand-break forest. It is a scenic-site description of one place on Suruga Bay, giving no dates for the planting, no village use and no measurements beyond a grove area of about 34 hectares in the Daijisen Plus entry, which is the present grove's area as listed today with its World Heritage registration, beside a harbor side the World Encyclopedia says is now reclaimed land and factories.
