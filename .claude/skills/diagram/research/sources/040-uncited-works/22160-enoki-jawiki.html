enoki-jawiki

ja.wikipedia エノキ ("enoki", the Chinese hackberry, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/エノキ)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the enoki (Celtis sinensis), a deciduous tree. It covers the tree's form, range, wood, uses and lore, including its planting at roadside mile markers and shrines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the enoki grows to about 25 m with a wide crown, is planted for shade at mile mounds (ichirizuka) and shrines, that the Edo-period mile-mound tree is said to be ordered by Tokugawa Hidetada, that it ranges over Japan, the Korean peninsula and central China, and that it is sometimes a sacred tree or planted as a grave marker. The mile-mound origin is given as a tradition with a rival story, and the shrine-tree and fox-fire lore is local legend, not a count of how often villages planted it.
