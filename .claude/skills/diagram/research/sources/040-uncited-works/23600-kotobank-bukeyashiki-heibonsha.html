kotobank-bukeyashiki-heibonsha

"武家屋敷" ("buke yashiki", samurai residence, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (concise edition), the Historical Place-Name Encyclopedia of Japan, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, the Nipponica and the Encyclopedia of Japan's Tourist Resources, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%AD%A6%E5%AE%B6%E5%B1%8B%E6%95%B7-128023)

What it is: A Kotobank page with several entries on the buke yashiki, the residence a samurai held from his lord in a castle town. The two long ones, the World Encyclopedia's (by Suzuki Mitsuru) and the Nipponica's (by Fujino Tamotsu), describe its layout, gate and fence, and its place in the town.

Why it applies, and its limits: The World Encyclopedia entry says that in a provincial castle town the house stood on a plot large for its size, was about the size of a prosperous farmer's house and differed from a farmhouse in having a formal entrance with a step-platform and a smaller earth-floored area, with fences of earthen wall, board wall or hedge; lower ranks such as ashigaru lived in row houses. The Nipponica entry gives the town ratios (Nagaoka 125 samurai blocks to 34 townsman blocks, Kanazawa samurai land three times the town land, Kagoshima seven to three) and places upper ranks near the bailey and ashigaru at the edge. These ratios are for named domains, and the entry's claim that provincial castle towns followed Edo's pattern is the encyclopedia's own supposition (it says they were 'thought' to), and it says surviving buildings are very few, found only in small provincial towns such as Iwakuni, Hagi, Sakura and Kakunodate, so its account of the houses rests on a handful of survivors.
