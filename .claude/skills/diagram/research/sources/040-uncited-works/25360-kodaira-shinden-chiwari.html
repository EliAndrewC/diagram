kodaira-shinden-chiwari

Kodaira City, "小平の土地の形状と暮らし(小平市の昔話)" ("The shape of Kodaira's land and life on it (old tales of Kodaira)", title translated), Kodaira City Hall website, reprinted from the city bulletin of 1 January 2012 (in Japanese; https://www.city.kodaira.tokyo.jp/kurashi/086/086882.html)

What it is: A city page on how the Edo-period new-field (shinden) land division in Kodaira, Tokyo, shaped daily life, told in the voice of an elderly woman: strip-shaped holdings running from the main road back to the Tamagawa Aqueduct, with house plot, small ditch, bamboo grove, a lane, fields with tea-bush rows and a coppice behind. It also retells how families fled into the bamboo grove in the 1923 Kanto earthquake.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names each band of a shinden holding in order from the road and what it was used for: house with barn, storehouse and outdoor privy, a water channel, a bamboo grove that gave shoots, baskets and shelter from the north wind and quake, then fields and a coppice (yama) that supplied leaf mold and firewood; that supports the order of bands on a Musashino shinden strip. The speaker recalls the 1950s and 1960s and her parents' accounts of 1923, and the narration is a retelling in a bulletin, and the narrator is the bulletin's way of describing the district's usual layout (she says nearly every house had the same division), so it is a city promotion piece with no survey or sources behind it, and it gives no measurements.
