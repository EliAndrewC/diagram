kodaira-shinden-chiwari

Kodaira City, "小平の土地の形状と暮らし(小平市の昔話)" ("The shape of Kodaira's land and life on it (old tales of Kodaira)", title translated), Kodaira City Hall website, reprinted from the city bulletin of 1 January 2012 (in Japanese; https://www.city.kodaira.tokyo.jp/kurashi/086/086882.html)

What it is: A city page on how the Edo-period new-field (shinden) land division in Kodaira, Tokyo, shaped daily life, told in the voice of an elderly woman: strip-shaped holdings running from the main road back to the Tamagawa Aqueduct, with house plot, small ditch, bamboo grove, a lane, fields with tea-bush rows and a coppice behind. It also retells how families fled into the bamboo grove in the 1923 Kanto earthquake.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names each band of a shinden holding in order from the road and what it was used for: house with barn, storehouse and outdoor privy, a water channel, a bamboo grove that gave shoots, baskets and shelter from the north wind and quake, then fields and a coppice (yama) that supplied leaf mold and firewood; that supports the order of bands on a Musashino shinden strip. The layout is the one the narrator remembers from fifty or sixty years before 2012 (the 1950s and early 1960s), with her parents' accounts of 1923. She says nearly every house had the same division and that it was the Edo-period shinden division, but the page shows no Edo plan or record behind that. The narrator, "Grandma Tama", is the bulletin's device for presenting the district's usual layout, not an interviewed informant, and the page gives no measurements of the strip or its bands.
