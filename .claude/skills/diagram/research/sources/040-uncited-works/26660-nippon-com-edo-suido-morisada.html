nippon.com, "水道を張り巡らせたハイテク都市・江戸の水事情 : 『守貞漫稿』（その7）" ("Edo, the high-tech city laced with waterworks: its water, from the Morisada Manko, part 7", title translated), 21 February 2021 (in Japanese; https://www.nippon.com/ja/japan-topics/g01041/)

What it is: A magazine article on Edo's water supply, using the late-Edo Morisada Manko and the 1791 Josuiki as sources. It describes buried stone and wooden pipes feeding shared neighborhood wells, the yearly well cleaning and the Ochanomizu aqueduct.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Edo's wells held water piped in from the aqueducts, because wells dug in the low ground near the old Hibiya inlet were too salty to drink, that pipe sections were 2 to 3 m long, and that townspeople cleaned each well together on the seventh day of the seventh lunar month under a specialist. It describes Edo only, a city with an aqueduct system, so it does not say how a smaller town or a village got its water.
