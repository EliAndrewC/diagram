tokuoka-hosogi-2012

Yoshinori Tokuoka and Daisuke Hosogi, "Spatial distribution and management of isolated woody plants traditionally used as farmland boundary markers in Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan", SpringerPlus 1:57 (2012), on PubMed Central (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3568473/)

What it is: The full text of an open-access paper surveying isolated trees and shrubs that mark the boundaries between upland fields in Ibaraki Prefecture, eastern Kanto, in 2011. It counts 2001 plants of 50 species at 177 sampled points and interviews 32 farm managers about how the markers are kept.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that a boundary between dry fields can be marked by single shrubs kept small by trimming, mostly Deutzia crenata (60.7 percent), and that the species varies by plateau. Its evidence is a 2011 survey of managers aged over 50, who mostly could say only that their markers had been kept about half a century and were probably planted by their parents, so it shows that the form survives, not how old it is; it covers upland vegetable fields, not paddy.
