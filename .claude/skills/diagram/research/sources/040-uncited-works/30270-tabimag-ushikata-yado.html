tabimag-ushikata-yado

Pressman Union editors (プレスマンユニオン編集部), "牛方宿" ("ushikata-yado", the cattle drovers' inn, title translated), Nippon Tabi Magazine (ニッポン旅マガジン) (in Japanese; https://tabi-mag.jp/nn130)

What it is: A travel-magazine article on the Ushikata-yado at Kutsukake in Otari village, Nagano, a thatched inn on the Chikuni road (the Salt Road from Itoigawa to Matsumoto) where cattle drovers and their cattle slept under one roof. It gives the house's frontage and depth, its interior, the drovers' loads and season, and a reconstruction from an 1864 floor plan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a working Edo-period inn for pack cattle in a snowy mountain district: a large thatched house 5.5 ken wide and 8 ken deep with an earthen-floored space for the cattle and a rear parlor for merchants, built to bear heavy winter snow, with each drover driving six cattle carrying two bales (94 kg) apiece from about May to the end of October. The house is dated only by inference to the late 18th or early 19th century and was restored from the 1864 plan, the article does not say how many cattle the stable held, and it describes a freight route, not a farming village.
