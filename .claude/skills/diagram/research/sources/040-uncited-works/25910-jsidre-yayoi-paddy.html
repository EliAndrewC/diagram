jsidre-yayoi-paddy

Japanese Society of Irrigation, Drainage and Rural Engineering (農業農村工学会), "水田の誕生，弥生水田" ("The birth of the paddy: Yayoi paddies", title translated), in its series on the history of regional development (地域づくりの歴史) (in Japanese; https://www.jsidre.or.jp/1-c/)

What it is: A short essay on a learned society's site on the earliest Japanese paddies, from the Jomon to the Kofun period, as found by excavation. It covers their layouts, where they were sited and how floods buried them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says excavated paddies from the Yayoi and Kofun periods took three layouts according to ground: large plots on low ground as at Toro, plots on gentle slopes from a rise down into wet ground, and small plots in a grid over flat land. It adds that plots run small where the ground is uneven and large where flat, that paddies began behind coastal dunes and natural levees and then spread to higher ground, and that one site often shows paddies of different forms buried one above another by floods. It dates the first bunded and sluiced paddies to the late Jomon and cites no site figures, so it supports early forms and their reasons, not the sizes or layouts of paddies in a later village.
