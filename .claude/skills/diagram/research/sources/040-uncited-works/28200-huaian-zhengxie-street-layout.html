huaian-zhengxie-street-layout

Ye Zhan'ao, "明清时期淮安府城街道市井" ("The streets and markets of Huai'an prefectural city in the Ming and Qing", title translated), Huai'an local history column of the Huai'an CPPCC history site, from the WeChat account Wenshi Huai'an, dated 2026-04-28 (in Chinese; https://zx.huaian.gov.cn/col/14069_277241/art/17749728/1776912943096qsazvHfO.html)

What it is: An article on the Huai'an CPPCC (political consultative conference) local-history site describing how the prefectural city of Huai'an, Jiangsu, was laid out in Ming and Qing times: three walled cities, a grid of main streets named for direction or for offices, the satellite towns of Hexia and Xiaguan, and then the form of its houses, from small two-courtyard homes to a mansion of some sixty mu.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives named street patterns (a central long street in three stages, east and west long streets, gate streets, streets named for the garrison, prefectural and county offices) from the Zhengde and Wanli gazetteers, and house plans such as three bays to a courtyard, seven purlins deep, eaves 3 to 4 meters high, with a lowered eave and a veranda against the wet climate. It is a modern summary of one Huai'an city on the Grand Canal, reproduced with no notes, and its house forms come from surviving buildings and late-Qing photographs (its one dated mansion is of 1882), two paragraphs are about twentieth-century widening and tree planting, and it calls the houses Jianghuai style, between the Yangtze and the Huai, a Hui style simplified and turned northern, so it speaks for that canal region and not for the dry north or the far south.
