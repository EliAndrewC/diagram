ashikaga-gakko-ato

Ashikaga City, "足利学校跡（聖廟及び附属建物を含む）" ("Ruins of the Ashikaga School, including the Confucian shrine and its annexes", title translated), on the Ashikaga City website (in Japanese; https://www.city.ashikaga.tochigi.jp/education/000029/000169/000627/p001274.html)

What it is: A municipal page, posted in 2023, on the Ashikaga School site (Tochigi), which it calls the oldest school site in Japan: its moat-and-earthwork enclosure, its area, its history from 1439 to its closure in 1872, and its 1990 restoration.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a school ground as a trapezoidal compound enclosed by earthen banks and a moat of more than 17,000 square meters next to the Banna-ji temple, supported by its 1982 to 1988 excavation of the eastern half, which makes it a measured anchor for a large school's ground and its Confucian shrine and gates. Its founding is, by the page's own words, uncertain; its peak was in the Sengoku period; it was a school of national fame, so it is no ordinary one; and the buildings now standing are a 1990 restoration to a mid-Edo form from excavation and old pictures.
