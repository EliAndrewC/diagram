en.wikipedia "Lingyin Temple" (the Chan Buddhist temple near Hangzhou, Zhejiang, title translated) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingyin_Temple)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Lingyin Temple, a Chan Buddhist monastery near Hangzhou, covering its tradition of founding in 328, its history through the Wuyue kingdom, Southern Song and Qing, its surroundings with the Feilai Feng rock carvings, and its hall-by-hall layout along the main axis.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, for a great southern Chinese Chan monastery, the scale it reached under Wuyue (18 pavilions, 72 halls, over 1300 dormitory rooms, more than 3000 monks) and the five-hall main axis of a Song Chan temple running up a hillside, with the carvings of Feilai Feng in front. Its hall dimensions and statues are mostly modern rebuilding: the page says the present buildings are largely late Qing restorations, the five-hall axis is a recent recreation of which only the front three halls belong to the Qing axis, and the last two halls were built in 2000-2002. The Wuyue counts are stated without a source on the page, and the Chinese article gives different ones (nine towers, seventy-seven halls, no dormitory count) and a founding in 326 rather than 328.
