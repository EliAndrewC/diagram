Wang Qiming, "诗情画意的江南水乡民居" ("Poetic Jiangnan water-town dwellings", title translated), in Splendid Chinese Civilization (灿烂的中国文明), Chinese Culture Research Institute, Hong Kong (in Chinese; https://chiculture.org.hk/sc/china-five-thousand-years/693)

What it is: A short illustrated article by a professor, posted in 2018 on a Hong Kong educational site about Chinese culture, describing the houses of the Jiangnan water towns of southern Jiangsu and northern Zhejiang and naming Zhouzhuang, Tongli, Mudu, Xitang, Nanxun and Wuzhen.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes how Jiangnan houses sit along the water: one side on the river with a landing stage, the other on the street, with stone embankments, bridgeheads as the busiest spots, and, in the commercial towns, shop-in-front dwellings under a roofed arcade (lang peng) along the river. Its description is of the surviving old towns, which it says are few, so it carries no date for the forms it names. It gives no measurements and no village-scale layout.
