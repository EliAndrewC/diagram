kyotofukoh-kamigamo-shakemachi

Kyotofukoh (京都風光), "上賀茂社家町・明神川 (京都市北区)" ("Kamigamo shake-machi, Myōjin-gawa, Kyoto-shi Kita-ku", the shrine priests' town of Kamigamo and the Myojin river, Kita Ward, Kyoto, title translated), on the Kyotofukoh site (in Japanese; https://kyotofukoh.jp/report3.html)

What it is: A Kyoto sightseeing site's page on the preserved district of hereditary priests' houses (shake) beside Kamigamo Shrine in northern Kyoto, with a dated timeline, its houses, the Myojin river that runs through the gardens, and the local pickled turnip (suguki) grown in them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries dated figures for a shrine settlement: 200 priest houses and 270 commoner (jige) houses in the late 17th century, 148 priest houses in the An'ei years (1772-1781), a 1719 map showing priest and farm houses alike thatched and hip-gabled, a 4.5 km channel feeding each house's garden, bridges 2 m wide, one per house, and suguki sown in the gardens in late August and harvested in late November. It describes a district beside one of Kyoto's great shrines whose households were priests, not ordinary farmers, much of its early history is tradition (it gives several origins of the turnip), and its dates are attributed only to maps and a closing list of references, not claim by claim.
