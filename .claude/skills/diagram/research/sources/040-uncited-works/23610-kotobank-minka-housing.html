kotobank-minka-housing

"民家" ("minka", the ordinary people's house, title translated), entries from the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (Suzuki Mitsuru on Japan), Shogakukan's Nipponica (Hirai Kiyoshi) and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%91%E5%AE%B6-171234)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering several dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the minka, the common person's house, which the Heibonsha and Nipponica entries divide into farmhouses and townhouses. The Japan sections cover roof, wall and plan types, the regional forms, the oldest surviving houses, and how the farmhouse plan grew from a single room to the four-room layout; short sections add China, Korea and Europe.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives for a premodern Japanese village the house forms of its farms: the dirt-floored doma beside plank or mat rooms, the three-room and four-room plans, thatch and stone-weighted board roofs, and that many Edo-period houses stood on posts set in the ground. The Nipponica entry cites an Edo-period survey of the villages near Utsunomiya in which a house of about 20 tsubo (about 66 square meters) was commonest and many were under 10 tsubo. It is regional only in a list of named types, so it does not say which form belongs to which kind of village, and the oldest surviving farmhouses it names are of the Muromachi period, so the earlier forms are inferred from scroll paintings and excavations.
