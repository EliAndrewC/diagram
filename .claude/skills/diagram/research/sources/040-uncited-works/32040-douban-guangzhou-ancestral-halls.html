douban-guangzhou-ancestral-halls

润仁儿～ (a Douban reader), reading notes quoting chapter 5, "第五章 广州府祠堂形制的渊源和流变" ("chapter 5, the origins and development of the form of ancestral halls in Guangzhou prefecture", title translated) of the book 祖先之翼 ("Wings of the Ancestors", title translated), on Douban Books, 2022 (in Chinese; https://book.douban.com/annotation/119571892)

What it is: A reader's annotation page on Douban that copies page-marked passages from a scholarly book on the ancestral halls (citang) of Guangzhou prefecture: the hall's buildings, its courtyards, the numbers of axes and halls in a layout, the gable forms and the rites held there.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries a classification of Guangzhou-prefecture halls by the number of axes (one, three or five) and of halls in depth (two to seven), a hall with a three-bay gate being the commonest, and a note that halls were often set to the east of the house or village; it also quotes the Daming huidian rules on the hall's rooms and its rites, and a Qing record of a ban on joint-lineage halls in provincial cities. It is a reader's excerpts, not the book: the page does not name the author or publisher, the page numbers are the reader's, and the passages describe the lineage halls of one prefecture, Guangzhou (Shunde, Nanhai, Panyu, Dongguan and nearby counties), in the Ming and Qing, so its counts of axes, halls and bays speak for that prefecture first.
