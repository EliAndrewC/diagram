kasukabe-yagisaki-chimei

Enomoto, Kasukabe Local History Museum, "#かすかべ地名の話 (7) #八木崎" ("Kasukabe place-name stories (7): Yagisaki", title translated), on the Hogo-log blog of the Kasukabe City Board of Education portal (in Japanese; https://schit.net/kasukabe/center/blogs/blog_entries/view/220/8851d000b58a17687a146fcd5c6b7523?frame_id=392)

What it is: A museum staff blog post of 8 March 2025 on the origin of the place name Yagisaki, a small district of Kasukabe (Kasukabe-juku, Saitama), with its Edo-period farmland and a Meiji-era account of the name.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records that Yagisaki's Edo-period farmland, listed in the 1697 survey register, was 8.8 cho of paddy and 29.5 cho of dry fields, mostly dry, within a post town where some households held shares in the post-horse service, and that place names ending in -saki mark the tips of high ground. The name's origin (eight large cedars by the shore) is an old man's tale quoted from an early Meiji text, which the author treats as tradition, and the page is about one district beside a highway post town, not a typical farm village.
