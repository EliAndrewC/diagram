kumagaya-teitetsu-memo-33

Kumagaya City Cultural Properties, "蹄鉄 覚書33 蹄鉄（ていてつ）" ("Notes 33: the horseshoe", title translated), in the museum's reading column (in Japanese; https://www.kumagaya-bunkazai.jp/museum/dokusyo/koramu/teitetu/teitetu.htm)

What it is: A city cultural-properties museum column on the horseshoe, written after a 2004 equestrian event: its European origin, the first Western-style shoeing in Japan, older Japanese hoof protection, shoe-change intervals and horseshoe lore.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that Japan had no nailed horseshoe in older times: the code of 701 told keepers to burn the hoof sole hard, and horses wore woven straw-fiber and other shoes, and that Western shoeing came with a Dutch farrier in 1725. It also gives shoe-change intervals for riding horses, draft horses and racehorses, which are those of modern horses and not of a premodern post-station.
