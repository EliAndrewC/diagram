taizhou-hailing-chenghe

Hailing District Place-Names Committee Office (海陵区地名委员会办公室), "城河" ("the city moat", title translated), in 地名文化 > 海陵区的河 (place-name culture, rivers of Hailing district), 28 April 2017 (in Chinese; https://hldm.05236.com/index.php?m=content&c=index&a=show&catid=35&id=89)

What it is: A four-paragraph entry from a district place-names office on the moat (chenghe) that surrounds the old walled city of Taizhou (Hailing), Jiangsu, giving its purpose, who dug it and its widths at the four corners.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the moat was dug at the start of the Southern Tang to add to the city's defense and to supply the city with water, was repaired by successive dynasties, and was last overhauled in the late Qianlong period, when it measured 270 m wide at the northeast corner, 281 m at the southeast, 260 m at the northwest and a printed "27 m" at the southwest, which looks like a dropped digit. The figures are a local office's, with no source and the one corner plainly garbled, and they describe a prefectural city's moat after a Qing overhaul, which is far wider than the moat of a small walled place.
