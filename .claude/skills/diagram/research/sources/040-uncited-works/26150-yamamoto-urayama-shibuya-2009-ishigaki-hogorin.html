Yamamoto Takatsugu, Urayama Ryuichi and Shibuya Shizuaki, "石垣市・旧四箇村における「抱護林」の現状" ("Ishigaki-shi, kyu Shika-mura ni okeru 'hogorin' no genjo", The present state of the 'hogorin' of the former Shika village, Ishigaki City, title translated), abstract for the Association of Japanese Geographers autumn meeting, 2009 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ajg/2009f/0/2009f_0_96/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A conference abstract, in full on J-STAGE, on the hogorin (belt of trees around a whole village) of the four old villages that are now Ishigaki's town center, Yaeyama, and how it disappeared after the Second World War.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the belt enclosed the village, marked its territory on village maps, and was traced to Sai On's directions, and that by the Edo-period village maps it was already unclear at the east and west ends; the north belt and the four corners, where it met the sea as sacred groves with wells, lasted longest because their plots were recognized as common land. The evidence is cadastral plots and US photographs of the twentieth century, and it records the loss, not the belt's width or species.
