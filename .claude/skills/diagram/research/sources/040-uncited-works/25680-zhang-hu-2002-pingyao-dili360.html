Zhang Guotian and Hu Gangfeng, "平遥 中国明清古城的原型" ("Pingyao, the prototype of the Ming-Qing walled city of China", title translated), China National Geography, 2002 no. 6, on the magazine's website (in Chinese; http://www.dili360.com/cng/article/p5350c3d6ab53220.htm)

What it is: A magazine article on Pingyao's walled town in Shanxi, describing its wall, its barbican gates, its six gates and the turtle shape its plan is said to take. The web page carries only the opening part of the article.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the wall's circuit as 6,157.7 m and says the wall is rammed earth faced wholly in brick, rebuilt and brick-clad in the early Ming, with a barbican at each gate whose doors open at the sides and an inner and an outer gate. It names the six gates with their turtle symbolism, and for the wall's origin cites the 1882 county gazetteer's claim of a Zhou-era founding, which the article itself says the histories do not confirm. The page is cut off before the rest of the article, and the symbolism is the article's reading.
