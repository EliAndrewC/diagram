kotobank-tenmacho

"伝馬町" ("tenmacho", the post-horse quarter, title translated), entries in the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha Historical Place Names of Japan (on a dozen castle-town Tenmacho), the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Matsuzaki Kinichi) and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BC%9D%E9%A6%AC%E7%94%BA-578978)

What it is: A set of entries on tenmacho, the quarter of an Edo-period castle town or post town charged with supplying post horses and porters, with place-name entries on those of Hikone, Nagoya, Utsunomiya, Okazaki, Ogaki, Kuwana, Sakata, Hamamatsu, Osaka, Sumpu, Iida, Mishima and Fukui. The Heibonsha encyclopedia entry covers Edo's three tenmacho (Odenma, Minami and Kodenma) and their duties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the post-horse quarter as a street of the town's main road, usually near the castle gate, whose householders paid its duty by frontage, and it gives dated cases: in Hikone's Tenmacho in 1665 there were 41 house-owners and 63 tenants, and plots about 13 ken deep with frontages from 2 to 13 ken that set the duty owed. The place-name entries are shortened for Kotobank and each dates its own street (about 1590 to 1700), so one town's count or frontage rule is not another's.
