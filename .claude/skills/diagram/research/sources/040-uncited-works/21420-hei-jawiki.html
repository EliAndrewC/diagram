hei-jawiki

ja.wikipedia "塀" ("hei", wall or fence, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A1%80)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the hei, the solid enclosure around a house or site, sorted by material (earth, stone, board, brick, concrete) and followed by a list of Japanese types and the modern building-code rules for block walls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It distinguishes a hei, a continuous wall that cannot be seen through, from a kaki such as a hedge, and names the traditional forms (tsuijibei, the earthen wall with a roofed top; itabei, the board wall; tateitabei, the vertical-board wall), though it does not say which compounds used which, only that roofed karabei went to temples and mausoleums and lattice-topped sukibei to shrines, adding that earth and stone walls rest on civil-engineering technique and board walls on garden technique. It gives no dimensions for those traditional walls: the only heights and thicknesses it states belong to concrete-block and stone or brick masonry walls under the modern Building Standards Act rules, which do not apply to the setting.
