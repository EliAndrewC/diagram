ja.wikipedia 水車小屋 ("suisha goya", the watermill house, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B0%B4%E8%BB%8A%E5%B0%8F%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the watermill, covering its history in the West, China and the Islamic world, how a mill works, its uses and a few Japanese examples; most of it is about Europe.

Why it applies, and its limits: For China it gives dated mentions (water-driven pestles by about 20 CE, a bellows in 30 CE, a Tang law of 737 that mills must not block waterways, a mill of five wheels in 748) and says one theory has the technology reaching Japan in 610 or 670. It says polishing rice by water-driven pestle spread in Japan from the mid-Edo period, mainly in cities. Its long Roman and medieval European sections are not about East Asia, and it gives no Japanese mill size or layout.
