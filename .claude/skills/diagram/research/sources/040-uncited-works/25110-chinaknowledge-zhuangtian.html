chinaknowledge-zhuangtian

Ulrich Theobald, "zhuangtian 莊田" (manors or large estates), in ChinaKnowledge.de - An Encyclopaedia on Chinese History, Literature and Art (www.chinaknowledge.de) (http://www.chinaknowledge.de/History/Terms/zhuangtian.html)

What it is: An English-language encyclopedia entry by one named author on the Chinese manor (zhuangtian), the large estate held by the imperial house, nobles, officials and monasteries, with its names, owners, tenants and rents from the Northern Dynasties through the Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives manor sizes (from several mu to twenty or thirty qing in the Tang), rents (often half the harvest, and 2.5 to 4.1 dou of grain per mu under the Kangxi reign, after 2 dou per mu at Yanzhou and Linqing in 1480), the tenant manager, and the mills, smithies, shops and pleasure gardens a Tang manor held, which supports a landlord's estate as a working holding with its own trades. It is a survey of nearly fifteen centuries, so its figures belong to named reigns and the entry does not say which apply to a given place; its focus is the estates of the court and nobility, not a village landlord's holding.
