chaya-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 茶屋 ("chaya", the teahouse, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%96%99%E7%90%86%E8%8C%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the chaya, the shop serving tea, food and rest, followed from its medieval beginnings as a pedestrian tea seller at temple gates to its Edo-period kinds on post roads, at passes and in towns. It also lists place names that keep the word.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the earliest form to the Oei era of the early fifteenth century, citing a 1403 Toji document naming a tea seller before the south gate, and says the early seller carried his kettle and pails on a pole with no fixed shop, with roadside stalls and later booths built at temple and shrine gates following. It names the Edo kinds by site (tateba-chaya, mizu-chaya at stations and passes, ryori chaya serving meals) but gives no sizes or plans of a building, and its Osaka distribution paragraph rests on a single 2024 study by Sugimoto.
