zh.wikipedia 康百万庄园 ("Kang Baiwan zhuangyuan", the Kang Baiwan manor, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/康百万庄园)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Kang Baiwan manor in Gongyi, Henan, a walled landlord estate begun in the late Ming and early Qing, with its history, its layout in some ten districts and its surviving counts of courtyards, houses and cave dwellings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a large merchant-landlord compound that backs onto a ridge and faces a river, with dwellings, a granary, workshops, vegetable and orchard grounds, a shrine and a stage in one walled place, and gives a total floor area of 64,300 square meters over 33 courtyards. The family's wealth came from salt, cloth and boats as well as land (the page gives its 3,000 boats as what later people say), so it is the upper bound of a Henan landholder's seat, and the page says the stock is of the Ming and Qing with its main buildings Qing.
