Kamiyu Rekishi Henshubu (Kamiyu history editorial staff), "超入門！お城セミナー 第88回【歴史】城下町はどうやって敵から守られていたの？" ("Super introduction! Castle seminar no. 88, History: how was a castle town protected from enemies?", title translated), on Shirobito, 13 April 2020 (in Japanese; https://shirobito.jp/article/1056)

What it is: A beginner's article on the soto-gamae, the moat and earthen bank line that enclosed a whole castle town, on a castle-fan site backed by the Japan Castle Association. It says what a soto-gamae was, contrasts it with the stone city walls of Europe and China, cites the sieges of Odawara and Osaka Winter as tests, ranks the longest three, and lists the castles known for one.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a castle town being ringed by a moat and bank with a few gated openings (not a wall), and says earlier town defenses were rivers, the street plan and a belt of temples at the edge. It speaks for the large castle towns of the late sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, giving round lengths for Edo, Odawara and Osaka only, and says a soto-gamae cost great labor, so not every lord built one.
