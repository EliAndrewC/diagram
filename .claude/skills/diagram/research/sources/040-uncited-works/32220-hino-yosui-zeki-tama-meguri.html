Aichan, "450年余の長きにわたって取水に携わる多摩川の堰～日野用水堰" ("The Tama River weir that has drawn water for over 450 years: the Hino irrigation weir", title translated), Tama Meguri blog, 2022-07-04 (in Japanese; https://tama-meguri.com/blog/2022/07/04/5/15224)

What it is: A local blog post on the Hino irrigation channel and its weir on the Tama River in Tokyo, covering the channel's cutting in 1567, its two branches and intakes recorded in 1684, and the weir's rebuilding in 1962.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a river-drawn irrigation channel feeding a district of rice paddies in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries: it names the channel's founder and date, the use of convict labor, two intakes that moved with floods, and a weir of stacked gabion baskets still standard into the Showa period. It is a blog citing a city exhibition and Ministry materials, and it says the early channel's course and weir are not known, so its description of the older works is thin.
