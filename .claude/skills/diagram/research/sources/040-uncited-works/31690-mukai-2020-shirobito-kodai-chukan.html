Mukai Kazuo (向井一雄), "古代城郭教室（Ⅴ）　中世城館はどのように誕生したのか？" ("Ancient castle classroom (5): how did the medieval castle-residence come to be?", title translated), 2020/10/30, in the series 古代城郭教室, on Shirobito (城びと), a site certified by the Japan Castle Foundation (in Japanese; https://shirobito.jp/article/1118)

What it is: A signed final installment of a web series by the head of the Ancient Mountain Fortress Research Society, surveying how Japan's medieval castles and moated residences arose, from the defended settlements of northern Tohoku to the square residences of the Heian and Kamakura periods. It gives excavated sites with their dimensions and dates and ends with a reference list of books.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured sites that bear on a moated residence compound: square plots of about 50 to 120 m on a side, ditches of only 1 to 2 m in the Heian examples, and the statement that the moat of a square residence may also have served as an irrigation regulating pond. It also says that the surviving earthwork-and-moat residences mostly date from the 14th to 15th centuries, not the Kamakura period, so the article speaks for the early residence only through excavation, mostly of elite or office sites, and gives no size for an ordinary farm household.
