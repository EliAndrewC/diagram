zh.wikipedia 社稷坛 (altar of the soil and grain, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/社稷坛)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the she-ji altar, where the gods of soil and grain were worshiped, with its place in capital planning and its history under the Ming.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the altar stood to the right of the ancestral temple in a capital by the Rites of Zhou rule, that Yuan Dadu set it inside the Pingze gate, and that the Ming merged the two altars into one in 1377, with three imperial altars and altars at princely seats. It covers imperial and princely sites only, says nothing of the altars a county or village kept, and cites no source.
