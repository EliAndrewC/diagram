ranhaku-kenchi-kanbun

Ranzan Town (嵐山町), "検地帳の記録" ("The records of the land survey registers", title translated), from the History of Ranzan Town (嵐山町誌, 1968), pp. 58-65, in the Ranzan Town Web Museum, vol. 6 (嵐山町web博物誌), on the Ranzan town site (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/10_017.html)

What it is: A section of the 1968 history of Ranzan town, Saitama, that lists the totals by land grade from the land surveys (kenchi) of the villages of the former Sugaya village area, mostly in 1665 and 1668 (Kanbun 5 and 8). It then explains the Edo survey rules and the terms for old and new surveys.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, village by village, the area in each grade of paddy (upper, middle, lower, lowest), dry field, house plot (yashiki), hill woodland, thatch land and mowing ground; for Sugaya village the dry fields run to tens of cho against a few cho of paddy, and Kamagata's common mowing ground is 22 cho 1 tan (a 1740 register). It also notes that two cover dates were rewritten in 1679 after old registers decayed, and that Hirasawa's survives only as a copy after a fire in 1879, and it argues the Kanbun surveys can be taken to follow the later Kyoho survey rules. It is one area of Musashi. The totals are not read from the survey registers themselves but copied from a later compilation the page calls the "Enkaku" (沿革), which mentions the 1879 fire and so was written after it. The argument about survey rules is the editors' own inference, and the figures are survey totals by grade, not harvests.
