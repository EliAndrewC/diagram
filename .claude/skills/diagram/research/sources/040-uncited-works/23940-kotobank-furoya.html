kotobank-furoya

"風呂屋" ("furoya", bathhouse), in the Digital Daijisen, the Concise Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Nipponica and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A2%A8%E5%91%82%E5%B1%8B-622811)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering dictionary entries on furoya, which meant a bathroom, a fee-charging public bath, a bath-tub shop and, in the Edo period, a bathhouse with bath women, with citations from 1593 to 1842.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records that the word covered both a bathroom and a public bathhouse, that an encyclopedia excerpt says steam baths were once called furoya and hot-water baths yuya and clearly told apart, and that a bathhouse shows in a Muromachi-end picture of Kyoto. The senses shifted over time, so the page does not say which type stood in a given town or when, and it gives no plan, size or fee beyond the definitions.
