kotobank-goyokiki

"御用聞き" ("goyokiki, the order-taker", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/御用聞き-505051)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for goyokiki, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nipponica and the Heibonsha encyclopedia on the word's three senses: a tradesman going round customers to take orders, a merchant supplying a shogunal or domain household, and an Edo-period constable's private deputy (okappiki).

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that in the Edo period the word named both a door-to-door order-taker (rice and sake dealers) and a supplier to the shogunate or domains, with dated citations from 1678 and 1714. It describes a role, gives no place, count or scale, and the page's own mentions of the order-taking sense are not tied to any period before the Edo citations.
