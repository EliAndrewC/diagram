kotobank-kasoba

"火葬場" ("kasoba", the cremation ground, title translated), entries from Shogakukan's Nipponica (Asaka Shosuke) and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%81%AB%E8%91%AC%E5%A0%B4-463108)

What it is: A Kotobank page of dictionary entries on the cremation ground. The Nipponica entry traces its names (sanmai, hiya, yakiba) from the Heian period, names the five cremation grounds of Edo and the seven graves of Osaka, and follows modern crematoria from the first modern furnace of 1878.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the premodern words for a cremation ground and where the cities put them: Edo's five sanmai at Kozukappara, Sendagaya, Kirigaya, Shibuya and Horokushinden, and Osaka's seven graves including Nagara, Umeda and Sennichi. It says that wood fuel was the rule until the modern furnaces, with the bones picked up on the next day. It names sites for two cities only and says nothing about a village's own burning place, and its latter half is about modern crematoria.
