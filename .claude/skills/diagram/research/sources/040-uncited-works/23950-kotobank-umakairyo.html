kotobank-umakairyo

"馬飼料" ("umakairyo", horse feed), in the Concise Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A6%AC%E9%A3%BC%E6%96%99-2011818)

What it is: A Kotobank dictionary entry on umakairyo, whose four senses are a medieval estate levy, the feed money paid to the shogunate's stable men, an allowance to Edo fire-brigade captains, and feed issued to a domain's horse-owning samurai in an emergency.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows horse feed being budgeted in money and grain: a fire-brigade captain's allowance included three sho of soybeans a day, and a domain in 1799 paid 14 ryo a horse in place of feed rice. Each sense is an administrative payment, so the page gives no feeding rations for a farm horse or stable layout, and its figures are individual allowances in single places and years.
