okinawa-kyodoson-shuraku

Ocean Expo Park, "集落の成り立ち" ("how a settlement is formed", title translated), in "郷土村に学ぶ" (Learning from the Okinawa folk village), Okinawa Kyodoson Omoro Botanical Garden, Kaiyohaku Park official site (in Japanese; https://oki-park.jp/kaiyohaku/inst/80/81)

What it is: A short explanatory page on the national park's open-air Okinawan village, describing how a traditional Ryukyuan village is laid out: backed by a wooded hill, with the main house at the top, branch houses fanning out below, and a sacred utaki grove behind the main house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the layout rule of a traditional Okinawan village in a few sentences: the village sits on the south slope below a sacred wooded hill, with the head family at the top, secondary families beside and below it, and a path from the head house to the utaki. It is Ryukyuan, not mainland, and is undated and unillustrated in its text; it says the word 'village' came into use around the time of the Shimazu invasion but gives no layout dates or sizes.
