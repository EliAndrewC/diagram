Isabella Bird Bishop, "Korea and Her Neighbors: A Narrative of Travel, with an Account of the Recent Vicissitudes and Present Position of the Country" (preface by Walter C. Hillier), New York: Fleming H. Revell, 1898, as the Project Gutenberg eBook #69300 (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/69300/69300-h/69300-h.htm)

What it is: The full text of a British woman traveler's account of four visits to Korea between January 1894 and March 1897: a five-week sampan journey up the Han River, overland rides to the Diamond Mountain monasteries, the east coast, Song-do and Pyongyang, Seoul and its palace, Korean settlers in Russian Siberia, and Manchuria. Later chapters describe the reorganized government, schools, trade, finance, burial and marriage customs, and the spirit-worship the author calls daemonism or shamanism.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives eyewitness detail on how ordinary Koreans lived in the Joseon countryside and towns that no setting note supplies: mud-walled houses heated by floor flues and screened by a 6-foot bamboo or reed fence, a pigsty sturdier than the house, 176 villages counted along the Han between Seoul and Yongchun, rice terraces and water-powered pestles, graves on south slopes backed by pines, spirit shrines, trees and stone heaps at passes, and decayed yamen compounds and walled magistracy towns. Its evidence dates from 1894 to 1897, after Korea's ports opened in 1876, and it is a hurried, often disparaging outsider's view of the places on her routes, with population and distance figures that are her own estimates and several towns shown as they stood after the Sino-Japanese war.
