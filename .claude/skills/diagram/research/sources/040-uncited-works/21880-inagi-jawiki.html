ja.wikipedia "稲木" ("inagi", a rice-drying rack, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A8%B2%E6%9C%A8)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the inagi, the frame of posts and crossbars (also hasa, hazae, odagake) on which harvested rice is hung to dry in the sun, with its regional names and forms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the crossbars are set so the lowest sheaves clear the ground, that the frame stands in the harvested field, and that Iwami in Shimane uses a conical form against wind. It also names the hazagi, living trees such as alder used as the posts, as common in Niigata and once seen in Kanto. It gives no date for the practice, no sizes, and its one photograph is from about 1980; the page carries a notice that it lacks references.
