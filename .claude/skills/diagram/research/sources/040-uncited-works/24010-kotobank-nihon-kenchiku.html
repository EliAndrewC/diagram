kotobank-nihon-kenchiku

"日本建築" ("Nihon kenchiku", Japanese architecture, title translated), dictionary and encyclopedia entries from Nippon Daihyakka Zensho, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/日本建築-110146)

What it is: A Kotobank page whose saved text is the entry on Japanese architecture, with a long signed history in Nippon Daihyakka Zensho from prehistoric pit dwellings to the present and an Ota Hirotaro overview in the Heibonsha encyclopedia.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries general statements a plan could use: timber frames with walls as partitions, floors raised over a crawl space with a veranda around, deep eaves, hinoki used first and pine and cedar for houses later (cedar from the early modern period), and a sawyer's saw only from the mid-Muromachi period. It also describes the shoin-style room and the meeting hall (kaisho) of the Muromachi period and townhouses with plastered walls or earthen storehouses. It is a survey over twenty centuries and speaks of temples, shrines and elite houses far more than farmhouses, so it cannot date any one building form beyond its period headings, and it says nothing of a village's layout.
