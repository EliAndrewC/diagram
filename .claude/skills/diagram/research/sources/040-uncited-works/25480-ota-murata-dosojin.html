ota-murata-dosojin

Ota City, "村田の双体道祖神" ("The paired dosojin of Murata", title translated), Ota City Cultural Properties Division website (in Japanese; https://www.city.ota.gunma.jp/page/4417.html)

What it is: A city page on a carved stone pair of male and female dosojin (road-and-boundary guardian deities) in Murata, Ota, Gunma, dated 1822, with its size, its original position and a short account of what dosojin are and where paired ones are found.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says dosojin stood at village borders and crossroads to keep out epidemics and protect travelers and later took on roles in childbirth, marriage and the fields, that this stone (46 cm tall, 28 cm wide, 4-5 cm thick) stood at the end of an old road about 30 m east of its present spot, and that paired dosojin are common from Nagano to Gunma and western Kanagawa, with four known in Ota. It describes one small stone of 1822 and gives no count of shrines per village, so it supports siting at village edges, not density.
