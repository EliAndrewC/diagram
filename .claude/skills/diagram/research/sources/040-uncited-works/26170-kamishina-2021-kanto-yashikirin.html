kamishina-2021-kanto-yashikirin

Kamishina Yoshitaka, "関東平野における屋敷林・防風林の変化とその要因" ("Kanto heiya ni okeru yashikirin, bofurin no henka to sono yoin", Changes in homestead woods and windbreaks on the Kanto plain and their causes, title translated), abstract for the Association of Japanese Geographers autumn meeting, 2021 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ajg/2021a/0/2021a_106/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A conference abstract, in full on J-STAGE, reporting a survey of homestead woods in compact villages on the Kanto plain at the Saitama-Gunma border, with residents' names for each kind of wood and what each was for.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records that the bamboo grove (takeyabu) stood chiefly on the village's north edge as a winter windbreak and a source of material for farm and silkworm tools, and that the oak hedge (kashigune) stood mainly on the north and west of house plots for fire protection, screening and boundary. The uses are residents' accounts from a 2021 questionnaire in five villages and interviews in two, of a past the abstract does not date; by 2021 the bamboo had lost its value as material and served only as a windbreak. The north and west placement answers this plain's strong, dry northwest winter wind (karakkaze), so it speaks for open, windswept plains first, and it says nothing on grove sizes.
