Aizu-Wakamatsu Tourism Bureau (会津若松観光ビューロー), "会津武家屋敷" ("Aizu Samurai Residence", title translated), in its official tourism guide Aizu-Wakamatsu Kanko Navi (in Japanese; https://www.aizukanko.com/spot/46)

What it is: The official tourism guide's entry for the Aizu Samurai Residence museum in Aizu-Wakamatsu, Fukushima: the 2,300 tsubo house of the Aizu domain's chief retainer Saigo Tanomo, with a history hall, rice mill, an administrative-office building and a teahouse, plus visitor details.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the size of a chief retainer's house ground (2,300 tsubo, about 7,600 square meters) and its timber (zelkova, cypress and cedar in the Japanese manner), and lists a rice mill, a magistrate's-office building and a teahouse among the buildings. It does not say which buildings are original, moved or rebuilt, or date them; the museum's own guide says the jin'ya is the old Nakahata jin'ya, moved and rebuilt here from Yabuki, so it was never part of a retainer's house ground. The Saigo residence is in fact a 1975 reconstruction, drawn from a bird's-eye picture of the house burned in the Boshin War and built at Higashiyama away from its original ground near the castle, and other accounts of the museum give the ground as 2,400 tsubo. It speaks of one domain's senior retainer, so it is an upper bound for a samurai plot, not the common one.
