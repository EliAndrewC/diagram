inamatsu-2024-shimo-kurosawa-kenchicho

Inamatsu Tomoko, "旧伊達宗勝一関藩領村落における寛文一三年検地帳の分析 : 西磐井郡下黒沢村の事例を中心に" ("Analysis of the Kanbun 13 land survey registers of villages in the former Ichinoseki domain of Date Munekatsu, centered on the case of Shimo-Kurosawa village, Nishi-Iwai county", title translated), in Kokushigaku (国史学), Kokushigakukai, on the K-RAIN repository of Kokugakuin University (in Japanese; https://k-rain.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2000416/files/kokushigaku_222_002.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a journal article on the land-survey registers (kenchicho) of Date Munekatsu's short-lived Ichinoseki domain in northeast Honshu, especially the register of 1673 (Kanbun 13) for Shimo-Kurosawa village. It asks why that one-village-at-a-time re-survey survives for only seven villages and what the registers' marginal notes (honshinden, 'original new paddy') mean, with tables of surviving registers, assessed yields and holdings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how the Sendai domain surveyed and re-surveyed its villages in the 1600s: one general survey in 1640, later surveys mostly of newly opened fields, and a rule of 1666 on re-surveying a whole village; it also shows a village's holders split into 'original' and 'new' peasants (41 new peasants in Shimo-Kurosawa in 1673) and plot entries given in ken with area and grade. It is about one domain's registers and their bookkeeping, argues from the surviving registers that the survey followed the retainers' and foot soldiers' holdings (and possibly the digging of an irrigation weir), and says nothing about how a village looked on the ground; it is a Tohoku case of the 1600s, not a general account of Japanese villages.
