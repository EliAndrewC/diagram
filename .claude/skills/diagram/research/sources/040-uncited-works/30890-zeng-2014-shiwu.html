zeng-2014-shiwu

Zeng Xiongsheng (曾雄生), "水稻插秧器具莳梧考 - 兼论秧马" ("Shiwu, a rice-transplanting tool, with a discussion of the seedling horse", title translated), 中国农史 (Agricultural History of China) 2014(2), pp. 125-132 (in Chinese; https://agri-history.ihns.ac.cn/scholars/zxs/水稻插秧器具莳梧考_兼论秧马_曾雄生.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a journal paper arguing that the Qing-period shiwu, a hand tool for planting rice seedlings used around Nantong in Jiangsu, was China's earliest rice-planting implement, and that the Song seedling horse (yangma) was a seat for pulling seedlings, not a planter.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes Su Shi's Song description of the seedling horse and a 1755 Nantong gazetteer entry for the shiwu, and it gives how rice was transplanted by hand, including the drum-paced gang planting and the share of labor planting took. Its shiwu evidence comes from Nantong alone, from a 1755 gazetteer to uses in the 1950s and 1960s. The drum-paced planting is quoted from an 1822 gazetteer of Changyang county in Hubei. The labor shares (8 to 16.8 percent) come from a 1957 study of seven southern rice provinces, so they describe mid-twentieth-century hand planting, not the setting's. Nothing in it speaks to northern fields.
