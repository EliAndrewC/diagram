ja.wikipedia 麦飯 ("mugimeshi", rice cooked with barley, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/麦飯)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on mugimeshi, rice cooked with barley or barley alone, covering its history as a daily food, how it was prepared and processed, and its later reputation as a poor man's food.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that plain white rice was eaten outside the cities mostly at festivals, that the farmland ring around Tokyo ate barley rice with 20 to 30 percent upland rice, and that some ate barley alone up to the Meiji and Taisho years, while the mix moved from 2 rice to 8 barley through 3 to 7 to half and half, reversing to 7 rice to 3 barley after the war; only the last step is dated. Its examples are Tokyo and Nagano regions in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and it gives no figures for earlier centuries.
