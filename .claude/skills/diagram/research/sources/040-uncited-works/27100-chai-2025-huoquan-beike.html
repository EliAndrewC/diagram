Chai Ruixiang (柴瑞祥), "洪洞霍泉水神庙水利碑刻调查" ("A survey of the water-conservancy stele inscriptions at the Water God Temple of Huo Spring, Hongtong", title translated), in the 营造视野 column, reposted on Sohu on 2025-02-07 from 藏书老王 (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/856610384_121124715)

What it is: An article by a former deputy head of the Guangsheng Temple cultural relics office, surveying about thirty stele inscriptions at the Water God Temple beside Huo Spring in Hongtong, Shanxi, and quoting them on how the spring's water was divided between two counties, managed by canal heads and paid for by levies on irrigated land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes dated steles from 1139 (Jin) to 1870 (Qing) on a 3:7 split of one spring's water between two counties, the rotation of turns by upper, middle and lower canal sections, the roles of ditch heads and water patrols, and the festival levies charged per mu, which gives a documented case of village-level water management in a wet-poor northern Chinese basin. It is one spring in Shanxi, its irrigated-area figures vary widely between steles (the author notes the 1139 total is more than twice the later ones), and the author interprets the inscriptions without showing the steles themselves.
