Chai Ruixiang (柴瑞祥), "洪洞霍泉水神庙水利碑刻调查" ("A survey of the water-conservancy stele inscriptions at the Water God Temple of Huo Spring, Hongtong", title translated), in the 营造视野 column, reposted on Sohu on 2025-02-07 from 藏书老王 (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/856610384_121124715)

What it is: An article by a former deputy head of the Guangsheng Temple cultural relics office, surveying about thirty stele inscriptions at the Water God Temple beside Huo Spring in Hongtong, Shanxi, and quoting them on how the spring's water was divided between two counties, managed by canal heads and paid for by levies on irrigated land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes dated steles from 1139 (Jin) to 1870 (Qing) on a 3:7 split of one spring's water between two counties, the rotation of turns by upper, middle and lower canal sections, the roles of ditch heads and water patrols, and the festival levies charged per mu, which gives a documented case of village-level water management in a drought-prone basin of the Shanxi loess plateau. It is one spring in Shanxi, and its irrigated-area figures vary widely between steles: the 1139 total of about 96,400 mu is roughly twice the 34,000-45,400 mu of the Ming and Qing steles, yet the 1870 stele gives over 80,000 mu for the northern canal alone. The 25-day rotation it gives (lower section eight days, middle ten, upper seven) is an unwritten custom told by a modern water official in 2005, not a stele text. The article is a reposted piece by a local cultural-relics official, not a peer-reviewed study, and it quotes the inscriptions without showing the steles themselves.
