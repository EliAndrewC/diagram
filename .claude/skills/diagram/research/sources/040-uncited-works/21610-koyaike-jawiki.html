ja.wikipedia 昆陽池 ("Koyaike", the Koya pond, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%98%86%E9%99%BD%E6%B1%A0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Koya pond in Itami, Hyogo, a large irrigation reservoir said to have been dug in the Nara period by the monk Gyoki, with its record in the Gyoki nenpu, its later filling-in and its present park.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a very large dug reservoir that served irrigation and flood control for several villages and was kept by a village as its pond keeper, with figures from an Edo-period source of about 50 ha and a Showa 15 area of 50 cho 5 tan. It gives the 741 record of two ponds and two channels with their lengths and widths, and says the lower pond was filled into fields in 1608 and a new village founded there. The Nara dating rests on a document compiled about 400 years after Gyoki's death, the pond's original extent is not recorded, and the present park of 12.5 ha and 4.5 ha is a late remnant.
