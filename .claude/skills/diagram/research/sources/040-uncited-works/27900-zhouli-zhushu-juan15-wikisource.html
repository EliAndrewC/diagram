"周禮註疏/卷十五" (Zhouli zhushu, scroll 15, title translated), in the Chinese Wikisource transcription of the Rites of Zhou with its notes and subcommentary, traditional-character version (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E5%91%A8%E7%A6%AE%E8%A8%BB%E7%96%8F/%E5%8D%B7%E5%8D%81%E4%BA%94)

What it is: The text of scroll 15 of the Rites of Zhou with Zheng Xuan's notes and a later subcommentary, on Chinese Wikisource. It covers the market officials (the contract keeper, the shop-tax collector, the price officers, the market police), the gate and pass officials with their tally system, and the officers of the six outer districts: the household groupings, the field allotment by land grade, and the ditch-and-road hierarchy.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the ideal layout of an administered countryside: five households to a neighbor-group, each household's 100 mu with 50, 100 or 200 mu left fallow by land grade, ditches stepping up from 2 chi wide to the large canals, and roads graded from a path that takes cattle and horses to a road of three carriage-ruts. It is a ritual-bureaucratic blueprint, not a record of a real district, and the commentary says more than once that parts of it are unsupported by the Zhou texts and are reasoned by analogy with Han practice (it cites Han tally and market customs and Wang Mang's tax), so its dimensions are idealized and its date is uncertain.
