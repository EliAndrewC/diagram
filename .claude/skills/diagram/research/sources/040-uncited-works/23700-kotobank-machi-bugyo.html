kotobank-machi-bugyo

"町奉行" ("machi bugyo", the town magistrate, title translated), entries from Shogakukan's Nipponica (Yoshihara Kenichiro), the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (Minami Kazuo), Mypedia, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%94%BA%E5%A5%89%E8%A1%8C-136418)

What it is: A Kotobank page of encyclopedia entries on the machi bugyo, the town magistrate of the Edo shogunate, the post that ran the administration, justice and police of Edo's townspeople. They give its history from 1590, its two offices (North and South) and their moves, its salary, its yoriki and doshin staff, and its monthly rotation, with short notes on the posts in Osaka, Kyoto and the daimyo's towns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a town magistrate with two offices alternating by month, an office that served as the magistrate's residence and as his place of work, and under him about 25 yoriki and 100 doshin per office from 1719. It states that the magistrate ruled only the town land of Edo, about 20 percent of the city, with samurai and temple land outside it. It is Edo's post, and for other castle towns says only that daimyo had magistrates of the same name, so the numbers are for the capital of the shogunate and not for a provincial town.
