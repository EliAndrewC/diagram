kotobank-kadoya-kadobyakusho

"門屋" ("kadoya", gatehouse and its dependent household), in the Digital Daijisen, the Concise Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Nipponica, the Yamakawa Dictionary of Japanese History and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%96%80%E5%B1%8B-1161669)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering five reference entries on the kadoya, the small hut beside a farm compound's gate and the dependent peasant household living in it in the Edo period, with excerpts from the encyclopedia's entries on gates, na-go, hikan, hyakusho and yashiki.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the kadoya stood near a farmhouse's entrance, that its dependent household was given a house and land by the main house, owed labor and paid its dues through the main house, and in some entries could be sold or pawned. It adds that a kadoya might have a married household yet was not counted a full village member and had separate graves, and that the register entry for a compound lumped such a household with the main house as one hearth. Names and degrees of dependence varied by region, and the entries do not give a hut's size or its exact position relative to the gate.
