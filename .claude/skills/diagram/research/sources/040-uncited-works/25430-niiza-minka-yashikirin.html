niiza-minka-yashikirin

Niiza City, "にいざ見聞録（第6回 民家と屋敷森）" ("Niiza Kenbunroku no. 6: farmhouses and house groves", title translated), Niiza City website, reprinted from the city bulletin of 1998-2000 (in Japanese; https://www.city.niiza.lg.jp/site/niiza-kenbunroku/bunkazai-kenbun006.html)

What it is: A short city article on the farmhouses and house groves of Niiza, Saitama, on the Musashino plateau, describing the Sato family house in Atago, a former village headman's house, with its thatched hip roof, four-room plan and dated storehouse.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Niiza was farm country surrounded by fields and house groves before the war and that tall zelkovas stood against the dry Musashino wind; and that the Sato house, built in 1839 with its storehouse in 1844 per documents in the storehouse, follows a plot division from new-field development and keeps its grove, with a plan of an earthen-floor area and four rooms in a field-character arrangement. It was written about 1998-2000 of what remained after urbanization from about 1955, gives no dimensions or plot sizes, and speaks for one headman's house, not an average farm.
