ja.wikipedia 八幡堀 ("the Hachiman moat", a canal at Omi-Hachiman, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%85%AB%E5%B9%A1%E5%A0%80)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Hachimanbori, a canal at Omihachiman in Shiga dug in 1585 around the castle town of Hachimanyama, with its later decline and its rescue from filling-in in the 1970s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the moat was dug as both a defense for the castle and a water route to Lake Biwa with a landing place, that the town was laid out on a grid with warriors north of the moat and townsmen south and merchants and craftsmen in set quarters, and that back drains carried waste while silt was dredged and used as field fertilizer. It gives the present canal as 4.75 km long and about 15 m wide, and the account of decline and restoration is twentieth-century. The article does not say that the 1585 width matched today's.
