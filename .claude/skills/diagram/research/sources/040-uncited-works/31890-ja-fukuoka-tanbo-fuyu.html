JA Fukuoka Chuokai, "田んぼの生き物、冬はどこに？" ("Where do the paddy's animals go in winter?", title translated), from the column Akiba Hakase's Food and Farming Class (アキバ博士の食農教室), 2022-03-31 (in Japanese; https://ja-gp-fukuoka.jp/education/akiba-hakase/003/002.html)

What it is: A children's column on a Fukuoka agricultural cooperative's site on how animals of the rice paddy pass the winter once the field is drained: burrowing in soil and mud, taking refuge in water, or surviving as eggs.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says frogs winter shallowly in soil and under leaves, loach, medaka and minnows leave drained paddies and narrow channels for channels and puddles that keep water (sometimes thousands in one small pool), and red dragonflies winter as eggs laid in the paddy. Its first example, the American crayfish, did not live in Japan before the twentieth century, and it gives no measurements or numbers of animals per field.
