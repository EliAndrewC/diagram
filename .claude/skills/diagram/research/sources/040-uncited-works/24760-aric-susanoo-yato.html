aric-susanoo-yato

ARIC (一般社団法人農業農村整備情報総合センター), "コラム 須佐男命と夜刀神" ("Column: Susanoo and the Yato deity", title translated), in "土地を拓く" (Clearing the land), "国土づくりの歴史", in Suido no Ishizue (水土の礎) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/01/07.html)

What it is: A short column on how the Nihon Shoki and the Hitachi Fudoki treat paddies and irrigation works, set around the god Susanoo's ruined fields and the clearing of a reed-grown valley in Namekata (Ibaraki) against a snake-bodied valley deity.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reads the myths as evidence of which land was opened first: in the Fudoki story only the valley head, which needed little work, was made into paddy (over 10 cho), and the whole valley was taken later once a reservoir gave a stable water source. It also notes that damaging ditches and ridges became a grave offense. The evidence is myth and legend with no dates, and the writer's picture of Susanoo's fields rests on guesses from their names.
