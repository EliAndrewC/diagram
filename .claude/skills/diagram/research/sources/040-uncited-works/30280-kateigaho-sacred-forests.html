kateigaho-sacred-forests

"The 10 most exquisite sacred forests in Japan", Kateigaho International Edition, spring 2005 issue (Sekaibunka Publishing), archived copy (https://web.archive.org/web/20070124042747/http://int.kateigaho.com/spr05/forests-sacred.html)

What it is: A magazine feature that picks ten shrine forests as representative of Japan's natural regions and gives each a paragraph with its area, tree species, age and protected status, including Atsuta Jingu, Shimogamo Jinja's Tadasu-no-mori, Kasuga Taisha, Keta Taisha and Seifa Utaki.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives present areas for the woods of great shrines: Atsuta about 20 ha, Tadasu-no-mori about 12.4 ha (said to have covered 495 ha originally), Keta Taisha 3.3 ha, Kirishima 887 ha, Dewa Sanzan 2,185 ha. These are the provincial supreme shrines and sacred mountains, chosen as the best-preserved forests, so they say nothing about a village shrine's grove, and the areas are the 2005 figures with no source given, with only the Tadasu-no-mori figure looking back to an earlier extent.
