kateigaho-sacred-forests

"The 10 most exquisite sacred forests in Japan", Kateigaho International Edition, spring 2005 issue (Sekaibunka Publishing), archived copy (https://web.archive.org/web/20070124042747/http://int.kateigaho.com/spr05/forests-sacred.html)

What it is: A magazine feature that picks ten shrine forests as representative of Japan's natural regions and gives each a paragraph on its trees and, for some, their age, the forest's area and its protected status, including Atsuta Jingu, Shimogamo Jinja's Tadasu-no-mori, Kasuga Taisha, Keta Taisha and Seifa Utaki.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives present areas for the woods of great shrines: Atsuta about 20 ha, Tadasu-no-mori about 12.4 ha (said to have covered 495 ha originally), Keta Taisha 3.3 ha, Kirishima 887 ha, Dewa Sanzan 2,185 ha. These are great shrines and sacred mountains, chosen as representative of Japan's natural regions, so they say nothing about a village shrine's grove; the areas are as of 2005, the Dewa Sanzan figure is the area of the whole three-mountain complex rather than of a forest, and only Tadasu-no-mori is given an earlier extent, which is undated.
