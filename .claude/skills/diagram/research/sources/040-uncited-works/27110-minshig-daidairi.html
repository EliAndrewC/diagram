minshig-daidairi

MinShig, "大内裏（宮城）" ("The Greater Imperial Palace (palace enclosure)", title translated), in 官制大観 (a reference to offices under the ritsuryo system; last updated 1998-10-02), on the site 和憩団欒房 (in Japanese; http://www.sol.dti.ne.jp/hiromi/kansei/r_daidairi.html)

What it is: A personal reference page on the palace enclosure of Heian-kyo, describing its three concentric zones and listing its fourteen surrounding gates in order with their former and later names, the guards who held them and the offices beside each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how a premodern Japanese capital's palace wall was pierced: twelve long-established tiled gates, plus two later earthen gates cut through the wall without roof or plaque for carrying goods, each tied to a named guard unit or office. It is the imperial palace of the capital, not a provincial seat, the page itself says layouts differed from one capital to the next, and it gives no dimensions.
