tsujiban-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 辻番 ("tsujiban", street-corner guard, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%BE%BB%E7%95%AA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the tsujiban, the guard posts set at street corners around the samurai quarters of Edo from 1629, with their three kinds of sponsor and their count.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the posts were set up in 1629 against sword-murders on the street, were staffed day and night in shifts, and numbered about 930 in the Genbun years (1736-1741), most of them joint posts of several lords; it also says the joint posts later passed to townsman contractors who were repeatedly warned about unfit guards and gambling in the post. Its text describes Edo alone, not a provincial castle town or a village (its one photograph shows a guard post kept beside a former foot-soldiers' house of the Hikone domain), and it carries a notice (March 2022) that its sources are absent or insufficient: it lists one reference, a 1994 dictionary of Edo studies, and footnotes nothing.
