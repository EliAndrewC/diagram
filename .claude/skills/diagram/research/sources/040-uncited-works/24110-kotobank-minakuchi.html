kotobank-minakuchi

"水口" ("minakuchi" or "mizuguchi", the water mouth), entries in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Nipponica and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/水口)

What it is: A page of Japanese dictionary and encyclopedia entries on minakuchi: the opening where water is let into or out of a paddy, a kitchen water opening, and the town of Minakuchi in Omi (Shiga), a castle town of the Kato and a Tokaido post town.

Why it applies, and its limits: The first sense is the paddy's water inlet, and the page's note from the older Heibonsha edition says the rice-seedbed rite held at the minakuchi built a small earth mound with seasonal flowers and twigs and offered roasted rice there. The page gives no size or construction of the inlet itself, and most of it concerns the town of Minakuchi, whose name shares the word.
