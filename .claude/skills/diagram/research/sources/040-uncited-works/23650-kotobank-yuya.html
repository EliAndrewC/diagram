kotobank-yuya

"湯屋" ("yuya", the bathhouse, title translated), entries from the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (Tamai Tetsuo), Shogakukan's Nipponica (Inagaki Shisei) and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B9%AF%E5%B1%8B-146040)

What it is: A Kotobank page of dictionary entries on the yuya, the bathing building: first a temple's bathhouse and then the public bathhouse that charges a fee. The encyclopedia entries trace temple bathhouses back to the 8th-century temple property lists, and the sento from the end of the Muromachi period, and distinguish the hot-water bath from the steam bath (furo-ya).

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a temple having a bathhouse as one of its buildings, with a large kettle or wooden tub and water heated elsewhere, and a paid bathhouse in a city from the late Muromachi period. It says that in Edo around 1810 very few households had their own bath, that commoners long used steam baths, that bathing by soaking in a tub began in the early Edo period, and that Edo bathhouses were recognized as a guild in 1810. It names the Todaiji and Horyuji bathhouses as rebuilt after the Kamakura period, and it speaks of cities, so it does not say whether a village had a bathhouse.
