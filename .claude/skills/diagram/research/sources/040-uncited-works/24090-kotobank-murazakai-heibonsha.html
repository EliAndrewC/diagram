kotobank-murazakai-heibonsha

Fukuda Ajio, "村境" ("murazakai", the village boundary), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典), with the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and Britannica entries on the same page, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/村境)

What it is: A signed encyclopedia entry reproduced on the Kotobank dictionary site, with shorter dictionary entries beside it, on what a Japanese village's boundary is: the line between the village's hill and field land and its neighbors', and the inner line where its houses meet its fields.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the boundary villagers actually felt and ritualized was the point where each road enters the settlement, so there is one per road, marked with a sacred straw rope, giant straw figures and the roadside Dosojin stone, and that it doubled as a meeting place for sending off and welcoming people. It also says the outer boundary of land was fixed only in the Edo-period survey of the early 1600s; it gives no distances and no region beyond saying the straw rope custom is common in Kinki and the straw figures in Tohoku.
