kotobank-ushiwaku

"牛枠" ("ushiwaku, the ox frame", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/牛枠)

What it is: A short Kotobank headword for ushiwaku, a flood-control work of bamboo or timber frames packed with stones, used mainly on the embankments of gravel rivers, with the old Heibonsha encyclopedia's passage on the ox-type (ushi-rui) river-bank devices.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the ushiwaku as a pyramid-shaped frame of piles resembling two horns, set on alluvial-fan rivers where driving piles is hard and weighted with stone-filled gabions (jakago). The entries give no dimensions, no date for the device and no place beyond the river type, and the Heibonsha entry itself only points to another headword.
