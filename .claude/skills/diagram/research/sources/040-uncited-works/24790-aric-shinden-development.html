aric-shinden-development

ARIC (一般社団法人農業農村整備情報総合センター), "平野の拡張、新田開発" ("Widening the plains: new-field development", title translated), in "土地に応じた開発" (Development suited to the land), "国土づくりの歴史", in Suido no Ishizue (水土の礎) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/03/04.html)

What it is: A short page on early modern new-field (shinden) development in Japan: its three peaks, what land was already opened before it, and how eastern and western Japan differed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the peaks to about 1660, 1720 and 1840, and says new fields and their settlements were made together as a set, giving the row-village or dispersed-farm landscapes. It adds that eastern Japan cleared mountain and moor and drained lakes and marshes, while western Japan reclaimed tidal flats, and it names the developers (village, retainer, domain, merchant contractors). It is a national summary with no plot sizes, counts or named examples.
