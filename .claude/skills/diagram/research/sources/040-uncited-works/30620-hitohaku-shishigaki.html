hitohaku-shishigaki

Osaki Masakazu, "猪垣（ししがき）：歴史と民俗" ("Shishigaki, the boar fence: history and folklore", title translated), Museum of Nature and Human Activities, Hyogo (Hitohaku), newsletter issue 43, copyright 1997 (in Japanese; https://hitohaku.jp/publication/newspaper/43/hm17-3.html)

What it is: A signed essay by a museum ecologist on boar fences (shishigaki): what they were before and after the Edo period, why paddy farming made the boar a pest, and the great fenced boar and deer cull of Tsushima from 1700. It is a short article in a prefectural natural-history museum's newsletter.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that until the Edo period there were two kinds of boar fence, one that funneled boar into traps and one that kept them from fields, and that the farmer's old defense before fences was a field hut and clappers kept up through the night. It gives one case with figures (Tsushima, 1700, about 80,000 boar killed in nine years across an island of 700 square kilometers), but that case is an island cull under a domain magistrate, not village practice, and the essay says nothing of fence height, material or length.
