teiboku-kotobank

Daijisen, Nihon kokugo daijiten, Nipponica, Mypedia and the Britannica small-item encyclopedia, "低木" ("teiboku", shrub, title translated), on Kotobank (the page saved under this address is the teiboku entry) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/坪庭-100436)

What it is: Seven dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the word teiboku (shrub), reproduced on Kotobank. They define it by height and form, and the Nipponica entry gives the ecologist Raunkiaer's classification. The address given here ends in the word for a small courtyard garden, but the page saved from it is the shrub entry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines a shrub as a woody plant usually under about 2 m, or about a person's height, with several stems rising from the base, and Nipponica gives 2 to 8 m in Raunkiaer's scheme. It says that the aboveground part of a true shrub lives about 50 years and renews from the root, and that the boundary with a tree is a matter of convenience. The definitions are of a botanical term, and the page says nothing of gardens, hedges or the Japanese landscape.
