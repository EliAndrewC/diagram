aric-tsuchi-no-sho

Agriculture and Rural Area Information Center (ARIC), "土の章" ("tsuchi no sho", the soil chapter, title translated), in "水土里の軌跡" ("the trail of water, soil and village", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.aric.or.jp/kiseki/jp/tsuchi/index.html)

What it is: A web chapter by the ARIC on the history of Japan's farmland from the arrival of rice to the Heisei era, with sections on the ritsuryo field allotment, the shoen, the Edo-period new fields and village organization, and the Meiji and later field-consolidation laws.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives national figures for the premodern period that the record can use as anchors: an allotment of 2 dan (about 24 a) for a male aged six or over, one tan yielding 1 koku a year in the 7th-8th centuries, arable land doubling from about 1.5 million ha to 3 million ha in the first Edo century, and the Edo village run through a nanushi and five-household groups. Its figures are national totals from an unsigned summary re-edited from ARIC's bulletin, with no region or village layout, and most of the text is Meiji to Heisei policy history; it also records the Meiji standard plot of 10 a and the postwar 30 a plot, which are modern forms.
