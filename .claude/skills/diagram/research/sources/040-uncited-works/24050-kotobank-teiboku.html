kotobank-teiboku

"低木" ("teiboku", a shrub or low tree, title translated), dictionary entries from Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nippon Daihyakka Zensho and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/坪庭-100436)

What it is: A Kotobank page whose saved text defines teiboku, a shrub: a woody plant usually under about 2 meters that sends several stems from the base. The address names the word tsuboniwa (an enclosed courtyard garden), but the saved page is the entry for shrub.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a definition and a size: the Daijisen says about 2 meters or less, Raunkiaer's life-form class takes trees of 2 to 8 meters, and the Nippon Daihyakka Zensho says the aboveground part of a true shrub lives about 50 years and renews from the root. These are botanical terms that hold in any era. The page puts the boundary between shrub and tree as a matter of convenience, and says nothing of Japan's species or the use of shrubs in a garden or hedge.
