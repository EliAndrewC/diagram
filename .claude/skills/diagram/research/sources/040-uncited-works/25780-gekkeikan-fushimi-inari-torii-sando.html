gekkeikan-fushimi-inari-torii-sando

Gekkeikan Sake Co., "伏見稲荷:朱色の鳥居が続く参道" ("Fushimi Inari: the approach lined with vermilion torii", title translated), in the series "[伏見探訪]稲荷と桃山" ("exploring Fushimi: Inari and Momoyama", title translated), on the company's own site (in Japanese; https://www.gekkeikan.co.jp/enjoy/bunko/inari-momoyama/fushimiinari.html)

What it is: A sake brewer's visitor page on Fushimi Inari Taisha in Kyoto: the head shrine of the Inari shrines, the sacred mountain behind the main hall, the fox statues, the Thousand Torii path, and the walking course of about 2 km one way to the summit, with its small shrines, tea stalls and mounds. It adds how sake brewers keep an Inari shrine in each brewery and hold fire-burning and second-horse-day rites.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the shrine's founding is dated to 711, that the mountain itself is the object of worship, that about 10,000 torii are said to line the paths and as many mounds stand on the mountain, and that the vermilion of a torii marks life force and opposes malign power. Those counts are the present-day shrine's, given as rumor ("said to") rather than counted, and the page is silent on when the torii path took its present form.
