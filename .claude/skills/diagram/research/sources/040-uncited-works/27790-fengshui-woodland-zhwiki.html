zh.wikipedia "風水林" ("Fengshui woodland", title translated), on Chinese Wikipedia (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A3%8E%E6%B0%B4%E6%9E%97)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on fengshui woodlands, deliberately kept or planted groves of southern Chinese, Korean and Ryukyuan villages, with a classification, a Hong Kong survey and the Confucius forest at Qufu.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names three kinds of grove by what they do (windbreak, the "dragon seat" on a hill behind a house, the "underlay" along a water edge) and reports that a 2002 Hong Kong survey of 116 groves found them mostly below 100 m elevation and about 600 square m to one hectare each. The Hong Kong figures describe groves surviving in the 2000s, the claim that villagers planted useful trees in them carries a "source requested" flag, and the type names come from fengshui teaching, not from a survey of villages.
