hattori-kurokawa-satoyama-hyogo

Hattori Tamotsu, "日本一の里山林" ("Japan's best satoyama forest", title translated), a booklet on the coppice woods of Kurokawa in Kawanishi, Hyogo, hosted on the Hyogo Prefecture website (in Japanese; https://web.pref.hyogo.lg.jp/hnk02/documents/satoyama.pdf)

What it is: A booklet by an emeritus professor of the University of Hyogo explaining why the charcoal-oak (kunugi) coppice woods of Kurokawa, in the upper Inagawa basin, are rated Japan's best satoyama forest, with a table of Edo-period books praising Hitokura (Ikeda) charcoal and a table of 1679 survey-register woodland areas by village.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries Edo-period evidence for a coppice-and-charcoal landscape: a 1600 tax record on mountain income, the 1679 Enpo survey register listing kunugi, brushwood and grass hills by area, a 1726 account of dividing a hill into ten and cutting one a year, and a 10-year cutting cycle in Kurokawa. It also describes high-stump pollard oaks first recorded in 1844 and says kunugi is an introduced tree, but it speaks for one Kinki-region charcoal district famed for tea-ceremony charcoal, and its present-day stand maps and counts are the author's 2018 survey, not a premodern measurement.
