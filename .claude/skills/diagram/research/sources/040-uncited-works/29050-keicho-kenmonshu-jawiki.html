ja.wikipedia 見聞集 ("Kenmonshu", also called the Keicho kenmonshu, title translated), on the article for the Miura Joshin kana-zoshi on early Edo (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/慶長見聞集)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Kenmonshu, a ten-volume collection of tales about early Edo written by Miura Joshin in the late Kan'ei era (the page redirects here from the Keicho kenmonshu title). It covers the work's authorship, its dating, the debate over its reliability, the works it drew on and its manuscripts.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its volume 1 tale of Edo's tile roofs is summarized: after a fire of 1601 the magistrates ordered shingle roofs, Takiyama Yajibei of Honcho 2-chome tiled the street half of his roof and was nicknamed 'Half-Tile Yajibei', and the tale says all of Edo is now tiled. The article says the work is disguised as written in 1614 but is judged to date from late Kan'ei (after 1635), that this confusion has long muddied studies of early Edo, and that a 1924 critic called the work a later forgery because tile roofs were held to have spread in Edo only about 1645-1648 (the article notes that fits a late-Kan'ei date). It is a summary of the scholarship on the book, not the book's own text, and its account of when the Yoshiwara quarter opened is likewise contested.
