shinshiro-yotsuya-senmaida

Shinshiro City, "四谷の千枚田" ("The Thousand Paddies of Yotsuya", title translated), in its listing of scenic sites, on the Shinshiro city website (in Japanese; https://www.city.shinshiro.lg.jp/kanko/meisyo/senmaida.html)

What it is: A municipal page on the stone-walled terraced paddies of Yotsuya, Shinshiro, Aichi, on the southwest slope of Mount Kurakake. It gives their extent, water supply and construction, the landslide of 1904 and the rebuilding that followed, and the preservation society that farms them today.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies figures for a mountain terrace: paddies from about 220 m to 420 m above sea level (about 200 m of relief), a spring of 7 liters a second that feeds the stone-lined channels, walls built only of field stones and stones from slides, and houses set on the same stone platforms. It says the 1904 landslide destroyed every terrace along the stream and killed 11 people, and that villagers rebuilt them in about five years with hoes and carrying baskets, so the walls seen now date from that rebuilding, and the page does not date the first terraces.
