otsubo-2005-jobo-chiba

Otsubo Kazuro, "住宅のアプローチ・構えの風景との関わり 第2回" ("Housing approaches and the landscape of the compound, part 2: analysis of the Jobo, the approach of hill farmhouses in Chiba", title translated), in Gekkan Sugi (月刊杉), 2005 (in Japanese; http://www.m-sugi.com/75/m-sugi_75_otsubo.htm)

What it is: A magazine article by an architect on the winding sloped approach (jobo) to farmhouses set on valley-side slopes in the hills of southeastern Chiba, in the Ichinomiya River basin. It sets out why the houses stand where they do: terrain, drainage, water, sun, wind, standing and house-direction beliefs.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives reasons for siting a farmhouse on a hill foot: the ground raised about 1.5 m for drainage, side wells (yokoido) dug into the slope for irrigation and fire water, a low homestead grove where the hills already break the wind, houses on the north slope in thatch days to shelter from storms, and the yard where harvested rice was dried. It is a survey of houses standing in 2005 in one set of narrow yatsu valleys (it finds the form mostly in valleys under 100 m wide), and the author's causes come from his own survey and interviews, with the older reasons given as what residents say; it offers no counts and it explicitly says the pattern need not hold outside such terrain.
