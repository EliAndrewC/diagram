kotobank-dozo-105247

"土蔵" ("dozo", the earth-walled storehouse, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Nipponica, the Mypedia and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%9F%E8%94%B5-105247)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering dictionary entries on the dozo, a storehouse of timber frame with walls coated in earth and plaster as protection from fire and theft. The Nipponica entry describes its construction and the Mypedia entry says what town houses and farms kept in one.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a built description: posts about a meter apart, a bamboo and palm-rope base coated in earth to a wall 20 to 30 cm thick, plaster outside, a tile roof, heavy doors sealed with clay in a fire, and the form complete in the Edo period. A town house stored goods and stock in it and a farm stored grain; it names no floor size, and the page also carries an unrelated entry on a Muromachi painter with the same name.
