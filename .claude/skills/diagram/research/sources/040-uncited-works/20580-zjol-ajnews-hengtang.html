Zheng Yong, "纵浦缘何称横塘?" ("Zongpu yuanhe cheng Hengtang?", why is the "Zongpu" called "Hengtang"?, title translated), in Anji News, on Zhejiang Online (in Chinese; https://ajnews.zjol.com.cn/ajnews/system/2018/08/21/031089164.shtml)

What it is: A 2018 local-news essay on the Hengtang irrigation works of Anji, Zhejiang, built along the West Tiaoxi to reclaim river-flat land, and on why the works carry the name Hengtang, "cross channel" (the essay, after the Wuxing prefectural gazetteer, glosses tang as a water channel with a road along each bank, not a pond), though their main channel runs north and south. It draws on county and prefectural gazetteers and ties the name to the Five Dynasties grid of lengthwise channels (zongpu) and cross channels (hengtang) in the Wusong River polders of the Lake Tai region, as a Fudan historical geographer describes it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a Song-dynasty drainage and irrigation scheme, the Tongzhi-era county gazetteer's description of it (five li long, over a hundred zhang wide, watering 56 qing of polder fields), the later reading of its channels, and a late-Ming poet's night on it, which the essay glosses as fields, mulberry and hemp on every bit of land. It dates the works to "1225", after the modern county water-conservancy gazetteer, while calling that year the Northern Song Xuanhe seventh year, which was 1125, and it puts a family's settling there in a "Jianyan fifth year (1132)" that did not exist, so its dates cannot be relied on without another source; and the size and the 56 qing are the gazetteer's of the 1870s, by when the essay says the original grid of channels was gone, so they describe the works as they then stood, not as built. That the works were first laid out as a grid of cross and lengthwise channels is the essayist's own inference from the name, the gazetteer's wording and its map, not a record of their building.
