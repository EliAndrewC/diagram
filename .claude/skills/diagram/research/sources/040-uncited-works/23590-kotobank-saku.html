kotobank-saku

"柵" ("saku", a fence or palisade, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (concise edition), the Fukyuban Jitsu (普及版 字通), the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, the Britannica Japan small-item encyclopedia and the Yamakawa Concise Dictionary of Japanese History, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9F%B5-68672)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering six dictionary and encyclopedia entries on saku, the fence of widely spaced posts and crossbars, which also names small timber forts and the ancient palisaded administrative posts of northern Honshu.

Why it applies, and its limits: The World Encyclopedia entry quotes the Chikujoki on how a palisade was built (posts standing a little over six shaku above ground, about five to an interval of one ken, set so a person cannot crawl through) and says a saku is less permanent than a wall and was used at the edge of a castle bailey; the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten gives a first example from the Shoku Nihongi of 698. It describes a defensive and boundary fence; the entry gives no length for a shaku or ken, and the fences of ordinary house plots and fields are mentioned only in the dictionary senses, with the 7th-century and 11th-century northern forts as the main historical examples.
