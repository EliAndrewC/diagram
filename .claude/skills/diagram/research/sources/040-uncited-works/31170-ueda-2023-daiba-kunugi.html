ueda-2023-daiba-kunugi

Ueda Moeko (Osaka Metropolitan University), "猪名川上流域の台場クヌギ林" ("Inagawa jōryūiki no daiba kunugi-rin", the pollarded oak woods of the upper Inagawa basin, title translated), in the column "研究者の眼" (researcher's eye) of "人が「営む」ランドスケープ資産" no. 3-11, Landscape Heritage Research Group, Kansai Branch, Japanese Institute of Landscape Architecture, posted 2023-08-31 (in Japanese; https://landscape-kansai.jila-zouen.org/archives/researcher/1548)

What it is: A short signed column by a university researcher, on a landscape-heritage society's site, on the pollarded kunugi (oak) woods of the upper Inagawa basin on the Hyogo and Osaka border, kept for charcoal.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the oaks are cut high on the trunk (daiba pollarding) and sprout again, that each stand is cut on an 8 to 10 year cycle with the stands rotated year by year, that the wood feeds a tea-ceremony charcoal (kikusumi, also called Hitokura or Ikeda charcoal), and that old documents trace the practice back almost 400 years. The cycle is stated as present management, the page gives no stand size or yield and no reference list (it points to a separate one), and it says the pollarded form survives as maintained woods only in this one basin.
