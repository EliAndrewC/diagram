sugi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia スギ ("sugi", the Japanese cedar, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/スギ)

What it is: The long Japanese Wikipedia article on the sugi (Cryptomeria japonica), a conifer. It covers its form, range, ecology, pests, famous trees, wood and bark uses, planting, cultural and shrine associations, regional timber brands and classification.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the tree's height up to 60 m, its preference for stream-side and lower-slope ground (planted in valleys, with cypress mid-slope and pine on ridges), a standard planting of 3,000 per hectare (the page's figure for plantations today, not a premodern one), planting for timber said to begin around the Muromachi period, use as windbreak groves on the Toyama plain and in northern Kanto, as roadside avenues and as sacred shrine trees, and use of bark for roofing and boughs for sake-shop balls. Much of the page is modern forestry, pollen allergy and municipal tree symbols, its planting figures are postwar totals, and the premodern claims rest on a few secondary books.
