ookunitama-temizuya

Okunitama Shrine (大國魂神社), "手水舎" ("The purification pavilion", title translated), in its precinct guide (in Japanese; https://www.ookunitamajinja.or.jp/meguri/temizu.php)

What it is: A page of the shrine's own precinct guide on its handwashing pavilion at Fuchu, Tokyo: where it stood in a precinct plan in the Edo meisho zue of the 1830s and in plans of 1891 and 1904, the basin carved in 1813, the pavilion completed in 1897, its carvings, and its move in 2011.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reads what seems to be a temizuya between the spirit-gate and the worship hall in a precinct plan of the 1830s, and gives a basin dated 1813 that it takes to be the one drawn there and around which, it suggests, the pavilion of 1897 was built, so it shows that a large shrine of the Musashi plain had one in the late Edo period and where in the precinct. The shrine says it is unsure since when it has had one, and the building now standing is of 1897 and was moved in 2011.
