kotobank-se-unit

"畝" ("se" / "ho", the land-area unit and the field ridge, title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Jitsu, Heibonsha World Encyclopedia and Nipponica, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%9D)

What it is: A page of dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the character 畝, defining the Japanese area unit (one tenth of a tan, 30 bu, about 99 square meters), the Chinese mu (100 bu under Zhou, 240 under Qin and later) and the raised ridge (une) of a field, with the encyclopedia's reasons for raising ridges.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Japanese unit in tsubo and square meters, notes that the tan-se-bu system came into use in the Tensho era (1573-92) and that the Chinese unit is not the Japanese one, and explains that high ridges drain better and warm faster but cost labor and cropped area. Its metric figure (about 99 square meters) is the unit as fixed by the 1891 weights-and-measures law, and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten notes that a se was formerly 36 bu, so a se recorded before the Taiko survey was not this size. Its reasons for raising ridges come from a modern agronomist's article that also weighs farm machinery and mechanical seeding, and it does not say what a ridge measured in any one village.
