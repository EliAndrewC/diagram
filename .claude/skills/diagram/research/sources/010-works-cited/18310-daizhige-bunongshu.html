daizhige-bunongshu

Daizhige classical texts site, 补农书 (Supplement to the Farm Manual), by Zhang Lüxiang, with the Shen family's farm manual as its first volume (in Chinese; https://daizhige.org/%E5%AD%90%E8%97%8F/%E5%86%9C%E5%AE%B6/%E8%A1%A5%E5%86%9C%E4%B9%A6.html)

What it is: A transcription, from an open-source collection of classical Chinese texts, of the 补农书: Zhang Lüxiang's supplement, which takes the Shen family's farm manual as its base and prints it first, beginning with the manual's month-by-month calendar of farm work and purchases.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lets the reader see what kind of farm the Shen manual was written for - rice seedling beds, mulberries, manure bought in Suzhou and Hangzhou - which places its advice on pigs on a Lower Yangzi rice-and-silk farm - and on a prosperous, market-bound one: the calendar hires laborers by the month and the day and buys manure, feed and tools in the cities and market towns, so it describes a landowner's managed farm in the most commercialized corner of seventeenth-century China, not an ordinary smallholding. Its limits: the online copy is incomplete (it stops partway, marked to be continued) and does not contain the manual's passage on pigs; it is a digital transcription with no page references, with gaps where characters could not be typed (marked （丕刂） and #), and the calendar is followed by 37 numbered notes (注释) that are a modern editor's glosses in modern Chinese, not the manual's words, so a term's meaning taken from them is a modern reading; and it names Zhang as of the Ming where other accounts put his work in the early Qing.

Used for: the Shen manual's farm: its rice-and-mulberry calendar (homesteads)
