teitetsu-kotobank

Kotobank, "teitetsu" (horseshoe) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B9%84%E9%89%84-100286)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the horseshoe, gathering the entries of several Japanese reference works (among them the Heibonsha world encyclopedia and the Nipponica encyclopedia), with a history of shoeing in Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its entries say the iron shoe was known in Japan through the Portuguese and the Dutch, that the shogun Yoshimune had a Dutchman teach shoeing in 1729, that the shoe was hardly used before the end of the Edo period, and that it spread with Meiji. It is a reference summary; other pages give 1725 or 1733 for the Dutch teaching, and it gives no count of shod horses in any period.

Used for: that Edo Japan knew the iron shoe and hardly used it (urban-features)
