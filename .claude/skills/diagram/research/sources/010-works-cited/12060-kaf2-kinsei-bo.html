kaf2-kinsei-bo

Kanagawa Archaeology Foundation, "近世の墓" (Early modern graves), in its archaeology museum's permanent exhibition pages (in Japanese; https://www.kaf2.org/josetsu/kinse/kinse_iko/kinse_grave)

What it is: A short museum page by the prefectural archaeology foundation of Kanagawa on Edo-period graves, drawing on an excavated grave area at a site in Sagamihara that had been reburied when its temple moved.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states, from excavation, that temple-attached graveyards spread in the Edo period and that the dead came more often to be buried at temples, and that a temple's grave area was so limited that burials overlapped. Its limits are that it is a brief exhibition text for a general audience, drawn from one excavated site in eastern Japan, and gives no figure for how common the temple graveyard was against the settlement's own ground.

Used for: that Edo-period graveyards were often attached to temples, and crowded (religion-and-death)
