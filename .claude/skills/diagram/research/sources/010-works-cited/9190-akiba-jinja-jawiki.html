ja.wikipedia 秋葉神社 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A7%8B%E8%91%89%E7%A5%9E%E7%A4%BE)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Akiba shrines, the fire-quelling cult whose small shrines are among the most widely distributed in Japan, noting that such a shrine is often built of stone because stone does not burn easily, that they are usually small, and that several of them may stand within a single town block.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the density our temple-quarter rule needs - several small shrines to one town block - and the reason a block would raise one, which is a placement fact a map can use. Its limits are that it is tertiary and describes one cult; that its statement about material runs against the vermilion wooden shed the map draws, which the record now labels a drawing convention on that evidence; and that it says nothing about a temple quarter, so the placement between temples remains unsupported.

Used for: the small neighborhood shrine, several to a town block, and the fire cult that explains why a block raised one
