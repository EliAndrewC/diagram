tabikoramu-hakone

年金人生爺婆の旅 ("Travels of a retired couple", title translated), 箱根関所 天下の関所が現代に蘇る 象も関所を通る ("The Hakone barrier: the realm's great barrier revived today - even an elephant passed it", title translated) (https://tabikoramu.muragon.com/entry/297.html)

What it is: A personal travel blog's account (by the handle 森の忍者, posted 2020-06-22) of a visit to the rebuilt Hakone barrier, with photographs of each building and notes on its history.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the foot soldiers' guard house across from the main guard house and the upper rest house, beside the Edo-side gate, which is the fullest statement read of where the barrier's two stations stood. It is an amateur's secondhand description of the 2007 reconstruction, of one barrier only; the town's own site for the barrier (hakonesekisyo.jp), which rebuilt it from a late-Edo repair record, is the closer source. And a barrier is a checkpoint on a highway, not a walled town's gate, so its layout stands in for a town's defenses rather than showing one.

Used for: the foot soldiers' guard house across from the main guard house, beside the Edo-side gate (defenses)
