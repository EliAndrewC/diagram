miyoshi-kurashi-mingu-1998

Miyoshi Town Museum of History and Folklore, くらしの民具（１９９８年度分） ("Tools of everyday life, the 1998 series", title translated), a monthly column from the town bulletin, Miyoshi, Saitama (http://www.jade.dti.ne.jp/~miyoshir/mingu/mingu98.html)

What it is: A year of short columns by the town's museum on the parts of the old Ikegami house, a large thatched farmhouse moved onto the museum's grounds: its roof, its three doorways, its earth walls, its veranda, its main post, its sills and its earth floor.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says how this one farmhouse's big door and veranda were opened by day and closed at night and in bad weather, and how its inner earth walls were built. It describes one large house, which the museum's own page on it (miyoshi-ikegami-house) dates to the end of the Edo period or a little after, as its museum shows it; how the doors and veranda were used is told by the museum in general terms and from the house's fittings, not from a period document. The one local resident it quotes remembers mixing plaster for an earth wall, not this house's daily use. Its steps for building an earth wall are the general method, and it does not say this house's inner walls were plastered.

Used for: the big door shut and barred at night and the wicket used then; the veranda's storm shutters and paper screens (Estates and other compounds); a farmhouse's earth walls on lath (Homesteads)
