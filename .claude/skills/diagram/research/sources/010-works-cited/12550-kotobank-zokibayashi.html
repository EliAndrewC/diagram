kotobank-zokibayashi

Kotobank, 雑木林 ("mixed broadleaf wood", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - entries from the Digital Daijisen, the Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and Heibonsha's Kaitei shinpan Sekai dai-hyakka jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%9B%91%E6%9C%A8%E6%9E%97-552019)

What it is: Dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the zokibayashi; the encyclopedia entry (by Tsutsumi Toshio) defines it as the secondary deciduous broadleaf wood near habitation, fuel woods included, and names its trees.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the wood nearest a settlement was made of and what it was for, in a forest scientist's words, which is what the wood drawn at a town's margin needs. It is a modern definition in a tertiary work: its only dates are the dictionary's two example quotations of the word, from 1903 and 1915, both after the Meiji Restoration, so it does not show the woods or the word before then; it hedges with "often"; and it gives no size of such a wood or distance from the settlement, so it supports what the wood was and what grew in it, not how much of it to draw; and its note that such woods are valuable near cities is about their conservation value today, not their use in the past.

Used for: the mixed wood near habitation as secondary deciduous broadleaf woods including fuel woods, and its trees (towns)
