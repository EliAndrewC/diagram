kotobank-zokibayashi

Kotobank, 雑木林 ("mixed broadleaf wood", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - entries from the Digital Daijisen, the Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and Heibonsha's Kaitei shinpan Sekai dai-hyakka jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%9B%91%E6%9C%A8%E6%9E%97-552019)

What it is: Dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the zokibayashi; the encyclopedia entry (by Tsutsumi Toshio) defines it as the secondary deciduous broadleaf wood near habitation, fuel woods included, and names its trees.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the wood nearest a settlement was made of and what it was for, in a forest scientist's words, which is what the wood drawn at a town's margin needs. It is a modern definition: it gives no date for the woods it describes, hedges with "often", and its note that such woods are valuable near cities is about their conservation value today, not their use in the past.

Used for: the mixed wood near habitation as secondary deciduous broadleaf woods including fuel woods, and its trees (towns)
