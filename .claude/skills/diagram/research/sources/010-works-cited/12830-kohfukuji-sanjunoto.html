kohfukuji-sanjunoto

Kohfukuji (興福寺), Nara, 三重塔（さんじゅうのとう） ("the three-story pagoda", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.kohfukuji.com/property/a-0002/)

What it is: The temple's own page on its Kamakura-period three-story pagoda, a national treasure, giving its height and the width of its first story.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one of the few pagoda pages that gives a base width beside a height. Kohfukuji is one of the great temples of Nara, and this is its smaller, secondary pagoda, built for an empress in 1143 and rebuilt soon after a fire in 1180. It is one surviving building measured today, not a range. It belongs to a great temple in the old capital, so it fits a city or great temple, while a village or small-town temple would usually have no pagoda. It is also a Japanese wooden pagoda with a square plan, so it does not stand for the Chinese pagodas of the setting, which were often brick or stone, often octagonal, and built to other proportions.

Used for: how tall and wide a three-story pagoda was (religion-and-death)
