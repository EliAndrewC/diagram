Wang Jingying and Ochiai Chiho, "Spatial composition and building techniques of farmhouses prone to windstorms: a case study in Arakawa Village, Shiga Prefecture, Japan", Journal of Asian Architecture and Building Engineering 21:6 (2022), DOI 10.1080/13467581.2021.1972810 (https://doi.org/10.1080/13467581.2021.1972810)

What it is: Wang Jingying and Ochiai Chiho, "Spatial composition and building techniques of farmhouses prone to windstorms: a case study in Arakawa Village, Shiga Prefecture, Japan", a peer-reviewed open-access article in the Journal of Asian Architecture and Building Engineering (2022). It reconstructs the house lots of one windstorm-prone village on the west shore of Lake Biwa from a Meiji-era land register - 61 lots, 46 of them households - verifies them by field survey and interviews, and analyzes where each building type stood on its lot.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a measured study of where the buildings of a Japanese farmstead stood, which is what places the outbuildings on our homesteads, and it is the source of one number the record uses as it stands - that 72.7% of the toilets sat on the southeast-to-south side of the lot, which the authors attribute to sunlight speeding the fermentation of night soil - and of one negative, that its wind-siting finding concerns storage buildings and retirement houses and says nothing about siting a privy downwind. Its limits are that it is one village and a small count behind the percentage - the paper prints no toilet total at all, and the 72.7% is a share of the toilets that exist among the Type V1 houses alone, 33 of the 46 households, so it says where the toilets on those lots stood and not what fraction of farmsteads had one there; that the village was chosen because it is windstorm-prone, so its wind-driven siting is a strong case rather than a typical one; that the layouts are Meiji-era, after the Restoration and so later than the setting, though from a register rather than from houses surviving today; and that the toilet finding is the authors' explanation, not a tested cause.

Used for: the 72.7% of toilets sited southeast-to-south for solar warmth to speed fermentation of night soil - the figure the GM ruled be used literally as the seat share. Also used for what it does NOT say: its wind-siting finding covers storage buildings and retirement houses rather than toilets, and it contains no leeward/downwind/odor/hygiene language, which is what contradicted the proposal to seat privies downwind.
