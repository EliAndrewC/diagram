masuami-yosui-ueda

上田地域こども自然電子図鑑 (Ueda Region Children's Nature Electronic Encyclopedia), 桝網用水 ("the Masuami channel", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://museum.umic.jp/kawa/segi/r-masuami.html)

What it is: A page of a children's online encyclopedia of the Ueda region (Nagano) on the Masuami channel, a farm-irrigation channel that runs through the city of Ueda: where it was first drawn, which villages it waters, and how its intake moved.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the channel was drawn in the Ueda domain's Sengoku-family era from Amagafuchi, at the foot of the castle town, to irrigate three villages downstream (Suwabe, Akiwa and Kami-Shiojiri) - river water taken in at the foot of a castle and put to the fields below the town. It also says the channel carries the urban area's rainwater, a role it calls one no other weir has. It is written for children and names no documents; the rainwater role belongs to the channel as it runs through the modern city today, beside the page's account of urban development and household wastewater, and is no evidence that the Edo-period channel drained the castle town; its intake has since moved upstream (to Hori, then Komaki), and its present course runs in a tunnel under the river and beneath a shopping street; the page itself is unsure when the channel took the name Masuami (probably before 1882); it gives no date beyond the era; and it does not say the channel was drawn from the castle moat itself.

Used for: a channel drawn at a castle town to water the villages below it (cities/hinterland)
