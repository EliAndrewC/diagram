Town News (Zama edition), 近世の秣場とは 郷土講演会 ("What was the early-modern fodder ground? A local-history lecture", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 2013-10-25 (in Japanese; https://www.townnews.co.jp/0403/2013/10/25/209793.html)

What it is: A short notice in a free local paper announcing a Zama city board of education lecture on the fodder grounds of early-modern Sagamino, with a one-sentence account of what a fodder ground was.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in plain words that the fodder ground was the villages' shared land where roofing material and livestock fodder were cut, which is what the grass margin beyond a town's fields was for. It is a lecture notice, not the lecture (given by Hara Kazuyuki of the Zama city-history compilation committee): one sentence, about one district of the Kanto in the Edo period, with no sizes and no author's text behind it. The ground it describes was the shared land of several villages, spread across what are now Sagamigaoka and Hibarigaoka: a wide upland common, not a strip at the edge of one town's fields. It says what such ground was cut for, not how large it was or how near a town it lay.

Used for: the fodder ground as village common land cut for thatch and fodder (towns)
