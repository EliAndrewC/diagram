visitbeijing-daqingmen

Li Zhe (李哲), 消失的中华门 ("The Vanished Zhonghua Gate"; title translated from the Chinese by this project), 《旅游》 (Tourism) magazine, 2022-12-01, carried on Visit Beijing (北京旅游网) (https://lyzz.visitbeijing.com.cn/news/detail?id=24260)

What it is: A popular feature by Li Zhe for 《旅游》 (Tourism) magazine, published 2022-12-01 and carried on Visit Beijing, the Beijing municipal tourism bureau's site. It traces the Daqing gate south of Tiananmen from the Ming to its demolition and lists the central offices that lined the streets either side of it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names, street by street, which ministries stood east of the gate and which west, which is how the record can say that five ministries stood together and Justice stood apart with the courts. It is a magazine feature for tourists, not a plan. It cites no source for its list of offices and gives no sizes. Its east-side row of five ministries holds for both dynasties. Its west-side list (Imperial Equipage, Sacrifices, Censorate, Justice, Judicial Review) is the Qing arrangement: in the Ming that side held a chief military commission, the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, the Office of Transmission and the Embroidered Uniform Guard. The three judicial offices then stood farther west in the city; the Qing built its Ministry of Justice on the Guard's old site (《日下旧闻考》). So Justice standing apart holds for both dynasties, but where the page puts it is Qing only. It describes one imperial capital, not a provincial seat.

Used for: where each of the six ministries stood in the Ming-Qing capital (cities/government)
