huiguan-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 会馆 ("huiguan, the guild or native-place hall", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/%E4%BC%9A%E9%A6%86)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia stub on the huiguan (also called gongsuo), the hall of a Ming-Qing association of fellow-provincials or of merchants of one trade.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the institution (the earliest known, in Beijing, from the Yongle reign of the early fifteenth century) and says what it began as and became - a place of fellowship and lodging for people from one home region, then for some a merchants' association. It is a short, tertiary article on a city institution. Its one scholarly citation (Qiu Pengsheng's 1989 study of eighteenth- and nineteenth-century Suzhou trade associations) is attached to its definition, not to the Yongle date, which it gives without a source; the date is the stub's claim, which a scholarly history of the huiguan (He Bingdi's of 1966, for one) would have to carry. It says nothing of county towns, of the building, or of where it stood.

Used for: the Chinese counterpart of a townspeople's hall (towns)
