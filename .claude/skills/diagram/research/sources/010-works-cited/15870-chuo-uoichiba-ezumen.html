chuo-uoichiba-ezumen

Chuo City (Tokyo), 日本橋魚市場絵図面 ("Plans of the Nihonbashi fish market", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the ward's cultural-properties page, 2024-07-24 (https://www.city.chuo.lg.jp/a0052/bunkakankou/rekishi/bunkazai/shiteibunkazai/nihonbashiuoichibaezumen.html)

What it is: A short column from the ward's bulletin (first printed 2017-09-21) by Masuyama Kazunari, the ward's chief cultural-properties advisor, describing three designated scrolls of 1745, 1837 and 1842 that plan the Nihonbashi fish market on the north bank of the Nihonbashi river. From the 1842 scroll it counts the landing piers (12), the storage sheds along the bank and the selling sheds (50), the wells (25) and the stalls pushed out into the street (126), and it gives the size of the boards fish were sold from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a local government's reading of period plans of a real city fish market at its landing, with counts taken from the plans, the best-grounded account of the form read. It is Edo's great market, far larger than a provincial city's, and the board size is given from outside the plans, which do not show the boards; that the boards crowded the narrow stalls is the author's inference, not a record. It is a short summary of the plans, not the plans: its counts come from the 1842 scroll alone, and it gives no positions or frontages beyond the stalls' usual 3 shaku, so it carries the market's parts and their numbers, not its layout.

Used for: where a city's fish market stood and what it looked like - piers, bankside sheds, wells, street stalls and the selling boards (cities/river-cities)
