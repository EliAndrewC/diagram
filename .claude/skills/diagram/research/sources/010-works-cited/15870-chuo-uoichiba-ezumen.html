chuo-uoichiba-ezumen

Chuo City (Tokyo), 日本橋魚市場絵図面 ("Plans of the Nihonbashi fish market", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the ward's cultural-properties page, 2024-07-24 (https://www.city.chuo.lg.jp/a0052/bunkakankou/rekishi/bunkazai/shiteibunkazai/nihonbashiuoichibaezumen.html)

What it is: The ward's description of three designated scrolls of 1745, 1837 and 1842 that plan the Nihonbashi fish market on the north bank of the Nihonbashi river: its landing piers, the storage and selling sheds along the bank, its wells and the stalls along the street, with the size of the boards fish were sold from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a local government's reading of period plans of a real city fish market at its landing, with counts taken from the plans, the best-grounded account of the form read. It is Edo's great market, far larger than a provincial city's, and the board size is given from outside the plans, which do not show the boards.

Used for: where a city's fish market stood and what it looked like - piers, bankside sheds, wells, street stalls and the selling boards (cities/river-cities)
