ja.wikipedia 大社造 ("taisha-zukuri, the Grand Shrine architectural style", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大社造) - the 1744 Izumo hall 「it is enormous, at about 24 m」 (translated; original: 「約24mと巨大である」); no plan dimension

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 大社造 (taisha-zukuri), on what it calls one of the oldest styles of shrine building (with shinmei-zukuri and sumiyoshi-zukuri), named for the main sanctuary of Izumo Taisha: its near-square plan divided into four around a central pillar, set against the other early styles, with a list of shrines built in it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the plan and the height of the largest shrine sanctuary in Japan, the upper bound for a shrine hall's size. Its limits are that its one height is for the Izumo sanctuary of 1744, the national outlier rather than a provincial shrine, and that it gives no dimension of the plan - only that the 1744 hall's floor is laid with 60 tatami mats, an area and not a plan, and since when it does not know - so the plan's size in ken is this project's reading.

Used for: the Izumo sanctuary's height of about 24 m and its near-square, four-part plan; the same negative - no dimension of the plan (buildings.html).
