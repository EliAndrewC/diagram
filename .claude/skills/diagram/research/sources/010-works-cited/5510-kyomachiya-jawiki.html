What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on 京町家, the Kyoto townhouse: the narrow-fronted, deep-lot merchant house, its plan and its history. It also takes up the popular story that the shape was a dodge against a tax on frontage width, and rebuts it, explaining that Edo-period Kyoto assessed a total on the block community, which the house-owning townsmen then divided per house.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the shape of an urban lot in a dense premodern city and for the correction to the frontage-tax story that a more popular source repeats. It is a tertiary summary, and it describes one city's building tradition, so our towns take the proportions of the form rather than Kyoto's specific detail.

Used for: the narrow-front deep-lot machiya, and its own rebuttal of the frontage-tax story (「Under the Edo-period tax system of Kyoto, without regard to frontage width, a total sum was first levied on the cho (the block community),」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の京都の税制は、間口幅に関係なく、まず町に対し総額が賦課され」))
