iwashimizu-mizugaki

Agency for Cultural Affairs, 国指定文化財等データベース ("Database of Nationally Designated Cultural Properties", title translated from the Japanese by this project), entry for 石清水八幡宮本社 ("Iwashimizu Hachimangu, main shrine"), building record of the Takeuchi-sha sanctuary with the designation's full commentary (in Japanese; https://kunishitei.bunka.go.jp/heritage/detail/102/00004828)

What it is: The national database's record for the main-shrine buildings of Iwashimizu Hachimangu, designated a National Treasure in 2016, with a commentary on their date, the builder and the layout of fence, cloisters and halls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the present buildings, among them a fence enclosing the sanctuary, to the shogunate's rebuilding of 1634, a premodern example of the form. Its limits: one of the great shrines of the realm, rebuilt by the shogunate, not a village shrine; the commentary is a present-day architectural description written from survey reports of 2007 and 2014 (by Yawata City's board of education and the shrine); and the fence it describes is an elaborate one - lattice bays under a copper-sheet roof with gates and carved, painted panels - evidence that such a sanctuary was fenced, not of what a village shrine's fence looked like.

Used for: a fence enclosing a great shrine's sanctuary in the shogunate's rebuilding of 1634 (religion-and-death)
