qimin-yaoshu-yangji

齊民要術 卷六 養雞第五十九, wikisource (四庫全書本) (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/齊民要術_(四庫全書本)/卷06)

What it is: The chapter on raising chickens (卷六 養雞第五十九) of the Qimin Yaoshu (齊民要術, "Essential Techniques for the Common People"), the sixth-century Chinese agricultural treatise compiled by Jia Sixie, read in the Siku Quanshu edition hosted on Chinese Wikisource. Its instruction on housing is direct: set a cage on the ground with a perch inside it rather than letting the birds roost in the trees, where wind and cold thin the big ones and kill the small.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a farming manual from the very tradition our setting is modeled on, telling us how a poultry shelter was meant to be built and why, which is what fixes the ground-level coop in a farmyard rather than a roost in a tree. Three limits worth stating: a manual prescribes good practice and does not record what an average household did; it is sixth-century northern China, far in both time and place from the Edo Japan our maps also draw on; and the text is a much later imperial edition of it, given here in this project's own English rendering of the classical Chinese.

Used for: the chicken roost as a ground-level enclosure with a perch, preferred to the trees.
