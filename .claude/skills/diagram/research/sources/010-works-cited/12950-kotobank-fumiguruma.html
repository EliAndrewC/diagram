kotobank-fumiguruma

kotobank 踏車 ("Treadle wheel", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B8%8F%E8%BB%8A-125762)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the reference-encyclopedia entries on the fumiguruma, the treadle wheel, chief among them Horio Hisashi's entry in the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, with an excerpt from that encyclopedia's older entry on water wheels.

Why it applies, and its limits: Horio's entry gives what a map needs to know of the device - portable rather than fixed, a lift of about a yard, two or three in series for higher paddies, one person instead of the chain pump's two, and the bucket swung by two people where a farm had no wheel - and the water-wheel excerpt says Japanese paddies depended little on lifting at all. It is a dictionary summary of the agrarian-history literature, with no counts per village.

Used for: the treadle wheel's lift, portability and crew; the swing bucket; how little Japanese paddies relied on lifting (water)
