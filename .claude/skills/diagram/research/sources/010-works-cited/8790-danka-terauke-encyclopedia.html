danka-terauke-encyclopedia

Duncan Ryūken Williams, "Parish (Danka, Terauke) System in Japan", Encyclopedia of Buddhism (Macmillan Reference, 2004), on encyclopedia.com (https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/parish-danka-terauke-system-japan)

What it is: The Encyclopedia of Buddhism's article on the Tokugawa parish system, signed by a historian of Tokugawa Buddhism and republished on encyclopedia.com: how every household came to be registered with a Buddhist temple, what the temple certified, and what it gave in return.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the parish logic our burial grounds rest on, in three parts: temple membership was not an individual affair but the household's, the household's main benefit was the funerary and continuing memorial services the temple provided for all its members, and the affiliation was certified by the local temple and the village officials. That is why a graveyard on these maps is the hall's own yard rather than a plot held apart from it. Its limits are that it describes a state-mandated anti-Christian registration specific to Tokugawa Japan while this setting reaches the same arrangement by another route, so the mechanism differs even where the outcome matches; and that it is a reference article rather than a study - though a signed one by a specialist in this exact subject, carrying its own bibliography, so it is not the anonymous summary a tertiary label usually means.

Used for: the household as the registered unit of temple affiliation, and the funerals and memorial rites the temple renders it
