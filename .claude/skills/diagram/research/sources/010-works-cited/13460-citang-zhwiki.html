citang-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 祠堂 ("citang, the ancestral hall", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A5%A0%E5%A0%82)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the ancestral hall: what it was for, its kinds, the history of who was allowed to build one, and where halls survive.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a hall served for a lineage's meetings and feasts as well as its rites, that commoners were widely allowed to build them only from the Ming's Jiajing reign, and that more survive in the south than the north - where a south-China village's lineage met. Its limits: a tertiary article; the meeting-and-feasting line rests on a lineage's own website and the Jiajing date on an anonymous web page; the southern survival has no citation and describes which halls outlasted the Cultural Revolution's destruction in the north, a modern survival pattern, not where halls were built before modern times, so it does not show that a south-China village had one; it gives no size or siting for a hall.

Used for: the hall as a lineage's meeting place, when commoners could build one, and where halls survive (homesteads)
