wuju-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 武舉 ("military examination", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E6%AD%A6%E4%B8%BE)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the wuju, the imperial military examination (revision of 2026-04-07), from its Tang origins through the Song, Ming and Qing, with sections on Korea's and Vietnam's and a list of the Qing top military graduates.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets out the upper levels of the Ming and Qing military examination - provincial, metropolitan and palace - and places the Qing palace examination in the Hall of Supreme Harmony before the emperor, which puts those levels at the provincial and imperial capitals. A tertiary summary, with limits: it does not itself say where the provincial examination sat; its Ming palace level is thin (its own list of Ming top military graduates names one man, Wang Laipin); and it omits the stage below, the county, prefectural and qualifying examinations a Qing military candidate passed to become a military licentiate, the first of them held at the county seat - so it cannot show that the county seat had no part in the military examination. It says nothing of where candidates trained or whether a county seat had private martial schools.

Used for: the levels of the Ming and Qing military examination (government)
