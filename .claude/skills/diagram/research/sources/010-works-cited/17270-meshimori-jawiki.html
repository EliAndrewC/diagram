meshimori-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 飯盛女 ("meshimori onna", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/飯盛女)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the meshimori onna, the serving women of the Edo-period post-town inns, most of them unlicensed prostitutes tolerated under the name of inn servants (the article notes the name also covered women who only waited at table), with the shogunate's caps of 1772 and the case of the three post towns under Mount Asama.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where the trade of a post town went on - in its inns, under the name of inn servants - which is what decides that an inn town draws nothing more for it. Its 1772 caps are for four post towns only - Senju, Itabashi, Shinagawa and Naito-Shinjuku, the four gateway post towns at the edge of Edo and the largest in the country - and are no measure of an ordinary post town; it gives no population figures, only the statement (from a 1987 local history) that women much outnumbered men in two of the three Asama post towns, Karuizawa and Oiwake; and its footnotes are to 1980s popular and local histories.

Used for: the serving women of the post-town inns, their tolerated status and the 1772 caps (Trades and services)
