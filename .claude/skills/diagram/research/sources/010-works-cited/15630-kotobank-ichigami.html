kotobank-ichigami

Kotobank, 市神 ("ichigami, the market god", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the entries of the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica) and others (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/市神)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering Japanese reference works' entries on the market god, two of them signed by the folklorists Kitami Toshio and Kamata Hisako, the rest unsigned dictionary and encyclopedia entries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the market god's shrine or stone at a village boundary, a landing, a bridge foot or a crossroads, and says a market is inferred from where one stands; one entry (Kitami's) dates the market-god festival to the year's first market, while the others give about the tenth of the first month, the year's start and end, or the storehouse opening or Little New Year, and one says few places fix a day. It is a set of short encyclopedia entries summarizing twentieth-century folklore observation of what survives in Japan, not a survey of any one town or a record of any period; one entry says most surviving stones now stand in village shrine precincts, so a stone's present place is not always its first; and it gives no size for the shrine or the stone.

Used for: where the market-god stone stands, and when its festival was held (towns)
