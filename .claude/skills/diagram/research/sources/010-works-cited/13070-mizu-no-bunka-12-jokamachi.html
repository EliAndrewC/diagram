mizu-no-bunka-12-jokamachi

Mizkan Water Culture Center, 水の文化 no. 12, 近世城下町に見る水道の知恵 ("The wisdom of the water systems of early modern castle towns", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.mizu.gr.jp/kikanshi/no12/02.html)

What it is: An interview in the Mizkan Water Culture Center's magazine with Kanki Kazuo, a Kobe University historian of civil engineering, on the town water systems of the Edo-period castle towns, with Omihachiman's townspeople's well associations as the worked example.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the river-fed water systems rulers built into castle towns nearly all doubled as irrigation and fed moats and garden ponds too, and describes the other kind: a parent well at the source, bamboo pipes to take-wells, run by a well association of its users. It is an interview in a food company's foundation magazine, though the speaker reports his own surveys and prints a table, from his 1994 chapter, of about forty known pre-Meiji water systems by town type, source and use: a list of known systems, not a share of all towns. It speaks of towns, not villages (only three villages are in the table), and it says the townspeople's kind was small and served only household and fire water. The Omihachiman details it prints (the 25 systems, the pipe mix, the well association's rules) come from a 1933 town history and rules of 1930, by which time clay and concrete pipe and pumps had come in; the bamboo-pipe layout is the older form.

Used for: castle-town water systems doubling as irrigation and moat supply; piped water to take-wells (water)
