en.wikipedia Abolition of the han system (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_han_system)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the Meiji government's replacement of the domains (han) by prefectures, in 1869-1871.

Why it applies, and its limits: It counts the domains that survived to the 1871 edict, 261, which is the nearest readable count to the end of the Edo period. It is a tertiary summary, and the sentence giving 261 carries no footnote (the article is flagged for lacking inline citations; Jansen, The Making of Modern Japan, which it lists, is the closer source that would trace the count). The count is taken just after the period rather than in it, once some domains had already been dissolved, so it likely understates the late-Edo figure: the same article's account of 1869 implies about 262 to 276 domains, and other pages give other figures (274 daimyo in 1869, "just under 300" domains). It is a round order of magnitude, not an exact census.

Used for: the number of domains at the close of the Edo period (government)
