Otsubo (TAKAYASU Co., LIQ), 台所の歴史 ―キッチンと呼ばれるまで― (The history of the kitchen, until it was called a kitchen) (in Japanese; https://www.liq-takayasu.com/blog/53)

What it is: A blog post by the public-relations staff of a kitchen-fittings company, tracing the Japanese kitchen from prehistory to the modern era, with pictures from the National Diet Library's digital collections, among them the kitchen of the Ishiyamadera engi scroll and a middle-rank samurai house's kamado.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says plainly that a samurai house's kitchen was board-floored and that, its doma being small, the kamado could stand on the board floor, which is the choice a residence's kitchen hearth is drawn from. It is a company's popular summary, not a study, it names no source for that sentence, and its general rule that the kamado stood on the doma is written beside a passage on commoners' rowhouses.

Used for: the samurai kitchen's board floor and the kamado on it or on the doma (buildings)
