Otsubo (TAKAYASU Co., LIQ), 台所の歴史 ―キッチンと呼ばれるまで― (The history of the kitchen, until it was called a kitchen) (in Japanese; https://www.liq-takayasu.com/blog/53)

What it is: A blog post (24 February 2023) by the public-relations staff of TAKAYASU, a Kyoto builder of custom houses, tracing the Japanese kitchen from prehistory to the modern era, with pictures from the National Diet Library's digital collections, among them the kitchen of the Ishiyamadera engi scroll, and a photograph of the kamado in the middle-rank samurai house reconstructed at Bōsō no Mura, Chiba Prefecture's open-air museum.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says plainly that a samurai house's kitchen was board-floored and that, its doma being small, the kamado could stand on the board floor, which is the choice a residence's kitchen hearth is drawn from. It is a company's popular summary, not a study, it names no source for that sentence, and its general rule that the kamado stood on the doma is written beside a passage on commoners' rowhouses. Its samurai sentence is in its Edo-period section and is illustrated by a middle-rank retainer's house, so it speaks for the kitchens of middle and lower samurai, not for a great lord's residence, whose large kitchen (the post's own header picture is a daimyo's ōdaidokoro) is another scale of place.

Used for: the samurai kitchen's board floor and the kamado on it or on the doma (buildings)
