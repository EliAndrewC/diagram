thcdc-half-moon-pond

Hakka Cultural Development Center (Taiwan Hakka Museum), "Half Moon Pond" (English page, published 2018-11-23, updated 2020-10-28) (https://thcdc.hakka.gov.tw/8184/8185/8200/8212/10620/post)

What it is: The English page of Taiwan's national Hakka museum on the half-moon pond dug in its own grounds, explaining the pond in front of a traditional Hakka house in Taiwan: the geomantic reason for water in front and high ground behind, that a household with no stream in front dug the pond by hand, the pond and the mound behind the house read as yin and yang, the museum's two hedged reasons why the pond is half a circle, and how field water was led into the pond by a channel and slowed so the pond lies still.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the half-moon pond's doctrine and its water supply plainly, so the crescent pond's explanation can rest on it instead of on general reading; but closer and fuller sources can be read and were not used - the museum's own Chinese page, of which this is a loose translation (https://thcdc.hakka.gov.tw/1241/12339/1285/2539/post), and Li Xuxian's referenced paper on the half-moon ponds of the Hakka enclosed houses of Meizhou on the mainland (https://www.sinoss.net/upload/resources/file/2024/07/16/35948.pdf). Its limits: it is a museum's popular page, not a study, and cites nothing; it describes the Hakka HOUSE of Taiwan, settled under the Qing, not a mainland village or a whole lineage village; it says itself that no record explains when the ponds began, it gives two reasons for the half shape and hedges both - "most likely" the pond before a school of the examination system, and, "inferred", deference to early rules against surpassing officials; its English is a loose translation (its "metamorphosis fengshui" is apparently the form school of Yang Yunsong), so a phrase quoted from it is the translator's wording, not the museum's; and its account of the water supply is of households standing among fields, which need not hold for a village pond elsewhere.

Used for: water in front and high ground behind, the pond dug by hand where no stream obliges, the pond and mound as yin and yang, the half shape's two hedged reasons, and the pond fed by a channel of field water (homesteads)
