Guide to Authentic Tokyo, 水都を偲ぶ －暮らしを支えた河川と掘割－ ("Remembering the water city: the rivers and canals that carried daily life", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 東京クロニクル series (https://guidetokyo.info/history/chronicle/chronicle11.html)

What it is: A local-history article on the Nihonbashi, Yaesu and Kyobashi districts of Edo and their water: the castle-building campaigns that cut the canals, the ten boat-entry canals dug to land stone for the castle, the chain of named landings along the Nihonbashi river, and the landings that became markets.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the canal district of a real Edo-period city - how its canals were cut, faced and used - in period terms. It is a district guide's history column, not scholarship, and names no sources; it is used for what the canals were cut for and what stood on their banks, and gives no widths.

Used for: Edo's boat-entry canals cut to land castle stone, the castle side faced in stone against a landing on the other bank, a river lined with landings, and a landing that served as a produce market (cities/river-cities, cities/capitals)
