guidetokyo-edo-horiwari

Guide to Authentic Tokyo, 水都を偲ぶ －暮らしを支えた河川と掘割－ ("Remembering the water city: the rivers and canals that carried daily life", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 東京クロニクル series (https://guidetokyo.info/history/chronicle/chronicle11.html)

What it is: A popular local-history article by Sugawara Kenji (菅原健二, author of reference books on the rivers of Edo and Tokyo such as 『川の地図辞典』), dated 2016-10-18 and reprinted from the magazine 東京人 (July 2016 special issue) on a district site run by the developer Tokyo Tatemono. It covers the Nihonbashi, Yaesu and Kyobashi districts of Edo and their water, from 1590 to the present: the castle-building campaigns that cut the canals, the ten boat-entry canals dug to land stone for the castle, the chain of named landings along the Nihonbashi river, and the landings that became markets.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the canal district of a real Edo-period city - how its canals were cut, faced and used - in period terms. It is a popular article by a local river historian, reprinted on a developer's district site. It is not scholarship with notes, and it names only a few sources: Suzuki Masao's 『江戸の川・東京の川』 and the 1632 and 1842 city maps its figures are drawn from. It describes Edo, the shogun's capital, and the ten boat-entry canals were shogunal works that daimyo were ordered to build, so it is a capital's example, not a provincial river town's. It is used for what the canals were cut for and what stood on their banks. The only widths and depths it gives (Nihonbashi River 47 m wide, Kaede and Kyobashi rivers 33 m, all 1.8 m deep) come from the 1920s rebuilding after the earthquake, which widened and deepened the rivers for engine-driven boats. They are not Edo widths and must not be drawn from. The formal landing names it lists were fixed in 1876-80, replacing the informal Edo names. It says 大根河岸 was a vegetable market until after 1923 but not when that began.

Used for: Edo's boat-entry canals cut to land castle stone, the castle side faced in stone against a landing on the other bank, a river lined with landings, and a landing that served as a produce market (cities/river-cities, cities/capitals)
