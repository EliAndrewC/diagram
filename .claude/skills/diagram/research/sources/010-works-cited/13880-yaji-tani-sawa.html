yaji-tani-sawa

谷治正孝 (Yaji Masataka, Yokohama National University), 地形図にみる「谷」地名と「沢」地名 (translated from the Japanese by this project, the title: "Tani and sawa place names seen on topographic maps"), 地図 (Journal of the Japan Cartographers Association) vol. 38 supplement, pp. 30-31 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjca1963/38/Supplement/38_Supplement_30/_pdf)

What it is: A short conference paper that counts the place names on Japan's topographic maps ending in 谷 (read tani, dani or ya) and in 沢 (sawa), and maps where each is found.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the two commonest terrain words in village names mean and where: tani names mostly in western Japan, sawa names almost all in eastern Japan, sawa meaning a mountain valley in the east and a marsh in the west, and ya names mostly in the east, where shallow valleys cut into uplands. Its limits: it counts modern map names, not Edo village names; the two pages state no year; and its glosses are one line each.

Used for: what tani, ya and sawa in a village's name say about its ground (homesteads)
