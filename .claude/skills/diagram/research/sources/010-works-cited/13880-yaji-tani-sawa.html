yaji-tani-sawa

谷治正孝 (Yaji Masataka, Yokohama National University), 地形図にみる「谷」地名と「沢」地名 (translated from the Japanese by this project, the title: "Tani and sawa place names seen on topographic maps"), 地図 (Journal of the Japan Cartographers Association) vol. 38 supplement (2000), pp. 30-31 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjca1963/38/Supplement/38_Supplement_30/_pdf)

What it is: A two-page conference abstract (2000) that counts, in Kanai Hiroo's index of the 516,655 names on Japan's topographic maps, the names containing 谷 (read tani, dani, ya, yatsu, yato, yachi or gaya) and 沢 (sawa), and maps where each is found.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what two common terrain words in Japanese place names mean and where (each is about 4% of the names on the maps): tani names mostly in western Japan, sawa names almost all in eastern Japan, sawa meaning a mountain valley in the east and a marsh in the west, and ya names mostly in the east, where shallow valleys cut into uplands. Its limits: it counts the names on modern topographic maps, not Edo village names, and many of them name valleys and streams rather than settlements (its eastern tani examples lie away from villages, and the author thinks some may date only from modern mountaineering and map surveys); its sawa meanings are today's usage; it is a two-page abstract whose glosses are one line each; and it covers Japan alone.

Used for: what tani, ya and sawa in a village's name say about its ground (homesteads)
