kotobank-teramachi

Kotobank, 寺町 - 改訂新版 世界大百科事典, デジタル大辞泉, ブリタニカ国際大百科事典 and 日本歴史地名大系 entries (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AF%BA%E7%94%BA-101789)

What it is: The kotobank reference page for the teramachi, carrying side by side the entries of seven Japanese reference works, four of which are quoted here, on the temple district: its collective, continuous placement at the key points of a castle town's perimeter, the military value of its large buildings and wide precincts in an emergency, and - in the historical gazetteer's place-name entries - the temple counts of particular towns' temple districts.

Why it applies, and its limits: Standard reference works on why a castle town's temples stood together at its edge, the rule our temple rim follows. The encyclopedias summarize the planning intent without citing the town plans it rests on; the gazetteer's counts are each dated by the town plan they are read from (Sakai's Genroku 2 (1689) map, Akita's plan of 1663, whose 43 includes three temples of a neighboring ward).

Used for: the collective placement of temples at the perimeter's key points for their military value; the temple counts of Sakai (~60 and ~50 in its two wards) and Akita (43 in 1663)
