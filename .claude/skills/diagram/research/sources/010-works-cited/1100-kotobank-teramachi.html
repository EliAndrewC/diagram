kotobank-teramachi

Kotobank, 寺町 - 改訂新版 世界大百科事典, デジタル大辞泉, ブリタニカ国際大百科事典 and 日本歴史地名大系 entries (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AF%BA%E7%94%BA-101789)

What it is: The kotobank reference page for the teramachi, carrying side by side the entries of eight Japanese reference works, four of which are quoted here, on the temple district: its collective, continuous placement at the key points of a castle town's perimeter, the military value of its large buildings and wide precincts in an emergency, and - in the historical gazetteer's place-name entries - the temple counts of particular towns' temple districts.

Why it applies, and its limits: Standard reference works on why a Japanese castle town's temples stood together, the rule our temple rim follows; nothing on the page speaks to Chinese walled cities. The encyclopedias summarize the planning intent without citing the town plans it rests on, and hedge it: the military value is what the placement "is thought" to have weighed (軍事的意義を考慮して配置されていたと思われる), beside the lord's control of religion, and the districts were often moved as towns grew in the later 17th century. The rim is one documented form among several - one outer district, a line along the edge, the road entrances, or key blocks inside the town. The gazetteer's counts are each dated by the town plan they are read from (Sakai's Genroku 2 (1689) map, Akita's plan of 1663, whose 43 includes three temples of a neighboring ward), and both are large places - Sakai a great merchant port under direct shogunate rule, Akita a large domain's capital; the same entries give far smaller districts elsewhere (Tottori 7, Yonago 9, Takamatsu 13).

Used for: the collective placement of temples at the perimeter's key points for their military value; the temple counts of Sakai (~60 and ~50 in its two wards) and Akita (43 in 1663)
