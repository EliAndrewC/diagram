basuiso-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, 馬水槽 ("basuiso, the horse-watering trough", title translated), version of 2025-06-03 (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A6%AC%E6%B0%B4%E6%A7%BD)

What it is: An encyclopedia article on the troughs set by roadsides and in stables for draft animals to drink from, with the history of the surviving Japanese examples: the stone trough the London Metropolitan Drinking Fountain and Cattle Trough Association gave to Tokyo City, set up in front of the city hall in 1906, and later ones of 1917 and 1928.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the arrival in Japan of the London-type public watering trough, a Meiji import with no Edo-period counterpart in that form, and a newspaper headline it cites (Yomiuri, 1906-11-29) calls the 1906 one the first. It is a tertiary article, and of Japan only; it says nothing of China or Korea. That no earlier trough is named is the article's silence, not a finding that none existed. It does not describe how horses were watered before 1906, though its own lead photograph (a hollowed-log trough in Manchukuo) and its see-also line (Batō Kannon shrines stand by horse watering places throughout Japan) show that plainer watering places existed, undated. It carries the date of the London-type trough, not an absence of animal watering places in the premodern setting.

Used for: that the London-type watering trough reached Japan in 1906 (urban-features)
