suido-ishizue-jori

水土の礎 (Suido no Ishizue), 地形に応じた地割、条理制 ("Land division suited to the terrain: the jori system", title translated), Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC) (https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/03/02.html)

What it is: A page of Suido no Ishizue, a history of Japan's land and water works published by the Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), on the ancient jori grid: its squares of one cho, how it spread from the Yamato and Yamashiro basins to Hokuriku, Tokai, the Inland Sea and northern Kyushu, and in eastern Japan to the fan-shaped basins of the middle reaches of rivers, and how the terrain bent it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that each square of the grid was planned to adjoin a road and a waterway (考えられている: the system's design, not a survey of squares found), and that in basins such as Omi the grid's axes ran with the main slope toward Lake Biwa and at right angles to it. So it carries a right-angled layout of channels on Japanese plains of the ancient and medieval periods, peaking about the ninth century, though that layout is this record's reading: the page never says the channels themselves ran at right angles. It is a general history for a lay audience by an agricultural-engineering body, citing no excavation for these sentences; it hedges the ninth-century peak as what is thought; and it describes land division at the scale of the 109 m square, not the ditches inside one. It says that outside the Yamato and Yamashiro core the grid lost its continuity and turned off true north-south. It says the grid was laid out in units of about 100 cho (about 120 ha) whose lines do not meet from one unit to the next (the Nobi plain). And it says that where floods, volcanic eruptions or large, shifting rivers struck, the grid itself did not take hold and vanished.

Used for: the jori square beside a road and a waterway, and the grid's axes on the main slope and at right angles to it (fields)
