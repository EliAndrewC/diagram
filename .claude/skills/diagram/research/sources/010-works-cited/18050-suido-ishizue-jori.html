suido-ishizue-jori

水土の礎 (Suido no Ishizue), 地形に応じた地割、条理制 ("Land division suited to the terrain: the jori system", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC) (https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/03/02.html)

What it is: A page of Suido no Ishizue, a history of Japan's land and water works published by the Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), on the ancient jori grid: its squares of one cho, how it spread from the Yamato and Yamashiro basins across the plains of western and central Japan, and how the terrain bent it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that every square of the grid adjoined a road and a waterway and that, in basins such as Omi, the grid's axes ran with the main slope and at right angles to it, so it carries a right-angled layout of channels on Japanese plains from the Nara and Heian periods. It is a general history for a lay audience by an agricultural-engineering body, citing no excavation for these sentences; it hedges the ninth-century peak as what is thought; and it describes land division at the scale of the 109 m square, not the ditches inside one, many of which it says did not survive later floods and river shifts.

Used for: the jori square beside a road and a waterway, and the grid's axes on the main slope and at right angles to it (fields)
