ja.wikipedia 別当寺 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%88%A5%E5%BD%93%E5%AF%BA) - 「Because the shrine's rites, such as sutra-reading before the kami, were performed in Buddhist form and their officiant was called the bettō (the head of the shrine monks), the temple where the bettō lived was called the bettō-ji.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「神前読経など神社の祭祀を仏式で行い、その主催者を別当（社僧の長のこと）と呼んだことから、別当の居る寺を別当寺と称した。」)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the bettō-ji, the temple attached to a shrine whose monks performed the shrine's rites in Buddhist form before 1868.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the arrangement itself - the shrine's rites performed in Buddhist form by the bettō, the head of the shrine monks, from the temple where he lived - and for its end at the Meiji separation. A short tertiary article with no count of how many village shrines had one.

Used for: the shrine's rites in Buddhist form from the resident bettō's temple (religion-and-death.html).
