Guernica, "Raising a Stink" (https://www.guernicamag.com/raising-a-stink/)

What it is: "Raising a Stink", a November 2021 piece by Lina Zeldovich in Guernica, a nonprofit magazine of art and politics, adapted from her book on human waste and sanitation. It describes the night-soil economy of premodern and early modern Japan - human excrement treated as a valuable farm commodity rather than as refuse, with collection contracts, contested collection rights and disputes over price - and sets it against European practice of the same centuries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, in a readable English account, the arrangements a map needs to know existed - the annual contract for a household's night soil paid in rice, and farmers supplying a lord's estate with firewood and seedlings for the right to empty its privies - and it names the scholarship it draws on. Its limits are that it is a popular magazine adaptation of a book rather than a study, used for the existence and form of the arrangements and never for prices, volumes or rates; and that the scholar it relies on for the market, Kayo Tajima, published her study of the Edo night-soil trade in an openly readable journal, so this is a retelling standing in front of a source a reader could reach.

Used for: farmers supplying daimyo estates with firewood and seedlings for the privilege of emptying their privies; the tsuke-tsubo annual contract paid in rice
