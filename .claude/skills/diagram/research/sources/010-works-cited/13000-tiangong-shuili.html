tiangong-shuili

Song Yingxing, 天工開物 ("The Works of Heaven and the Inception of Things", 1637), chapter 乃粒, section 水利 ("Irrigation", translated from the Chinese by this project), on Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E5%B7%A5%E9%96%8B%E7%89%A9/%E6%B0%B4%E5%88%A9)

What it is: The irrigation section of the late-Ming encyclopedia of crafts and farming, describing how water was lifted onto rice fields: the river noria, the chain pump trodden or ox-turned, the small hand pump, the wind-driven pump, the well-sweep and the windlass.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary description of Chinese practice with rates: one person's day waters 5 mu, an ox's double, the small hand pump 2 mu, and a noria on a river works day and night for a hundred mu. It is Ming, two to four centuries after the Song, written by an observer of the south, and it states rates rather than how many devices a village owned.

Used for: the noria, the chain pump's crews and rates, the small hand pump (water)
