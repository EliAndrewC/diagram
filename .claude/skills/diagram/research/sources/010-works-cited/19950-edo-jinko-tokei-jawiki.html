ja.wikipedia, 江戸時代の日本の人口統計 ("Population statistics of Edo-period Japan", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B1%9F%E6%88%B8%E6%99%82%E4%BB%A3%E3%81%AE%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E3%81%AE%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E7%B5%B1%E8%A8%88)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article gathering the population figures of Edo Japan - the shogunate's national surveys from 1721 to 1846, and the birth rates, infant mortality and life expectancies that historical demographers have estimated from village population registers (shumon aratame cho) and temple death registers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives premodern Japanese village figures - crude birth rates per 1,000 for seven villages between 1671 and 1871, life expectancies at birth such as Hayami Akira's 36.8 and 36.7 years for Saijo village in Mino, 1773 to 1869 - and says the national surveyed population was nearly flat from 1721 to 1846. It is an encyclopedia article compiling other scholars' estimates, which rest on registers that miss infants who died between yearly counts (the article itself says actual births may have been 10 to 20% more than were recorded, and several life tables assume an infant mortality of 200 per 1,000); it gives no crude death rate directly, so a death rate taken from it is this project's own inference from these figures. Its figures are for farming villages: the article itself says castle towns and Edo had higher death rates than the countryside, citing life expectancies at birth of about 20 to 21 years estimated from Edo skeletons, so a village rate understates the deaths of a town or a city.

Used for: the Edo death rate behind the burial-ground sizing (religion and death)
