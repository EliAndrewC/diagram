澎湃新闻, 这口大缸能防火？！ (https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_23268207)

What it is: 这口大缸能防火？！ ("This great vat can fight fire?!"), an article in 澎湃新闻 (The Paper) about the bronze and iron water vats standing in the courtyards of the Forbidden City - how many there were, the grades they came in, and the rule that governed where each grade stood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the water vats of the Forbidden City - 308 originally, 231 surviving, their type and number per courtyard fixed by Qing regulation, set where a timber hall stood far from water - which is the principle our compound yards borrow: standing water where the buildings are. Its limits are that it is a post by a government-affairs account on a news platform, popular writing rather than a study, carrying the statute's figures at one remove; and that the palace was the most heavily regulated and best funded compound in the empire, so the principle carries down to a magistracy yard and the count does not.

Used for: 308 originally, 231 surviving; Qing regulation fixing which vat type and how many per courtyard
