bujutsukarate-tategi

武術空手研究帳・増補（１０） 大間違いの鍛錬法 (A martial-arts and karate notebook, supplement 10: a badly mistaken training method) (in Japanese; http://bujutsukarate.com/zouho/10.htm)

What it is: An essay of August 2016 on his own site by Hasegawa Mitsumasa (born 1958), a self-taught researcher of old Okinawan karate, criticizing a sword school's training method as it is practised today. It sets the standing timber struck by a "main line" against the knee-high bundle of branches struck, under the same name, by its "branch line". It argues that the bundle of a dozen or more branches is a modern enlargement, made when the school was revived, of what had been a children's exercise with one to a few springy branches.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page found that gives the standing timber a size - a log of a little over 2 m set about 70 cm into the ground - and the height it is struck at. Its limits are that it is one practitioner's personal essay from another martial art, arguing a case, not a reference work; that it describes the two schools as they train today (2016), not from a period record, so its sizes are a modern measurement of an old form; that it deliberately does not name the two schools, which the tategi-uchi name and the bundle mark as the Satsuma schools of Jigen-ryū and its relatives only by inference; that its measurements are the writer's own, without a source; and that by the writer's own argument the knee-high bundle of a dozen or more branches is the branch line's form only since a twentieth-century revival, which he says enlarged an Edo-period children's exercise of one to a few branches. That claim is his own inference, but either way the bundle is attested only as a modern form.

Used for: the standing timber about 2 m long, set about 70 cm into the ground and struck from shoulder to solar-plexus height; the knee-high bundle of branches of the branch line (buildings)
