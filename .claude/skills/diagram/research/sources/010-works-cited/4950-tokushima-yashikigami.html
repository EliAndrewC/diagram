tokushima-yashikigami

徳島県立図書館 紀要 50, pp. 131-133, 屋敷神 (https://library.bunmori.tokushima.jp/digital/webkiyou/50/131-133.pdf)

What it is: A short field report, "On the yashikigami of Misato Village" (美郷村の屋敷神について) by Yuasa Yasuo, published in 2004 in issue 50 of the Bulletin of the Awa Academic Society (阿波学会紀要, pp. 131-133) by that society's history section, the Tokushima Historical Society, and posted online by the Tokushima Prefectural Library. It walks house by house through one village in Tokushima Prefecture on Shikoku, recording where each family's household guardian deity is enshrined and what its little shrine is made of.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a house-by-house record of a real village's household shrines - which houses kept one, what it was made of, where it stood, when it was honored - which is what puts a shrine of the right size in the right place on our homesteads, and it gives one measured shrine, a stone box about 40 cm on a side, and one under a persimmon tree 20 m east of the house. Its limits are that it is a 2004 record of a dying custom, taken from elderly informants' memory; that it is one village on Shikoku; and that its statement that the shrine usually stood at the southwest corner is a general remark in its opening paragraph, while its own table of fifteen houses puts the shrines overwhelmingly east and southeast and not one in the southwest - so the southwest corner is a handbook generalization the village itself does not show, and our maps roll the corner rather than fix it.

Used for: the two patterns (every house / old families only), one 40 cm stone hokora, one persimmon 20 m east of a homestead; the SW corner is the report's general statement, while its own fifteen-house table puts the shrines east and southeast and none in the southwest
