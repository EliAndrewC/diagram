Tonami Dispersed Village Museum (となみ散居村ミュージアム), "About the dispersed village" (散居村について) (https://sankyoson.com/about/)

What it is: The explanatory page of the Tonami Dispersed Village Museum, the museum in Tonami of the plain's dispersed farmsteads, in Japanese. It sets out how the dispersed village formed on the alluvial fan of the Sho and Oyabe rivers, what the homestead grove (kainyo) did for a house, how the irrigation water was also used for the household, and how the landscape has changed since the war.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the canonical Japanese dispersed settlement, the pattern one of our settlement forms is drawn from, and it is the one page read that says where a dispersed farm's household water came from. Its limits are that it dates the channel mesh the household water ran in to the Kaga domain's early-modern works (近世), the era the setting draws on, but gives the household use itself only as "in former times" (かつては), which may reach into the twentieth century; that it speaks of one plain, whose deep water table under a fan is not every dispersed plain's; and that "in many areas" leaves the rest unsaid. The practice presupposes a channel mesh that passes every farm; a dispersed plain without one is not described here.

Used for: the dispersed farm's own water on the Tonami plain - a small channel led off the irrigation water into the house's grounds for cooking, washing and drinking, because the water table was deep and wells hard to dig
