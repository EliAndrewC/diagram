furo-kotobank

Kotobank, 風呂 (Furo) - Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A2%A8%E5%91%82-127856)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the bath, carrying among other entries the Heibonsha world encyclopedia's history of bathing in Japan, from the steam baths of temples through the public baths of the cities to the home bath of the late Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says who had a bath of their own in the Edo period: the upper-rank samurai's shoin-style residence had a yudono, while middle and lower samurai and ordinary people washed from a tub or used the public bath, and it gives the cost of a home bath in a city. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary covering the whole country and period in a sentence, that before the late Edo period the word furo meant a steam bath so the page's terms shift with the era, and that it says nothing of where in a house or compound the yudono stood.

Used for: the yudono of upper-rank samurai houses and the lack of one below them; the cost of a home bath; the spread of home baths late in the Edo period (buildings)
