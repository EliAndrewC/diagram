zh.wikipedia 店屋 ("shophouse", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%BA%97%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the shophouse, the vernacular building of South China and Southeast Asia with a shop on the ground floor and the family living above, usually two or three stories.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the height of the shophouse of South China and Southeast Asia: two or three stories, with the shop below and the family above. It is a tertiary source, a short encyclopedia article flagged since 2017 as needing expansion. The sentence we use cites two studies of Singapore (a 2006 Berkeley working paper on Niucheshui and a 1992 Singapore book on public space), not any South Chinese town. Most of what it shows is the colonial-era shophouses of Singapore, Malaysia, Hong Kong and Taiwan, of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and it credits their narrow plan and five-foot arcade to colonial tax, structural and building rules. So it is late, southern and mostly Southeast Asian evidence for a premodern county town, good for the form of the building and not for a number.

Used for: the southern shophouse of two or three stories, shop below and family above (towns)
