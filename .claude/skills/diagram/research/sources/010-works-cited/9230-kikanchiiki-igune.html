kikanchiiki-igune

季刊地域 (Kikan Chiiki, the rural-affairs quarterly of the Rural Culture Association Japan), 「Protecting the traditional homestead grove, the igune, by working together」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「伝統的な屋敷林「居久根」を共同作業で守る」) (https://kikanchiiki.net/archives/6543)

What it is: A short magazine article, on the quarterly's own site, about the igune homestead groves of Osaki in Miyagi prefecture - groves of sugi, pine, bamboo and other trees built around the house against strong winds for several hundred years - and the preservation society that the 23 households of the city's Sawadaue district whose igune remain formed to do the pruning and clearing of fallen trees together, with part of the cost met by the city.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says how an igune is kept - by pruning its limbs and clearing what falls - which is what our reading of a working grove's height rests on. Its limits are that it is a modern magazine piece describing present-day preservation work, not a historical account, so the practice is read back into the premodern grove; that it is one district of one Miyagi city, where some households had already felled their igune for want of hands to keep them; and that it names limb-pruning as one of the society's jobs without saying whether the trees' tops were ever cut, so it cannot by itself show that a grove was never height-capped.

Used for: the limb-pruning (edauchi) of igune
