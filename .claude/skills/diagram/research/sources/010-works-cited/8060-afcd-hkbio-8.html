Yip, Ngar, Yip, Liu and Lai, "AFCD Survey of Local Fung Shui Woods", Hong Kong Biodiversity 8 (March 2005) 2-5 (https://www.afcd.gov.hk/english/publications/publications_con/files/hkbonewsletter8.pdf)

What it is: An article by Yip, Ngar, Yip, Liu and Lai reporting the Hong Kong government's survey of local fung shui woods, published in the Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department's newsletter Hong Kong Biodiversity, issue 8 (March 2005). It describes what these village woods are, notes that they typically form a crescent behind a village, and explains how the surveyors drew each wood's boundary.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a government field survey of the actual feature we draw - the planted or protected belt of trees standing behind a village - by the people who measured it, which is why we take the crescent form and the boundary convention from it. The woods surveyed are in Hong Kong, in subtropical southern China rather than Japan, and they are the twenty-first-century remnants of a much older practice, so the form is better attested by this source than any historical extent is; the surveyors themselves note that boundaries were drawn when there were significantly fewer large trees than the wood once held.

Used for: the form - a crescent behind the village - and the boundary rule the area survey used
