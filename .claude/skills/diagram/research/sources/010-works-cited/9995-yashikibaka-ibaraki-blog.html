yashikibaka-ibaraki-blog

tak-shonai (Shonai Takuaki), "On yashiki-baka and ie-baka" (title translated by this project), Today's Crack blog, 6 November 2019 (in Japanese; https://tak-shonai.cocolog-nifty.com/crack/2019/11/post-476c20.html)

What it is: A short post on a long-running personal blog by a writer living in Ibaraki prefecture, northeast of Tokyo, who grew up in Tohoku, describing the family graves he sees on private land that is not a graveyard, and the names they go by.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says where on a family's land such a grave stands - the garden, before the entrance, or the corner of a field - at the field's corner rather than in its middle. Its limits: it is a present-day personal observation in one prefecture, not a study; the writer calls it a Kanto custom, rare in Tohoku where he was born and, by a Nara stonemason's account, in Kansai, so it is a regional placement and not a Japan-wide one; it dates these graves only as older than Japan's 1948 burial law, on the word of an end-of-life planning site it quotes, not to the Edo period or earlier; the writer hedges the names as what such graves are apparently called; and the field it names is a dry field, not a paddy.

Used for: that family graves on private land in Kanto (eastern Japan) stand in the garden, before the house or in the corner of a field (fields)
