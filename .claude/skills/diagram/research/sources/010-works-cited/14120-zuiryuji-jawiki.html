ja.wikipedia 瑞龍寺 (高岡市) ("Zuiryuji (Takaoka)", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/瑞龍寺_(高岡市))

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Zuiryuji, the Soto Zen temple the Maeda lords founded in the castle town of Takaoka in 1614 to mourn the lord who had built its castle, its halls raised between 1645 and about 1663, with its plan, its buildings, the stone mausolea behind its cloister and that lord's grave at the end of an 870 m approach road.

Why it applies, and its limits: A lord's great temple in a provincial castle town, which is nearer the setting's provincial city than any capital temple, and its plan is given building by building. It gives no area for the precinct; it is a tertiary summary; and part of the plan it describes is not seventeenth-century fabric: the kitchen-hall (kuri) was rebuilt and the monks' hall returned to its original size in 1985-1996 from excavated foundations, the bath and the privy were dismantled in the Meiji era and are known only from excavation, and the gate is an 1820 rebuild. It is also the memorial temple of the Maeda lords of Kaga, the largest domain in Japan, with 300 koku of temple land, so its plan is the most a provincial great temple could be, not the usual one.

Used for: a provincial great temple's plan: gate, Buddha hall and dharma hall on one line inside a cloister, the kuri, monks' hall, bath and privy to the sides, the bell tower on the cloister, the lords' mausolea outside it (religion-and-death)
