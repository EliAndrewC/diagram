kotobank-seki

Kotobank, "Seki (weir)" (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A0%B0-429861) - the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia's entry (revised edition), signed by section, among them Kitamura Toshio's on Edo-period weirs

What it is: The dictionary aggregator's page for the weir, carrying signed encyclopedia entries on its history in Japan, among them a section on the weirs of the Edo period and their classification.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records that Edo-period weirs were classed by their angle to the river's main flow, and that the oblique minote weir, following the flow, put its intake on the side toward which the water ran; it also says oblique weirs were common before the Meiji period. It gives no angle in degrees, neither for the weir nor for the canal leaving at its intake.

Used for: that an Edo-period intake took the river's water with the flow, at the downstream end of an oblique weir; the stake weirs excavated at Itazuke and Kodera, and the Edo classing of weirs into grass weirs and washing weirs (water)
