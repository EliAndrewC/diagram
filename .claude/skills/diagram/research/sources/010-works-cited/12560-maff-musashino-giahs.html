Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, 埼玉県 武蔵野地域 ("Saitama prefecture, the Musashino area", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on the Globally Important Agricultural Heritage Systems (in Japanese; https://www.maff.go.jp/j/nousin/kantai/giahs_3_101.html)

What it is: The ministry's page on the Musashino fallen-leaf compost farming system, recognized as a world agricultural heritage system in 2023: woods planted on the upland grass plain from 1654, when the Kawagoe domain opened it to relieve Edo's food shortage, their leaves raked for compost and the woods holding the light soil against the wind.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the clearest readable case of woods deliberately raised beside the fields of a new settlement for what they gave - leaf compost, and shelter for blowing soil - which is a reason a margin is clothed rather than bare. It is one district on one kind of ground, volcanic ash on a waterless upland, and a heritage-listing summary rather than a history; it does not say how much of the ground was wood, nor that towns did the same.

Used for: woods planted beside new fields from 1654 for leaf compost and to hold the soil (towns)
