ikikata-yashiki-bochi

"屋敷墓地とは？" ("What is a house-plot graveyard?", title translated from the Japanese by this project), イキカタ glossary, Nishinippon Shimbun, 21 July 2023 (https://ikikata.nishinippon.co.jp/term/6815/)

What it is: A newspaper-run end-of-life glossary entry on the graveyard within a household's own land, and on its status under the 1948 graveyard and burial law.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one statement read on how common the form is by region - few nationally, few in Kansai, said to be many in Kanto - and that no new one has in principle been allowed since 1948. The ones that survive are only those licensed under the Meiji-era burial rules that came before, and unlicensed ones do not count as legal graveyards. So where they survive has passed through two modern laws, and is not a map of where the form was once made. It is a present-day consumer glossary with promotional content, it reports the regional pattern only as hearsay, and it says nothing of premodern frequency.

Used for: where house-plot graves survive (religion and death)
