xuli-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 胥吏 ("clerks and runners", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/胥吏)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the xuli, the sub-official clerks of imperial Chinese government, quoting Tang to Qing essays, notebooks and memorials; an encyclopedia article (a tertiary source), read in its revision of 2025-12-23.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests the clerks as entrenched local staff, the official's dependence on their knowledge and their enrichment out of the revenue they handled, which is the clerk this page's county runs on; a Chinese picture, and it says nothing of the Japanese tedai. It is a tertiary summary, and its thousand is not a count. It is the critic Hong Liangji's complaint of the 1790s, "今州縣之大者，胥吏至千人" (in translation, "now in the largest departments and counties the clerks reach a thousand"), which the article quotes without a footnote; its notes add only Hou Fangyu's seventeenth-century "每县殆不止千人" (in translation, "each county has hardly fewer than a thousand"). Both are polemics against the clerks, not counts: Hong's is of the largest counties in the Qianlong-Jiaqing years, when county populations had swelled, and Hou's, of the mid-seventeenth century, is a flourish about every county. The article's only empire-wide figures, about 350,000 clerks central and local under Tang Xuanzong and about 440,000 in the Northern Song Yuanyou years, would average a few hundred at most to a county if spread across the empire, well below a thousand. The article gives no statutory quota of clerks to set against the number, so we take it as the upper end of what period critics complained of, not a figure for an ordinary county.

Used for: the permanent local clerk; a large county's clerks numbering up to a thousand (buildings)
