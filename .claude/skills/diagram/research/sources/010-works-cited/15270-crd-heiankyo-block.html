crd-heiankyo-block

Collaborative Reference Database (National Diet Library), reference case from the Kyoto Institute, Library and Archives, "I want to know the size of one block of Heian-kyo, and how many house lots such a block held" (in Japanese, question translated by this project; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?page=ref_view&id=1000141349)

What it is: A Kyoto prefectural library's answer to a reader's question, recorded in the national library's shared reference database in 2013, giving the size of a Heian-kyo block and the house lots it was divided into, from two printed books it names.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the block and the lesser official's lot of the ancient capital's plan, the Japanese baseline for how a gridded city was cut up. It is a librarian's summary of the books, not the books; it describes the ideal plan of the capital founded in 794, not a later provincial town. It says itself that no record of lot allotment survives for Heian-kyo: its allotment rules come from the earlier capitals Fujiwara-kyo and Naniwa-kyo. The 1/32-block lot is the lower official's lot "in principle" under the shigyo-hachimon grid, and higher ranks were given a quarter block up to several whole blocks, so a block cut into 32 lots is the lower officials' case, not a typical block. It also says some households lived on less than the standard lot.

Used for: the Heian block of 40 jo square and its 32 lots (cities/fabric.html)
