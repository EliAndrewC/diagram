孟元老, 東京夢華錄 卷三 (Chinese Wikisource) (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%9D%B1%E4%BA%AC%E5%A4%A2%E8%8F%AF%E9%8C%84/%E5%8D%B7%E4%B8%89)

What it is: The third chapter of 東京夢華錄 (Dreams of Splendor of the Eastern Capital), Meng Yuanlao's recollection of daily life in Kaifeng, the Northern Song capital, written in the twelfth century after the city had fallen, given here in the original literary Chinese on Chinese Wikisource. The passage we use describes the military patrol posts set through the wards and lanes and the brick-built 望火樓 fire-watch tower, with its lookouts above and its quartered soldiers and firefighting gear below.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary account, by someone who lived in the city, of exactly the institution our city maps draw - a permanent fire-watch tower with a manned station under it - in a dense premodern East Asian capital, which is the strongest kind of evidence the record can have for it. Its limits are that it describes one imperial capital at the very top of the urban hierarchy and not a provincial seat; that it is a nostalgic memoir composed from memory after the city was lost, so its figures are recollection rather than record; and that it says nothing about smaller towns, so it cannot be read either way on what an ordinary market town kept.

Used for: the Song capital's brick-built fire-watch tower, with lookouts above and soldiers and firefighting gear below, and the military patrol posts set through its wards and lanes
