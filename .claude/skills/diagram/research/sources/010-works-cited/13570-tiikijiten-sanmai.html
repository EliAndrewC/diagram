tiikijiten-sanmai

地域事典 (tiikijiten.jp), "わが町歴史探索" series, "さんまい（三昧）" ("Sanmai", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 27 April 2017 (in Japanese; http://tiikijiten.jp/~pickup/tansaku2/index.php?no=0012)

What it is: A short piece in a local-history series on a community website whose other pieces are on the history of Kaga (southern Ishikawa), written as a conversation about the memorial a hamlet raised in 2016 on the site of its own old cremation ground, with the memorial's inscription and three quoted passages from published books.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a witness from inside a Shin Buddhist farming district that a hamlet kept and ran its own cremation ground, the sanmai, and burned its own dead there until the ground closed in 1977, and it quotes a published scholar that, until cremation became universal after the Second World War, cremation as a custom had been confined to the Shin regions of Hokuriku and to Hiroshima and Yamaguchi. Its limits: an anonymous community site with no editorial review; one hamlet, remembered rather than surveyed, and the page itself does not name it or its province; the scholarly passage is quoted second-hand; and it gives no size, distance or siting for the ground.

Used for: a hamlet's own cremation ground, run by its people; cremation as the custom of the Shin regions (religion-and-death)
