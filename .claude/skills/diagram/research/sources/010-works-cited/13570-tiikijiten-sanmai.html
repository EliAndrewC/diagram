tiikijiten-sanmai

地域事典 (tiikijiten.jp), "わが町歴史探索" series, "さんまい（三昧）" ("Sanmai", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 27 April 2017 (in Japanese; http://tiikijiten.jp/~pickup/tansaku2/index.php?no=0012)

What it is: A short piece in a local-history series on a community website whose other pieces are on the history of Kaga (southern Ishikawa), written as a conversation about the memorial a hamlet raised in 2016 on the site of its own old cremation ground, with the memorial's inscription and four passages quoted or summarized from three published books.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a witness from a farming district - Shin Buddhist by inference from the series' Kaga setting, which the page itself does not state - that a hamlet kept and ran its own cremation ground, the sanmai, and burned its own dead there until the ground closed in 1977, and it quotes a published scholar that, until cremation became universal after the Second World War, cremation as a custom had been confined to the Shin regions of Hokuriku and to Hiroshima and Yamaguchi. Its limits: an anonymous community site with no editorial review; one hamlet, remembered rather than surveyed, and the page itself does not name it or its province; the scholarly passage is quoted second-hand; and it gives no size, distance or siting for the ground. What it witnesses is the practice's twentieth-century survival, the ground's last use in 1977 within its speakers' memory, not its Edo-period form, and it does not date the ground's founding. And the quoted claim goes further than the national figures: in 1896 cremation was 26.8% of Japanese burials and over 65% in eight prefectures, among them Tokyo, Osaka, Niigata and Hokkaido as well as the Hokuriku three and Hiroshima, and the great cities cremated too; so the page supports cremation as the custom of the Shin regions, not as confined to them.

Used for: a hamlet's own cremation ground, run by its people; cremation as the custom of the Shin regions (religion-and-death)
