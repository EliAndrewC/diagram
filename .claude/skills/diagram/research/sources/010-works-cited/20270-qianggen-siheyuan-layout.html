qianggen-siheyuan-layout

Unsigned, "老北京的四合院——布局与礼数" ("The courtyard houses of old Beijing: layout and etiquette", title translated from the Chinese by this project), Qianggen (墙根网), 5 March 2012 (in Chinese; https://culture.qianggen.net/2012/0305/23273.html)

What it is: An unsigned article, dated 5 March 2012, on Qianggen, a Beijing culture-and-tourism website that also sells park passes and hotel bookings, describing the Beijing courtyard house range by range: which rooms open onto the court, which open only onto a hall, and who lived in each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the door rule of a Beijing range plainly, a three-bay range with a door only in its middle bay, which is the Beijing (north Chinese) courtyard house's form of one door for several bays. The article itself sets the Beijing house apart from courtyard houses in Shanxi, Shaanxi, Sichuan and the south, and does not say whether those regions kept the same door rule. It is popular writing, unsigned and without sources, and gives no date for the practice it describes; it speaks of the family's north range, not the servants' south range, and says nothing of how servants' rooms were entered.

Used for: a Beijing house's three-bay range entered by one door in its middle bay (buildings)
