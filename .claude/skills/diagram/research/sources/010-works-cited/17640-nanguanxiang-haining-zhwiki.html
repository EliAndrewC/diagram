zh.wikipedia 南关厢 ("Nanguanxiang", title translated) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/%E5%8D%97%E5%85%B3%E5%8E%A2)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Nanguanxiang, a protected historic street district in Xiashi, Haining (Zhejiang): its measured extent, the late-Ming checkpoint it is named for, its rice-market prosperity and its surviving shop-houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one measured extent found for a place named a guanxiang: about 350 m by 50 m, a single street of shop-houses. Its limits are large: Xiashi is a Jiangnan market town, not a walled administrative seat, and the guanxiang it is named for was a checkpoint on a water and land route, not a gate suburb; the extent is today's protected district, bounded on the west by a modern road (Renmin Road) and on the east by the Luotang River, so its 50 m is the strip of street and the lots behind it back to the river, not the width of a street, and the article cites no source for the figure; the buildings standing in it are mostly Qing to Republican, restored by the city government in 2008-2012.

Used for: the length and width of one historic guanxiang street (cities/hinterland)
