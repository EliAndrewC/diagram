xuanquanzhi-neac

National Ethnic Affairs Commission of China, reprinting Daozhonghua, 【道中华】探秘悬泉置：大汉朝的"官方招待所"见证了怎样的丝路繁华？ ("Exploring Xuanquanzhi: what Silk Road prosperity did the Han dynasty's official guesthouse witness?", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 2023-02-28 (in Chinese; https://www.neac.gov.cn/seac/c103391/202302/1161310.shtml)

What it is: An interview with Zhang Junmin, a researcher at the Gansu Provincial Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology, on the excavated Han post station of Xuanquanzhi near Dunhuang: its walled compound and stable area in meters, its offices, and the number of horses a station of the commandery kept.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page that gives a measured Chinese post station with its stable area and its horse count side by side, which is the nearest thing to a size for the horse buildings of a road town. Its limits are large: it is a Han station of the first centuries BCE and CE on the desert frontier, far from the late-imperial county town, one of the highest-ranked stations of its day; it is a popular interview rather than the excavation report; it names the granary among the station's offices but says nothing of where fodder was kept; and its 30 x 30 m is a stable zone jutting from the compound's corner, a round figure "from the excavation so far", which the page never says was a roofed building, so it bounds a stable yard rather than giving the footprint of a stable building.

Used for: a 50 x 50 m station compound with a 30 x 30 m stable area, and about forty horses a station (towns)
