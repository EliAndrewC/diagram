fudasashi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 札差 ("fudasashi, the rice brokers of Edo", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9C%AD%E5%B7%AE)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the fudasashi, the merchants at Kuramae in Edo who received and sold the shogunate's retainers' stipend rice for them and lent against it: how the trade began at the granary's front, the licensed guild of 109 houses formed in 1724 and its three street groups, its rules, and its decline after the shogunate's debt cancellations.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says who kept the brokers' row in front of the shogunate's granary, where their shops stood and how the guild was bound to the place, which is what our brokers' row is drawn from. It is a tertiary summary that Wikipedia itself flags (since 2018) as short of inline citations, so its figures rest on the works it lists (Kitahara Susumu's studies of the fudasashi, the Kokushi daijiten), not on footnotes. It is almost wholly about Edo, whose brokers served the shogunate's own retainers - a population no domain seat matched - so it gives the form of the row, not its length for a castle town. Its one provincial case, seven Kōfu merchants licensed in 1724 to serve about forty stipend-drawing guardsmen, is the nearer scale for a seat, though Kōfu was shogunal land rather than a daimyo's seat. Its counts belong to their dates: the 1724 groups of 31, 47 and 31 were recast in 1778 as three wards of 30, and the Kuramae residence rule was an agreement among the guild members, not a shogunal order. Its account of the trade's origin is told as a story rather than dated.

Used for: the trade's origin at the granary's front; the 109-house guild in three street groups; the posted rice price; the rule that members live at Kuramae; the brokers' commission and lending (cities/capitals)
