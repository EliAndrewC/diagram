Storozum et al., "Geoarchaeological evidence of the AD 1642 Yellow River flood that destroyed Kaifeng", Scientific Reports 2020 (PMC 7048742) (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-60169-1)

What it is: Storozum and colleagues, "Geoarchaeological evidence of the AD 1642 Yellow River flood that destroyed Kaifeng", a peer-reviewed open access paper in Scientific Reports, 2020. The authors read the flood deposits under the modern city and set the 1642 disaster in the river's long record, which brought roughly forty floods to Kaifeng over the past three thousand years.

Why it applies, and its limits: Primary fieldwork on exactly the question a river-city map asks - five excavations through the flood layers of a city that sat below its river - so it is the strongest kind of evidence the record has for the danger of a bank. Its limits are that the frequency we quote (about forty floods in three thousand years) is a background statement in the paper rather than a result of its excavations, which measure the 1642 deposit and what was built over it - still a peer-reviewed statement, which is why we prefer it to uncited counts; that it is one city on the most flood-prone river in the world, the extreme rather than the average; and that it is the Chinese half of a setting also modeled on Japan.
