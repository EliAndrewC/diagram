xutan-2000-market-network

Xu Tan (许檀), 明清时期城乡市场网络体系的形成及其历史意义 ("The formation of the urban-rural market network in the Ming and Qing and its historical significance", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 中国社会科学 (Social Sciences in China) 2000 no. 3, reposted in full on Aisixiang (in Chinese; https://www.aisixiang.com/data/21367.html)

What it is: An economic historian's survey of how China's trading cities, regional centers and rural periodic markets formed one network between the mid-Ming and the mid-Qing (the Qianlong to Daoguang reigns), with a closing look at the treaty ports of the 1840s-60s, with national estimates of the number of rural markets and of their density and reach in the mid-Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates its market figures to the mid-Qing, the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, so it places the rural market's catchment before modernity rather than in twentieth-century fieldwork. Its figures are province-wide averages synthesized from other scholars' provincial studies and from the author's own 1997 paper, not a measurement of any one market, and the page gives the ranges without the tables behind them. Each market's area is a territory divided by its count of markets, so it takes in hill and water no one farmed. The radius follows from that area rather than from paced roads, and the one-to-two-hour walk is the author's inference from the radius. The figures are the reach of the lowest of its three tiers, the rural periodic market. They are not the reach of a county seat or a commercial town, whose trade the paper puts at one or two prefectures or more.

Used for: the mid-Qing rural market's area and walking radius, on the plains and in the mountains (towns)
