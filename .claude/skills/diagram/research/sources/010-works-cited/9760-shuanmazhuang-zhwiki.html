zh.wikipedia, 拴马桩 ("shuānmǎzhuāng, a stone hitching post", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/拴马桩)

What it is: The short Chinese Wikipedia article on the 拴马桩, the stone (sometimes wooden) hitching post of traditional Chinese and East Asian building: what it was made of, where it stood, how a visitor's horse was tied to it, and the museum near Xi'an that holds over 8,600 of them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests the hitching post as a Chinese fixture set beside the gates of houses, temples and palaces, which is the tethering our yards draw. It is a stub with one popular-press reference, it describes the carved stone posts at a gate rather than the rails of a working cart-yard, and it gives no dates, so it licenses the fixture and not the yard's layout.

Used for: the hitching post as a Chinese fixture (stable yards)
