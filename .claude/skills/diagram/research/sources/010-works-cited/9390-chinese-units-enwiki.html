en.wikipedia, Chinese units of measurement (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_units_of_measurement)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on traditional Chinese units, with a table giving, dynasty by dynasty, the length in meters of the foot (chi), of the pace (bu) at five and at six feet, and of the li.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page read that turns the paces of a sixth-century treatise into meters, and it gives the two reckonings of the pace side by side. It is a tertiary summary; its table has a Wei-Sui row (220-266 and 581-618) and nothing for the centuries between, so a foot for the 540s is carried across a gap the table leaves - and probably carried short: zh.wikipedia's own table (中国度量衡, itself unsourced by dynasty) gives the northern foot of the Qimin yaoshu's time as about 29.6 cm against this row's 25.5 cm, which would make feet reckoned from the row some 14% small. It gives the pace at five and at six feet without saying which held when. Its length table rests on a single citation, Schinz's The Magic Square (1996), a book on the planning of ancient Chinese cities rather than a study of measures, and the article itself notes that it omits the several kinds of foot in use in some periods.

Used for: the pace as five or six feet, and the Wei-Sui foot, to put the Qimin yaoshu bed in feet (cities/hinterland)
