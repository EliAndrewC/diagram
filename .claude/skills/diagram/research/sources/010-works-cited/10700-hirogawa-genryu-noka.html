hirogawa-genryu-noka

Sakai Takeo for the Hirogawa Town "Genryu" Project (Wakayama Prefecture), "Hirogawa in the old days: farm life" (in Japanese; https://note.com/hirogawa/n/n2bb20448c5c5), September 2024

What it is: A local-history piece by a town heritage project describing how farm households of Hirogawa lived in the Meiji, Taisho and early Showa periods - by the page's own reckoning up to about 1960 - illustrated with photographs of exhibits at the Gobo City Museum of History and Folklore.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one read page that says how long the straw stacked in the paddy was kept - into the next year, as fodder, manure and fuel - which is the rick's duration the record owed. It is a local recollection of one district of Wakayama, written in 2024 for a general audience, and the life it describes is of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries; that an earlier village kept its straw the same way is carried back by inference. It names no source for the sentence, and it does not say how far into the next year the stack lasted.

Used for: the threshed straw stacked in the paddy and kept into the next year for fodder, manure and fuel (fields, homesteads)
