ome-shinmachi-machinami

集落町並みWalker, 青梅新町（東京都青梅市） ("Ome Shinmachi, Ome, Tokyo", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a private survey site of historic townscapes (in Japanese; http://www.shurakumachinami.natsu.gs/03datebase-page/tokyo_data/oumeshinmachi/oumeshinmachi_file.html)

What it is: A private enthusiast's page on Ome Shinmachi, a new village opened on the Ome road early in the Edo period: the road through its middle, the strip house lots on each side with their fields behind, their frontage and count, and the told reason the front yards were wide.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a second planned row's frontage - 9 ken, far narrower than Santome's - on a new-field farming village whose houses stood on the road with their fields in strips behind them. The page says only that it is told the narrow lots and wide front yards were meant for a future market and post town, and nothing of a market or post town coming of it; so it is the narrow end of a farming row laid out with a street in mind. It is an amateur page (dated 2011) that names one published guide (図説 日本の町並み3 関東編, 第一法規) as its reference and cites no period record for the lot figures, though it dates the laying-out to the Keicho era (1596-1615); so its numbers are a secondary report of a single village in the Kanto. It says too that how the village looked at its opening is not known - its houses were rebuilt as sericulture farmhouses by the end of the Edo period - and the fields in strips behind the houses are its caption to a present-day photograph, so only the lot plan (the 9-ken frontage and the 33 lots a side) is dated to the founding.

Used for: a planned farming row with 9 ken of frontage and 33 lots each side, its fields in strips behind (homesteads)
