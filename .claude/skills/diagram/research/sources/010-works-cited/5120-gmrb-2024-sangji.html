What it is: A March 2024 feature in Guangming Daily, a national Chinese newspaper, by Zhao Yu and Hou Nannan, part of a series on cultural heritage still in living use, about the mulberry-dike fish ponds of Huzhou. It describes how the form is made - low ground dug out deep for the pond, the spoil heaped around the edge to build the dike - notes that the ponds are dredged in the seventh and eighth months to rebuild those dikes, and dates the practice from Spring and Autumn origins to a Ming and Qing peak.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the single clearest sentence we found on the physical logic of pond and dike - that the dike is literally the material taken out of the pond - and a maintenance round tied to the calendar, which is exactly the kind of thing that decides how such a landscape is drawn. It is a newspaper feature for a general reader rather than a study, its dating is the paper's own compressed summary, and the site it is standing in is a working heritage landscape today, so the pond it describes is a present-day one whose form we are treating as continuous with the old one.

Used for: the form in one line - 将地势低洼处深挖为鱼塘，塘泥堆在鱼塘四周形成塘基; dredging in the seventh and eighth months to rebuild the dikes (七八月清淤，培固塘基); the Huzhou dating (Spring and Autumn origin, Ming-Qing peak)
