"伏見稲荷大社に千本鳥居が見られるようになったのは明治以降" ("The Senbon Torii came to be seen at Fushimi Inari Taisha after Meiji", title translated), a post of 2024-08-23 on the blog 京都観光旅行のあれこれ (in Japanese; https://kyotohotelsearch.com/blog/2024/08/23/fushimiinaritorimeji/)

What it is: A Kyoto sightseeing blog's post on when Fushimi Inari's rows of vermilion gates took their present form, reporting what a book by Kyoto high-school social-studies teachers says of an early-Meiji drawing of the precinct.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page found that sets a period picture against the shrine's own dating: the shrine's drawing of the first years of Meiji, as the book reports it, shows six gates with their bark on and no vermilion one. Its limits: a present-day blog passing on a book at second hand; the drawing itself is not reproduced and the book was not read; a precinct drawing may leave out the mountain paths; and its conclusion, that the rows formed after Meiji, is the blogger's inference.

Used for: the early-Meiji drawing of Fushimi's precinct with six gates, as a book reports it (religion-and-death)
