ctie-michi-nazenaze

株式会社建設技術研究所 (CTI Engineering Co., Ltd.), 道のなぜなぜ 1-4, "江戸時代の道路はどのようなものだったのですか？" (https://www.ctie.co.jp/service/publishing/img/michi-nazenaze/1-4.pdf)

What it is: A two-page section (question 1-4, pp. 8-9) of 新 道なぜなぜおもしろ読本 (2011), a popular illustrated question-and-answer book on roads edited by CTI Engineering, a large Japanese civil engineering consultancy, which posts the section as a PDF. It covers when the Five Highways were established, their political and military role, their post-station counts, their width and the length of the road network.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the width of the trunk highways in ken, and it is careful in exactly the way that mattered to us: it states the ordinary figure with mountain roads expressly excluded, so the two are not to be confused. Its limits are that it is a company's popular publication and cites no survey or period document behind the number; that its figures are for the shogunate's trunk highways, the top of the road network, so for a village lane or the road at a compound's gate they are an upper bound and not a width; that it is Japanese only, with nothing on Chinese roads; that it gives no figure at all for the mountain roads it excludes, so that narrowing remains unsourced; and that a second readable account of the same highways gives a wider ordinary width, so the two bracket the figure rather than confirm it.

Used for: the Five Highways at 3 to 4 ken (about 5.4 to 7.2 m) excluding mountain roads, and 5 ken (about 9 m) where they ran close to Edo (ways, for village lanes and for the road at a compound's gate)
