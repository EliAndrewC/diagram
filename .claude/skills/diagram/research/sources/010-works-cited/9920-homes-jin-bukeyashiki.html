LIFULL HOME'S, 【間取り探偵㊹】JIN～仁～の武家屋敷を解説！ (Floor-plan detective 44: the samurai house of JIN) (in Japanese; https://www.homes.co.jp/cont/living/living_00374/)

What it is: A 2018 column on a Japanese real-estate site by Kageyama Akihito, a real-estate agent and building consultant who draws the floor plans of houses in manga, on the house in Edo of the Tachibana, a low-ranking hatamoto family of the manga and television drama JIN, set at the end of the Edo period. The column says its plan is not an inference: it was drawn from a plan sent by the manga's author, who had the gate moved and rooms removed or placed to suit the story. It walks through who used which room and door.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states who came in by the doma rather than by the entrance, which bears on who used a residence's kitchen door. It is the weakest kind of source: a popular column by a real-estate agent, naming none of its references, on a fictional household's house whose plan the manga's author altered for the story, not a documented building. Its house is a samurai town house in Edo with no inner entrance (uchi-genkan). A samurai house could have one, kept for the household in addition to the formal entrance (see kotobank-uchigenkan), so the column's rule that everyone below the master used the doma is a simplification. It says nothing of a farmhouse's or a townsman's doors. It can support a practice only beside a dictionary or a surviving house.

Used for: those of lower status than the master coming and going by the doma (buildings)
