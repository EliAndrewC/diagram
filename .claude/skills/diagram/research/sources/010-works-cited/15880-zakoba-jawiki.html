zakoba-jawiki

ja.wikipedia ざこば ("Zakoba", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ざこば)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Osaka's Zakoba fish market, which stood on the bank of the Hyakkenbori canal from the seventeenth century until 1931: why the fish merchants moved to it, how near it lay to the river mouth, and how often it met.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a real Edo-period city fish market sited on a canal bank for the boats, the second case beside Edo's. It is a short tertiary article on Japan's second city and gives the market's site and rhythm, not its buildings.

Used for: a city's fish market on a canal bank near the river mouth, and a market meeting three times a day (cities/river-cities)
