zakoba-jawiki

ja.wikipedia ざこば ("Zakoba", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ざこば)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Osaka's Zakoba fish market, which stood on the bank of the Hyakkenbori canal from the seventeenth century until 1931: why the fish merchants moved to it, how near it lay to Kawaguchi, where Osaka's two great waterways, the Aji and Kizu rivers, branched toward the sea, and how often it met.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a real Edo-period city fish market sited on a canal bank for the boats, the second case beside Edo's. It is a short tertiary article, a stub whose account of the market cites nothing (its three notes support side points: a painting of the Tenma market, the prefectural office across the canal, the ward's name), so the move to the canal and the three meetings a day are the article's word, used for the shape and not as a checked figure. It gives the market's site and rhythm, not its buildings. And it describes a larger scale of place than ours: the wholesale fish market of Japan's second city, which took in nearly all the catch shipped from Kyushu, Shikoku and the Inland Sea coasts, with 84 licensed wholesalers by 1772; a smaller city's fish market would meet less often and on a smaller scale.

Used for: a city's fish market on a canal bank near the river mouth, and a market meeting three times a day (cities/river-cities)
