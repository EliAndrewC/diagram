農林水産省, 自然環境に果たす役割 - 2. ため池 (https://www.maff.go.jp/j/council/seisaku/nousin/seibibukai/gijutu_syoiinkai/h14-1/pdf/data3-2.pdf)

What it is: A document prepared for an advisory committee of the Japanese Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries on the part irrigation reservoir ponds play in the natural environment. Its figure shows the bands of plants that succeed one another on a gently sloping shore, from waterside woodland through wet meadow, emergent plants such as reed, cattail and wild rice, then floating-leaved plants and finally submerged ones.

Why it applies, and its limits: That banding is what we use to draw the edge of a village pond, and it is ecology rather than history, so it changes little between a modern pond and a premodern one of the same shape. Two limits: the paper is a modern government document about present-day ponds, and the zonation figure is credited to a book we were not able to read, so we cite the banding from this document at one step from its original.

Used for: the land-to-water zonation of a gently-sloping natural shore - riparian forest, wet-meadow, emergent, floating-leaf, submerged - with the emergent belt named as ヨシ・ガマ・マコモ. Note the diagram credits 桜井義雄『水辺の環境学』, which was NOT read; the zonation is cited from this document, one step from that figure.
