oomachi-saijogaki

"Omachi no Saijogaki" (大町の西条柿, the Saijo persimmon of Omachi), on the site Kyoju to hana no peji (巨樹と花のページ, big trees and flowers), updated 2019 (in Japanese; http://www.tree-flower.jp/33/oomachi_saijougaki_1916/oomachi_kaki.html)

What it is: An amateur big-tree enthusiast's page on one old persimmon at Kagamino in Okayama prefecture, a town-designated natural monument, giving its girth, height and estimated age from the national big-tree database and from the notice board that stands at the tree, and, in the site keeper's own text, the spread of its crown.

Why it applies, and its limits: A tree whose age is estimated at 400 years was growing long before 1868, so it shows how big a persimmon planted in the Edo period can grow. Its limits: the measurements are of today's tree, not of its size in the Edo period; the age is an estimate; the page does not say whether it stood at a farmhouse; one of its two great limbs has broken off, so the crown figures may be older than the tree's present shape; and it is the largest persimmon in the prefecture and is said to be the largest in Japan (since a larger one was half destroyed by lightning), so it is the extreme, not an ordinary dooryard tree. The page is a hobbyist's; its height and age are given as the notice board's and the national database's, and the crown figures are in the site keeper's own text.

Used for: the crown of the largest old persimmon measured, as an upper bound on a dooryard persimmon's size (Homesteads)
