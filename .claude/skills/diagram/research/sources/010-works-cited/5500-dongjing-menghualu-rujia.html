dongjing-menghualu-rujia

吴钩, 大宋消防队 (儒家网, https://www.rujiazg.com/article/11577), quoting Yuan Jiong's Southern Song recollection of Kaifeng's fire watch and paraphrasing 東京夢華錄

What it is: An article by 吴钩 (Wu Gou, a popular historian of the Song), "大宋消防队" (The fire brigade of the Great Song), published in 2017 on 儒家网 (Rujiazg, a Chinese website on Confucian culture and history). It reads the Song sources on Kaifeng's fire watch - the twelfth-century memoir 東京夢華錄 (Dongjing meng hua lu) in paraphrase, and, in the passage we quote, the recollection of Yuan Jiong, a centenarian living in Hangzhou after the capital had fallen: a military patrol post every three hundred paces in each ward, and on high ground a fire-watch tower with lookouts above and a hundred soldiers quartered below with buckets, brooms, hooks, saws, axes, ladders and ropes.

Why it applies, and its limits: The quoted passage is a firsthand description of the institution we draw - a watch tower that is its own building, staffed and equipped, standing apart from the ward gate - and that is precisely what we take from it. Its limits are that we are reading the Song testimony through a popular history article rather than in a scholarly edition, with no edition or chapter given; that the passage is a nostalgic recollection of the capital written after it had fallen, so its regularity may be tidier than the city was; and that it describes one great Chinese capital and gives no count of towers for Kaifeng (the tower counts on the page are Southern Song Hangzhou's), let alone for a provincial town.

Used for: 「In every ward, at every three hundred paces, there is a military patrol post; and at a high place there is a fire-watch tower, with men above keeping lookout and, below, a hundred soldiers stationed together with water buckets, sprinkling brooms, hooked saws, axes and levers, ladders and ropes, and the like.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「每坊三百步有军巡铺，又于高处有望火楼，上有人探望，下屯军百人及水桶、洒帚、钩锯、斧权、梯索之类」) - the Song fire-watch tower as a separate institution; no citywide tower count
