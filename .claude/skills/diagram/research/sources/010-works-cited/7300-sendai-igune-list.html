sendai-igune-list

Sendai City, the list of tree species suited to an igune, a PDF published from the city's igune conservation pages (https://www.city.sendai.jp/ryokuchihozen/kurashi/shizen/midori/hyakunen/documents/igunejyumokurist.pdf)

What it is: The city of Sendai's own list of the tree species suited to an igune, the farm windbreak grove of the Sendai plain, sorted by height: tall trees of 15 m and over (sugi, black pine, the hinoki group, keyaki), sub-canopy and middle trees around 10 m, and shrubs around 3 m.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the species and the height classes of a real windbreak grove - tall trees of fifteen meters and more, a middle story around ten, shrubs around three - which is what sets the drawn height and the mix of our homestead belts. Its limits are that it is a modern city's conservation list, guidance about what to plant and keep rather than a survey of what stands, though the city says it drew the species from what it sees standing in its own igune; that it is one region's list - the cold, snowy, salt-windy Sendai coastal plain, where the black pine is a coastal species - standing in for a windbreak anywhere in the setting; and that its species set is observational only in a loose sense - the city says it chose trees commonly seen in the igune of its own municipality, but gives no counts and no measurements. Two details date it: the salt-tolerance column records which species were found still living in a survey made after the tsunami, and the heights are stated by the list itself as a rough guide that changes with how a tree grows and is tended.

Used for: the igune species list behind the windbreak belt's composition and height classes
