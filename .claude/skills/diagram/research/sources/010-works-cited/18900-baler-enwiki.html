Wikipedia (English), "Baler" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baler)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the baler, the farm machine that compresses cut hay or straw into bound rectangular or round bales, with the history of the hay press and the baling machine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It bounds, loosely, when the bale's machinery came: mechanical cutting devices of the 1860s, from which it says mowers and balers came, and a knotter it says bound hay into bundles, invented in 1872 and sold from 1874 - both resting on a twine maker's trade page, in an article flagged as needing more citations, and other accounts make that 1872 knotter a grain binder's. It gives no date for the first hay press, the stationary machine that first made the rectangular bale, which other accounts put in the United States in the 1840s-1850s - earlier than its 1860s, but still Western and after the setting's period, so it bounds the bale's arrival rather than fixing it. It is a modern encyclopedia article on farm machinery, written from the North American record; it does not say when balers reached Japan or China, and what it says of hay before the machine - cut by hand and kept in haystacks before the nineteenth century - is one unsourced sentence about the Western record, not East Asia.

Used for: the rectangular hay bale a product of the baling machine of the late nineteenth century (towns)
