ranzan-kinsenji

Ranzan Town web museum (嵐山町web博物誌), 比企郡寺院明細帳, 曹洞宗 金泉寺 七郷村大字越畑 (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/09ter04.html)

What it is: The Ranzan town office's transcription of the Hiki district temple register entry for Kinsenji, a Sōtō temple of Oppata village, Saitama, with its buildings, precinct and land held outside the precinct, and the site editors' glosses of the terms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists a village temple's kuri and monks' dormitory, glosses the kuri as the house of the head priest and his family, and enters the temple's graveyard among its land outside the precinct at another place in the village. Its limits: one rural temple; a Meiji-era register (land values in yen, a 1942 note) kept in a 1963 prefectural file, not an Edo record, and its line between the precinct and the land outside it was drawn after the Meiji state took temple lands (1871, 1875), so a graveyard "outside the precinct" may be Meiji's boundary rather than the Edo arrangement; the glosses are the web editors', not the register's, and describe modern Japanese usage - a Sōtō priest's family in the kuri dates from after the 1872 decree letting priests marry.

Used for: who lives inside a temple's precinct, and a graveyard held apart from it (religion-and-death.html).
