Ma Lei, Shen Chunyu, Lou Duo, Fu Shenglei and Guan Dongsheng, "Patterns of ecosystem carbon density in edge-affected fengshui forests", Ecological Engineering 107 (2017) (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0925857417304548; the full text is paywalled: DOI 10.1016/j.ecoleng.2017.07.037)

What it is: A peer-reviewed article by Ma Lei, Shen Chunyu, Lou Duo, Fu Shenglei and Guan Dongsheng, "Patterns of ecosystem carbon density in edge-affected fengshui forests", published in Ecological Engineering in 2017. It measures forty-five plots of 40 by 40 m across five surviving fengshui forest fragments around Guangzhou in southern China, reporting among other things how much tree trunk area each hectare of grove carries.

Why it applies, and its limits: The basal area it reports is our best readable measure of how densely a village's protective grove actually stands, which is what decides whether a grove is drawn as scattered trees or as a solid canopy. Its limits are real: the full text is behind a paywall, so we use only the abstract and the publicly visible passages; it measures subtropical Guangdong fragments as they survive today, so it is a modern measurement of the remains of an old practice, and a southern Chinese figure applied to a setting that leans Japanese.

Used for: basal area 30.0-52.5 m²/ha in five Guangzhou fengshui fragments (45 plots of 40 x 40 m)
