What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Changmen (阊门), the northwest gate of Suzhou. It describes the district that grew up outside the gate, which in Ming and Qing times was, by the article's account, the busiest commercial quarter in China, with streets radiating outward from the gate and shops in the thousands.

Why it applies, and its limits: Our city maps have to decide how much of a place lives outside its wall, and this is a concrete, named case of a suburb that outgrew the walled town beside it, with the street pattern that goes with it. It is a tertiary summary making an unattributed superlative claim, and Suzhou at its height was one of the wealthiest commercial cities of the empire, so it shows the extreme end of extramural growth rather than what an ordinary county seat would have outside its gates.

Used for: the Ming-Qing Changmen district 「the most flourishing commercial street district in all of China」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「全中国最繁盛的商业街区」), its radial streets outside the wall and tens of thousands of shops
