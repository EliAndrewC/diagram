Weblio, 京の百姓葬送の地 ("The commoners' burial ground of the capital", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a subsection of the ja.wikipedia article やぐら (yagura, rock-cut tombs) as mirrored by Weblio's Wikipedia-subheading dictionary, version of 2022-06-14 (https://www.weblio.jp/content/%E4%BA%AC%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BE%E5%A7%93%E8%91%AC%E9%80%81%E3%81%AE%E5%9C%B0)

What it is: A short passage of an encyclopedia article on medieval burial, mirrored on a dictionary site, saying that a Council of State order of 871 forbade turning the lower reaches of Kyoto's Kamo River into farmland because it was "the burial ground of the common people and a place for grazing".

Why it applies, and its limits: It places a Japanese commoners' burial ground on a river's banks in the ninth century, long before modern times. It is a modern paraphrase in which only that phrase is quoted from the order, it names no exact place or size, it is about the capital rather than a village, and the mirror warns that its entries are generated by program.

Used for: whether a burial ground kept back from water before modern times (religion and death)
