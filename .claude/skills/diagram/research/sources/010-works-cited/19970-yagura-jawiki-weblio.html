yagura-jawiki-weblio

Weblio, 京の百姓葬送の地 ("The commoners' burial ground of the capital", title translated), a subsection of the ja.wikipedia article やぐら (yagura, rock-cut tombs) as mirrored by Weblio's Wikipedia-subheading dictionary, version of 2022-06-14 (https://www.weblio.jp/content/%E4%BA%AC%E3%81%AE%E7%99%BE%E5%A7%93%E8%91%AC%E9%80%81%E3%81%AE%E5%9C%B0)

What it is: A short passage of the Japanese Wikipedia article on yagura (the rock-cut tombs of medieval Kamakura), from its digression on the capital's commoners' burial ground, mirrored on a dictionary site, saying that a Council of State order of 871 forbade turning the lower reaches of Kyoto's Kamo River into farmland because it was "the burial ground of the common people and a place for grazing".

Why it applies, and its limits: It places a Japanese commoners' burial ground in the lower reaches of a river in the ninth century, long before modern times. The page does not say riverbanks; a place-name dictionary (Kadokawa Nihon chimei daijiten, entry 石原郷) reads the same order as naming lots toward the Katsura River, a closer source for the riverside placement. It is a modern paraphrase in which only that phrase is quoted from the order, it names no exact place or size, it is about the capital rather than a village, and the mirror warns that its entries are generated by program. The same passage says most commoners from the Heian to the Kamakura period were not buried but laid out in the open, so this was a ground where bodies were left, not a yard of dug graves: it says where the dead were taken, not how a graveyard stood to water.

Used for: whether a burial ground kept back from water before modern times (religion and death)
