Yang Naiyun (杨乃运), 那个领军古衙文化的直隶总督署 ("The Zhili governor-general's yamen, leader of old-yamen culture", title translated), 《旅游》 (Tourism) magazine, 2020-01-07, carried on Visit Beijing (北京旅游网) (https://lyzz.visitbeijing.com.cn/news/detail?id=17810)

What it is: A travel essay by Yang Naiyun for 《旅游》 (Tourism) magazine, published 2020-01-07 and carried on Visit Beijing, the Beijing municipal tourism bureau's site, on the Zhili governor-general's yamen at Baoding, the highest-ranking yamen to survive. Written from the writer's third visit, it walks the plan court by court and compares it with the county yamen the writer had seen.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the bay count of the six offices at the top of the provincial hierarchy, the higher half of the record's one comparison of the six offices across ranks; the county's two bays come from other sources, not from this essay. It is a magazine essay that cites no source; its counts are of the buildings standing today, restored (the essay says only that the eastern side was opened after restoration), at one yamen of exceptional rank, and its comparison with county yamen - that the layout is much the same, and the yamen less imposing than the writer expected - is an impression. A count of bays is not a size: the essay gives neither a bay's width nor its depth, which a higher seat's halls may have had larger, so three bays against two compares bays, not floor area, and cannot by itself set a ceiling on how much larger a higher seat's office was.

Used for: the six offices of the Zhili governor-general's yamen, nine bays a side and three to each office, and the writer's finding that its plan follows a county yamen's (cities/government)
