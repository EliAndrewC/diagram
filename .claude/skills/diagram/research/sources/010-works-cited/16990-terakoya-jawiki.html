terakoya-jawiki

ja.wikipedia, 寺子屋 ("Terakoya", title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%AF%BA%E5%AD%90%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the commoners' writing schools of the Edo period: what they taught, the national and Edo counts from the Meiji survey Nihon kyoikushi shiryo, pupils per school, who the masters were in early-Meiji Tokyo (726 masters, ranked by former status but not counted, 86 of them women) and in one small Bingo district (seven schools), and a Meiji report on why the name points to temples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes Edo-period Japan directly and gathers the standard figures, naming the survey behind them. Its limits are that it footnotes none of its counts: the national count, the Edo count and the ten to a hundred pupils per school carry no citation; that its national count (16,560) differs by a thousand from other retellings of the same survey (15,560); that the survey looked back from the 1880s and recorded only the schools localities reported, and is generally read as an undercount, so its counts are floors; that its Edo figure (400-500 large schools, 1,000-1,300 in all) is an estimate for the largest city, not a town of ours; that its masters' breakdowns are for early-Meiji Tokyo (a ranking, not counts) and one small Bingo district of seven schools; and that it gives no provincial castle town's count.

Used for: what a terakoya was, who kept it and where, and how many there were (Trades and services)
