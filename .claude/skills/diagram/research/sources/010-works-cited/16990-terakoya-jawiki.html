terakoya-jawiki

ja.wikipedia, 寺子屋 ("Terakoya", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%AF%BA%E5%AD%90%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the commoners' writing schools of the Edo period: what they taught, the national and Edo counts from the Meiji survey Nihon kyoikushi shiryo, pupils per school, who the masters were in one district, and a Meiji report on why the name points to temples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gathers the standard figures and cites the survey and the studies behind them. Its limits are that it is tertiary; that its national count (16,560) differs by a thousand from other retellings of the same survey (15,560); that its masters' breakdown is for one small area; and that it gives no provincial castle town's count.

Used for: what a terakoya was, who kept it and where, and how many there were (urban-features)
