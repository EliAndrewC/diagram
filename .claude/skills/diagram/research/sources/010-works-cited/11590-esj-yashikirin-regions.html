esj-yashikirin-regions

「The structure of homestead groves (yashikirin): differences between regions」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷林の構造 - 地域による相違 -」), a paper abstract from the 51st annual meeting of the Ecological Society of Japan, on J-STAGE (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/esj/ESJ51/0/ESJ51_0_271/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A conference abstract comparing the woody species of homestead groves in four farming districts - Isawa (Iwate), Iide (Yamagata), Tonami (Toyama) and Hikawa (Shimane) - giving the species found in all, the average per homestead, and the most frequent species in each district.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a field count of what grows around working farmhouses in four regions, which is what says which fruit and nut trees were common in a farmstead's grounds. It is a modern survey of living groves, not a record of the pre-modern ones; its samples are very small (four to ten homesteads a district); and it is an abstract, so only its headline figures can be read.

Used for: chestnut and persimmon among the commonest trees of a farmstead's grounds, and the number of woody species per homestead (vegetation)
