esj-yashikirin-regions

「The structure of homestead groves (yashikirin): differences between regions」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷林の構造 - 地域による相違 -」), a paper abstract from the 51st annual meeting of the Ecological Society of Japan, on J-STAGE (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/esj/ESJ51/0/ESJ51_0_271/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A conference abstract comparing the woody species of homestead groves in four farming districts - Isawa (Iwate), Iide (Yamagata), Tonami (Toyama) and Hikawa (Shimane) - giving for each district the total number of woody species found, the average per homestead, and the three most frequent species. By Takehara, Murata, Hirabuki, Fukuoka and Miura (Iwate University and Miyagi University of Education), 2004, from counts of every tree, vegetation surveys and interviews.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a field count of what grows around working farmhouses in four regions, which is what says which trees were common in a farmstead's grounds. Chestnut is among the three commonest only in the two north-eastern districts (Isawa, Iide), and persimmon only in Tonami. In warm Hikawa neither is, and cedar leads in the other three. The abstract gives the groves' purposes as shelter from wind and weather, fuel and building timber, and does not say the chestnut or persimmon were kept for their fruit. All four are districts of dispersed farmsteads, where such groves are largest, so a nucleated village's house plots need not match. It is a modern survey of living groves, not a record of the pre-modern ones; its samples are very small (four to ten homesteads a district); and it is an abstract, so only its headline figures can be read.

Used for: chestnut and persimmon among the commonest trees of a farmstead's grounds, and the number of woody species per homestead (vegetation)
