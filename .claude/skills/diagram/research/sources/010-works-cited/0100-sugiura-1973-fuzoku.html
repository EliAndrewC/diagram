Sugiura Tadashi, 「On the outbuildings of farmhouses in rural settlements: the case of Miyazaki town, Miyagi prefecture」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農村集落における農家の付属建物について - 宮城県宮崎町の例」), Tōhoku Chiri 25(3): 145-152, 1973 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tga1948/25/3/25_3_145/_article/-char/ja)

What it is: A peer-reviewed geography article by Sugiura Tadashi, "農村集落における農家の付属建物について - 宮城県宮崎町の例" ("On the outbuildings of farmhouses in rural settlements: the case of Miyazaki town, Miyagi prefecture"), published in Tōhoku Chiri 25(3), 1973, pages 145-152. It counts, household by household, in July 1972, the outbuildings of all 87 households (77 farms and 10 non-farm households) in three hamlets of the town - Asahi, Kitanagashida and Minaminagashida. The count is by use, and a building with several uses is counted once under each (Table 5). From the town office's floor plans and a questionnaire, it also lists which uses stood inside the main house (Table 6). Both tables are broken down by the age of the main house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a household-by-household count of the outbuildings of three hamlets, by function, which is the strongest kind of evidence the record has for what stood on a farmstead and is what populates our farmyards. Its limits are that the survey was made in July 1972, a quarter century after the postwar land reform and well into mechanization, when, on this project's reading and not the paper's, the draft ox and horse had largely left Tohoku farms, so the barn and livestock-shed frequencies are a floor rather than a premodern figure and some sheds are twentieth-century equipment; that it is the cold, snowy northeast and not the whole country; that it is three hamlets of one town, 87 households, a sample the author calls surprisingly rich in outbuildings for Tohoku (4.38 a household, against 3.90 and 2.97 in the two surveys the paper compares it with); that the figures the record takes are mostly the row for the 38 farms whose main house was built in or before 1944, which is an older house and not a premodern one; that its table gives average counts per household by use, with a building of several uses counted once under each, so the figures cannot be added up into a number of buildings, and the record reads each one as an upper bound on the share of households owning one - the two agree only where no household kept two; and that the paper's own note 3 says that splitting farm uses out into separate outbuildings happened mainly between the Meiji era and the postwar years, here perhaps because stables were built inside the house as horse breeding grew, pushing other uses out. It is a modern count, and none of its figures is a premodern one: where the record has a count from before 1868 (a village record of 1824, a village count of 1885 read back to the bakumatsu), the map is calibrated to that instead, and those counts find far fewer outbuildings.

Used for: the per-household inventory of farmstead outbuildings by function (Table 5) and what stood inside the old main house (Table 6) - privy 0.87, firewood shed 0.76, straw shed 0.68, barn 0.58, work shed 0.55, livestock shed 0.32, kura 0.24, manure shed 0.24, bath 0.39 (0.29 over all 87 households), coop 0.16 (0.28 over all 87), household shrine none (0.01 over all 87) - every figure without a note is Table 5's row for the 38 farms whose main house was built in or before 1944; scope 1972 Tōhoku, stated wherever quoted
