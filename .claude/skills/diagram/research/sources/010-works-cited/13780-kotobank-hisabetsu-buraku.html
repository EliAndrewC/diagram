kotobank-hisabetsu-buraku

Kotobank, 被差別部落 ("hisabetsu buraku, the outcaste communities", title translated from the Japanese by this project), quoting Heibonsha's Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (revised new edition), entry by Yokoi Kiyoshi (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A2%AB%E5%B7%AE%E5%88%A5%E9%83%A8%E8%90%BD-154583)

What it is: The Heibonsha encyclopedia's long historical entry on the outcaste communities, from the medieval riverbank workers to the Edo-period status order, including where the Sengoku lords housed the leather workers they protected and controlled.

Why it applies, and its limits: A signed encyclopedia entry by the medieval historian Yokoi Kiyoshi, a tertiary summary general across Japan. The siting passage concerns the Sengoku domains, just before the Edo castle towns, rests on a few eastern domains, and is itself hedged ("apparently"); it gives no compass direction and no distance. The riverbed as the leather workers' workplace comes from a different passage, on the medieval "riverbank people" of the Kamakura to Nanbokuchō periods, not the Edo period.

Used for: the leather workers housed at the edge of the castle town, at traffic chokepoints and on riverbank ground, and the riverbed as their workplace (urban-features)
