nakanishi-1994-kogyu-koba

Nakanishi Ryotaro, "Meiji zenki ni okeru kogyu, koba no bunpu to gyubako fukyu no chiikisei ni tsuite" (The distribution of plow cattle and plow horses in the early Meiji period and the regional character of the spread of animal plowing), Rekishi Chirigaku (Historical Geography) 169, 1994, pp. 2-22 (in Japanese; http://hist-geo.jp/img/archive/169_002.pdf)

What it is: A paper in the journal of the Japanese Association of Historical Geographers that maps, prefecture by prefecture and district by district, the plow cattle and plow horses counted in the army's requisition inventory of 1886 (Meiji 19), and relates them to where animal plowing had spread.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the nearest national count to the Edo village: an inventory taken within a generation of the end of the domain era, as the Meiji campaign to spread horse plowing was beginning, which the author reads as showing an eastern horse country, a western cattle country and a mixed Kyushu and Shikoku. Its limits are that the figures are Meiji, not Edo, gathered for military requisition rather than for farming; that by 1886 the Meiji campaign to spread horse plowing (plowing teachers sent out from Fukuoka from the early 1880s) had begun, so some of the pattern may be Meiji promotion rather than Edo practice; that a count of the animals a district kept shows which animal it relied on, not how much of its land was plowed rather than hoed; that the author himself shows the counting rules changed between years, and warns that a national average per unit of farmland means almost nothing; and that the public PDF is a scan whose text layer is an imperfect OCR of a two-column page, so only sentences readable on the scan are quoted.

Used for: which draft animal plowed in which part of Japan (homesteads)
