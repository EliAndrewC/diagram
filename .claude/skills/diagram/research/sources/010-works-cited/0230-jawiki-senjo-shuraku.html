"Linear settlement", Japanese Wikipedia (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/線状集落)

What it is: An encyclopedia article defining the linear settlement - a settlement in the shape of a long line, developing along a road, river or canal, with buildings usually on both sides of a single street.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is cited here as a TRAP rather than as support: its "both sides" modifies a street, not a watercourse, and a reader meeting the term could take it for evidence that settlements straddle streams. It says a linear settlement may FOLLOW a river; it does not say its buildings stand on both banks of one. Tertiary, and its worked examples - in Quebec, Norfolk, Merseyside and Hong Kong - are Western and modern rather than premodern East Asian.

Used for: the both-sides that belongs to a street, not to a watercourse
