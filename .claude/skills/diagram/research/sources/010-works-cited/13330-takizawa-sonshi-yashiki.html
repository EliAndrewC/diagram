takizawa-sonshi-yashiki

Fukuda Takeo (compiler), 『農民生活変遷中心の滝沢村誌』 ("Takizawa village history, centered on changes in farmers' lives", title translated from the Japanese by this project; Takizawa village, 1974), part 3, chapter 3, 屋敷 ("house plots"), and chapter 5, 本家と分家 ("head houses and branch houses"), as published on the city's website (https://www.city.takizawa.iwate.jp/about-takizawa/gaiyo/bunkazai-history/sonshi/sonshi-sonrakukozo/p20241125122940)

What it is: Two chapters of the history of Takizawa village, in Iwate prefecture, in the lands of the old Nanbu domain, compiled by Fukuda Takeo and published by the village in 1974, now posted on Takizawa City's website; they cover the siting of houses, the domain's rules for house plots, and the customs of branch households.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Nanbu domain's survey rule of 1737, taken from the domain record 『郷村古実見聞記』, on how much house land a farmer working ten koku or more had counted as a house plot at survey (before 1737 three to five se; from 1737 up to three se on dry-field land and five on paddy land, anything beyond that entered into the assessed yield - a rule on how land was classed and taxed, not a cap on the size of a plot; farmers of five koku or more were judged separately), the rule that a dependent nago was in principle given no plot, though a house allowance might be granted according to the fief-holder's assessed yield, the order of 1715 that sent new branch houses to vacant plots, the field edge and the hill-foot roads, and the least a branch house was given by custom in neighboring Shiwa district, undated ("from of old") - main house, stable and privy, with one chō of field thought the most fitting. Its limits: one northern domain's regulations, read through a 1974 village history that names its documents but is itself secondary; what a domain allowed is not what was built; and the chapter says nothing of a headman's plot.

Used for: the house plot allowed an ordinary farmer, the nago's lack of one, and where a new branch house was allowed to go (homesteads)
