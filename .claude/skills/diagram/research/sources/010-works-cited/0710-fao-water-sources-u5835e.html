FAO irrigation training manual "Scheme Irrigation Water Need and Supply", chapter 2 "Water sources and water availability", section 2.2.2 on tapping water from rivers (https://www.fao.org/4/u5835e/u5835e03.htm)

What it is: The chapter on water sources of an FAO training manual for irrigation scheme planning: how a river's level rises and falls over the year, why a weir across the river steadies the level upstream of it so a canal can take water in the dry season, that the excess flows over the weir when the river is high, and how a control gate at the intake keeps the canal's discharge even.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states in the plainest words read what a diversion weir does and what becomes of the water it does not take - it goes over the crest and on down the river - which is the fact our maps rest on when the brook runs on past the intake. Its limits are that it is modern training material for scheme planners anywhere in the world, so it is used for the mechanics of a weir and not for what any premodern village built.

Used for: a weir steadies the upstream level; water flows over the weir in high discharge; a control gate at the intake
