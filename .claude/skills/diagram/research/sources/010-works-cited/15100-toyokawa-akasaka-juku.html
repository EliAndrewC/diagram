toyokawa-akasaka-juku

Toyokawa City, 赤坂宿 ("Akasaka-juku", the post station) (in Japanese; https://www.city.toyokawa.lg.jp/kanko_bunka_sports/rekishi/2/2/11746.html)

What it is: Toyokawa city's page on Akasaka, a Tokaido post station, describing its relay office, its lords' lodgings, its inns and the surviving inn Ohashiya, with counts from a 1733 record.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists the rooms an inn was made of - bath and privy among them - says the road front was usually two stories, and gives the frontage and depth of one large inn and of a lord's lodging. It is one station on the busiest highway, summarized for visitors by the city's board of education. The page names period documents elsewhere (town plans of 1711 and 1868, a 1789 servant's contract, a diary), but none as the basis for the room list or the sizes we take. Ohashiya's size is given only as approximate, and it is a large inn's, the top of the page's large, middle and small grades; the lord's lodging's size is stated exactly.

Used for: the rooms of a post-station inn and one large inn's frontage and depth (buildings)
