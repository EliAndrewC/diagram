kotobank-ipponbashi

Dictionary and gazetteer entries s.v. 一本橋 (ippon-bashi), aggregated at kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/一本橋)

What it is: Two modern Japanese dictionaries' entries for the one-log bridge (Shogakukan's Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the abridged edition, with its earliest attestation of the word in a haikai collection of 1722, the Hokkoku-kyoku; and the Digital Daijisen), and a gazetteer entry (Heibonsha's Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei) on a bridge of that name in Tottori that began as a single log.

Why it applies, and its limits: The dictionary names the water such a bridge crossed - a brook or a mountain stream - which is the scale of water our hamlet maps draw. That is a modern dictionary's gloss, not a period description: the 1722 attestation dates the word to the Edo period, but its line (a mantis waiting on a one-log bridge) does not name the water crossed. It says nothing of paddy ditches, gives no size, and the gazetteer's account of the Tottori bridge's first form is hedged ("it is said") and concerns a river crossing, not a field one.

Used for: that a one-log bridge was laid across a brook or mountain stream (water)
