jikata-monjo-kotobank

Kotobank, 地方文書 ("village documents", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%B0%E6%96%B9%E6%96%87%E6%9B%B8-72435)

What it is: A dictionary aggregator's page (Kotobank) carrying three Japanese reference entries on jikata monjo - Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen, the Nipponica encyclopedia's entry signed by the historian Kitahara Susumu, and Britannica's concise Japanese encyclopedia - on the administrative records Edo-period villages produced - land registers, tax records and the like.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where those records were kept and survive: in the houses of the families that served as village officials, nanushi and shoya. Its limits: it is a tertiary source, encyclopedia summaries and not the records themselves; it speaks mostly of where the papers survive today, not of where they were kept in the Edo period; and it does not keep all of them in the headman's house - the Nipponica entry says the most important official papers were handed from one headman to the next with an inventory, or else kept in wheeled chests (kuruma-nagamochi) in the gōgura, the village's communal storehouse, so they could be saved from fire. It is Japanese only.

Used for: the village's registers kept in the headman's house (homesteads)
