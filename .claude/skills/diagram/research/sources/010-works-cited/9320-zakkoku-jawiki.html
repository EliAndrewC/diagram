zakkoku-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 雑穀 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/雑穀)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on zakkoku, the coarse grains - generally the grains other than rice, wheat and barley (millets, barnyard millet, sorghum and the like; in Japanese usage beans and buckwheat too) - with their uses round the world and a history section mostly on prehistoric China, Korea and West Africa; a short encyclopedia article marked as a stub.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in Japan coarse grain was once an important staple, eaten as the main grain everywhere but Edo, until rice output rose in the Showa era. That sentence carries no citation in the article and gives no date for when coarse grain was the staple, so it is used for the fact that coarse grain was eaten, not for how much, when or where; its one cited example, Shiramine village in Fukui eating barnyard millet from slash-and-burn fields as its staple, is a single mountain village. It gives no shares of the diet, and it is about Japan only.

Used for: that coarse grain filled part of the diet, behind the acreage-from-population rule (fields)
