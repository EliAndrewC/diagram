koshoji-jiin-zoka

Kumano Tsuneharu, "【百八十四】寺院の増加 農民層が家を興していったことから寺が増えた" ("184: The increase in temples - temples multiplied as farming households were founded", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Honzan Kōshōji, Kyoto, 27 August 2019 (in Japanese; https://www.koshoji.or.jp/shiwa_154.html)

What it is: One of a series of short historical essays published by the head temple of the Kōshō branch of Shin Buddhism, on why Shin temples multiplied in the early and middle Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains how a village came to have a temple at all: a meeting hall of the faithful became a temple once the farming households round it became its parishioners and paid for its name and image. Its limits: it is written by and for one Buddhist denomination, and the meeting hall becoming a temple is told of that denomination's temples only - of the other schools it says only that they too built more temples; it does not call the halls village halls, and gives no village counts.

Used for: a village's meeting hall becoming its temple as farming households became its parish (religion-and-death)
