ekika-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 駅家 ("ekika, the relay station of the ancient highways", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A7%85%E5%AE%B6)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the relay stations of Japan's ancient state highways, from the seventh century into the Heian period: their horses by class of road, their staff and the station master's duties, and the difficulty of telling a station from a district office in excavation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the nearest historical institution to the setting's waystation - a state relay station on a trunk road with a fixed complement of horses - and it says some stations were run by the district office. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary; that the horse numbers are a rule of the eighth-century statutes (the page cites them to a 2015 popular history, and the Stable and Pasture Code is the source of the station rules beside them), which the page says rose or fell with each station's terrain; that these stations served only official couriers and their attendants (travelers of court rank could stay by exception), so they stand in for a state courier post and not an inn open to travelers; and that it describes the eighth to tenth centuries, some nine centuries earlier than the Edo sources beside it.

Used for: 20, 10 and 5 horses by class of road; stations run by the district office or kept in the station master's house (towns)
