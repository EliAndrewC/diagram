kotobank-funadama

kotobank, "船霊" (funadama) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%88%B9%E9%9C%8A-125522)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting several Japanese reference encyclopedias' entries on the funadama, among them the Nipponica and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its entries are reference-work articles, two of them signed by folklorists (Noguchi Takenori in the Nipponica, Takakuwa Moroshi in the Heibonsha), stronger than an open encyclopedia, and they say where in the boat the guardian is kept and that some boats only paste up a shrine or temple talisman instead. Every entry puts the guardian in the tube at the foot of the mast, and they disagree on where that is - the Nipponica at the bow, the Heibonsha in the middle of the boat. They describe folk practice recorded in modern fieldwork among fishermen and sea-going sailors, which the Nipponica traces to Edo-period coastal cargo ships. None speaks of river boats, and a poled river boat with no mast has no mast tube, so where the guardian goes on such a boat is our inference from these entries.

Used for: where in the boat the guardian is kept, and the talisman used in its place (river cities)
