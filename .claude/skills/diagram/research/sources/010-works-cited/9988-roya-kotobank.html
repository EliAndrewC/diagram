roya-kotobank

Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha; Hayashi Yukiko) and the Britannica concise encyclopedia, s.v. 牢屋, aggregated at kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%89%A2%E5%B1%8B-152554)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the jail, carrying encyclopedia entries on Japanese jails from the ancient period to the Edo jail compound at Kodenmacho - its kinds of cell by status, the overcrowding of its great cell - with the Britannica entry's note that the Edo-period magistrate's office (bugyosho) kept a temporary cell for prisoners called to its court; and a Heibonsha section on Chinese jails from the Zhou to the Qing, with the three-building form of a Qing jail.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is what says a magistracy held a cell of its own for those awaiting its court, which is the cell a county office draws, and it gives the great cell's size. Its limits are that the entries are tertiary; that the sizes are the capital's jail's; that the temporary cell is the Edo shogunate's, in a magistrate's office of a great city, so a county office in our setting holding one is our inference from a larger Japanese office, not a Chinese county's recorded practice; and that it gives no size or form for the temporary cell itself.

Used for: the 30-mat great cell; the temporary cell inside the magistracy (buildings)
