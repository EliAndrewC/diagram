tiangong-gongdao

Song Yingxing, 天工開物 ("The Works of Heaven and the Inception of Things", 1637), chapter 粹精, section 攻稻 ("Processing rice", translated from the Chinese by this project), on Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E5%B7%A5%E9%96%8B%E7%89%A9/%E6%94%BB%E7%A8%BB)

What it is: The rice-processing section of the late-Ming encyclopedia of crafts, on hulling with the husking mill, pounding in the mortar and the water pestle.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says who pounded where: a household of eight or more buried a stone mortar and trod a foot pestle; the water pestle was built by people of hill country living by rivers, with two or three mortars on a small stream and up to ten on a large one, in one prefecture on an earth-filled boat; and some wheels ground, pounded and lifted water at once. It is Ming and southern, and it gives forms and mortar counts rather than how many mills a district had.

Used for: the household foot pestle; the water pestle's setting and mortar counts (water)
