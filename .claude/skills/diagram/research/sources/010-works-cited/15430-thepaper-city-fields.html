thepaper-city-fields

Wei Zhou, 城市里的田园：都市为何离不开农村 ("Fields in the city: why the city cannot do without the countryside", translated from the Chinese by this project), The Paper, history column, 16 July 2016 (https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1477443)

What it is: An essay in a Shanghai news site's history column gathering evidence, from the Southern Dynasties to the twentieth century, that Chinese cities long held fields and gardens inside their walls and that city dwellers farmed them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the readable page that says who worked the ground inside a Chinese city: its own residents. It is a popular essay, not a study; it quotes historians and travelers rather than giving sources in full, one of its figures for Nanjing belongs to 1921 rather than the date its neighbor gives, and much of its evidence is of war-emptied cities of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Used for: city dwellers growing rice and vegetables inside the wall (cities/fabric.html, cities/hinterland.html)
