suido-ishizue-iseki

ARIC (Agricultural and Rural Integrated Information Center), Suido no Ishizue, "Taking water: the iseki" (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/04/03.html)

What it is: A page of the history of Japan's land and water published by ARIC, an association in the agricultural-engineering field, explaining why irrigation weirs were built and how they developed, with captioned illustrations of a weir excavated at the Kodera site (Matsuyama) and a stake-and-reed "grass weir" still in use in Saga.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the condition that decides between a bare intake and a weir, and it describes the earliest and simplest weirs, which were built on small streams - the scale of a hamlet's brook. It is a popular history from the modern irrigation profession, it gives no dates for most of its general statements, no dimensions, and no count of how many intakes took each form.

Used for: when a bare intake served and when a weir was needed; the earliest weirs, of nearby wood and stone, on small streams; the stake-and-reed grass weir; the stake-and-log weir of the Kodera site (water)
