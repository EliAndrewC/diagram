Wang Jian (王健), "明清以来江南民间信仰中的庙界：以苏、松为中心" ("Temple boundaries in Jiangnan popular religion since the Ming and Qing, centered on Suzhou and Songjiang", title translated from the Chinese by this project), Institute of History, Shanghai Academy of Social Sciences (in Chinese; https://ih.sass.org.cn/_upload/article/files/85/9b/3c530d694fb39357215c6e822c8c/a8324f93-0201-498d-8e0f-c2086ec92055.pdf)

What it is: A research article by a historian at the Shanghai Academy of Social Sciences on the territories served by earth-god temples and market-town temples in the Suzhou and Songjiang region from the Ming onward, drawing on gazetteers and on Hamashima Atsutoshi's fieldwork.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the Ming statute that set one altar to the gods of soil and grain for every hundred rural households, and reports Hamashima's count of earth-god temples against villages - about one to every ten-odd lowland villages, one to about forty small upland ones - which is the Chinese village's count. Its limits: it is one region, the Yangzi delta; the ratios are Hamashima's, reported rather than recomputed, and the author himself doubts them, because a township gazetteer could not record every small temple of its area; and the statute is an ideal of the early Ming rather than a measured pattern.

Used for: the Ming altar for every hundred households; earth-god temples per village in Jiangnan (religion-and-death)
