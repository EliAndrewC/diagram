nagaokakyo-muraezu-1801

Nagaokakyo City Lifelong Learning Division, Cultural Properties Section, "Exhibition corner news no. 49" (in Japanese; https://www.city.nagaokakyo.lg.jp/cmsfiles/contents/0000005/5235/hurusatofile49.pdf)

What it is: A two-page exhibition leaflet of January 2012 from the city of Nagaokakyo, southwest of Kyoto, which sets Edo-period village maps of the district beside photographs of the same places, and which reproduces and describes a village map of 1801 drawn when two villages settled a boundary dispute.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the 1801 map draws a local lord's house surrounded by a thicket (yabu), with houses along the road, so it shows a map before modern times drawing a thicket as a feature of its own. It does not say the thicket was bamboo. The map is reproduced in colour at about half a page: the thicket is a dark, mottled grey-green wash filling the ground around the residence, and the houses are small roofed figures along the road, though the map's written labels are too small to read. It is one map, of a local landholding family's residence rather than a commoners' hamlet, and the leaflet itself warns that maps of the period are loose in scale and detail.

Used for: a thicket drawn on a village map of 1801 (vegetation)
