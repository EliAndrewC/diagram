hattori-ikedazumi

服部保, 南山典子, 松村俊和 (Hattori Tamotsu, Minamiyama Noriko and Matsumura Toshikazu), 猪名川上流域の池田炭と里山林の歴史 ("The history of Ikeda charcoal and the satoyama woods of the upper Ina River", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 植生学会誌 (Vegetation Science) 22(1): 41-51, 2005 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/vegsci/22/1/22_KJ00005989601/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A peer-reviewed paper by vegetation scientists that gathers the old documents, books and traditions on Ikeda charcoal and compares them with how the district's kunugi woods are managed today; cited here from its abstract on J-STAGE.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its abstract states that old documents and traditions record 27 charcoal items between 1145 and 1833, rotational cutting among them, that a land survey of the Enpo era records kunugi hills in the district's villages, and that the old records agree with today's practice on points including the rotation period - which is what dates a short coppice cycle before modernity. Only the abstract was read here. The abstract counts documents and traditions together: it does not say which of the 27 records are oral traditions, nor where within 1145-1833 the rotational cutting falls. So the span dates the records, not the rotation, and its early end need not be a document. The abstract gives no cycle in years, and the paper is about one charcoal district, famous for tea-ceremony charcoal.

Used for: rotational cutting of kunugi among 27 charcoal records (old documents and traditions) of 1145 to 1833, and the old rotation agreeing with today's (towns)
