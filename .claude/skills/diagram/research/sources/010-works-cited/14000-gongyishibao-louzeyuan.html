gongyishibao-louzeyuan

"古代慈善救济机构'漏泽园'与佛教" ("The louzeyuan, a charitable relief institution of old, and Buddhism", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 公益时报 (China Philanthropy Times), April 2024 (http://www.gongyishibao.com/html/xunzhaocishanchuantong/2024/04/27099.html)

What it is: A newspaper's history feature on the Song public pauper cemeteries that quotes the Song local gazetteers and Xu Du's Queshao bian on how the grounds were laid out and kept.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the quoted details of the ground itself - a wall or palisade round it against intruders and grazing animals, trees planted in it, 8 chi of ground a burial (9 for one with kin who wished it buried there), graves grouped in thirties with ditches, a corner kept for a temple, and one gazetteer's walled cemetery of plots 7 chi by 1 zhang. It is a popular article quoting its sources in fragments; its reading that the walled cemetery was terraced on a hillside is the author's guess; and it describes Song China only.

Used for: the layout, fence and planting of a pauper cemetery (religion and death)
