gongyishibao-louzeyuan

"古代慈善救济机构'漏泽园'与佛教" ("The louzeyuan, a charitable relief institution of old, and Buddhism", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 公益时报 (China Philanthropy Times), April 2024 (http://www.gongyishibao.com/html/xunzhaocishanchuantong/2024/04/27099.html)

What it is: An unsigned article from the Journal of Hunan University of Science and Engineering (湖南科技学院学报), reprinted by a charity newspaper without its notes, on the Song public pauper cemeteries; it quotes the Song local gazetteers and Xu Du's Queshao bian on how the grounds were laid out and kept.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the quoted details of the ground itself - a wall or palisade round it against intruders and grazing animals, trees planted in it, 8 chi of ground a burial (9 for one with kin who wished it buried there), graves grouped in thirties with ditches, a corner kept for a temple, and one gazetteer's walled cemetery of plots 7 chi by 1 zhang. It is a journal article reprinted without its notes, quoting its sources in fragments; its reading that the walled cemetery was terraced on a hillside is the author's guess; its "8 chi a burial" does not say whether that is a length or the side of a square; the grounds were ordered for every prefecture and county seat (1104), so they fit a town's or city's pauper ground, not a village's; and its layout details are Song China's only (it says the institution lasted until the Qing but quotes nothing later on the layout).

Used for: the layout, fence and planting of a pauper cemetery (religion and death)
