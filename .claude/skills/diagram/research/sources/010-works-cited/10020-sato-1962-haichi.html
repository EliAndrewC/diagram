sato-1962-haichi

Sato Jinjiro, 「The building composition and layout of Japanese farmsteads」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「日本農家の建物構成と配置方式」), Jinbun Chiri (Human Geography) 14(6): 445-464, 1962 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjhg1948/14/6/14_6_445/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed geography article surveying, across Japan, which buildings a farmstead had besides the house and how they were laid out around the front yard, with worked examples from the Shonai plain on the Japan Sea side and the Hadano basin on the Pacific side, both surveyed in 1948.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a geographer's survey of how the farmstead's buildings and yard were arranged, and it says what the front yard was used for in each climate. Its limits are that its examples were surveyed in 1948, not in the Edo period, and that by the author's own account the regional types it describes took their form between 1900 and 1930, when new rice and sericulture techniques changed which buildings a farmstead had, and were changed again after the war by land reform and mechanized farming, so a building, or where it stands in the yard, may be a modern form rather than an Edo one; that it describes regional types, so a single example stands for a region; and that its layout figures are images whose detail cannot be read from the text.

Used for: the Shonai front yard planted with fruit trees; the Shonai woodshed for winter fuel (homesteads)
