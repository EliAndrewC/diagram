sato-1962-haichi

Sato Jinjiro, 「The building composition and layout of Japanese farmsteads」 (translated; original: 「日本農家の建物構成と配置方式」), Jinbun Chiri (Human Geography) 14(6): 445-464, 1962 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjhg1948/14/6/14_6_445/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed geography article surveying, across Japan, which buildings a farmstead had besides the house and how they were laid out around the front yard, with worked examples from the Shonai plain on the Japan Sea side and the Hadano basin on the Pacific side (one farmstead each, surveyed in 1948) and a survey of ten farmsteads in one Shonai hamlet made in December 1961; it also draws on national farm-household statistics of 1955 and, for Kyushu and the snowy northeast only, on Edo-period records such as a 1633 Higo register of houses and livestock.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a geographer's survey of how the farmstead's buildings and yard were arranged, and it says what the front yard was used for in each climate. Its limits are that its Shonai examples were surveyed in 1948 and 1961, not in the Edo period, and the Edo-period records it reads concern Kyushu and the snowy northeast, not the Shonai yard or woodshed, and that by the author's own account the regional types it describes took their form between 1900 and 1930, when new rice and sericulture techniques changed which buildings a farmstead had, and were changed again after the war by land reform and mechanized farming, so a building, where it stands in the yard, or what the front yard is planted with (the flower beds above all, though the author puts the yard's narrowness and its little use as a drying floor, and the woodshed, down to the rain and snow that set in after the Shonai harvest and the cold winters, causes that held in the Edo period too) may be a modern form rather than an Edo one; that it describes regional types, so a single example stands for a region; and that its layout figures are images whose detail cannot be read from the text.

Used for: the Shonai front yard planted with fruit trees, or made into flower beds or vegetable beds; the Shonai woodshed for winter fuel (Homesteads)
