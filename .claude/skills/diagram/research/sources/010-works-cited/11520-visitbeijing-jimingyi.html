Beijing Tourism Network, 古城鸡鸣驿，邮政几春秋 ("The old town of Jimingyi, how many springs and autumns of the post", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 2021 (in Chinese; https://www.visitbeijing.com.cn/article/47QoO7ycoec)

What it is: A short article on the Beijing municipal tourism site about Jimingyi in Hebei, the largest and best-preserved surviving Chinese relay station, giving its area, its wall's circuit and height, its gates, its five roads and twelve blocks, and where the relay office, stables and granary stand.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable account found of a Chinese relay town's plan, with figures, which gives the Chinese side of the post-town question a measured example. It is a tourism article, not a survey; Jimingyi is a single station, singled out as the largest of its kind, on an important route near the capital, so it shows what a relay town could be rather than what an ordinary one was. Jimingyi stood on the Ming northern frontier, on the road to the Xuanfu garrison near the Great Wall, and was fortified as part of that frontier's defences: its tall wall is evidence of what the frontier required, not that relay towns in the interior were walled. Its figures are modern measurements of the surviving and partly restored Ming and Qing town, rounded ("about 2000 metres") and given without a source.

Used for: Jimingyi's area, wall, roads and the placing of its office, stables and granary (towns)
