kotobank-banta

Kotobank, 番太 ("Banta, the hired watchman", title translated) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%AA%E5%A4%AA-118241)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the watchman hired by towns and villages, carrying the Digital Daijisen, the Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten, Nipponica, Britannica Japan and a snippet of Heibonsha's older World Encyclopedia, all on the Edo period: his duties in town and village, his status, his hut and the fact that he was paid.

Why it applies, and its limits: Four reference works, and a snippet of a fifth, agree on the watchman's duties and status and separate the village's from the town's. Their limits are that they are tertiary; that they say his status varied by place (commoners in Edo, mostly hinin elsewhere); and that they give no count of villages with one, no size of his hut, and no siting of a village's hut (only the town's, at a crossroads or beside the ward gate). They also give no amount for his pay, saying only that a watchman's stipend was paid; the side trade in sweets and sandals belongs to the Edo town watchman.

Used for: the village watchman's duties, status, hut and pay (urban-features)
