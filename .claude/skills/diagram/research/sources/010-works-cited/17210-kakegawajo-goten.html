Kakegawa Castle (Kakegawa city park office), 掛川城御殿 ("The Kakegawa Castle palace", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kakegawajo.com/goten/)

What it is: The castle's own visitor page on its surviving palace: the palace's three functions, its seven buildings and about twenty rooms, its move from the honmaru to the ninomaru, and which entrance each rank used.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what a domain palace was for - ceremonies and audiences, the lord's residence, and the domain's offices - and how big one was in buildings and rooms. It is a visitor page for one palace of a small domain, without measurements. That palace is one of only four castle palaces left in Japan, so the page cannot say whether its size was typical. The building is the 1855-1861 rebuild after the Ansei earthquake, and its kitchen was removed in the Meiji era. Its move from the honmaru to the ninomaru followed the 1707 earthquake at this castle, so it shows where one late-Edo palace stood, not a rule for where they stood.

Used for: what a domain's palace held and where it stood in the castle (cities/capitals)
