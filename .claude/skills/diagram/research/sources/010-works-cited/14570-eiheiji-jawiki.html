ja.wikipedia 永平寺 ("Eiheiji", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B0%B8%E5%B9%B3%E5%AF%BA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Eiheiji in Fukui, the head temple of the Soto Zen school founded by Dogen, with its history, buildings and gate town.

Why it applies, and its limits: It relays the tradition that the carpenters Dogen brought from Song China made a carpenters' village near the gate and, as the Eiheiji carpenters, handled the temple's enlargements and repairs for generations - a retained craft community housed by its temple. It gives the account as a tradition (it is said), its footnote is a television program, and Eiheiji is a mountain head temple rather than a city one.

Used for: a temple's retained carpenters living by its gate (religion and death)
