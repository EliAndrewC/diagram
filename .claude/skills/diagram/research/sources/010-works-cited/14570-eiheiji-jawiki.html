What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Eiheiji in Fukui, the head temple of the Soto Zen school founded by Dogen, with its history, buildings and gate town.

Why it applies, and its limits: It relays the tradition that the carpenters Dogen brought from Song China made a carpenters' village near the gate and, as the Eiheiji carpenters, handled the temple's enlargements and repairs for generations - a craft community settled at a temple's gate and working on it for generations. It gives the account as a tradition (it is said) and its only footnote is a television program; it does not say the temple housed, paid or retained the carpenters, or that they worked for it alone, and a closer study of one of the village's carpenter families, read from its own account book (Kunikyo Katsumi, Jakuetsu Kyodo Kenkyu vol. 65 no. 1), finds it in 1838-39 working mostly for other houses, shrines and temples across Echizen and as far as Osaka. Eiheiji is a mountain head temple of some seventy buildings, one of its school's two, rather than a city one: a standing carpenters' village fits that scale and does not show that an ordinary town temple kept carpenters of its own.

Used for: a temple's retained carpenters living by its gate (religion and death)
