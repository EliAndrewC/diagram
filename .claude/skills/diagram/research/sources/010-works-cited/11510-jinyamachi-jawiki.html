ja.wikipedia 陣屋町 ("jin'ya town", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%99%A3%E5%B1%8B%E7%94%BA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the jin'ya town, the Edo-period town formed around a small lord's or an intendant's administrative seat, with samurai houses and the merchants and craftsmen who served them, followed by a list of examples by region.

Why it applies, and its limits: The jin'ya town is the closest Japanese counterpart to this setting's county seat, a town grown around a magistrate's compound rather than a castle, and the article says what gathered there. It is a short article tagged as needing sources, with no plan or measurement, so it is used only for its one-sentence description.

Used for: the town that grew around a jin'ya: samurai houses and the tradesmen who served them (towns)
