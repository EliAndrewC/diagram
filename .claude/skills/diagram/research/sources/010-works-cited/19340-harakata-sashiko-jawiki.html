Japanese Wikipedia contributors, "原方刺し子" [Harakata quilting] (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8E%9F%E6%96%B9%E5%88%BA%E3%81%97%E5%AD%90)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the quilted work clothes of the harakata-shu, the lower samurai of the Yonezawa domain settled on the moors around the town after the Uesugi house's fief was cut in 1601; its opening sections give their history and the lots the domain granted them, from cited local histories.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a domain's own lot for its lowest samurai in the early 1600s, larger than the lower lots of Fukui's table, with half of it a vegetable field. It is an encyclopedia article resting on secondary works, not the domain's order; it clashes with itself on two figures (some 6,000 retainers moved, yet some 8,000 became harakata-shu; a lot of 150 tsubo, yet a frontage of 6 to 8 ken by a depth of about 25 ken, which makes 150 to 200), and these were half-farming samurai on the town's edge, not a castle town's ordinary ward.

Used for: how generous a domain's lots for its lower samurai could be (cities/government)
