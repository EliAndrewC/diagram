What it is: Francesca Bray's chapter "Where Did the Animals Go? Presence and Absence of Livestock in Chinese Agricultural Treatises", in Animals through Chinese History, edited by Roel Sterckx, Martina Siebert and Dagmar Schafer (Cambridge University Press, 2018). It is a peer-reviewed scholarly chapter reading four centuries-spanning Chinese farming manuals to ask whether livestock really vanished from Chinese farms, and argues that they remained ordinary even where the manuals stopped writing about them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the modest claim our farmyards need, that an ordinary smallholding kept a pig and some poultry in the yard, and it does so from an authority that has weighed the counter-argument rather than assumed it. Two honest limits: the picture of the ordinary household leans on twentieth-century survey data, so a 1930s count is standing in for the older practice the treatises describe, and it is about China, while the farmstead our maps draw is Japanese in its surface detail.

Used for: "farmers in most regions of China managed to keep a pig and some chickens in their yard".
