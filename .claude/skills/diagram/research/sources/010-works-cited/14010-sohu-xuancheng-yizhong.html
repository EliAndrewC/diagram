"宣城'漏泽园'历史概述" ("An outline history of Xuancheng's louzeyuan", title translated from the Chinese by this project), a local-history article by Yan Liping (晏利平), issue 668 of the Xuancheng history society's series 宣城历史文化研究, posted on Sohu 2021-01-15 (https://www.sohu.com/a/375277815_99949262)

What it is: A local-history article on the pauper and charity burial grounds of Xuancheng county in Anhui, from the Song louzeyuan to the Qing charity graves, quoting the county gazetteer's list of them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the gazetteer's sizes for a county's charity graves - several separate grounds of one to three mu each, and a later purchase of twenty-two mu - the one set of areas read for a county's pauper grounds. The list is Qing, not Song, it is one county, the article elides the gazetteer around the figures it quotes, and it is a popular local-history essay by a schoolteacher, Yan Liping (晏利平), a member of the Xuancheng history society, reposted on Sohu in 2021 - not a historian's study. Xuancheng was also the seat of Ningguo prefecture in prosperous Jiangnan, and the article itself calls its grounds unusually large, so its sizes are those of a large, rich county town, not of a typical or poor county.

Used for: the number and size of a county's charity burial grounds (religion and death)
