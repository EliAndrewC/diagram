Kotobank, 城郭都市 ("walled city", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9F%8E%E9%83%AD%E9%83%BD%E5%B8%82-530860)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the walled city, whose Nipponica entry, signed by the historical geographer Asaka Yukio, describes the walled towns of Europe and Asia and then sets Japan apart: after the low banks of some ancient provincial capitals, only the lord's castle was fortified, and the castle town's residents lived outside it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a signed encyclopedia statement of the contrast this record's walled-or-open knob turns on, Japanese against Chinese. It is a short tertiary entry; it generalizes over medieval and early-modern Japan and does not treat the moated exceptions.

Used for: that in Japan only the lord's castle was fortified (towns)
