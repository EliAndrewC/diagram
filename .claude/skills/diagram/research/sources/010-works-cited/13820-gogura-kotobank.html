gogura-kotobank

Kotobank, 郷蔵 ("gogura", title translated from the Japanese by this project): the entries of the 精選版 日本国語大辞典 and the 山川 日本史小辞典 (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%83%B7%E8%94%B5-2035847)

What it is: A dictionary aggregator's page carrying two Japanese reference entries on the gogura, the Edo-period village storehouse for the tax rice and the famine reserve, one of them quoting the 1794 administrative handbook 地方凡例録 on where such a storehouse was built.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the headman's own storehouse had to do with the tax rice: not every village had a gogura, several villages might share one, a headman's storehouse might serve instead, and before the shogunate's 1789 order the village officials' private storehouses were often used. Its limits: these are short dictionary entries, the 1789 order is a shogunal one and the entries do not say how far domains followed it, they give no count of villages with and without a gogura, and they say nothing of China.

Used for: where the tax rice waited - the village gogura, or the headman's own storehouse in its place (homesteads)
