東京都公文書館 (Tokyo Metropolitan Archives), the publisher's page for 都史紀要42 The hirokoji of Edo: their use and their management (title translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 『江戸の広小路－その利用と管理』) (https://www.soumu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/01soumu-archives/06kanko_butsu/0604t_kiyo/0604t_kiyo42)

What it is: The Tokyo Metropolitan Archives' own page for its published study of the Edo hirokoji, summarizing what those widened streets were - fire-clearance open ground on which no permanent building could stand - and what filled them: removable stall shops trading goods, markets, street performance and reed-screened entertainment booths, until many became pleasure quarters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the archive's own account of the firebreak this record describes and of the reason a cleared strip did not stay clear, which is the whole of what our entry on why no firebreak is drawn depends on. Its limits are that it is a publisher's summary of a book rather than the book; that it is Edo, the largest and most fire-prone city of the period, so the scale is a ceiling; and that it names no dimension for a strip.

Used for: the hirokoji as fire-clearance ground closed to permanent building, and the stalls, markets and booths that filled it
