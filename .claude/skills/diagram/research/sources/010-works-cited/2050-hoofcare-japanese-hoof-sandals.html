hoofcare-japanese-hoof-sandals

Fran Jurga, "Hoofcare History: Japanese Hoof Sandals Gave Horses Removable Traction", Hoofcare + Lameness blog, 2013 (https://hoofcare.blogspot.com/2013/02/hoofcare-history-japanese-hoof-sandals.html)

What it is: A post on a farriery trade publication's blog about the Japanese straw horse-sandal, drawing on two Western periodicals of the late nineteenth century - a farriers' journal correspondent's report of their price and a Scientific American piece on their cheapness and wear.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that the sandals were cheap, quickly worn out and easily replaced, which is why a courier station on these maps stocks them. Its limits are that it is a trade blog retelling a traveler's account rather than scholarship, and that it gives no rate of wear per distance. The observations are of Meiji Japan in the 1890s, after the setting's period.

Used for: straw sandals as cheap consumables that wore out fast
