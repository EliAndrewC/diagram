kotobank-shiboku-sanso

Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho and Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten, s.v. 四木三草 ("the four trees and three grasses", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/四木三草-75024)

What it is: Two Japanese reference entries on the "four trees and three grasses" of the early-modern period - tea, mulberry, lacquer and paper mulberry, with hemp, safflower and indigo - the high-value crops grown for sale and for processing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says these were crops of the field, restricted by the shogunate's rules on what a field might grow and later grown as local specialties, which is why a mulberry or a tea planting is a crop rather than a dooryard tree. The two entries differ in emphasis - one says the domains encouraged the crops, the other that the shogunate restricted them - and neither says how much of any village's ground they took.

Used for: tea and mulberry as field crops grown for sale, not farmstead trees (vegetation)
