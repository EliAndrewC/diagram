en.wikipedia, Qingming Festival (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingming_Festival)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Qingming, the Chinese tomb-sweeping festival: the visit to the ancestors' graves, the cleaning and maintenance of the ground, and the offerings burned there - incense, joss paper, spirit money and paper replicas of goods.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives us what actually happens at a Chinese grave, which is what makes a burial ground a tended place on our maps rather than waste ground, and it names the paper goods whose makers we put among the funeral trades. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary; that it describes the festival as practiced rather than a historical period, so it cannot date anything; and that it says nothing about where the goods were made or sold, nor how far from a mound the tending reached, which is the figure our drawn collar would need.

Used for: the spirit money and paper replicas a family burns for its dead, and the tomb-sweeping and groundskeeping at a grave
