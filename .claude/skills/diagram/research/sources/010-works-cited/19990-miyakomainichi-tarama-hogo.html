miyakomainichi-tarama-hogo

Miyako Mainichi Shimbun, 「Tarama's hogo forest named forestry heritage」 (translated; original: 「林業遺産に多良間「抱護林」」), June 5, 2019 (in Japanese; https://www.miyakomainichi.com/news/post-120598/)

What it is: A local newspaper's report, on the Miyako islands, of Tarama's hogo belt being named forestry heritage, giving its course, its length and width, and what it shelters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the belt's shape on the ground - a band about 1.8 km long and 10 to 15 m wide, formed so as to surround the settlement, sheltering the settlement and its farmland from typhoons and salt wind - which the forestry-heritage page does not. The dimensions are the belt as it stands in 2019, with parts of it lost, not a measurement of the belt as planted in the eighteenth century; the page is the free opening of a longer article, and a newspaper's figures, not a survey it names; it describes one island of the Ryukyus; and the settlement the belt surrounds is a small island's village, so its length is a village's and does not scale directly to the ring around a town.

Used for: the Tarama hogo's course around its settlement, and its length and width today (towns)
