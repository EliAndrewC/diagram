What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the tensuioke, the traditional firefighting cistern: a vessel for storing rainwater, used in the Edo period mainly as firefighting water in urban areas.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests the stored water our fire posture lists, dated to the period and to the town. Its limits are that it is a short tertiary article, Japanese and Edo-specific, giving no count of how many a street kept and no size, so nothing on the map is drawn from it.

Used for: rainwater stored against fire in an Edo town
