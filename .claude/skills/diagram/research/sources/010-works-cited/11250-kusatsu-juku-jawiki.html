ja.wikipedia 草津宿 ("Kusatsu-juku", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8D%89%E6%B4%A5%E5%AE%BF)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Kusatsu, the post station where the Tokaido and the Nakasendo met, with its 1843 counts of houses, lodgings and inhabitants and a list of its facilities.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets one station's inns against its houses and population, which shows how much of a post town's street was inn. It is a tertiary summary; Kusatsu, a junction of two highways, is busier than most stations, and the page itself gives a higher inn count at another time (132 against 72). Its 1843 counts are credited to the shogunate's Tōkaidō shukuson taigaichō survey, but without a footnote. Its own line that one house in five or six was an inn has no source and does not follow from its 1843 figures, where 72 inns among 586 houses is about one in eight.

Used for: Kusatsu's 586 houses, 2,351 inhabitants and 72 inns in 1843, about one house in eight (the page's own "one in five or six" is undated and unsourced) (towns)
