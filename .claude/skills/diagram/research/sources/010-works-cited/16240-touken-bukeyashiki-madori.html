Touken World, 間取りで見る武家屋敷 ("samurai residences seen through their floor plans", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.touken-world.jp/tips/105023/)

What it is: A popular, unsigned article on Touken World, a site made by the Token Corporation (a real-estate and construction company) with its sword museum's foundation. It walks through the plan of the Maeda lords' main Edo mansion and of two samurai houses shown to visitors today, the Nomura house in Kanazawa and the Kuchiba house in Hagi, giving their stipends, one plot size (the Nomura house's) and their room layouts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the plot of a 1,200-koku retainer of Kaga (about 1,000 tsubo) and the place of the kitchen in the Kuchiba house of Hagi, a house of about 1,000 koku whose main building (late eighteenth or early nineteenth century) still stands. That anchors the upper rungs of a castle town's samurai ladder but not its top: Kaga's senior houses held ten thousand koku and more, and Kaga was the largest domain in Japan, so its stipends run higher than a smaller lord's town would. It is a general-audience article that cites no documents, and the Nomura plot is a round figure for the house as it was, not a surveyed one. The Nomura house itself was pulled down in the Meiji era and its plot divided; the rooms shown there now, which the article describes, were moved in from a shipowner's mansion, so they are no samurai's plan. It describes only well-known houses of rich retainers and says nothing of the ordinary retainer's house.

Used for: the plot of a 1,200-koku retainer and where a senior house's kitchen stood (cities/government)
