alic-iwakuni-renkon

Agriculture and Livestock Industries Corporation (ALIC), "Producing district report: Yamaguchi prefecture, JA Iwakuni-shi", Yasai Joho monthly, February 2015 (in Japanese; https://vegetable.alic.go.jp/yasaijoho/santi/1502_santi1.html)

What it is: A producing-district report written by Sasaki Jun of the sales section of JA Iwakuni-shi, the district's own growers' cooperative, and published in the vegetable-market monthly of a government agricultural corporation (ALIC), describing the Iwakuni lotus-root district (Yamaguchi prefecture): its origin, its growth, today's planted area and shipments, and its marketing.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its history is a modern retelling that hedges the 1796 start as what "is said", and it does not date the growth it credits to the domain's successive lords; like the Iwakuni city tourism page, it has someone other than the lord bring the seed lotus - here Muramoto Sangorō of Atago, on the lord's order. It is the growers' cooperative describing its own district, ending in a sales appeal, not an independent account, and it says the variety was changed in the Meiji period and that production in today's Oze-Monzen district flourished only from about 1955. Its area figures (331 hectares in 1972, lotus about six times the rice in 2015) are modern, and both come after the 1970 incentives to convert paddy from rice; the six-to-one covers the whole cooperative's area, the pre-2006-merger Iwakuni city, after housing and commercial building took farmland. So they measure a modern municipality's lotus district, not a premodern village.

Used for: Iwakuni lotus said to begin in 1796 and spread by the domain's lords on reclaimed land fit for no other crop; the one lotus area share read, a modern one (archetypes)
