kotobank-murazakai

Fukuta Ajio (福田 アジオ), 「Village boundary」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「村境」), in the Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha, revised edition), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9D%91%E5%A2%83-140837)

What it is: The Heibonsha world encyclopedia's signed entry on the village boundary, by a leading folklorist of the Japanese village, reproduced on the kotobank dictionary site beside shorter dictionary and Britannica entries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the ideal-type layout of a Japanese village - houses at the center, fields around them, hill and wild land beyond - and says when its outer boundary, the edge of the hill and wild land, became clear: gradually through the self-governing medieval village, and then fixed by the land surveys and village partition at the start of the Edo period. The entry never names woods; that a village's woods stood in that outer ring of hill and wild land is this project's reading. It is an ideal type, as the author says, not a survey of real villages. It is a short encyclopedia summary without references, fit for the shape and not for any measure. Its hedge ("it is thought") falls only on the outer boundary having once been unclear. And it describes the Japanese village only: bounds fixed by the Edo land survey are a Japanese institution, and it says nothing of how Chinese villages were bounded.

Used for: the concentric order of houses, fields and hill land, and the late clarity of the outer boundary (vegetation)
