meiji-1884-bochi-saimoku

"近代の墓地行政と宗教法人墓地 - 近代日本の宗教 4" (Modern graveyard administration and the graveyards of religious corporations), Chugai Nippo, 2017-02-22, quoting the Home Ministry instruction 内務省乙第40号達 of 1884-11-18 (in Japanese; https://www.chugainippoh.co.jp/article/ron-kikou/ron/20170222-002.html)

What it is: An essay in a religious-affairs newspaper on how the Meiji state came to govern graveyards, which prints an extract of the 1884 Home Ministry instruction setting the standard rules under the Graveyard and Burial Control Regulations, and comments on it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the earliest written Japanese rule on where a new graveyard may be sited - away from great rivers, on high, dry ground, clear of drinking water - so it states in law what a village had to weigh. Its limits are that it is a modernizing state's hygiene rule of 1884, sixteen years after the shogunate fell, governing NEW graveyards, so it is evidence of the concerns rather than of Edo practice; and that the page quotes an extract, marked as abridged.

Used for: where a new graveyard might be sited, relative to rivers, houses and drinking water, and the rule that a village's graveyard was open to all its inhabitants (religion-and-death)
