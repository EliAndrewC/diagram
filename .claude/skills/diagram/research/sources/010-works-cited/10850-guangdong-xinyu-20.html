guangdong-xinyu-20

Qu Dajun (屈大均), Guangdong xinyu (廣東新語, New Sayings of Guangdong), chapter 20, "Bird talk" (禽語), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E5%BB%A3%E6%9D%B1%E6%96%B0%E8%AA%9E/%E5%8D%B720)

What it is: The bird chapter of a late-seventeenth-century description of Guangdong by a scholar of the Pearl River delta; its entry on ducks describes how village ducks were herded in the coastal rice fields and how duck-keeping was organized.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary account of where ducks were kept in the delta in the period the dike-pond hamlet is drawn from, and it puts them in the rice fields rather than at the fish ponds. It describes the coastal fields of Guangzhou, not the dike-pond villages as such, so its silence on ducks at ponds is an absence in one entry, not a statement that none were kept there; and it gives no count of households.

Used for: that the delta's ducks were herded in the coastal rice fields, eating crabs and gleaned rice, under landing-holders answerable for the damage (archetypes)
