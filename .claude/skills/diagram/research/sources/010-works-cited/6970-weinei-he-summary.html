圩内河 canal forms - unsupported by any readable page: a 中国国家地理 feature seen in search: 「The channels take many kinds of form - cross-shaped, T-shaped, crescent-shaped, bow-shaped and so on ... The two ends of a channel run right up to the embankment of the polder field, and sluice gates are built to control water storage and to detain floodwater.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「河道有十字、丁字、月状、弓状等多种形状 ... 河道两端直达圩田堤岸，并建闸控制蓄水和滞洪」) (https://www.dili360.com/cng/article/p5fb1f39c3bbad41.htm)

What it is: A November 2020 feature article by Huang Tao in 中国国家地理 (Chinese National Geography), a popular geography magazine, on the polder fields (圩田) of southern China - farmland made by throwing up a dike around low-lying ground beside a river, a lake or the sea. It describes the constant of the form (a dike at the low place, fields enclosed within, water held without) and the way a polder reads from the air as a chessboard of dikes, channels and roads.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for that plain definition of a polder and for the chessboard look of one from above, which is exactly what a general-audience geography magazine reports well. It is written for readers rather than reviewed by scholars, and it describes polders as they survive today; the more particular claim that a polder's internal channels take crescent and bow shapes and are gated at their ends is not on the page that can be read, so nothing on our maps rests on it.

Used for: the crescent and bow canal forms and the gated ends of the polder's internal channels
