kayabun-kayabuki

Japan Thatching Culture Association (日本茅葺き文化協会), 「What is thatching?」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「茅葺きとは」) (https://www.kayabun.or.jp/kayabuki/)

What it is: The explanatory page of a national association of thatchers and researchers, on the plants used for thatch and the thatch fields (kayaba) they were cut from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that thatch grass - susuki above all, and reed among others - was cut every year from a field kept for it, that the cutting and burning are what held such ground back from turning into forest, and that these fields lay in the hills around a settlement or in its wetlands as common land. It is a practitioners' association writing for the public, and it cites no sources. It speaks of Japan's thatch fields in general rather than of any one marsh. It gives only broad dates: field burning going back to the Jomon period, and thatched farmhouses developing from the Edo period on. It never says in which period the common-land arrangement held; it says only that it has long been the practice.

Used for: that reed and grass for thatch were cut every year from common thatch fields, including wetlands, and that the cutting kept them open (vegetation)
