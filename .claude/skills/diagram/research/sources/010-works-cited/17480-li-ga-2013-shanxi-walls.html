li-ga-2013-shanxi-walls

Li Ga 李嘎, 邊方有警：蒙古掠晉與明代山西的築城高潮 ("Alarm on the frontier: the Mongol raids into Shanxi and the peaks of wall-building in Ming Shanxi", title translated), 明代研究 (Ming Studies) 21 (2013), pp. 31-74 (https://ming.cnhis.ncnu.edu.tw/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/ming21_02Lee_full.pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed article in the Taiwanese journal of the Ming Studies Association counting the 646 wall-building works of Ming Shanxi from the local gazetteers, and showing that the three largest waves followed the Mongol raids of 1449, 1540-1542 and 1567; its tables list each city's works, enemy platforms included.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the evidence that a region's wars drove its cities to add towers and dredge moats, city by city, which is what ties a map's tower density to a city's history. It covers one frontier province of the Ming, its counts are of building works recorded in gazetteers, most of them compiled under the Qing, rather than of towers standing at any one date; it counts only the walled seats of counties and prefectures, never market towns or villages, and a counted work runs from a new suburb wall to the repair of a single gate; and it does not compare the number of towers a city carried with the number of sieges it saw.

Used for: the raids that drove Shanxi's waves of wall-building, the 91% of the raided basin's seats (19 of 21 in central Shanxi) with some wall work recorded in 1540-1543, and Pingyao's 94 added enemy platforms (defenses)
