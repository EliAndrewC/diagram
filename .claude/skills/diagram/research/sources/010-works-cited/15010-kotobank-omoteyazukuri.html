kotobank-omoteyazukuri

Kotobank, 表屋造り ("front-building construction", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the entry from Kodansha's Ie to Interia no Yogo ga Wakaru Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A1%A8%E5%B1%8B%E9%80%A0%E3%82%8A-796071)

What it is: A two-sentence entry in a Kodansha dictionary of house and interior terms, defining the omoteya-zukuri, a townhouse form common in Kyoto with the shop and the dwelling built as separate buildings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names a townhouse form: the shop on the street, the dwelling behind, an inner garden between and a narrow entrance wing joining them. The entry does not say who built this way; that it was a wealthier merchant's house is this project's reading. It is a dictionary definition, not a study: it gives no date, no size and no share of houses built this way, and it speaks of Kyoto, not of small provincial towns. It describes the form from the houses that survive, and most of those were rebuilt after Kyoto's 1864 fire and date from Meiji or later (one named example's shop wing is of about 1869). How widely the form was built before Meiji is not shown here.

Used for: the merchant house built as shop in front, garden between and dwelling behind (buildings)
