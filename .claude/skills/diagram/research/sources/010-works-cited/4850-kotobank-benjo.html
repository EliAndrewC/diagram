kotobank-benjo

日本大百科全書(ニッポニカ) 「privy (benjo), the toilet」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「便所」), via kotobank.jp/word/便所-131200 (https://kotobank.jp/word/便所-131200)

What it is: The entry for 便所 (benjo, the privy) from Shogakukan's encyclopedia Nipponica, reprinted on the reference site kotobank. It describes how privies were arranged in different kinds of Japanese household - paired and attached to the main buildings in samurai and court-noble residences, gathered into one small separate building for a compound's rowhouses, set behind the guest parlor in townhouses and farmhouses, and provided in farm households as a single building standing clear of the main house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It answers the placement question a map has to answer - whether the privy is inside the house or a separate outbuilding - and it says the detached farm privy holding both the urinal and the privy was the usual arrangement, which is what we draw. Being an encyclopedia entry it is a tertiary summary and it describes a custom rather than counting it: "usual" is not a percentage, so how often our farmsteads show one is our own choice resting on the form the entry gives.

Used for: the farm privy as ONE independent outbuilding holding the urinal and the privy, "普通".
