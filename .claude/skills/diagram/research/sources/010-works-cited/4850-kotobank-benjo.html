kotobank-benjo

日本大百科全書(ニッポニカ) 「privy (benjo), the toilet」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「便所」), via kotobank.jp/word/便所-131200 (https://kotobank.jp/word/便所-131200)

What it is: The entry for 便所 (benjo, the privy) from Shogakukan's encyclopedia Nipponica, reprinted on the reference site kotobank. Its history section is signed by the architectural historian Hirai Kiyoshi and describes the Edo period from residence plans that survive and from surviving townhouses and farmhouses. It describes how privies were arranged in different kinds of Japanese household - paired and attached to the main buildings in samurai and court-noble residences, gathered into one small separate building for a compound's rowhouses, set behind the guest parlor in those townhouses and farmhouses that had one, and provided in farm households as a single building standing clear of the main house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It answers the placement question a map has to answer - whether the privy is inside the house or a separate outbuilding - and it says the detached farm privy holding both the urinal and the privy was the usual arrangement, which is what we draw. Being an encyclopedia entry it is a tertiary summary and it describes a custom rather than counting it: "usual" is not a percentage, so how often our farmsteads show one is our own choice resting on the form the entry gives. It describes Japan only. A different entry on the same kotobank page (Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia) says Chinese households often kept the privy over the pig pen, and that in north China many households had none and used chamber pots. So on a farmstead modeled on China, the separate privy is the Japanese form standing in.

Used for: the farm privy as ONE independent outbuilding holding the urinal and the privy, "普通"; the privy at the rear of the guest parlor (buildings).
