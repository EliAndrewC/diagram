huc-journey-chinese-empire

Evariste Regis Huc, A Journey through the Chinese Empire, vol. II (New York: Harper, 1855), pp. 314-315 (https://archive.org/stream/journeythroughch02huceiala/journeythroughch02huceiala_djvu.txt)

What it is: The English translation of a French missionary's account of his years in Qing China in the 1840s; the pages used describe the beggars of Peking and the "House of the Hens' Feathers", a paid night shelter of one great hall.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a premodern eyewitness description of a Chinese mass dormitory for the poor, one hall and one floor of bedding for all comers at a coin a night. It describes a charity-like shelter in the capital, not a market town's lodging for peasants, gives no count of sleepers or size of the hall, and Huc wrote for effect; the publication year is on the Internet Archive's catalog record, not on the text page.

Used for: that a premodern Chinese cheap lodging could be one great hall bedding all comers together (towns)
