Douban Books, a reader's annotation (by the user 润仁儿～, 2022) of chapter 5, 广州府祠堂形制的渊源和流变 ("The origins and changes of the forms of the ancestral halls of Guangzhou prefecture", title translated from the Chinese by this project), of Feng Jiang, 『祖先之翼』 ("The Ancestors' Wings", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://m.douban.com/book/annotation/119571892)

What it is: A reader's transcribed excerpts, with page numbers, from the chapter of an architectural historian's book (Feng Jiang, South China University of Technology; China Architecture & Building Press, 2010) on the Ming and Qing ancestral halls of Guangzhou prefecture, drawn from the surviving halls and period documents, that classifies them by width in bays, depth in halls and number of parallel lines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the common size of a village hall - three bays wide, one line three halls deep, the form most halls of standing took, while the branch halls of small or less wealthy lineages were mostly two halls deep - the rarity of five-bay halls, and one measured depth, 8.5-9 m, for the thirteen-purlin rear halls of several halls in one village (Langtou, Huadu), which is what the hall's drawn footprint is built from. Its limits: a reader's transcription on a social site, not the book itself, so a word may be mistyped; it covers one prefecture's surviving halls; and it gives no whole footprint in meters.

Used for: the common width and depth of a Cantonese village ancestral hall, and one rear hall's depth (homesteads)
