T/JSSLKX 002-2021, 小型農田水利工程規劃設計導則 ("Guideline for the planning and design of small farmland water-conservancy works"), Jiangsu Provincial Water Conservancy Survey and Design Association, draft for comment, 2021 (http://jsszy.org.cn/UserFiles/file/20221103/20221103155002_6437.pdf)

What it is: A provincial design guideline for the small canals and ditches of a farmland irrigation scheme, issued for comment by the Jiangsu Provincial Water Conservancy Survey and Design Association in 2021, and referred by its own clause to the national standard GB 50288. It sets out where each grade of canal is laid, how the field canals relate to the plots and the roads, and the minimum dimensions a built channel must meet.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states as design law the canal doctrine our comb layout rests on, which no readable page carried before: every grade of canal sited on the higher ground within its own command area, mains and branches along the contour lines or the watershed divides, the laterals below them crossing the contours, two or three grades of fixed canal in the field with successive grades at right angles on flat ground, and the watering direction following the field surface's own fall for irrigated dry land as much as for paddy. It also gives the alignment maxim behind our swept bends - let the large bends take the lie of the land and straighten the small ones, which the guideline shows to be design law in Jiangsu today and cannot date any further back than that - and the two figures we use as floors, a 0.3 m minimum canal bottom width stated for the reason we give it, so the thing can be operated and maintained after it is built, and a 1.0 m minimum bank crest on a lateral or farm canal. Its limits are large, and every number taken from it carries them. It is a modern standard, published in 2021, for concrete-lined and machine-worked consolidated farmland, so it is evidence for the geometry and for the reasons its own clauses state, never a measurement of a premodern earthwork; its 200 m block length and its 1/1000 to 1/2000 grades are machinery-era figures we do not carry across; its 1.0 m is the width of the bank CREST, the earth walked on beside the channel, and reads as a channel width only to a careless eye; it fixes no side-slope coefficient and no canal width by grade at all, which is why the widths our record derives from it stay derivations; that nothing read establishes a 0.3 m floor for a HAND-dug field ditch, so that figure may inflate a drawn one rather than bracket it; and it is a draft for comment rather than a ratified national standard. Its own clause 9.1.6 puts its canal designs under GB 50288, the national irrigation and drainage standard this project has not been able to read on any page, which is what makes this guideline the nearest openly readable carrier of those numbers and also the reason an attribution taken through it is second-hand.
