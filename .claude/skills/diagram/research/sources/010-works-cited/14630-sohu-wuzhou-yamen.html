sohu-wuzhou-yamen

Sohu (岭南文化看梧州), 观历史，走进明清衙署建筑文化 ("Looking at history: the architecture of the Ming and Qing yamen", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://www.sohu.com/a/241036935_741671)

What it is: A 2018 post by a Wuzhou (Guangxi) local-culture account on Ming and Qing yamen architecture, that opens on the lost Wuzhou prefectural yamen (the prefecture's seat from 1368 to 1913) and then describes a generic yamen by function, from the screen wall across the street to the rear garden and the shrines, pictured from the surviving Baoding governor-general's office and Nanyang county yamen. No author is named; the post is credited to the account's construction-investment and planning departments.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets out, part by part, where things stood in a yamen of any rank - the six rooms two bays each flanking the path to the great hall, the screen wall facing the gate across the street - and says the ranks differed in area but shared the layout. It is an unsourced popular post, not scholarship and not a survey of one site. It names the Ming Zhusi zhizhang and Ming huidian, but its shrine list comes from the Qing huidian, and most of that list is the city's altars; the shrines inside the yamen are a shorter list. It hedges the two bays per room as "generally" and says some yamen added more rooms. It also contradicts itself on the west side's order: one sentence gives north to south as war, punishments, works, and the next gives south to north as works, war, punishments. The west-side order has to be confirmed from a closer source.

Used for: the six rooms' size and order, the screen wall across the street, the yamen's shrines (cities/government)
