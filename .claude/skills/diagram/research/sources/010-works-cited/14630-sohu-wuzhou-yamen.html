sohu-wuzhou-yamen

Sohu (岭南文化看梧州), 观历史，走进明清衙署建筑文化 ("Looking at history: the architecture of the Ming and Qing yamen", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://www.sohu.com/a/241036935_741671)

What it is: A 2018 post by a Wuzhou (Guangxi) local-culture account on Ming and Qing yamen architecture, written around the lost Wuzhou prefectural yamen: the parts of a yamen by function, from the screen wall across the street to the rear garden and the shrines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets out, part by part, where things stood in a yamen of any rank - the six rooms two bays each flanking the path to the great hall, the screen wall facing the gate across the street - and says the ranks differed in area but shared the layout. It is a popular local-history post drawing on the Ming statutes, not a survey of one site.

Used for: the six rooms' size and order, the screen wall across the street, the yamen's shrines (cities/government)
