ja.wikipedia 小区画水田 ("small-plot paddy fields", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B0%8F%E5%8C%BA%E7%94%BB%E6%B0%B4%E7%94%B0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the tiny paddy plots of the Yayoi and Kofun periods known from excavation: large bunds a meter or more wide enclosing blocks divided by small bunds into plots of under a hundred square meters, the smallest about five.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one dimension of a hand-built dividing bund read from excavated ground - half a meter or less, with no core, rebuilt each season - a floor for the ridge between two plots. That small bund split one block inside a large bund to spread water evenly over it; the article does not say it separated one holder's land from another's, and the bunds between parcels in later, larger fields (the jōri grid, the Edo village) were permanent and could be wider, so it bounds the ridge from below and is not the width of a boundary between holdings. It describes paddies of two thousand years ago, not of the setting's period, and the article gives the whole sentence - the width, the missing core, the rebuilding each season - as "thought to have been"; it is an encyclopedia summary of archaeological reports.

Used for: the small dividing bund of the earliest paddies, half a meter wide or less (archetypes)
