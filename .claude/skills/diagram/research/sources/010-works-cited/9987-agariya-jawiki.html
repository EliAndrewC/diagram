agariya-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 揚屋 (牢獄) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%8F%9A%E5%B1%8B_(%E7%89%A2%E7%8D%84))

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the agariya, the shared cells of Edo's Tenmacho jail (and Nagasaki's) that held women of any status, those sentenced to exile, lower-ranking and rear-vassal samurai, middling priests, the sick and returned castaways (higher ranks went to the separate agarizashiki), with the number and size of their rooms and a men's cell of the kind added at Osaka's jail in 1739; last revised 2022.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that sizes individual cell rooms, in mats, which is what a holding cell's size is calibrated against. Its limits are that it is a short tertiary article whose size figures carry no footnote (its references are three Japanese historical dictionaries), that each room held several prisoners together rather than one, that its cells are the great city jails', not the temporary cell of a country office, and that it gives each room's floor in mats and one side, not both sides.

Used for: the sizes of the Tenmacho and Osaka agariya cells (buildings)
