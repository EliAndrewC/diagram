shirasu-imidas

Yamamoto Hirofumi, 白洲（しらす）, in 時代劇用語指南 (A guide to period-drama terms), imidas (Shueisha), 2010-04-01 (in Japanese; https://imidas.jp/jidaigeki/detail/L-57-223-08-04-G252.html)

What it is: A short entry by a historian of the Edo period, in a glossary written to explain the terms of period dramas, on the town magistracy's court: its gravel, who sat on mats and who on the veranda, the examinations, and the courts of other offices.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that intendants' offices and barriers had courts too, which is what carries the court from the capital's magistracy to a county office, and it puts commoners on mats. Its limits are that it is a popular glossary without references, that it describes the court as a garden facing the hall without saying whether it was roofed, and that it seats an accused samurai or monk on the board veranda where two other sources put samurai on the upper, tatami one.

Used for: commoners on mats and an accused samurai on the board veranda; courts at intendants' offices (buildings)
