chikuma-obasute

Chikuma city, 姨捨の棚田とは? ("What are the Obasute terraces?", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.city.chikuma.lg.jp/soshiki/norin/kanko/3/1659.html)

What it is: The city of Chikuma's (Nagano prefecture) page on the Obasute terraced paddies, a designated scenic place: the slope they lie on, their altitude, and how many paddies lie in how many hectares.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a slope and a paddy count over a stated area for one famous terrace landscape, which is what an average paddy size is worked out from. The figures are rounded tourism-page summaries ("about"), with no survey method given, and they count the terraces as they are now, part kept by volunteer preservation groups and a paddy-owner rental scheme and the rest farmed by local farmers. The page gives two areas that disagree: about 1,800 paddies in 75 ha overall, and about 1,500 in some 40 ha of the protected area. Both are areas the paddies lie in, not paddy surface: the 75 ha takes in more walls, banks and other ground than the denser 40 ha, so an average worked out from either is gross - about 420 square meters from the first, about 270 from the second - and even the smaller figure includes the banks. It is one steep mountain landscape (a 1/7 slope), dated to a 1578 mention and developed from the Edo period on, and it gives no wall height and no date for the paddies' present size.

Used for: a terrace's slope and the number of paddies to its area (Field archetypes)
