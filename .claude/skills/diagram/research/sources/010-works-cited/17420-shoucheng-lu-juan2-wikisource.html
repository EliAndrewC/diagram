Chen Gui 陳規, 守城錄 卷2 守城機要 ("Record of Defending a City, book 2: the essentials of defending a city", title translated from the classical Chinese by this project), on zh.wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/守城錄/卷2)

What it is: The second book of the Shoucheng Lu, a Southern Song treatise on the defense of walled cities by Chen Gui, who held De'an against siege in the 1120s and 1130s; this book criticizes the old rules for gates, barbicans, mamian and parapets and gives his own. The classical text as transcribed on Wikisource.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the old rule for the projecting towers, one every sixty paces standing out at least two zhang, in a primary text by a man who defended a city. It is twelfth-century, it records the rule in order to argue against much of the old practice, and it gives paces without saying how long a pace was.

Used for: the old rule of a mamian every sixty paces (defenses)
