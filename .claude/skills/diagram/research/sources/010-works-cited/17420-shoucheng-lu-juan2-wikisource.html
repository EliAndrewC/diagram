Chen Gui 陳規, 守城錄 卷2 守城機要 ("Record of Defending a City, book 2: the essentials of defending a city", title translated), on zh.wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/守城錄/卷2)

What it is: The second book of the Shoucheng Lu, a Southern Song treatise on the defense of walled cities by Chen Gui, who held De'an against siege in the 1120s and 1130s; this book criticizes the old rules for gates, barbicans, mamian and parapets and gives his own. The classical text as transcribed on Wikisource.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the old rule for the projecting towers, one every sixty paces standing out at least two zhang, in a primary text by a man who defended a city. It is a twelfth-century Song rule, not a Ming, Qing or Japanese one, and it records the old rule without saying whose or how old. Chen Gui argues against the wooden towers built on the mamian, which heavy trebuchet stones knock down, but not against the sixty-pace spacing, which he lets stand. He says each tower's width varied with the ground, and he gives paces without saying how long a pace was. He writes for the walled prefectural and county cities (州郡城池), so the spacing belongs to a city's wall and is not a rule for a town's or a village's lighter wall. The Wikisource transcription names no base edition.

Used for: the old rule of a mamian every sixty paces (defenses)
