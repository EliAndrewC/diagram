zhouli-zhushu-suiren

《周禮註疏》 卷十五 ("The Rites of Zhou with commentary and sub-commentary, chapter 15", title translated from the Chinese by this project), the note of Zheng Xuan (Han) and the sub-commentary of Jia Gongyan (Tang) on the Suiren passage of the Ministry of Education chapter, Chinese Wikisource transcription (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%91%A8%E7%A6%AE%E6%B3%A8%E7%96%8F/%E5%8D%B7%E5%8D%81%E4%BA%94)

What it is: The standard annotated edition of the Rites of Zhou, one of the Thirteen Classics with Commentaries: the classical text, the note of the Han scholar Zheng Xuan (2nd century) and the sub-commentary of the Tang scholar Jia Gongyan (7th century). This chapter carries their reading of the Suiren passage, the ladder of field channels and paths, including how each grade of channel lies against the next.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the rule of each channel grade crossing the one it feeds was a stated idea of Chinese field layout from the Han at the latest: the note says the lesser channel runs lengthwise and the next crosswise, and the sub-commentary draws it out allotment by allotment. Its limits are those of the classic it explains: it reads an idealized Zhou scheme for the farmland of the royal domain's outer districts, drawn for a block of ten thousand allotments rather than a village, and says nothing of how far any real countryside followed it. The note says every grade of channel leads water off to the river, and the passage says nothing of where the water comes from or of flooding a field (elsewhere in the chapter the sub-commentary puts millet on high fields and rice and wheat on low ones). The compass directions are the note's own choice: it pictures the fields as the Book of Songs' "southern acres", and the classic itself names no direction. The sub-commentary itself says this channel system differs from the well-field layout and borrows only its widths and depths. The page's transcription has at least one slip (萬大 for 萬夫), and no base edition is stated.

Used for: each grade of field channel laid crosswise to the one it feeds - a crosswise channel at the head of every ten allotments, a north-south one on the east side of every hundred, a crosswise one on the south side of every thousand (fields)
