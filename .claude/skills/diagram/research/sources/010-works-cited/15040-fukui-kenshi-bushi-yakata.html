fukui-kenshi-bushi-yakata

Fukui Prefecture, Fukui Kenshi (history of Fukui Prefecture), general history vol. 2, the Middle Ages, ch. 5 sec. 3, "Warriors' residences and the medieval town" (in Japanese; https://www.library-archives.pref.fukui.lg.jp/fukui/07/kenshi/T2/T2-5-01-03-02-02.htm)

What it is: A section of the prefecture's official history, put online by the prefectural archives, on the residences of medieval warriors in Echizen and Wakasa and the towns that grew from them, drawing on surviving deeds.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the usual size of a medieval warrior's moated residence, the fields its servants worked before its gate, and two deeds transferring a residence with its house site, fields and paddy - what a samurai house among its own fields held. It is a scholarly regional history, but its size is a general statement ("about one chō", some 109 m, square), not an excavated or surveyed measurement, and its two deeds are Kamakura-period (1220 and 1310). The larger deed is a powerful vassal's (gokenin), whose residence held several buildings, so it shows the top of the local warrior class, not an ordinary samurai's house. The page itself says Warring States lords were already working to gather their retainers into castle towns, so none of this describes an Edo-period retainer's house. It says the local documents are few.

Used for: the size of a medieval warrior's residence, its fields at the gate, and a deed's house site and paddy (buildings)
