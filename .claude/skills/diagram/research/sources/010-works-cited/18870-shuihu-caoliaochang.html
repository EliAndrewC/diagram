施耐庵 水滸傳 (attributed to Shi Nai'an, Water Margin), 第十回 林教頭風雪山神廟 陸虞候火燒草料場 ("Chapter 10: Arms Instructor Lin at the Mountain Spirit Temple in the snow; Captain Lu burns the fodder depot", title translated from the Chinese by this project), on Guoxue Daohang (http://www.guoxue123.com/xiaosuo/jd/shqs/10.htm)

What it is: The tenth chapter of the Ming-dynasty novel Water Margin, in a modern simplified-character text on a Chinese classics site, in which the exiled Lin Chong is set to keep an army fodder depot outside a garrison town and its keeper hands it over, counting the stacks.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest premodern description readable here of how a fodder store looked on the ground: a yard inside an earth wall with a two-leaved gate, thatched buildings used as storehouses, the horse fodder in stacks all round, and two thatched halls in the middle. It is fiction, written in the Ming about a Northern Song setting, so it shows what a Ming writer and reader took a fodder depot to be rather than any one depot; it is a state army store, not a town's or a farmer's; and it gives no sizes and does not say what the storehouses held.

Used for: hay kept in stacks in a walled yard with thatched storehouses (towns)
