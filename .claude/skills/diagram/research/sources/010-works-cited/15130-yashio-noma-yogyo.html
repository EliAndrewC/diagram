yashio-noma-yogyo

Yashio City, Rekinavi - Yashio rekishi jiten, "農間余業" ("Work in the intervals of farming", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; http://yashio-rekinavi.com/reki-navi/index.php?title=%E8%BE%B2%E9%96%93%E4%BD%99%E6%A5%AD)

What it is: An entry in the Yashio city history encyclopedia, a wiki of the city's local history, summarizing the late-Edo surveys of the trades kept by the households of its nineteen villages: a table of 1829 giving each village's inhabitants, households, full-time farmers, traders and craftsmen, taverns, hairdressers, bathhouses and pawnshops, and lists of 1794 to 1836 naming each trading household's goods.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the city history museum's tabulation of the shogunate's own surveys of the trades in these villages: the survey of 1829 was taken under the Bunsei reform of the Kanto countryside, and the lists of 1794 to 1836 surveyed the trading households within the shogun's falconry grounds. Each is named with its document and its printed edition, so its counts are near-primary figures for one district of villages near Edo, with Hachijo, a village of 856 on a road with a ferry crossing, as its largest place. Its limits are that it is one district of the Kanto plain in the last decades of the Edo period, close to the great city and richer in trade than a remote one; that its places are villages, the largest smaller than a county seat; that the page is a local-history summary, not the survey itself; and that the surveys were taken to restrain farmers' side-trades, so a household had reason to under-declare one, while the list of 1836 still counts trades that had lapsed or whose keeper had died, and the list of 1794 mixes in entries from later surveys.

Used for: how many shops, taverns, pawnshops and bathhouses a large village and its district kept, that the trades were kept by farming households, a brewer and an oil presser in that village (urban-features)
