kotobank-ryorijaya

kotobank 料理茶屋 ("restaurant teahouse", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%96%99%E7%90%86%E8%8C%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting two short dictionary definitions (Digital Daijisen, Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten) and the Heibonsha Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry, signed by Suzuki Shin'ichi, on the ryorijaya, the Edo-period restaurant: eating shops from the Nara-chameshi house of Asakusa after 1657, true restaurants from the Meiwa-An'ei years (1764-81), and the famous restaurants of Edo, Kyoto and Osaka.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names where Kyoto's restaurants stood - before the gate of the Gion shrine and along the Takase canal - an example of eating houses at a shrine gate and, many of them, along a canal. At the gate it names one pair of houses, the Niken-chaya ("two teahouses"), not a cluster; and it lists famous houses, drawing on Bakin's Kiryo manroku (1802), not a count or map of where eating houses stood. A signed encyclopedia entry, tertiary, on the great cities' fine restaurants of the late Edo period (after 1764), not a small town's eating house or an earlier era's.

Used for: Kyoto's restaurants at the Gion shrine's gate and along the Takase canal (urban-features)
