zakkoku-kotobank

kotobank 雑穀 (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%9B%91%E7%A9%80-68803)

What it is: The kotobank page gathering several Japanese dictionaries' entries on zakkoku, coarse grain; the entry cited is the Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten (revised edition), a short dictionary of Japanese history.

Why it applies, and its limits: The history dictionary says coarse grain was historically outside the land tax and was grown, mostly on dry and slash-and-burn fields, as the farmers' own food and as a famine crop, especially in mountain areas, and that its share grows the further back one goes; that is the reason a village's rice need not feed it alone. It is a short tertiary entry with no quantity, so it supports the practice and not a figure. It is about Japan, where "outside the land tax" means coarse grain was not what the tax was paid in (dry fields were still assessed); in north China millet was itself tax grain, so only the diet point carries to China-modeled places. And it weighs the point toward mountain villages: a lowland rice village leaned on coarse grain less.

Used for: that coarse grain filled part of the diet, behind the acreage-from-population rule (fields)
