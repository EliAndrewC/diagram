kotobank-souen

Kotobank, "Souen" (桑園, mulberry field), with the entry by Hazama Kazuo in Heibonsha's Kaitei shinpan Sekai daihyakka jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%A1%91%E5%9C%92-89135)

What it is: A dictionary page gathering several Japanese encyclopedias' entries on the mulberry field grown to feed silkworms; the Heibonsha entry gives how many bushes a field of each training form is planted with.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives planting numbers for root-cut and mid-cut leaf mulberry, the low forms a dike's mulberry takes, so it brackets how thickly such bushes stand. It describes twentieth-century Japanese sericulture (its area figure is for 1995), whose standardized training forms, bred varieties and fertilizer schedules set these planting densities, in dedicated fields in Japan rather than on the pond dikes of the Chinese deltas; it is an encyclopedia summary citing no survey, and it gives no crown width, so it is a modern comparison, not a pre-modern measurement.

Used for: how many root-cut and mid-cut mulberry bushes a Japanese mulberry field carried (archetypes)
