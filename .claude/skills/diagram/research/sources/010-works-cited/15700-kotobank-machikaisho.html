kotobank-machikaisho

Kotobank, 町会所 ("machi-kaisho, the ward office", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the entries of the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten and others (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/町会所)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering Japanese reference works' entries on the machi-kaisho, the office of the townspeople's own officials in an early-modern town.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page that sorts the kinds of town: in self-governing commercial cities (Kyoto, Osaka, Sakai) every ward had one; in a castle town an office in every ward (as at Utsunomiya) was exceptional and some kept none at all, their officials working in the town magistrate's office - the entry does not say how common a single office for the whole town was; a post town with one was unusual. The claims come from Fukai Jinzō's signed entry in the Heibonsha encyclopedia, a specialist's summary rather than period records, and it describes Japanese towns only; for towns on the Chinese model it is an analogy. It says what the office did - administration, civil justice, policing, sometimes with a white-sand court and a small jail. It gives no plan or size.

Used for: which towns kept a townspeople's office, and what it did (towns)
