Petr Bobák, Ladislav Čmolík and Martin Čadík, "From Top-Right to User-Right: Perceptual Prioritization of Point-Feature Label Positions", arXiv:2407.11996v2, 2024 (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11996v2)

What it is: A study in visualization research, read as its 2024 arXiv preprint (submitted for peer review when read), that surveys the ranked label positions around a point published by cartographers from Yoeli (1972) and Imhof (1962, 1975) to the mapping textbooks and software, tabulating each one's order, and then tests with nearly 800 online participants from 48 countries which positions readers actually prefer, proposing a new order from the results.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable source found that lays the published orders side by side, including those in books and papers the project could not read (Imhof, Zoraster, Slocum and others), and the one that tests an order on readers rather than stating it. Its tables are its authors' reading of those works, not the works themselves; its participants judged modern small-scale maps (map zoom levels 5 to 10) of settlement dots with Latin, left-to-right labels on an otherwise blank "blind" map, not large-scale plans of drawn objects. So its order, which the maps follow, is evidence about how modern readers read a label beside a point, which is what a plan drawn in a modern cartographic style asks of them, rather than about building plans or the premodern setting. It ranks the positions only when all are free: it says nothing about which to take when a building or path blocks the preferred one, and its authors note that other things on a map, and scripts not read left to right, may change the preference.

Used for: the order the maps follow around a small thing (its PerceptPPO), the readers' preference for a name directly above its point, why the "slightly" places are left out, and the other published orders (Map conventions)
