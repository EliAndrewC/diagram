Petr Bobák, Ladislav Čmolík and Martin Čadík, "From Top-Right to User-Right: Perceptual Prioritization of Point-Feature Label Positions", arXiv:2407.11996v2, 2024 (https://arxiv.org/html/2407.11996v2)

What it is: A study in visualization research, read as its 2024 arXiv preprint (submitted for peer review when read), that surveys the ranked label positions around a point published by cartographers from Yoeli (1972) and Imhof (1962, 1975) to the mapping textbooks and software, tabulating each one's order, and then tests with nearly 800 online participants from 48 countries which positions readers actually prefer, proposing a new order from the results.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable source found that lays the published orders side by side, including those in books and papers the project could not read (Imhof, Zoraster, Slocum and others), and the one that tests an order on readers rather than stating it. Its tables are its authors' reading of those works, not the works themselves; its participants judged modern maps of point features such as places and points of interest, not plans of drawn objects, so its preference for a name directly above its point is evidence about readers in general rather than about building plans.

Used for: the other published orders, and the user-tested preference for a name directly above its point (presentation)
