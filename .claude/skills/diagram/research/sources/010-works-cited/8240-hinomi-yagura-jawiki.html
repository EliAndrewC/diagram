hinomi-yagura-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 火の見櫓 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%81%AB%E3%81%AE%E8%A6%8B%E6%AB%93)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the 火の見櫓 (hinomi-yagura), the fire-watch tower of Japanese towns and villages: a raised lookout platform used to spot a fire early, summon the fire brigade and sound the alarm to the neighborhood, generally carrying a hansho bell at its top. It covers how the towers developed alongside the firefighting system from Edo-period Edo, how far across the country they had spread by the early Showa era, and the town-by-town code by which the bell was rung.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the plain definition of the structure our city maps draw and of what the watch on it was for, and it is the only source in the record that dates the institution and its spread, which is what settles whether a small unwalled seat would carry one. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary rather than a study; that it describes Japan alone; that much of the article is about the surviving steel towers and the modern volunteer companies, so the premodern form has to be taken from its historical passages and not from what it shows standing today; and that its two datings do different work and must not be run together - it puts the ORIGIN of the tower in Edo-period Edo, the shogunal capital, but the SPREAD of the form across the country in the early Showa era, which is the twentieth century, so the fact that such towers became common everywhere cannot be used to put one in a small premodern settlement.

Used for: what a fire-watch tower was for, the watch house beneath it with its permanently stationed watchman, and the dating of the institution and of its spread across the country
