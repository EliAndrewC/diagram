kyoto-city-jobosei

Kyoto City, "都市史03 条坊制" ("Urban history 03: the jobo grid", title translated) (https://www2.city.kyoto.lg.jp/somu/rekishi/fm/nenpyou/htmlsheet/toshi03.html)

What it is: A page in Kyoto City's series on the city's history, explaining the jobo system, the grid of avenues and lanes on which Heian-kyo was laid out in 794: the large wards bounded by avenues, their division into sixteen blocks by lanes, and the fixed size of a block.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a planned grid's block size and lane width from a public-education page in the city's own history series, a summary that names no source for its figures, which is what a drawn block is measured against and a street share can be checked against by arithmetic. It describes an eighth-century imperial capital laid out on a Chinese model, much larger and more regular than a provincial city, and the widths are the plan's round figures rather than excavated measurements.

Used for: the Heian block fixed at 40 jo (about 120 m) a side exclusive of the roads, and the lanes about 12 m wide between blocks (cities/fabric)
