ja.wikipedia 御旅所 ("otabisho, the resting place of a shrine's procession", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/御旅所)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia stub on the otabisho, the place where a shrine's portable shrine rests or stays the night during its festival procession, or which the procession sets out to reach.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the ground a Japanese shrine festival keeps outside the shrine - one or several stops along the procession's route, or the place it sets out to reach - and says what that ground may be: an existing shrine building (the shrine's original seat, a subsidiary shrine, the shrine of the god's consort), a temporary altar set up on the god's old site, or the house of the parishioners' head (tōnin). It is a short encyclopedia article with no references, flagged as needing sources since 2017. It supports the outline of the practice, not any figure, and gives no dates and no size. It describes a Japanese practice, which a town modeled on imperial China borrows only as an analog. Signed dictionary entries on Kotobank (日本大百科全書, 世界大百科事典) date the practice to the Heian period and give its distances from the shrine; this article does neither.

Used for: what a festival keeps on the ground in a town (towns)
