Hong Kong Herbarium (AFCD), "The Fung Shui Story of Lai Chi Wo" (https://www.herbarium.gov.hk/en/special-topics/fung-shui-woods/the-fung-shui-story-of-lai-chi-wo/index.html)

What it is: A short page in the Hong Kong Herbarium's series on fung shui woods, telling how the village of Lai Chi Wo came to protect its wood: marked boundaries, a ban on destroying it, a fine and public denunciation for damage, and firewood gathering allowed on one or two days a year.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one account read of a southern Chinese village's own rules for its fengshui wood, from the region whose groves the record measures. It tells the village's story in a paragraph, dates the custom only to after a fengshui wall built "more than a hundred years ago", names no stele, covenant or document, and says neither what the fine was nor who levied it.

Used for: Lai Chi Wo's fine and public denunciation for harming its wood, and its firewood days (The countryside)
