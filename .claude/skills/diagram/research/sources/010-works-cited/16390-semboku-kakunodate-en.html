Semboku City, "Important Preservation District for Groups of Traditional Buildings: Kakunodate's Historic Samurai District" (in English; https://www.city.semboku.akita.jp/en/sightseeing/spot/07_buke.html)

What it is: The city's English page on Kakunodate's samurai district and each of its open houses, with the families' ranks and stipends, their fences and gates, and the sizes of some houses and grounds.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets the upper-ranked samurai streets' black wooden fences, once taller than a person, against a low-ranked family's brushwood fence with no formal gate in its own attendants' quarter, and gives one family's stipend and the extent of its ground. It is a visitor page about one small castle town, whose lots were more spacious than a large city's, and some of the ground it measures is today's preserved estate. That family, the Aoyagi, came as middle-ranked samurai on 45 to 60 koku but took 200 koku by the end of the Edo period, and the page calls them upper-ranked from their house; their ground of just under a hectare is that later, richer family's estate, not a mid-rank lot.

Used for: the enclosure by rank, the front garden, and a small town's lot (cities/government)
