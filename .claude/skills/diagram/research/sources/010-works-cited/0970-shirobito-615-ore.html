城びと (Shirobito), 第46回【構造】お城の塀や石垣はどうして折れ曲がっているの？ (https://shirobito.jp/article/615)

What it is: An explainer of 2018-12-10 by the Kamiyu History Editorial Department in the castle web magazine Shirobito's structure series on why Japanese castle walls and stone revetments are built with folds and projections: to bring flanking fire onto an attacker and to leave no blind angle.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the flanking logic behind the angular Japanese wall, which is why our castle's stone revetments are drawn in straight runs with hard corners rather than curves. Its limit is that it is a popular explainer rather than a survey of surviving plans.

Used for: folds and projections in a castle wall for flanking fire
