Wei Fucheng (魏甫丞), 台灣古厝門前為什麼常挖一座“半月形水池”？ ("Why do old Taiwanese houses so often dig a half-moon pond before the door?", title translated from the Chinese by this project), The Storm Media (風傳媒), 2026-09-18 (in Chinese; https://www.storm.mg/lifestyle/11165537)

What it is: A magazine article for a general audience on the half-moon pond before Taiwan's old farmhouses and Hakka compounds, gathering what the Taiwan folk-artifact dictionary (臺灣民俗文物辭典), the Hakka Cultural Development Center, a city museum (Taoyuan's Daxi Wood Art Ecomuseum) and the National Cultural Memory Bank say of its names, uses, water supply and shape.

Why it applies, and its limits: It puts the pond's everyday uses - washing, laundry, fish, water against fire, irrigation - and its other names in one readable place, each attributed to the work it came from, and it says plainly that the origin of the half shape lacks the sources to settle it. Its limits: it is journalism relaying other works rather than a study; its examples are Taiwan's, mostly of the Qing and later, so it speaks for south-Chinese settler practice and not for the mainland or an older period; its uses are the pond's possible uses, not a count of how often each was made; some of what it relays is twentieth-century living memory, not premodern practice (the Pingtung Guangyu hall's fish include tilapia, brought to Taiwan in 1946), and its illustration is AI-generated; and the works it relays are themselves public (the folk-artifact dictionary at dict.th.gov.tw, the Hakka center's 半月池 page, the National Cultural Memory Bank's entry), so a claim that matters is better taken from them.

Used for: the half-moon pond's names, its everyday uses and the unsettled origin of its shape (homesteads)
