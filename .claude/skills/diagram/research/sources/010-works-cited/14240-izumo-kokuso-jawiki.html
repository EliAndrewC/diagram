izumo-kokuso-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 出雲国造 ("Izumo no kuni no miyatsuko", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出雲国造)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the hereditary office of the Izumo kokuso, the chief priesthood of the Izumo shrine, held in the Senge and Kitajima lines, with the list of its holders by generation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It numbers the holders of one hereditary shrine priesthood down to the present holder (84 in the Senge line, 79 in the Kitajima line, both counted through the single line before the two houses split in the 1340s), which is the longest such an office could run, not what a common one did. It is a tertiary summary, and its list follows the Senge house's own genealogy, the Izumo kokuso dento ryaku, which the page names as the source of the list. This is the priesthood of a shrine of the first rank, one of the very few houses allowed to keep the ancient title of provincial governor, so it does not show that a village or town shrine's priestly family reached such a count. The count passed eighty only after the Edo period: the page gives the 80th Senge holder's birth as 1845. The numbers count holders, not father-to-son generations: brothers succeeded brothers (the 55th after the 54th, the 81st after the 80th), the page's genealogy marks adoptions, and the page judges six of the early generations to have really been three. The early generations are traditional rather than historical, the first holders being gods, and the page numbers holders inconsistently: it calls the present holder's father both the 83rd and the 84th, Takatoki both the 53rd and the 54th, and Hatayasu both the 23rd and the 24th.

Used for: a shrine priesthood counted past eighty generations, handed down within one house, the present holder succeeding his father (religion-and-death)
