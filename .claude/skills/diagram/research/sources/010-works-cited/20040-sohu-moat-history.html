古人今事 (a Sohu self-publishing account; no author named), 作为城池防御的第一道防线：护城河，是怎样在古代中国大放异彩的 ("The moat, the first line of a walled city's defense: how it shone in ancient China", translated), Sohu, 2020 (https://m.sohu.com/a/370100434_120243407/)

What it is: A popular history article on a Chinese news portal tracing the Chinese city moat from the Mozi to the Ming and Qing, quoting the period military texts on how wide and deep a moat should be, among them the Ming treatise Wubei Zhi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the readable page found that quotes the Wubei Zhi's rule for a moat's width and depth; the treatise itself was not read, because the text library that holds it refuses automated reading, so the rule stands on the article's quotation. It is written for a general audience and ends with a list of four references (among them the Song Wujing Zongyao, not the Wubei Zhi) but cites none of them at any passage; its own summary that Ming and Qing moats were generally ten zhang wide and three zhang deep is unsourced and conflicts with the treatise it quotes, and is not used. The treatise sets a standard, not a measurement of any built moat, and the article presents the Ming widening as the answer to firearms in siege war; the pre-gunpowder rule it quotes, from the Tang general Li Jing, is a moat two zhang wide at the top and one zhang deep.

Used for: the Ming military treatise's rule for a moat's width and depth (City defenses)
