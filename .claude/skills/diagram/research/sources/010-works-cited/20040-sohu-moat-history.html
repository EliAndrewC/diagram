sohu-moat-history

计功 (pen name), 作为城池防御的第一道防线：护城河，是怎样在古代中国大放异彩的 ("The moat, the first line of a walled city's defense: how it shone in ancient China", translated from the Chinese by this project), Sohu, 2020 (https://m.sohu.com/a/370100434_120243407/)

What it is: A popular history article on a Chinese news portal tracing the Chinese city moat from the Mozi to the Ming and Qing, quoting the period military texts on how wide and deep a moat should be, among them the Ming treatise Wubei Zhi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the readable page found that quotes the Wubei Zhi's rule for a moat's width and depth; the treatise itself was not read, because the text library that holds it refuses automated reading, so the rule stands on the article's quotation. It is written for a general audience and gives no sources; its own summary that Ming and Qing moats were generally ten zhang wide is unsourced and conflicts with the treatise it quotes, and is not used. The treatise sets a standard, not a measurement of any built moat.

Used for: the Ming military treatise's rule for a moat's width and depth (cities/defenses)
