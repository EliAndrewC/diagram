Zhihui Sannong (an agricultural knowledge service), "The long-vine-bearing-melons irrigation system" (in Chinese; https://www.pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/547878.shtml)

What it is: An encyclopedia-style entry on the Chinese irrigation system in which a canal from a headwork links many reservoirs and ponds, named for the look of melons hung on a vine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the system - canals joined to reservoirs and ponds, drawing, storing and lifting water together - and names the Baiqi canal of the Warring States period as the earliest, which puts the form before modern times. It says the modern form was developed in the late 1950s from older pond and canal works and suits hill country. It is an unsigned reference entry on China Agriculture Press's knowledge platform that cites no period text, and it gives no count or size of the ponds on an early system.

Used for: the name and form of the linked canal-and-pond system, and that it is older than modern times (Water)
