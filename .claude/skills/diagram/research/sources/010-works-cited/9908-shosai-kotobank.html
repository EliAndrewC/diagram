shosai-kotobank

Kotobank, 書斎 (shosai, the study) - the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Nipponica entry by Hirai Kiyoshi (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9B%B8%E6%96%8E-534509)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the study, with dictionary definitions and a Nipponica encyclopedia entry by the architectural historian Hirai Kiyoshi tracing it from a gakumonsho in upper-class houses of the late Heian and early Kamakura periods (attested in the Azuma Kagami) and the writing desk built into a medieval monk's room, to the shoin-style gakumonsho of the early modern imperial and retired emperor's palaces, and on to a Meiji novelist's study and postwar homes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in so many words that a study could be a room or a building, which is the question a detached study raises. It is a tertiary summary; its historical examples are upper-class houses of the late Heian and early Kamakura periods (from the Azuma Kagami, which does not say whose house), medieval temples, and the early modern imperial and retired emperor's palaces, and it names no Edo-period warrior house. It gives no size for a detached study. Its only room sizes, six and seven mats, are Natsume Soseki's Meiji-era study, a modern house, and not a premodern figure; the "two to four ken" it gives is the width of a bookshelf in the court's gakumonsho, not of a room.

Used for: a study as a room in a private house; a shoin or gakumonsho as a room or a building (buildings)
