kawashiri-komegura-kunishitei

Agency for Cultural Affairs, 国指定文化財等データベース, 熊本藩川尻米蔵跡 ("Database of Nationally Designated Cultural Properties: the Kumamoto domain's Kawashiri rice storehouse site", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://kunishitei.bunka.go.jp/heritage/detail/401/00003685)

What it is: The national cultural-property database's designation text for the Kawashiri rice storehouse site, the river port south of Kumamoto where the domain gathered its tax rice; it says what stood there, how much rice came in and where it went, how many storehouse buildings the old drawings show, and how long the surviving landing is.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an official account of a large domain's tax-rice store standing at a river port rather than in the castle, with its buildings counted and its intake given, which is what a provincial city's granary is drawn from. Its limits: it is a modern official summary of the period drawings and records, not the records themselves; Kumamoto was one of the largest domains, so its nine buildings are the top of the range; the 200,000 bales were the usual intake from five of the domain's districts only, not the whole domain's tax rice, and the text does not say how large a bale was; the store gathered the rice to ship it on to the domain's Osaka warehouse, and also served as a naval port, so it shows a place rice passed through rather than a reserve held for the town; the text gives no building's size; and the two surviving storehouses, built around the end of the Edo period, are only thought to belong to the old set.

Used for: a domain's tax-rice store at a river port: its nine buildings, its intake and its landing (buildings)
