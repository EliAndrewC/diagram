haneishi-2011-kushiro-alder

羽石崇・中津川誠・工藤俊, "Study of influences of ground water on spread of the alder swamp forest area in Kushiro Mire", 土木学会論文集B1 67(4) I_1357-I_1362 (2011), DOI 10.2208/jscejhe.67.I_1357 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jscejhe/67/4/67_4_I_1357/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A peer-reviewed hydraulic-engineering paper, free on J-STAGE, relating ground height, groundwater level and vegetation across the Kushiro Mire in eastern Hokkaido to work out why alder forest has been replacing the mire’s reed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It settles what a drying wet margin turns into and what holds the change off: 87% reed in 1977 against about 45% alder by 2004, reed on ground inundated the whole time, and alder barely able to establish where the water stands 0.4 m or more above the ground, spreading instead around rivers and old channels where the table sits below the surface and swings widely. It is the source that reversed this record’s own claim, which had a fluctuating table resisting alder when fluctuation is where it expands. Its limits: one mire in the coldest part of Japan, measured in the modern period and after the river works that are themselves the cause it studies, so it gives the DIRECTION a drying wetland moves rather than the state of a premodern marsh; and being a hydrology paper it says nothing about how inhabitants cut or used either plant. Read the two figures together and not separately: the paper's abstract gives alder INCREASING where the groundwater sits 0.4 m or more below the ground, and its body gives alder unable to establish where the relative water level is 0.4 m or more ABOVE it. Those are the two ends of one gradient, not a contradiction.
