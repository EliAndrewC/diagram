秋山好則 (Akiyama Yoshinori), 多摩川中流域の屋敷林の研究 - 特に玉川上水周辺の屋敷林の構成 ("A study of the homestead groves of the middle Tama River basin, especially the make-up of the groves around the Tamagawa aqueduct", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a 1992 research report by a Tokyo metropolitan high-school teacher, published by the Tokyu Foundation (in Japanese; https://foundation.tokyu.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/G075.pdf)

What it is: A field survey of the homestead groves still standing on the Musashino upland west of Tokyo, town by town, with the owners' own accounts of how each grove was planted and used, and the order of house lot, field and woodland on several of the Edo-period new-field farms laid along their roads.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one read source that describes farms in a row along a road with the road on their north, and says what their groves did there - a line of trees kept along the road, the grove thinner on that side, the bamboo set to the south because the row of grove farms across the road already broke the wind, so none was needed there as a windbreak - and a farm whose house was turned to face its road. Its limits: the groves are those standing in the 1990s, told by the families of the time, not dated to the founding; the farms are dry-field colonies on a water-poor upland, not a paddy plain; and it is an unrefereed foundation report whose cases are the ones the author happened to visit.

Used for: a row farm's grove where its road ran on the north, a house turned to its road, and the order of house lot, field and woodland on an upland row farm (homesteads)
