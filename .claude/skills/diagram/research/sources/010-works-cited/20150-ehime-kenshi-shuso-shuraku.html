ehime-kenshi-shuso-shuraku

Ehime Prefecture, Ehime kenshi: chishi II (Toyo tobu) (1988), chapter 2 "Shuso heiya", section 4 "交通・集落" ("Communications and settlements", title translated from the Japanese by this project), in the database Ehime no kioku (in Japanese; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/32/view/4555)

What it is: The prefectural gazetteer's account of the Shuso plain's settlements: which sit at the head of an alluvial fan and which at its foot where the spring water rises, and two villages described in detail, with their water supply.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a scholarly regional geography that names the settlements strung in a line along a fan's foot, where the water that sank into the fan comes up again - the spring line of the textbooks, seen on the ground. Its limits: it describes the 1980s landscape with memories reaching back to the Meiji era, its row is a row of villages rather than of houses, and it gives no frontage or spacing.

Used for: settlements lined up in an arc at the foot of an alluvial fan, where the spring water rises (homesteads)
