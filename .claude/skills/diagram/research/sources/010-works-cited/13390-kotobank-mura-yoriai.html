kotobank-mura-yoriai

Kotobank, 村寄合 ("mura-yoriai, the village meeting", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho entry by Takeuchi Toshimi, the Heibonsha MyPedia and the Britannica International entries (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9D%91%E5%AF%84%E5%90%88-140911)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the village meeting, carrying three published encyclopedias on who attended, what it decided, how often it met and where.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the places a village met - an official's office-house, a temple, a shrine or a hall for chanting the Buddha's name, the festival's host house, and later meeting halls - and says the Edo meeting was called under the village officials and made up mainly of the landholding farmers, the small holders and landless apparently joining later (the entry hedges it, "it seems"); its rule that every household head attend, usually twice a year, is stated of the meeting as it survived after Meiji, said to carry on the early-modern tradition. Its limits: a folklorist's entry that runs the Edo meeting together with its modern survival, dating its changes only as "in old times" and "gradually", with no size for any hall; all three entries are encyclopedia summaries of Japan as a whole, naming no region and citing no records.

Used for: the places a village met, who came and how often (homesteads)
