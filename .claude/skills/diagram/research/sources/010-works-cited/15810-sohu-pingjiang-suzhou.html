sohu-pingjiang-suzhou

风浮说星座 (a Sohu contributor), 从宋《平江图》追寻苏州古城的全貌 ("Tracing the whole of Suzhou's old city from the Song Pingjiang tu", title translated from the Chinese by this project), Sohu, 2023-02-28 (https://www.sohu.com/a/647521251_121607363)

What it is: A popular article on the 1229 stone plan of Suzhou and the city it shows: the moat, the "three across and four down" rivers, the streets paired with rivers, the stone-faced banks and the houses fronting a street and backing on the water, with stone steps down to it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the canal city that the record's river cities draw their in-city canals from, and states the bank form - stone revetment, railings in places, stepped house landings - in plain words. It is a contributor's post on a news portal, not scholarship, and it describes the city's canals from the plan and from what survives without dating the revetments or measuring anything; it is used for the bank form and for how the moat met the city's canals, never for a number. Suzhou is also a particular case: a prefectural seat of nearly 300,000 people (the article's 1184 count) in the canal country of the lower Yangzi, among the richest cities of the empire, so stone-faced banks along its canals show what a large, wealthy canal city built, not what a smaller or poorer river city, or one outside that region, would have lined its banks with. It posts under an account named for astrology, with no named author, so it is corroborated where it can be (the paired land-and-water gates by pingjiang-tu, the moat-canal junction at Pan Gate by pan-gate-enwiki).

Used for: what faces and lines a Chinese city canal's banks; the moat joined to the city's canals through the water gates (cities/river-cities)
