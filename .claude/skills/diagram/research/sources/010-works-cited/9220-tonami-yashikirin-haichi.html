tonami-yashikirin-haichi

Tonami City board of education's digital archive 砺波正倉, 「4-1 The homestead grove that wraps the house」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「④－１家をつつむ屋敷林」), from the booklet 『砺波平野の散村』 (https://1073shoso.jp/www/sankyo/detail.jsp?id=18168)

What it is: A chapter of a Tonami city booklet on the dispersed farmsteads of the Tonami plain, published on the city's own archive, describing the plain's homestead groves (kainyo): how they began, the Kaga domain's protection of seven kinds of tree, the tree species found in them classed by height, which trees stand on which side of the house - tall sugi, kashi and keyaki from south to west, sugi, enoki and alder with bamboo from west to north, and little grove on the east, where the house fronts, but flowering and fruit trees - and what the grove did for the household.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a local authority's account, drawn from survey, of where the trees stood around a farmhouse in the one Japanese landscape where a homestead grove is at its most developed, which is the question our grove and garden placement asks. Its limits are that it is a popular booklet that does not give its survey's date, sample or method; that it describes groves as they stand in living memory and notes itself that ornamental garden trees have been brought in lately, so part of what it lists is modern; that the Tonami plain is the dispersed snow country of Hokuriku, with an isolated farmstead's grove at the developed end of the Japanese range, not a nucleated village's; and that it gives no distances between the house, its plots and the trees.

Used for: the east-facing house front with fruit trees rather than grove on the east; the sides of the house the tall trees and the bamboo stand on; the winds the grove held off
