suido-ishizue-kishu

Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), Suido no Ishizue, "Fixing the flow: the Kishu school" (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/05/06.html)

What it is: A page of the same foundation's illustrated history, on the Kishu school of river and irrigation works that Izawa Yasobei brought to the Kanto plain from 1722, set against the older Kanto school.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the older Kanto-school water use made one channel serve both supply and drainage, and that the Kishu school separated the drainage rivers, which dates the separated layout's arrival on the Kanto plain to the 1720s and the combined one there to before it; the techniques were Izawa's own, brought from Kishu, so the page does not say separation was new everywhere in 1722, nor that the combined layout ended where the Kishu school did not reach. It is written about the large alluvial plains of eastern Japan, and it describes trunk canals and drainage rivers, not the ditches between one village's plots.

Used for: that the combined layout was the older one and the Kishu school separated supply from drainage (water)
