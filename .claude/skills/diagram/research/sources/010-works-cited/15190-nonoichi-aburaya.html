nonoichi-aburaya

Nonoichi City, "江戸時代の油屋" ("The oil shops of the Edo period", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.city.nonoichi.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/31557.pdf)

What it is: A one-page local-history sheet from Nonoichi (Ishikawa prefecture), written for schoolchildren, on the rapeseed of Ishikawa county and the households of Nonoichi that kept oil shops, with a picture from the oil manual Seiyuroku of seed ground by a water wheel.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a city's own account of one village's oil trade and shows that such a place kept oil shops. Its limits are that it is brief and for children; that Nonoichi was a village (野々市村) just outside the castle town of Kanazawa, and its one named oil man sold his oil wholesale to a Kanazawa shop, so it shows a village trade feeding a large city rather than a small town's own; that by the bakumatsu its oil shops were failing and turning to other trades, one becoming an inn; that it gives no count of oil shops and no building, and names pressing as a step without describing the press; and that the water-wheel sentence is the city's general caption under a picture from Seiyuroku (Ōkura Nagatsune, 1836, a manual of the Osaka-area oil trade), not evidence that Nonoichi milled seed by water wheel.

Used for: oil shops in a small town, and the water-wheel mill (urban-features)
