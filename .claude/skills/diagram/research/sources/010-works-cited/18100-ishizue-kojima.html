ishizue-kojima

水土の礎 (the history site of land and water run by the Agricultural and Rural Integrated Information Center, ARIC), 新田と機械化農村、児島湾干拓地 ("New fields and the mechanized village: the Kojima Bay reclamation", title translated) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/03/11.html)

What it is: A short, unsigned page in ARIC's popular history of land development on the reclamation of Kojima Bay in Okayama, from the Ikeda domain's projects of the Kanbun era through the Meiji-era Fujita farm to the mechanized village of Kojo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the Ikeda domain's reclamation of the bay chiefly to the Kanbun era (1661-72), with other domains' projects carrying the pre-Meiji reclamation to about 6,800 ha and the Fujita works following from 1899, and Kojo village's to the very end of the early modern period, and says Kojo's subdivided fields were consolidated into blocks by readjustment under its upper farmers - the evidence that the blocks there are a later work, not the Edo layout. It gives no date for that consolidation (its context places it before the war, alongside the mechanization of the 1920s-30s) and does not describe the Edo-period plots or say how they came to be subdivided.

Used for: Kojo's Edo-reclaimed land, its subdivided fields consolidated into blocks only in the twentieth century (fields)
