zh.wikipedia 文庙 ("Confucian temple", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/文庙)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Confucian temples, including how a local Confucian temple was joined to the official school of its prefecture or county and the layouts that joining took.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the general arrangement of temple and school in one sentence. It is a community-edited encyclopedia summary, not a survey, and that sentence carries no footnote of its own (the article's reading list names Gao Mingshi's paper on the Sui-Tang temple-school system, which this project has not read), so it is used for the shape of the arrangement and not as an authority. The counts and site areas it gives elsewhere (temples across China, courtyards per temple, a few surviving temples' areas) describe surviving and often rebuilt temples, and are not used. It describes China: it says Korean temples put the school in front and the temple behind, and its Japan section is only a list of temples, so it gives no Japanese pattern.

Used for: how a Chinese seat's Confucian temple and school were laid out together (religion-and-death)
