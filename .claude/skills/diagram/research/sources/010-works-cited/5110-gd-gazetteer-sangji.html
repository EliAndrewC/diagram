Guangdong Provincial Gazetteer Office (广东省人民政府地方志办公室), 【粤故事】中国第一个机器缫丝厂与桑基鱼塘有何渊源, 2023-08-16 (https://dfz.gd.gov.cn/index/dqyj/content/post_4237091.html)

What it is: A 2023 article in the 粤故事 (Tales of Yue) series published by the Local Gazetteer Office of the Guangdong provincial government, with no individual author credited, retelling the history of the mulberry-dike fish ponds of the Pearl River delta from the local record: the dig-the-pond, raise-the-dike method, the classic proportion of water to dike, the crops the dikes carried, and the arrival of machine silk reeling in 1873.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the classic proportion of water to dike in the local record's own words, which is what a dike-and-pond block is drawn to. Its limits are that it is a popular, unsigned government retelling written for a general audience rather than scholarship; that the sentence giving the proportion ("three to seven or four to six") never says which side is the water and which the dike, so the direction our maps read is a decision of ours and not a finding of the page; that its narrative runs into nineteenth-century industrial silk reeling, later than our maps; and that southern China stands in for the wet-farming pattern of a Japan-flavored setting.

Used for: the water-to-dike ratio stated as 水基比 「a thirty-seventy to forty-sixty split」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「三七至四六开」) (3:7 to 4:6 - the page does not say which side is water); dikes planted with mulberry (sugarcane and banana are named in the entry but were not found on the page); Chen Qiyuan's 1873 filature as the point where household reeling met the factory
