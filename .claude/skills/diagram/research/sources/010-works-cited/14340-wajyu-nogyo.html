wajyu-nogyo

Wanouchi town tourism committee, 輪中の農業とその進化 ("Farming in the waju and how it changed", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 輪之内町〜輪中が息づく平らな町〜 (in Japanese; https://wajyu.jp/mania/evolution/)

What it is: A page of the town of Wanouchi (Gifu prefecture), which lies inside the Fukutsuka waju, on how the ring-diked community got water in and out: the intake culverts that were hard to build, the drain culverts opened when the river fell, a village-by-village count of both from 1789 to 1872, and the raised horita fields.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes one waju of the Nobi plain from the town's own records, with a dated count of its culverts and the size of its main drains, which is the only such count read. It is a town promotional history written for visitors, it names no documents for its counts, and the Nobi waju faced great rivers, so a village polder on a creek would have needed fewer and smaller openings.

Used for: how a waju drained through culverts opened at low river, how many it had, and the horita field (archetypes)
