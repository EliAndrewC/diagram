Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (Japan), Kanto Regional Bureau, Yokohama office, "東海道と宿場の施設: 川" (the Tokaido and its post-town facilities: rivers) (in Japanese; https://www.ktr.mlit.go.jp/yokohama/tokaido/02_tokaido/03_sisetu/1201.htm)

What it is: One page of the road ministry's regional series on the old Tokaido through Kanagawa, on its river crossings: which rivers were bridged, ferried or forded, why, and the Rokugo and Banyu ferries in detail.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lays out two real ferry landings - the river office, the notice board, the boatmen's huts, the boats with their kinds, numbers and sizes, the crews, and who held the contract - the nearest thing found to a description of a landing, though it lists what stood there without saying where each thing stood or how large it was, so it is not a plan. It is a modern heritage page by a government road office that cites no sources, so its counts and sizes rest on the office's word. It gives the ban on bridging the great rivers only as competing theories (a river as a checkpoint, protecting the post towns and river porters, weak bridge-building), not a settled reason. Both are ferries on the busiest highway under shogunate regulation, far larger than a crossing a village would keep for itself.

Used for: why the great rivers were not bridged, the designated landings, and two ferries laid out with their boats and crews (ways)
