katsushika-horikiri

Katsushika City, 子ども葛飾区史 第3章 第9節 小菅（小菅・堀切・東堀切） ("Katsushika history for children, ch. 3 sec. 9: Kosuge, Horikiri and Higashi-Horikiri", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.city.katsushika.lg.jp/history/child/3-9-169.html)

What it is: The ward's undated local-history page for young readers on the Horikiri iris garden. It says gardens for viewing irises began at Horikiri about 220 years ago, at the end of the Edo period, and that the present garden grew from Horikiri-en, which Isogai Chūjirō started in the middle of the Meiji period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates flower gardens at Horikiri, a village near Edo, to the late Edo period, and records one garden that was turned into paddy. Its limits: it is a short page written for children by the ward, and it gives the Edo date only as about 220 years ago and as something "it is said". It does not say what the ground was before the gardens. The garden turned into paddy was a Meiji one, and that happened in 1942 because of a wartime food shortage, a modern state measure. So it shows that such ground could grow rice, not that an Edo garden went back to paddy. It gives no area for any garden, and its plant count (200 varieties, 6,000 plants) is for today's ward park.

Used for: the iris gardens of Horikiri, and the Meiji garden that was turned into paddy in 1942 (fields)
