iwamuro-kenchicho

Keio University Old Documents Room, exhibition 2013, item 5: "Survey register of Iwamuro village, Koka district, Omi" (in Japanese; https://kmj.flet.keio.ac.jp/exhibition/2013/05.html)

What it is: An exhibition page of Keio University's old-documents room showing a village survey register of 1602 (Keicho 7) from Omi province, with a transcription of a dozen of its entries, each a plot with its grade, area and holder.

Why it applies, and its limits: A primary register from the start of the Edo period, so its plot sizes are premodern Japanese fields as surveyed. The page transcribes only a dozen entries (nine paddies, three dry fields) of one village in the hills of Koka in Omi, not a polder, and gives no count or average, so it shows a range of plot sizes, not a typical one. Each entry is a register parcel (hitsu), a unit of holding and tax that may take in more than one diked paddy, so its area is a parcel's, not necessarily a single field's. And the smallest paddy, of 2 se, is marked eiara - land that could not be farmed and was excused from tax with no end date - so the smallest paddy in cultivation is 3 se 22 bu. Its se and bu are the survey's own, measured with a rod of more than six shaku (Hideyoshi's surveys used 6 shaku 3 sun), so each is a few percent to about a tenth larger than a modern se or tsubo, and a conversion to metres should say so; the page does not name the rod this survey used.

Used for: that register parcels of paddy in 1602 ran from 3 se 22 bu in cultivation (2 se counting one plot marked permanently waste) to 1 tan 8 se 1 bu (archetypes); the same range set against the size of a town's paddy plot (towns)
