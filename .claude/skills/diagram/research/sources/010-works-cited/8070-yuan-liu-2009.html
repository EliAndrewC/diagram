Yuan Juanwen and Liu Jinlong, "Fengshui forest management by the Buyi ethnic minority in China", Forest Ecology and Management 257(9) (2009) 2002-2009 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378112709000632; the full text is paywalled: DOI 10.1016/j.foreco.2009.01.040)

What it is: A peer-reviewed article by Yuan Juanwen and Liu Jinlong, "Fengshui forest management by the Buyi ethnic minority in China", published in Forest Ecology and Management in 2009, on how Buyi villages in Guizhou province hold and manage the sacred groves kept for a settlement's fengshui.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the shape of a village's sacred forest holdings in a real case - two fengshui parcels per village in the two villages studied - which is what sets how many groves a village on our maps keeps. Its limits are that it is two villages of one ethnic minority in one southwestern province, so a shape and its extremes rather than an average; that it is a count of what survived in the 2000s, after a century in which collectivization and forestry law reshaped village forests; and that the 133-hectare lineage forest the record quotes as the large tail of the range is not this paper's own measurement - it is a Han lineage's forest in Fujian, cited secondhand in the paper's introduction from another author - so the outlier comes from a different province and a different community.

Used for: two fengshui parcels per village in two Buyi villages (Guizhou); the 133 ha Letu lineage forest as the large tail
