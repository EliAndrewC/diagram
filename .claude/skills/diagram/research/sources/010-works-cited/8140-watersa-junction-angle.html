watersa-junction-angle

Water SA, "Numerical study of junction-angle effects on flow pattern in a river confluence located in a river bend" (https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1816-79502016000100006)

What it is: A 2016 paper in Water SA, the journal of South Africa's Water Research Commission, reporting a three-dimensional numerical model of a river confluence that sits inside a river bend. It runs the model across junction angles from 30 to 115 degrees and reports what each angle does to the zone of separated flow at the mouth, to the streamwise velocity near the inner wall, to the strength of the secondary spiral flow, and to the bed shear stress inside the junction.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for one thing: the DIRECTION in which a junction's angle changes the way a side channel enters a larger one, which is what sets the two feet of a river-fed moat where it meets its river. Its limits are worth stating plainly - it models a confluence located in a bend, so its figures (a 59% rise in peak bed shear between 30 and 115 degrees) belong to that geometry rather than to a straight reach; it is a numerical model rather than a survey of a real waterway; it is modern hydraulics, so it describes what water does and says nothing about what a premodern builder intended or achieved; and the slack water behind the mouth and the attack on the far bank that our record reads from it are not among its findings. One limit deserves to be spelled out, because it bears on whether the direction we take from the paper carries at all: the model holds the tributary's width and its share of the discharge fixed, at the proportions of a laboratory channel, whereas the offtake that feeds a moat draws a share of its river's flow smaller by orders of magnitude - a trickle beside a current rather than one stream meeting another - so we cannot be sure even the direction of the effect survives the change of scale.

Used for: the direction in which a junction's angle changes the way a side channel enters a larger one: a wider angle enlarging the separation zone at the mouth and raising the bed shear inside the junction
