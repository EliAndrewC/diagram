zh.wikipedia 留园 ("Lingering Garden", title translated) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/%E7%95%99%E5%9B%AD)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Lingering Garden (Liuyuan) of Suzhou, a private garden outside the Changmen gate, with its history from its founding in 1593 as the East Garden of Xu Taishi, a vice-minister of the Court of the Imperial Stud who had by then been dismissed from office, through its Qing rebuildings of 1798 and 1873-76.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places one named Ming official's private garden, built after he was dismissed from office, just outside a city gate, which is the near-city retreat this record asks about. It is one garden, in Suzhou, the richest garden city of late imperial China; it says the garden was his but not whether he lived there or in town, gives no distance from the wall, and says nothing about how common such a garden was. The garden whose size and layout it describes (20,390 m² in its infobox, "over 30,000 m², 50 mu" in its text) is the Qing rebuilding of 1798 and the enlargement of 1873-76, made on the site of a Ming garden that had fallen into ruin, so only the garden's founding and its place outside the gate are Ming.

Used for: a Ming official's private garden just outside a city gate (cities/hinterland)
