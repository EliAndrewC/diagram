horikawa-2011-nakaboshi

Horikawa Naoki, Yoshida Takeo and Masumoto Takao, "Nakaboshi ga kangai chiku no shusui patan e oyobosu eikyo no jirei bunseki" (A case analysis of the effect of mid-season drainage on the intake pattern of irrigation districts), Noson kogaku kenkyujo giho (Technical Report of the National Institute for Rural Engineering) 211, pp. 109-119, 2011 (in Japanese; https://www.naro.go.jp/publicity_report/publication/archive/files/211-9.pdf)

What it is: A technical report by three researchers of Japan's national rural-engineering institute on how the mid-season drain changes how much water irrigation districts take. Its second section reviews the drain's history: Edo-period farm books that describe it, its practice in 22 prefectures in 1933, its confinement before the war to regions with plentiful water, its postwar spread, and the reasons given in 1966 for not doing it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that traces the drain back before modern times, and it states how thinly it was practiced then. Its history is a review of other studies (Nagata 1964, Tanaka 1970, Ikehashi 2005), read here at second hand. It does not name the Edo farm books or say which regions they describe, and it gives no premodern timing or length for the drain.

Used for: that some paddies were drained in midsummer by the Edo period, only where water was plentiful (fields)
