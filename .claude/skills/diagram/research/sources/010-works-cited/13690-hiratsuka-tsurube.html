hiratsuka-tsurube

Hiratsuka City Museum, "The well bucket and the well" (title translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「釣瓶と井戸」), from the catalog of the 2004 special exhibition on food implements (in Japanese; https://www.hirahaku.jp/hakubutsukan_archive/minzoku/00000065/24.html)

What it is: A catalog text from a municipal museum in Kanagawa prefecture on how the city's households raised water: a bamboo pole straight into a very shallow well, a sweep on a post with a stone counterweight for a deeper one, and the pulley well, which was less common there.

Why it applies, and its limits: A museum's folklore collection, describing the gear in use in one coastal plain city and saying WHY one form prevailed there - the wells were shallow. It describes the late 19th and 20th centuries as much as earlier, and one district only; its remark that pulley wells were few is about Hiratsuka, not Japan.

Used for: the sweep for a shallow well and the pulley for a deeper one (urban-features)
