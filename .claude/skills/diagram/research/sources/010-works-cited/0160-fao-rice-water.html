fao-rice-water

Thierry Facon, "Water Management in Rice in Asia: Some Issues for the Future", FAO (https://www.fao.org/4/x6905e/x6905e0g.htm)

What it is: "Water Management in Rice in Asia: Some Issues for the Future", a paper by Thierry Facon published by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization, surveying how rice irrigation in Asia is run and where it is heading. Along the way it describes the ordinary mechanics of a paddy landscape: water passing from plot to plot so that irrigation and drainage cannot be told apart, and runoff being taken up again further down.

Why it applies, and its limits: That plot-to-plot cascade is the behavior our field layouts depend on, and it is a consequence of flat, bunded, gravity-fed paddies rather than of any modern works. The limits are that the paper is written about contemporary irrigation systems and their reform, and that what we draw from the cascade - which plot ends up wettest - is our own inference, labeled as such where we use it, not something the paper says.

Used for: the plot-to-plot cascade that the wettest-plot-on-the-drain inference rests on - "water flows from one plot to another and no distinction can be made between irrigation and drainage"; and drainage reuse - surface runoff "can be re-used, i.e., recycled within the system". NOT used for the inference itself, which the entry labels an inference
