oshirasu-jawiki

ja.wikipedia お白洲 (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%8A%E7%99%BD%E6%B4%B2)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the oshirasu, the court of an Edo-period magistracy: the tiers of the Edo South Town Magistracy's court by a floor plan of 1810, who sat in each by office and by status, where the accused and the other parties sat, the torture instruments set out in it, and its roof.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that sets out a court's seating tier by tier and says outright that a magistracy's court was roofed or indoor, which is what the hearing court of our county offices is drawn from. Its limits are that it is a short tertiary article, a stub, whose four footnotes all cite one popular book on the truths and fictions of period dramas (Sasama Yoshihiko, 2004), with a scholarly dictionary entry (Andō Yūichirō, 2002) listed but tied to no sentence; that its sentence saying the court was roofed or indoor carries no footnote at all, where its seating and its torture statements cite Sasama; that its plan is of the capital's town magistracy, far grander than a country intendant's office, and is Edo Japanese practice only, standing in for our setting's county courts; and that it gives only two measures, a plank verandah of 3 shaku along the front of the officials' rooms with a stair of three steps 1 ken wide at its center, and no size for the court or its gravel floor; its statement that the instruments were for show is hedged as what is thought.

Used for: who sat where at a hearing and on what; the court roofed or indoor; no torture in the court (buildings)
