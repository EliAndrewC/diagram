suido-ishizue-kochi-seiri

水土の礎 ("Foundations of Water and Land", the Japan Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center's history site), 農地を改良する、耕地整理 ("Improving farmland: kochi seiri", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/03/09.html)

What it is: A short history page, written by the agricultural-engineering information center that runs the site, on Japan's farmland consolidation under the 1899 law: the Konosu model plot, and where the work was done in eastern and western Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states what the paddy looked like before consolidation - narrow, irregular plots handed down from the early modern period - and that western Japan kept regular plots such as the jori grid until Meiji, which bears directly on whether a pre-modern bund runs straight. It is a general-audience summary by an engineering body, without footnotes, and it describes Japan only; it gives no measurements of the pre-consolidation plots themselves. The Konosu plot it describes (30 by 10 ken, one tan, begun in 1902) is a Meiji engineered standard, not a pre-modern form: that every plot touched a channel and a road is what consolidation introduced, alongside drainage to dry the wet paddy, so it can serve only as the modern contrast to the pre-modern paddy, never as that paddy's layout.

Used for: the narrow, irregular early-modern plot; the jori grid's survival in western Japan to Meiji; the Konosu plot, every one of which touched a channel and a road (fields)
