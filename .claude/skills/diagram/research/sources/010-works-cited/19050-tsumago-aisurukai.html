tsumago-aisurukai

Tsumago wo Aisuru Kai (the Society for Loving Tsumago), 妻籠宿の歴史 ("The history of Tsumago-juku", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.tumagowoaisurukai.jp/abouttsumago/)

What it is: The local preservation society's history of Tsumago, a small post town on the Nakasendo in the Kiso valley, covering its Edo-period street, its inns, its village's houses in 1642 and the preservation movement of the twentieth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the station's street length in the period's own units, 2 cho 30 ken (about 270 m), the smallest such length found. It hedges the figure: the length "is said to be" this and "appears" not to have changed through the Edo period. It is a society's page for visitors with no source or date for that figure, and it gives no house count for the station alone, only the whole village's in 1642. Elsewhere the page says Tsumago-juku extends to about 1 km, which describes today's settlement, not the Edo-period station's street. Tsumago was a mountain station on a narrow river terrace, so the ground may have limited its length.

Used for: one small post town's street length in the Edo period (towns)
