Japanese Wiki Corpus, Jokamachi (translation of the ja.wikipedia article) (https://www.japanesewiki.com/history/Jokamachi.html)

What it is: The same translated castle-town article in the Japanese Wiki Corpus, of how a castle town was arranged: the castle at the center, rings of samurai and foot soldier quarters graded by rank, streets named for the trade that worked them, the temple district on the rim, main roads led past the castle's front to show off the lord's standing, and the total outer enclosure that a few great towns built.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the whole grammar of the castle town in English - the castle at the center, the rank-graded rings of samurai residence, the occupational merchant wards, the temple quarter on the rim, the main road led past the castle's front - which is the skeleton every capital map is laid out on. Its limits are that its examples are the largest Japanese castle towns, a scale that has to be reduced before it reaches a provincial seat; that it is a tertiary summary at one further remove, the English being a machine-translation research corpus (the bilingual corpus of Wikipedia's Kyoto-topic articles made by NICT, Japan's National Institute of Information and Communications Technology) and a frozen snapshot rather than the maintained article; and that two other entries in this registry point at the same article and at the Japanese original it translates, so the three cannot corroborate one another.

Used for: the castle at the town's center; concentric rank-graded zoning (Sange / Kamiyashiki-cho for samurai, Ban-cho / Teppo-cho for ashigaru); occupational machi (Gofuku-machi, Kaji-machi); teramachi at the outer rim as part of the city defenses; main roads routed past the castle's front "to indicate the glory of the ruler"; sogamae total enclosure (Odawara, Osaka)
