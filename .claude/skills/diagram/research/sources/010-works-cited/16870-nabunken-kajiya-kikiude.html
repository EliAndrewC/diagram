nabunken-kajiya-kikiude

Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, Nabunken blog, "鍛冶屋さんの利き腕" ("The smith's dominant hand", title translated from the Japanese by this project), August 2013 (in Japanese; https://www.nabunken.go.jp/nabunkenblog/2013/08/20130801-2.html)

What it is: A short essay by a researcher at the national institute on why smithies are laid out for a right-handed smith, giving the order of a workshop's fittings: bellows, hearth, anvil and water tub.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the working arrangement of a smithy from the author's own visits to working swordsmiths and village smiths, from an institute whose field is historical craft. Its limits are that it describes smithies seen in modern times, that it asks rather than answers when the arrangement became general, and that it gives no dimensions.

Used for: the order of a smithy's fittings (urban-features)
