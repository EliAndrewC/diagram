nabunken-kajiya-kikiude

Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, Nabunken blog, "鍛冶屋さんの利き腕" ("The smith's dominant hand", title translated), August 2013 (in Japanese; https://www.nabunken.go.jp/nabunkenblog/2013/08/20130801-2.html)

What it is: A short essay by a researcher at the national institute on why smithies are laid out for a right-handed smith, giving the order of a workshop's fittings: bellows, hearth, anvil and water tub.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the working arrangement of a smithy from the author's own visits to working swordsmiths and village smiths. It also says that smiths in medieval and early-modern Japanese picture scrolls are almost all right-handed with their fittings set out the same way, so the order is attested for the premodern period. It also suggests, as a guess, that the layout may go back to an excavated early Nara-period smithing yard at Heijō-kyō. Its limits are that it is a short blog essay without references, naming no particular scroll; that it covers Japan only; that the modern smiths it saw may use a blower in place of bellows; and that it gives no dimensions.

Used for: the order of a smithy's fittings (Trades and services)
