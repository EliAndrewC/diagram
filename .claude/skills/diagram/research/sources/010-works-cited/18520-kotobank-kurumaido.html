kotobank-kurumaido

Kotobank, 車井戸 ("kuruma-ido, the pulley well", title translated), quoting the Digital Daijisen, the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nipponica (entry by Ogawa Naoyuki) and Heibonsha's Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%BB%8A%E4%BA%95%E6%88%B8-486513)

What it is: Dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the pulley well, a grooved pulley hung over the well with a bucket at each end of the rope: the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's earliest attestation, a comic verse of 1753 saying every well in Kyoto was a pulley well; Nipponica's note that the pulley served deep water and the sweep shallower; and Heibonsha's that before the pump well water was mostly drawn by one or the other.

Why it applies, and its limits: The dated verse places the pulley well in eighteenth-century Kyoto, which is the premodern attestation the map's pulley needs. A verse is a joke, not a count; the depth rule is Nipponica's general statement, undated; and none of the entries says what held the pulley up, gives a size, or speaks for China.

Used for: the pulley well in premodern Japan and the depth that chose it (urban-features)
