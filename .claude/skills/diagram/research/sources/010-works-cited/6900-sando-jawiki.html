What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the sando (参道), the approach road to a shrine or temple. It gives the term both narrowly, as the path inside the sacred bounds within the torii or the temple gate, and broadly, as the whole road running to the shrine from somewhere busy such as the line of a highway; it also notes the torii-front and gate-front towns that grew up along such approaches, and highways whose course was bent to bring them nearer a shrine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the relation between a shrine's approach road and the highway - the road running from the busy highway to the hall, the gate-front town along it, and highways bent to bring them nearer a shrine - which is what places a shrine and its approach on our town maps. Its limits are that it is a short tertiary article carrying the encyclopedia's own notice that it lacks sources, and none of its three references is attached to the sentences we quote, so it is an unsourced definition rather than a referenced finding; that it is a general Japanese case with no date; and that it supports the relation between road and hall and no distance or width.

Used for: the approach 「a road running to a shrine or temple from a place of heavy foot traffic such as the line of a highway」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「街道筋など人通りの多いところから社寺に至る道」); highways sometimes bent to bring them nearer a shrine
