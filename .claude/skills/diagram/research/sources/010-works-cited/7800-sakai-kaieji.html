sakai-kaieji

Sakai City, 海会寺 (the combined hondō and kuri) (https://www.city.sakai.lg.jp/kanko/rekishi/bunkazai/bunkazai/shokai/bunya/kenzobutu/kaiedaji.html) - 「Main hall and kuri, one building: ridge span 25.2 meters, beam span 8.0 meters on the left side, 13.0 meters on the right side」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「本堂及び庫裏　一棟　桁行25.2メートル　梁間左側面8.0メートル　右側面13.0メートル」)

What it is: A municipal cultural-property page on a Zen temple whose main hall and kuri are ONE building of the early Edo period, with its dimensions.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the one attested example of the hall-and-dwelling under one roof - the GM's form for the country shrine - and its measurements, 25.2 m long by 8.0 to 13.0 m deep, which give the one-roof band; and for the page's own word that the combination was unusual for its time. Its limits: one building, called rare by the page that records it; an urban Zen temple in the port city of Sakai, with a designated gate corridor of its own - not a country shrine or a village parish temple; a Buddhist hall-and-kuri rather than a shrine's hall and a monk's house, so the fused shrine form rests on this record's jingūji finding and not on this building; and at 25.2 m long a large building, so a band set from it makes the country shrine two to three farmhouses on the authority of one city example.

Used for: hall and kuri under one roof, 25.2 by 8.0-13.0 m, called rare (religion-and-death.html).
