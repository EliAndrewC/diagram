shirobito-612-goten

Kamiyu Rekishi Henshūbu, for Shirobito (a site endorsed by the Japan Castle Foundation), 超入門！お城セミナー 第45回【構造】御殿には"表"と"奥"があるって本当？ ("Castle seminar for beginners, 45: is it true that a palace had an omote and an oku?", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://shirobito.jp/article/612)

What it is: A 5 December 2018 introductory article by Kamiyu Rekishi Henshūbu, a commercial history-content editing house, on Shirobito, a castle-fan site endorsed (not run) by the Japan Castle Foundation, explaining what a castle palace (goten) was for, why it could stand in a bailey other than the honmaru, and its division into a public and a private side.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states in one place the palace's two roles (seat of government and the lord's residence), the omote/oku split, and that in hill-and-plain castles the palace was often built in the wider second or third bailey. It is a general-audience explainer with no references, written around the Nagoya palace reconstruction, so it is used for the palace's shape and placement, not for any figure. It describes Japanese castles from the late sixteenth century through the Edo period only: the article itself says medieval castles had no palace and the lord governed from a residence apart from the castle, and it says nothing of a Chinese-modeled seat of government.

Used for: what a castle palace was and where in the castle it stood (cities/capitals)
