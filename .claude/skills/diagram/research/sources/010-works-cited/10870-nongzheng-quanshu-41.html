Xu Guangqi (徐光啟), Nongzheng quanshu (農政全書, Complete Treatise on Agricultural Administration), chapter 41, "Herding and rearing" (牧養), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E8%BE%B2%E6%94%BF%E5%85%A8%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B741)

What it is: The livestock-and-fish chapter of the great agricultural compendium of the late Ming, printed in 1639, which gathers older texts on each animal and adds its compiler's own remarks, signed as "Master Xuanhu".

Why it applies, and its limits: In his own remarks the compiler advises penning sheep on the bank of a fish pond and sweeping their dung into the water to feed the carp - the earliest statement read of a livestock pen standing on a pond bank so that its waste feeds the fish. He wrote from the Yangtze delta, not the Pearl River delta, of sheep rather than pigs or ducks, and as advice, not as a count of how many did it; the chapter tells it twice with a small difference in which fish the dung feeds.

Used for: a late-Ming instruction to pen livestock on a fish-pond bank so that the dung feeds the fish (archetypes)
