suido-ishizue-tanada

ARIC (Association of Rural Information and Communication), 水土の礎 ("Foundations of Water and Land", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 耕して天に至る棚田・段畑 ("Terraced paddies and terraced fields, tilled up to the sky", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/01/09.html)

What it is: A page of a history of Japan's farmland and water works published by a public-interest foundation of the land-improvement sector, on terraced paddies and fields: who opened them, with what, and what the plots were like.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the terraces were small hand developments by the farmers themselves from the later middle ages on, and that each paddy was narrow and irregular, which is the hill-terrace form and cell this record draws. It is a general-audience history with no footnotes, written from the sector's own standpoint; it describes Japan only, and gives no dimensions.

Used for: terraces opened by hand with the hoe, each paddy small and irregular (archetypes)
