rekilabo-shokeijo

歴史文化探訪ラボ (rekilabo), 江戸時代の処刑場が人通りの多い街道沿いにわざわざ設置されたワケ (https://rekilabo.com/syokeijyo/)

What it is: A popular-history web article on why Edo's execution grounds were deliberately set along the busy highways into the city, naming Suzugamori on the Tokaido and Kozukappara on the Nikko road.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the siting and gives the shogunate's motive as deterrence, which is what this page's rule about the ground standing on the busiest way rests on. Its limits are that it is a popular site signed only with the site's name, with no citations a reader can follow (it names the 公事方御定書 of 1742 in its text), and that the motive is its reading of the record rather than a quoted ordinance.

Used for: the grounds at the highway entrances, passed by everyone entering, as a deterrent
