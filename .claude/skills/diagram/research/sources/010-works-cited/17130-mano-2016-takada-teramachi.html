mano-2016-takada-teramachi

Shinno Toshikazu (真野俊和, romanized SHINNO on the paper's title page), 近世城下町における寺町と寺院 越後国高田寺町の場合 ("Temple quarters and temples in the early modern castle town: the case of the Takada teramachi in Echigo", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 頸城野郷土資料室学術研究部研究紀要 1(1), 2016, pp. 1-25 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kfa/1/1/1_1/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: An open-access research paper by a folklorist (formerly a professor at Jōetsu University of Education and the University of Tsukuba), in the first issue of the research bulletin of a local-history NPO in Jōetsu rather than a peer-reviewed journal, on the temple quarter of the castle town of Takada (Echigo), asking what the quarter did for the town: faith and pilgrimage, entertainment, and social welfare.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that the defensive reading of the temple quarter is the near-settled view, names the works that hold it and the one that has objected, and gives the author's own doubt for Takada, whose plan left the highway nearly undefended. The doubt is argued for one town and the author says it is too little to generalize, so it makes the defensive purpose contested rather than refuted. It reports the settled view (Harada 1983, Toyoda 1983) and the objection (Nakanishi 1998) at second hand. In a note the author grants that temples did serve in war (Aizu's temples in the Boshin War, Nobunaga's Honnōji), but calls this unforeseen and a separate question from whether they were placed for it. It speaks for Japanese castle towns only, not for Chinese cities.

Used for: whether the temple quarter was placed for defense (cities/capitals)
