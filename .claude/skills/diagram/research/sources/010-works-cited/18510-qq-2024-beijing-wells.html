差评XPIN, 从井水到自来水，14亿人的喝水问题是如何解决的？ ("From well water to tap water: how 1.4 billion people's drinking water was solved", title translated), Tencent News, 2024-07-22 (in Chinese; https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20240722A07CF500)

What it is: A popular article on the history of China's drinking water that gives, from the Qing gazetteers, the number of wells in Beijing at the end of the Qing (1,228, from the Jingshi fangxiang zhi) and the city's official population then (about 800,000, from the Guangxu Shuntian fu zhi), and divides the one by the other to reach at least 652 persons to a well.

Why it applies, and its limits: Both counts are premodern records of a premodern city, which is what the well's capacity needs in place of a modern relief standard. The article is by a technology writer, not a historian; the division is its own; it cites no page, and its well count cannot be checked here - another retelling of the same gazetteer (Zhu Yixin's Jingshi fangxiang zhigao, c. 1885) gives 701 wells in the inner city and 557 in the outer, 1,258 in all, which makes about 636 persons to a well - so the figure holds as "roughly 650", not as an exact count; the population counted only registered residents, so the true figure per well was higher; and Beijing is a capital where much water was carried from sweet-water wells by water sellers, so it shows that a well COULD serve hundreds, not how many a village well served.

Used for: the persons to a well in late-Qing Beijing (urban-features)
