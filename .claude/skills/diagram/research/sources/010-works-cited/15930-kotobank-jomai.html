kotobank-jomai

Kotobank, s.v. 城米 ("jomai, castle rice", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the entries of the デジタル大辞泉 (Digital Daijisen), 精選版 日本国語大辞典 (Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, selected edition), 改訂新版 世界大百科事典 (Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, revised edition; its entry signed by the historian 大野瑞男 Ōno Mizuo) and 百科事典マイペディア (Heibonsha Mypedia) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9F%8E%E7%B1%B3-533371)

What it is: Dictionary and encyclopedia entries on jomai, the rice the shogunate and the domains stored inside castles against war and famine: where the shogunate's stock was held (its own castles, the fudai lords' castles and the Tōkaidō post stations, placed chiefly in Kantō, Tōkai and the Kinai for shipment to Edo and Osaka; no castle is named), that it was set up in the fudai domains in 1633, that it passed with the castle to the next lord like the castle's arms, and how it was later drawn on in famines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It establishes the overlord's reserve rice as a thing kept INSIDE castles and bound to them, which is the contrast that explains why a working granary stood elsewhere, and it names the castle's own arms as the same kind of castle-bound holding. It is reference-work summary, and it describes the shogunate's system of stocking its vassals' castles, an arrangement between overlord and vassal that the setting's own notes do not describe for the Emperor. Even in Japan, "inside the castle" is the narrow sense only: part of the stock sat at the Tōkaidō post stations, and in the broad sense 城米 means all the tax rice of the shogunate's own lands. The castle-bound war reserve is also the seventeenth-century arrangement; from 1689 some was sold for the shogunate's finances, and later it went to famine relief and price control.

Used for: reserve rice kept inside the castle against war and famine; the castles that held the shogunate's stock; castle rice handed on with the castle like its arms (cities/capitals)
