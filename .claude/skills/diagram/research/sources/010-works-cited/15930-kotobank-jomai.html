kotobank-jomai

Kotobank, s.v. 城米 ("jomai, castle rice", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the entries of the デジタル大辞泉 (Digital Daijisen), 精選版 日本国語大辞典 (Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, selected edition) and 改訂新版 世界大百科事典 (Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, revised edition) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9F%8E%E7%B1%B3-533371)

What it is: Dictionary and encyclopedia entries on jomai, the rice the shogunate and the domains stored inside castles against war and famine: which castles held the shogunate's stock, that it was set up in the fudai domains in 1633, that it passed with the castle to the next lord like the castle's arms, and how it was later drawn on in famines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It establishes the overlord's reserve rice as a thing kept INSIDE castles and bound to them, which is the contrast that explains why a working granary stood elsewhere, and it names the castle's own arms as the same kind of castle-bound holding. It is reference-work summary, and it describes the shogunate's system of stocking its vassals' castles, an arrangement between overlord and vassal that the setting's own notes do not describe for the Emperor.

Used for: reserve rice kept inside the castle against war and famine; the castles that held the shogunate's stock; castle rice handed on with the castle like its arms (cities/capitals)
