ranzan-kamagata-hachiman

Ranzan Town web museum (嵐山町web博物誌), 比企郡神社明細帳, 八幡神社 菅谷村大字鎌形 (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/09jin14.html)

What it is: The Ranzan town office's transcription of the Hiki district shrine register entry (in the Saitama prefecture's 1963 copy) for the Hachiman Shrine of Kamagata, Saitama, a district shrine from 1927, with its precinct, parishioners and history, and an editors' footnote quoting the Council of State's district-shrine rules of 1871.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives one district shrine's precinct (3,373 tsubo, remeasured in 1948 and 1950) and the 1871 rule that set one district shrine per household-register district, giving as its example some twenty villages of about a thousand households with five shrines, the foremost of which became the district shrine. Its limits: the district-shrine rank is a Meiji state category (1871-1946) with no Edo counterpart, and this shrine rose to it only in 1927; its precinct was enlarged in 1907 by some 1,300 tsubo of forest the state had confiscated after 1868, it took in three moved shrines in 1908, and its size and parish (195 households) are post-war figures, so it is a 20th-century village shrine, not an Edo town shrine - its Edo trace is the Tokugawa grant of 20 koku in 1591; the 1871 rule is quoted in the editors' footnote, not in the register, and its twenty villages are the rule's example, not its measure; one district.
