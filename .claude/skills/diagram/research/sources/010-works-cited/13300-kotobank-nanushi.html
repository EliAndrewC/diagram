kotobank-nanushi

Kotobank, 名主 ("nanushi, the village headman", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, revised edition, entry by Kimura Motoi among others (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%90%8D%E4%B8%BB)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the nanushi, carrying several published Japanese encyclopedias and dictionaries; the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry on the Edo-period office sets out the headman's standing, privileges and duties, and the page also carries entries on the medieval myoshu written with the same characters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that a headman, though a peasant, could build a gate and a formal entrance step and did the village's business at home, where the other officials met - the privileges and the office work behind the headman's homestead on our maps. Its limits: tertiary encyclopedia entries on Tokugawa Japan in general, with no regional count of how many headmen used the right to a gate; and the page mixes the Edo headman with the medieval myoshu, so a passage must be read against its headword.

Used for: the headman's right to a gate and a formal entrance, and the office work done in the headman's house (homesteads)
