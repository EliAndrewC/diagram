yuya-2007-kanazawa-shaso

Yuya Hiroya 2007, 「Domain-period shrines and shrine monks as told after the separation of kami and Buddhas: examples from the former Kanazawa city area」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「神仏分離後に語られた藩政期の神社と社僧 : 旧金沢市域の例から」), Journal of Religious Studies (宗教研究) 81(2), 437-461, doi:10.20716/rsjars.81.2_437 (in Japanese; abstract at https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/rsjars/81/2/81_KJ00004718210/_article/-char/ja)

What it is: A peer-reviewed paper in the journal of the Japanese Association for Religious Studies on the shrines of the Kanazawa area, the Kaga domain's castle town, and on how their shrine monks were described after 1868; cited here for its abstract's words only.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its abstract states the received view for Kanazawa - that shrines served by lay priests were rare in the domain period, and that the many shrine monks and mountain ascetics of the late Edo period became Shinto priests after Meiji. It is one castle town's area, not the countryside or the country, and the abstract presents Kanazawa as a place where, if anything, lay-priest shrines were rare - a distinctive case, not the norm for Japan. It reports the received view, and the paper itself questions part of it: one Meiji-era local historian, Morita Heiji (1823-1908), described monks and ascetics who had returned to lay life as domain-period shrine monks, and so made more than twenty institutions that cannot clearly be called shrine or temple into shrines without lay priests. That inflates the very rarity of lay-priest shrines we cite it for.
