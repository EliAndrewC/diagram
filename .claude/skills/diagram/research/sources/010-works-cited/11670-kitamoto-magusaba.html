kitamoto-magusaba

北本市史 通史編 近世 ("Kitamoto City History, general volume, early modern", title translated from the Japanese by this project), chapter 2 section 2, 秣場と論争 ("the fodder grounds and the disputes", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on the Kitamoto digital archives (in Japanese; https://kdas.jp/detail_display.php?t_cd=1&acc_cd=1&aclc_cd=40&chap=2&hp_page=19&bc_cd=1)

What it is: A page of the city history of Kitamoto (Saitama) on the fodder grounds of its Edo-period villages: what the fodder was and how it was used, and the disputes of the Genroku era (1688-1704) as new fields shrank the commons.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in so many words that the fodder - the undergrowth and young branches of woods and wild land - was put into the stable as the horse's feed, where the horse trod it into manure, and that villages short of grass turned to the river's floodplain - the use and the siting of the commons in one plain. It is a modern municipal history, reading the villages' own documents, of one district of the Kanto plain. And the floodplain it names (the Arakawa's Upper and Lower Marsh) was a commons in which several villages claimed rights and fought over them, a late resort as new fields shrank their own grounds: it is evidence for a riverbed fodder ground shared among villages, not for one village's own meadow.

Used for: fodder cut and fed in the stable, and the riverbed as a village's fodder ground (vegetation)
