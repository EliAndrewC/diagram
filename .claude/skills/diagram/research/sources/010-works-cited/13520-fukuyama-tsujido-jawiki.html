ja.wikipedia 福山藩の辻堂 ("the tsujidō of the Fukuyama domain", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A6%8F%E5%B1%B1%E8%97%A9%E3%81%AE%E8%BE%BB%E5%A0%82)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the small open wayside halls of the Fukuyama domain in Bingo (eastern Hiroshima): their form, where they stood, how many there were, and the period records that listed them - a land survey of 1700, taken while the domain was briefly under the shogunate's direct rule, and the village returns of 1711 - which the article names but does not tabulate.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a rate - about one hall for every twenty to thirty households, kept by the settlement - and a form and size, four posts under a tiled roof over a floor of about two mats, which is the only per-household count of wayside shrines read. Its limits: it is a tertiary article; the tiled roof is the halls' form today, and it cites histories saying many were once thatched; the form and the rate are both regional, concentrated in Bingo and southern Bitchū and rare elsewhere, as the article itself says (a gazetteer of 1809 already called them rare in other provinces), so one hall for every twenty to thirty households is the density of the one district where these halls were thickest, not a general Japanese rate; the article takes that rate from a prefectural culture encyclopedia and gives no survey or year behind it; and the halls began as rest-houses for travelers that later took in jizo, so they are a wayside shrine by adoption.

Used for: a wayside hall for every twenty to thirty households, its form and its floor (religion-and-death)
