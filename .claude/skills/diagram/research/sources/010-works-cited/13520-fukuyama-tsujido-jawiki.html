ja.wikipedia 福山藩の辻堂 ("the tsujidō of the Fukuyama domain", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A6%8F%E5%B1%B1%E8%97%A9%E3%81%AE%E8%BE%BB%E5%A0%82)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the small open wayside halls of the Fukuyama domain in Bingo (eastern Hiroshima): their form, where they stood, how many there were, and the domain surveys of 1700 and 1711 that counted them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a rate - about one hall for every twenty to thirty households, kept by the settlement - and a form and size, four posts under a tiled roof over a floor of about two mats, which is the only per-household count of wayside shrines read. Its limits: it is a tertiary article; the tiled roof is the halls' form today, and it cites histories saying many were once thatched; the form is regional, concentrated in Bingo and southern Bitchū and rare elsewhere, as the article itself says; and the halls began as rest-houses for travelers that later took in jizo, so they are a wayside shrine by adoption.

Used for: a wayside hall for every twenty to thirty households, its form and its floor (religion-and-death)
