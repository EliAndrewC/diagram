jsidre-senmaida

"The Shiroyone Senmaida, the terraced paddies that represent the Noto satoyama and satoumi agricultural heritage site" (title translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「世界農業遺産「能登の里山里海」を代表する棚田「白米の千枚田」」), 水土の知 (Journal of the Japanese Society of Irrigation, Drainage and Rural Engineering) 90(1) (https://www.jsidre.or.jp/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/90-1-18.pdf)

What it is: A two-page cover-photograph column by Hotta Taku of Ishikawa Prefecture's agricultural infrastructure division, in the journal of the Japanese Society of Irrigation, Drainage and Rural Engineering, on the Shiroyone Senmaida - the terraced paddies on the Noto peninsula that represent the Noto satoyama and satoumi agricultural heritage site.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the Senmaida figures our smallest-paddy finding rests on - about 18 square meters per basin, and 1,004 terraced paddies spread over about 4 hectares in all - and it states the reason for them, that no large machine can enter the terrace so the rice is still grown by hand with hoe and sickle. Its limits are the terrace's and the column's. Shiroyone is an extreme case, its basins made tiny by a steep slope worked entirely by hand, so it gives a floor and never a typical size. The figures carry no survey behind them - they are the official numbers the preservation council also publishes, restated in a journal, so the container is not better provenance than the source. And the terrace it describes is not an ancient one: the column dates the present layout to the first half of the Meiji period, the ground having been abandoned after a landslide in 1684 and an earthquake in 1729.

Used for: the Shiroyone Senmaida figures - about 18 m2 per paddy, 1,004 paddies over about 4 ha - from an engineering journal rather than a tourism page
