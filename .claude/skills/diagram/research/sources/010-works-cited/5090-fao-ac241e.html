FAO/NACA, The Integration of Mulberry Cultivation, Sericulture and Fish Farming (https://www.fao.org/4/ac241e/ac241e00.htm)

What it is: The Integration of Mulberry Cultivation, Sericulture and Fish Farming, a 1984 working paper by Hu Bao-tong and Yang Hua-zhu for the Network of Aquaculture Centres in Asia, hosted by the UN Food and Agriculture Organization. It describes the dike-and-pond farming of two Chinese regions, the Pearl River delta and the Lake Tai basin near Suzhou, as practiced on the collective farms of the early 1980s: mulberry grown on raised dikes, silkworms fed on the leaves, and the worm waste fed to fish in the ponds between, with worked examples and farmers' rules of thumb for the proportions.

Why it applies, and its limits: This is the same mulberry-dike-fishpond system our maps draw, and the area proportion it reports - roughly one unit of dike to one of water where the worms alone feed the fish, six or seven to four or three with other feed - follows from the feed cycle itself rather than from any modern input, so it describes the shape of a landscape that had been worked that way for centuries. The limits are that it is a 1980s development report on collective-era Chinese practice, not a historical record; that the pond depth it gives (3.5 m, drawn down in summer) reflects modern stocking and modern earth-moving and is the least premodern of its numbers; that its figures are two worked examples and a rule of thumb, which the paper itself says is "governed by traditional practice" in the field, not regional averages; and that China stands in for a setting modeled as much on Japan.

Used for: the dike:water ratio - "each mu of mulberry plants can yield worm dregs, pupae and waste water to provide feeds and fertilizers sufficient for 1 mu of fish ponds. Hence, the ratio of areas of dike and water surface is 1:1", and "the water/dike ratio is 6:4" where other feed is added; pond depth 3.5 m falling to 2.5 m in summer with a 1.5 m slope to the dike top
