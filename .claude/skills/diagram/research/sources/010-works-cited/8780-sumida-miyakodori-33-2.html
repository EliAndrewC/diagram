sumida-miyakodori-33-2

墨田区 (Sumida City, Tokyo), 『みやこどり』 no. 33-2, the ward's buried-cultural-property bulletin, on the graves of Honjo (https://www.city.sumida.lg.jp/kosodate_kyouiku/tiiki_kyouiku_shien/bunkazai_hogo/maizoubunkazai/index.files/miyakodori-33-2.pdf)

What it is: A numbered issue of the buried-cultural-property bulletin published by Sumida ward in Tokyo, reporting the excavated graves of the Honjo district and beginning with the 1985 dig at the Fugenji site, a branch temple of Sensoji that moved away after the Great Tokyo Earthquake.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only measured grave DENSITY this record has: a trench of just 40 m2 held the traces of ten graves, one box coffin and nine barrel coffins, and the bulletin draws the conclusion itself, that a great many graves were made within one narrow burial ground. That is about 4 m2 a grave, which is the check on how tightly a burial ground packs - but a cumulative one: the ten graves were laid into that ground over the temple's whole period of use, in a district the same bulletin describes as layered, coffins stacked several deep, over two hundred years. So the figure is a FLOOR on the ground a graveyard needs and not its packing at any one moment. Its limits are that 40 m2 is the excavation trench and not the graveyard, so the density is local rather than whole-ground; that the graves are coffined body burials rather than the cremated urn plots these maps draw, so the figure corroborates the Chinese full-body side rather than the Japanese urn band; and that a ward bulletin reports one site rather than a distribution.

Used for: ten graves in a 40 m2 trench at an Edo commoner temple graveyard - about 4 m2, some 43 sq ft, a grave
