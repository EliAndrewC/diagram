Carol H. Shiue, "Transport Costs and the Geography of Arbitrage in Eighteenth-Century China", American Economic Review 92(5): 1406-1419, December 2002 (author's copy) (https://spot.colorado.edu/~shiue/p1406_s.pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed economic-history paper using Qing archival grain-price lists to measure how far markets were integrated in eighteenth-century China, with a survey of what carriage cost by water and by land and how the major waterways connected the cities.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the size of the water-over-land cost advantage in premodern China from the scholarly literature, which is the ground for drawing a towpath and a wharf rather than a river road. Its limit is period and place: eighteenth-century China, with the figure taken from an earlier study the paper cites rather than measured afresh.

Used for: the nine- to tenfold cost advantage of junks over land carriage
