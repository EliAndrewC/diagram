Beijing Evening News, 菜园街与南菜园街 "菜园"遗迹 ("Caiyuan Street and South Caiyuan Street: traces of the vegetable gardens", translated), 27 November 2017, on the Beijing tourism bureau's site (https://www.visitbeijing.com.cn/article/47QsCmrXIS9)

What it is: A local-history newspaper article on two Beijing streets named for vegetable gardens, tracing the gardens from the Jin and Yuan through the Ming office that managed the court's vegetable farmers, and saying which city wall came to enclose them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates vegetable land inside a Chinese city's wall to the Ming: the outer wall built in the Jiajing years enclosed garden land the Yuan had opened. It is a newspaper's local history, not a study, citing the Qing compilation Rixia jiuwen kao for one site; and it puts the court's own vegetable office and most of its farms outside the Guang'anmen gate, so it does not show that the court's farms lay inside the wall. The enclosure itself is the article's own statement, with no source given for it. It also concerns the imperial capital's outer wall, added in the Jiajing years around suburbs and fields already there, so it does not show that a county seat's or an ordinary town's wall enclosed garden land.

Used for: vegetable land enclosed by Beijing's Ming outer wall (cities/hinterland)
