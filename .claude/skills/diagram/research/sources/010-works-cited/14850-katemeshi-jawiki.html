katemeshi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia, かて飯 (katemeshi, rice stretched with other grain and vegetables) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%B3%85%E9%A3%AF)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on katemeshi, the everyday rice of the countryside cooked with barley, millet, tubers, radish and greens to make the rice go further, with regional examples drawn from a folklore dictionary of food and the prefectural oral-history series on traditional diets.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the share of rice in the everyday grain by kind of district - even paddy districts mixing in large amounts, dry-field districts eating millet and barley with a little rice, the Kanto dry-field belt eating rice that was 70 to 80 percent barley, and one paddy district cooking about two parts rice to half a part barley - which brackets how much of a farmer's grain was not rice. It is a tertiary summary: its figures trace to a folklore dictionary of food (2013) and the prefectural oral-history volumes (1988-90), which record the diets of about 1930 as recalled in the 1980s, so they describe farming after the Meiji-era changes and are not an Edo-period count; the article says only that most could not eat plain rice as a staple until at least 1955-64, and it carries a notice that it needs sources; it gives the mix in the pot, not a share of the whole diet.

Used for: the share of rice in the everyday grain by kind of district (fields)
