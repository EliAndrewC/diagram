adachi-matsue-bukeyashiki

足立正智 (Adachi Masatomo), "'Buke yashiki' soken-ji no sugata o saguru" (Seeking the samurai house as first built), Shimane University (in Japanese; https://coc.lib.shimane-u.ac.jp/files/attach/1/1504/SMC002-07.pdf)

What it is: An architectural survey of the Matsue samurai house on the Shiomi Nawate street, a middle-ranking retainer's house: its site and buildings, the restorations of 1969 and later, and the alterations a partial survey of 2012 found in the main house (measured drawings throughout, the roof space and underfloor examined at its west end only); it leaves the plan as first built unrestored, for want of further survey.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a measured survey that says which buildings on the site are old and which later, so it can place the well beside the main house and set aside the privy now in the grounds as a later building. It is one house in one castle town, of a house the domain lent out and whose occupants changed, shown as the Ujiie's on a castle-town map of 1825-1851 and, the author supposes, theirs until the end of the shogunate (the family's record runs to 1866); their ninth head had 550 koku as a guard commander (bangashira); the author calls it a middle-ranking retainer's house, on a street he puts at middle to upper-middle, and its building date is unknown: the survey of the roof space found no ridge tag, a pamphlet says it was rebuilt after the fire of 1733, and the author judges from the timber that it is under 200 years old, so late Edo. It dates the shelter over the well as comparatively recent without dating the well itself, and calls the well's place east of the house only plausible beside the kitchen and bath, since none of the drains it would have had survive.

Used for: the well on the east side of a samurai house, and the later date of the privy in its grounds (Estates and other compounds)
