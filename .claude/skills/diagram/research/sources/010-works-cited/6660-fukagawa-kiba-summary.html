fukagawa-kiba-summary

Fukagawa kiba, the Edo lumber yards - unsupported by any readable page: fifteen timber wholesalers bought ~300,000 m2 in 1701; the yards were ordered out of the city after the 1657 Meireki fire because in-town timber yards spread fire (https://www.mokuzai-tonya.jp/03about_us/history/index.html)

What it is: The history page of a Japanese timber wholesalers' trade body, recounting the origin of Kiba, the lumber quarter of Fukagawa in Edo: after the great fire of 1641 the spread of the flames was blamed on the high timber stacks of merchants scattered through the city, the merchants were gathered onto Eitai-jima, another great fire followed in 1657, and Kiba Fukagawa-cho was laid out in 1703 over some 90,000 tsubo of made ground.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives us the thing we want to draw - a lumber yard as a large works on the water, deliberately pushed out of the built-up core because stacked timber carries fire through a wooden town - and it is a Japanese account of a Japanese quarter. The limits are that it is an industry body's own promotional history rather than a scholarly one, with no references a reader can follow, and that it gives an area for the whole quarter and none for a single yard, so the size of the yard on our maps is our own estimate.

Used for: the lumber yard as a waterfront works far larger than a shophouse, sited out of the built core
