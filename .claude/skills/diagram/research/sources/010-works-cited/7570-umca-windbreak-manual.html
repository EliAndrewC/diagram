umca-windbreak-manual

University of Missouri Center for Agroforestry, Training Manual for Applied Agroforestry Practices, 2024 edition, ch. 6 "Windbreaks" (https://centerforagroforestry.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/06_Windbreaks_TrainingManual_2024Master_0410_Digital-7.pdf)

What it is: The windbreak chapter of a present-day agroforestry training manual: a practitioner’s guide to designing field, farmstead and livestock shelter belts - how many rows, how tall, how porous, how far apart, and what a break in a planted run does to the wind.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states what a hole in an occupied belt does - the gap funnels the wind, and the air downwind of it often moves faster than over the open field - which is the physics behind this project’s rule that a bare gap is a defect rather than a neutral feature. It also gives the remedy an access crossing actually gets: the opening cut at an angle to the prevailing wind, and the lane placed 100 to 500 feet from the belt’s end - a placement the manual states for a SINGLE-ROW barrier, which a homestead grove of several rows is not. Its limits are that it is modern United States extension advice for machine-farmed fields of conifer and hardwood rows. The aerodynamics hold in any century; the design practice does not, and it is no evidence that a Chinese or Japanese planter angled a crossing. It gives no width for an opening, which is why this project’s 30 ft threshold stays a drawing convention.

Used for: what a gap in a shelter belt does to the wind, and the attested remedy for an access crossing
