What it is: A newspaper article from 黄山日报 (the Huangshan Daily), "林长制的黄山探索" ("Huangshan's exploration of the forest-chief system"), dated 1 January 2022 and mirrored on a Yangtze River Delta party news site. It reports on a modern forest-stewardship program in Anhui province, and in passing describes the water-mouth grove standing at the entrance to Jiekou village in Shexian.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for one concrete measure of a water-mouth grove - over a hundred mu (roughly seven hectares), nearly a thousand old trees, and fifty-six yews more than five centuries old - and the record treats that as the extreme end of the range rather than the ordinary case; a stand that has been kept at a village mouth for that long is evidence about the form even though the article around it is current news. Its limits: it is journalism rather than scholarship and it is promotional about a government program; the numbers are the reporter's, unsourced; the article is in Chinese and we read it in machine translation, so the wording is not the newspaper's own English; and it describes the largest example anyone in the region can name, so nothing in it should be read as typical.

Used for: the Jiekou (Shexian) water-mouth grove - over a hundred mu with nearly a thousand ancient trees and 56 yews over 500 years old, described as the largest in East China: the tail, not the type
