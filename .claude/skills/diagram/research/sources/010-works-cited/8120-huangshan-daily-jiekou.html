黄山日报 (Huangshan Daily), 「Huangshan's exploration of the forest-chief system」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「林长制的黄山探索」), 2022-01-01, mirrored by the Yangtze River Delta dual-carbon news site (长三角双碳网) of the Anhui Yangtze River Delta Dual-Carbon Development Research Institute (http://www.yrdcpcn.org.cn/c189/20240913/i238685.phtml; journalism, not scholarship) - read in the Chinese original

What it is: A newspaper article from 黄山日报 (the Huangshan Daily), "林长制的黄山探索" ("Huangshan's exploration of the forest-chief system"), by staff reporter 许晟 (Xu Sheng), dated 1 January 2022 and mirrored on the Yangtze River Delta "dual-carbon" (carbon-peak and carbon-neutrality) news site run by the Anhui Yangtze River Delta Dual-Carbon Development Research Institute, a policy research institute. It reports on a modern forest-stewardship program in Anhui province, and in passing describes the water-mouth grove at the head of Xin'anyuan village in Hecheng Town, Xiuning County - the article opens on Jiekou in Shexian, but the grove is in its account of Xin'anyuan.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for one concrete measure of a water-mouth grove - over a hundred mu (roughly seven hectares), nearly a thousand old trees, and fifty-six yews more than five centuries old - and the record treats that as the extreme end of the range rather than the ordinary case; a stand that has been kept at a village mouth for that long is evidence about the form even though the article around it is current news. Its limits: it is journalism rather than scholarship and it is promotional about a government program; the numbers are the reporter's, unsourced; the article is in Chinese, and the passages the record quotes are this project's own translation; the grove is measured as it stands in 2022, not by any survey from the period: the village is a provincial "forest village" beside the Liugujian provincial nature reserve and its yews carry national protection, so the hundred mu is what survives under modern protection, not a record of the grove's premodern extent; the village sits at the Xin'an River's mountain source with its land 95% forested, where woodland cost a village far less than on a farmed plain; and the reporter calls it the largest, most varied and oldest water-mouth grove in East China, so nothing in it should be read as typical.

Used for: the Xin'anyuan (Xiuning) water-mouth grove - over a hundred mu with nearly a thousand ancient trees and 56 yews over 500 years old, described as the largest in East China: the tail, not the type
