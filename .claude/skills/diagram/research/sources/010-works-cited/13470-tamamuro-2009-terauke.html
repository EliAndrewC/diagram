Tamamuro Fumio, "The Development of the Temple-Parishioner System", translated by Duncan Williams, Japanese Journal of Religious Studies 36/1 (2009), pp. 11-26 (https://nirc.nanzan-u.ac.jp/journal/6/article/1268/pdf/download)

What it is: A journal article by a leading historian of Tokugawa Buddhism, in English translation, tracing in five stages how every Japanese household came to be registered with a Buddhist temple in the seventeenth century, published open-access by the Nanzan Institute for Religion and Culture.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in so many words that when certification was demanded not every one of Japan's close to 65,000 villages had a temple that could take parishioners, that households then chose a temple by nearness, and that village halls were raised to temples to fill the gap - which is exactly whether a village kept a temple of its own. Its limits: it is a national synthesis that gives no count of villages with and without a temple, and it speaks of the seventeenth-century founding rather than of any one region's settled pattern.

Used for: not every village keeping a temple; households choosing a temple by nearness; village halls raised to temples (religion-and-death)
