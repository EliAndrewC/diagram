tamamuro-2009-terauke

Tamamuro Fumio, "The Development of the Temple-Parishioner System", translated by Duncan Williams, Japanese Journal of Religious Studies 36/1 (2009), pp. 11-26 (https://nirc.nanzan-u.ac.jp/journal/6/article/1268/pdf/download)

What it is: A journal article by a leading historian of Tokugawa Buddhism, in English translation, tracing in five stages, from the late medieval period to after 1700, how every Japanese household came to be bound to a Buddhist temple, a binding made compulsory in the seventeenth century by the shogunate's anti-Christian temple registration, published open-access by the Nanzan Institute for Religion and Culture.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in so many words that when certification was demanded not every one of Japan's close to 65,000 villages had a temple that could take parishioners, that households then chose a temple by nearness, and that village halls were raised to temples to fill the gap - which is exactly whether a village kept a temple of its own. Its limits: it is a national synthesis that gives no count of villages with and without a temple and cites no document for these passages, and it speaks of the seventeenth-century founding rather than of any one region's settled pattern. The villages without a temple are Japan at the moment compulsory registration was imposed, before the rush of foundings that followed (it says nearly seventy percent of the temples standing today were founded then), so it tells us villages lacked temples before that rush, not after it. The choosing by nearness (by "many" parishioners, not all) and the raising of halls were answers to that registration, a Japanese state measure with no counterpart in imperial China, of which it says nothing.

Used for: not every village keeping a temple; households choosing a temple by nearness; village halls raised to temples (religion-and-death)
