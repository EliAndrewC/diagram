artic-pigsty-latrine

Art Institute of Chicago, "Model of a Pigsty and Latrines", Eastern Han, object 37716 - (https://www.artic.edu/artworks/37716)

What it is: The Art Institute of Chicago's catalog entry for a Han dynasty tomb model of a pigsty and latrine - a gray earthenware miniature of about 40 by 22 by 28 cm, dated 100 BCE to 100 CE - whose curatorial note records that latrines were customarily built above a pigsty and connected by pipes to a cesspool, comparing the form to models excavated at Huixian. The museum's own page may not open, but its catalog text is public.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives one attested customary structure - the privy over the sty, the muck collected beside it - which is the placement rule that keeps the muck heap and the privy together on our homesteads. Its limits are that it is a catalog note rather than an excavation report; that a tomb model is an idealized miniature; that it is Han China, far earlier than the setting's Japan-leaning period; and that farm-scale pig keeping is a Chinese practice rather than an Edo Japanese one, so what transfers to a Japan-leaning map is the privy-and-muck adjacency, not the sty itself.

Used for: the Han pigsty-privy as one customary structure - the muck clusters with the privy.
