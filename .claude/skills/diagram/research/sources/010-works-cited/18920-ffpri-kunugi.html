ffpri-kunugi

Forestry and Forest Products Research Institute, 自然探訪2012年2月 クヌギ ("Nature walk, February 2012: kunugi", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.ffpri.go.jp/snap/2012/2-kunugi.html)

What it is: A short popular page by Japan's national forestry research institute on the kunugi oak, its use for charcoal, the coppice cycle it is cut on, and the daiba kunugi of northern Osaka, quoting a late-Edo farm book.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes, in the original, the passage of the Koeki Kokusanko, a farm book of the late Edo period, on kunugi branching from about one jo up and cut in winter for Ikeda charcoal, which is premodern evidence for a cut-over oak wood of many thin stems. It is a popular page, not a study; the seven-to-ten-year cycle it gives is present-day coppice practice for charcoal kunugi, and the book it quotes describes one charcoal district, not woods in general.

Used for: a late-Edo farm book on kunugi branches cut in winter for charcoal (towns)
