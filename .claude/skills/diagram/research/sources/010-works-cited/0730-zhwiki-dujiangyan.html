zhwiki-dujiangyan

zh.wikipedia 都江堰 (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/都江堰)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Dujiangyan, the irrigation works built across the Min River in 256 BC: the fish-mouth divide that splits the river into an outer channel for floods and an inner channel for irrigation in proportions that shift with the season, the spillway that returns the surplus and the sand to the outer river, and the bottle-neck opening that limits what the inner river takes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the best-known premodern Chinese case of a river divided rather than taken, with the split stated in numbers, and it grounds the rule that the parent stream keeps flowing below an intake. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, that Dujiangyan is a river-scale state work and not a village brook, and that its weirless form is exceptional rather than typical.

Used for: the Min divided into outer (flood) and inner (irrigation) rivers, four to six in spring and six to four in flood; the spillway returning surplus and sand to the outer river; a weirless diversion
