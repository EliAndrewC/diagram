sukumo-charcoal

宿毛市史 近代・現代編 林業 ("Sukumo City History, modern volume, forestry", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on the Sukumo city site (in Japanese; https://www.city.sukumo.kochi.jp/sisi/110601.html)

What it is: A chapter of the city history of Sukumo (Kochi) on its forestry, with a section on charcoal burning that tabulates the kilns and burners of each of its former villages at the end of 1936 and their output month by month.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says charcoal was burned both by specialists and by farmers in the slack season, which kind a village had by how much of its ground was field, and how many kilns stood in each village - the evidence that a farming village burned its own charcoal and how thick its kilns were. Its counts are from 1936, not the Edo period, and it is one district of Shikoku - Tosa, a province that had exported its charcoal since the Edo period. Its villages are the administrative villages of 1936, each several hamlets, so a count of kilns is for a group of hamlets, not for one village of the kind we draw. And those kilns burned for sale - the charcoal was graded at an inspection office and shipped out through the ports to city markets, in years when the state had opened national forest to charcoal burners as depression relief - so they stood thicker than kilns burning only for a village's own hearths would have.

Used for: charcoal as a slack-season side job in field villages, and kilns counted per village (vegetation)
