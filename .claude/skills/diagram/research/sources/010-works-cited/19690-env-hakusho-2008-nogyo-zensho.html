env-hakusho-2008-nogyo-zensho

Ministry of the Environment, Japan, Annual Report on the Environment and the Sound Material-Cycle Society, 2008, the section on the Edo period's use of night soil (in Japanese; https://www.env.go.jp/policy/hakusyo/h20/html/hj08010202.html)

What it is: A chapter of the Japanese government's environment white paper for 2008 on the recycling of the Edo period, which, on the use of night soil in rice and vegetable farming, gives a passage of the Nogyo Zensho (Miyazaki Yasusada's farming manual of 1697) in modern Japanese.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one public page the record has found that renders the Nogyo Zensho's instructions on the manure house and the dung heap, so it is evidence that both were part of Edo farming advice. Its limits: it is a modern paraphrase in a secondary source, not the 1697 text, and names no edition; it dates the book 1696 where its publication was 1697; the manual is advice, not a record of what farms did; and the passage does not say where on the farm the manure house or the heap stood.

Used for: the manure house for night soil and the dung of cattle and horses piled layer on layer, as the Nogyo Zensho advises (homesteads)
