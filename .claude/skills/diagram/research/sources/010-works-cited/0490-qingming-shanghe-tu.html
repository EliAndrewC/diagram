Qingming Shanghe Tu 清明上河図, gate scene (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Along_the_River_During_the_Qingming_Festival)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Along the River During the Qingming Festival (清明上河圖), the early twelfth-century handscroll attributed to Zhang Zeduan, which describes the painting scene by scene - the wheeled wagons and the donkeys and mules moving goods, and the handful of guards at the city gate.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, through an encyclopedia's account of the painting, the scene at a Song city gate: wagons, a crowd of donkeys and mules, a few guards - open trodden ground with traffic standing in it, which is the arrangement our gate yards copy. Its limits are that we read the scroll through a tertiary summary rather than the painting or its scholarship; that it is Song China, not Japan; that the article records a scholarly dispute over whether the city is Kaifeng or an idealized city, so the scene carries no measurement; and that the hitching posts and the absence of a fence or a lawn on our gate yards are our reading of the picture, not statements on the cited page.

Used for: the city-gate cart yard: wagons, a crowd of donkeys and mules, a few guards - open ground with traffic standing in it (hitching posts, and the absence of a fence or a lawn, are our reading of the picture, not the page's words)
