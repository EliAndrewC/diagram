matsui-2014-edo-temples

Matsui Keisuke (松井圭介) 2014, 寺社分布と機能からみた江戸の宗教空間 ("The religious space of Edo seen through the distribution and functions of its temples and shrines"), 地学雑誌 (Journal of Geography) 123(4), 451-471 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jgeography/123/4/123_123.451/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed geography paper on how Edo's temples and shrines were distributed and what they did, including a worked example of one temple's gate-front town houses, drawing on an earlier study by Nishiyama (1983).

Why it applies, and its limits: It calls a temple of about 1,000 tsubo average for Edo, which is a size anchor for an ordinary city temple's precinct. The judgment is made in passing of one Azabu temple, not from a stated count, and Edo, the shogun's capital, was far larger than any provincial city.

Used for: how large an ordinary city temple's precinct was (religion-and-death)
