matsui-2014-edo-temples

Matsui Keisuke (松井圭介) 2014, 寺社分布と機能からみた江戸の宗教空間 ("The religious space of Edo seen through the distribution and functions of its temples and shrines"), 地学雑誌 (Journal of Geography) 123(4), 451-471 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jgeography/123/4/123_123.451/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed geography paper on how Edo's temples and shrines were distributed and what they did, largely a synthesis of earlier studies, including a worked example of one Azabu temple's (Kyōzenji's) gate-front town houses in 1843, drawn from an earlier study by Kaneyuki (2000).

Why it applies, and its limits: It calls a temple of about 1,000 tsubo average for Edo, which is a size anchor for an ordinary city temple's precinct. The judgment is the author's, made in passing of one Azabu temple in 1843, not from a stated count. That 1,000 tsubo is the whole precinct, including the strip along its edges leased out as gate-front town houses (22 households), so the temple's own grounds were smaller. And Edo, the shogun's capital, was far larger than any provincial city, and short enough of land that temples were pushed out to its edges, so a provincial temple's precinct need not match it.

Used for: how large an ordinary city temple's precinct was (religion-and-death)
