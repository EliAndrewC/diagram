ishizue-jugo-yosui

水土の礎 (the agricultural-engineering history site of ARIC, the Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center, 農業農村整備情報総合センター), 水争いの歴史 ―千年の悲願［九頭竜川の用水］ ("A history of water disputes - the thousand-year wish [the canals of the Kuzuryu River]", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://suido-ishizue.jp/nihon/13/03.html)

What it is: A page on the Juugou canal of the Echizen plain, dug in 1110 to water ten villages of one estate, following its division rules from the 1537 regulations under the Asakura through the Edo period, when it served 118 villages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the physics of the split plainly - a small difference in the cross-section where channels part, or one stone thrown in, changes each share - and records that the fix was a rule setting channel width and depth to the bu (about 3 mm; its examples are a width of 6 shaku 1 sun and a depth of 3 shaku 2 sun 1 bu), kept under a supervising official. It is an uncited popular history on a land-improvement information center's site, not the 1537 regulation itself. Its two figures are given as examples without saying which channel or split they belong to, so they show how precise the rule was, not the size of any one channel. The canal is a large estate-scale system rather than one village's ditch.

Used for: channel widths and depths fixed by rule at a split; why a split is sensitive to its cross-section (water)
