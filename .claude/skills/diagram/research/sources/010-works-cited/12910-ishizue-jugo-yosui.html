ishizue-jugo-yosui

水土の礎 (the Japanese Society of Irrigation, Drainage and Rural Engineering's history site), 水争いの歴史 ―千年の悲願［九頭竜川の用水］ ("A history of water disputes - the thousand-year wish [the canals of the Kuzuryu River]", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://suido-ishizue.jp/nihon/13/03.html)

What it is: A page on the Juugou canal of the Echizen plain, dug in 1110 to water ten villages of one estate, following its division rules from the 1537 regulations under the Asakura through the Edo period, when it served 118 villages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the physics of the split plainly - a small difference in the cross-section where channels part, or one stone thrown in, changes each share - and records that the fix was to fix each channel's width and depth to a tenth of an inch under a supervising official. It is a popular history on an engineering society's site, and the canal is a large estate-scale system rather than one village's ditch.

Used for: channel widths and depths fixed by rule at a split; why a split is sensitive to its cross-section (water)
