qimin-yaoshu-yangyu

賈思勰 齊民要術 (Jia Sixie, Qimin Yaoshu, c. 540 AD) ("Essential Techniques for the Common People", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 卷第六 ("Volume 6", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 養魚第六十一 ("Raising Fish, Chapter 61", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E5%85%AD)

What it is: The fish-raising chapter of the Qimin Yaoshu, the sixth-century Chinese agricultural treatise, on Chinese Wikisource; after the fish ponds it gives short methods for growing water plants in them, lotus root among them. The bracketed passages are the treatise's own interlinear commentary.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text, and the oldest surviving written instructions for planting lotus in China: in early spring the root's joints are set in the mud of a fish pond. It does not say the root was harvested as food - what it promises that year is the flower, and the food it names at the chapter's end is the kernel of lotus, water caltrop and foxnut, grown in plenty against lean years. It is northern Chinese advice written about a thousand years before the settlements these maps depict, it plants the lotus in a fish pond rather than a paddy, and it says nothing of how much ground a household gave it.

Used for: lotus root planted in the mud of a fish pond in the sixth century (archetypes)
