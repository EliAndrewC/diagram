qimin-yaoshu-yangyu

賈思勰 齊民要術 (Jia Sixie, Qimin Yaoshu, c. 540 AD) ("Essential Techniques for the Common People", title translated), 卷第六 ("Volume 6", title translated), 養魚第六十一 ("Raising Fish, Chapter 61", title translated) and 養羊第五十七 ("Raising Sheep, Chapter 57", title translated) (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E5%85%AD)

What it is: The fish-raising chapter of the Qimin Yaoshu, the sixth-century Chinese agricultural treatise, on Chinese Wikisource; after the fish ponds it gives short methods for growing water plants in them, lotus root among them. The same volume's sheep chapter gives the making of hay and how to stack it for the winter. The bracketed passages are the treatise's own interlinear commentary.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text, and the oldest surviving written instructions for planting lotus in China: in early spring the root's joints are set in the mud of a fish pond. It does not say the root was harvested as food - what it promises that year is the flower, and the food it names at the chapter's end is the kernel of lotus, water caltrop and foxnut, grown in plenty against lean years. It is northern Chinese advice written about a thousand years before the settlements these maps depict, it plants the lotus in a fish pond rather than a paddy, and it says nothing of how much ground a household gave it. The sheep chapter's hay stack is advice for a large flock, a thousand head, on northern ground. What it stacks is not grass mown from a hay meadow: it is a qing of beans sown with grain and cut green in autumn, or, failing that, weeds, wild peas, brambles and bean stalks gathered and dried thin, so it cannot show that a settlement kept a hayfield; it is a stack for feeding sheep through the winter, not a horse's or a town's, and its measures (paces and a zhang) are the sixth century's.

Used for: lotus root planted in the mud of a fish pond in the sixth century (archetypes); hay stacked in round fences on high, dry ground (towns)
