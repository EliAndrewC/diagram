kotobank-tategi-uchi

Kotobank, 立木打ち (tategi-uchi; the page's headword reads it たちぎうち, tachigi-uchi), from Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia (old edition), under 示現流 (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AB%8B%E6%9C%A8%E6%89%93%E3%81%A1-1361654)

What it is: The Kotobank page quoting the World Encyclopedia's entry on Jigen-ryū where it mentions tategi-uchi: the school's basic technique of practicing with a round wooden staff (丸棒) against a standing timber, its Satsuma character, and its founder.

Why it applies, and its limits: A standard encyclopedia names practice against a standing timber as the school's basic technique and dates its founder. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, an excerpt of a few lines from an older edition, and gives no size, count or setting for the post. It also describes the practice as one Satsuma school's own: the entry calls the conservatively surviving regional character of Jigen-ryū singular (特異), so it does not show that striking posts were a general feature of training grounds elsewhere, and a map that draws them at a generic training ground extends the source rather than following it.

Used for: tategi-uchi as Jigen-ryū's basic technique, a Satsuma form; its founder Tōgō Shigetaka (1561-1643) (buildings)
