wanli-fishpond-summary

The Shunde (顺德) dike-pond figures: 40,084 mu of ponds in 1581 (万历九年) and 58,094 mu in 1642 - 广州日报 (https://gzdaily.dayoo.com/h5/html5/2023-07/05/content_871_829964.htm) and the Shunde Archives timeline; the 4.6% / 6.7% shares are those figures over the 8,700 顷 (870,000 mu) of cultivated land 吴建新 gives from 广东通志 (https://gzdaily.dayoo.com/h5/html5/2023-07/05/content_871_829964.htm) ; 「By the end of the Guangxu era, the grain fields within the county made up less than one tenth of the total cultivated area, most of it having become fish ponds.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「至光绪末年，县境禾田占总耕地面积不到十分之一，大部分成为鱼塘」) (https://www.163.com/dy/article/D4OSJLAR0514D0GJ.html); the late-1980s survey - the main dyke-pond area 86,632 ha, 35% fishponds, 25% irrigated rice (https://www.i-sis.org.uk/DykePondSystem.php); the 1581 taxable fishponds of Nanhai, Shunde and Panyu, ~160,000 mu, from 珠江三角洲农业志 (https://www.gz.gov.cn/zt/lmgzssst/content/post_10195754.html). Longshan (龙山) had 8,124 of 44,947 mu in ponds in 1581, 18% (https://www.163.com/dy/article/D4OSJLAR0514D0GJ.html); "over half" (75%, 乡之塘倍于田) is the Qianlong-Jiaqing figure;

What it is: Four pages of three kinds on the extent of the Shunde dike-pond zone: a 2023 Guangzhou Daily feature carrying the Ming county figures (40,084 mu of dike-pond in 1581, 58,094 in 1642); a Guangzhou municipal portal page quoting the delta's agricultural gazetteer for the 1581 taxed-pond total; a page by an aquaculture trade account on the NetEase platform retelling the Shunde archives' timeline, its own sources given as the archives, the agriculture bureau and unattributed internet material; and an article by Mae-Wan Ho on the Institute of Science in Society's site reporting a late-1980s Guangzhou Institute survey of the dike-pond area.

Why it applies, and its limits: Between them they give the scale of the sixteenth-century dike-pond landscape - how much of a county was pond and dike - which is what sets how far the pattern spreads on our delta maps. Their limits are that they are journalism, a government portal, a trade account and an advocacy site rather than scholarship, so every Ming number reaches us at one or two removes from the gazetteer or archive that holds it; that the 1980s figure is an advocacy site's report of a survey we have not read, and a modern measurement of a landscape that kept growing; and that the shares our text gives are our own arithmetic - the pond figures are on a readable page, but the county's cultivated-land total that makes the denominator is on none we can read, so the percentage rests on the registry's word and is labeled as such.
