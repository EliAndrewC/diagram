The Paper (澎湃新闻), 三江木，天下屋！这个码头曾是清水江木材时代最早的三大"官定"木商市场之一 ("Timber of the Three Rivers roofs the world: this landing was one of the three earliest officially appointed timber markets of the Qingshui river's timber age", title translated) (https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_12150670)

What it is: A first-person travel essay of 2021, posted on The Paper's self-publishing channel (澎湃号; the site says such posts carry only their author's views) with photographs credited to 单洪根 (Shan Honggen), on Guazhi, one of the three timber-market landings of the Qingshui river in Jinping county, Guizhou, quoting the river rule of 1797 carved on a boulder on the bank.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes a dated Qing inscription, a compact agreed by the three merchant guilds with the local brokers rather than a government ordinance, that zones a river's bank for moored timber: above a boundary marker the water is reserved for the upland sellers to moor their timber, and downstream buyers may not moor their rafts there. It is an essay on a self-publishing channel quoting the stone, not an edition of it, and it reads the rule chiefly as the line between the upland sellers' and the downstream buyers' trading rights, the mooring ban being how that line was kept; it gives no length of the mooring, no drawing and nothing on how the rafts were fastened, and the Qingshui trade was a specialist timber market of the southwest.

Used for: timber rafts moored along a river's bank in Qing China, the mooring zoned by rule (urban-features)
