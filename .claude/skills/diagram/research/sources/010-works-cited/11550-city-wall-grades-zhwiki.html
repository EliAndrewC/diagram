city-wall-grades-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 城墙 ("city wall", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/%E5%9F%8E%E5%A2%99)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on city walls, whose Chinese section grades the Ming and Qing walls in five classes by the length of their circuit, from the capitals over 25 km to the smallest class under 2 km that held most county seats, and says that most county, prefectural and garrison cities were walled and that most county walls were 4 to 10 m high.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the size of a county seat's wall as a class, which is what a walled town's rampart is scaled against. It is an encyclopedia article that cites no source for either its grading or its 4 to 10 m height. Its grades are rough and its own class assignments overlap: class 3 is "each provincial capital", but class 2 already lists several provincial capitals. It qualifies that the smallest walls were found especially on the western frontier and that many eastern county seats were larger than western prefectural cities. So a class gives a range for a county seat's wall, not a length fixed by the town's rank. It describes China only and says nothing about a Japanese town, which in the Edo period was mostly unwalled.

Used for: the Ming-Qing wall grades by circuit, and a county wall's height (towns)
