ja.wikipedia 石畳 ("stone paving", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%9F%B3%E7%95%B3)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on stone paving: why roads were paved at all (bare earth turns to mud), with a short section on Japan naming the old highways' paved stretches.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the shogunate ordered the highways' difficult stretches (難所) paved with stone and names where that paving survives (Hakone and Kikugawa on the Tokaido; the 430 m at Kanaya is a 1991 reconstruction), which says which kind of stretch was stone but gives no length or share of a Japanese road. It also names stone on temple approaches, on some city streets, on the Ryukyu kingdom's official roads and at Kuragari pass (laid by the Koriyama domain, not the shogunate), so it does not carry that stone was found only on the highways' difficult stretches. It is a tertiary encyclopedia article written by volunteers. Its general account of the bare-earth road is not about Japan in particular (its examples are European). The article carries a banner saying it lacks sources, and the shogunate's order is itself uncited: its footnotes are tourist-association pages on the surviving stretches. It says nothing of Japanese village lanes.

Used for: the bare-earth road and its mud, and stone paving only on the highways' difficult stretches (ways)
