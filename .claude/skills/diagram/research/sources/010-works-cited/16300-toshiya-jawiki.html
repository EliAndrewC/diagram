ja.wikipedia 通し矢 ("toshiya", the long-shot archery contest, title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%80%9A%E3%81%97%E7%9F%A2)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the contest of shooting the length of the Sanjusangendo temple's veranda in Kyoto, with one sentence, in its section on the venues, on how the domains practiced for it. Wikipedia itself flags the article as a stub short of verifiable references (since February 2013).

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the domains built practice structures for the contest and names five that had a dogata, a wooden mock-up of the hall that was sometimes full length and sometimes only the top of the arrow's arc, which shows archery practice as a built place a domain kept. It hedges the list as hearsay and the sentence carries no citation. It is a tertiary article that Wikipedia flags as under-sourced, so it gives the practice and not a count or a size. It does not say the structures stood in the castle town; its one located example, Sendai's, was on ground bounded by the Hirose river so stray arrows fell in the water. The contest was a 17th-century Japanese specialty of the great domains. The article's own bibliography names a closer study, Irie Kohei's 2007 paper on the dosha facilities in Budogaku Kenkyu 40(2), which this record did not read.

Used for: that domains built archery practice halls (cities/government)
