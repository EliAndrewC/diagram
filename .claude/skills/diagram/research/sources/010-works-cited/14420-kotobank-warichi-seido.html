kotobank-warichi-seido

Kotobank, 割地制度 ("the warichi system", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica) entry by Yoshitake Kaichiro (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%89%B2%E5%9C%B0%E5%88%B6%E5%BA%A6-880122)

What it is: A signed national-encyclopedia entry on warichi, the Edo-period custom by which a village periodically re-divided its fields among its landholders by lot: where it was found, why it arose, and how the land was graded, parcelled and drawn.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a short encyclopedia summary (a tertiary source) that describes the method step by step and cites the monograph on the custom, Aono Harumi's 1982 study, the place to take any number or finer detail from. It calls warichi a special custom, found mostly where harvests were unstable - newly reclaimed paddy on the low, wet ground of big rivers' lower courses, and landslide country - while in ordinary villages each farmer's plots stayed fixed, so it models that kind of village and not the usual one. Beyond listing gobanwari ("chessboard division") among the custom's other names, it says nothing about how the redistributed shares looked on the ground - whether they were cut as equal strips or marked - and it describes Japan only.

Used for: warichi's periodic redistribution of fields by lot and how the shares were made up (archetypes)
