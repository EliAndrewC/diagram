cleanup-edo-hettsui

Takahashi Tatsuro, 「Edo walks, no. 29」 (translated; original: 「江戸散策 第29回」), with materials from the Fukagawa Edo Museum, on the site of the kitchen maker Cleanup Corporation (in Japanese; https://cleanup.jp/life/edo/29.shtml)

What it is: One of a series of short columns on Edo life by a writer on the city, published by a kitchen maker with the help and photographs of the Fukagawa Edo Museum, describing the kitchen of a tenement row house as the museum reconstructs one of the 1830s and 1840s: its kamado, the hettsui, its sink, its water jar and its smoke window.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the kamado was either built onto the earth floor or set on the board floor in a legged board box, which is the form a movable kamado on a kitchen's boards takes. That the box could be moved and cleaned under is the columnist's own remark, made while saying he does not know whether it did anything against fire; the column gives no reason the box was built so, and its legs of about 10 centimeters are his figure, with no source named. It is a popular column resting on a museum's reconstruction, not on a period text it names, and it describes the one-room tenement of a commoner in Edo; nothing in it bears on a samurai house's kitchen except by analogy.

Used for: the kamado set on the board floor of an Edo tenement, boxed on legs, which the columnist remarks made it movable (buildings)
