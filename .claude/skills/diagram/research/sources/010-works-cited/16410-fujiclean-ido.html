Fuji Clean, 水の話: 井戸 ("stories of water: the well", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.fujiclean.co.jp/fujiclean/story/vol09/part102.html)

What it is: A popular column on the website of a maker of household water-treatment tanks, on how well-digging spread in the Edo period and how castle towns got their water.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports, from a book of 1814, that digging a well once cost more than a samurai household could pay until a cheaper boring method brought the price down, and that most wells were shared. It is a company's column, not a history, and its one document is named without a title; it speaks of Edo and of castle towns in general.

Used for: what a private well cost, and when it came within a samurai's reach (cities/government)
