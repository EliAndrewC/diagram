matsumoto-dosojin

Matsumoto city, "(4) 道祖神にみる歴史的風致" ("The historic landscape seen in the dōsojin", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a section of the city's plan for maintaining its historic landscape (in Japanese; https://www.city.matsumoto.nagano.jp/uploaded/attachment/20342.pdf)

What it is: A section of a Nagano city's official historic-landscape plan describing its dōsojin: their total, their forms and dates, where in a settlement they stand, and the festivals held at them, hamlet by hamlet.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states where the stones stand - most at a settlement's entrance - and shows, hamlet by hamlet, that each of a village's hamlets kept its own, often two side by side, and at two of the hamlets it describes beside a koshin tower or image and other stones, which is the count and the grouping a map needs. Its limits: it is one city in the dōsojin's heartland, where the stones are unusually many; the stones it dates run from 1715 to 1900, plus one set up in 1993, and some have been moved; a hamlet's second stone is often a later addition, so a pair seen today can be more than the hamlet had in the Edo period; it describes only the twenty or so stones in hamlets whose festivals it records, out of more than 600, and counts the city, not its hamlets, in total; and it says the carved stone is itself an Edo-period form, a Japanese practice with no Chinese counterpart.

Used for: dōsojin at a settlement's entrance, one or two to each hamlet, standing with the other stones (religion-and-death)
