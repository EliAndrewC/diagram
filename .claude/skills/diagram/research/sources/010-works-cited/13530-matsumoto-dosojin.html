matsumoto-dosojin

Matsumoto city, "(4) 道祖神にみる歴史的風致" ("The historic landscape seen in the dōsojin", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a section of the city's plan for maintaining its historic landscape (in Japanese; https://www.city.matsumoto.nagano.jp/uploaded/attachment/20342.pdf)

What it is: A section of a Nagano city's official historic-landscape plan describing its dōsojin: their total, their forms and dates, where in a settlement they stand, and the festivals held at them, hamlet by hamlet.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states where the stones stand - most at a settlement's entrance - and shows, hamlet by hamlet, that each of a village's hamlets kept its own, often two side by side with a koshin tower and other stones, which is the count and the grouping a map needs. Its limits: it is one city in the dōsojin's heartland, where the stones are unusually many; the stones it dates run from 1715 to 1900 and some have been moved; and it counts the city, not its hamlets, in total.

Used for: dōsojin at a settlement's entrance, one or two to each hamlet, standing with the other stones (religion-and-death)
