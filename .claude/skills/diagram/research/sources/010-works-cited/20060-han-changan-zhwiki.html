Chinese Wikipedia, 汉长安城 ("Han Chang'an", translated) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B1%89%E9%95%BF%E5%AE%89%E5%9F%8E)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Western Han capital at Chang'an, whose wall was begun in the first year of Emperor Hui (194 BCE); in one unsourced sentence it gives the wall's height (12 m) and base (12 to 16 m) and the moat outside it (8 m wide, 3 m deep). Its claim to archaeological measurement (根據考古實測, "by archaeological survey") is made in the next sentence, for the lengths of the four walls, not for these figures, and the article has been tagged as needing more sources since May 2013.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the moat of a Chinese capital whose site has been excavated since 1956, some fifteen centuries before the setting's model period, a premodern figure where the other great-city moats read are measured on surviving and restored moats. It disagrees with the English Wikipedia article on Chinese city walls for the same moat (8 m wide and 3 m deep against 45 m and 4.5 m). The English Wikipedia article on Chang'an, which cites a source, gives the moat as first 6.13 m wide and 4.62 m deep and makes 8 m by 3 m a later enlargement. This article cites no excavation report for its figure and does not say which phase it gives, so it bounds the question rather than settling it; and an imperial capital's moat is not a provincial seat's.

Used for: the excavated moat of the Han capital (cities/defenses)
