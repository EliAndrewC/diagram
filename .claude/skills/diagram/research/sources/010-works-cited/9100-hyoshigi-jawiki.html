hyoshigi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 拍子木 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%8B%8D%E5%AD%90%E6%9C%A8)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the hyoshigi, the pair of wooden clappers, including the section on night rounds in which a watch walks calling out to mind your locks and your fire while striking them together.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests the night patrol with clappers that our fire posture describes, and the call that goes with it. Its limits are stated where it is used: the bodies the article names for that round are twentieth-century civil-defense and fire corps, so the page carries the practice without dating it to the Edo period, and the Edo dating remains uncited.

Used for: the night round walked with clappers and its fire-watch call
