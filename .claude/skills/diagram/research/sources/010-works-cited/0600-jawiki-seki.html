jawiki-seki

ja.wikipedia 堰 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/堰)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the seki, the weir: a structure other than a dam built across a river to control its flow, without the function of an embankment. It lists the kinds by purpose - the intake weir that regulates a river's level so that irrigation, drinking, industrial or power water can be taken - and names the overflow type that lets water over its crest at all times.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the plain definition of the thing our maps now draw across a brook at the intake, and the one fact about it a map needs: that the ordinary weir passes water over its crest rather than stopping the stream. Its limits are that it is a tertiary encyclopedia summary written about weirs in general and modern ones in particular, with nothing on what a village weir was built of before modern times or where a canal leaves it, so those rest on other sources.

Used for: the definition of a weir; the intake weir as a level-regulating structure; the araizeki, the weir water overflows at all times
