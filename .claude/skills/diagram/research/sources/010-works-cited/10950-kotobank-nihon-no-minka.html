kotobank 日本の民家 ("the folk houses of Japan", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E3%81%AE%E6%B0%91%E5%AE%B6-1616904)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting a reference work's gazetteer of designated folk houses across Japan, region by region, each with its place, its date, its roof form and material and a line on what makes it notable.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how large the greatest farmhouses grew and that some roofs now tiled or sheeted were thatched first. Its limits are those of any list of designated houses: they are the survivors, the large and the notable, so it says nothing of an ordinary house; and it is a tertiary summary.

Used for: the size of a hereditary headman's house, and roofs re-covered from thatch (homesteads)
