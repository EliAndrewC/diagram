bunka-mori-naya

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Online, 森家住宅納屋 ("Mori house, barn", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/545121)

What it is: The national register's entry for the barn of the Mori house, a farm of a village on the Koto river in the Sanuki plain, Kagawa, built in 1907: where it stands on the plot (the east edge, facing west), its building area (a footprint of 99 square meters; it is partly two-storied, so its floor area is larger), its build.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the siting and size of a real farm barn. Its limits: it is a Meiji building (1907), after the Edo period the setting is modeled on, and its fireproof walls plastered up to the eaves and its pantile roof are a late, wealthy build; it is a large barn of an old household grown rich on rice and barley milling, so its size reflects that trade more than an ordinary farm's needs; it is one example, in Kagawa, described in a register entry of a few sentences.

Used for: the siting and size of a farm barn (homesteads)
