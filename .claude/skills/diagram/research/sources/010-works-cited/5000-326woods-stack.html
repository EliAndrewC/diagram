326woods-stack

326-woods.com, 薪の保管方法 (https://326-woods.com/2021/02/09/keepwoods/)

What it is: A 2021 how-to post, 薪の保管方法 ("how to store firewood"), on the site of 326-woods, a Japanese firewood supplier. It tells a customer where to put a woodpile and how high to build it, and says that even heavy split wood can be stacked to about 1.5 m and is best kept under an eave.

Why it applies, and its limits: A woodpile is one of the few farmyard features whose height is set by what a person can lift and stack rather than by fashion, so a working supplier's own advice is a reasonable guide to how tall the stack beside a farmhouse should be drawn. It is nonetheless a modern trade page and not a survey of anything old: the 1.5 m figure describes present-day practice with present-day tools and sheeting, and we use it as an upper bound rather than as a measurement of a premodern woodpile.

Used for: a stack to ~1.5 m high, labeled modern.
