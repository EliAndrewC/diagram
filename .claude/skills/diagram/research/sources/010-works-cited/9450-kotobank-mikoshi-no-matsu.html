kotobank-mikoshi-no-matsu

Kotobank, 見越しの松 (the pine seen over the wall), entries from Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen and Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A6%8B%E8%B6%8A%E3%81%97%E3%81%AE%E6%9D%BE-637526)

What it is: The Kotobank page defining the mikoshi no matsu, a pine planted at the edge of a wall so that it can be seen from outside, with the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's attestation of the phrase in the Jesuit Japanese-Portuguese dictionary of 1603-04.

Why it applies, and its limits: It fixes one place a pine was planted in a walled garden, and dates the practice's name to the early seventeenth century. Its limits are that it is a one-line definition, silent on whose houses kept one and on the tree's size.

Used for: a pine planted at the wall to be seen from outside, a phrase attested in 1603-04 (vegetation)
