kotobank-tanada

Kotobank, 棚田 ("terraced paddy", title translated from the Japanese by this project), entries from the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia and the Heibonsha MyPedia (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%A3%9A%E7%94%B0-93872)

What it is: The Japanese reference-site entries for the terraced paddy from three encyclopedias: the Nipponica entry by the crop scientist Hoshikawa Kiyoshi on the narrow strip fields and the high stone-kept walls between them on steep ground; the World Encyclopedia entry by the historian Kuroda Hideo on the medieval register notation for how many paddies made up a tan, quoting the Kōyasan documents and dating terrace-building in the Kinai records from the late Kamakura period into the early modern; and the unsigned MyPedia entry giving the terraces' share of Japan's paddy.

Why it applies, and its limits: Two signed encyclopedia entries describe the form and give a medieval Japanese notation for the size of a terrace paddy. They are summaries, good for the shape and not for a number. Kuroda's entry places terrace-building in the setting's own eras, from the late Kamakura period into the early modern. Hoshikawa's entry describes the terrace as it is today (it notes that machines can hardly work it), the old form seen in modern times, and its high walls are those of steep mountain ground, not of every terrace. They give no wall height in meters, and the notation is one example, not a count; the share of paddy land is a modern, undated national figure from the unsigned MyPedia entry.

Used for: the terrace's narrow strips and high walls, and four paddies to a tan (archetypes)
