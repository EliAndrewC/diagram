wenfengta-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 文峰塔 ("wenfeng pagoda", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/文峰塔)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the wenfeng pagoda, a pagoda raised by a town or village for its geomantic fortune in the examinations rather than as part of a temple, and on where it was usually placed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the usual bearing of a Chinese town's non-temple pagoda: most often the southeast (xun), with east-northeast, south-southeast and south-southwest also allowed. Such a pagoda often stood at the water-mouth where the stream leaves the settlement, and local terrain and water flow bent the rule in practice. It is a tertiary source, a short unsigned encyclopedia article (last revised 2020) resting on two modern books, chiefly Chen Zhihua's 2007 study of educational architecture. Its four bearings are what a Qing-dynasty geomantic manual, the Xiangzhai jingzuan, prescribes, not a count of where such pagodas actually stood, and only a handful of examples illustrate them. It describes mainly southern China and Vietnam, says such pagodas were fewer in the north and rarer still in the northeast and northwest, gives no era for the practice, and names nothing like it in Japan. It gives about three metres for a small stone brush-shaped marker, but no pagoda heights and no count of towns that built one.
