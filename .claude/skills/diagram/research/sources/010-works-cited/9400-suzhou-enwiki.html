en.wikipedia, Suzhou (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzhou)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the city of Suzhou in Jiangsu, covering its history, its hydrology and its canals and historic districts, and describing the old city as a network of rivers and canals that, into the early twentieth century, linked it to the surrounding countryside.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page read for Suzhou as a premodern Chinese city laid over an engineered water network, the example given of a city whose water ran into canals rather than marsh. It is a tertiary summary of a modern city, its description of the canals is general and undated beyond "as late as the early 20th century", and that sentence is itself taken from the 1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica, a late-Qing observation made before most of the city's industry. It says nothing of drainage at all: it describes the canals as linking the city's islands to the countryside, never as where the city's water or the fields' water went, so "a city draining into engineered water" is our reading of the network rather than the page's statement, and the fields' drainage rests on no page read.

Used for: Suzhou's network of canals, as the Chinese instance of a city draining into engineered water (water)
