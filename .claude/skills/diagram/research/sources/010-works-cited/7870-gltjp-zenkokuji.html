gltjp-zenkokuji

GOOD LUCK TRIP (Arukikata), Japan travel directory, Zenkoku-ji, Kagurazaka (https://www.gltjp.com/en/directory/item/16293/) - 「The tiger motif can also be found on the temple’s ema (votive plaques), as well as on charms, straps, and papier-mâché figures.」 (the source's own English)

What it is: A commercial travel-directory page, marked as supervised by the temple itself, on Zenkoku-ji, the Bishamonten temple of Kagurazaka in Tokyo: the deity's image, the pair of stone tigers at its entrance, donated in 1848, and the tiger motif on its votive plaques and charms.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for one thing - that at a Bishamon dedication the guardian pair at the entrance is tigers in place of the usual lion-dogs, which the page states outright. Its limits: a promotional listing with no author and no sources, trusted for the fabric of its own precinct because the temple checked it; one city temple in the shogunal capital, so it says nothing about a village shrine or about how common the form was; the one date it gives is 1848, at the very end of the Edo period, so the custom is attested late, not across the era; and the page itself calls the tiger pair "a rare sight" against the usual lion-dogs, which is why the rule it supports is the convention at a Bishamon dedication, not its reach.

Used for: tigers in place of lion-dogs at a Bishamon dedication (religion-and-death.html).
