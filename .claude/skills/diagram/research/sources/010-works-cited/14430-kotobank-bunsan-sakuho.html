kotobank-bunsan-sakuho

Kotobank, 分散錯圃制 ("scattered and intermixed holdings", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a passage from the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia's entry on the postwar land reform (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%88%86%E6%95%A3%E9%8C%AF%E5%9C%83%E5%88%B6-1410026)

What it is: The encyclopedia's passage naming the old pattern of Japanese farm holdings - each farmer's land in tiny parcels scattered in many places and intermixed with other people's - with the average farm size in the 1930s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the scattered, intermixed holding as the long-standing pattern the land reform failed to break. It is a tertiary source: a partial extract from the old edition of a modern general encyclopedia, in an entry on a twentieth-century reform. It describes Japan only, so it stands in for the Chinese half of the setting by analogy. It dates the pattern only as "from of old", and it gives no count of parcels to a household. Its 1.1-hectare average farm is a 1930s figure from after the Meiji land-tax reform, not a premodern household's holding, and is not to be used as one.

Used for: a household's fields scattered in small parcels among its neighbors' (archetypes)
