ehime-kenshi-seiryoki

愛媛県史 民俗 上 ("History of Ehime Prefecture, Folklore, vol. 1", title translated), 1983, chapter 1, section 2, "毎日の食事" ("Everyday meals", title translated), with its transcription of the monthly crop lists of the 清良記 (Seiryoki), vol. 7, in the prefecture's database えひめの記憶 (in Japanese; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/49/view/6464)

What it is: The folklore volume's section on what Ehime's villages ate, which reprints, from the farming volume of the Seiryoki (a chronicle of the Iyo warlord Doi Kiyoyoshi, thought written between 1629 and 1654 and known as Japan's oldest farm book), its month-by-month lists of what to plant, to save seed from and to eat, and sets beside them the older villagers' accounts of their side dishes in several districts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the record's first Edo-period list of the vegetables a farm household could plant and eat through the year - a farm book's calendar of what to plant and eat, not a record of what one household ate - two of whose monthly headings (the second and third months) name the 菜園 (vegetable garden), and it gives later Ehime accounts of vegetables grown only at home. Its limits: the lists mix crops with plants and even snails gathered wild - the two headings that name the 菜園 head lists that are mostly wild greens - and never say which grew beside the house rather than in the fields; the farm volume's date is disputed, from the 1629-1654 this volume gives to after 1701 (Nagai Gien, because it lists the Ryukyu sweet potato, which this same section says reached Ehime only in 1711-15), so it is Edo-period evidence but not certainly early Edo; the text is a modern editor's annotated version, his notes in parentheses; and the later accounts are largely undated recollections from a few districts (Matsumae's alone dates itself, to before bought fish grew common in late Meiji and Taishō) that say the vegetables were the household's own growing but not where on the farm they grew, save Matsumae's soybeans planted on the paddy bunds.

Used for: the vegetables eaten month by month in Edo-period Iyo (a farm book's calendar, dated 1629-1654 or after 1701); vegetables from a household's own growing, dried and salted for the lean season, in later Ehime accounts (Homesteads)
