jtb-kitakata

Japan Travel Bureau Foundation, National Tourism Resources Register, 喜多方蔵のまち並み ("Kitakata's storehouse townscape", translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://tabi.jtb.or.jp/res/070048-)

What it is: The foundation's register entry for Kitakata's storehouse streets, current as of 2023: how many storehouses stand in the city, what they were for, and when and why they spread.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the spread of the earthen storehouse in one town and gives the reasons, which says how far that town's count stands above the ordinary. It is a tourism register's short entry, a tertiary summary drawing on the town's tourist association and the Edo-period gazetteer Shinpen Aizu Fudoki, not a survey. Its count of about 4,000 is for the whole modern city, rural settlements included, counts the storehouses standing today (brick ones among them, a Meiji-and-later build), and is given only as what is said. The spread it dates begins among the wealthy from 1804-1830, but it reached ordinary houses and farmhouses only after the fire of 1880, in the Meiji era: the count that sets Kitakata apart is mostly post-Edo, and an Edo-period count would have been lower.

Used for: when and why Kitakata's storehouses multiplied (cities/fabric.html)
