hansho-kotobank

kotobank 半鐘 (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8D%8A%E9%90%98-118280)

What it is: Kotobank's entry for 半鐘 (hansho), aggregating standard Japanese reference dictionaries: the small bronze hanging bell hung on a fire-watch tower, and the code by which the number of strokes told a hearer what kind of incident had happened - for a fire, a continuous peal for a near one, fewer strokes the further off it was. The entries also date the usage: one of the dictionaries puts the hanging of the bell on a fire-watch tower from the Edo period onward, and the historical dictionary among them carries an attestation of the word from 1686.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies the one thing our fire-tower explanation needs beyond the tower itself: that the bell carried information by its cadence rather than simply making a noise, which is why a watchman on a tower is worth drawing, and it puts that bell on a tower within the period our settlements are pitched in rather than leaving it undated. Its limits are that it is a short tertiary dictionary entry with no citations; that its 1686 attestation is of the WORD in another of its senses, the bell of a striking clock, the earliest example it gives for the fire-alarm sense being dated 1706; and that it describes Japan only.

Used for: the alarm bell hung on a fire-watch tower and the code its strokes carried - a continuous peal for a near fire, fewer strokes the further off it was - and the bell's dating to the Edo period
