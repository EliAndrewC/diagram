oniwa-takayama-jinya

Oniwa-san, 高山陣屋庭園 (the garden of the Takayama Jin'ya), Ito Masatoshi (in Japanese; https://oniwa.garden/takayama-jinya/)

What it is: A page on a garden-visiting site by its author, from a visit in 2021, describing the garden of the Takayama Jin'ya, the shogunate's intendancy office for Hida: which rooms look onto it, the tea room and stepping stones, and the site pamphlet's dating of the garden's remaking.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable account found of how the garden of a surviving intendancy office sat against the intendant's own rooms - the kind of posting our compounds draw. Its limits are that it is a garden-media site written by its editor after one visit, not the keeper's own account (the keeper's site confirms only that the great hall looks onto the garden and that the Arashiyama room was the intendant's daily room, with a tea room at the back); that its dating is the author passing on the site pamphlet; that, as the page itself says, most of the buildings standing today are reconstructions of the late Showa and Heisei years, rebuilt to a late-Edo drawing, though the office block with the great hall dates from 1816; that the intendant walking the stepping stones is the author's guess, the garden being only viewed from the rooms today; and that it is a single compound, the only one of more than sixty such offices to survive, and a daimyo's house taken over for an office governing a whole province - grander than most postings, and one example rather than a pattern.

Used for: one garden seen from both the great hall and the intendant's living room, with a tea room and stepping stones from each; a pond garden further in (buildings)
