warichi-kotobank

Kotobank, 割地 (warichi, land re-allotment), the entries of several Japanese dictionaries, the one used being the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (精選版 日本国語大辞典) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/割地-154351)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering the dictionary entries for warichi, the Edo-period custom by which a village divided its farmland among its cultivators for a fixed term and then divided it again.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the historical counterpart of the setting's headman allotting the common fields: a village re-allotting its land to even out the tax burden and the quality of the soil. It says the custom was practiced where production was unstable, such as land damaged by rivers - so it was a regional practice, not the general Edo-period tenure, where a household held its fields outright - and it gives no term or share size.

Used for: village re-allotment of farmland for a term, and where it was practiced (fields)
