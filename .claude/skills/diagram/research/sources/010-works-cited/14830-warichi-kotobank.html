warichi-kotobank

Kotobank, 割地 (warichi, land re-allotment), the entries of several Japanese dictionaries, the one used being the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (精選版 日本国語大辞典) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/割地-154351)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering the dictionary entries for warichi, the Edo-period custom by which a village divided its farmland among its cultivators for a fixed term and then divided it again.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the historical counterpart of the setting's re-allotment of the village fields: a village re-allotting its land to even out the tax burden and the quality of the soil. The counterpart is not exact. One entry on the page says the re-allotment was made by the agreement of the farmers of one or several villages, and that it redivided the land they already held. It was not common land handed out by a headman. It says the custom was practiced where production was unstable, such as land damaged by rivers - so it was a regional practice, not the general Edo-period tenure, where a household kept the same fields from year to year. Another entry on the page does note that Satsuma domain enforced a re-allotment (kadowari) across the whole domain. The page gives no term or share size. It is made of modern dictionary definitions, a tertiary summary: good for the shape of the custom, not for how any one village ran it.

Used for: village re-allotment of farmland for a term, and where it was practiced (fields)
