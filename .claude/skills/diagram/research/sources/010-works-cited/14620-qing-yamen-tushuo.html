Lin Qian 林乾, 《清代衙门图说》 (Zhonghua Book Company, 2006), sample chapter on Douban Books: 《清代衙门图说》试读：“居中而治”的衙署 ("Illustrated Qing Yamen, sample chapter: the yamen that governs from the middle", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://book.douban.com/reading/10187786/)

What it is: A free sample chapter, posted on the Douban book site, of Lin Qian's illustrated general-audience history of Qing government offices, 《清代衙门图说》 (Zhonghua Book Company, Beijing, 2006): the Qing rule for what a yamen holds, its axial order, its place in the city and where its jail stood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the Qing regulation on yamen - the halls, the gates, the residence, the clerks' rooms, and that a senior official has the full set and a junior one a reduced set - which is the tier rule a governor's compound follows. It is a popular book, not a survey, and it quotes the regulation without naming where it is recorded, so the rule is second-hand. Its worked layouts are county offices (Youxian, Mayang), so a governor's compound follows them only by carrying the tier rule upward. It gives the yamen's place at the city's middle as the usual rule and a fengshui ideal, with one county town as its example, not as a count of cities; the same chapter says many county offices were run-down or worked out of rented houses. Its explanation of the jail's direction by divination is the book's own gloss and is not used here.

Used for: the program and order of a Qing yamen, its place at the city's middle, and the jail outside the ceremonial gate (cities/government)
