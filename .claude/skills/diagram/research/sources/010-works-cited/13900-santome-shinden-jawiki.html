ja.wikipedia 三富新田 ("Santome Shinden", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%89%E5%AF%8C%E6%96%B0%E7%94%B0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Santome Shinden, a dry-field colony on the Musashino plateau cleared at a domain lord's order in the 1690s and surveyed in 1696, whose allotments survive in the landscape.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the long-strip allotment: farmhouses along both sides of a road, each with its field and coppice running in an equal strip behind it, 40 ken of frontage by 375 ken of depth in Kamitome. Its limits: it is one planned colony on dry upland, not a paddy village, and a lord's survey rather than a form that grew by itself; it says nothing of tenancy. It is a different work from the architectural paper on the same colony's allotments, which the record also cites.

Used for: long-strip plots where a planned new-field colony was laid out (homesteads)
