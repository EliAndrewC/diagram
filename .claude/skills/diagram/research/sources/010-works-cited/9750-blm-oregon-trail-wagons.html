Bureau of Land Management, National Historic Oregon Trail Interpretive Center, Wagons on the Oregon Trail (https://www.blm.gov/learn/interpretive-centers/national-historic-oregon-trail-interpretive-center/explore/wagons)

What it is: An interpretive page of the US Bureau of Land Management's Oregon Trail center describing the emigrant wagons of the 1840s-1860s overland trail, the oxen and mules that drew them, and the routine of a travel day, including the midday halt called the nooning.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes how long an ox-drawn train halted at midday and what the animals did while it rested, the one readable account found of a draft team's rest at a stop. It is nineteenth-century America, not East Asia, and an emigrant train rather than a post-station; we take only what an ox team at a halt did, a matter of the animals' work rather than of any institution, and its halts are camp stops in open country, not a yard in a town.

Used for: how long a draft team rested, unyoked, at a halt (stable yards)
