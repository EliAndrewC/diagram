ja.wikipedia 死牛馬取得権 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%AD%BB%E7%89%9B%E9%A6%AC%E5%8F%96%E5%BE%97%E6%A8%A9)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 死牛馬取得権 ("the right to take fallen cattle and horses"), on the hereditary privilege by which the kawata - the outcaste leatherworking communities of Tokugawa Japan - claimed the carcasses of dead livestock across a defined stretch of countryside, a right abolished in 1871.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the outcast community's right to fallen cattle and horses over a defined territory, and its abolition in 1871, which is what ties the hide trade on our maps to a district. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, good for the terms and the outline of the arrangement and not for a number; that it is Tokugawa-specific; and that the territory it describes is a legal and economic one - the article says nothing about where a tannery quarter stood, so its siting in relation to the settlement is our inference.

Used for: the kawata's right to fallen cattle and horses over a defined territory (「A defined district from which fallen cattle and horses were collected was called a kusaba (草場, "grass ground") or a dannaba (旦那場, the ground of one's patrons).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「死牛馬を集める特定の地域を「草場」ないしは「旦那場」といった」)), abolished 1871
