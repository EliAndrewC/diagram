Kweon Deogkyu and Youn Yeo-Chang, "Factors influencing sustainability of traditional village groves (Maeulsoop) in Korea", Forest Policy and Economics 128 (2021) 102477 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1389934121000836; open access) - Korean analogue

What it is: A peer-reviewed article by Kweon Deogkyu and Youn Yeo-Chang, "Factors influencing sustainability of traditional village groves (Maeulsoop) in Korea", published open access in Forest Policy and Economics in 2021. It surveys twenty-four red pine village groves around Mt. Jiri in Namwon, South Korea, reporting each grove's area and how many trees it holds along with the social conditions that keep such groves alive.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the closest thing to a measured answer we could read on a public page to the question of how big a village's planted grove actually is and how many trees stand in it, so it sets the size band our groves are drawn to. Two honest limits: it is Korean, standing in for Japan and China because no comparable open survey of Japanese or Chinese village groves could be read, though village grove planting is a shared regional practice; and it measures groves as they survive today, which records what has lasted rather than what was originally planted.

Used for: 24 red-pine village groves around Mt. Jiri: mean 2,197 m², range 250-6,330 m²; "less than 10 to more than 100" trees per grove
