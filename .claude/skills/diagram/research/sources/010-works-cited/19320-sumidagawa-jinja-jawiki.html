Japanese Wikipedia, "隅田川神社" (Sumidagawa shrine) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9A%85%E7%94%B0%E5%B7%9D%E7%A5%9E%E7%A4%BE)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Sumidagawa shrine in Sumida ward, Tokyo, formerly called the water-god shrine (Suijinja, Suijingu) of the Sumida river, on its gods, its founding legend and those who worshipped it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the shrine, as guardian of the Sumida river, drew the faith of those who worked on the river, water-transport operators and boat inns among them, which is a water god kept by the river trades of a great city. It is a short encyclopedia article: it gives that faith no date, calling its founding unknown beyond a legend that Minamoto no Yoritomo founded it, and dating only its renaming in 1872, so it cannot by itself place the boatmen's worship before the modern period.

Used for: a river's water-god shrine worshipped by the river trades, undated (river cities)
