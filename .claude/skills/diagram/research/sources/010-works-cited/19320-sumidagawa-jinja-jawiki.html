Japanese Wikipedia, "隅田川神社" (Sumidagawa shrine) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9A%85%E7%94%B0%E5%B7%9D%E7%A5%9E%E7%A4%BE)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Sumidagawa shrine in Sumida ward, Tokyo, formerly called the water-god shrine (Suijinja, Suijingu) of the Sumida river, on its gods, its founding legend and those who worshipped it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the shrine, as guardian of the Sumida river, drew the faith of those who worked on the river, water-transport operators and boat inns among them, which is a water god kept by the river trades of a great city. It is a short encyclopedia article, a stub that cites no sources of its own, so nothing it says can be traced back to evidence. It gives that faith no date, calling its founding unknown beyond a legend that Minamoto no Yoritomo founded it, and dating only its renaming in 1872. It shows that the shrine stood in Edo (it carries Hiroshige's print of the Suijin grove from his One Hundred Famous Views of Edo, and lists a signpost written by the late-Edo writer Santo Kyozan), but not when the boatmen's worship began, so it cannot by itself place that worship before the modern period. And it is one shrine in one great city, standing in for a general pattern.

Used for: a river's water-god shrine worshipped by the river trades, undated (river cities)
