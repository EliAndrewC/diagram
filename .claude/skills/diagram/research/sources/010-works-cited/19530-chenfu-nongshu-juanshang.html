chenfu-nongshu-juanshang

陳旉 農書 卷上 ("Treatise on Agriculture, Book One", title translated) (Chen Fu, Nongshu, completed 1149), Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%BE%B2%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B7%E4%B8%8A)

What it is: The first of the three books of Chen Fu's farming manual of the Song dynasty, its fourteen chapters on farming, in a Wikisource transcription whose editor is not named and which prints chapter eleven (器用之宜篇) twice and leaves out chapter twelve (念慮之宜篇), so shows thirteen; among them the siting of fields by the lie of the land (地勢之宜篇), manuring (糞田之宜篇), weeding (薅耘之宜篇) and the seedbed (善其根苗篇).

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text on the wet-rice farming of the lower Yangtze, written in the Southern Song and so within the imperial China the setting is modeled on, and gives a pond sized to the field it waters, the draining of paddies one plot at a time for weeding, and a seedbed's water kept shallow or deep by the weather. It is about China, not Japan; its pond of two or three mu in ten is a rule of thumb for high fields, not a survey, and its advice on water depth is for a seedbed just sown and gives no figure. It is one man's advice, not a record of common practice: on weeding he says most farmers of his day drained the whole field at once, not plot by plot from the lowest up, so the plot-by-plot draining is his recommended method, not what every village did. In the same way, his manure house beside every farm dwelling is what he says a farm must have, not a count of farms that had one, and he gives it no size.

Used for: the pond given two or three mu in every ten of the field it waters; the paddies drained plot by plot for weeding and the seedbed's shallow, sun-warmed water (Fields); the pond's embankment planted sparsely with mulberry and cudrania and trodden firm by tethered cattle (Water); the low-eaved manure house with its brick-lined pit beside every farm dwelling (Homesteads)
