People's Daily Overseas Edition, "水往高处流"，中国古人这样"链动"河川 ("'Water flowing uphill': how the ancient Chinese chained the rivers", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 2026 (in Chinese; http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrbhwb/pad/content/202603/26/content_30147352.html)

What it is: A newspaper feature by the staff reporter Zhu Jinyi (26 March 2026; academic support credited to Chen Pu and Hu Zexue) on the square-pallet chain pump (longgu shuiche, "dragon-bone water wheel", also called fanche): how it lifts water, where it spread, and that it drained polders as well as watering them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in plain words that the chain pump lifted water out of low fields in flood as well as into them in drought, and ties that to the growth of the Jiangnan polders. It is a popular newspaper piece, not a history. It puts the drainage use only broadly, in the Song dynasty in present-day Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Jiangxi and Fujian, and names no period text for it, though it names the Tiangong Kaiwu and Wang Zhen's Nong Shu for other claims. It gives no dated record, named place or count, and does not say where on a polder the pumps stood.

Used for: the chain pump draining a polder in flood (archetypes)
