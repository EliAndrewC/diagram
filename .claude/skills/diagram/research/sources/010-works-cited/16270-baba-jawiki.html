ja.wikipedia 馬場 ("riding ground", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A6%AC%E5%A0%B4)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the riding ground: ground kept for riding, for military horses and their training. It has sections on the ancient court riding ground and the medieval warrior house's riding ground, Edo's riding grounds (Takada, Bakurocho, Uneme-ga-hara, and who looked after them), the domains' "cherry riding grounds" (桜の馬場) across Japan, and racecourses. It was last revised in 2023 and has a single footnote.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in one sentence that an early-modern castle town had a riding ground in its samurai district, used by the samurai living there. It is a secondary encyclopedia summary, and that sentence cites no source: the article's one footnote (Kanayama, 中央区史跡散歩, 1993) supports only an Edo passage. The article's own Edo account qualifies the sentence. Edo's riding grounds were scattered across the city. Many were placed in the care of commoner wards, which rented part of the ground to showmen and stalls, and some riding grounds later became samurai land. So "in the samurai district" is the general rule, not every case. Its sizes (Takada about 5,000 tsubo, 200 by 25 ken, c.1869; Uneme-ga-hara 890 tsubo, half of it used for riding) are the capital's and do not scale to a provincial castle town. Outside Edo it names domain riding grounds (Zeze's castle town, Fukuyama, Kumamoto, Matsushiro and others) by name and place only.

Used for: that the riding ground stood in the samurai district (cities/government)
