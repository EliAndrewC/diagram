guangdong-xinyu-22

Qu Dajun (屈大均), Guangdong xinyu (廣東新語, New Sayings of Guangdong), chapter 22, "Scale talk" (鱗語), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E5%BB%A3%E6%9D%B1%E6%96%B0%E8%AA%9E/%E5%8D%B722)

What it is: The fish chapter of a late-seventeenth-century description of Guangdong by a scholar of the Pearl River delta, covering the pond fish of Guangzhou, the fry caught in the West River and sold by the households of Jiujiang in Nanhai, the dikes and ponds of the villages, and the fishery levies.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary account from inside the Pearl River delta at the time the dike-pond system was spreading there, so it speaks directly to what a premodern dike-pond village kept on its dikes and in its ponds. It is one observer's description, not a survey: its shares (how much of Jiujiang's pond water raised fry) are his reckoning of one township, and a Wikisource transcription of a printed edition may carry copying slips.

Used for: that nursery ponds belonged to the fry township of Jiujiang, where most of the pond water raised fry, and that other places bought their fry and raised grown fish; that the villages of Guangzhou's large counties planted their pond dikes with fruit trees, lychee above all, and with tea and mulberry (archetypes)
