kotobank-kamado

Kotobank, "竈" [kamado, hearth], the entry in the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (Shogakukan) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%AB%88)

What it is: The concise edition of Japan's largest historical dictionary of the language, on Kotobank, defining the word for a cooking hearth and its extended senses, with dated examples of use.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that a "hearth" in old counts means a household, the unit of family living used in levies, which is how a premodern tally of houses and hearths is read. It is a dictionary sense of a Japanese word (the example it quotes is from about 1120), not a statement about any one record, and it speaks only for Japanese usage; for a Chinese count of hearths it is a stand-in; it notes that some regions of the Edo period counted by the pot-hook instead, and it does not say how any one town's count was taken.

Used for: reading a count of hearths as a count of households (cities/fabric)
