松田芳夫 (Yoshio Matsuda), "River anecdotes" no. 21, "The head works and the intake weir" (河川こぼれ話 21 頭首工と取水堰), Japan River Association newsletter (https://www.japanriver.or.jp/merumaga/koborebanashi/bn/no21.pdf)

What it is: A short essay by Yoshio Matsuda, a river engineer, in the Japan River Association's newsletter series of river anecdotes, on the head works and the intake weir: how the weir across a river raises its surface to make taking water convenient, how old the practice is (the prototype of a small mountain-stream weir for paddy is placed in the Yayoi period), and how the name for the whole set of works came to mean the weir alone.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a Japanese river engineer's account of the intake as a premodern institution, and it names the whole sequence of works - weir, intake mouth, settling basin, division works - which is the sequence our maps follow at the head of a field. Its limits are that it is a popular essay rather than a study, that its Yayoi date is reported as hearsay ("is said to be seen"), and that it gives no dimensions and no description of any one village weir.

Used for: the weir across a river to raise its surface for intake, since the Yayoi period on small mountain streams; the original meaning of the head works as the whole sequence weir - intake mouth - settling basin - division works
