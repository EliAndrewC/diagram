okamoto-1956-sonraku

岡本兼佳 (Okamoto Kaneyoshi), 村落形態と営農構造 ―関東低地における調査― ("Village form and farm structure: a survey in the Kanto lowland", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 地理学評論 (Geographical Review of Japan) 29(2), February 1956, pp. 77-89 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/grj1925/29/2/29_2_77/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A geography paper that measures how clustered or dispersed the farm settlements are in 58 towns and villages of the Kanto lowland in Saitama Prefecture (the Kita-Katsushika and Minami-Saitama districts), town by town, and relates that to how the farms were worked. It uses the 1950 farm census, prefectural statistics and settlements counted on 1:50,000 maps.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a working rule for where one settlement ends: houses within 200 m of each other count as one settlement, because a household farming about 1 chobu needs about that much room. Its limits: the 200 m rule is the author's own convention for counting settlements on a 1:50,000 map for his statistical index, not a rule from the period or from the villagers, and it even counts a lone farmhouse 200 m from its neighbors as a settlement. The 1 chobu it rests on is the 1950 census average farm, after the postwar land reform, not a premodern figure. The village forms it counts are older, but the survey is of one lowland in Saitama in 1950. Its measure is households per area per settlement rather than any built share of a village's ground, and it gives no house density or lot size.

Used for: when two clusters of houses count as one settlement (homesteads)
