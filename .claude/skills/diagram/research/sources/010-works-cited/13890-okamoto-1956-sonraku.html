okamoto-1956-sonraku

岡本兼佳 (Okamoto Kaneyoshi), 村落形態と営農構造 ―関東低地における調査― ("Village form and farm structure: a survey in the Kanto lowland", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 地理学評論 (Geographical Review of Japan) 29(2), February 1956, pp. 77-89 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/grj1925/29/2/29_2_77/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A geography paper that measures how clustered or dispersed the farm settlements of the Kanto lowland are, town by town, and relates that to how the farms were worked.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a working rule for where one settlement ends: houses within 200 m of each other count as one settlement, because a household farming about 1 chobu needs about that much room. Its limits: it is a 1950s survey of a modern lowland, its measure is households per area per settlement rather than any built share of a village's ground, and it gives no house density or lot size.

Used for: when two clusters of houses count as one settlement (homesteads)
