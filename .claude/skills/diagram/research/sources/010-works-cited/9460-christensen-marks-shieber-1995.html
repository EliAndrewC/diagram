christensen-marks-shieber-1995

Christensen, Marks and Shieber, "An Empirical Study of Algorithms for Point-Feature Label Placement", ACM Transactions on Graphics 14(3), 1995 (https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~shieber/Biblio/Papers/tog-final.pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed computer-graphics paper by three Harvard and Mitsubishi researchers comparing algorithms for placing point labels on a map. It sets out the problem as cartographers and earlier programs had framed it: a small ranked set of positions around each point, and a cost made of overlaps and of the positions' ranking.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the standard formal statement of point-label placement, and it names its sources: the objective follows Yoeli (1972) and the idea of labeling quality Imhof (1962, 1975), neither of which could be read publicly. Its concern is the search algorithm, not the design rules, which it takes from cartography as given; its figure ranking eight positions is a typical set, not the only one. That ranking follows Imhof's preference for Latin type read left to right (the upper right first); it says nothing of how period Chinese or Japanese maps, commonly written in vertical columns, placed names, so it carries our captions' convention and not a period one.

Used for: the eight ranked positions around a point; a labeling's quality as overlaps plus position preference (presentation)
