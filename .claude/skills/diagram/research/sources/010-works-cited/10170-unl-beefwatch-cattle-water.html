University of Nebraska-Lincoln, BeefWatch, Meeting Water Needs of Cattle in the Feedlot (2022) (https://beef.unl.edu/beefwatch/2022/meeting-water-needs-cattle-feedlot/)

What it is: An extension article by Alfredo DiCostanzo, a Nebraska Extension educator, in the University of Nebraska-Lincoln's BeefWatch newsletter (June 2022) on getting water to feedlot cattle in hot weather: daily intake against dry matter eaten, the rate at which cattle drink, the minutes each animal spends at the trough, and how much trough edge and flow a pen needs.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the rate at which a bovine drinks from a trough, which is what sets how fast a trough's edge turns over. It is written for modern feedlot beef cattle under heat stress, not for draft oxen at a premodern yard; we take only the drinking rate (1.1 to 3.7 gallons a minute), which belongs to the animal more than the farm. But that rate is also measured on heavy, grain-fed finishing steers drinking in hot weather, like its intake figures. A smaller working ox in ordinary weather probably drinks more slowly, so the figure bounds how fast a trough's edge turns over from above rather than measuring it. The article gives the rate as an extension educator's expected figure, without citing the study behind it.

Used for: the rate at which cattle drink at a trough (stable yards)
