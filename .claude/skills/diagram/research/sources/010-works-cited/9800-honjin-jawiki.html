honjin-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, 本陣 (Honjin) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9C%AC%E9%99%A3)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the honjin, the house in an Edo-period post town where daimyo, hatamoto, shogunal officials and imperial envoys lodged, with its privileges, its size and a list of surviving examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is where a travelling official of rank actually slept, and it names the three things a honjin was allowed that an inn was not: a front gate, a genkan with a shikidai and a jodan-no-ma. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, and one the article itself flags as inadequately sourced: the sentences naming who lodged there, the front gate, the shikidai genkan and the jodan-no-ma carry no footnote, and the closer work its bibliography names, Okuma Yoshikuni's 1942 study of the Tokaido post stations and their honjin (National Diet Library Digital Collections), was not read; that a honjin was a post town's lodging house kept by a commoner family, not a samurai's residence or an office; and that it does not say where in the house the jodan-no-ma lay.

Used for: who lodged at a honjin, and its front gate, shikidai genkan and jodan-no-ma (buildings)
