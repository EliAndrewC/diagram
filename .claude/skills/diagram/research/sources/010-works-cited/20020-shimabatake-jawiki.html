shimabatake-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 島畑 ("island fields", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/島畑)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on shimabatake, the raised dry plots heaped up inside paddy from the soil dug out when the ground was lowered to bring the paddy within reach of its water: how they were made, where they are recorded, when they began and what was grown on them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that sets a dry field inside the paddy on an alluvial fan, and it dates the practice to about the thirteenth century on the mainstream view. Its limits: a short summary resting on Mizoguchi (2002) and other studies centered on Aichi, whose count comes from the 1884 cadastral maps; and its crops are a short list given as examples - cotton, mulberry and figs "and so on", which it says "varied sharply by era" - so its silence on grain is not evidence that none was grown (in Owari, cotton commonly followed a winter wheat or barley crop on dry fields).

Used for: the island dry field in the paddy on fans, terraces and levees, and the crops the page names on it, none of them grain (fields)
