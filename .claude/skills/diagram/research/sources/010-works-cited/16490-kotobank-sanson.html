kotobank-sanson

Kotobank, 散村 ("dispersed village", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the entry by Nakata Eiichi from Shogakukan's Nihon daihyakka zensho (Nipponica) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%95%A3%E6%9D%91-70971)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the dispersed village, gathering dictionary and encyclopedia entries; the Nipponica entry distinguishes planned and irregular dispersal and names the Japanese examples of the irregular kind.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows the dispersed village in Japan as a set of named regions rather than a general form, which is what lets the record call it regional. It is a signed modern encyclopedia entry naming examples, not a survey. Its list of irregular dispersed villages (the Tonami plain, the lower Ōi, the Sanuki and Izumo plains) ends "and others", so it shows where the form is typical, not that it is confined to those places. It names them as a modern geographer sees them and does not date each dispersal; the Heibonsha extract on the same page dates Tonami's to medieval reclamation. It gives no count. Its planned kind (the Meiji-era tondenhei villages of Hokkaido, the American township) is not premodern and is not used here.

Used for: the regions of dispersed villages in Japan (archetypes)
