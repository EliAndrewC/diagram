jochi-kotobank

Kotobank, 除地 (jochi, exempted land), the entries of several Japanese dictionaries, those used being the Yamakawa dictionary of Japanese history (山川 日本史小辞典 改訂新版) and Takano Toshihiko's in the Encyclopedia Nipponica (日本大百科全書) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/除地-80471)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering the dictionary entries for jochi, Edo-period land the lord exempted from the annual tax, and how the land survey recorded it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one historical form of the setting's tax-free temple land: the precincts, fields and house land of temples and shrines exempted from tax and levies, either left out of the village's assessment altogether or entered and then exempted. It says nothing of how large such land was or where in the village its fields lay, and several of its entries count as jochi the land of privileged farmers and townspeople too, and its entries set jochi apart from, and below, the vermilion-seal land (shuinchi) held under the shogun's certificate, and from land exempted at a new survey without a certificate (misuteji). So it carries one of Edo Japan's several kinds of exempted temple land, and the exemption and not the size or the scatter.

Used for: temple and shrine fields exempted from tax, and how the survey recorded them (fields)
