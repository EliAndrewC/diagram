yokushitsu-kotobank

Kotobank, 浴室 (Yokushitsu) - Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), Nakamura Hitoshi (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B5%B4%E5%AE%A4-653701)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the bath room, carrying the Nipponica entry by Nakamura Hitoshi on the bath room abroad and in Japan, from the temple bath to the modern unit bath.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the family bath of noble and warrior houses around 1600 as a one-person tub with a built-on hearth in a corner of the doma, which is where a residence's bath sat and how small it was. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary; that the sentence is dated by the one before it to the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries, so it describes the early Edo house rather than the later one; that it gives no dimensions; and that it cites no period source, while the Heibonsha Sekai Daihyakka excerpt on the same page says that until about the first half of the early modern period "furo" meant the steam bath, and that soaking in a hot-water tub became the main way of bathing only in the later Edo period, so the one-person tub is this entry's account of one form of house bath, not the only form a warrior house could have had.

Used for: the family bath of warrior houses around 1600 in a corner of the doma, and why it was seldom used (buildings)
