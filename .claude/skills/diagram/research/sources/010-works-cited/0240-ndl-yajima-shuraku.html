ndl-yajima-shuraku

National Diet Library Collaborative Reference Database, case 1000071171, quoting Jinkichi Yajima, Nihon no Shuraku (https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?page=ref_view&id=1000071171)

What it is: A reference-desk answer in the National Diet Library's database, quoting the settlement-morphology definitions of Jinkichi Yajima's Nihon no Shuraku (1967), answered by a prefectural library in 2010 - among them the roson, the farming village strung along a narrow street.

Why it applies, and its limits: The definition states one-side and both-side as alternatives WITHIN one settlement type, which is the closest the readable record comes to saying both arrangements are normal for anything - but the linear feature the houses attach to is a ROAD, so it is an analogy and not evidence about brooks. It is also a library's quotation of a book rather than the book, the quoted span is a definition without its worked cases, and the type is defined with no era attached, so nothing in it says a village of that form is premodern.

Used for: a farming linear village's houses on one side of its street or on both
