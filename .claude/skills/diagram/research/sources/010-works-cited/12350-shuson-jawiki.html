ja.wikipedia 集村 ("nucleated village", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9B%86%E6%9D%91)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the nucleated village in settlement geography, with sections on the clustered village, the row village (retsuson), the green village and the marsh village. The row-village section gives three subtypes: the road village (roson), the street village (gaison) and the medieval forest-strip village of southern Germany and Poland (Waldhufendorf), of which only the first two bear on us. It is a stub article; its sources are Yajima's settlement-geography textbook (1956), a 1997 dictionary of human geography and a high-school geography study guide.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets out the two forms a village takes along a road - houses on both sides with little trade, or packed close with shops and lodging - and says they look alike in plan. The categories are those of modern settlement geography, borrowed from German usage, not Edo terms, and the article gives no share of villages in either form. Its road- and street-village examples are Japanese only (road villages common in Edo-period new-field settlements such as Santome Shinden; street villages such as market towns, post towns and temple-gate towns, many of which later grew into regional cities). It names no road or street village in China; its one mention of China is the Yangtze delta, as an example of the marsh village. Of the other forms, only two bear on us. The clustered village it calls widespread across Japan and most common in Kinki and Hokuriku. For the marsh village it gives Japanese examples (the ring-levee villages of the lower Kiso rivers, the lower Tone water country). The green village and the round village it places in Europe only.

Used for: the row village and the street village along a highway (ways); the nucleated village packed closer than a dispersed one, with no definition of how dense it must be, and the forms it takes (homesteads)
