kotobank-kataarashi-nipponica

Kuroda Hideo, "Kataarashi", Nihon daihyakka zensho (Nipponica), Shogakukan, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%89%87%E8%8D%92%E3%81%97-1517498)

What it is: A general encyclopedia's entry on kataarashi, by a historian of medieval Japan, reproduced on Kotobank. It defines the word as Heian-period paddy and dry field rested in alternate years, quotes two poems that use it, and traces it to the ekiden of the ancient land code. Kotobank gives the entry no date.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the resting plot this record asks about and gives two reasons for the rest, poor soil and short water. It is a short dictionary entry, and it describes the Heian period, not the medieval village or the early modern one. That the rest came in strict alternate years is disputed elsewhere: one historian's review treats the resting land as ground idle for that one year, and an unsourced encyclopedia note denies that alternate-year farming existed at all.

Used for: that a plot of paddy could be rested for a year, for poor soil or short water (fields)
