kiso-unzaihou

Rinya-cho (Japan Forestry Agency), Kiso-style felling and transport method pages (https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/chubu/kiso/morigatari/unzaihou.html)

What it is: A page published by the Chubu Regional Forest Office of Japan's Forestry Agency, part of a public-history series on the Kiso forests, describing the Kiso-style felling and timber transport method - axe felling, the sledge and chute haul down to the water, loose-log drives, and rafting - as it was practiced from the late sixteenth century until 1920. The page notes that its illustrated guide is what the office supposes to be a Meiji-era copy of late-Edo originals, and gives no author.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for how timber reached a town, and it describes that chain in order: the loose-log drive handed off to rafts at the tying station, three men riding each raft, and the run down to the timber storage yard at Nagoya and the bay port beyond, which is what puts a log yard where we put it. It is a government agency's explanatory page rather than a study, and it describes one valley's system, so the crew size and the yard locations are a worked example of how such a trade ran and not a national standard.

Used for: the loose-log drive (the page's terms are kotari-gari and okawa-gari) handing off to rafts at the tsunaba; three-man raft crews; onward transport to the Nagoya and bay-port storage yards
