kimura-1934-bochi-menseki

Kimura Hideo, "我が國都市に於ける共同墓地に關する研究の中その面積の算出法に就いて" (On how to calculate the area of common cemeteries in the cities of our country), 造園雑誌 (Journal of the Japanese Institute of Landscape Architecture) vol. 1 no. 2, 1934, pp. 102-110 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jila1934/1/2/1_2_102/_pdf)

What it is: A lecture paper by a landscape architect proposing how to size a Japanese city's common cemetery, from the plots in use in Tokyo's municipal cemeteries: it quotes a North American formula (plot area times yearly burials times the years before a grave is reused, times a factor for paths and buildings) and replaces it, for cities that cremate and inter a household's urns in one family plot, with plot area times the number of registered households times that factor.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source read that turns a plot size and a population into a burial ground's area, and it does so for the very mode this setting follows - cremation, with a household's urns interred together. Its limits are that it describes Tokyo's municipal cemeteries of the early 1930s, laid out with paths, offices and rest houses, not premodern village grounds; that its figures are few (six cemeteries), as the author says; that the text available is an OCR of the scanned journal, whose characters are spaced and occasionally misread and whose tables came through without their figures, so only the numbers its prose repeats can be used (plots in use of 0.5 to 2.61 tsubo, 2.075 on average; a path factor of 1.46 to 4.41, 1.78 without the new Tama cemetery; and the author's own recommendation of 1 tsubo and 1.5 to 2); that the one-tsubo plot it recommends is sized for a concrete vault holding twenty to thirty urns, which the author says was lately devised to cut the cost of city plots, a 1930s form rather than a premodern family grave, which would take more ground; and that the author himself sets country grounds apart as still burying the dead whole and using land far less economically, so its plot and path figures are a lower bound for a village's burial ground, not a measure of it.

Used for: how much ground a household's family plot takes, and how much a burial ground needs beyond its plots for paths (religion-and-death)
