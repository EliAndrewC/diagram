aso-kayaba

Aso-pedia (the Aso digital museum), 茅場 ("thatch meadow", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://aso-dm.net/article/article-2652)

What it is: A page of the Aso region's digital museum (Kumamoto) on the kayaba, the grass ground kept for the pampas grass that thatched the roofs, and on how a settlement cut it and shared the reroofing work.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one figure the record found for how much thatch meadow a settlement of a given size kept, and when the grass was cut. It describes the Aso grasslands within living memory (the page says most roofs had gone to tile by the decade from 1955), so its figure is one region's and not a general rule. The page is an unsigned summary (updated 2020) that cites no survey or document for the figure. It gives the figure as a round rule of thumb (a settlement of 50 to 60 households "must secure" 20 to 30 hectares), so it carries an order of magnitude, not a measurement. It describes the Japanese commonly held pampas-grass meadow and says nothing of the China-modeled half of the setting.

Used for: the thatch meadow a settlement of 50 to 60 households kept (vegetation)
