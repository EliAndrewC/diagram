What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the cattle crush or livestock crush, the frame that restrains a beast, with its history as the ox-shoeing frame of England, Spain and America.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that the frame's old use was shoeing oxen, that it carries a belly sling, and that in Spain it was a village fixture, which is the standing frame this page's smithy apron carries. A tertiary summary used for the form and the fixture; it gives no dimension. And the frame is a Western fixture: the page names England, Spain and America and no East Asian use, while Japan's own hoof practice was straw, so the frame on these maps is a transplant the smithy sentence labels as this page's reading.

Used for: the ox shod slung in a standing shoeing frame
