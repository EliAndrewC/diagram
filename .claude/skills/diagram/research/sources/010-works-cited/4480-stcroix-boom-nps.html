NPS, "The St. Croix Boom" (https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/stcroixboom.htm)

What it is: A public-history article by Dan Ott, written in 2013 for the National Park Service's St. Croix National Scenic Riverway, on the log-handling works of the St. Croix river between Minnesota and Wisconsin from 1856 to 1914: the river divided lengthwise into log channels and holding pens beside a maintained navigation channel, the hinged sheer boom, and the lawsuits and negligence rulings the works drew.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the physical arrangement of a working boom - a river divided lengthwise, the logs held in pens along one side and a channel kept open beside them - which is why our timber-town booms run along a bank rather than across the river. Its limits are that it is industrial-era North America, where steam tugs, an army engineers' navigation channel and sawmill-scale volumes share the description with the physical arrangement, so the two have to be separated by hand and only the arrangement transfers; that it is a public-history summary rather than a study or a primary record; and that the navigation lawsuits it records are a modern American statutory and common-law regime with no premodern East Asian counterpart, so they explain that a boom must leave a channel, not who would have sued over it in our setting.

Used for: lengthwise division into log channels and holding pens beside a maintained navigation channel; the hinged sheer boom; navigation lawsuits and negligence rulings
