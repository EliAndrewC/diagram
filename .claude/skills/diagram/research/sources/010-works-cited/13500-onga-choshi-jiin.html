onga-choshi-jiin

Onga town, 『遠賀町誌』 (the history of Onga town), part 7, chapter 3, "遠賀町の寺院と堂塔" ("The temples and halls of Onga town", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.town.onga.lg.jp/site/choshi/26919.html)

What it is: A chapter of a Fukuoka farming town's official history, put online by the town, describing each of its temples - founding, sect, buildings - with the entries of the Edo gazetteers of Chikuzen, of the Fukuoka prefecture gazetteer (福岡県地理全誌) and of the Onga district gazetteer quoted for each, and then the town's wayside halls and stone images one by one.

Why it applies, and its limits: The gazetteer entries it quotes give, temple by temple, the main hall's size in ken, the temple's land and its count of parish households, which is the measured village temple this record otherwise lacks, and its wayside chapter places each jizo and hall - at a crossroads, beside a household, in a shrine's grounds - and tells of one whose cult is tied to the village's great well. Its limits: it is one small district of northern Kyushu; the page does not date the prefecture gazetteer, a compilation of the early Meiji years rather than of the Edo period; the land figure it gives is in two cases smaller than the temple's own main hall, so what that figure measures is not stated and it is not read as the precinct; and the wayside chapter describes the images as they stand today, some moved or rebuilt.

Used for: village temples' hall sizes and parish households; a temple at the edge of the houses on slightly higher ground; a whole village becoming one temple's parish; jizo at a crossroads, and a jizo whose cult is tied to a village well (religion-and-death)
