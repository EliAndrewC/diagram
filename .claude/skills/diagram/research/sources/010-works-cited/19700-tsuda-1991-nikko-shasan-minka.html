Tsuda Yoshiki, "On the room's names, constructions and facilities of the farm houses in the north Kanto area in the Edo period from the historical materials of Nikkou Shasan" (in Japanese), Journal of Architecture, Planning and Environmental Engineering (Architectural Institute of Japan) no. 426, August 1991, pp. 173-182 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/426/0/426_KJ00004076561/_pdf)

What it is: A paper by an architectural historian of Kanagawa University that reads 19 house-plan registers of the 18th and 19th centuries, drawn up by farm villages along the shogun's road to Nikko so that his retinue could be lodged, for the room names, floor mats, walls, doors and windows, alcoves, closets, hearths, baths and indoor privies of the houses the registers draw; each feature is read only from the registers that record it (the bath from five villages', the indoor privy from six).

Why it applies, and its limits: The registers were made before 1868 and most draw a whole village house by house, so the paper gives counts and dates for the bath and the indoor privy that no later survey can. But they were drawn to lodge the shogun's retinue, not to count baths: two of them (Makuta, Ekazaki) draw only 3 to 5 houses in 10, the copy Kotsutsumi sent up left out its "unsightly houses", and the author finds features drawn in one copy and left out of another (the stove at Kotsutsumi), so a count is of what the drawings show and may run low. Its limits: it reads the main house (bar one line on the lattice windows of Tsuruta's retirement houses), so it says nothing of other outbuildings, nor where a house without an indoor privy kept its privy, and whether the baths it places beyond the stable wing were joined to the house it does not say. The villages lie in north Kanto near Utsunomiya, Koga and Iwatsuki (two near Imaichi), the registers are from 1712 to 1842, and the author's reason for putting the bath beside the stable is offered as a guess. The paper gives the year of one village's register two ways (Kyoho 13, 1728, on its first page and in its tables, Kyoho 12 elsewhere in the text).

Used for: the bath and the indoor privy in farmhouses of 1770-1842, how many houses had each and where the bath stood (homesteads)
