Tsuda Yoshiki, "On the room's names, constructions and facilities of the farm houses in the north Kanto area in the Edo period from the historical materials of Nikkou Shasan" (in Japanese), Journal of Architecture, Planning and Environmental Engineering (Architectural Institute of Japan) no. 426, August 1991, pp. 173-182 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/426/0/426_KJ00004076561/_pdf)

What it is: A paper by an architectural historian of Kanagawa University that reads 19 house-plan registers of the 18th and 19th centuries, drawn up by farm villages along the shogun's road to Nikko so that his retinue could be lodged, for the room names, floors, walls, windows, closets, hearths, baths and indoor privies of every house in each village.

Why it applies, and its limits: The registers were made before 1868 and cover whole villages house by house, so the paper gives counts and dates for the bath and the indoor privy that no later survey can. Its limits: it covers the main house alone, so it says nothing of outbuildings, and whether the baths it places beyond the stable wing were joined to the house it does not say. The villages lie in the north Kanto plain near Utsunomiya and Iwatsuki, the registers are from 1712 to 1842, and the author's reason for putting the bath beside the stable is offered as a guess. The paper gives the year of one village's register two ways (Kyoho 12 in the text, Kyoho 13, 1728, in its tables).

Used for: the bath and the indoor privy in farmhouses of 1770-1842, how many houses had each and where the bath stood (homesteads)
