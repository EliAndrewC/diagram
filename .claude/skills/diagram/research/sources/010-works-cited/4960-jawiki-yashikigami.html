jawiki-yashikigami

ja.wikipedia 屋敷神; minka-en.com 屋敷神; satologue.com 屋敷神様 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/屋敷神)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on 屋敷神 (yashikigami, the guardian deity of a household's own plot), together with matching pages on the same subject from two Japanese folk-culture sites, minka-en.com and satologue.com. They cover the two patterns of household worship and the form the shrine takes, which is almost always a small stone or wooden hokora rather than a building.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells us the form of the household shrine - a small stone or wooden hokora, almost never a shrine hall - and where it commonly stood, which the page gives as the northwest or the northeast of the plot without preferring either; the page goes on to say that treating the northeast as the unlucky demon gate is a later overlay from Onmyodo and a comparatively recent custom, so our maps take a corner and not a compass rule. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary which Japanese Wikipedia itself flags as under-sourced and possibly original research, a heavier discount than the usual encyclopedia caveat; that it describes a folk custom that varied by region and household, so it gives a common pattern and not a rule; and that it dates none of it, so nothing here fixes the practice to the era our maps depict.

Used for: the two patterns; a small stone or wooden hokora at the plot's corner, especially the NE 鬼門.
