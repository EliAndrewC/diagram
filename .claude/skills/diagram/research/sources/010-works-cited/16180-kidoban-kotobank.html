kidoban-kotobank

Kotobank, 木戸番 ("kidoban, the ward-gate keeper", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering the entries of Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen, Nihon kokugo daijiten and Nipponica and Heibonsha's Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9C%A8%E6%88%B8%E7%95%AA-475012)

What it is: The published encyclopedias' entries on the gates set across the streets of Edo-period towns and on their keepers: the gates' form, how many a town set, the hours they were shut and what the keeper did.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that gives a count of gates below the great cities - one to several blocks, against one to each block in a large town - and it describes the gate's form. It is a tertiary source: dictionary and encyclopedia entries that give no references of their own, all of them on Japan. The entries place the gates chiefly in the great cities - Edo, Kyoto, Osaka - and the form, the closing hour and the keeper's night round they give are the city of Edo's. The count is one sentence of the old Sekai Daihyakka's entry on the gate, written of the great cities and not of any named town. Its 町 can be read as a ward-block rather than a town (a large block with a gate of its own, small blocks sharing one), so it gives a shape for a small town, not a figure. The Nipponica entry is by Inagaki Fumio, a period-drama consultant and novelist rather than a historian, and the entries give the great gate's width in two slightly different figures.

Used for: how many gates a small town set, the gate's form and hours, and the keeper's night round (urban-features)
