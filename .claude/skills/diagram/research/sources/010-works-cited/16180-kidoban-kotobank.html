kidoban-kotobank

Kotobank, 木戸番 ("kidoban, the ward-gate keeper", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering the entries of Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen, Nihon kokugo daijiten and Nipponica and Heibonsha's Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9C%A8%E6%88%B8%E7%95%AA-475012)

What it is: The published encyclopedias' entries on the gates set across the streets of Edo-period towns and on their keepers: the gates' form, how many a town set, the hours they were shut and what the keeper did.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says how many gates a middle or small town set - one to several blocks, against one to each block in a large town - and it describes the gate's form. The form it gives is Edo's, the counts are general rather than any named town's, and the entries give the great gate's width in two slightly different figures.

Used for: how many gates a small town set, the gate's form and hours, and the keeper's night round (urban-features)
