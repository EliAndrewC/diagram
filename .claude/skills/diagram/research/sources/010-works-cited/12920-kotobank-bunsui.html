kotobank-bunsui

kotobank 分水 ("Water division", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%88%86%E6%B0%B4-128545)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the reference-encyclopedia entries on 分水, among them the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia's entry and an excerpt from that encyclopedia's older edition, under its entry 分木 (bungi; the entry is by Kitamura Toshio), defining the graduated division stake.

Why it applies, and its limits: The bungi excerpt says what one kind of fixed marker at a two-way village split was (the entry calls it one of the technical means, 技術的手段の一種): a graduated stake at the division point, by whose height the water was divided, with a Shiga example. It is a cut-off excerpt of a dictionary entry, so it gives the device and one place, not how common it was or what it looked like beyond a stake. It also gives no era: it names the Shiga example by a twentieth-century town (Asai-chō, merged into Nagahama in 2006) and stops before any date. The full entry, on Kotobank's own 分木 page, is the closer source for when the stake was used.

Used for: the bungi (分木), a graduated stake at a two-way split (water)
