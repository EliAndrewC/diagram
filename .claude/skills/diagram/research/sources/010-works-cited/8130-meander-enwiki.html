meander-enwiki

Wikipedia, "Meander" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meander)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on meanders - the bends a channel makes as it winds across its own floodplain. It gives the geometry of a bend (its radius against the channel's width, its wavelength, its sinuosity) and the erosion and deposition that cut one, and it credits the radius figure to Leopold and Langbein's 1966 Scientific American article on river meanders.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the one number our ditches are drawn from - how tight a turn running water makes, measured against the channel's own width - together with the bank behavior that produces it. Its limits are that it is a tertiary encyclopedia article rather than the survey work behind it; that the radius it gives is the curvature a FREE channel settles into over years by cutting its own outer bank, while a farm ditch is dug and kept to a line, so its curve is set by whoever dug it and by the field boundary it runs along rather than by the water; and that it gives no figure for a maintained earthen ditch at all, so the step from a river's bend to a ditch's is this project's reasoning and not the article's.

Used for: how far a natural channel's bend swings against its own width - a bend radius of two to three channel widths - and what a turn does to the two banks
