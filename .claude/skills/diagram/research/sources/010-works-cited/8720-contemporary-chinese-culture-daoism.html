What it is: The "Daoism" entry of the Routledge Encyclopedia of Contemporary Chinese Culture, republished on the en-academic reference mirror, on how Daoist clergy of the Zhengyi and Quanzhen traditions live, train and serve.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the residence half of this project's householder clergy in the source's own words: a Zhengyi priest does not usually live in a temple though he is normally affiliated with one, and such priests are members of families of hereditary priests serving local communities. That is what puts temple families in ordinary houses around a precinct on these maps rather than in cells inside it. Its limits are that it is a tertiary encyclopedia entry read on a mirror rather than in the publisher's own edition, and that, like the Patheos article beside it, it describes China and Taiwan today rather than any premodern century - which bites harder here than a date usually does, because the contemporary Zhengyi clergy's non-residence was shaped by the twentieth century, temples confiscated and the tradition rebuilt from the 1980s around household ritual specialists. The historical form of the claim would want a historian of the Chinese clergy, which this record has not reached.

Used for: the Zhengyi priest who does not live in the temple he serves, and the hereditary priestly family
