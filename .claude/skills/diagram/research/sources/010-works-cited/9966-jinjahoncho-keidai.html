jinjahoncho-keidai

Jinja Honcho (the Association of Shinto Shrines), "境内について" (about the shrine precinct) (in Japanese; https://www.jinjahoncho.or.jp/omairi/keidai/)

What it is: The national association of Shinto shrines' own guide for visitors to what stands in a shrine precinct, from the torii and the approach to the small subsidiary shrines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where a torii stands - at the boundary between the shrine and the outside - and that small subsidiary shrines stand inside the precinct besides the main hall, which is what decides whether one arch serves more than one shrine. It is a general guide for present-day visitors from the body that has overseen most Japanese shrines since 1946. It describes the precinct as it has stood since the Meiji separation of kami worship from Buddhism (1868), which removed the Buddhist halls many Edo-period precincts held. The torii at the boundary and the subsidiary shrines inside it are older than that separation, but the page gives no dates for any form it names. It also says itself that precinct layouts vary from shrine to shrine, so it gives the general rule and not a fixed plan. It has no measurements and nothing on a shrine inside a residence.

Used for: that the torii marks the precinct's boundary and that subsidiary shrines stand within the precinct (religion-and-death).
