hudson-munoz-2023

Mark Hudson and Irene M. Munoz Fernandez, "Henceforth fishermen and hunters are to be restrained: towards a political ecology of animal usage in premodern Japan", Asian Archaeology (2023), open access (https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3527987_3/component/file_3528006/content)

What it is: A peer-reviewed open-access REVIEW article - not a report of new fieldwork - on how animals were used in premodern Japan - hunted, herded, kept, eaten - which sets out to test the standard account rather than repeat it. Its concern is the whole archipelago across a long span, and it draws on excavated bone assemblages, genetics and documentary sources together.

Why it applies, and its limits: These maps depict a setting modeled on pre-Meiji Japan as much as on imperial China, so whether a penned pig belongs on a Japanese-flavored map at all is a question the record has to answer, and this is the most current scholarly treatment of it. It is cited here for a NEGATIVE: it lets the map say plainly that a pig on a pond dike is a Chinese form rather than leaving the provenance implicit. The limit that matters most is what the paper is arguing AGAINST: the authors name "Buddhism suppressed pig-keeping" as an assumption under review, so the paper is evidence against the standard account and not for it. The second limit narrows what we may claim from it: the paper records pigs KEPT in early modern Japan - significant finds of Sus bones at the Satsuma residence in Edo, Osaka castle, Hakata and Nagasaki - so the honest negative is about the FORM, a pen on a fish-pond dike, and not about Japanese pig-keeping at all. It says nothing about fish ponds or about pens beside water, so it bounds the question without answering it.

Used for: the two standard assumptions named as assumptions; pork consumption declining from the middle of the first millennium AD; Okinawa as the stated exception and pannage - pigs loose in forest - as the mainland form
