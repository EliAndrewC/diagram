hirahaku-rokujizo

Hiratsuka City Museum, "六地蔵" ("Six jizo", title translated), in its stone-Buddha walk of the old Hiratsuka post town (in Japanese; https://www.hirahaku.jp/hakubutsukan_archive/minzoku/00000063/34.html)

What it is: A page of a city museum's folk-culture archive describing the two sets of stone six jizo at the gate of the Hoshakuin temple in Kami-Hiratsuka (Kanagawa), with their inscribed dates, and what a six jizo is.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates two sets by their inscriptions, 1753 and 1797, and says six jizo stand at the entrance of a temple or a graveyard, and that stone ones are said to appear at temples and graveyards from the end of the Muromachi period. Its limits: the two dated sets stand at a temple gate, not at a graveyard or a cremation ground; the Muromachi dating is hedged ("it is said") and unsourced; it describes one town in eastern Japan.

Used for: stone six jizo at temple and graveyard entrances before 1868 (religion-and-death)
