jataff-fuyu-kaki

Nishio Toshihiko, 「Spreading the backyard persimmon across the country: Fukushima Saiji, who popularized the Fuyu」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「裏庭の柿を全国に普及 〜「富有」を広めた福島才治〜」), reprinted from the Nogyo Kyosai Shimbun of 12 December 2001 by the Japan Association for Techno-innovation in Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (JATAFF) (in Japanese; https://www.jataff.or.jp/senjin2/33.html)

What it is: A short newspaper article, one of a series on figures who advanced Japanese farming, telling how the Fuyu persimmon spread from a single old tree in a Gifu village in the Meiji period; reprinted on the website of JATAFF, an agricultural research foundation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that every house in the farm villages of the past had an old persimmon in its dooryard, and it names one such tree standing in the bamboo grove behind a house. Its limits are that the every-house line is a 2001 writer's generalization for a popular readership rather than a survey, about the farm villages of an undated past (most likely the prewar villages its readers remembered, not the Edo period). The one tree it locates is a single instance. The page says it was already over a hundred years old in the mid-Meiji period, so it was planted in the Edo period. But it stood in the bamboo grove behind the house rather than in the dooryard, in a district known for persimmons since the Edo period, so it is less typical of an ordinary farmhouse.

Used for: the old persimmon in every farmhouse's dooryard; one standing behind the house (homesteads)
