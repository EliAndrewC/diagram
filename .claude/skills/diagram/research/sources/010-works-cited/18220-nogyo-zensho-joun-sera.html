JK, 農業者ネットせら (the Sera farmers' network blog), J.Kの書斎：農業全書『鋤芸』抄訳（1） ("JK's study: a partial translation of the Nogyo Zensho's section on hoeing and weeding, part 1", title translated), 2019 (in Japanese; http://3d.txt-nifty.com/blog/2019/07/post-8cc77c.html)

What it is: A blog post by a writer signed only JK rendering into modern Japanese part of the Nogyo Zensho, the farming manual Miyazaki Yasusada published in 1697, from its general section on hoeing and weeding.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lets a reader see what Japan's standard Edo-period farming manual says of a dry field: that it had ridges, that weeds were spread on the ridge tops, and that millet was hoed before its seedlings rose to the ridge tops. It is an amateur's translation of an excerpt, it does not say which edition it worked from (it names the Iwanami Bunko only as where the book is in print), and this passage follows Chinese agronomy almost line for line: the horse's-ear seedling, "ten hoeings give eight parts of hulled grain", and the very rule cited here, hoe millet once before its seedlings are level with the ridges, are all in the Qimin yaoshu, a sixth-century north Chinese manual that Miyazaki knew in large part through the Nongzheng quanshu. So it shows north Chinese dry-field ridging as Edo Japan's leading manual taught it. It is weaker evidence that Japanese dry fields were actually worked that way, and it states what a learned manual advised rather than what every village did.

Used for: that an Edo dry field was worked in ridges, its millet hoed before the seedlings rose to the ridge tops (fields)
