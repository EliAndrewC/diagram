ubusuna-jinja-ameblo

「At shrines with no temizuya」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「手水舎が無い神社では」), a post on the blog 幸せを呼び込む神社 神さまからの後押しを頂く参拝とは, on Ameba Blog (in Japanese; https://ameblo.jp/ubusuna-jinja/entry-11582938316.html)

What it is: A personal blog post, undated on the page, by an anonymous writer on a devotional blog about luck-bringing shrine visits, on how to purify oneself at a shrine that has no roofed water pavilion.

Why it applies, and its limits: It observes that many shrines have no pavilion at all and that unstaffed shrines are especially many in the provinces, which bears on whether a village shrine's basin stood unroofed. It is one writer's observation of shrines today, not a survey, and says nothing of the period before 1868. Its numbers (100,000 to 120,000 shrines, over eight in ten unstaffed) are the writer's own hedged guesses. The neglect it describes, with basins left dry or missing at unstaffed rural shrines and one priest serving many of them, is a condition of modern Japan, so a shrine with no pavilion today does not show that a village shrine before 1868 never had one.

Used for: the basin with no pavilion (religion-and-death.html).
