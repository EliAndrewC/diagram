chongming-yanwuchang

Chongming District People's Government (Shanghai), 崇明演武场摭谈 ("notes on the Chongming drill ground", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 2021 (in Chinese; https://www.shcm.gov.cn/zjcm/006008/20210423/4fba98a9-d675-4ad7-89cf-e1ead920d501.html)

What it is: A local-history article on the district government's site on the drill ground of Chongming county, built in 1586, drawing on the county gazetteers from the Ming to the Republic and on the last drill hall, measured before it was pulled down about 2000.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that a Chinese county seat generally kept a drill ground, and gives one county's ground, its buildings and its hall's measured size - the Chinese counterpart of a state martial hall. It is one coastal county with a garrison against pirates, so its ground may be larger than an inland county's; the measured hall is a later rebuilding, not the three-bay hall of 1586; the article is popular local history, though it names the gazetteers it draws on.

Used for: a Chinese county's drill ground and drill hall, their parts and size (buildings)
