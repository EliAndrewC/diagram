chongming-yanwuchang

Chongming District People's Government (Shanghai), 崇明演武场摭谈 ("notes on the Chongming drill ground", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 2021 (in Chinese; https://www.shcm.gov.cn/zjcm/006008/20210423/4fba98a9-d675-4ad7-89cf-e1ead920d501.html)

What it is: A local-history article on the district government's site on the drill ground of Chongming county, built in 1586, drawing on the county gazetteers from the Ming to the Republic and on the last drill hall, measured before it was pulled down about 2000.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that a Chinese county seat generally kept a drill ground, and gives one county's ground, its buildings and its hall's measured size - the Chinese counterpart of a state martial hall. It is one coastal county, and an exceptional one: from 1658 its ground was enlarged by a regional commander (zongbing, of the second rank, where the magistrate was of the seventh) for a garrison the 1662 stele puts at 8,000 men, so the later ground and hall are a large garrison's, bigger than an ordinary county's - the magistrate's ground of 1586 (about a li, a three-bay hall) is the nearer to a county's; the measured hall (nine bays, 29 m by 11.5 m) is a later rebuilding of unstated date, not the three-bay hall of 1586; the ground's 37.5 mu is the article's own reconstruction from the gazetteers laid on modern streets; the article is popular local history, though it names the gazetteers it draws on.

Used for: a Chinese county's drill ground and drill hall, their parts and size (buildings)
