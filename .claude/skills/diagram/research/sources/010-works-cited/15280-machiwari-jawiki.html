machiwari-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 町割 ("town lot division", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%94%BA%E5%89%B2)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on machiwari, the laying out of streets and lots in a pre-modern Japanese town, with examples from Heian-kyo to the castle towns and a note on modern Kyoto's land readjustment.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states how the Heian-kyo block was divided in practice, from mansions to commoner lots. It is a short tertiary article summarizing Japanese historical encyclopedias, and its Heian sentence has no footnote of its own, so it is used for the kinds of division and not for any size or count. Its sentence on Kyoto's early twentieth-century land readjustment, which treated both sides of a street as one ward, describes a modern scheme and is not used; the medieval both-sided ward (ryogawa-machi) that scheme inherited is a separate, pre-modern statement in the article.

Used for: the many ways the Heian block was divided (cities/fabric.html)
