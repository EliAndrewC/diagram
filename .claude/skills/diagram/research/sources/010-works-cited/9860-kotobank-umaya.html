kotobank-umaya

Digital Daijisen, Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha Kaitei shinban Sekai dai-hyakka jiten (article by Miyazawa Satoshi), s.v. 厩 (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8E%A9-35131)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of the dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the umaya, the stable: the farmhouse's stable inside or beside the house, and the warrior's stable of a shoin-style residence, with its parts, the formal three-bay form of the carpenters' manual Shomei, and the large stable of Hikone castle.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a signed encyclopedia article by an architectural historian that sets the warrior's stable apart from the farmer's and names what the warrior's held, which is what a posting's stable is drawn from. It gives the formal stable's length and a farm stall's size but no depth for a warrior's stall and no count of horses for any rank; its formal form is that of the sixteenth- and early seventeenth-century sources it names. That formal stable and Hikone's 21 bays are the stables of top-rank residences and of a great daimyo's castle, far larger than any posting's, so they bound a posting's stable from above and are not a model to copy. The Kinki farm stable's 3 m comes from modern studies of surviving farmhouses, with no date given, not from a record of the period. The article cites no references, so its numbers carry an encyclopedia's authority and no more.

Used for: the parts of a warrior's stable, the formal gable-entered stable three ken long, Hikone's 21 bays, and the Kinki farm stable about 3 m square (buildings)
