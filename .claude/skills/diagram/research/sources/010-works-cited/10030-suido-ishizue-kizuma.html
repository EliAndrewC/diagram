suido-ishizue-kizuma

Suido no Ishizue, 「Living on the land: the life and culture of the Isawa plain」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大地に暮らす［胆沢平野の生活文化］」), in the series on the Isawa plain (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/nihon/04/07.html)

What it is: A page of Suido no Ishizue (水土の礎), a website on the history of Japan's irrigated farmland, describing the dispersed farmsteads of the Isawa plain in Iwate: the windbreak grove (egune) on each homestead's northwest side, the kizuma fence of stacked firewood beneath it, the gatehouse and the buildings inside.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the plainest readable description of where a firewood stack stood in relation to the farmstead in one region, and of what it was for. Its limits are that it is one region's form, written for a general audience with no date or count, and that it describes the kizuma as it survives today, when it has become a mark of a well-off family rather than a fuel store.

Used for: the kizuma: firewood stacked under the northwest windbreak as a wall against wind and snow (homesteads)
