jinja-goshi-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, 神社合祀 ("Shrine mergers") (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A5%9E%E7%A4%BE%E5%90%88%E7%A5%80)

What it is: The Japanese encyclopedia article on the Meiji government's shrine policy and the mergers of the 1900s, which quotes, in its section on the shrine-rank system, the 1897 internal rules for promoting shrines to prefectural, district and village rank.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the precinct, parishioner and cash floors that separated a village shrine from a district and a prefectural one. Its limits: the ranks themselves are Meiji categories, set up in 1871 and keyed to the prefecture, district and village as units of government, with no Edo counterpart, so matching them to our villages, towns and cities is our analogy, not the source's; a prefectural shrine had also to be among the most famous in a whole prefecture, a province-sized place; these are the 1897 floors for promoting a shrine, not the sizes of shrines already ranked, not Edo sizes and not averages; the cash floors are Meiji yen and the required shrine office a Meiji institution; the article quotes the rules with omissions marked, citing a 2005 paper (Okuda Haruki, in 日本海域研究 36) rather than the rules' own printing, so it is an encyclopedia's quotation at second hand.

Used for: the precinct floors of the three shrine ranks (religion-and-death.html).
