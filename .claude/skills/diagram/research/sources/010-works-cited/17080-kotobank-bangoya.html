kotobank-bangoya

Kotobank, 番小屋 ("Bangoya, the watch hut", title translated) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%AA%E5%B0%8F%E5%B1%8B-606389)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the watch hut, carrying the Digital Daijisen, the Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten and excerpts from Heibonsha's Sekai dai-hyakka jiten, with an early example quoted from Minkan seiyo (1721), Tanaka Kyugu's memorial on rural administration, of a watch hut built at a village's entrance.

Why it applies, and its limits: The quotation is the one period statement found of where a village's watch hut stood. Its limits are that it is one line, quoted secondhand by a modern dictionary and cut off mid-sentence; that it concerns a watch named for the shogun's hawks, possibly one laid on villages as a burden, since it is reported in a memorial on rural administration, rather than the ordinary village watchman; that the page's own encyclopedia excerpt says watchmen differed between city and village and from region to region; and that the dictionary gives no size.

Used for: a village's watch hut standing at its entrance (Trades and services)
