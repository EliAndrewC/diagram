University of Pittsburgh HAA news, "Tao and colleagues publish on a 400-year-old Chinese chicken coop" (https://www.haa.pitt.edu/news/tao-and-colleagues-publish-400-year-old-chinese-chicken-coop)

What it is: A news item from the University of Pittsburgh's Department of History of Art and Architecture announcing that graduate student Yuqing Tao and colleagues had published, in the Chinese journal Popular Archaeology (大众考古), a report on a chicken coop of about the early seventeenth century found during a 2023 salvage excavation at Zhengzhou. The coop was a square structure with six niche-like openings in its west wall, some of them holding eggshell fragments.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is excavated evidence, from late Ming China, of how an ordinary urban household actually housed its chickens, which is the form our farmyard coops are drawn from. Its limits are that it is a short departmental announcement rather than the excavation report itself, that it rests on a single structure at a single town site, and that the page carries no measurements at all, so it can settle what a coop looked like and never how big one was.

Used for: a late-Ming square coop with six niche openings and eggshell. No size given.
