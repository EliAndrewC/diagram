Kato Ribun, 理文先生のお城がっこう 城歩き編 第32回 勾配と反り ("Professor Ribun's castle school, walks 32: batter and curve", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Shirobito (https://shirobito.jp/article/1213)

What it is: A 2021 article for young readers by the castle archaeologist Kato Ribun, on the Japan Castle Foundation's site, on why castle stone walls lean and curve and how their batter changed over time.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the batter figures this record has - early walls low and nearly vertical, walls of Toyotomi-lineage lords at about 45-50 degrees, steeper after 1600, and the fan-shaped curve that rises at about 45 degrees for two-thirds of its height and ends near vertical. It is written simply for children and gives angles as round figures from named examples, not a survey.

Used for: the batter of a castle's stone wall (cities/capitals)
