Kato Masafumi (加藤理文), 理文先生のお城がっこう 城歩き編 第32回 勾配と反り ("Professor Masafumi's castle school, walks 32: batter and curve", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Shirobito (https://shirobito.jp/article/1213)

What it is: A 2021 article for young readers by the castle scholar Kato Masafumi, a director of the Japan Castle Foundation and a Shizuoka junior-high social-studies teacher, on Shirobito, a site the Foundation endorses, on why castle stone walls lean and curve and how their batter changed over time.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the batter figures this record has - early walls low and nearly vertical, walls of Toyotomi-lineage lords at about 45-50 degrees, steeper after 1600, and the fan-shaped curve that rises in a straight line at about 45 degrees for the lower half to two-thirds of its height and ends near vertical. It is written simply for children and gives angles as round figures from named castles (Kumamoto, Iga Ueno, Imabari), not a survey. It describes only the Japanese stone castle wall of the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries; a Chinese-model city or capital wall, rammed earth faced with brick, has a different profile and cannot take its batter from here.

Used for: the batter of a castle's stone wall (cities/capitals)
