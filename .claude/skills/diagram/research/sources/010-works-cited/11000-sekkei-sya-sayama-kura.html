sekkei-sya-sayama-kura

Suzuki Keiji / Kenchiku Sekkei-sha, "How to look at a folk house 6: the kura" (in Japanese; https://sekkei-sya.o.oo7.jp/sayama/sayama6.htm)

What it is: An architect's page on the design of a kura for a reconstructed farmhouse in the Sayama hills west of Tokyo, which he modeled on the surviving kura of a nearby new-field village after walking the district and surveying where they stood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable account found of where a farm's kura stood relative to the house and how big the usual one was, from the architect's own survey of a village's kura. Its limits: a practitioner's web page, not a published survey, with no counts behind "most" or "few"; one district of western Musashi; and the architect says, hedging it twice, that kura came to its farmhouses probably only in the last years of the shogunate; the kura he walked among are ones still standing and in use today, which the page does not date, so the usual size and siting he reports probably describe kura built from the end of the shogunate onward, not Edo-period ones; and the 270 mm wall he gives is of the one kura he copied.

Used for: a farm kura's siting (front or back, no fixed compass side), its usual size and its lateness (homesteads)
