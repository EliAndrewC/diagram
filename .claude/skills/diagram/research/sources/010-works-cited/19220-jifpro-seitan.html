Japan International Forestry Promotion and Cooperation Center (JIFPRO), ChiePro, 日本の製炭技術 - 高品質は築窯と精煉の技 - ("Japanese charcoal-making technique: high quality from kiln building and refining", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://jifpro.or.jp/chiepro/charcoal/)

What it is: A Japanese forestry-cooperation body's explainer on how charcoal is made in Japan, black and white, from building the kiln to putting out the fire.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where the charcoal is cooled: black charcoal inside the kiln, sealed so that no air enters, and white charcoal drawn out red-hot and smothered beside the kiln under a damp powder of ash and river sand; it says the white-charcoal method is held to have taken its settled form in the Heian period. It describes the craft as practiced today and dates only the white-charcoal method, as tradition; it says nothing of where charcoal was kept once it left the kiln.

Used for: that charcoal is cooled at the kiln, in it or beside it (urban-features)
