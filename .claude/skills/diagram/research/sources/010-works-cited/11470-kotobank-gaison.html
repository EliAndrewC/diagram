Kotobank, 街村 ("street village", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - entries from the Hyakka Jiten Mypedia, the Britannica Kokusai Daihyakka Jiten and the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A1%97%E6%9D%91-47955)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the street village, the settlement geographers' term for houses standing densely along a road, with three encyclopedia entries: on its dependence on the street and its commercial character, on the Edo post, market and temple-gate towns that almost all took this form and their strip lots, and on the one-sided form along narrow coasts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states in words that the Edo period's small towns were almost all street towns, which is the frequency behind this record's plan knob, and names the one-sided variant. The entries are short tertiary definitions from settlement geography, for Japan only, with no figures.

Used for: the street town, how common it was, its lots and its one-sided form (towns)
