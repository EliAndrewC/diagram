What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on 姫路白なめし革細工, the white-tawed leatherwork of Himeji - a regional craft in which hides are salted, steeped in the river for several days to loosen the hair, then worked with salt and rapeseed oil, beaten dry and kneaded soft while exposed to sunlight.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the reason a tannery on our maps stands on running water and has open drying ground beside it: the process needs a river for the soak and sun for the finish, so the trade cannot be placed anywhere else in the town. It is a tertiary summary of a named local craft, its account of the process is not dated or attributed, and it gives only "several days" for the soak - so we describe the stage without attaching a length to it, and the practice it records is one district's, not every tanner's.

Used for: the white-tawing process - salted hides soaked in the river "数日間" (several days) to loosen the hair, then salt and rapeseed oil under the sun; NOT the "one week summer, two weeks winter" figure the entry carried
