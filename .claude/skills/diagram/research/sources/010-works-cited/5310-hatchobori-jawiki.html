What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Hatchobori, a district of what is now Chuo ward in Tokyo. Among its local history it records that the many temples standing there were ordered to move to Asakusa in 1635, and that the group residences (組屋敷, kumi-yashiki) of the yoriki and doshin serving under the Edo town magistrate were afterward established on the vacated temple sites - the origin of the constables of period drama calling themselves "Hatchobori".

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for one structural fact about how a magistracy was housed: the magistrate's enforcement staff lived together in their own quarter, at a remove from the office where they worked, rather than inside its walls. It is a tertiary summary of a single district in a single city, the article itself hedges the origin story as something that "is said", and Edo was far larger than any settlement we draw, so what we take is the arrangement and not its scale.

Used for: the yoriki/doshin 組屋敷 district of the Edo town magistracy standing apart from the office after the 1635 temple relocation
