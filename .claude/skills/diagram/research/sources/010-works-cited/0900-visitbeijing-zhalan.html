Visit Beijing (Beijing Municipal Culture and Tourism Bureau), 《乾隆京城全图》展现清代治安管理思路 大栅栏是'封闭小区'的雏形 (https://www.visitbeijing.com.cn/article/47QkXx9caZ2)

What it is: A popular urban-history article by Zhang Tian, reprinted on 2020-07-21 from the Beijing Evening News on the Beijing municipal tourism bureau's portal, reading the Qianlong-era map of the capital for how the Qing kept order: the palings set across street and lane mouths from a Ming edict of 1488, their number in the inner city, the three soldiers at each, and the curfew behind them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the count and the manning of the palings, which is the Chinese half of the two-traditions argument our night gates rest on, and it dates the institution to the Ming. Its limits are that it is a newspaper feature on a tourism portal rather than the Qing statutes it summarizes, and that Beijing was the imperial capital and the most policed city in the empire, so its counts and manning are an upper bound a provincial town's night gates do not approach.

Used for: palings at the lane mouths from 1488, more than a thousand in the inner city, three soldiers at each, and the curfew
