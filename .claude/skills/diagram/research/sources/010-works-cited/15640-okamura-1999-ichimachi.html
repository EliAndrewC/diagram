Okamura Osamu, 近世関東における市町と市掛商人の展開 ("The development of market towns and itinerant market merchants in early-modern Kanto", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Rekishi Chirigaku (Historical Geography) 41-1 (1999), pp. 20-31 (in Japanese; http://hist-geo.jp/img/archive/192_020.pdf)

What it is: A report given at the annual symposium of the Association of Historical Geographers in Japan and printed in its journal, Rekishi Chirigaku, together with a discussant's comment on the six-day market towns of western Kanto (Chichibu, Yorii, Ogano) from the late sixteenth century, with a section, "the landscape of the market ground", reconstructing where a market day's stalls stood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that describes a market town's ground: a strip one or two ken deep between the street and the house lots, kept for market-day stalls, with stalls also inside the house lots and out on the street itself. It is a regional study of the Kanto market towns in the Edo period, drawn from their documents and a schematic figure of Ogano, not a measured survey, and it does not say how common the strip was elsewhere.

Used for: the market ground along a market town's street, and the stalls of a market day (towns)
