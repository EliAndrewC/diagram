aba-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 網場 ("aba, the timber boom", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%B6%B2%E5%A0%B4)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the aba, a line of floats strung on a rope or net across a river to hold floated timber, today the trash boom in front of a dam's intake.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page found that describes a floating log boom in Japan: a main rope run from the far bank at a sharp angle to the stream, floats tied along it, drawing the logs to the near bank. Every source it gives is modern (a paper company's forestry history of 1976, a Hokkaido town history of 1993, a forestry encyclopedia of 1984), its one example is the former imperial Kiso forest (a Meiji-and-later estate), whose main rope was four 20 cm ropes of sarunashi vine and two 6 cm steel cables, and its one dated picture, of a landing boom in Hokkaido, is of 1948. It says timber was floated by river until the late 1940s and early 1950s, and calls the boom a timber-floating device "originally" (元来), but gives no date for when it began; its silence is not evidence that the boom is modern. It is a tertiary summary, good for the boom's shape and its names (the landing boom, the holding boom) as modern Japan practiced them; that the boom is older must be shown from elsewhere. The catch rope of a tsunaba, stretched across the Kiso to stop floated timber, is on record from 1422 (see tsunaba-nikkoku and nishikori-tsunaba), but no premodern record was found of floats strung along such a rope.

Used for: the shape of a floating log boom in Japan - a main rope angled from the far bank, floats tied along it, drawing the logs to the near bank - as modern records describe it (urban-features)
