ja.wikipedia 網場 ("aba, the timber boom", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%B6%B2%E5%A0%B4)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the aba, a line of floats strung on a rope or net across a river to hold floated timber, today the trash boom in front of a dam's intake.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page found that describes a floating log boom in Japan: a main rope run from the far bank at a sharp angle to the stream, floats tied along it, drawing the logs to the near bank. Every source it gives is modern (a paper company's forestry history of 1976, a Hokkaido town history of 1993, a forestry encyclopedia of 1984), its one example is the former imperial Kiso forest with steel cables in the rope, and its one dated picture is of 1948; it says timber was floated by river until the late 1940s and early 1950s and dates the float-and-rope structure no earlier. It is evidence for the boom in modern Japan only.

Used for: that the floating log boom is attested in Japan only from modern records (urban-features)
