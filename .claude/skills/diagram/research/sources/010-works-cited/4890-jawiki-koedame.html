jawiki-koedame

ja.wikipedia 肥溜め and 下肥 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/肥溜め)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia articles on 肥溜め (koedame, the night soil pit) and 下肥 (shimogoe, night soil used as manure). They describe the pit or buried jar in which a household's human waste was collected and left to ferment before it went onto the fields, giving the two forms it took and the rough size of the jar kind.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells us the two forms the night-soil pit took and how big the buried-jar kind was, which is what the pit glyph beside a farmhouse is drawn from. Its limits are that it is a tertiary encyclopedia summary whose descriptive body cites no references at all, so the size figure we take is an uncited statement we rely on for want of anything better; that it frames the practice as a long continuity running from the Edo period into the twentieth century, which is what makes it readable and also what stops it being a period measurement; and that it says nothing about where the pit stood on a plot, so its placement on our maps rests on other sources.

Used for: night soil fermented in buried jars/plastered pits, 1-1.5 m across (no fermentation duration on the page). Location not stated.
