kotobank-monzenmachi

Kotobank, 門前町 - several signed Japanese reference works printed side by side (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%96%80%E5%89%8D%E7%94%BA-142614)

What it is: The Kotobank aggregation page for the monzen-machi, the town that grows in front of a great temple or shrine, printing the entries of several signed Japanese reference works one after another.

Why it applies, and its limits: Three independent reference works agree on the form our gate quarters take: a settlement built in a street-village shape along both sides of the pilgrimage road, where merchants and craftsmen raised shops for the worshippers, with inns and shops and craftsmen's shops gathering, and lodging and commerce as the principal livelihood - the frontage following the approach, which is the map-relevant part. The Britannica entry adds the eating-and-drinking establishments as what was added to the festival market once it became permanent. Its limits are that these are tertiary dictionary entries with no sourcing of their own, that they describe Japan, and that none of them puts a sweet-seller at the gate, which is why that trade is cited to the highway teahouses instead.

Used for: the gate town's shops and inns along both sides of the approach, in a street-village form; the eating houses at the gate
