sohu-yamen-gate

Sohu, 【史海钩沉】衙门六扇开：中国古代县衙的门户 (The yamen's six leaves stand open: the gates of the ancient Chinese county office), 2021-04-06, reprinted from 文史天地 (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/459260036_612398)

What it is: A popular-history article by a Fudan University law professor (the reprint gives his post, not his name), reprinted on a Chinese news portal from the magazine Wenshi Tiandi (issue 278), on the gates along a county office's central axis - the main gate, the ceremonial gate and the gate of the residence - and the saying about the six-leaved yamen gate.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the form of the county yamen's main gate - a roofed building limited to three bays - which is how a yamen gatehouse is drawn. Its limits: it is popular history without footnotes - it names period works for the word yamen and for the gatekeepers, but gives no source for the three-bay rule beyond saying law and ritual fixed it; it speaks of the late imperial county office in general rather than a dated building; it gives no measurement, only a depth of two or three rafter-spans (jia); and it says some counties raised the gate into a two- or three-story drum tower, so the single-story gate of three bays is the common form, not the only one.

Used for: the county yamen's main gate as a roofed building of three bays with six leaves (buildings)
