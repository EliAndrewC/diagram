sakaori-ishizumi

Sakaori Terraced Paddies Preservation Society (Ena city), 棚田の石積みと用水 ("The terraces' stone walls and water", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://sakaori-tanada.com/about/wastewater/)

What it is: The preservation society's page on the stone-walled Sakaori terraces (Gifu prefecture), said to date from the early Edo period: how the walls differ on the steep and the gentle side of the valley, how often they fell and were rebuilt, and how the paddies were watered.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets the steep side's high walls and small paddies against the gentle side's low walls and larger paddies within one valley, which is the relation between slope, wall and paddy this record needs. It gives no figures for either, and it is a preservation society's account of one landscape. It says the steep side also gave only small stones, which it piled high, and the gentle side gave large stones, laid lower. So in this one valley, wall height follows the stone available as well as the slope. It also says the walls were rebuilt and the paddies widened from the Edo period until the Taishō era, and enlarged again after the war, so today's paddy sizes are not the Edo ones.

Used for: higher walls on steeper ground, and larger paddies on gentler (archetypes)
