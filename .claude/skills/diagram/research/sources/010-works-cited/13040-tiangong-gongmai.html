tiangong-gongmai

Song Yingxing, 天工開物 ("The Works of Heaven and the Inception of Things", 1637), chapter 粹精, section 攻麥 ("Processing wheat", translated from the Chinese by this project), on Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E5%B7%A5%E9%96%8B%E7%89%A9/%E6%94%BB%E9%BA%A5)

What it is: The wheat-milling section of the late-Ming encyclopedia of crafts, on the millstones and what turned them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It ranks the mills by power - the large turned by an ox, the lesser by a donkey, the smallest pushed by hand - and says the water mill is built like the water pestle and is three times as convenient as the ox, from which this project reads that a Chinese town without a stream milled its flour by ox, donkey or hand, though the text itself says nothing of towns or streams. It is Ming, and it describes the machines, not where in a town they stood or how many a town kept. Its wheat is mainly a northern crop: it says the millstones south of the Yangzi heat up, blacken the flour and wear out in twenty days, against half a year for the northern stones. It speaks for China only and is no evidence that Japan milled the same way.

Used for: ox, donkey, hand and water mills for flour (water)
