Zhihui Sannong (an agricultural knowledge service), "Ancient Chinese water conservancy science and technology" (in Chinese; https://www.pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/549328.shtml)

What it is: An encyclopedia-style survey of ancient Chinese water engineering by period, noting among much else what the intake gates of Han canals were built of.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the intake gate of the Western Han Langtang Canal was a timber structure, and that the mouth of the Eastern Han Bian Canal was later given a stone gate - two different works, not one gate rebuilt - which is the material of an intake mouth where one was built. It is a tertiary summary: an unsigned reference entry on China Agriculture Press's knowledge platform that gives one line to the point and cites no period text, so it carries the material and nothing finer. It concerns great state canals of the Han period, not village works, and gives no dimensions.

Used for: that a Western Han canal's intake gate was of timber and a later canal mouth was changed to stone (water)
