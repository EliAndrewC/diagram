heino-bunri-jawiki

ja.wikipedia, 兵農分離 (heinō bunri, the separation of warrior and peasant) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/兵農分離)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on heinō bunri, the late sixteenth- and seventeenth-century separation of the warrior class from farming: warriors giving up the land and gathering in the castle town, the lord ruling the peasants directly, and the fixing of status, with a long account of where and how far it was carried out.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the Edo-period samurai moved off the land into the castle town, which is the Japanese half of this page's claim that the well-off live inside the city rather than on country estates, and it states the limits itself: no policy moved the whole warrior class into towns, domain-level compulsion is doubted, and in Tosa, Kyushu, Tohoku and several named domains many retainers stayed in the countryside to the end of the period. It is an encyclopedia article flagged on its own page since 2018 as short of verifiable sources, and it gives no count of how many samurai lived where.

Used for: the shift from warriors living on the land to residence in the castle town, and its stated limits (cities/hinterland)
