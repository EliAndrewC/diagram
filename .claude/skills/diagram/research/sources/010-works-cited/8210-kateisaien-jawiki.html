ja.wikipedia 家庭菜園 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/家庭菜園)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the household kitchen garden, a tertiary entry which records that ground a Japanese household cultivates to feed itself is called yashikibatake, the homestead field. It also gives the plot a short history: its origin can be traced as far back as the Yayoi period, and by the Edo period such a plot stood inside the residences of the warrior class as well as those of farmers.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the object and its name - the bed beside the house that grows a household's own food, as distinct from the fields it farms for the tax and the market - which is what the garden on our homesteads is, and its two dates are what tell us the plot is far older than the modern gardening the rest of the article is about. Its limits are sharp and worth stating: the article gives no area and no count, so it cannot support the size band our maps hold a garden to, and that band is labeled a guess on the page; its history is two sentences rather than an account, so it fixes that the plot existed early and nothing about what it looked like; and it says such cultivation is often done on odd scraps of ground away from the residence as well, so it does not by itself put the bed against the house.

Used for: the name of the household's own cultivated ground, the yashikibatake
