Takayama City, 高山陣屋跡 (https://www.city.takayama.lg.jp/kurashi/1000021/1000119/1000847/1000954/1000956.html)

What it is: The city of Takayama's official page for 高山陣屋跡 (the site of the Takayama Jin'ya), the one surviving Tokugawa provincial governor's office and compound in Japan, published by the municipality that manages the designated historic site. It lists the buildings and how they are built, notes which structures were moved in from the castle and when, and gives the extent of the protected ground.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the strongest source in the record for what a provincial office contained: an intact compound described by the body that keeps it - the examining court with its white-gravel yard, the storehouses moved from the castle, the office range of 1816 - and its designated area. Its limits are that this is the only survivor of more than fifty such offices, in a mountain province under direct shogunal rule; that parts of what is described are a 1996 reconstruction from an 1830 drawing; and that the area figure, 11,219 square meters, is an exact sum of ten surveyed parcels under a modern legal boundary accumulated across four designations between 1929 and 1989, not a measured Edo footprint - so the compound area our maps derive from it, the total less the front plaza, carries the hedge rather than the source.

Used for: the 吟味所 standing with the 白州 (「The ginmisho (examination hall) and the shirasu are characteristically laid with guri stone (coarse cobble ballast) and roofed.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「吟味所、白州はグリ石敷で屋根のあることが特徴的である」)); the 御蔵 moved from the castle's third bailey in 1695; the designated site figure 「Number/quantity: 11,219.05 square meters」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「員数 11,219.05平方メートル」) (the label reads oddly - recheck against the 史跡 register before relying on it)
