王少卿 (Wang Shaoqing), "昭示大道 融通古今——古代陆地交通活化石之翠云廊概要" ("A great road shown forth: an outline of the Cuiyun Corridor, a living fossil of ancient land transport", title translated from the Chinese by this project), a Sichuan local-gazetteer office essay republished on The Paper (https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_24153613)

What it is: An essay by a Sichuan local-history office on the Cuiyun Corridor, a stretch of old official road lined with ancient cypresses, which traces the Chinese practice of planting trees along official roads from the Zhou through the Yuan, quoting the classical texts and dynastic histories.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gathers in one readable place the Chinese record that official roads were lined with trees - to mark the road and to shade travelers - and that distance was marked by roadside mounds, later by trees in their place. It is a popular history essay by a government office, quoting its classical sources without page references; the Cuiyun Corridor itself is a mountain road in Sichuan, not a town's approach.

Used for: the tree-lined official road in China, its species, and the roadside distance mound (ways)
