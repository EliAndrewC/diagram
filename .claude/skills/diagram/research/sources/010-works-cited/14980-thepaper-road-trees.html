thepaper-road-trees

王少卿 (Wang Shaoqing), "昭示大道 融通古今——古代陆地交通活化石之翠云廊概要" ("A great road shown forth: an outline of the Cuiyun Corridor, a living fossil of ancient land transport", title translated from the Chinese by this project), a Sichuan local-gazetteer office essay republished on The Paper (https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_24153613)

What it is: An essay by a Sichuan local-history office on the Cuiyun Corridor, a stretch of old official road lined with ancient cypresses, which traces the Chinese practice of planting trees along official roads from the Zhou through the Qing, quoting the classical texts and dynastic histories, and closes with the corridor's protection since 1935. Its author, 王少卿, is a former director of the Guangyuan city gazetteer office; the essay was published in August 2023.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gathers in one readable place the Chinese record that official roads were lined with trees - to mark the road and to shade travelers - and that distance was marked by roadside mounds, later by trees in their place. It is a popular, commemorative history essay by a government office, and its quotations are loose: it names its classical sources without page references, some of its "quotations" are paraphrases (its line from the Zhouli is not the Zhouli's wording), and at least one is misattributed (it gives the Zhouli's "不树者，无椁" as a Qing local rule) - so a classical text is quoted from the text itself, never from this essay. Its species change with the era - pine under the Qin, sophora for the Western Wei distance marks, elm, willow and sophora in a Yuan edict, cypress at Cuiyun - and the eucalyptus, black locust, plane and poplar it names were brought in from abroad at the end of the Qing, too late for this setting. Its tree counts, girths and ages are a modern survey of the trees that survive, not a planting density, and its dating of trees to the Qin or to Zhang Fei by girth is local tradition, not a planting record. The Cuiyun Corridor itself is a mountain road in Sichuan, not a town's approach.

Used for: the tree-lined official road in China, its species, and the roadside distance mound (ways)
