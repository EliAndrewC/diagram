fao-farm-structures-cattle

FAO, Farm structures in tropical climates, chapter 10, Cattle housing (https://www.fao.org/4/s1250e/s1250e11.htm)

What it is: The cattle-housing chapter of Farm structures in tropical climates, a design manual published by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations for builders and extension workers in the tropics. It gives the space a beast needs - a shade structure at 2.5 to 3 square meters per animal, a free stall 2.2 m long by 1.2 m wide for a milking cow of 500 to 600 kg, a bull pen with 12 to 15 square meters of shaded rest and 20 to 30 of exercise yard.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the readable answer to a question the historical record was silent on - how much room one large draft animal takes under a roof - and it is what keeps our byre shed sized against a stated figure rather than guessed at. Its limits are three, and the first two are stated on the page beside the figure. It is a MODERN standard, written for modern stock and modern welfare expectations, and a twentieth-century dairy cow is not an Edo-period ox or a water buffalo. It is written for a different part of the world - the manual addresses builders and extension workers in tropical Africa, so nothing in it is a record of East Asian practice, and the animals it sizes for are the ones kept there. And the third is a matter of DIRECTION: these are welfare minimums, what a builder today is told to provide, not measurements of what anyone built, so treating them as a target makes the shed we draw generous rather than accurate, and the error runs one way.

Used for: a modern standard the drawn byre is measured against: the stall length and width for a milking cow, and the shaded area a beast is allowed
