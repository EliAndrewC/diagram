seiyo-karihama-ido

Seiyo City Board of Education, Cultural Landscape, "Settlement: wells" (title translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「【集落】井戸」) (in Japanese; https://www.city.seiyo.ehime.jp/miryoku/seiyoshibunkazai/keikan/juuyounakouseiyouso/17511.html)

What it is: A page of Seiyo city (Ehime prefecture) on the wells of Karihama, a fishing and terraced-farming village whose landscape is a designated Important Cultural Landscape, drawing on the city's 2018 survey report: how many wells there were, how many households each served, and how many of the communal ones were paved round with stone.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a surveyed count of a real village's wells, with the share of households per well and the paved wellside - the kind of figure the record otherwise lacks. But Karihama was water-poor, relying on dug wells and springs until piped water in 1952, so it is likely (our reading, not the page's) a village that needed MORE wells than most; the survey is modern, of wells surviving into the 20th century, and the page does not date them. The one-per-five-to-ten-houses rate counts every dug well, most of them private or shared by a few houses: of about 150, only four (three in Honura, one in Edaura) gave water good enough to drink, and only twelve were found to be communal - so it is not a rate of drinking wells or of communal wells. The stone paving is said of many of those twelve communal wells, not all.

Used for: a village's wells at about one per five to ten houses, many of the communal ones paved round with stone (urban-features)
