kotobank-mitsu-dogu

Kotobank, 三つ道具 (the three tools), entries from Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen and Encyclopedia Nipponica (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%89%E3%81%A4%E9%81%93%E5%85%B7-638755)

What it is: The Kotobank page on the "three tools", chiefly the Edo-period set of long-shafted arrest weapons - the tsukubō, sasumata and sodegarami - kept at checkpoints and guard posts, with the Nipponica entry's note that an early Edo screen painting shows them stood in a row beside a playhouse entrance.

Why it applies, and its limits: A signed entry in a modern encyclopedia (Saitō Shin'ichi, in Nipponica) says the three long arrest weapons were kept on hand at checkpoints and guard posts. It also says an early Edo screen painting shows them stood in a row, and from that it guesses they were used early on as symbols of order and dignity. Its limits are that it is a modern encyclopedia summary, not a period record; that it says only that the weapons were kept on hand at guard posts, not that they were stood up or how they were stored there; that "symbol of order" is the author's guess ("であろう") from one painting, not a stated fact; that it describes arrest weapons, not practice weapons; that its one picture of them standing is a theater's entrance on a screen painting, not a guard post; and that it gives no rack, size or count.

Used for: the three long arrest weapons kept at checkpoints and guard posts, and stood in a row as symbols of order (buildings)
