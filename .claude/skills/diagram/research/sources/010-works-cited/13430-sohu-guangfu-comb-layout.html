Sohu, reposting through the account 乡村与城镇建设 from the journal 小城镇建设 ("Small Town Construction", translated from the Chinese by this project), June 2018, Wang Dong (王东) and Yan Zhenggang (颜政纲), 广府水乡村落梳式布局形态衍化及当下性初探 ("A first study of the evolution of the comb layout of Cantonese water-country villages and its present relevance", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://www.sohu.com/a/244054483_654278)

What it is: An article on the comb-plan villages of the Pearl River delta, by two architecture and planning lecturers at the Guizhou Institute of Technology, Wang Dong and Yan Zhenggang, reposted on Sohu in abridged form from the journal Small Town Construction: how such a village faces its channel or pond, where its ancestral halls and drying floors stand, and Feng Jiang's sorting of the villages into three layouts in the order they arose, taken from Feng Jiang's 2010 dissertation on Ming-Qing Guangzhou prefecture; it works through Songtang village as an example.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the ancestral halls at the front of the village facing the pond, with the drying floor between, and says most Cantonese villages' halls stand so, and it records that some villages had no hall - which is where the map puts a hall and whether it draws one. Its limits: an article by two architecture lecturers, abridged in the repost, describing villages as they survive today from fieldwork and maps, and dating no hall's placement to a period; the villages with no hall come from Feng Jiang's three layouts, cited at second hand from Feng Jiang's 2010 dissertation, which puts them in time order and is the closer source for when they stood; one region's form, the water country of the Cantonese delta (which the article says departs from the regular comb plan of Cantonese villages on hills and terraces), not south China at large.

Used for: the ancestral halls at the village front facing the pond, the drying floor between, and the villages with no hall (homesteads)
