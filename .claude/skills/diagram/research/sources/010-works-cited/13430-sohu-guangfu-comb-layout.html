sohu-guangfu-comb-layout

Sohu, reposting from the journal 乡村与城镇建设 ("Rural and Town Construction", translated from the Chinese by this project), 2018 no. 6, 广府水乡村落梳式布局形态衍化及当下性初探 ("A first study of the evolution of the comb layout of Cantonese water-country villages and its present relevance", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://www.sohu.com/a/244054483_654278)

What it is: An article on the comb-plan villages of the Pearl River delta, reposted on Sohu from a planning journal: how such a village faces its channel or pond, where its ancestral halls and drying floors stand, and a historian's sorting of the villages into three layouts; it works through Songtang village as an example.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the ancestral halls at the front of the village facing the pond, with the drying floor between, and says most Cantonese villages' halls stand so, and it records that some villages had no hall - which is where the map puts a hall and whether it draws one. Its limits: a planners' article whose repost does not carry its authors' names in the saved text, describing villages as they survive and dating nothing to a period; one region's form, the Cantonese delta, not south China at large.

Used for: the ancestral halls at the village front facing the pond, the drying floor between, and the villages with no hall (homesteads)
