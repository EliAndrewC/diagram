takayama-onkura-heritage

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Japan Heritage portal, 高山陣屋（御蔵） (The Takayama Jin'ya: the rice storehouse), text by the Takayama city board of education (in Japanese; https://japan-heritage.bunka.go.jp/ja/culturalproperties/result/2060/)

What it is: The Japan Heritage portal's page on the rice storehouse of the Takayama Jin'ya, written by the city's cultural-properties office: where it was first built, when it was moved, the age of its frame and how it was built.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the rice storehouse of an Edo intendancy, the Takayama Jin'ya: an earth-walled grain storehouse with sloping walls and ventilation gaps, which is the model a magistracy's granary is drawn from. Its limits: it is a portal summary a few sentences long by the city's cultural-properties office, not a survey report. It presents the sloping walls and ventilation gaps as the technique of the Hida carpenters of a cold mountain province, so they are a regional practice standing in for a general one. It calls the building one of the oldest and largest grain storehouses in the country, so it is exceptional rather than typical in size. It is one building, first built for a castle about 1600 and moved in 1695, and it says nothing of the floor.

Used for: the Takayama rice storehouse as an earth-walled grain storehouse with ventilation gaps, moved from the castle in 1695 (buildings)
