saitama-museum-izawa

Saitama City Museum, "Web commentary on the exhibits (Edo period, part 2): the story of Izawa Yasobei Tamenaga" (in Japanese; https://www.city.saitama.lg.jp/004/005/004/005/008/shihakukaisetsu/p113142.html)

What it is: A municipal museum's online commentary on its Edo-period gallery, on the engineer Izawa Yasobei and his works in what is now Saitama city: the Minuma canal (1727-28) and the reclamation of the Konuma marsh (1729-31).

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the separation of supply from drainage as a feature of Izawa's method and says that at Konuma the drainage was gathered down the center, so that the villages below kept their water, which is a second dated Edo case of the separated layout. It is a museum's short text for visitors, without footnotes, and describes a reclaimed marsh, not an old village's fields. The layout it gives is the trunk layout of a whole reclamation ordered by the shogunate: supply brought in from the Minuma canal's western branch, one drain down the former marsh's center, and the new fields divided among nine villages. It does not describe the ditches of single fields. It presents the separation of supply from drainage as the method of the Kishu school, brought in during the 1720s, and sets it against the older Kanto school's river dams, retention ponds and flood basins. So it dates the separated layout in planned reclamation from that time, not in the fields of villages settled long before.

Used for: that the Konuma reclamation of 1729-31 gathered its drainage down the center, separate from the supply (water)
