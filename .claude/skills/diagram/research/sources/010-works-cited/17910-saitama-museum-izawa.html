Saitama City Museum, "Web commentary on the exhibits (Edo period, part 2): the story of Izawa Yasobei Tamenaga" (in Japanese; https://www.city.saitama.lg.jp/004/005/004/005/008/shihakukaisetsu/p113142.html)

What it is: A municipal museum's online commentary on its Edo-period gallery, on the engineer Izawa Yasobei and his works in what is now Saitama city: the Minuma canal (1727-28) and the reclamation of the Konuma marsh (1729-31).

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the separation of supply from drainage as a feature of Izawa's method and says that at Konuma the drainage was gathered down the center, so that the villages below kept their water, which is a second dated Edo case of the separated layout. It is a museum's short text for visitors, without footnotes, and describes a reclaimed marsh, not an old village's fields.

Used for: that the Konuma reclamation of 1729-31 gathered its drainage down the center, separate from the supply (water)
