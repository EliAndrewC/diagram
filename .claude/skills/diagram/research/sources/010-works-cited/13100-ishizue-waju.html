ishizue-waju

水土の礎 (the history site of land and water run by the Agricultural and Rural Integrated Information Center, ARIC), 水から土地を守る、輪中 ("Guarding the land from water: the waju", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/03/06.html)

What it is: A short, unsigned and undated page in ARIC's popular history of land development in Japan, on the ring-diked communities of the Nobi plain and their growth in the Edo period (the embankments of 1609 and 1753).

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that farmers long put their villages and fields on the natural levees and other slight rises of the plain, and that an intake cut through a ring levee so often caused a breach that ring-diked villages found it hard to draw river water, so the use of groundwater from bored wells (掘抜井戸) developed. It also says a ring-diked village generally had an intake at its upstream head and a drain at its tail, so wells were an addition to intakes, not a replacement. It is a modern popular summary of one region, with no citations, and it does not say when the wells came into use, so it cannot show that they belong to the Edo period rather than to Meiji.

Used for: villages on natural levees; wells beside the intake inside a ring levee (water)
