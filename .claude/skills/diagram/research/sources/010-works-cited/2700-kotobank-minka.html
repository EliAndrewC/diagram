kotobank-minka

kotobank 民家 ("commoner's house", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%91%E5%AE%B6)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the Nipponica encyclopedia's entry on the minka, the Japanese folk house, with its plan types - the three-room hall type, the four-room type - and how the complex plans grew from them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that a complex farmhouse plan is the standard type with rooms added, the growth by addition these maps' varied houses follow; its Nipponica entry reports the eighteenth-century count of house sizes in villages near Utsunomiya, from floor plans drawn up to lodge the shogun's retainers; and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry on the same page classifies roof shapes, roofing materials and the regional plan forms. A tertiary summary of published reference works; it gives no proportions and no direction of growth, the house count gives floor areas for one district and no length or depth, and the regional forms come with no share of households.

Used for: the minka plan growing by added rooms from a standard type; the commonest farmhouse of about 20 tsubo; the three roof shapes, the roofing materials and where each regional plan and roof form stood (homesteads)
