kotobank-minka

kotobank 民家 ("commoner's house", title translated) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%91%E5%AE%B6)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting several reference works' entries on the minka, the Japanese folk house, chiefly the Nipponica entry by Hirai Kiyoshi and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry by Suzuki Mitsuru, with their plan types (the three-room hall type, the four-room type) and how the complex plans grew from them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that a complex farmhouse plan is the standard type with rooms added, the growth by addition these maps' varied houses follow, though its Heibonsha entry cautions that the arrangement of rooms varies too widely to sort into one national series and Nipponica says almost all, not all, complex plans grew this way; its Nipponica entry reports which house sizes were most numerous, many and few in eighteenth-century villages near Utsunomiya, from floor plans drawn up to lodge the shogun's retainers; and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry on the same page classifies roof shapes, roofing materials and the regional plan forms. It gives no proportion beyond the hall type's earth floor taking about half the house, and no direction for the rooms added to the four-room plan (only the regional wings have one, such as the stable wing jutting from the front of the earth floor in the L-shaped magariya); the house sizes are floor areas for one district, given as most, many and few rather than counted, with no length or depth; and the regional forms come with no share of households. They are known from surviving houses, very few older than the later seventeenth century, and the page names two as the houses of the well-off (the yatsumune-zukuri mostly of wealthy farmers, the honmune-zukuri of upper farmers), not an ordinary household's house.

Used for: the minka plan growing by added rooms from a standard type; the commonest farmhouse of about 20 tsubo; the three roof shapes, the roofing materials and where each regional plan and roof form stood (Homesteads)
