takamori-karishiki

Matsukami Kiyoshi, 「Learning from Edo-period soil-making」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の土づくりに学ぶ」), lecture handout for the third "Toki no Eki" lecture, Takamori town, Nagano, December 2022 (in Japanese; https://takamori-tokinoeki.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/R4%E3%80%8C%E6%99%82%E3%81%AE%E9%A7%85%E3%80%8D%E7%AC%AC3%E8%AC%9B%E5%BA%A7.pdf)

What it is: A local-history lecture handout on green manure (karishiki) in the Shimo-Ina district of Nagano, drawing on the district history (Shimo-Ina shi, vol. 8, which quotes a household's 1854 memorandum book), the town history, and the village disputes over the hills where the manure was cut; its closing sections argue for a modern no-fertilizer farming method, which we do not use.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a working household's own figure for how much green manure went on a paddy, repeats Mizumoto's calculation of how much hill and moor that took, and says that villages without hills borrowed them from villages that had them. It is a lecture by a local historian, not a study; its per-paddy figure is one household's in one valley, and it reaches us third-hand (the 1854 memorandum as quoted by the district history as quoted by the handout; the conversion to kilograms is the lecturer's); the area ratio is Mizumoto's calculation, quoted without its basis and made for fields in general, not for this valley; and the borrowing of hill ground is the lecturer's own general statement, with one named case (Zakoji borrowing hill from Ushimaki).

Used for: the green manure per paddy, hill ground more than ten times the fields, and villages borrowing hill ground (vegetation)
