News Wakayama, 南守る寺町と新堀 城下町の堅固な防御 ("The temple quarter and the new moat that guarded the south: the castle town's solid defense", title translated from the Japanese by this project), in the series ふるさと和歌山城 by Mizushima Taiji (https://www.nwn.jp/feature/171118_teramati/)

What it is: A local newspaper column (2017) by Mizushima Taiji, a committee member of the Japan Castle History Society, on how Wakayama's castle town gathered its temples into a temple quarter and moved it south around 1633.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the defensive argument in its concrete form: a temple's tile-roofed buildings inside stone or earthen walls became a fort in an emergency, and a temple could build or repair stone walls without the shogunate's leave. It is a popular column giving the standard view for one town, with no references, so it is evidence of what the view is, not proof that it was the builders' purpose.
