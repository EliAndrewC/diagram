tiangong-kaiwu-gongdao

Song Yingxing (宋應星), Tiangong kaiwu (天工開物, The Works of Heaven and the Inception of Things), 1637, chapter 4 "Cuijing", section "Gong dao" (攻稻, Working the rice), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E5%B7%A5%E9%96%8B%E7%89%A9/%E6%94%BB%E7%A8%BB)

What it is: The section on threshing and husking rice from a late-Ming technical encyclopedia by a scholar of Jiangxi, a rice province, describing how the grain was taken from the straw by hand, by an ox-drawn stone roller on a threshing floor, and in a tub in the field.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a first-hand description of premodern Chinese rice threshing in the period the setting borrows from, and it names the threshing floor and its stone roller. It describes the rice-growing south as Song knew it (he notes that the north, with little rice, winnowed differently). By his own rough estimate, not a count, only about half the rice went under the ox and roller, chiefly in the southern households that grew much of it; the other half was beaten by hand against a tub or a stone slab, and seed grain always was, so a small household may have had no roller floor at all. It says nothing about who owned the floor - one household, several, or a village - nor how big it was, and it is a Wikisource transcription of the classical text rather than an edition with its variants.

Used for: threshing on a floor with an ox-drawn stone roller, and in the field in a wet harvest (homesteads)
