ja.wikipedia 籾すり機 ("rice huller", title translated from the Japanese by this project; reached from 籾摺り) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%B1%BE%E6%91%BA%E3%82%8A)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the rice huller, the machine that strips the husk from paddy to leave brown rice, with a short history from the pestle and mortar through the wooden hulling mill and the clay hulling mill (the clay mill introduced in the Genroku era, 1688-1704) to the rubber-roll machine that spread from about 1935.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the clay hulling mill broke many grains and that the farm household ate the broken rice because it could not be shipped, which puts the hulling on the farm, before the rice left it. A tertiary summary centered on modern machinery; its history is a paragraph, and it does not say in so many words who hulled. The broken-rice passage is cited to one town's history (Kamiichi, Toyama, 1970) and gives no date. It describes the clay-mill era that ended in the 1930s, most likely late Meiji to early Showa with its shipping of rice, not the Edo period, so it stands in for the Edo practice rather than showing it.

Used for: hulling done on the farm before the rice was shipped (urban-features)
