bunka-saito-nagayamon

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Online, 齋藤家住宅長屋門 (The nagaya-mon of the Saitō house) (in Japanese; https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/582926)

What it is: The national database entry for a registered tangible cultural property (registered 2021), the gate range of the Saitō house at Kugenuma, Fujisawa (Kanagawa), dated by the entry to Meiji, 1890, with the registration's short description: its length and depth, the gate opening in its central two ken, the rooms either side, the thatched hipped roof and the zelkova frame of the doorway.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a surveyed measurement of a nagaya-mon's opening in ken, which is what a gate is drawn from. Its limits: the gate was built in 1890, after the Meiji Restoration, so it is a traditional form built after our setting's era rather than an Edo building; the entry names the family only as Saitō, and the gate's thatch and its village site mark a rural house rather than a samurai's; it is one building, not a survey of many; and a registration's two ken are counted between post centers, so the clear opening is a little less.

Used for: a nagaya-mon opening in its central two ken, with double-leaf board doors (buildings)
