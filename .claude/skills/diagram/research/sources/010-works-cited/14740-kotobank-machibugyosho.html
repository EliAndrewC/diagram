kotobank-machibugyosho

Kotobank, 町奉行所 ("town magistracy", title translated from the Japanese by this project), from Heibonsha's 日本歴史地名大系 (Japanese historical place names) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%94%BA%E5%A5%89%E8%A1%8C%E6%89%80-1419532)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the Edo town magistracy, carrying the place-name dictionary's account of where the offices stood and when they moved, and the world encyclopedia's note on the yoriki.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sites Edo's town magistracies inside named castle gates - the Yayosu riverbank and the Gofukubashi, Tokiwabashi and Kajibashi gates - from their founding in 1604 through the moves of 1631 and 1698. That these gates stood on the castle's outer moat, facing the commoners' town, is this project's reading of the gate names, not the extract's words. It is a reference-work extract (the full entry is behind a paywall) about Edo alone, the shogun's capital with two (for a time three) magistracies, so for a smaller city or a domain's castle town it gives a pattern, not a rule.

Used for: where Edo's town magistracies stood (cities/government)
