nagano-shishi-yokota

Nagano City, Nagano Shishi (the Nagano city history), vol. 4, history, early modern 2, ch. 12 section 2, 「The former Yokota house」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「旧横田家住宅」), pp. 617-619, on the ADEAC digital archive (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/nagano-city/texthtml/d100040/ct00000004/ht002070)

What it is: The Nagano city history's account of the Yokota house at Matsushiro, an Important Cultural Property: the household's stipend and office, the main house's date from an ink inscription found in the dismantling repair, its size, its rooms, and the alterations made to it before about 1868.

Why it applies, and its limits: A municipal history's summary of the house, one of the few accounts that dates a samurai house's parts and says which were altered: it dates the main house to 1794 from an ink inscription found in the dismantling repair (and by its style), dates the retirement house, gate and storehouses by style to the early 19th century, and takes its plan from the prefectural history rather than giving the repair report itself. The household held about 150 koku and served as district magistrate among other offices, and moved to this site in the late 18th century; it is a middle-ranking retainer's house, not a lower samurai's or a commoner's. It counts two privies and describes the kitchen as all board floor without saying whether either was there in 1794, and it names the kitchen among the parts altered by about 1868; its figures (17.1 by 9.6 m, grounds of about 479 tsubo) differ from the national register's 16.2 by 8.7 m and from the city volunteers' page's 1,012 tsubo, without saying why; and where it finds the original plan considerably altered by about 1868, the national register calls the house little altered. One house of one domain in Shinano.

Used for: the two privies of a samurai house of 1794, the all-board kitchen with its sink on the floor, and how the Yokota house is dated and how much of it was altered (buildings)
