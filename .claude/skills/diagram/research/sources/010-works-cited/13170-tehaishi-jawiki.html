tehaishi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 手配師 ("labor broker", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%89%8B%E9%85%8D%E5%B8%AB)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the tehaishi, the broker who finds day laborers work, tracing the trade back to the Edo-period placement broker who stood guarantor for work-seekers in the cities, lodged them and found them work.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the Edo-period placement broker (口入屋) in the cities provided a lodging (人宿) for the work-seekers he stood guarantor for. That is all it carries for our use. It does not say the lodging was in the broker's own house, and it says this of the broker who placed servants in samurai households and processions, not of the labor supplier (人夫出し) or gang boss. So \"a labor boss's house is larger than a laborer's\" is this project's inference from it. It is weak: it is a tertiary article, mostly about the modern trade, and its Edo passage cites nothing (its one footnote, a 1935 Osaka report, covers only Meiji and Taishō rules). It carries the encyclopedia's own warnings that it cites no sources, may hold original research and has had its accuracy disputed. It glosses the Edo lodging with a Meiji-era term (たこ部屋). It describes Edo and the castle towns, a larger place than a small town. It gives no size or plan for the lodging. A closer public source, linked from the article itself, is Yoshida Masashi's 2010 typology of Edo servant brokers (Ritsumeikan Hōgaku 333-334); read it before relying on this claim.

Used for: the broker's lodging for the men placed (towns)
