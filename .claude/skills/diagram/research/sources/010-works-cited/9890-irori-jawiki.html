irori-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 囲炉裏 (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9B%B2%E7%82%89%E8%A3%8F)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the irori, the square hearth cut into the floor of the traditional house: its uses, the seating around it, and where in the house it was set.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where a common house put its sunken hearth, in the floor of a room beside the earth-floored doma or kitchen or in the doma itself, which is what a plan's irori is checked against. It is tertiary, though its placement passage is footnoted to a 2008 dictionary of folk implements (絵引 民具の事典) and a Chiba Prefectural Central Museum teaching page. It gives no era: its traditional house is the minka as it stood until the irori went out of use in the 1950s and 60s. It says placement varied by region: usually the centre of the floored part, in some regions its edge beside the doma, and in the Nanbu magariya the doma itself. It describes the minka, the house of farmers and townspeople, not the samurai house, and it is Japanese practice only, with nothing to say about the Chinese-modeled half of the setting.

Used for: where a common house set its irori (buildings)
