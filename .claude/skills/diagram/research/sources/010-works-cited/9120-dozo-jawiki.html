dozo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 土蔵 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9C%9F%E8%94%B5)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the dozo, the thick-walled plastered storehouse: the kinds it is divided into by what it holds, the rice store among them, and a section on fire resistance giving wall thicknesses of about 300 mm and more, outer doors faced in earth and plaster, and the record of interiors that survived the great fires.

Why it applies, and its limits: It turns "fireproof kura" from an adjective into something a map can draw and a reader can check, and it names the rice store as one of the kinds such a building was built for, which is the thing our unwalled seat protects first. Its limits are that it is tertiary and Japanese, and that its fire-survival claim is stated generally rather than from a study, so it supports the form and not a survival rate.

Used for: the plastered storehouse as the fire-resistant form, its wall thickness and earth-faced doors, and the rice store among the kinds it held
