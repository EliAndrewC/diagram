ritter-timber-bridges

Michael A. Ritter, Timber Bridges: Design, Construction, Inspection, and Maintenance, USDA Forest Service EM 7700-8 (1990), 944 pp (https://www.ltrc.la.gov/ltap/pdf/timber_bridges_design_construction_inspection_maintenance.pdf)

What it is: The United States Forest Service's standard manual for timber bridges, written by a Forest Service engineer: the timber itself, the design of beams, decks and railings, the kinds of abutment, and inspection and maintenance, with worked design examples running to 944 pages.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the one thing our own record had only reasoned about, what a timber beam actually needs to bear on, and for what an abutment is there to do, which is to hold the roadway embankment. Its limits are large, and they are why it settles only half of the question it was fetched for: it is a modern manual, and its worked examples are highway-loaded glulam beams on spans up to about 94 feet, far heavier and longer than a plank over a farm ditch, so its bearing lengths are an upper bound and not a premodern measurement; and in 944 pages it never gives a distance from the water's edge to the abutment, which is the figure our maps actually draw.
