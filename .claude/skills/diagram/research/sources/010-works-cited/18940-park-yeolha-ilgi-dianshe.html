Park Ji-won, Yeolha ilgi (Rehe riji, "The Jehol Diary"), chapter 3, the travel notes Ilsin supil, section "Inns" (店舍), in classical Chinese, on Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E7%86%B1%E6%B2%B3%E6%97%A5%E8%A8%98/%E5%8D%B703)

What it is: The travel diary of a Korean scholar who went to the Qing court with an embassy in 1780; this chapter covers nine days on the road from Xinguangning to inside Shanhaiguan, and its topical notes describe Chinese carts, markets, inns and bridges as a foreign eyewitness saw them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a premodern eyewitness account of the north-Chinese roadside inn - its broad courtyard for carts and horses, its stable troughs of stone or brick and the wooden troughs set out in the yard - in the same northeast of China the twentieth-century wagon inn served, though on a different road. It generalizes about the inns of one road in one summer, from a visitor's eye, gives no plan or building height, and the year of the journey is not on this page (it is in the Chinese Wikipedia article on the diary).

Used for: that an eighteenth-century north-Chinese inn kept a broad yard for carts, a stable with long troughs and troughs in the yard (towns)
