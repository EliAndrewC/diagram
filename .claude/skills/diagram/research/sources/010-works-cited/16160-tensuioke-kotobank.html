tensuioke-kotobank

Kotobank, 天水桶 ("tensuioke, the rain-water tub", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering the entries of Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen, Nihon kokugo daijiten and Nipponica (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A4%A9%E6%B0%B4%E6%A1%B6-578334)

What it is: The published dictionaries' and encyclopedia's entries on the rain-water tub kept against fire in the Edo period: what it was, where it stood, when it became general and how big the street-corner tub was.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the tubs on roofs, at eaves and at street corners and gives the corner tub's capacity, about 6 koku. It is a modern reference work's summary (the Nipponica entry is signed by Miyamoto Mizuo) and cites no period record for the 6 koku or for where the tubs stood, so it carries the shape of the practice better than the number. It speaks of Edo-period towns generally without naming one, hedges the Kansei dating with "it is said" (though the Nihon kokugo daijiten entry on the same page quotes a shrine's tub from 1608), and its own conversion of 6 koku to "about 108 liters" disagrees with the koku figure by a factor of ten (a koku is about 180 liters; 108 liters is 6 to), and nothing on the page says which figure is the slip.

Used for: the rain-water tubs, where they stood and the corner tub's size (urban-features)
