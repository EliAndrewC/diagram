njg-horita

Morioka Hiroshi, 輪中と堀田 ("The waju and the horita", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Nippon Jitsugyo Publishing column, 2025 (in Japanese; https://www.njg.co.jp/column/column-41456/)

What it is: A publisher's column by a writer on Japanese surnames, explaining the surname Horita from the horita fields of the Nobi waju: part of a badly drained paddy dug down, the mud piled on the planting surface to raise it, the dug part (horitsubure-chi) a channel traveled by field boat.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the form clearly and says what the dug channels were used for. It is a popular column whose subject is a name, it gives no figures and dates the form only loosely (spreading from about the middle of the Edo period), and it draws on the author's visit to Suigo Park's reconstructed horita and the Kiso Sansen Waju Museum rather than on records.

Used for: the horita's dug channels traveled by field boat (archetypes)
