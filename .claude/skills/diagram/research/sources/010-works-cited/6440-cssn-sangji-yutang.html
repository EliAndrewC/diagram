中国社会科学网, 从生态美学视角研究桑基鱼塘 (https://www.cssn.cn/ztzl/jzz/rwln/wh/lnfw1/202209/t20220923_5541481.shtml)

What it is: An article by 吴建新 (Wu Jianxin) of South China Agricultural University, "从生态美学视角研究桑基鱼塘" (Studying the mulberry-dike fish pond from the perspective of ecological aesthetics), in 人文岭南, September 2022, on 中国社会科学网 (the Chinese Social Sciences Net). It describes the dike-and-pond farming of the Pearl River delta, in which low ground is dug out into fish ponds and the spoil raised into planted dikes, and gives the proportions and the crops the dikes carried.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the proportion of dike to water and for what grew on the dikes, which is what decides how a dike-and-pond block is drawn and tinted, and it is written by a scholar for an academic institute's public site rather than by a promoter of the landscape. Its limits are that the "six dike, four pond" figure is a remembered classic proportion rather than a measured average; that the piece is an essay on aesthetics and heritage rather than a field survey; that the practice is specific to Lingnan in southern China, so we treat it as one attested way of farming wet ground and not as a norm for the whole setting; and, on date, that by the article's own account the all-mulberry form only settled in the late Qing - earlier dikes carried fruit, tea and vegetables as often as mulberry - so a map set in an earlier era should draw mixed dikes rather than the fully formed six-four landscape.

Used for: the classic ratio named as "基六塘四" (dike six, pond four - note the order); early dikes planted with mulberry, tea, vegetables and fruit, fruit-dike ponds the commonest; mulberry dominant from the late Ming in Nanhai and Shunde
