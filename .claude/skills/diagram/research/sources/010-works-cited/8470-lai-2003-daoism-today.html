lai-2003-daoism-today

Lai Chi-Tim, "Daoism in China Today, 1980-2002", The China Quarterly 174 (2003) 413-427, DOI 10.1017/S0009443903000275, read on a library mirror rather than in the publisher's own edition (https://library.fes.de/libalt/journals/swetsfulltext/17041744.pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed survey of Daoism in China in the two decades after 1980, built from the Chinese Daoist Association's own journals and statistics and from the author's fieldwork at temples in Beijing, Chengdu, Suzhou, Nanjing, Shanghai and Guangzhou and in a village on the Guangdong coast. It distinguishes the two orders - the monastic Quanzhen and the Zhengyi - and describes how each is ordained, where its clergy live, and how the state has regulated them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source for the thing that puts a Daoist priest's household on a map rather than a dormitory: that the majority of Zhengyi priests lived married at home rather than in a temple, which is what makes their housing invert the Buddhist pattern. The author states that of the premodern tradition directly and in the past tense, so the record is not projecting a modern survival backward on its own authority. The limits are of two kinds. What is his own is modern: the fieldwork, the association statistics and every count in the article belong to the years its title names, so no proportion for any earlier century can come from here, and the twentieth-century form he describes was shaped by confiscation, a ban on the at-home clergy and the burning of their manuscripts before the rebuilding of the 1980s. What is premodern is RELAYED: the sentence the record quotes carries his own footnote to Schipper's The Taoist Body, and the state-pressure sentence beside it a footnote to Goossaert on the Chinese clergy - neither read by this project, so the historical claim reaches the record at one remove and those two are where a session should go to put it on its own feet. It is mainland China throughout, with Taiwan only in passing and nothing at all on Japan. And it is read on a third-party library mirror of a subscription aggregator rather than at the publisher, which is a link of exactly the kind that vanishes: the DOI is recorded above against that day.

Used for: the married Zhengyi priest living at home rather than in a monastery, and the state's standing objection to it
