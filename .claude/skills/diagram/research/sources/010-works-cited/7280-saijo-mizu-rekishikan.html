saijo-mizu-rekishikan

Saijo City (Ehime) Water History Museum, on digging a well (https://www.city.saijo.ehime.jp/site/mizunorekishikan/lineup3-8-3.html)

What it is: A page from the Water History Museum run by the city of Saijo in Ehime Prefecture, part of a series on local groundwater, explaining how a well is dug by hand: a vertical shaft about a meter across, wide enough for a digger to get down inside it, sunk by human labor until it reaches the aquifer.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the true diameter of the thing our oversized wellhead glyph stands for, from a municipal museum describing the well-digging of its own famously watery district, and the figure is not really local - a shaft has to be about that wide for a digger to work at the bottom of it. The limits are that a museum's public-education page is a summary written for visitors rather than a survey, and that it describes the technique in the present tense, so it is the dimensions of hand-digging we take from it and not a dated historical measurement; the English here is this project's translation of the Japanese.

Used for: a hand-dug well's shaft is about 1 m in diameter, large enough for a person to enter - the true size the oversized wellhead glyph stands for
