Fumoto Sumio, "鹿児島の歴史（４） 江戸時代の薩摩支配" ("The history of Kagoshima (4): Satsuma's rule in the Edo period", title translated from the Japanese by this project), NetIB-News (Data-Max), 2018-06-14 (in Japanese; https://www.data-max.co.jp/article/23153)

What it is: One part of a newspaper series on the history of Kagoshima by a former elementary-school principal and author of an introductory history of the city, describing Satsuma domain's outer-district system, its village land system and its ban on one Buddhist sect.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one head count found of a domain's retainers split between the castle town and the countryside: 1,015 castle-town samurai against 8,202 rural samurai in 1639. It is popular history in a business news site and names no primary source for the figures; it does not say whether the 1,015 includes the upper retainers its koku average leaves out, and it says the two names came into use only in the late eighteenth century. Satsuma is the extreme case of a domain that kept its retainers on the land, so the split describes one unusual domain, not the Edo-period norm.

Used for: Satsuma's retainers mostly housed in outer districts rather than the castle town, and the 1639 count (cities/sizing)
