zh.wikipedia 斜塘土地庙 (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/%E6%96%9C%E5%A1%98%E5%9C%9F%E5%9C%B0%E5%BA%99) - 「The surviving timber-framed main hall is three bays wide and one bay deep, 10 meters long and 7 meters wide」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「现存木构正殿面阔三间深一间，长10米，宽7米」)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the earth-god temple at Xietang in Suzhou, a Southern Song foundation whose surviving timber hall is probably of the Yuan-Ming transition, a Jiangsu provincial protected site since 2002, restored in 1997 and repaired in 2004 and 2006.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for a three-bay hall of 10 by 7 m, which stands at the large end of the earth-god shrines this record measures - a comparison this project makes against the few-square-meter shrines; the page itself never calls the temple large, and says its timber is smaller than comparable Yuan buildings. Its limits: one designated monument, a survivor selected for quality rather than a typical village shrine; a hall standing after three modern restorations; dimensions relayed from a gazetteer and a thesis; tertiary.

Used for: a three-bay earth-god hall of 10 by 7 m (religion-and-death.html).
