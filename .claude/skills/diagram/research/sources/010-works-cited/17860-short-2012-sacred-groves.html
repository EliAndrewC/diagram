short-2012-sacred-groves

Kevin Short, "Sacred groves amuse nature lovers, offer spiritual refreshments", Daily Yomiuri, 2 February 2012, reposted as "More on Sacred Groves" on Green Shinto, 15 February 2012 (https://www.greenshinto.com/2012/02/15/more-on-sacred-groves/)

What it is: A newspaper column by a naturalist who taught at the Tokyo University of Information Sciences, reposted in full on Green Shinto, a site on Shinto and the environment, describing where the big trees stand at Japan's shrines and temples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in plain words where the trees of a shrine or temple stand: single sacred trees in front of the buildings or along the approach, and a small grove behind the main building and around its sides, often a remnant of the old forest; and that the smallest local groves hold only a handful of trees. Its limits are that it is a modern popular column, not a survey; that it speaks of shrines and temples together and of today, not of the Edo period; and that it gives no counts or measurements.

Used for: which sides of a shrine's hall its grove stands on, and that the grove is a remnant stand (religion-and-death.html).
