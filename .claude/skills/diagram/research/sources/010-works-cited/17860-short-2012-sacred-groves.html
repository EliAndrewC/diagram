short-2012-sacred-groves

Kevin Short, "Sacred groves amuse nature lovers, offer spiritual refreshments", Daily Yomiuri, 2 February 2012, reposted as "More on Sacred Groves" on Green Shinto, 15 February 2012 (https://www.greenshinto.com/2012/02/15/more-on-sacred-groves/)

What it is: A newspaper column by a naturalist who taught at the Tokyo University of Information Sciences, reposted on Green Shinto, a site on Shinto and the environment, describing where the big trees stand at Japan's shrines and temples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in plain words where the trees of a shrine or temple stand: single sacred trees in front of the buildings or along the approach, and a small grove behind the main building and around its sides, often a remnant of the old forest; and that the groves at the tiniest local shrines and temples hold only several to a dozen or so trees each. Its limits are that it speaks of shrines and temples together and of today, not of the Edo period; that it gives no counts or measurements; and that its placement of the grove is a general statement whose named examples are all great shrines (Shimogamo, Omiwa, Togakushi, Ise, Kasuga). It never says where the few trees of a tiny local shrine stand, so the behind-and-sides layout at a village shrine is our reading of it, not something it shows at that scale.

Used for: which sides of a shrine's hall its grove stands on, and that the grove is often a remnant stand (religion-and-death.html).
