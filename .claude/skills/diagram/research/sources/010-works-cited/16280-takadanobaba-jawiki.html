ja.wikipedia 高田馬場 ("Takadanobaba", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%AB%98%E7%94%B0%E9%A6%AC%E5%A0%B4)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Tokyo district named after the Takada riding ground, which the third shogun laid out in 1636 for his bannermen's riding and mounted archery; it gives the ground's length and breadth.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures a real riding ground of the period: about 6 chō (about 650 m) east to west by 30-odd ken (about 55 m) north to south. The article footnotes this figure to the Edo meisho zue, the illustrated gazetteer of Edo published 1834-36, about two centuries after the ground was laid out; the breadth is approximate ("30-odd") and the metric conversions are the article's own. It was the shogunate's ground in the capital, built for its bannermen, so we use it to bound a riding ground's length from above rather than to set it; that a domain's ground was smaller is our reading of scale, not something the article says. The article is a tertiary encyclopedia entry, mostly about the modern district, and carries a banner saying its sources are insufficient. The gazetteer it cites is public (National Diet Library, NDLJP:1174144/283) and is the closer source for the number.

Used for: the upper bound of a riding ground's length (cities/government)
