kotobank-ryobosei

Kotobank, 両墓制 ("the two-grave system", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%A1%E5%A2%93%E5%88%B6-151106)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the two-grave system, gathering the entries of several Japanese reference works (the Heibonsha encyclopedia, the Yamakawa dictionary of Japanese history, Nipponica, Mypedia).

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for where each grave of the two lay: the grave where the body lay, most often on common land and set on a riverbank, a hillside or a beach away from the houses, marked with no more than a stone and reused in order of death, and the visited grave in the precinct of a temple or hall in the village (in one entry, within the house plot); and for its distribution. Its limits are that it is a set of dictionary entries, not a survey; that the entries disagree on the northeast (one has the custom reaching Tōhoku, another hardly seen there) and agree only that Kyūshū hardly has it; that none gives a distance from the houses or says how many a village kept - they say only that the burial grave lay away from the houses (Mypedia), beyond the village boundary on one theory (Yamakawa), or anywhere from far outside the village to beside the visited grave (Britannica); and that the custom is the one twentieth-century folklorists recorded, which the Heibonsha entry dates to the early Edo period at the earliest and Nipponica says in some places arose only when cemeteries were reorganized after Meiji.

Used for: the two-grave split - the body's grave on common land by the settlement, the visited grave at a temple or hall in the village - and where the custom is found
