kotobank-ryobosei

Kotobank, 両墓制 ("the two-grave system", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%A1%E5%A2%93%E5%88%B6-151106)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the two-grave system, gathering the entries of several Japanese reference works (the Heibonsha encyclopedia, the Yamakawa dictionary of Japanese history, Nipponica, Mypedia).

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for where each grave of the two lay: the grave where the body lay on common land by the settlement, marked with no more than a stone and reused in order of death, and the visited grave in the precinct of a temple or hall in the village; and for its distribution. Its limits are that it is a set of dictionary entries, not a survey; that the entries disagree on the northeast (one has the custom reaching Tōhoku, another hardly seen there) and agree only that Kyūshū hardly has it; and that none says how far the burial grave lay from the houses or how many a village kept.

Used for: the two-grave split - the body's grave on common land by the settlement, the visited grave at a temple or hall in the village - and where the custom is found
