magusa-jawiki

Wikipedia (Japanese), 秣 ("fodder", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A7%A3)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article on magusa, the grass fed to horses and cattle, citing two modern reference works (a 1994 dictionary of Heian-period history and a 2000 dictionary of Japanese folklore); it gives the word's two old spellings, the ancient court's fodder rules and the fodder fields villages kept.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the ancient evidence for dried grass as a fodder in Japan: the Wamyo Ruijusho glosses one of the two characters as karakusa, "dried grass", and the Engishiki had it collected from the home provinces for the court's horse bureaus. It is a stub summarizing two dictionaries, not a study, and it gives the Engishiki's rule in its own words, without quoting or citing the clause; it says only that the two characters are "thought" to have differed at first, and it does not say the grass the Engishiki collected was dried - that rests on reading the Wamyo Ruijusho's gloss onto the Engishiki's word; it says nothing of how or where the dried grass was stored, and the court's rule is about the capital's horses, not a village's or a town's.

Used for: dried grass named and collected as fodder in ancient Japan (towns)
