kotobank-gono

Kotobank, 豪農 ("gōnō, the rich farmer", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, revised edition, entry by Sasaki Junnosuke, with the Nipponica entry by Denda Isao and other dictionaries' entries beside it (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B1%AA%E8%BE%B2-170491)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the gōnō, carrying published dictionaries and the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry on the rich farmer-landlords of the later Edo period and on into early Meiji: when they arose, from what, and what they did.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the rich farmer of the question - a village landlord of the mid-eighteenth century on, out of older landlords or out of trade - so the record can say who such a homestead belonged to. Its limits: it is a tertiary source, encyclopedia summaries rather than the research behind them. Sasaki's definition belongs to one school of economic history, and the Britannica entry on the same page says the gōnō's nature is disputed. The class runs from the mid-eighteenth century into early Meiji, so traits the page gives for Bakumatsu and Meiji (new seed stock and new-style implements, silk-reeling ventures, 5-10 chō farms worked by hired labor) are later than the setting. It says nothing about houses, plots or buildings, and it describes Tokugawa Japan's later commercial economy rather than any one village.

Used for: who the rich farmer-landlords were (homesteads)
