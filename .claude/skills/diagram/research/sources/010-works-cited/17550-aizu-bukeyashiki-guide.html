Aizu Bukeyashiki, 会津武家屋敷 ("Aizu samurai residences", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the museum's guide page (in Japanese; https://bukeyashiki.com/guide)

What it is: The guide page of the Aizu Bukeyashiki, a private museum at Aizu-Wakamatsu, describing its karo residence - the house of the Saigo, hereditary chief retainers of the Aizu domain - rebuilt in 1975 from a bird's-eye drawing of the house that burned in the Boshin War.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the house's four groups of rooms - the reception suite for the lord, the rooms where retainers worked and kept guard, the family's rooms, and the maids' and servants' rooms - and a one-sided range beside the front gate holding retainers' dwellings. It is a museum's description of a reconstruction, not a survey of the lost house, and a karo's house is the top of a domain's retainers, not a middle-rank one.

Used for: where a great retainer's servants lived inside the house and beside its gate (government)
