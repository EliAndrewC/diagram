What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 玉川上水 (Tamagawa Josui), on the aqueduct built in the 1650s to carry drinking water from the Tama River across the Musashino plateau into Edo - its route and the ridge lines it follows, its total length and fall, the water-watch station at the city edge, and how the water was distributed once inside.

Why it applies, and its limits: Our capital-tier maps have to show how a very large premodern city was watered, and this gives the whole shape of the answer: an open cut of some forty-three kilometers dropping about ninety meters, run deliberately along the high ground so gravity does the work, then a control point at the city boundary and buried wooden and stone pipes beyond it. It is a tertiary summary whose figures we take only where its own references support them, and it describes one exceptional work built for the shogun's own capital, so it marks the top of what a premodern state could manage rather than ordinary practice; the article also gives no total length for the buried pipe network, so we do not state one.

Used for: 「a total length of 42.74 kilometers across an elevation difference of 92.3 meters」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「高低差92.3メートルの間に全長42.74キロメートル」); distribution into the city from the 水番所 at Yotsuya Ōkido; wooden and stone pipes underground below it (no total km given)
