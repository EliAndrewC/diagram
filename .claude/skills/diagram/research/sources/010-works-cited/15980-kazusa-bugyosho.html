kazusa-bugyosho

To KAZUSA (a local-history blog), 南町奉行所と大岡越前 ("The South Town Magistracy and Ooka Echizen", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kazusa.jpn.org/b/archives/2883)

What it is: A local-history blog post (2014, by the site's pseudonymous author "kazusa", whose site is chiefly on the Kazusa domains) on the Edo South Town Magistracy and its best-known magistrate, Ooka Tadasuke, with the site and building sizes of the two Edo magistracies; it ends with a list of works it drew on (the Tokyo shishi-ko, Okuma Yoshikuni's Edo kenchiku sowa, Matsudaira Taro's study of Edo institutions, among others) but ties no figure to any of them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one readable figure found for the size of an Edo magistracy's ground, which is the anchor our Imperial Magistrate's compound is calibrated to. It is an enthusiast's post that hedges the figure with "it seems" and does not say which of its listed works the figure comes from (a search summary attributes a 2,617-tsubo figure to a plan of Ooka's official residence, not read). The figure describes the two magistracies on the sites they held from 1707 to the end of the shogunate, and those were the town magistracies of the shogun's capital, not a provincial office; they were not certainly the largest of their kind (a search summary, not read, gives Osaka's East magistracy about 3,000 tsubo).

Used for: the two Edo magistracies each on about 2,500 to 2,600 tsubo with a mansion of over 1,000 tsubo (cities/capitals)
