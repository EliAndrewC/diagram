zojoji-keidai

Zojoji (増上寺), 境内散歩 ("A walk through the precinct", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the temple's own site (in Japanese; https://www.zojoji.or.jp/keidai/)

What it is: The temple's own guide to its precinct and buildings, with the distances along its approach and the size of its sutra repository.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the distances from the outer gate to the main gate and from the main gate to the main hall, the spacing of a great temple's approach. The page ties both distances to Buddhist numbers (108 passions, 48 vows), so they are as much symbol as survey, and the page does not date them. They run from the outer gate, rebuilt in concrete in 1937 and larger than the original, through the main gate of 1622, to the main hall rebuilt in 1974. So they measure the precinct as it stands, not a survey from the Edo period. The sutra repository is different: the shogunate paid for it in the Edo period and it still stands, so its size is a period measurement. Zojoji was one of the two family temples of the Tokugawa shoguns, so these figures are the top of the range, the largest temple a city in the setting would have, not an ordinary great temple.

Used for: the approach of a great temple, about 108 ken from outer gate to main gate and 48 ken on to the main hall; a sutra repository 9.5 ken square (religion-and-death)
