Wikisource (Chinese), 齊民要術 (四庫全書本)/全覽 ("Qimin yaoshu, the Siku Quanshu text, whole", title translated from the Chinese by this project), the Siku editors' abstract at its head (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93_(%E5%9B%9B%E5%BA%AB%E5%85%A8%E6%9B%B8%E6%9C%AC)/%E5%85%A8%E8%A6%BD)

What it is: The full text of the sixth-century Chinese farm manual Qimin yaoshu as the eighteenth-century imperial library copied it, headed by the library editors' critical abstract of the book.

Why it applies, and its limits: The abstract is the standard old judgment on how the manual's text came down: it says the "miscellaneous remarks" set before the first book read unlike the rest and were suspected of being added by a later hand, though, because a Song catalogue (Chen Zhensun's) already quoted the passage's first line, a Song edition must have had them. It is an eighteenth-century judgment from style, not a dating: its only bound is that the passage was in the text by the Song. Modern textual scholarship (Miao Qiyu's annotated editions, a study of the passage's vocabulary) dates it more narrowly, to a Tang-era writer, and finds it in the earliest Northern Song printing. It says nothing about farming or towns itself.

Used for: the doubt over when the manual's advice on vegetables near a city wall was written (towns)
