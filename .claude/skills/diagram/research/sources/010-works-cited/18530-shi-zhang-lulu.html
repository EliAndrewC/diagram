shi-zhang-lulu

Shi Xiaolei and Zhang Baichun, 我国单曲柄辘轳普遍应用的年代考 ("When the single-crank windlass came into common use in China", title translated from the Chinese by this project), on the agricultural-history site of the Institute for the History of Natural Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences, 2009 or later (in Chinese; http://agri-history.ihns.ac.cn/tools/shixiaolei1.htm)

What it is: A paper by two historians of technology that dates the crank windlass over a Chinese well from paintings, tomb murals and excavated models, and on the way describes the pulley hung on a well frame in a Western Han tomb mural from Liaoyang and the many pulleys on Han reliefs, bricks, murals and pottery well models.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a dated historian's reading of premodern images, which is what places a pulley on a frame over a Chinese well two thousand years ago. It concerns the lifting gear only: it gives no size of frame or curb and no count of how many wells had which gear, and it calls its own dating of the crank windlass provisional.

Used for: the pulley on a well frame in Han China (urban-features)
