shi-zhang-lulu

Shi Xiaolei and Zhang Baichun, 我国单曲柄辘轳普遍应用的年代考 ("When the single-crank windlass came into common use in China", title translated from the Chinese by this project), on the agricultural-history site of the Institute for the History of Natural Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences, 2009 or later (in Chinese; http://agri-history.ihns.ac.cn/tools/shixiaolei1.htm)

What it is: A paper by two historians of technology that dates the crank windlass over a Chinese well from paintings, tomb murals and excavated models, and on the way describes the pulley hung on a well frame in a Han tomb mural from Sandaohao, Liaoyang (which it calls Western Han) and the many pulleys on Han reliefs, bricks, murals and pottery well models.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a dated historian's reading of premodern images, which is what places a pulley on a frame over a Chinese well two thousand years ago. It concerns the lifting gear only: it gives no size of frame or curb and no count of how many wells had which gear, and it calls its own dating of the crank windlass provisional. It also calls the Liaoyang mural Western Han, but the Sandaohao mural tombs are dated to the late Eastern Han or the Han-Wei transition (the Western Han find at Sandaohao is a settlement). So the mural shows a Han pulley about 1,800 years old, and it is the Han reliefs and pottery well models it names that carry the wider Han date.

Used for: the pulley on a well frame in Han China (urban-features)
