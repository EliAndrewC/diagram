kotobank-hinoban

Kotobank, 火の番 ("Hi no ban, the fire watch", title translated) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%81%AB%E3%81%AE%E7%95%AA-612078)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the fire watch, carrying the Digital Daijisen and the Sekai daihyakka jiten: the shogunate's fire watches in Edo, the ward watch houses and gate watchmen, and who fought fire in the villages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one reference found that says who fought fire in a village. Its limits are that it is a tertiary encyclopedia entry (the Sekai daihyakka jiten section, signed by Oguma Makoto); that its village passage is two sentences, which name the groups and their night rounds with wooden clappers and iron staves but describe no firefighting apparatus; that it dates the practice only as lasting "until the modern era", without naming a period or a region; and that it describes Japanese villages only, so for the setting's Chinese side it stands in as an analog.

Used for: village firefighting by the young men's association and the five-household groups (urban-features)
