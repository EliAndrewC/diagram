fcp-gyoaido-kiln

Nakano Kodai, 「On the charcoal kilns of the Gyoaido B site」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「行合道B遺跡の木炭窯について」), research column no. 53, Fukushima Cultural Foundation, archaeological survey department (in Japanese; https://www.fcp.or.jp/iseki/column/359)

What it is: An excavator's column on four charcoal kilns dug at a site on a hill slope in Date city, Fukushima, giving the full measurements of the best-preserved of them (kiln no. 1) and a few of the others, their structure (dug into the slope, with a chimney running up it and a work area before its mouth) and a radiocarbon date.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a measured charcoal kiln of the right age - late 16th to early 17th century - which is what sizes a kiln on a map. It is one site in one district of the Abukuma hills, a cluster of kilns standing alone on a hill slope with nothing of their age beside them, so it shows how a kiln sits on a slope but not how far it stood from a village. The date rests on radiocarbon alone, with no datable finds, and the excavator lists the date and its span among his open questions. The site's largest chamber, 2.92 by 2.10 m, is the smallest of its type: the column gives 3.29 by 2.06 m and 4.00 by 2.60 m at two other Fukushima sites. The flue, forecourt and work area are measured for kiln no. 1 alone. The column is the excavator's informal summary of the 2015 excavation report, on a subject he calls outside his specialty, and he leaves open whether some kilns were fully or half sunk into the ground.

Used for: the size, siting and form of an early-modern charcoal kiln (vegetation)
