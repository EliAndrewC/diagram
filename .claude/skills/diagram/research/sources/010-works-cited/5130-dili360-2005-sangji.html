邓芬, 「Mulberry-dike fish ponds: the commonest of beautiful landscapes is no more」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「桑基鱼塘 最常见的美景风光不再」), 中国国家地理 2005(02) (https://www.dili360.com/cng/article/p5350c3d7e437f08.htm)

What it is: A 2005 magazine feature by 邓芬 (Deng Fen) in 中国国家地理 (Chinese National Geography), "桑基鱼塘 最常见的美景风光不再" (Mulberry-dike fish ponds: the commonest of beautiful landscapes is no more), on the disappearance of the dike-and-pond country of the Pearl River delta. It describes what the landscape looked like: fish raised in the ponds, mulberry and fruit trees on the dikes, banana groves and seas of sugarcane, and the net of creeks running through it all.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the look of the thing - which plants stood where, and how the water threaded between them - which is what a map has to show, and a photojournalistic feature is unusually good at that. Its limits are that it is popular magazine writing rather than research, that it gives no dimensions at all, and that the scene it describes is the twentieth-century remnant the writer remembers rather than a Ming or Qing landscape, so we take the arrangement from it and any measurement from elsewhere.

Used for: the lost traditional landscape's elements - 塘里养鱼，基上种桑树、种果树; 蕉林蔗海; the 河涌 creek net. No dimensions
