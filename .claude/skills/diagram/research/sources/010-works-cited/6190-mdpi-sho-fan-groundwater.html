mdpi-sho-fan-groundwater

"Geochemical Study on the Annual Variation of Oxygen Isotope and Chemical Composition of Groundwater in the Sho River Alluvium Fan, Toyama, Japan", Geosciences 11(8):352 (2021) (https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3263/11/8/352)

What it is: A peer-reviewed open-access paper in Geosciences (2021) tracing, through a year of oxygen-isotope and chemical measurements, where the shallow groundwater of the Sho River alluvial fan in Toyama comes from.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the ground under the scattered farmsteads of the Tonami plain: the plain is that river's alluvial fan, and the shallow water beneath it is a mixture of river water and rainfall, which is the setting for a farmstead that draws its own water. The paper is a modern geochemical study concerned with where the water comes from rather than how deep it lies, and it does not discuss paddies at all, so the idea that a farmstead there could reach water with a shallow well is an inference of ours and is labeled as a guess where it is used.

Used for: the Tonami plain as the Sho River alluvial fan and its shallow groundwater as a mixture of river water and precipitation (NOT paddy recharge - the paper never mentions paddy); it gives no water-table depth, so 'easily-reached water' is the record's inference, labeled GUESS since 2026-09-07
