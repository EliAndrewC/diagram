bunka-fujino-naya

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Online, 藤野家住宅納屋 ("Fujino house, barn", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/284149)

What it is: The national register's entry for the barn of the Fujino house in Fujiidera, Osaka, built between 1883 and 1897: where it stands on the plot, its length and depth, its roof and walls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the measured size and the siting of one real farm barn. Its limits: a Meiji building near Osaka, belonging to the household that had been Fujiidera village's headman (shōya) for generations, with a 396-square-meter main house, so its barn sits at the top of the village and is larger than an ordinary farm's would be; built fireproof like a storehouse, which an ordinary Edo farm's shed was not; one example.

Used for: the size and siting of a farm barn (homesteads)
