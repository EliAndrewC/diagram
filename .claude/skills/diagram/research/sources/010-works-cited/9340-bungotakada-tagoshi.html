bungotakada-tagoshi

Bungotakada City Cultural Properties Office, "Walking the medieval village of Tashibu-no-sho Osaki, part 3: following the water" (in Japanese; https://www.city.bungotakada.oita.jp/site/bunkazai/2175.html)

What it is: A page by the cultural properties office of Bungotakada city (Oita prefecture), one of a series on Tashibu-no-sho Osaki, a village of a medieval estate whose farm landscape is a designated Important Cultural Landscape, describing its weirs, its springs and its paddy-to-paddy (tagoshi) irrigation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets modern paddies, each fed from a supply canal and emptied into a drain, against the old-style irrigation the village keeps, in which water passes from paddy to paddy, and says the old way saves water. It describes one hill village as it survives today, written for a general audience; it does not say how common the form was, nor count the village's ditches, and the same village keeps many weirs.

Used for: that pre-modern paddy water passed from paddy to paddy rather than down a ditch to each (fields)
