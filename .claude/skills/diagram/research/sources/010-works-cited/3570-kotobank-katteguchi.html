kotobank-katteguchi

Digital Daijisen, Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho Kodansha's dictionary of house and interior terms and HomePro's renovation glossary, s.v. 勝手口, on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/勝手口)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of five dictionaries' entries (Digital Daijisen, Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho, and two modern house-terminology glossaries) on the katteguchi, the kitchen door of a Japanese house and the host's door of a tea room.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the kitchen door as a named entrance set against the guests', which is the service circulation this page inverts; dictionaries of the present-day language, giving the door as the way from outside into the kitchen and apart from the formal entrance; the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten attests the word for a kitchen door in a haikai collection of 1666 and for the tea room's host door in a tea record of 1608, so the name and the thing are Edo-period. The Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho's fuller account of the door's use (rubbish, laundry, tradesmen, neighbors, children, utility meters) describes a modern house and is not carried back; a door apart from the genkan presupposes a house that has one, in the Edo period a warrior's or a wealthy house; and no entry says the kitchen door is the kitchen's only door to the outside, so that claim needs another source.

Used for: the kitchen door as the service entrance (buildings)
