kotobank-katteguchi

Digital Daijisen, Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho, a house-terminology dictionary and a renovation firm's glossary, s.v. 勝手口 ("kitchen door", title translated), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/勝手口)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of five entries on the katteguchi, the kitchen door of a Japanese house and the host's door of a tea room: three present-day dictionaries (Digital Daijisen, the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten with its dated examples, Kodansha's house and interior terms), Nakamura Hitoshi's signed article in the Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho, and a renovation firm's (HomePro's) glossary.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the kitchen door as a named entrance set against the guests', which is the service circulation this page inverts; dictionary lines of the present-day language, giving the door as the way from outside into the kitchen and apart from the formal entrance. The Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's dated examples put the word in the kitchen sense in 1666, which dates the door, not its use; who uses the door (tradesmen, neighbors, children) is given in the present tense, in the Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho article that also advises putting the electricity, gas and water meters by the door, so of houses today; the one dated example of a caller at the door is a shop's man announcing himself in Futabatei Shimei's novel Ukigumo (1887-89), which is Meiji Tokyo, not the Edo period.

Used for: the kitchen door as the service entrance, the word's first attested use, and who uses the door today (buildings)
