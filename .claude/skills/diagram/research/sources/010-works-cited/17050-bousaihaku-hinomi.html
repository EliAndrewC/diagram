bousaihaku-hinomi

Fire and Disaster Management Museum (online), Kazuo Tsuchiya, "火の見櫓は何を語るか" ("What do fire-watch towers tell us?", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.bousaihaku.com/firetower/4293/)

What it is: An essay by a lecturer in design who, with a group, counted and located nearly a thousand fire-watch towers in Shizuoka over two and a half years, noting their forms; dates came only where a plaque at a tower's foot gave one.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the towers standing today to the postwar decades, which is why a surviving village tower is no evidence for an Edo-period one. Its limits are that it is one prefecture's survey; that its dating ("most" built from 1945 to the early 1970s, and "presumably" in the high-growth years) is the author's estimate in a reflective essay, not a tabulated count; and that it says only, without evidence, that the tower goes back to the Edo period and spread nationwide in the Showa era, not what a village had before the towers.

Used for: the modern date of the fire-watch towers standing today (urban-features)
