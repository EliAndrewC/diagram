en.wikipedia Economy of the Song dynasty (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Song_dynasty)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article "Economy of the Song dynasty", a survey of Chinese agriculture, industry, trade and money in the tenth to thirteenth centuries. We use its account of the iron trade: Robert Hartwell's estimate that per capita iron output rose sixfold between 806 and 1078, and the description of iron-smelting households working with government support such as charcoal supplies and skilled craftsmen.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives us the scale and the organization of premodern heavy industry - how a smelting operation was staffed and supplied, and how much iron a large premodern state could make - which is what sizes and sites an ironworking quarter on a town map. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary whose figures we take only where its own references support them, and the article itself reports that Donald Wagner questions Hartwell's method, so we treat the tonnage as a contested estimate; and a dynasty-wide output figure has to be scaled down hard before it says anything about one town's forges.

Used for: "Historian Robert Hartwell estimates that per capita iron output rose sixfold between 806 and 1078, such that, by 1078 China was producing 127,000,000 kg (125,000 t) in weight of iron per year"; Bao Qingtian on iron households given "government support, such as charcoal supplies and skilled craftsmen"
