ja.wikipedia 道切り ("michikiri, cutting the road", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%81%93%E5%88%87%E3%82%8A)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on michikiri, the folk custom of barring evil at the roads into a village: the boundary god, the rope or straw serpent, the offered sandals, and the forms found in Kinki and Chiba.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the custom at the village's entrance roads and crossroads and lists its physical forms, among them a rope stretched across the road and one fixed to a boundary tree. It is a short encyclopedia article, marked by Wikipedia as a stub, drawn from customs still practiced today: one 2013 book on the rope form, a weather-association column, municipal education-board pages and a Chiba prefectural museum's pages. Its list of forms rests on one of those Chiba museum pages. It dates the custom only as "from of old", and dates none of its forms to the Edo period or earlier, so the forms are what survives in modern Japan, standing in for what a premodern village put up. It gives no share of villages that practiced any one form.

Used for: the forms of cutting the road at a village's entrance (ways)
