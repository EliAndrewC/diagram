ja.wikipedia 地蔵盆 ("Jizō-bon, the street jizo's festival", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9C%B0%E8%94%B5%E7%9B%86)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the late-summer festival a town block holds for its street-corner jizo, mostly in the Kinki region.

Why it applies, and its limits: It qualifies the block-by-block count - in Kyoto a block with no jizo of its own borrows one from a temple or hangs a painted one for the festival - and reports it "said" that from the Kantō eastward the dōsojin did the street jizo's work, which is why Jizō-bon never spread east. Its limits: a tertiary article describing the custom as kept today, with no counts and no date for when the custom arose; the borrowing is a Kyoto observation, cited to a Kyoto City page; the eastern point is a hedged explanation of the festival's spread, cited to a town's report (Sekigahara), names the Kantō and the east rather than everything east of the Kinki (parts of Chūbu keep Jizō-bon), and does not say the east has no street jizo - so it lets an eastern map show a dōsojin, not leave the jizo out.

Used for: a town block without its own jizo; the dōsojin in the street jizo's place in the east (religion-and-death)
