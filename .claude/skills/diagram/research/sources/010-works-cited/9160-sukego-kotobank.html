sukego-kotobank

Kotobank, 助郷 - 日本大百科全書 (the entry signed 丸山雍成) and ブリタニカ国際大百科事典 (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8A%A9%E9%83%B7)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the sukego, the villages near a highway post station that supplied men and horses when the station's own were not enough, carrying two published works on the 1694 settlement that fixed the duty at two men and two horses per hundred koku of assessed yield, on the catchment beginning at two or three ri and widening past ten, and on its commutation into money.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the evidence that a trunk-road settlement swamped by arriving traffic did not answer with a bigger yard but by widening the ring it drew animals from, which is what withdraws this record's town-against-city contrast. Its limits are that these are tertiary summaries of published reference works; that they describe a corvee obligation rather than commercial stabling; and that the system is Tokugawa Japanese.

Used for: the neighboring villages supplying men and horses at two per hundred koku, and the catchment widened from two or three ri past ten
