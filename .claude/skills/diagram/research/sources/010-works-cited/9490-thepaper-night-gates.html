thepaper-night-gates

The Paper (澎湃新闻), 在古代的夜晚，城门一旦关上就真的出不去了吗？, a 2020 article by 湘桥蓬蒿人 for the magazine 国家人文历史 (https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_9963480)

What it is: A popular-history magazine article, republished on the news site The Paper, asking whether anyone could get out of a Chinese city once its gates were shut for the night. It describes the night curfew from the Zhou onward, above all Tang Chang'an's, where the ward and market gates were closed at the dusk drum and the palace and ward gates, the streets and the outer wall's gates were all barred by checkpoints at night, and then walks through the moat, the outer wall and the gate defenses of Song and Ming cities.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page read for a city's gates, and not only its ward gates, being barred from dusk, which is why a traveler arriving late stayed outside. It is a popular article rather than scholarship, it quotes its classical passages without saying which edition, and its detailed account is of one capital, Tang Chang'an, with the later dynasties covered in a sentence; it asks about leaving a city at night rather than entering one, and that a county town's gate kept the same hours is our extension of it, not its statement.

Used for: the dusk closing of a city's gates, the ground for the traveler shut outside (towns)
