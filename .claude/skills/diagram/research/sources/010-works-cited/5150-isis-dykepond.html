isis-dykepond

Mae-Wan Ho, "The Circular Economy of the Dyke-Pond System", ISIS, 2006-10-09 (https://www.i-sis.org.uk/DykePondSystem.php)

What it is: "The Circular Economy of the Dyke-Pond System" by Mae-Wan Ho, published on 9 October 2006 by the Institute of Science in Society. It is a summary account of the mulberry-dike and fish-pond farming of the Pearl River Delta in southern China, taking its figures from the research literature on that system (Ruddle and Zhong, Zhong and colleagues, and Deng); it puts the system's evolution over two thousand years and the start of water-control works in the low ground in the mid-fourteenth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the dimensions and the working rhythm of the dike-and-pond landscape our delta maps are built from - ponds mostly rectangular, 0.4 to 0.6 hectares and 2 to 3 meters deep, dikes 6 to 10 meters wide, pigs and poultry kept on the dikes, the ponds drained two or three times a year and the bottom mud dredged up onto the dikes to raise and repair them. Its limits: it is a secondary summary from a campaigning organization rather than the original field studies, and those studies have no publicly readable copy, so its numbers rest on work we cannot check; the studies measured the system in the late twentieth century, when its crop mix answered modern markets, even though the dike-and-pond form itself is far older; and it is southern China, not Japan.

Used for: the numbers it carries from Ruddle & Zhong - "most ponds are rectangular, 0.4 to 0.6 ha in area and 2 to 3 m deep. The dykes are usually 6 to 10 m wide"; the late-1980s land use (fishponds 35%, mulberry 12%, sugar cane 18%); "pigs, chickens and ducks are reared on the dykes"; "ponds are drained two or three times a year, and the mud at the bottom is dredged up to put on the dykes"; dike construction from the mid-fourteenth century
