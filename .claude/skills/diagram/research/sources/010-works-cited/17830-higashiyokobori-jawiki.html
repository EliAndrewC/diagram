ja.wikipedia 東横堀川 ("Higashi-Yokobori canal", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9D%B1%E6%A8%AA%E5%A0%80%E5%B7%9D)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Higashi-Yokobori, Osaka's oldest canal, dug in 1585 (or, on another view it gives, 1594) on Hideyoshi's order as the western outer moat of Osaka Castle, which branches south from the upper Tosabori River; it lists the bridges over it from upstream.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a canal that was also a castle's outer moat opening straight off a river, and counts the bridges over a city canal of about 3 km. It was a moat only from 1585 to 1615: it was filled under the peace that ended the Winter Campaign, re-dug after the castle fell, and was a town canal through the Edo period, so it shows a canal's mouth on a river rather than a lasting moat's. It is a tertiary source by anonymous editors; its list of fourteen bridges is of today's crossings and does not say which stood in the Edo period, so it bounds rather than gives a period density; and Osaka was an unwalled castle town, not a walled Chinese city.

Used for: a city canal opening its own mouth on a river (cities/river-cities); the bridges over one city canal (ways)
