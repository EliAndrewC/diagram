kotobank-ujigami

Kotobank, 氏神 ("ujigami, tutelary god", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the entries by Sasaki Masaru in Shogakukan's Nihon daihyakka zensho (Nipponica) and by Sonoda Minoru in Heibonsha's Sekai daihyakka jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%8F%E7%A5%9E-34471)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the ujigami, gathering signed encyclopedia entries on how the clan god became the god of the village and its land, with Yanagita Kunio's three kinds of ujigami.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a tutelary shrine is found in every village and that the idea of the village as the unit of shrine and parish spread over almost the whole country under the Edo order, which is the baseline for asking whether a hamlet below the village had one. It is a tertiary summary, good for the shape and not for a count. Its "every village" describes the present day, and Sonoda says the Meiji government made the village shrine as ujigami official policy, so today's uniformity is partly a Meiji product; for the Edo period it claims only that the idea spread, not that every village had a shrine. It speaks of the village, not of hamlets within it; the gods it names below the village belong to a kin group (the maki or ikke ujigami) or a single house, not to a hamlet.

Used for: a tutelary shrine in every village, and the village as its unit since the Edo period (archetypes)
