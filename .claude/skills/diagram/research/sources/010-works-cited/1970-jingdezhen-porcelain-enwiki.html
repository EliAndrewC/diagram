What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the porcelain of Jingdezhen, the Chinese kiln city: its siting on its clay deposits, its imperial and private kilns, and its workforce across the centuries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that the kiln city stood at its clay and that its imperial kilns sat at the city's center with the others four kilometers out, which is the kiln-at-the-clay siting this page follows. A tertiary summary; its one passage on where the workers lived describes seasonal migrants in sheds in 1905, not resident kiln households. The page gives no period for the arrangement of the imperial kilns at the center and the others four kilometers out, and Jingdezhen is the exceptional case - a national industry grown into a city - standing behind a county seat's single works.

Used for: the kiln city sited on its clay; imperial kilns at the center, others four kilometers out
