l7r-budgets

The GM's campaign notes, budgets.md, "Capital city", "Provincial city", "Town", "Cross-cutting project negotiation", "Imperial roads: a special case", "Imperial roads and waystations: line-item detail" and "Samurai stipend convention" (URL: none - the GM's own unpublished writing, at /host-l7r-repo/setting/budgets.md)

What it is: The setting's own notes, written by the game master, on what each tier of settlement and government holds and spends. They give each settlement tier's caste table - a provincial city's inhabitants a quarter merchants, as a domain capital's are, against a tenth in a town - and say who pays to keep the roads up: the Imperial roads from the central treasury, the domain's everyday roads by its daimyo, provincial governors and county magistrates. Its "Samurai stipend convention" counts a county town's samurai, among them the magistrate's karo, the chief retainer, at Rank 4.

Why it applies, and its limits: This is canon rather than evidence: how a settlement's inhabitants divide by caste and who keeps a road in repair are the setting's to decide, and these maps follow them. There is no historical limitation to state, because the notes are not making a historical claim; they do not say which caste row an artisan is counted under, or who keeps up the stretch of an Imperial road inside a city's walls, or where a magistrate's karo lives, which the buildings pages work out from the historical record.

Used for: a provincial city's merchant share, behind the ribbon of shops on the Imperial road, and who keeps up the Imperial road and the domain's own roads, behind where the road's label sits (cities/fabric); the ferry stations and bridges over major rivers counted among the Imperial road's engineered structures (ways); the county magistrate's karo, behind where a posting's chief retainer lives (buildings)
