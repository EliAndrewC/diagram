The GM's campaign notes, budgets.md, "Capital city", "Provincial city", "Town", "Cross-cutting project negotiation", "Imperial roads: a special case", "Per-waystation staffing breakdown", "Imperial roads and waystations: line-item detail", "Samurai stipend convention", "Kazuma's discretionary breakdown" and "Imperial magistrates and their staff: line-item detail" (URL: none - the GM's own unpublished writing, at /host-l7r-repo/setting/budgets.md)

What it is: The setting's own notes, written by the game master, on what each tier of settlement and government holds and spends. They give each settlement tier's caste table - a provincial city's inhabitants a quarter merchants, as a domain capital's are, against a tenth in a town - and say who pays to keep the roads up: the Imperial roads from the central treasury, the domain's everyday roads by its daimyo, provincial governors and county magistrates. Its "Samurai stipend convention" counts a county town's samurai, among them the magistrate's karo, the chief retainer, at Rank 4.

Why it applies, and its limits: This is canon rather than evidence: how a settlement's inhabitants divide by caste and who keeps a road in repair are the setting's to decide, and these maps follow them. There is no historical limitation to state, because the notes are not making a historical claim; they do not say which caste row an artisan is counted under, or who keeps up the stretch of an Imperial road inside a city's walls, or where a magistrate's karo lives, which the buildings pages work out from the historical record.

Used for: a provincial city's merchant share, behind the ribbon of shops on the Imperial road, and who keeps up the Imperial road and the domain's own roads, behind where the road's label sits (cities/fabric); that the setting's ashigaru are peasants, not samurai (government); the ferry stations and bridges over major rivers counted among the Imperial road's engineered structures (ways); the county magistrate's karo, behind where a posting's chief retainer lives (buildings); the Town table's families by caste and wealth, its samurai note and the village's headman, behind how many houses of each caste a town map draws (towns); a county budget that keeps a jail and pays for no execution, and the ceremonial execution costs met at the capital, behind a county seat that executes outside the town (urban-features)
