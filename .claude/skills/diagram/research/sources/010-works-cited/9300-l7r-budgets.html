What it is: The setting's own notes, written by the game master, on what each tier of settlement and government holds and spends. They give each settlement tier's caste table - a provincial city's inhabitants a quarter merchants, as a domain capital's are, against a tenth in a town - and say who pays to keep the roads up: the Imperial roads from the central treasury, the domain's everyday roads by its daimyo, provincial governors and county magistrates. Its "Samurai stipend convention" lists a county town's samurai by rank, the magistrate at Rank 5 and their karo, the chief retainer, at Rank 4.

Why it applies, and its limits: This is canon rather than evidence: how a settlement's inhabitants divide by caste and who keeps a road in repair are the setting's to decide, and these maps follow them. They do not say which caste row an artisan is counted under, or who keeps up the stretch of an Imperial road inside a city's walls, or where a magistrate's karo lives, which the buildings pages work out from the historical record.

Used for: a provincial city's merchant share, behind the ribbon of shops on the Imperial road, and who keeps up the Imperial road and the domain's own roads, behind where the road's label sits (Urban fabric); that the setting's ashigaru are peasants, not samurai (The government quarter); the ferry stations and bridges over major rivers counted among the Imperial road's engineered structures (Ways); the county magistrate's karo, behind where a posting's chief retainer lives, and a county office's three or four heimen clerks, who live in town, behind the clerks' room and their housing (Estates and other compounds); the Town table's families by caste and wealth, its samurai note and the village's headman, behind how many houses of each caste a town map draws (Towns); a county budget that keeps a jail and pays for no execution, and the ceremonial execution costs met at the Imperial capital, Otosan Uchi, behind a county seat that executes outside the town (Trades and services); the capital staffed by the senior samurai, the kin of the daimyo lifted into the senior ranks, and a Rank 4 samurai's stipend, behind where a capital's samurai live and the ground a retainer terrace takes; the Imperial Magistrate stationed at the domain capital and what its budget pays for, behind the Imperial Magistrate's compound (Domain capitals)
