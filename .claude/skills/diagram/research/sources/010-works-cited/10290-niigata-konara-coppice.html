niigata-konara-coppice

Niigata Prefecture Forest Research Institute, 「A coppicing method that keeps aged konara woods from thinning out」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「高齢化したコナラ林の低密度化を抑制する萌芽更新方法」), Ringyō Niigata, December 2024 (https://www.pref.niigata.lg.jp/site/shinrin/rin-nii-202412.html)

What it is: A short article by the prefectural forest research institute on a trial of two-stage felling in a 60-year-old konara wood at Murakami, to keep old stumps alive when the wood is returned to coppicing.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its opening sentence states the cutting cycle of the konara fuel woods of Niigata as 15 to 30 years. The article is about a modern trial; the old practice is one line of background, and it has three limits. "Formerly" is not dated: most plausibly it means the fuel and charcoal woods worked until the switch to oil and gas in the 1950s and 60s, and it is not shown to be an Edo-period or earlier practice. The figure has no source behind it. And it describes one Japanese prefecture, which stands in for the setting's Chinese side as an analog. We use it as the rough length of a coppice cycle, not as a measured premodern figure.

Used for: the cutting cycle of a konara fuel wood (vegetation)
