mlit-mizubunka-sanko

Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, reference tables of local water culture (水の郷 materials), 参考 ("Reference", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.mlit.go.jp/tochimizushigen/mizsei/mizusato/files/mizubunsanko2.pdf)

What it is: A table compiled by the national land ministry's water-resources department of local water customs and facilities reported by municipalities across Japan - washing places, water wheels, old waterworks - one short paragraph a place, under headings that also run to water legends, rain rituals and festivals. The photographs are credited, most to a municipality and some to a website or a company's water-culture centre; the rows without one carry no credit. The document is undated: a photograph stamped '98 and a row citing a dam finished in 1994 put it no earlier than 1998.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its rows show the shared washing place as a common thing in town and village alike - Gujo Hachiman's castle-town channels of the 1650s and their kawado, Hirosaki's spring-fed washing places with drinking and washing tanks kept apart, washing spots with rules of use on the hamlet channels of Fuji town in Saga (a row that gives no date), and Omihachiman's piped town water from a parent well. It is a modern list, compiled no earlier than 1998 from what municipalities reported, uneven row by row, and what it describes is what survives. Its rows date their things unevenly: Gujo's channels to 1652, Hirosaki's Dagi spring to before 1689 but its strict separation of drinking and washing tanks only to the years after the Second World War, and the Saga and Omihachiman rows not at all. So a row shows the form of a practice, not that the form is premodern, unless the row dates it. It is Japan only, standing in for the Chinese side of the setting.

Used for: shared washing places in towns and villages; Gujo's castle-town channels; Omihachiman's piped water (water)
