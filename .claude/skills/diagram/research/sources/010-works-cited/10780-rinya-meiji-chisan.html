rinya-meiji-chisan

Forestry Agency (林野庁), 「On the erosion-control works of the Meiji period」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明治期の治山事業について」) (https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/j/kouhou/archives/tisan/tisan.html)

What it is: An archive page of Japan's Forestry Agency on the hillside-restoration works begun in the Meiji period, with the degraded mountains they were built to repair.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the national forestry authority's statement that felling rose with demand until the forests were considerably degraded by the Edo period, and it names Tanakami hill in southern Shiga, known in the Edo period as 「the bald of Tanakami」. It is a short public history written to introduce later restoration works, not a study, and its examples are the worst cases (Tanakami near Kyoto; Seto in Aichi, one of what the page calls the three great bald-mountain prefectures) rather than a measure of a typical village's hill. The page itself says the degradation grew worse in the Meiji period with rapid population growth and industry, and its cases and photographs show that later Meiji and Taisho state; only Tanakami's baldness is dated to the Edo period, so the pictures overdraw how bare an Edo-period hill was.

Used for: that Japan's hills were widely degraded by felling by the Edo period, with the worst stripped bare (vegetation)
