What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on farmers' kabuki stages, with the national count of 1,777 and their usual siting on shrine ground.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows the stage real and shrine-sited but far from universal. Its limits: tertiary (its own sources, Tsunoda 1971 and Gotō 1996, are closer); the 1,777 is a 1970 survey of surviving and lost stages that also counts noh and puppet stages, not a premodern prevalence; permanent stages date only from the early 1800s, farmers' theater was officially forbidden and suppressed in the Tenpō reforms, and the stages cluster in central Japan (Tokushima, Hyōgo, Nagano), with thirteen prefectures having none.

Used for: the farmers' stage as a knob (religion-and-death.html).
