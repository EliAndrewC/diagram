zh.wikipedia 围龙屋 (weilongwu, the Hakka hall-and-wing house), the sections on the hall, wings and rear ring and on the drying floor, fengshui pond, well and grove (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9B%B4%E9%BE%99%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the weilongwu, the Hakka walled house built around an ancestral hall, with a section on the open ground and pond in front of it, the well to one side and the grove behind it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the open ground before a hall as a named and proportioned feature whose first stated use is drying grain and other crops, with the gatherings of the new year, the festivals, weddings and funerals second, and its width carefully matched to the height of the main hall; and it gives the half-moon pond before it holding water against drought, raising fish, watering the fields, tempering the air and standing ready against fire. It also states that the arrangement is the rule for the hall-fronted forms rather than one instance. Its limits are that its dating of the type's rise and the drying floor's paragraph, the width matched to the hall's height among it, carry no reference, and its age of the houses only a 2009 page on a Chinese news portal (the pond's paragraph cites Zeng Zhaoxuan's 1994 study of Hakka house forms); that its own dating pulls two ways - the type matured in the late Ming and Qing but was at its height under the Republic (1912-1949), so some of the houses it generalizes from may postdate China's cut-off of about 1895, yet it calls the houses generally two to three hundred years old and some five to six hundred - so its arrangements are taken as the late Ming and Qing form, not dated by any house; that it describes one regional house type with its ancestral hall on the axis, not a free-standing village shrine; that the width matched to the hall's height is the TYPE's prescriptive norm rather than a measured range, so it is a proportion to draw to and not a figure to check against; that it gives no market and no opera on that ground; and that a weilongwu is a lineage compound housing a dozen to over a hundred households on, ordinarily, 8-10 mu (about half a hectare to two-thirds of one; the large ones over 30), which the article itself classes with the mansions often built by rich merchants and sets apart from the simpler three-hall and similar houses of ordinary families, so its drying floor and pond are a compound's, to be scaled down for a single farm's yard rather than copied, and its placing of the hen coops in the shared rear ring, with the kitchens, stores, privies and pigsties, says where a large Hakka compound kept its hens and not where a single farm put its coop, and that sentence carries no reference of its own.

Used for: grain drying and festival use on the ground before a hall; the fengshui pond's stated purposes; the drying floor's width matched to the hall's height; the pond's name, its widening with the house, the circle it closes with the rear wing, the grove behind and the dragon hall where the gods are worshipped; the hen coops kept in the rear ring with the kitchens, stores, privies and pigsties (Homesteads)
