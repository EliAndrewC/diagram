zh.wikipedia 围龙屋 (weilongwu, the Hakka hall-and-wing house), the section on the drying floor, fengshui pond, well and grove (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9B%B4%E9%BE%99%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the weilongwu, the Hakka walled house built around an ancestral hall, with a section on the open ground, pond, well and grove that stand in front of it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the open ground before a hall as a named and proportioned feature whose first stated use is drying grain and other crops, with the gatherings of the new year, the festivals, weddings and funerals second, and its width carefully matched to the height of the main hall; and it gives the half-moon pond before it holding water against drought, raising fish, watering the fields, tempering the air and standing ready against fire. It also states that the arrangement is the rule for the hall-fronted forms rather than one instance. Its limits are that it is a tertiary article whose dating and normative statements carry no individual references; that it describes one regional house type with its ancestral hall on the axis, not a free-standing village shrine; that the width matched to the hall's height is the TYPE's prescriptive norm rather than a measured range, so it is a proportion to draw to and not a figure to check against; and that it gives no market and no opera on that ground.

Used for: grain drying and festival use on the ground before a hall; the fengshui pond's stated purposes; the drying floor's width matched to the hall's height
