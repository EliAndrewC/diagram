What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Danzaemon, the hereditary head of the eta of eastern Japan under the shogunate, and on his Asakusa Shinmachi compound: its enclosure, its buildings, its court and jail, and the officials housed there.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes an outcaste head's precinct standing inside the city as an enclosed block with residences, storehouses, a shrine, a court and a jail, which is the in-city outcaste quarter this page argues for. A tertiary summary used for the compound's contents; it does not say where the residents worked. And the compound was the headquarters of the man who administered every eta community in eastern Japan, inside the largest city in the country, with a court and a jail because it held delegated jurisdiction; an ordinary provincial seat's outcaste block is a far smaller and plainer thing.

Used for: the Danzaemon compound inside Edo and what stood in it
