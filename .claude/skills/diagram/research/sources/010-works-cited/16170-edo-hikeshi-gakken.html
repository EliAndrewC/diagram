edo-hikeshi-gakken

Gakken, お江戸の科学: 江戸の三火消と消防技術 ("The science of Edo: Edo's three fire brigades and firefighting technique", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.gakken.jp/kagakusouken/spread/oedo/06/kaisetsu1.html)

What it is: An educational publisher's explanatory page on Edo's three kinds of fire brigade, their men, their seven tools and the bell signals, written to accompany a series on the science of Edo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says how a fire was fought - by pulling down the houses downwind - and that the townsmen firemen, called out, gathered at the ward's watch house (jishinban) before going to the fire. The page does not say the gear was kept there; drawing it at the watch house is this project's inference from that gathering. It is a popular explainer without author, date or citations, about Edo only - a capital of about a million whose town brigades numbered some ten thousand men paid for by the wards, beside daimyo and shogunal brigades, so its organization bounds a smaller town's rather than describing it - and its reading of how the hand pump was used is hedged ("it seems").

Used for: demolition as the method, and the hand pump's use (urban-features)
