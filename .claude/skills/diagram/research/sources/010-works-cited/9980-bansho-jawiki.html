bansho-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 番所 (in Japanese; https://ja.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%95%AA%E6%89%80)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the bansho, the guard post: the checkpoints and harbor posts of the Edo period, the guardrooms at Edo castle's gates, the ward and bridge watches, and the few surviving buildings (one original, the Nuruyu border post of Sendai domain); a short second section gives the Ryukyu meaning, a district office. It is a stub, last edited 2023, flagged since 2017 for thin footnoting.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the guardroom at a gate as an institution of Edo castle and points to a surviving pair beside the Akamon, which supports a gatehouse set beside a gate. It is a tertiary stub flagged for thin footnoting, and its Akamon sentence rests on one footnote to the University of Tokyo's own page. It gives no sizes; the national designation record (文化遺産オンライン, 旧加賀屋敷御守殿門) is the closer source and gives each guardroom as 3 by 2 ken, single-story, with cusped gables front and back. Its gate examples are the shogun's castle and the Kaga Maeda mansion gate of 1827, built for a shogun's daughter's marriage, far above a county post; its one rural example, the Nuruyu domain border post, is nearer that scale.

Used for: the guardrooms at Edo castle's gates and either side of the Akamon; the Nuruyu border post as a rare survivor (buildings)
