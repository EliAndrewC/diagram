What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 東海道 (the Tokaido), on the highway between Edo and Kyoto - its post stations, its administration under the shogunate, and the orders that shaped its surface, its width and its markers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the 1604 order that made the highway five ken wide, about nine meters, which is the upper bound our trunk roads are measured against. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, and the paragraph we use carries no citation on the page; that the Tokaido was the busiest highway in the country, so its width is a ceiling; and that a decree records what was ordered rather than what was built - the same paragraph reports that the post stations' mandated porters and horses never reached the ordered numbers.

Used for: 「In the second month of Keicho 9, Okubo Nagayasu and others were ordered to make the width of the highway 5 ken,」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「慶長9年2月に大久保長安その他に命じて街道の幅員を5間とし」) - 5 ken (~9 m, ~29.5 ft) in 1604
