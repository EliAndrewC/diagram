usu-windbreak

Utah State University Extension, Windbreak Benefits and Design (https://extension.usu.edu/forestry/publications/utah-forest-facts/005-windbreak-benefits-and-design)

What it is: "Windbreak Benefits and Design," a Utah Forest Facts extension bulletin published by Utah State University Extension, which explains what a planted windbreak does and how far its shelter reaches, expressed as multiples of the windbreak's own height.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use its rule of thumb - shelter extending downwind up to thirty times the height of the trees, with full protection of a large area needing a windbreak every ten to twenty heights - to decide how far apart sheltering tree belts stand and how much ground one belt can be said to protect. The limits are place and era: this is a modern American agricultural extension document about Utah, written for tractors and center-pivot country, so what we take from it is the physics of air flowing over a barrier, which does not change with century or continent, and never its planting recommendations or species.

Used for: "Windbreaks reduce wind speeds up to 30 times their height (H) downwind"
