usu-windbreak

Utah State University Extension, Windbreak Benefits and Design (https://extension.usu.edu/forestry/publications/utah-forest-facts/005-windbreak-benefits-and-design)

What it is: "Windbreak Benefits and Design," a Utah Forest Facts extension bulletin published by Utah State University Extension, which explains what a planted windbreak does and how far its shelter reaches, expressed as multiples of the windbreak's own height.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use its rule of thumb - shelter extending downwind up to thirty times the height of the trees, with full protection of a large area needing a windbreak every ten to twenty heights - to decide how far apart sheltering tree belts stand and how much ground one belt can be said to protect. What we take from it is the physics of air flowing over a barrier, never its planting recommendations or species. Its reaches assume a planted belt of a chosen density: 60 to 80 percent shelters a small area such as a farmstead, and 40 to 60 percent shelters a field. A village's grove or hedge is of unknown density, so thirty heights is a bound on its shelter, not a measure of it.

Used for: "Windbreaks reduce wind speeds up to 30 times their height (H) downwind"
