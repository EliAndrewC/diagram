wuhurec-wanchun

Mu Changgen, 万春圩与圩田五说--附沈括《万春圩图记》 ("The Wanchun polder and the five arguments on polders, with Shen Kuo's Record of the Map of the Wanchun Polder", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 话说鸠兹 (a Wuhu local-history site), 2021 (in Chinese; https://wuhurec.com/archives/153/)

What it is: A local historian's article on the Wanchun polder at Wuhu, rebuilt in 1061 by fourteen thousand laborers, with the full text of Shen Kuo's eleventh-century record of it appended: the dike's breadth, height and length, the road inside, the rows of mulberry and willow, the reeds at the foot and the five water gates.

Why it applies, and its limits: Shen Kuo's record is a Northern Song account of a polder's dike, the earliest with figures, written at the time by the brother of Shen Pi, the official who rebuilt it; Shen Kuo was then a guest at Ningguo and says he wrote from the polder's map and his brother's arguments, and whether he took part in the works is disputed (the article gives both sides), so its figures are the builders' own, not a measurement he is known to have seen. Wanchun was a great official polder, enclosing over a thousand qing, so its dike is the upper end of the range, not a village polder's; the appended text is a web transcription not checked here against a printed edition, and the article's own glosses (its twenty-two kilometers for twenty-two li, the gates draining when the outer river fell) are a modern reading.

Used for: a Song polder dike's breadth, height and planting, and its water gates (archetypes)
