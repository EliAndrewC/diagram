qimin-yaoshu-zhongzhu

賈思勰 齊民要術 卷第五 ("Essential Techniques for the Common People, Scroll Five", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (Jia Sixie, Qimin Yaoshu, c. 540), 種竹第五十一 (the chapter on planting bamboo) and 種榆白楊第四十六 (the chapter on planting elm and white poplar), Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94)

What it is: Book 5 of the sixth-century Chinese agricultural treatise, on trees and useful plants, in a Wikisource transcription; its bamboo chapter says what ground bamboo wants, which shoots to lift, and where in the garden to set them, with the author's own note giving the reason; its elm and white-poplar chapter says that no grain grows in an elm's shade, that the shade reaches east, west and north as far as the tree, and that elms are sown on the north edge of garden ground (園地北畔).

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text from long before the setting's period that places bamboo on one side of a planted plot - the northeast corner of a garden, because bamboo spreads toward the southwest and will fill the garden in a few years. It describes North China in the sixth century and a garden or orchard (園), not a homestead or its windbreak, and says nothing of Japan, of a kitchen drain or of service sheds. The elm chapter is the same: its shade rule is the author's rule of thumb, not a measurement, and its grain is the dry-farmed grain of the north, not paddy rice; its north edge is the edge of garden ground where elms are grown as a crop for wood and pods, not a village's windbreak, and the same chapter warns against elms along a field's edge (they bring sparrows that eat the grain, and grow crooked) and advises giving them a plot of their own.

Used for: bamboo set in a garden's northeast corner so that it spreads southwest (homesteads); from its elm chapter, that no grain grows in a tree's shade, that the shade reaches east, west and north as far as the tree, and that elms are planted on the garden's north edge (vegetation)
