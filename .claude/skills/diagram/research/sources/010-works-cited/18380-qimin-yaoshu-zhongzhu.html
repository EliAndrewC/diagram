qimin-yaoshu-zhongzhu

賈思勰 齊民要術 卷第五 ("Essential Techniques for the Common People, Scroll Five", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (Jia Sixie, Qimin Yaoshu, c. 540), 種竹第五十一 (the chapter on planting bamboo), Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94)

What it is: Book 5 of the sixth-century Chinese agricultural treatise, on trees and useful plants, in a Wikisource transcription; its bamboo chapter says what ground bamboo wants, which shoots to lift, and where in the garden to set them, with the author's own note giving the reason.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text from long before the setting's period that places bamboo on one side of a planted plot - the northeast corner of a garden, because bamboo spreads toward the southwest and will fill the garden in a few years. It describes North China in the sixth century and a garden or orchard (園), not a homestead or its windbreak, and says nothing of Japan, of a kitchen drain or of service sheds.

Used for: bamboo set in a garden's northeast corner so that it spreads southwest (homesteads)
