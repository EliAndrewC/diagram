Ukai Hidenori, 『お寺の日本地図 名刹古刹でめぐる47都道府県』 ("A map of Japan's temples: the 47 prefectures by their famous old temples", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Bunshun Shinsho, 2021 - the introduction, as a preview excerpt on Bungeishunju's site (https://books.bunshun.jp/articles/-/6195)

What it is: The introduction to a general-audience book on Japan's temples by a journalist who is himself the head priest of a Jodo temple in Kyoto, published by Bungeishunju as a web preview.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives today's 76,970 Buddhist temples and says that, views differing, the Edo period is thought to have had some 90,000, about half of which the Meiji anti-Buddhist movement is said to have swept away. It is a popular book citing a 1926 study for the figure, and it hedges the figure itself, so it stands as an order of magnitude, not a count.

Used for: the number of temples in Edo-period Japan (religion and death)
