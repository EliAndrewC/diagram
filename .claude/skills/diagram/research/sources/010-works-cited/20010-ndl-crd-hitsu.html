National Diet Library, Collaborative Reference Database, 「How large a unit is a hitsu?」 (translated; original: 「筆（ヒツ）という単位はどのぐらいの広さ・大きさなのか？」), a reference record of the Nishinomiya Municipal Central Library, 2018 (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000255851&page=ref_view)

What it is: A public library's answer to a reader's question about the land unit counted in hitsu, quoting a dictionary of historical units, the Kokushi daijiten (the standard Japanese historical encyclopedia) and a legal dictionary.

Why it applies, and its limits: Through a dictionary of historical units (Koizumi, 1989) and the Kokushi daijiten, both quoted, it says what one entry of a land-survey register (kenchi-chō) was: a lot of paddy, dry field or house land written one place at a time, whatever its size - so a register's paddy entries are parcels, and their areas are not the size of a single diked paddy. It is a library's answer, not a history: both dictionaries are quoted second-hand, the encyclopedia with an omission, and the library's reference list names the Nihon kokugo daijiten rather than the Kokushi daijiten its text quotes, so which encyclopedia is quoted is uncertain. Neither dictionary gives a period for the practice (calling the registers Edo is ours), and neither they nor the library says whether one parcel held one paddy or several.

Used for: that a survey register's entry was one lot of land written one place at a time, regardless of its size (towns)
