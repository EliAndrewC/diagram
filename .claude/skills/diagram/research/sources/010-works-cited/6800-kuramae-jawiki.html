What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on 蔵前 (Kuramae), the Edo district named for the Asakusa Okura, the shogunate's great rice storehouse ground on the Sumida River, in what is now Taito ward in Tokyo. It describes the eight numbered channels cut into the right bank of the river and the rows of rice storehouses that faced them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the form of an official granary quarter - eight numbered boat channels cut into the river bank with rows of rice storehouses facing them - which is what our granary quays are drawn from. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary with sparse citations, and the channel sentence itself carries none; and that this was the shogunal capital's central granary, some 36,000 tsubo with 67 storehouses, an exceptional scale from which only the form transfers to a provincial seat.

Used for: 「At the Asakusa Okura, eight channels, counted from upstream as number one, number two, and so on, were cut on the right bank of the Sumida River,」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「浅草御蔵は、隅田川の右岸に上流から一番、二番と数える8本の堀を作り」) - eight numbered channels into the storehouse ground
