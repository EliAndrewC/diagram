umaya-kotobank

Kotobank, s.v. 厩 (umaya, the stable; the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha Sekai dai-hyakka jiten, among others; in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8E%A9-35131)

What it is: The Kotobank dictionary page gathering Japanese reference works' entries on the stable, including an encyclopedia article on the farmhouse stable: inside the house or standing apart, how it was closed and walled, what was above it and how big it was by region.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only readable source that sizes a Japanese stable. Its limits are that it describes the farmhouse stable, not an inn's or a relay's; that its size is given for one region and only as "about"; and that it gives no date, describing the folk house as surveyed in modern times.

Used for: the inner and outer stable, the barred entrance and plank walls, the room above, and a stall about 3 m square in the Kinki region (towns)
