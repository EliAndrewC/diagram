umaya-kotobank

Kotobank, s.v. 厩 (umaya, the stable; the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha Sekai dai-hyakka jiten, among others; in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8E%A9-35131)

What it is: The Kotobank dictionary page gathering Japanese reference works' entries on the stable, including two signed encyclopedia articles (Miyazawa Satoshi in Heibonsha's Sekai dai-hyakka jiten; Takeuchi Yoshitarō in Shōgakukan's Nihon dai-hyakka zensho) on the farmhouse stable: inside the house or standing apart, how it was closed and walled, what was above it and how big it was by region. The first also describes the warrior-house stable: gable-entered and three bays long, as drawn in the Rakuchū rakugai screens and the carpenters' manual Shōmei.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only readable source that sizes a Japanese stable. Its limits are that these are tertiary articles with no references, though each is by a student of the folk house; that the stable it sizes is the farmhouse's, not an inn's or a relay's, and its warrior-house stable is given only by its form and length; that its 3 m square is the stall for one horse (the usual size by one article, the narrow Kinki size by the other), with the stables of the long-wintered northeast and of horse-breeding districts much larger; and that it describes the folk house as surveyed in modern times, dating only the northeastern house-stables to the Edo domains and noting that Meiji encouragement of horse-breeding for the army came after them.

Used for: the inner and outer stable, the barred entrance and plank walls, the room above, and a stall about 3 m square in the Kinki region (towns)
