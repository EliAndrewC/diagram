ja.wikipedia 木炭 ("charcoal", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/木炭)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on charcoal, with a history of how it was made in Japan period by period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the soft black charcoal, cooled inside a closed kiln, to the late Muromachi through Edo periods. It does not say who worked such kilns or at what scale; that a farming village worked one is this project's inference, not the article's. It is an encyclopedia summary, and Wikipedia has flagged it since December 2015 as lacking sources. The sentence that dates black charcoal has no footnote of its own. The paragraph's one footnote, to Tamura Norimi's entry 「木炭」 in the 『歴史学事典』 vol. 14 (Kobundo), sits on the next sentence, which says the firm distinction between black and white charcoal is said to be modern. The date is a span of periods: good for which kind of kiln, not for a year or a region.

Used for: black charcoal from a closed kiln, late Muromachi into the Edo period, and white charcoal smothered outside it (vegetation)
