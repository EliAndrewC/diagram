machidoshiyori-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 町年寄 ("machi-doshiyori, the town elders", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/町年寄)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the town elders, the townspeople who headed the commoners' administration of an early-modern city under its town magistrate, chiefly Edo's three hereditary houses, with shorter sections on Kyoto, Osaka, Kofu and Nagasaki. It is an anonymous encyclopedia summary; its Edo sections are drawn largely from Yoshihara Ken'ichirō's Edo no machi-yakunin (Yoshikawa Kōbunkan).

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where Edo's elders worked: their lots, granted by the shogunate, were the corner lots of Honcho 1 to 3 on Honcho street, and, as with samurai, the residence doubled as the office. The passage carries no footnote of its own, and it is Edo's case: three elders over the whole of the largest city under its magistrate, a different scale of place from a town's headman. The same article cuts against carrying it further: in Osaka each ward's elder did the ward's business through a ward meeting-house (machi-kaisho) that kept its registers, and in Nagasaki the magistrate Matsura Nobumasa's reform in the mid-1700s forbade the elders to do business at home and moved it to the Nagasaki kaisho. It carries that a city's elders could work from their own houses, not that a town's did.

Used for: that a town's elders worked from their own houses on the main street (towns)
