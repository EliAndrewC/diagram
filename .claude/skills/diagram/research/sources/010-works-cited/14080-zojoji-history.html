zojoji-history

Zojoji (増上寺), 歴史 ("History", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the temple's own site (in Japanese; https://www.zojoji.or.jp/info/history.html)

What it is: The history page of Zojoji, the Jodo head temple in Edo that served as a family temple of the Tokugawa shoguns, giving its size at its Edo-period height and its precinct and halls today.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the precinct of one of the greatest city temples of Edo, with its count of sub-temples and dormitories, which is the upper end of what a city temple could be. It is the temple describing itself: the Edo figures are given without a source or a year, only as its height in the Edo period, and the monk count is given as what is said ("it is said"). Its hundred-odd dormitories (gakuryo) were those of a seminary, the first of the eighteen Jodo seminaries of the Kanto, said to hold three thousand trainee monks, so they measure a teaching temple and not a city temple as such. And a shogun's family temple in the capital is far above anything a provincial city carried.

Used for: a great Edo temple's precinct of 250,000 tsubo with 48 sub-temples and a hundred-odd dormitories, and its 16,000 tsubo today (religion-and-death)
