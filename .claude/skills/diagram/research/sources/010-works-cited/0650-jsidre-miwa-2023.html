三輪弌 (Hajime Miwa), "Taking water from rivers on the principles of how rivers flow" (川の流れの原理に基づく河川取水), JSIDRE special lecture slides, 2023 (https://www.jsidre.or.jp/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ouyouR5_tokubetsukoen.pdf)

What it is: The slides of a special lecture by Hajime Miwa to the Japanese Society of Irrigation, Drainage and Rural Engineering on taking water from rivers according to how rivers flow: the intake forms (natural intake on the bank the current follows, a fixed weir, a combined weir, a fully movable weir), why a weir is needed when the river falls, and the old oblique weir that ran diagonally upstream from the intake mouth to dam a shallow riffle.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the rule for where a canal leaves a stream - on the bank along which the current's deep line runs - and describes the historical oblique weir starting at the intake mouth, which is the form our weir glyph takes. Its limits are that it is a lecture rather than a paper, that its subject is rivers rather than brooks (the Tama, the Kizu, the Tone, the Chikugo) and its interest modern intake failure, and that its one worked old case is a river weir, the Yamada weir on the Chikugo.

Used for: natural intake on the bank the current follows; the weir as what keeps the intake level when the river falls; the old oblique weir extended diagonally upstream from the intake mouth to dam the riffle
