sendai-igune-modelplan

Sendai City, 「The thinking behind the model plan for regenerated igune」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「再生居久根モデルプランの考え方」) (https://www.city.sendai.jp/ryokuchihozen/kurashi/shizen/midori/hyakunen/documents/modelplan_kangaekata.pdf)

What it is: A one-sheet planting plan from Sendai City's green-space office for replanting the homestead groves (igune) of the Sendai plain after the 2011 tsunami, setting out the grove's job against the winter north-west wind and the kinds of tree it is built from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states, for the Sendai plain, what a working igune is made of: tall evergreens such as sugi and black pine as the main body, bamboo filling the branchless lower part of the trees against the wind, and a few fruit trees. It is a modern design brief, not a survey or a historical record. These points come only from its four-line sketch of the grove as it stood before 2011 (「the conventional igune」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「従来の居久根」)), which it draws as the foil for its own plan; that plan replaces the sugi with shorter salt-tolerant evergreens and the bamboo with evergreen shrubs, and the sketch does not say how old the older make-up is. It speaks for the Sendai plain of north-east Japan only, standing in for homestead groves elsewhere, and it gives no proportions, so it can say where bamboo sat in the grove and not how much of it there was.

Used for: bamboo filling the low, branchless part of a windbreak grove; the igune's evergreen-conifer main body (vegetation)
