chori-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 長吏 (賎民) ("chori, the outcaste status", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%95%B7%E5%90%8F_(%E8%B3%8E%E6%B0%91))

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on chori, a name for outcaste status that in the medieval period meant the heads of the kawata and hinin, and in the Edo period meant the kawata in some regions (the Kanto, the Shonai domain, Shinano, Kurashiki, Nagato and Suo) and the hinin heads in others (Osaka, Sakai, Nara, Iga), with a list of what the word meant region by region, including the castle towns of Shinano and of the Shonai domain.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page that places named castle towns' quarters on the ground - Matsumoto's at the castle town's entrances in 1588, Ueda's likewise in 1649 - and gives their local name, machi-hanare. It is an encyclopedia article, and it cites no source at all for these lines; its one footnote supports a different point. What it says of Shinano is one province's policy, and the article itself says the word's meaning differed region by region: Osaka's chori headed large hinin settlements, not small quarters at a town's gates. Its only household figure, also uncited, is that when they were founded the Shinano quarters were overwhelmingly settlements of one to a few households.

Used for: the quarters of the Shinano castle towns at their entrances, their duties, and the name machi-hanare (urban-features, cities/fabric)
