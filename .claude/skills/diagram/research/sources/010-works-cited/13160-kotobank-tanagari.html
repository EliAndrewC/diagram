kotobank-tanagari

Kotobank, 店借 ("house tenant", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - dictionary entries with excerpts from the older Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BA%97%E5%80%9F-1183490)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the tanagari, the townsman who rented his house in an early-modern Japanese city, with a signed entry on tenancy by the historian Yoshida Nobuyuki from the revised Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, short entries from two dictionaries of Japanese history, and excerpts from the older edition: its entry on the house-rental guarantee (tanauke) gives the estimate that about 70% of the residents of Edo and Osaka were tenants, and its entry on the town ward (machi) gives the tenant share of early-modern city residents by region, 20-30% in Ōu and Hokuriku and 50-60% around the Inland Sea.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says how many of a city's residents rented, and that the share differed widely by region, which is what sets a town's back tenements apart from a great city's. The figures are estimates quoted in short excerpts, not a survey the page shows; the regional shares are of all urban residents, not of laborers, and it does not say which towns were counted or when, nor whether the Edo and Osaka estimate counts the samurai quarters or only the townsmen's wards. It is an encyclopedia, a tertiary summary, and it describes Japan only: it carries the tenant share for the setting's Japanese-modeled towns and says nothing about a Chinese-modeled town.

Used for: the share of tenants in Edo and Osaka and by region (towns)
