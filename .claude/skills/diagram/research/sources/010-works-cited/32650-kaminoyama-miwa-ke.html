kaminoyama-miwa-ke

Kaminoyama rekishi bunkazai sanpo (Walks among Kaminoyama's history and cultural properties) no. 37, "Buke yashiki" (Samurai houses), Kaminoyama City Board of Education, June 2021 (in Japanese; https://www.city.kaminoyama.yamagata.jp/uploaded/life/37937_108086_misc.pdf)

What it is: A two-page leaflet for children from the city's board of education, written as a dialogue of children visiting the Morimoto and Miwa houses, two of the four samurai houses that survive on the town's Nakacho street. Its second page is a labeled plan of the Miwa house, a thatched house of about 1804 whose family served the domain's lord as a chamberlain, with notes on each room taken from the guide at the house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places what few pages place at a samurai house: the servants' hut outside the back door, the privy on the house's own plan and the well indoors in the doma. It is one house, of a retainer who served the lord as sobayonin and taught at the domain school, on a plot of 400 tsubo in a small castle town in snowy Yamagata, and it is an L-shaped thatched house with a projecting entrance wing, a snow-country form whose indoor well a house farther south may not share. The servants' hut is told, not drawn: the leaflet says only that a hut stood outside the back door, in the past tense, and gives neither its size nor its distance. The house was remodeled (kaichiku) in the Showa era, so the plan is the house as it stands after that work; the leaflet names some changes (the alcove once on the east side, a corridor that once ran on to the kitchen, no ceiling once in the family rooms) but not whether the privy or the well moved.

Used for: where a samurai house kept its servants' hut, its privy and its well (Estates and other compounds)
