kaminoyama-miwa-ke

Kaminoyama rekishi bunkazai sanpo (Walks among Kaminoyama's history and cultural properties) no. 37, "Buke yashiki" (Samurai houses), Kaminoyama City Board of Education, June 2021 (in Japanese; https://www.city.kaminoyama.yamagata.jp/uploaded/life/37937_108086_misc.pdf)

What it is: A two-page leaflet for children from the city's board of education, walking through Kaminoyama's four surviving samurai houses. Its second page is a labeled plan of the Miwa house, a thatched house of about 1804 whose family served the domain's lord as a chamberlain, with notes on each room taken from the guide at the house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places what few pages place at a samurai house: the servants' hut outside the back door, the privy on the house's own plan and the well indoors in the doma. It is one middle-ranking house in a small castle town in Yamagata, and the house was remodeled in the Showa era, so the plan it labels is the house as it stands, not as built; the leaflet does not say which rooms the remodeling moved.

Used for: where a samurai house kept its servants' hut, its privy and its well (Estates and other compounds)
