suido-ishizue-yachida

ARIC (Association of Rural Information and Communication), 水土の礎 ("Foundations of Water and Land", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 山ふところに拓かれた谷地田 ("Valley paddies opened in the folds of the hills", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/01/08.html)

What it is: A page of the same land-improvement history on the yachida, the paddy opened in a small valley: how old the form is, the view that it was the standard medieval farmland, and the scattered houses that went with it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It establishes the narrow-valley paddy as a common, old form all over Japan and describes the settlement pattern beside it, which bears on both the valley-chain field and the settlement forms. Its strongest claims are hedged by the page itself - "there is also a view" for the standard farmland, "it is thought" for the scattered houses - and rest on late-Heian estates in Satsuma and Bingo, centuries before the setting's period; it is a general-audience history without footnotes.

Used for: the valley paddy found everywhere and old, and the scattered small settlements of medieval valleys (archetypes)
