What it is: The English Wikipedia article on cremation: its history, the modern process, what remains afterward and why families choose it; an encyclopedia article.

Why it applies, and its limits: The record uses its weight figure as a bound on how little a cremation leaves, not as a measurement of a premodern burial. Its figures come from modern crematories, whose retorts burn more completely than an open pyre, and are of remains ground by machine, though it notes that in most Asian countries the bones are kept unground; the weight follows skeletal mass and "varies greatly from person to person", so the 2.4 kg mean, taken from modern bodies, runs high for smaller premodern East Asian adults, and a pyre's remains would also hold partly burned bone and fuel ash. It gives weight and never volume, and its line on cemetery space is a picture caption with no reference of its own.

Used for: how little a cremation leaves to bury - about 2.4 kg for an adult, roughly 3.5% of body mass - and its economy of cemetery space (religion and death)
