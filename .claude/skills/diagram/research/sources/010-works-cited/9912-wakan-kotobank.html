wakan-kotobank

Kotobank, 倭館 (wakan, the Japan House in Korea) - the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry by Yazawa Kōsuke, with Nipponica, Mypedia and other entries (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%80%AD%E9%A4%A8-153933)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the Japan House (wakan), the guest compound the Joseon court set aside for Japanese envoys and traders - in the fifteenth century at the three southern ports and at the capital Hanseong, from 1547 at Busan alone, and at Choryang from 1678 to 1873, where officers sent from Tsushima domain lived and conducted diplomacy and trade - collecting seven short Japanese dictionary and encyclopedia entries on its history, which name its halls but give no plan or measurement.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a readable account of how two neighboring powers actually did business across a frontier: in a compound one side set aside for the other, with the host's banquet hall and lodgings adjoining it. It is a tertiary summary, a set of short encyclopedia entries: good for which halls there were and that the Korean banquet hall adjoined the compound, not for any size or arrangement, since it gives no plan, dimensions or count of residents. The institution is a Korean (Joseon) and Japanese one, standing in for the setting's own frontier practice; the frontier is a sea crossing, not a land border between clans; and the Japan House was a residential quarter of many buildings (three offices in the East House, three lodgings in the West), not a single hall, so it speaks to what a frontier meeting place held rather than to the size of one room. It says nothing of a meeting on a line.

Used for: the Edo-period Japan House at Choryang, its trade hall and the adjoining Korean banquet hall (buildings)
