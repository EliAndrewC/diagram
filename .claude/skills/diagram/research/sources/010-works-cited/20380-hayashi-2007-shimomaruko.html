hayashi-2007-shimomaruko

林和光 (Hayashi Kazumitsu), 近世・多摩川における河川氾濫と下流域農村に関する歴史人口学的分析 - 平川家文書からみた荏原郡・六郷領・下丸子村 ("A historical-demographic analysis of river floods and a lower-basin farming village on the early modern Tama River: Shimomaruko village as seen in the Hirakawa family papers", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a 2007 research report published by the Tokyu Foundation (in Japanese; https://foundation.tokyu.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/G172.pdf)

What it is: A study of one village on the lower Tama River from its headman's papers: the land registers of successive surveys rebuilt into the areas of paddy and dry field, the floods that moved the river, and the households.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets a floodplain village long described as a typical paddy village against its own registers, which show its dry field about equal to its paddy by the Genroku survey (1688-1704) and larger than it after the Kyoho survey (1716-1736). Its limits: one village near Edo, whose dry field may have grown for the city's market; it gives the trend in words and charts, not a share this record could quote as a number; and it says nothing of where the dry fields lay or of a row.

Used for: the share of dry field in a floodplain paddy village (homesteads)
