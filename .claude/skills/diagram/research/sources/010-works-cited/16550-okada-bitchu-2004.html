okada-bitchu-2004

Okada Aoi, "Bakumatsu ishin-ki ni okeru setai kozo no tokucho - Bitchu no kuni no shumon aratame cho wo chushin ni" ("Characteristic of Household Structure in Nineteenth Century Japan", the paper's own English title), Teikyo Shakaigaku no. 17, March 2004 (in Japanese; https://appsv.main.teikyo-u.ac.jp/tosho/aokada17.pdf)

What it is: A historical-demography paper that reads the 1870 religious-investigation registers of twenty-two villages of Bitchu province (today's Okayama prefecture) for household size and make-up, and compares them with registers of the same years from Echizen and from two villages near Nihonmatsu in the northeast.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured household sizes, the commonest size and the shares of stem and joint households from real village registers, and Bitchu lies on the Inland Sea, the region much of this record draws on. Its registers are of the 1860s and 1870, the very end of the Edo period and the first years after it, when households were smaller than a century or two earlier; its "Nihonmatsu" is two villages only, and some of its comparison figures are counted in person-years rather than households. It counts people per household, not buildings per plot, so it cannot say how many roofs a household had.

Used for: the average and commonest household size in three regions' village registers, and the shares of stem and joint households (settlements)
