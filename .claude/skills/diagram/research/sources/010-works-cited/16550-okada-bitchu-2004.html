okada-bitchu-2004

Okada Aoi, "Bakumatsu ishin-ki ni okeru setai kozo no tokucho - Bitchu no kuni no shumon aratame cho wo chushin ni" ("Characteristic of Household Structure in Nineteenth Century Japan", the paper's own English title), Teikyo Shakaigaku no. 17, March 2004 (in Japanese; https://appsv.main.teikyo-u.ac.jp/tosho/aokada17.pdf)

What it is: A historical-demography paper that reads the 1870 religious-investigation registers of twenty-three villages of Bitchu province (today's Okayama prefecture) - twenty-two of them for household size and make-up, the twenty-third (Koijo) set aside as an outlier for its many co-resident collateral kin - and compares them with single-year registers of 1860-1875 from fifty-six villages of Echizen and the 1862 registers of two villages near Nihonmatsu in the northeast.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives household sizes and the shares of stem and joint households from real village registers, and Bitchu lies on the Inland Sea, the region much of this record draws on. But its headline figures are counted over inhabitants, not households: its chart of household size is drawn in persons (Figure 3, "unit: person"), so its commonest size (five) is the size of household the commonest inhabitant lived in, and its stem and joint shares (18.23% and 26.60% in Bitchu) are shares of person-years (Tables 3 and 4). The paper does not say how its averages (6.37 in Bitchu, 5.78 in Echizen, 6.63 in Nihonmatsu) are counted, but its Table 6 counts the same 6,737 Bitchu inhabitants (servants excluded) under 1,431 household heads, 4.71 to a household (4.79 in Echizen), so the averages too are best read as counted over inhabitants. Counted by household, Table 5 gives Bitchu 13.87% stem and 13.18% joint households; the paper states no average or commonest size per household in its text. Its registers are of the 1860s and 1870, the very end of the Edo period and the first years after it, when households were smaller than a century or two earlier; its "Nihonmatsu" is two villages only, and its Bitchu household figures leave out Koijo, the one village whose households were most complex. It counts people per household, not buildings per plot, so it cannot say how many roofs a household had.

Used for: the average and commonest household size in three regions' village registers, and the shares of stem and joint households (settlements)
