semboku-bukeyashiki

Semboku City, 武家屋敷 石黒家 ("the Ishiguro samurai residence", with the district's other open houses, title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.city.semboku.akita.jp/sightseeing/travel/details/01-bukeyashiki.html)

What it is: Semboku City's school field-trip page on the six samurai houses of Kakunodate open to visitors, one short paragraph each: their gates, fences, roofs, gardens and the families that held them, with one well (the Iwahashi house's) mentioned.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the surviving houses' fences and gates are, from a foot soldier's brushwood hedge to black board fences with peep windows, and that one of them, the Iwahashi house, a mid-rank samurai's, has its own well-sweep (tsurube) well; it says nothing of the other houses' water, which is no evidence that they lacked wells. It is a municipal visitor page without references, for one small castle town in northern Japan (Akita) whose samurai served a branch of the Satake house, describing houses as preserved and restored today; what it dates is late Edo (the oldest gate is dated 1809).

Used for: samurai houses' own wells and their fences (cities/government)
