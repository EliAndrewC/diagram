ishizue-ariake

水土の礎 (the history site of land and water run by the Agricultural and Rural Integrated Information Center, ARIC), 干潟から沃野へ、有明海干拓 ("From tidal flat to fertile plain: the Ariake reclamations", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/03/07.html)

What it is: A short, unsigned page in ARIC's popular history of land development, on the reclamation of the Ariake Sea's tidal flats in Kyushu, stage by stage, behind successive sea dikes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the problem a sea polder's drainage met - the tide's range, the flats growing until the older land lost its outlet - and where its settlements stood, on the previous stage's sea dike. It is a popular summary without sources for its figures. It dates the serious reclamation to the Edo-period domains, but carries the same work on through Meiji landlords and the state projects of 1933-1978 and 1986. It reads the settlements on the old dikes off today's map of all those stages together, so it does not show that the Edo-period stages alone were settled that way, and it does not date the subsidence it adds to the outlet problem. It does not describe the outlets themselves or say how they were worked.

Used for: a sea polder's drainage problem and its settlements on the old sea dikes (archetypes)
