dongjing-menghualu-chongyang

孟元老, 東京夢華錄 卷八 ("Dreams of Splendor of the Eastern Capital, Scroll Eight", title translated) (Chinese Wikisource) (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%9D%B1%E4%BA%AC%E5%A4%A2%E8%8F%AF%E9%8C%84/%E5%8D%B7%E5%85%AB)

What it is: The eighth chapter of Meng Yuanlao's twelfth-century memoir of Kaifeng, the Northern Song capital, in the original literary Chinese; its entry on the Double Ninth festival (重陽) tells how the city viewed chrysanthemums of several named kinds, found them everywhere, and how the wine shops bound them into doorways.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a premodern eyewitness account that the ninth-month festival brought chrysanthemums into a city in quantity, which is the festival the setting's notes give the flower. It describes the capital as the author knew it from 1103 to 1126, recalled after its fall (the preface is dated 1147); it says where the flowers were shown, not where or how they were grown, and nothing of fields or their size.

Used for: chrysanthemums shown throughout a Song capital at the Double Ninth festival (Fields)
