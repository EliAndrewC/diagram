Ginza Design Council, "家康がつくった町割" ("The town plan Ieyasu made", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.ginza-machidukuri.jp/column/29/)

What it is: A short column by the Ginza Design Council, the district's town-planning body, on how Ginza was laid out as part of Edo's commoner quarter after 1603: the 60-ken square block, the open ground at its center, the shops on the street and the tenements up the alleys behind them, and the new lanes cut after the fire of 1657.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the block, the shop frontage and the back alley of an Edo commoner quarter in one place, which is the fabric our city maps pack. It is a popular column by a present-day planning council, not a study, it gives no sources, and it speaks of Ginza only, one district of the shogun's capital, which is a larger and more regular place than the provincial city we draw.

Used for: the 60-ken square block with its open core and 20-ken-deep lots; the two new lanes cut through Ginza after 1657; shops three to five ken wide on the street with the alley between them and the tenements behind; the shared privy and well (cities/fabric)
