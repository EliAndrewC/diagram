ginza-machidukuri-machiwari

Ginza Design Council, "家康がつくった町割" ("The town plan Ieyasu made", title translated) (https://www.ginza-machidukuri.jp/column/29/)

What it is: A short column, unsigned and undated, by the Ginza Design Council, a design council set up by Ginza's own merchant and town associations to consult on new buildings, on how Ginza was laid out as part of Edo's commoner quarter after 1603: the 60-ken square block, the open ground at its center, the shops on the street and the tenements up the alleys behind them, and the new lanes cut after the fire of 1657.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the block, the shop frontage and the back alley of an Edo commoner quarter in one place, which is the fabric our city maps pack. It is a popular column by a present-day planning council, not a study, it gives no sources, and it speaks of Ginza only, one district of the shogun's capital, which is a larger and more regular place than the provincial city we draw. It measures in the longer Kyoto ken, by which it puts its 60 ken at about 120 m, not in the shorter Edo ken. Its three-to-five-ken shop frontage comes in a passage asking the reader to picture the old lots from today's street, where it says the same frontage still holds, so it is the column's picture and not a dated Edo measurement. It also says the lots' interiors changed after 1657 without saying how, and that what the open ground at the block's center was used for is not known.

Used for: the 60-ken square block with its open core and 20-ken-deep lots; the two new lanes cut through Ginza after 1657; shops three to five ken wide on the street with the alley between them and the tenements behind; the shared privy and well (cities/fabric)
