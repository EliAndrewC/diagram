kotobank-rokusaiichi

Kotobank, 六斎市 ("rokusai-ichi, the six-day market", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the entries of the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and others (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/六斎市)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering several Japanese reference works' entries on the rokusai-ichi, the periodic market held six days a month in medieval and early-modern Japan; the longest is by the medieval historian Sasaki Gin'ya in the Heibonsha encyclopedia.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the market's schedule (day pairs such as 1 and 6, twice in each ten days), how it grew out of the three-day market, and where it lasted into the Edo period - as the center of a region's trade in Tohoku and Kanto, while in the Kinai the market towns became permanent towns and the name died out. It is a dictionary summary: it names no catchment and describes nothing of the market's ground.

Used for: how often a Japanese market town held its market, and where the periodic market survived (towns)
