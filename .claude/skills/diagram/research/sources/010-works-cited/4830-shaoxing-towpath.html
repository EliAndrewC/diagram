shaoxing-towpath

Shaoxing ancient towpath coverage (CGTN; Shanghai Daily); Grand Canal surveys (https://news.cgtn.com/news/3d3d674d7755444d33457a6333566d54/index.html)

What it is: A 2019 travel feature on CGTN, the Chinese state broadcaster's English site, credited to Shanghai Daily and unsigned, on the qiandao towpath at Shaoxing on the Eastern Zhejiang Canal - the stone path along which boats were hauled, in its two forms, one laid on the bank and one carried on piers about half a meter above the water - as it survives and is presented to visitors today. The page dates the canal itself to the fifth century BC and gives the best-preserved stretch as about eight kilometers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the form of the towpath - a real riverside way, and the slab-on-piers kind standing just above the water - which is what a towpath on our canal maps is drawn from. Its limits are that it is a broadcaster's travel feature rather than a survey or an archaeological report; that it describes a preserved heritage structure as it stands now, whose fabric is later than the institution; that the date, the length and the Marco Polo detail are not on the page and rest on nothing readable, so they stand as guesses; and that a Chinese canal stands in for a setting whose surface is often Japanese.

Used for: the qiandao (纤道) towpath as the real riverside way, in its two forms: bank-side, and slab stones on stone piers standing ~0.5 m above the water parallel to the bank (the page dates the canal to the 5th century BC and gives the best-preserved stretch as about 8 km; an 815 CE date, a 40+ km length and Marco Polo's horse teams are not on it)
