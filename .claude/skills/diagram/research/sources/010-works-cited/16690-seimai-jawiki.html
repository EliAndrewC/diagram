ja.wikipedia 精米 ("rice polishing", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%B2%BE%E7%B1%B3)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on polishing brown rice white, with a short history of the Japanese methods: the pestle and mortar, the clay mill brought from Qing China in the Genroku era, the treadle mortar, and the waterwheel on the Onden river that Hokusai drew in the 1830s, which drove pestles up and down to polish rice.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests two forms of polishing in the Edo period, the treadle mortar worked by foot and the water-driven pestle at waterwheels on a stream (several stood on Edo's Onden river, around today's Jingumae), which is why a town's polishing has two forms. A tertiary summary and a stub: its history rests on one footnoted source, an introductory article in the Journal of the Japan Society for Precision Engineering, and the water-driven mill appears only in an uncited caption to Hokusai's print rather than in the body text. The article itself says water-driven mills needed a riverside and did not spread widely. It covers Japan only, not China, and says nothing of how many mills a town had or who worked them.

Used for: the treadle mortar and the water-driven pestle mill (urban-features)
