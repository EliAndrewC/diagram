kotobank-nimosaku

Kotobank, 二毛作 (nimosaku, double cropping), reprinting the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (revised edition; the encyclopedia entry by Shiotani Tetsuo, with a section on the history of double cropping in Japan) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BA%8C%E6%AF%9B%E4%BD%9C-110356)

What it is: Kotobank's page of dictionary and encyclopedia entries on double cropping, whose encyclopedia entry describes the paddy's rice-and-barley year, the crops sown after the rice, the prewar northern limit of the practice and its history from the Heian period through the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one read account of which paddies carried a winter crop, where and when, which decides whether a map's paddy ground was bare or sown over winter. It is a signed encyclopedia entry, a summary that names only one primary source (a 1264 shogunate law); its northern limit is the one measured before the Second World War, not an Edo line; and it names the Kyoto-Osaka region and the Inland Sea coast as where the practice grew in the Edo period without giving shares.

Used for: rice in summer and barley in winter on one paddy; the other winter crops; the prewar northern limit and what stopped it; the practice in the Heian and Kamakura periods and its growth in the Edo period (fields)
