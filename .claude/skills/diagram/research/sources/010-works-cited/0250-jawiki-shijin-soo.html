"Shijin-soo" (the four gods in correspondence), Japanese Wikipedia (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/四神相応)

What it is: An encyclopedia article on the East Asian siting doctrine of the four directional gods, giving the classical formulations - water on the left, a road on the right, a pond in front, hills behind - and their Japanese application to capitals such as Heian-kyo.

Why it applies, and its limits: Every formulation it quotes places the watercourse on ONE side of the site and none contemplates a site divided by water, which is the shape our rule takes. The limit is severe and stated where the claim is used: this is capital- and residence-siting doctrine drawn from Chinese classics, not a rule anyone states for a farming hamlet's brook, and the project must not promote it into one.

Used for: the doctrine placing flowing water on one side of a site
