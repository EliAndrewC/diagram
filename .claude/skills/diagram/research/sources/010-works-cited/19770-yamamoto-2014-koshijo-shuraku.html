yamamoto-2014-koshijo-shuraku

Yamamoto Takatsugu, Kamata Seishi, Urayama Ryuichi and Shibuya Shizuaki, "Okinawa-honto nanbu ni okeru 'koshijo shuraku' no keisei" (沖縄本島南部における"格子状集落"の形成, The formation of "grid villages" in southern Okinawa Island), abstract of a paper at the Association of Japanese Geographers' spring meeting, 2014 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ajg/2014s/0/2014s_100329/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A conference abstract by four researchers at Chubu University, Ariake National College of Technology and Toyama International University who reconstruct the original layout of Maekawa, a village of southern Okinawa laid out in rows of house plots, from the cadastral maps of the land survey around 1900, interviews and fieldwork, and date its move to its present site from the Ryukyu kingdom's official history.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the layout of a planned village founded in the eighteenth century back behind the modern houses, to the plots and streets as the land survey found them, before the house plots multiplied and were split, so it speaks for the village before modern times. Its limits: it is an abstract, not the full paper; it describes the plots and the streets, not which way each house faced; it studies Maekawa and names one other village, both in the south of Okinawa Island under the Ryukyu kingdom, whose planned villages differ from those of mainland Japan; the eighteenth-century date of the grid villages is given as what is said, not shown; and the cadastral maps are only thought to date from the land survey of about 1900, so the layout before then is inferred from them. And the maps show the village as it had grown by then, some 160 years after its move in 1736: its blocks were extended first uphill and later downhill, so which streets date from the eighteenth century is not known. That the streets curved to follow the contours of the slope is the authors' supposition, drawn from what residents say and from the terrain.

Used for: that the streets of an eighteenth-century Okinawan village curved and its blocks bowed, so houses along them could not all face one way (homesteads)
