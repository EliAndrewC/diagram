yamamoto-2014-koshijo-shuraku

Yamamoto Takatsugu, Kamata Seishi, Urayama Ryuichi and Shibuya Shizuaki, "Okinawa-honto nanbu ni okeru 'koshijo shuraku' no keisei" (沖縄本島南部における"格子状集落"の形成, The formation of "grid villages" in southern Okinawa Island), abstract of a paper at the Association of Japanese Geographers' spring meeting, 2014 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ajg/2014s/0/2014s_100329/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A conference abstract by four geographers who reconstruct the original layout of Maekawa, a village of southern Okinawa laid out in rows of house plots, from the cadastral maps of the land survey around 1900, interviews and fieldwork, and date its move to its present site from the Ryukyu kingdom's official history.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the layout of an eighteenth-century planned village back behind the modern houses, to the plots and streets as the land survey found them before the old land-sharing system ended, so it speaks for the village before modern times. Its limits: it is an abstract, not the full paper; it describes the plots and the streets, not which way each house faced; it studies Maekawa and names one other village, both in the south of Okinawa Island under the Ryukyu kingdom, whose planned villages differ from those of mainland Japan; the eighteenth-century date of the grid villages is given as what is said, not shown; and the cadastral maps are only thought to date from the land survey of about 1900, so the layout before then is inferred from them.

Used for: that the streets of an eighteenth-century Okinawan village curved and its blocks bowed, so houses along them could not all face one way (homesteads)
