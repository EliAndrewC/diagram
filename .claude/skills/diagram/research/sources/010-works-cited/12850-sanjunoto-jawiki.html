ja.wikipedia 三重塔 ("three-story pagoda", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三重塔)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on three-story pagodas, a tertiary source flagged since 2021 as lacking citations, whose only listed references are two pagoda-pilgrimage guidebooks by Hasegawa Shū (Tokyo Shoseki, 2000). It lists Japan's surviving wooden three-story pagodas built before 1868, but gives heights for only seven of them, all National Treasures of the Asuka to Kamakura periods (about 19 to 25 m, plus Yakushiji's East Pagoda at about 34 m); the list of roughly a hundred later and lesser pagodas gives no heights.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the heights of real premodern three-story pagodas, so it bounds the height of the one we draw. Its limits: none of its heights is footnoted; they come only from the oldest and grandest National Treasures, not from the ordinary Muromachi and Edo pagodas the rest of the list names; Yakushiji's 34 m is an outlier, a Nara form with skirt roofs that looks six-storied; it lists survivors at notable temples and says nothing of how common pagodas were; and it is Japan only, while the article itself notes that wooden tiered pagodas are rare in China and Korea and that Chinese ones were usually climbable, so its heights stand in for a Chinese-modeled pagoda only as an analog.

Used for: how tall a three-story pagoda was (religion-and-death)
