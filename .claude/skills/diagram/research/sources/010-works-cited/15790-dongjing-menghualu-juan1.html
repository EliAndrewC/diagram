孟元老 (Meng Yuanlao), 東京夢華錄 卷一 ("Dreams of Splendor of the Eastern Capital, Scroll One", title translated from the Chinese by this project), Chinese Wikisource transcription (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/東京夢華錄/卷一)

What it is: The first chapter of Meng Yuanlao's memoir of Northern Song Kaifeng, written about 1147 after the city had fallen, in the original literary Chinese: the gates of the outer wall side by side, the old city, the rivers through the capital with their bridges, and the palace. It names each of the outer wall's water gates and describes the East Water Gate's closing grille.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness account of a real walled Chinese capital whose rivers and canals entered through gates in its wall, which is the case a river city with a canal inside its wall draws. It is one city, an imperial capital of the eleventh and twelfth centuries far larger than a provincial seat, recalled from memory and literary rather than measured: it gives no gate widths, and its water gates are the Song capital's, not a count that holds for smaller cities.

Used for: the water gate's night grille, its footgates on the banks, and the number of water gates in one capital's wall; an aqueduct stream carried over a river on a wooden trough and brought through its own water gate (cities/river-cities, cities/capitals)
