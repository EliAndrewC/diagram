ginza-machiwari-column

銀座街づくり会議・銀座デザイン協議会 (Ginza Machizukuri Council and Ginza Design Council), 家康がつくった町割 ("The town plan Ieyasu made", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.ginza-machidukuri.jp/column/29/)

What it is: An undated popular column, citing no sources, by the present-day Ginza street-planning councils on the early Edo-period (1603) plan of the townsmen's land around Ginza: blocks 60 Kyō-ma ken (about 120 m) square, an open lot (kaishochi, whose use the column says is unknown) in the middle, town lots 20 ken deep along the streets, and the small alleys running between the shops to the row tenements behind.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the block and lot dimensions of a planned Edo commoner district, the measure against which a town block's depth can be read. It describes the shogun's capital, not a provincial town, and one district of it; and its picture of small alleys between shops 3 to 5 ken in frontage is offered as a scene to imagine, not as a measured record (the 3 to 5 ken is the shops' frontage; the column gives no width for the alleys). The square block with its open middle is the plan of 1603: after the Meireki fire of 1657, new streets were cut between the blocks and the lots' interiors changed, so it is the early-Edo form. And it is a popular column by today's district councils that cites no sources, so its figures give the shape and should be checked against a scholarly study of the Edo town plan before being carried as numbers.

Used for: the depth of a planned Edo town block and its street-front lots, and the alley between shops leading to the tenements behind (ways)
