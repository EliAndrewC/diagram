anju-osaka-fires

Tani Naoki (director, Osaka Museum of Housing and Living), "Disasters in Edo-period Osaka: fire, earthquake, tsunami", Anju no. 84, Osaka Municipal Housing Information Center (in Japanese; https://www.osaka-angenet.jp/ange/ange84/3000347)

What it is: A short article by a historian of the Japanese town house, the director of Osaka's city museum of housing, in the city housing center's magazine, on the fires of Edo-period Osaka and how its town houses were built against them; it reads a one-sheet print issued after the fire of 1863, held by the museum, which lists the damage of three great fires.

Why it applies, and its limits: The print gives a count of earthen storehouses beside the counts of houses and households for the Osaka fire of 1724, the one premodern whole-city figure found. It counts only what burned, not what stood; it is a popular print's tally quoted in a magazine article, not a census; and Osaka, a merchant capital the article thinks probably the best guarded against fire in Japan, is not a provincial town. The article gives no issue date.

Used for: how many storehouses a premodern city held against its households, and where on the lot they stood (cities/fabric)
