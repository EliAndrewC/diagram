kotobank-machiya

Kotobank, 町家 ("townhouse", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - entries from the Digital Daijisen, the Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/町家)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the machiya, the Japanese townsman's house, carrying dictionary definitions and an encyclopedia article on its history, its usual form (the ridge parallel to the street, a low storage story, the gable-fronted variant) and its regional looks: tile and plastered walls in the west, board roofs weighted with stones and exposed posts in the east, and the plastered storehouse style that spread in late-Edo Edo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a signed scholarly encyclopedia's summary of what a Japanese town's houses looked like, with the east-west contrast a roof knob needs. It generalizes across the whole country and period and gives no counts, so it says what forms existed, not how common each was in any one town; its regional contrast reads from surviving houses, most of them late, and it says nothing of China.

Used for: the low storage story, the roofs by region, the plastered front in Edo, and the ridge along or the gable to the street (towns)
