fao-industrial-charcoal

FAO, Industrial charcoal making, FAO Forestry Paper 63, chapter 5, earth pits for charcoal making (https://www.fao.org/4/x5328e/x5328e06.htm)

What it is: The FAO's manual on charcoal production at industrial scale, whose chapter on the earth pit - the traditional method, documented at about thirty cubic meters gross with miniature village pits beside it - gives the fuelwood-to-charcoal ratio of that method.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the wood consumed per unit of charcoal - about four and a half to one by weight in a working pit - which is the ratio that sends the kiln to the wood on these maps. A modern technical manual standing in for a physical ratio that does not change with the century; the method it documents is the earth pit itself, close to a premodern burn; the ratio is a yield of one method at one size, so it bounds rather than measures a household's burn.

Used for: the fuelwood-to-charcoal ratio
