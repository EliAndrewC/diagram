kojodan-dobei

攻城団 ("Castle-Siege Group", title translated), お城の基礎講座 40. 土塀の種類 ("Castle Basics Course 40: Types of Earthen Walls", title translated) (https://blog.kojodan.jp/entry/2020/08/26/180000)

What it is: A 2020 post by the writer Takamaru in "Takamaru's Castle Basics course", a long-running explainer series on Kojodan, a Japanese castle enthusiasts' site. It sets out the kinds of earthen wall used at castles - the framed and plastered wall, the rammed-earth tsuijibei built up in layers inside wooden forms, and the frameless neribei of old tile and stone bedded in clay - with how each was built and how thick it came out.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it to decide how thick to draw a compound wall: a rammed-earth tsuijibei about a meter thick and three high, a frameless neribei perhaps 30 cm. Its limits are that the author hedges the 30 cm figure himself while stating the 1 m one flat; and that these are the walls of castles, a different scale of place and a different job for a wall, which we apply to a magistracy or manor compound as an upper bound.

Used for: frameless neribei 「compared with a tsuijibei ... about 30 cm」 (translated; original: 「築地塀に比べて…30cm程度」); tsuijibei up to about 1 m thick (Estates and other compounds, Religion and the dead)
