F. H. King, Farmers of Forty Centuries; Or, Permanent Agriculture in China, Korea and Japan (1911), Project Gutenberg ebook 5350 (https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5350/pg5350-images.html)

What it is: The American soil scientist F. H. King's account of what he saw farming in China, Korea and Japan on a journey made in 1909, published in 1911 - field sizes, holdings, manuring, and the movement of night soil out of the cities, described at first hand and long out of copyright.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states plainly for Japan the two things our tract-size entry needed and could not get from Buck, whose own survey is in a lending-restricted scan on no page a reader can open: that the lands worked by one family are seldom contiguous, may be widely scattered and are very often rented - with the consolidation laws in force since 1900 named as what was passed to undo it, which is the strongest form the statement can take - and that the average worked by one family is nearer 2.5 acres, which sits beside our roughly 1 cho figure as an independent reading. Its limits: it is a traveler's eyewitness account rather than a survey, so its averages are ones he was told or read rather than ones he measured; it describes 1909, at the very end of the order our maps depict and after Meiji land reform had begun, so a figure from it is the last state of that world and not its typical one; and the book's admiration for what it describes is well known and shapes what it chose to record.

Used for: a Japanese family's lands seldom contiguous, often widely scattered and often rented, and the consolidation laws in force from 1900; the average worked by one family nearer 2.5 acres; and, at the city tier, the night soil carried out of a city to its gardens by the cartload and the liquid manure diluted before it is fed to the plants
