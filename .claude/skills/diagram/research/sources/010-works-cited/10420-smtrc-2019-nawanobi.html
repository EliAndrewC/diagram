smtrc-2019-nawanobi

Naraha Tomoyuki (Accel land and house surveyors), "'Kobo' to 'genkyo' ga awanai! (1) Nawanobi ni tsuite" (The register and the ground do not match! 1: on nawanobi), for Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Realty, March 2019 (in Japanese; https://smtrc.jp/useful/knowledge/kyokai-hikai/2019_03.html)

What it is: A column by a licensed land surveyor on a real-estate company's advice pages, explaining why old registered land areas are often smaller than the ground. It traces this to the Edo-period land survey and lists the survey's allowances. One of them, azekiwa-biki, took the bund to be a shaku wide and moved the boundary a shaku on each side.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a land-survey convention for the bund's width, which bears on how wide an Edo bund was reckoned. It is a practitioner's column, not a history. It hedges the allowances ("there seem to have been"), names no source for them, and gives no date or domain within the Edo period, though surveys varied by domain and by period. On its own, then, the one-shaku figure is weak evidence. A closer public source gives the same rule: the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry 畔引 (on Kotobank) cites the Tokugawa Kinreikō, a collection of shogunate laws, for 1726. The column itself calls the allowances tax relief, so the shaku is a reckoning in the farmer's favor, a survey convention, not a measured bund. It is about Japan only.

Used for: the land survey's one-shaku allowance for a bund (fields)
