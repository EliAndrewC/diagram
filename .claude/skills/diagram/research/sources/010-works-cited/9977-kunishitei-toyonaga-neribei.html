kunishitei-toyonaga-neribei

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Database of National Cultural Properties, 豊永染物店土塀 (The earth wall of the Toyonaga dye shop) (in Japanese; https://kunishitei.bunka.go.jp/heritage/detail/101/00006724)

What it is: The national database entry for a registered wall at Yasuda in Kōchi prefecture, of the late Edo period (1830 to 1867): a neribei of head-sized stones laid in red earth along the north edge of a dye shop's plot, 14 m long, with its base width and height measured and a tiled coping.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a surveyed thickness for the lighter kind of earth wall, which is how a compound's wall is drawn on the town and city tiers. Its limits: it is a merchant's wall, not a samurai's or an office's; it is built of stones in earth rather than of tile and clay; and the width is of the base, where such a wall is thickest.

Used for: a neribei 0.6 m wide at the base and 1.6 m high (buildings, religion-and-death)
