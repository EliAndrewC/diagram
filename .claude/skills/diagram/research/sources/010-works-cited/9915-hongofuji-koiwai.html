hongofuji-koiwai

Hongō Fujiyama Park (Yokohama), provisional site, "古民家ミニ博物館" (the old farmhouse mini-museum) (in Japanese; https://hongofuji.stars.ne.jp/guidance/cultural-property/museum/)

What it is: A question-and-answer page by the park's operating committee, who are volunteers, explaining the parts of the old Koiwai house the park preserves - its entrances, doors, wicket, gate and storehouse - written for the park's visitors. The house was built in 1847, late in the Edo period, in Kajigaya village, now part of Yokohama, as a village headman's (nanushi) house; it was taken down from 1997 and rebuilt in the park, reusing about six parts in ten of its old timber.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, in plain words, the everyday entrance of a farm or fishing-village house as a one-ken sliding door with a wicket, the wicket being the door in daily use. It is a visitor's page for one preserved house near Yokohama, not a survey, and it gives no source for its general statement about farm and fishing-village houses. The house it preserves was a village headman's, of higher standing than an ordinary farmer's (its gatehouse, the page says, was allowed only to leading farmers), so its own door is an example, not a measure of the ordinary one. It speaks of commoners' houses, not warrior residences, and the same page at the park's main address could not be fetched, so the provisional site is the one cited.

Used for: a farmhouse's one-ken main door with a wicket; the wicket as a low, narrow doorway (buildings)
