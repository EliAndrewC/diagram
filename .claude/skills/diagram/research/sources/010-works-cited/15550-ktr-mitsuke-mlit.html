ktr-mitsuke-mlit

Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, Kanto Regional Bureau, Yokohama office, 東海道と宿場の施設【見付】 ("The Tokaido and its post-town facilities: the mitsuke", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.ktr.mlit.go.jp/yokohama/tokaido/02_tokaido/03_sisetu/0401.htm)

What it is: A page of a government road office's history of the Tokaido in Kanagawa, on the mitsuke at the ends of post towns, read from a survey map, a Hiroshige print and a late-Edo drawing of Kanagawa's entrance banks with their measurements.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures one post town's entrance banks and gives the page's reading of what they were for, which is what a Japanese-model town's entrance is drawn from. It is an unsigned public-history page that rests on pictures and one drawing. Another period illustration, the Kanagawa sunago, shows the same entrance as a square mound rather than as banks, so the measured section is one drawing's account of it. That the banks were a simple defense is the page's inference from their form, and its reading of how much they mattered late in the period is its own hedged guess. The figures are Kanagawa's alone.

Used for: the mitsuke at a post town's ends and the town lying between them; Kanagawa's banks measured in section (defenses)
