hamura-tamagawa-josui

Hamura City (Tokyo), "玉川上水をまもる" (Protecting the Tamagawa aqueduct) (in Japanese; https://www.city.hamura.tokyo.jp/0000001544.html)

What it is: A page of the city of Hamura, where the Tamagawa aqueduct takes its water from the Tama river, on how the aqueduct was built in 1653 to supply Edo, why its intake was put at Hamura, and how its weir and intake gates work and were managed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates an Edo-period river intake and gives the reason its site was chosen, that the river is thrown against a ridge on one bank so that the water gathers toward the other. It is a modern municipal page written for a general audience: the reason for the site is hedged ("thought to be"), it describes the weir in the present tense and does not say that the weir or the intake is unchanged since the Edo period, and it is one intake of one aqueduct, which gives no angle at which the intake leaves the river.

Used for: an Edo-period intake sited where the river's water gathers toward one bank (river cities)
