hamura-tamagawa-josui

Hamura City (Tokyo), "玉川上水をまもる" (Protecting the Tamagawa aqueduct) (in Japanese; https://www.city.hamura.tokyo.jp/0000001544.html)

What it is: A page of the city of Hamura, written by its local history museum (Hamura City Board of Education) and first published in 2012 (updated 2023), where the Tamagawa aqueduct takes its water from the Tama river, on how the aqueduct was built in 1653 to supply Edo, why its intake was put at Hamura, and how its weir and intake gates work and were managed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates an Edo-period river intake and gives two reasons its site was chosen: the ground there is high enough to carry the water by gravity onto the plateau and on to Edo, and the river is thrown against a ridge on its south bank so that the water gathers toward the other bank. It names the two together and does not say which counted for more, so it supports the bank where the water gathers as one reason for an intake's site, not the only one. The reason for the site is hedged ("thought to be"), it describes the weir in the present tense and does not say that the weir or the intake is unchanged since the Edo period, and it is one intake of one aqueduct, which gives no angle at which the intake leaves the river.

Used for: an Edo-period intake sited where the river's water gathers toward one bank (river cities)
