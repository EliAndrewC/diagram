Zhou Qian, 跟着学者逛故宫，看藏在水井中的古代智慧 ("Touring the Forbidden City with a scholar: the ancient ingenuity hidden in its wells", title translated from the Chinese by this project), China Youth Daily, 2026-05-24, via Beijing Daily (in Chinese; https://xinwen.bjd.com.cn/content/s6a12364be4b03fa51a7eb7ec.html)

What it is: A newspaper piece by a Palace Museum researcher on the Forbidden City's more than eighty wells: a pavilion built over every one, most drawn by rope and bucket by hand, and one pavilion in the Imperial Garden with two beams under its roof carrying an iron pulley over the well, very likely of the Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a pavilion roof over every well of the Ming and Qing palace, and an iron pulley hung from beams over one of them. It says that in the Ming and Qing some well pavilions had pulleys, but it dates no single pavilion, and many palace buildings were rebuilt under the Qing. It calls the pulley "very likely" Qing from the Imperial Household Department's workshop archives, which record iron pulleys being made, not from the building itself. Nothing in it dates the roof or the pulley to the Ming in particular. It describes a palace, the richest possible case, so it says nothing of how often an ordinary town or village well had a roof; it is a popular article, not a survey.

Used for: the roofed well and the pulley on a beam in Ming and Qing China (urban-features)
