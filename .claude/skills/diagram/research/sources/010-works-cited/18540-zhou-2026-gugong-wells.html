Zhou Qian, 跟着学者逛故宫，看藏在水井中的古代智慧 ("Touring the Forbidden City with a scholar: the ancient ingenuity hidden in its wells", title translated from the Chinese by this project), China Youth Daily, 2026-05-24, via Beijing Daily (in Chinese; https://xinwen.bjd.com.cn/content/s6a12364be4b03fa51a7eb7ec.html)

What it is: A newspaper piece by a Palace Museum researcher on the Forbidden City's more than eighty wells: a pavilion built over every one, most drawn by rope and bucket by hand, and one pavilion in the Imperial Garden with two beams under its roof carrying an iron pulley over the well, very likely of the Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates a roofed well and a pulley hung from a beam to the Ming and Qing, from the buildings themselves. It describes a palace, the richest possible case, so it says nothing of how often an ordinary town or village well had a roof; it is a popular article, not a survey.

Used for: the roofed well and the pulley on a beam in Ming and Qing China (urban-features)
