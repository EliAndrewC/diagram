kusawake-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 草分け ("kusawake, the founding house", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8D%89%E5%88%86%E3%81%91)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the kusawake, the person or house held to have first cleared the land and founded a village, and the standing such houses kept.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that founding houses, historical or legendary, held the village offices such as headman generation after generation and held first place at the places of worship and in the shared land and water - the link between the headman and the village's oldest house. Its limits: a short stub article resting on two encyclopedia entries it names in its bibliography, with no footnotes to individual statements, no period narrower than "before modern times" and no region narrower than Japan (nothing on China), and nothing on where a founding house stood in the village. It also hedges its own claim: where a founding house did not hold the office, it still had a say as the main house of the village's branch families, so the office was usual rather than certain. A closer signed source is readable on Kotobank: Fukuda Ajio's entry in the Heibonsha world encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典) places the pattern in the early modern period and says the founding houses often, not always, held the village offices by hereditary monopoly.

Used for: the founding houses holding the headman's office and first place at worship and in the shared land and water (homesteads)
