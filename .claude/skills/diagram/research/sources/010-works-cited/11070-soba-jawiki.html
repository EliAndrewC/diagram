What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on buckwheat, the plant and the crop: its height, flowers and stems, and its summer and autumn sowings in Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: A tertiary encyclopedia article, and its description of the plant (60-130 cm tall; white, pale-red or red flowers; green to deep-red stems; harvest about 70-80 days after sowing) carries no footnote of its own. The height and the stem colors are botany and hold in any century, within the spread of variety and soil. The flower colors do not wholly: Japan's red-flowered buckwheat (Takane Ruby, registered 1993) was bred from a Himalayan landrace, so a pre-modern field in this setting is drawn white to pale pink. The 70-80 days is a modern figure for modern varieties (the article gives 60-75 days for those bred since the 1980s); a pre-modern landrace's season was short too, but the number is not a period count. Its cultivation figures describe modern Japanese farming; of pre-modern practice it says only that buckwheat was grown in slash-and-burn fields from the Yayoi period and on mountain land, nothing of how a field was sown.

Used for: buckwheat's height, the color of its flowers and stems, and its short season (fields)
