kitakyushu-saienba-kiln

Kitakyushu City, Kokurakita Ward, "The Saienba kiln site" (title translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「菜園場窯跡」) (in Japanese; https://www.city.kitakyushu.lg.jp/kokurakita/file_0090.html)

What it is: A ward sightseeing page on an excavated kiln that the page says is thought to be the hobby kiln of Hosokawa Tadaoki, the early Edo lord of Kokura: a climbing kiln of a stoke mouth and four chambers, 16.6 m long, as confirmed by two excavations.

Why it applies, and its limits: An excavated climbing kiln in Kyushu with its whole length measured, the smallest in the record. The page dates it only through its link to Tadaoki, which it gives as a belief, not a fact, and it says that when the kiln fired and what it made are not yet clear. The page is the ward's sightseeing summary, not the excavation report, and gives no width. It was a lord's kiln fired for his own pleasure rather than a trade works, so it may be smaller than a working potters' kiln.

Used for: a four-chamber climbing kiln 16.6 m long (urban-features)
