shoinzukuri-kotobank

Kotobank, 書院造 (shoin-zukuri) - Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Kawakami Mitsugu (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9B%B8%E9%99%A2%E9%80%A0%E3%82%8A-291732)

What it is: The Kotobank page for shoin-zukuri, the formal residential style of the warrior household, carrying the Heibonsha world encyclopedia entry by the architectural historian Kawakami Mitsugu on its development and on the daimyo mansions of the Keicho and Genna years (1596-1624): the halls divided between the omote and the oku, the rooms ranked from an upper seat down, the verandas round them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a scholar-written account of how a great warrior house was divided and ringed, the model our residences scale down. Its limits are that it describes the largest establishments of the early seventeenth century, not a provincial posting, and that it is a tertiary summary.

Used for: the omote and oku of a daimyo mansion, with the goza-no-ma on the omote side; rooms ringed on four sides by hiroen and irikawa (buildings)
