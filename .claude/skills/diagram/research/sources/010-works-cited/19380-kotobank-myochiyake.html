kotobank-myochiyake

Kotobank, "妙知焼" [Myōchi-yake, the Osaka fire of 1724], the entries in the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (Heibonsha; the article by Fujimoto Atsushi) and the Hyakka Jiten Mypedia (Heibonsha) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A6%99%E7%9F%A5%E7%84%BC-872796)

What it is: Two general encyclopedia entries, on Kotobank, on Osaka's great fire of 1724: its course through the city, its damage in houses, households, storehouses, bridges and temples, and the city's recovery, with the death toll given as the several figures the sources record.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the standard account's count of the storehouses that burned beside the houses and households, for a premodern city: 11,765 houses (60,292 households) and 1,097 earthen storehouses, over about two-thirds of the city. It is a modern encyclopedia summary that does not name the period record its figures come from; it counts only what burned, and a storehouse is built to survive a fire, so the ones that held are left out and a ratio taken from it runs low; and Osaka, a merchant capital, is not a provincial town.

Used for: the standard record's count of Osaka's storehouses burned in 1724, against its houses and households (cities/fabric)
