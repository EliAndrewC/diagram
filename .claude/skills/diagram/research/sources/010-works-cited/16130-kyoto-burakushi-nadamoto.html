kyoto-burakushi-nadamoto

Nadamoto Masahisa, 知りたいあなたのための京都の部落史(超コンパクト版)その２ ("Kyoto's buraku history for those who want to know, the ultra-compact edition, part 2", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Memento 9, Kyoto Buraku Problem Research Center, 2002 (in Japanese; https://www.cc.kyoto-su.ac.jp/~nadamoto/work/20020725.htm)

What it is: A historian's short survey article, on his university page, of how Kyoto's outcaste communities came through the warring-states era and grew through the Edo period, citing the multi-volume Kyoto no burakushi and the studies of Yamamoto Naotomo and others.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Tenbu as Kyoto's oldest riverbank people's village and the counts, by period, of buraku temples founded in three western provinces, from which it argues that the communities were settled villages well before the Edo period. It is a popular summary of other scholars' findings. The temple counts are Yamamoto Naotomo's, pooled across Yamashiro, Tanba and Harima rather than given for any one quarter, and the article does not say what evidence the founding dates rest on. That the temples prove settled villages is the author's inference. It covers Kyoto and the surrounding Kansai provinces. Its only household figure is a total for eleven villages near Kyoto (386 households and 2,064 people in 1715, roughly doubled by 1870). It gives no plans, and no household count or size for any single village.

Used for: Kyoto's riverbank village, and the number and age of the quarters' temples (urban-features)
