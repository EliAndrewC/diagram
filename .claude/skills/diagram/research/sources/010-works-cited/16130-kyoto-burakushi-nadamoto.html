kyoto-burakushi-nadamoto

Nadamoto Masahisa, 知りたいあなたのための京都の部落史(超コンパクト版)その２ ("Kyoto's buraku history for those who want to know, the ultra-compact edition, part 2", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Memento 9, Kyoto Buraku Problem Research Center, 2002 (in Japanese; https://www.cc.kyoto-su.ac.jp/~nadamoto/work/20020725.htm)

What it is: A historian's short survey article, on his university page, of how Kyoto's outcaste communities came through the warring-states era, citing the multi-volume Kyoto no burakushi and the studies of Yamamoto Naotomo and others.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Tenbu as Kyoto's oldest riverbank people's village and the counts, by period, of buraku temples founded in three western provinces, from which it argues that the communities were settled villages well before the Edo period. It is a popular summary of other scholars' findings, about Kyoto and the Kinai only, and gives no plans, household counts or sizes.

Used for: Kyoto's riverbank village, and the number and age of the quarters' temples (urban-features)
