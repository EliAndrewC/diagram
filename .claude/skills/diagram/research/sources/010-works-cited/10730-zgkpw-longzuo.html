China Science Communication (中国科普网), expert column of Wang Yusheng, "Ridge culture: a crystallization of the wisdom of traditional agriculture" (in Chinese; https://www.zgkpw.org.cn/expert/wangyusheng/content_442476.html)

What it is: A popular-science article on the history of Chinese ridge-and-furrow cultivation, from prehistoric and Shang ridge remains through the Han to the southern "high ridge, deep furrow" form.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says ridge culture was shaped to the ground it was on - higher ridges and deeper furrows for drainage in the rainy, low-lying south - and sums the practice up as suiting the measure to the place, which supports reading the direction of rows as a matter of the land. A present-day popular article with no references; it says nothing of which way the rows ran, and the "suit it to the place" phrase is its summing-up of ridge culture as a whole.

Used for: high ridges and deep furrows for drainage in the rainy south; ridge culture suited to local conditions (fields)
