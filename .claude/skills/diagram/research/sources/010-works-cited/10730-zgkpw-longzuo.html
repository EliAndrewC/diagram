China Science Communication (中国科普网), expert column of Wang Yusheng, "Ridge culture: a crystallization of the wisdom of traditional agriculture" (in Chinese; https://www.zgkpw.org.cn/expert/wangyusheng/content_442476.html)

What it is: A popular-science newspaper column (reprinted from 科普时报, Popular Science Times, 5 December 2025, part 61 of a series on ancient Chinese inventions) by Wang Yusheng, a historian of science and former director of the China Science and Technology Museum, on the history of Chinese ridge-and-furrow cultivation from Neolithic and Shang ridge remains through the Han and the southern "high ridge, deep furrow" form to the Ming-Qing farming books.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says ridge culture was shaped to the ground it was on - higher ridges and deeper furrows for drainage in the rainy, low-lying south - and sums the practice up as suiting the measure to the place, which supports reading the direction of rows as a matter of the land. A present-day popular article with no references. Some of its quotations of period books and its centimetre figures (a "垄欲广，沟欲深" credited to Fan Shengzhi, a "凡种五谷，先须起垄" credited to the Qimin Yaoshu, ridge heights credited to the Nongzheng Quanshu) could not be traced to those texts. Its southern high-ridge form is told as a Wei-Jin migration story without a period source, so it carries the general point and not a figure or a quotation. It says nothing of which way the rows ran, and the "suit it to the place" phrase is its summing-up of ridge culture as a whole.

Used for: high ridges and deep furrows for drainage in the rainy south; ridge culture suited to local conditions (fields)
