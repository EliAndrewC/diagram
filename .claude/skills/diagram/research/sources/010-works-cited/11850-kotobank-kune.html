kotobank-kune

Digital Daijisen and Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (Shogakukan), with a passage of the Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha), s.v. くね ("kune", a fence or hedge, title translated from the Japanese by this project), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%8F%E3%81%AD-484656)

What it is: Two dictionaries' entries on the word kune, a fence of woven bamboo or a hedge, with its earliest example from a kyogen play, and the passage of the Heibonsha encyclopedia that uses the word for the farmstead grove of the Kanto.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the same word named a boundary hedge and, in the Kanto, a farmstead grove. Its one dated example of the hedge sense is a kyogen play that the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten dates to the late Muromachi or early Edo period, quoted from a text of the play written down in 1792. The grove sense comes from the old edition of Heibonsha's encyclopedia, a modern summary of flat-country farmsteads that gives no period, so it does not say when the Kanto grove was called kune. These are dictionaries and an encyclopedia's summary: they define the word and give no sizes and no count. The encyclopedia names the grove's other regional names (igune in Tohoku, tsuijimatsu in Izumo) but not where the word kune itself was used outside the Kanto.

Used for: the kune as a boundary hedge or grove (homesteads)
