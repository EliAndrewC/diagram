kotobank-kune

Digital Daijisen and Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (Shogakukan), with a passage of the Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha), s.v. くね ("kune", a fence or hedge, title translated from the Japanese by this project), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%8F%E3%81%AD-484656)

What it is: Two dictionaries' entries on the word kune, a fence of woven bamboo or a hedge, with its earliest example from a kyogen play, and the passage of the Heibonsha encyclopedia that uses the word for the farmstead grove of the Kanto.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the same word named a boundary hedge and a farmstead grove, and dates the hedge sense to at least the end of the Muromachi period. It is a dictionary, so it defines the word and gives no sizes, no regions beyond the Kanto grove and no count.

Used for: the kune as a boundary hedge or grove (homesteads)
