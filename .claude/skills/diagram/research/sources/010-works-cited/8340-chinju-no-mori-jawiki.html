What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the chinju no mori, the forest attached to a Shinto shrine: a wood established and maintained within the precinct and around it so that it surrounds the sanctuary, the approach and the place of worship.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is why the ground around a shrine is drawn swept but the trees around it are drawn standing: the grove is deliberately KEPT, the opposite of cleared, so our sweeping rule touches only the loose scatter and never the wood. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary with no measurements - no depth, no species count, no distance from the hall - so it gives the principle and none of the numbers, and that it describes Japan.

Used for: the kept grove around a shrine, established and maintained so as to surround the precinct
