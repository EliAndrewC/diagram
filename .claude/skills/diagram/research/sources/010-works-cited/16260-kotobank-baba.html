kotobank-baba

Kotobank, 馬場 ("riding ground", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering entries from Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia and its Historical Place Names of Japan (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A6%AC%E5%A0%B4-441386)

What it is: A dictionary aggregator's page that gathers several reference works' entries for the word: a general encyclopedia entry on riding grounds in castle towns and Edo, and gazetteer entries for places named after a riding ground at Kanazawa, Daishoji, Mito, Soma, Imaisurugi and Ako.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says a castle town always kept riding grounds, gives their usual shape in ken, and places several real ones against a moat, a senior retainer's house or a street. It is a reference work, not a period record: the encyclopedia entry (signed by Tamai Tetsuo, a historian of Edo's buildings and city) summarizes, and its size of about 100 ken by 10 ken is a typical figure hedged as the common case, with every example drawn from Edo, the shogun's capital, so a lesser castle town's ground may differ from it (Ako's was 5 ken wide on two and a half cho of riverbank). The gazetteer entries are excerpts, each describing one Japanese castle town of the Edo period or just before it; nothing here speaks for a Chinese city.

Used for: the castle town's riding grounds, their shape, size and place (cities/government)
