「Why are persimmon trees planted in the front yards of farmhouses?」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農家の庭先にはなぜ柿の木が植えられているのか？」), ameblo.jp/toyoko-housing (https://ameblo.jp/toyoko-housing/entry-11418946119.html)

What it is: A blog post of December 2012, "Why are persimmon trees planted in the front yards of farmhouses?" (農家の庭先にはなぜ柿の木が植えられているのか？), on the Ameba blog of Toyoko Housing, a rental-property company. It is a conversational piece about why the persimmon is the dooryard tree of the Japanese farmhouse, citing the Edo-period agronomist Miyazaki Yasusada's encouragement to plant them around the house and listing the tree's practical virtues.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the persimmon standing in every dooryard, the Edo-period agricultural recommendation behind that habit, and the summer shade it throws over the house - the reasons our homesteads get a fruit tree by the door rather than an ornamental one. It is a company blog written for general readers, not a scholarly source: the Miyazaki attribution is reported at second hand, and the "every dooryard" is a modern writer's impression of the countryside, so we use it for the practice and its rationale and never as a measured prevalence.

Used for: a persimmon in every dooryard; Miyazaki Yasusada's encouragement; summer shade for the house.
