中国社会科学网, 古代江南圩田开发及其社会效应 (https://cssn.cn/lsx/lsx_zgs/202502/t20250217_5844872.shtml)

What it is: An article by 庄华峰 (Zhuang Huafeng), a professor of history at Anhui Normal University, "古代江南圩田开发及其社会效应" (The development of polder fields in premodern Jiangnan and its social effects), published in 中国社会科学报 (Chinese Social Sciences Today, the newspaper of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences) in February 2025 and carried on its portal 中国社会科学网. It traces how the Lake Tai plain of the lower Yangtze was turned into diked polder fields from the Five Dynasties and Song onward, quoting the Song officials who described the system.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the kind of source we want for how a wet-rice polder actually worked - channels inside the dike, gates and sluices on the outside, opened in drought and shut in flood - and for the spacing of the embankment channels that gives a polder landscape its grid, and it carries the Song-era wording rather than a modern paraphrase. Its limits are that it is a specialist's newspaper article rather than the primary texts themselves, so we are reading Song descriptions at one remove with no edition or chapter given; that it synthesizes seven centuries, so a detail is not tied to a date unless the quoted text ties it; and that this is Chinese Jiangnan standing in for our setting's rice country, which leans as much on Japan.

Used for: 「Within there are watercourses and canals; without there are gates and sluices. In drought the sluices are opened, drawing in the benefit of the river's water; in flood the sluices are shut, keeping out the harm of the river's water.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「中有河渠，外有门闸。旱则开闸引江水之利，涝则闭闸拒江水之害」) - channels inside, gates on the outside, opened in drought and shut in flood; the tangpu spacing 「a transverse embankment-channel (hengtang) every five or seven li, a longitudinal channel (zongpu) every seven or ten li」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「五里七里一横塘、七里十里一纵浦」)
