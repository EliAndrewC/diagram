koshoji-yoshizaki-taya

Kōshōji head temple (本山興正寺), 興正寺史話 55, 蓮如上人の行実 その二 ～吉崎での教化～ (2019) (in Japanese; https://koshoji.or.jp/shiwa_55.html)

What it is: One of a Shin Buddhist head temple's short history talks, on Rennyo's years at Yoshizaki from 1471, where a town grew up and several lodging houses (taya) were built for the priests stationed there, which also lodged visiting believers; the talk is signed 熊野恒陽 and dated 27 August 2019.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates priests' lodging houses at a temple to the 1470s, in Muromachi Japan. Its limits: Yoshizaki was no ordinary temple but the seat of the sect's head, where believers crowded in, so its lodging houses are a pilgrimage center's and not a sign of what an ordinary village or town temple had round it; it is the sect's own popular history; it gives only "several" houses, and it does not say they stood round the temple, lay out where they stood or say who lived in them besides the priests and visiting believers - the priests' wives are in the encyclopedia article on bōmori in Kotobank.

Used for: the priests' houses built round the Yoshizaki temple in the fifteenth century (religion-and-death.html).
