National Agriculture and Food Research Organization (NARO), Institute of Fruit Tree and Tea Science, 「3. How should wind-protection facilities be laid out?」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「3.防風施設の配置はどのようにしたらよいか」), in its manual of disaster measures for fruit trees, apples (in Japanese; https://www.naro.go.jp/laboratory/nifts/kajyusaigai/chogai/measures/apple/130662.html)

What it is: A page of a national agricultural research institute's manual of measures against wind and salt-wind damage in apple orchards, on how to lay out windbreak installations, which it illustrates with the windbreak forest: the main, secondary, auxiliary and sleeve belts, and their siting on broken ground and slopes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, from an official research body, the layout of the main windbreak against the prevailing wind and the secondary belts set mostly at right angles to it. It is present-day orchard practice, written for windbreak installations in general and laid out across an orchard's blocks rather than around a settlement. It is not a description of how pre-modern farmsteads or villages were sheltered: it carries the physical principle, not evidence that any period village was laid out this way.

Used for: the main windbreak against the prevailing wind, the secondary belts mostly at right angles to it (towns)
