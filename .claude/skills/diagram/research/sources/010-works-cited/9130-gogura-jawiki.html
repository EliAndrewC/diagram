What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the charitable granary, which carries within it the description of the gogura: an Edo-period store placed one to a village or to several, a temporary holding place for tax rice and other produce on its way out, and kept under strict guard.

Why it applies, and its limits: It identifies the building our fire posture says was protected before anything else, and confirms that the tax rice had a store of its own rather than sitting in a general warehouse. Its limits are that it is tertiary; that it describes the village store rather than one inside an intendant's compound, which is the attachment this record still cannot support; and that it gives no dimension.

Used for: the tax-rice store placed one to a village or to several, and kept under guard
