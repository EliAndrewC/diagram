atariya-nisho

Atariya Nōen, 「Vegetables' sunlight conditions」 (translated; original: 「野菜の日照条件」) (in Japanese; https://www.atariya.net/kiso/nisho.htm)

What it is: A page of home-garden basics from Atariya Nōen, a Japanese seed company, sorting vegetables into three classes by the hours of direct sun they need, with a table of 44 crops.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the hours in plain numbers - about 6 or more for sun crops, about 3 to 4 for half-shade crops, 1 to 2 for shade crops - and puts daikon, eggplant, cucumber and the beans among the first, turnip, komatsuna, taro, negi and ginger among the second, mitsuba, shiso and myoga among the third. Its limits are that it is a modern seed company's advice for modern cultivars, not an Edo farm manual; that its hours are round figures for good growth, not a measured threshold below which a crop fails; that it does not say which season the hours are counted in; and that only its sun class is a firm threshold - it says half-shade crops also grow under dappled light all day, so their 3 to 4 hours of direct sun is not a minimum. The page is in Shift_JIS, an older Japanese text encoding, and reads correctly only when decoded as such.

Used for: the hours of direct sun a kitchen bed needs (homesteads.html).
