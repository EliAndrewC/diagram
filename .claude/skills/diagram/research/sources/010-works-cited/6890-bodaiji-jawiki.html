bodaiji-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 菩提寺 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8F%A9%E6%8F%90%E5%AF%BA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the bodaiji (菩提寺), the temple whose sect a household follows generation after generation and at which it holds the memorial rites for its ancestors. It also covers the related danna-dera and the Edo-period danka system that bound each household to a temple.

Why it applies, and its limits: Our small urban temples are drawn as places with named patron households rather than as public monuments, and this is the article that defines that relationship. Its named examples are at the very top of society - the Tokugawa house's temples at Kan'eiji and Zojoji, the Date house's Zuihoji - so applying the pattern to the modest households of a town carries it down several tiers, and the registration system it describes is an Edo administrative institution, where an earlier setting inherits only the patronage. It is a tertiary summary of a religious institution, definitional rather than quantitative: it fixes what a small temple is for and who pays for it, and carries nothing about how large such a temple is, where it sits on a street, or how many a town holds.

Used for: the lineage temple 「a temple to whose doctrine a household has given its allegiance generation after generation, and at which it holds memorial rites for the enlightenment of its ancestors」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一家が代々その寺の宗旨に帰依して、先祖の菩提を弔う寺院」); 檀那寺 under the danka system
