kanazawa-teramachidai-plan

金沢市 (City of Kanazawa), 金沢市寺町台伝統的建造物群保存地区保存計画 ("Preservation plan for the Teramachidai preservation district for groups of traditional buildings", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://digilib.city.kanazawa.ishikawa.jp/preview/pdf/FXHmogAAA)

What it is: Kanazawa city's official preservation plan, a modern planning document written for the district's national selection in 2012 and since revised, for its Teramachidai temple district, one of the three temple quarters the third Maeda lord began gathering in 1615-1643 and which, by the plan's own account, took until the later seventeenth century to fill, describing the district's size, its temples, shrines and town houses, its two streets and how the precincts meet them, and listing its buildings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest description read of how a castle town's temple quarter was built: how many houses of worship to how much ground, the two ways a precinct met its street, and what stood on a precinct. It is a modern plan, not a period record: it describes the district as it survives today, after four great fires, and gives no plot's frontage or area. Its 22 ha is the preservation district's boundary drawn in 2012, with streets and town houses inside it, not the whole Edo quarter, which the plan counts at 59 temples in 1820; so its count of temples to ground is that of part of one great castle town's quarter, standing in for a temple quarter of any size.

Used for: 52 temples and 2 shrines on 22 ha; the walled row and the gate-land row; the townsmen's narrow deep lots; the graveyard behind the hall; a small shrine's buildings (religion-and-death)
