Government of Japan, Highlighting Japan (October 2021), 大阪城の石垣 ("The stone walls of Osaka Castle", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/202110/202110_07_jp.html)

What it is: The Japanese text of a government public-relations magazine article on the stone walls of Osaka Castle, quoting the castle museum's staff on the walls' stones and heights.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the tallest castle wall in Japan - the honmaru east wall, 34 m from its buried base stones and 24-25 m above the moat water - which is the ceiling for a Japanese castle's stone wall. The page claims the record only among Japan's castles, so it does not bound a Chinese city wall, a different form of rammed earth faced with brick. Its heights are a popular article's round figures, given by the museum's researcher, not a survey's. It is the shogunate's own rebuilt castle, an extreme, not a domain seat's wall.

Used for: the upper bound of a castle stone wall's height (cities/capitals)
