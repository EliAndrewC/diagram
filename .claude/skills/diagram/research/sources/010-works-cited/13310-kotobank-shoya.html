kotobank-shoya

Kotobank, 庄屋 ("shoya, the village headman", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and other entries (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BA%84%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the shoya, one of the names of the Edo-period village headman, carrying the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and other published dictionaries and encyclopedias on how the office was filled and what it did.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the office was sometimes filled by the landholding farmers' election or agreement but most often inherited by an old family of the village, which is what ties the headman's house to the village's old families. Its limits: dictionary entries that generalize across all of Tokugawa Japan, giving no proportion between elected and hereditary headmen and nothing about where the headman's house stood. The "old family" wording is the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's alone. The other entries say one or several powerful or locally dominant farmer families, name yearly rotation and the ballot beside inheritance, and Britannica's lists hereditary, lifetime and alternate-year terms without saying which was usual. Only the Heibonsha entry ties the early headmen to old lineages (descendants of medieval myōshu and estate officials).

Used for: the headman's office held by an old family of the village (homesteads)
