kato-1999-ittanbu-kukaku

Kato Toru, "Kochi seiri jidai no hyojun ittanbu kukaku no rekishiteki kosatsu" (A historical consideration of the standard one-tan plot in the era of land consolidation), Nogyo doboku gakkaishi (Journal of JSIDRE) 67(8), August 1999 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjsidre1965/67/8/67_8_843/_pdf)

What it is: A paper in the journal of Japan's society of irrigation, drainage and rural engineering, for the centenary of the 1899 land consolidation law, tracing the standard one-tan paddy plot back through Nishikimi Chodayu's replanning of Inazato, Gifu, in 1869-70 to the land survey and the ancient grid. It gives the plot, road, ditch and bund sizes of that replanning, and what share of the land later consolidation methods lost to bunds, ditches and roads.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its 1869 replanning is the earliest measured bund and farm-road width read, made on the eve of modern consolidation and near the setting's period. It is a new layout on a grid, not a survey of old irregular paddies, so its two-shaku bund and one-ken road bound the old field's widths without measuring them. That layout was planned for horse plowing, and the paper calls it generous: more roads than later layouts, ditches too wide and much land lost to both. So its one-ken road is a wide figure, not a typical one. The paper takes these widths from Ogawa's 1953 history, not from the plan itself. It covers one village in Gifu.

Used for: bunds of two shaku and farm roads of one ken in a paddy replanned in 1869 (fields)
