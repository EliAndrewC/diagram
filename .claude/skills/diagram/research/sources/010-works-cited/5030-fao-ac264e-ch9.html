fao-ac264e-ch9

FAO/NACA, Integrated Fish Farming in China, ch. 9 "Animal raising" (https://www.fao.org/4/ac264e/AC264E11.htm)

What it is: The animal-raising chapter, by Chen Yaowang, of the same report - NACA Technical Manual 7, Network of Aquaculture Centres in Asia and the Pacific, Bangkok, 1989. Where ch. 7 introduces the models, this chapter describes how the animals are actually housed and fed beside the water: where a pigsty stands, how its floor is laid so the waste runs the way it is meant to, and what the manure does once it reaches the pond.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the PURPOSE of the arrangement a map has to draw - the shed is on the dike so the excreta reach the pond - and it gives the mechanism by which manure is food rather than fouling, which is the question a reader of the map asks. Its limits are the same as its parent chapter's and no smaller: it is training-manual guidance from a Chinese research and training center in the 1980s, prescribing as much as it observes, with cement floors and discharge ditches that a premodern shed did not have, so we take the ARRANGEMENT and the reasoning from it and never a number, a rate or a claim about how common such a pond was before the modern period.

Used for: pigsties built on the pond dike so the excrement is flushed directly into the pond; manure fertilizing the water to produce plankton as natural fish food
