northampton-tannery-1996

Shaw, "The excavation of a late 15th- to 17th-century tanning complex at The Green, Northampton", Post-Medieval Archaeology 30(1), 1996 (https://doi.org/10.1179/pma.1996.002)

What it is: The excavation report on a late-15th to 17th-century tanning complex at The Green, Northampton, in Post-Medieval Archaeology: the circular and rectangular pits the tanneries were composed of, the cattle, horse and sheep hides the bone evidence says were worked in them, and the size of each of the tanneries found.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its pit counts are the only excavated measure our record has of how large an urban tanning works actually was, and the sentence we take from it makes the comparison for us - all of the tanneries were small, the largest running 36 to 37 pits - which is what keeps our town yard a seasonal works of a few pits rather than an industry. Its limits are that only the abstract is public, so nothing beyond those sentences is ours to claim; and that it is English tanning in a late medieval and early modern town, a different trade organization and a different scale of place from the county seats our maps draw, used as an upper bound rather than as a model.

Used for: the largest excavated urban tannery (the Western Tannery, 36-37 pits) as an upper comparison, and the abstract's own "all of the tanneries were small"
