zh.wikipedia 客家裔臺灣人 (Hakka Taiwanese), the section on deities (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%AE%A2%E5%AE%B6%E8%A3%94%E8%87%BA%E7%81%A3%E4%BA%BA)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Hakka Taiwanese, whose section on deities covers the tutelary Minzhu Gongwang and the temples that enshrine him.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states where a village's tutelary hall stands: usually built at the village's water mouth, the shuikou, with a named surviving instance built in 1760 on the bank of a stream in what is now Sanzhi, New Taipei. That is the geometry our village shrine follows, dated and on a watercourse rather than asserted in the abstract. Its limits are that it is a tertiary encyclopedia, and that its "usually built at the water mouth" is the encyclopedia asserting a custom rather than a survey of where such halls stand, so the record holds one dated instance and a generalization; that the deity so sited is a Hakka tutelary of Tingzhou and Zhangzhou origin rather than the earth god this record's own prose names; and that its instances are Taiwanese Hakka settlements, one regional tradition rather than a general Chinese rule.

Used for: the village tutelary's hall built at the shuikou, with a named 1760 instance on a stream bank
