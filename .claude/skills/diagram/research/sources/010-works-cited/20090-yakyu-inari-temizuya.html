yakyu-inari-temizuya

Higashimatsuyama City, 「Yakyu Inari Shrine's basin pavilion, with its basin」 (translated; original: 「箭弓稲荷神社手水舎付手水鉢」) (in Japanese; https://www.city.higashimatsuyama.lg.jp/soshiki/55/3760.html)

What it is: The city's entry for a designated cultural property (2015): a one-bay roofed pavilion carved with the date 1836 over a stone basin inscribed 1826, both given by one donor living in Edo, at Yakyu Inari Shrine in Higashimatsuyama, Saitama.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates a roofed pavilion at a shrine below the great ones before 1868, from the inscriptions themselves. The shrine drew a following across the Kantō and a donor from Edo, so it is not a village shrine, and the page gives no rank; it says nothing of how common such pavilions were. Updated 2024.

Used for: the earliest dated pavilion found at a shrine with a regional following, against the village shrine's unroofed basin (Religion and the dead).
