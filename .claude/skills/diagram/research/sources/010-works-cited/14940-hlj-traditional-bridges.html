hlj-traditional-bridges

Government of Japan, Highlighting Japan, September 2023, 日本の伝統的な橋の歴史と名橋の数々 ("The history of Japan's traditional bridges, and its famous bridges", title translated from the Japanese by this project), an interview with the bridge engineer Matsumura Hiroshi (https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/202309/202309_00_jp.html)

What it is: A government magazine interview with a former Osaka city bridge engineer and author on Japan's traditional bridges: that nearly all were timber girder bridges, why, how the castle towns built theirs, and where stone arches were the exception.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a specialist's summary of what bridges a Japanese castle town of the early modern period actually built, which is the question for a town's river crossing. It is an interview for a general audience, not a survey, and gives no count or size for any town's bridges. It speaks for Japan only, and its reasons why bridges were timber (steep, fast rivers an arch would obstruct, little wheeled transport) are Japanese conditions offered as the engineer's opinion. So "stone arches were the exception" holds for the setting's Japanese-modeled castle towns and not for its towns modeled on imperial China, where stone arch and stone beam bridges were common, above all in the canal towns of Jiangnan.

Used for: that the early-modern castle towns built timber post-and-girder bridges, and that stone arches were the exception (ways)
