hakubaku-omugi-ichinen

Hakubaku, おいしい大麦研究所, 日本の風土で育つ大麦の一年 ("The year of barley grown in Japan's climate", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.hakubaku.co.jp/omugi-lab/hyakka/oomugi-ichinen/)

What it is: A page by a Japanese barley-foods company describing barley's year in Japan month by month, from November sowing to June harvest, and the rotation of rice, barley and soybean.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the stages at which a barley field changes color. A company's general-audience page (2019) about today's farming, with no sources. Its months are a modern national average of today's varieties, and it says itself that timing varies by district. The 麦秋 season word it cites is an older witness to the gold field in early summer. It hedges the rotation as common rather than universal and gives it no history. It pairs the rotation with modern drainage pipes buried in the paddies, and today's rice-barley-soybean in drained paddies is largely the form that spread after the 1970s policy of switching paddies to other crops. So the page supports the rotation as practised now, not as premodern; a premodern rice-barley-soybean on rested paddy has to come from a source on the older practice.

Used for: the month barley heads and the month the field turns gold; the rice-barley-soybean rotation (fields)
