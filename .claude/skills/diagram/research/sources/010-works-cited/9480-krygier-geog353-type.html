John Krygier, Ohio Wesleyan University, GEOG 353 lecture notes on type and labeling (https://krygier.owu.edu/krygier_html/geog_353/geog_353_lo/geog_353_lo10.html)

What it is: Lecture notes on map type and labeling by John Krygier, co-author of the cartography textbook Making Maps whose guidelines QGIS cites for its ranked positions.

Why it applies, and its limits: They come from the author the placement order is attributed to, in his own teaching words, including that horizontal type is easiest to find and read. They are outline lecture notes (updated 2019) that follow chapter 11 of the textbook rather than the textbook itself, which is not publicly readable, and they state the rule without evidence. The rule is about Latin type in modern Western cartography; period Chinese and Japanese maps commonly set names in vertical columns, so it governs our captions' legibility, not a period look.

Used for: horizontal type as the easiest to read, the default for a label with no axis to follow (presentation)
