irikawa-kotobank

Kotobank, 入側 (irikawa) - Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten; Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (old edition), from its article 廊下 (corridor) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%85%A5%E5%81%B4)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the irikawa, the inner veranda-corridor of a shoin-style house, carrying the Shogakukan unabridged Japanese dictionary's definition of it as a passage one ken wide between the zashiki and the veranda, and an excerpt of the Heibonsha world encyclopedia's article on the corridor, on where corridors were built in Momoyama and Edo houses and where they were not.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a width for the veranda-corridor our residences draw and the social line across which corridors and wrap-around verandas were and were not built. Its limits are that a dictionary width is a norm and not a survey (one surviving house, Katsura, has a half-ken irikawa); that the dictionary's one-ken definition is undated and its only example of that sense is from a Meiji magazine (Fuzoku Gaho, 1902), so the page does not by itself tie the one-ken width to the Edo period; that the corridor excerpt is cut short on the page and names no author; and that both are tertiary summaries.

Used for: the irikawa one ken wide; corridors joining buildings in the mansions of shoguns and daimyo; almost no corridors in farmhouses, townhouses and middle and lower samurai houses, and the late-Edo veranda on two or three sides (buildings)
