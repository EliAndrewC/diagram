What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the Nanbu-Date border mounds, the earth mounds raised in the early Edo period to mark the boundary between Morioka domain and Sendai domain - a line of about 130 km running from the Ou mountains east to the Pacific - which the shogunate re-confirmed along with the two clans' holdings in 1642.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the fact that a domain boundary in this period was a made thing, marked on the ground by heaped earth, which is why such mounds can appear at the edge of a map. It is a tertiary summary whose details we take only where its own references support them, and it records one boundary in the far northeast standing for the general practice, so we draw the type of marker from it rather than any particular spacing.

Used for: the earth-mound boundary between Morioka and Sendai, reconfirmed 1642
