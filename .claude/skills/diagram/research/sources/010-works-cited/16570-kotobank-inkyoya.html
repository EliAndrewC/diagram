kotobank-inkyoya

Heibonsha, Sekai daihyakka jiten (the earlier edition), s.v. 隠居屋 ("inkyoya", the retirement house, title translated from the Japanese by this project), the passages on the retirement house quoted from its entries on retirement and on the house plot, on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%9A%A0%E5%B1%85%E5%B1%8B-1271853)

What it is: A short page gathering what an encyclopedia's other entries say about the retirement house, among them a passage from its entry on the house plot describing the early Edo period's registered plots, where a master's family lived in the main house and dependent small farmers in huts, gate-houses and retirement houses inside the same enclosure.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that in the early Edo period a plot could hold several families under several roofs while the registers counted it as one hearth - the one case found where a register's household and a map's houses would not match one to one. It is an excerpt of an excerpt, placed by its own entry in the early part of the period, before the dependents split off into households of their own; it says nothing of how many plots were like this, and the same passage is on the fuller kotobank page for the house plot. Japan alone.

Used for: the early Edo house plot holding dependents' huts, gate-houses and retirement houses, and the register counting it as one hearth (settlements)
