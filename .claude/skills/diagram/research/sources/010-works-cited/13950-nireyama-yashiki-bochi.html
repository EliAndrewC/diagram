nireyama-yashiki-bochi

"屋敷内の墓地" ("The graveyard within the house plot", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 楡影談, a local-history blog of the former Hatara district, 24 February 2019 (http://nireyama.main.jp/hatara/eid17.html)

What it is: A local historian's blog post on graves within or beside a farm household's plot, reporting Yanagita Kunio's and Takatori Masao's views and describing the writer's own family's three successive graveyards from the family's documents and the land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page found that says where on the plot such graves stood - a corner of the house plot, the oldest in the house's grove - and where they went when moved: not far, onto the household's own fields or woods within the hamlet. It is one family's case in one district of the Kanto plain, written by a descendant on a personal blog, and several of its points are the writer's surmise, marked as such on the page; Yanagita's regional distribution is reported secondhand.

Used for: where on and near the plot a household buried its dead, and where the form was common (religion and death)
