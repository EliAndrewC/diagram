kamigamo-shake-okeihan

Keihan group, 京都ツウのススメ 第152回 京の社家 ("Tips for Kyoto connoisseurs, no. 152: Kyoto's priest families", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.keihan.co.jp/navi/kyoto_tsu/tsu202103.html)

What it is: A railway company's popular guide (the Keihan group's 京都ツウのススメ series, written by Taniguchi Mayumi of the Kyoto guide company Rakutabi, March 2021) to the priest families' quarter at Kamigamo in Kyoto: when it formed, how many houses it held, and what a priest family's house looked like from the street.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in so many words that a priest family's house was walled with a gate, and dates the quarter. It is a 2021 tourist guide, not a survey or a record of the period. It describes the about thirty houses that survive today in a preserved district, so the wall and gate are an old form seen now, and its figure of about 200 houses in the late 17th century is only "said to be". It speaks of one quarter, and that quarter housed the hereditary priest families of one of the great shrines near the capital; a village or small-town shrine's priest household would have been humbler.

Used for: a priest family's house enclosed by an earthen wall with a gate at its entrance (religion-and-death)
