ja.wikipedia 高床式倉庫 (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%AB%98%E5%BA%8A%E5%BC%8F%E5%80%89%E5%BA%AB)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the raised-floor storehouse, built on posts sunk in the ground: why its floor was high, its rat-guards, its prehistoric remains in Japan, and where it was still built in modern times.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the reasons a granary's floor was raised, rats and damp, and where in Japan the form lasted, which is what a granary drawn on posts is checked against. It is tertiary, and its Japanese cases are prehistoric sites and the islands and far north where the form survived; it says nothing of the earth-walled storehouses of the Edo mainland, and almost nothing of Chinese or Korean granaries, so for the Chinese side of the setting it gives the reason for the raised floor and not whether one was built.

Used for: the raised floor against rats and damp, and the storehouse on posts on Amami Oshima, Hachijojima and among the Ainu (buildings)
