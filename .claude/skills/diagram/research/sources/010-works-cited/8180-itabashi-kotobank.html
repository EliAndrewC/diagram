itabashi-kotobank

Encyclopedia entry s.v. 板橋 ("plank bridge", translated), aggregated at kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/板橋)

What it is: The dictionary and encyclopedia entry for 板橋 (itabashi) at kotobank, which aggregates the major Japanese reference works, giving the word's plain sense: a bridge made of boards.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it to fix the name of the small crossing our maps draw over a farm ditch, so that the glyph and the word a reader clicks mean the same object - a planked deck and not an earthen one. Its limits are that it is a dictionary definition and nothing more: it gives no construction, no size and no picture of how such a bridge was used, so everything else our entry says about the plank comes from elsewhere. Its datings are the national dictionary's earliest example of the word, a poem in the Fuboku wakashō of about 1310 about a plank bridge in a misty valley, and the reference works' note that the Engyō-bon Heike monogatari has Yoritomo camp at "the Itabashi" in 1180 - a place name in a war tale's telling of that year, not a document made in it, and one that another text gives as Matsuhashi, so that some place it elsewhere. The encyclopedias say, by tradition, that the place took its name from a plank bridge carrying the old Edo-Kawagoe road over the Shakujii River. So every dated plank on the page crosses a river or a valley on a road, and none is the crossing of a farm ditch, which the page never shows. The Jitsū entry adds the same word, read banqiao, in a Tang poem (Wen Tingyun's "Shangshan zaoxing"), showing the word was used in China, again with no size or setting.

Used for: what an itabashi is: a bridge made of boards; that the word was in use in Japan by about 1310
