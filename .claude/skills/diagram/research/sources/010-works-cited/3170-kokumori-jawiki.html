kokumori-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 石盛 ("grain-yield assessment", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%9F%B3%E7%9B%9B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on kokumori, the assessed standard yield per tan set for each grade of paddy, dry field and house plot in the early-modern cadastral surveys; an encyclopedia article.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Taiko-survey paddy ladder (upper paddy 1.5 koku, middle 1.3, lower 1.1 per tan, stepping down 0.2 a grade; house plots 1.2; dry field left to the surveyor's discretion), the Edo-period dry-field rule (upper dry field 1.2, stepping down 0.2 a grade), and the mid-Edo reset (house plots and upper dry field set to lower paddy's 1.1, so middle dry field 0.9). It derives the paddy rates from a sampled crop-cut hulled to half. Its limits: these are tax-assessment rates, not measured yields. They were bent by the surveyor's judgment and by politics, and by the later Edo period actual harvests often exceeded them. Dry-field rates are values in rice-equivalent koku, not harvests of millet, barley or soybeans. The paddy and dry-field rungs come from different surveys. And it is an unreferenced encyclopedia stub, good for the shape of the ladder rather than as the authority for a number.

Used for: the 1.3 koku/tan figure and the paddy-over-dry-field value ladder (fields); the paddy-to-dry-field assessed ratio (middle paddy 1.3 against middle dry field 1.0 early-Edo, or 0.9 mid-Edo) behind the GUESS of about 0.13-0.18 acre of dry field a person for the coarse-grain third of the diet
