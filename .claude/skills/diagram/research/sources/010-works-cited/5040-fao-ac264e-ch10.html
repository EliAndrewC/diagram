fao-ac264e-ch10

FAO/NACA, Integrated Fish Farming in China, ch. 10 "Design and construction of an integrated fish farm" (https://www.fao.org/4/ac264e/AC264E13.htm)

What it is: The design chapter of the same report (NACA Technical Manual 7, Bangkok, 1989): how a fish farm is laid out - the widths of dikes, the arrangement of inflow and outflow canals, where the animal houses stand relative to the ponds and the roads.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only source read that puts a NUMBER on the thing a dike-pond map draws at every parcel edge - how wide a dike must be, and how much wider when it carries a pigsty rather than only a footpath. That is a constructional requirement rather than a regional fashion, so it travels better than most of this report's figures. Two limits, both real: it is modern design guidance for farms being built in the 1980s, not a survey of what old dikes measured, so it tells us what a dike carrying a shed NEEDS and not what any particular premodern dike WAS; that only the FLOOR of its 5-10 m range bears on a premodern map, because the quoted clause bundles pigsties with "cow sheds piping, or traffic" and the top of the range is plausibly set by carts and pipework a hand-piled mulberry dike never carried, so the source supports "at least 5 m, because it carries a shed" rather than a two-sided corroboration; and its account of pig waste going to septic tanks for fermentation before use describes a modern sanitary practice that sits oddly beside ch. 7's direct-flow shed, so the two are recorded as a tension rather than reconciled.

Used for: a dike carrying a pigsty or cow shed runs 5-10 m wide against 5 m or less for a plain inter-pond dike; duck and goose pens built separately along the dikes; pigsties on dikes or on high ground close to the pond
