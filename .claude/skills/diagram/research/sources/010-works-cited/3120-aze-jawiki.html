What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the aze, the paddy bund of heaped mud that divides the fields and holds their water, with the named bund along an open channel.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the bund's purpose - that the water not leak out - and names the bund on the ditch side, which is why a basin on these maps is sealed on every side. A tertiary summary, a stub whose definitions are uncited, used for the definitions only. It describes today's Japanese paddies: its dimensions (a 300-600mm crown, 1:1 slopes under modern field consolidation) and materials (PVC bund sheets, concrete bund blocks) are modern and are not carried to these maps. Its tie to the premodern era is its Yayoi-Kofun section, cited to two archaeology books, which dates bunded small-plot paddies to the Early Yayoi period.

Used for: the bund holding the paddy's water on every side; the field path running on the bund at the plot boundary; a bund of no set shape outside modern works, repaired every year, and set on the boundary between owners (archetypes)
