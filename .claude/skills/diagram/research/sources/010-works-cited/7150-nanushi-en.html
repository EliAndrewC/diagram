"Nanushi", en.wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanushi)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the nanushi (名主), the Edo-period village headman who administered a village under a district magistrate, with a number of group heads (kumigashira) and one or more peasant representatives (hyakushodai) beneath him - one nanushi to a village.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for that ratio, one headman to one village, which is the general rule our settlements follow. It is a tertiary summary, and it has a specific gap that matters here: it never says whether its "village" is the natural cluster of houses or the larger administrative unit that could contain several, which is the very question at issue, so it fixes the ratio and leaves the unit open.

Used for: one headman per (administrative) village as the general rule - and recorded as NOT resolving whether "village" there means the natural hamlet or the unit containing several, which is the question at issue
