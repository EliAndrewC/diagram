kotobank-zakoba

Kotobank, 雑喉場魚市場 ("The Zakoba fish market", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei entry (https://kotobank.jp/word/雑喉場魚市場-3075924)

What it is: The historical-gazetteer entry on Osaka's Zakoba fish market: its site facing the Hyakkenbori canal, its standing as one of Osaka's three great markets beside the rice and produce markets, its history from the seventeenth century, and the other fish-trade sites of the city, among them a river-fish market at the Kyobashi bridgehead.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a standard historical gazetteer entry on a real city market, cited to the market's own history and the city's. It describes Osaka, one of the largest commercial cities of Edo Japan, whose fish market held the right to all fresh fish sold in the city; a lesser city would keep fewer and smaller markets. It places the fish-trade sites on particular canals and a bridgehead, but names the rice and produce markets only by district (Dojima, Tenma), so it does not itself say the produce market stood on the water; and it says where the markets stood, not how they were built. Kotobank prints only part of the entry; the rest, behind JapanKnowledge, was not read.

Used for: a city keeping its fish and produce markets as separate sites on its water, and a separate river-fish market (cities/river-cities)
