yanagawa-jogo-mizuochi

Yanagawa City, 掘割、城堀の水落ち ("The canals, and draining the castle moat", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.city.yanagawa.fukuoka.jp/kurashi/dorosuiro/suiro/suiro_horiwari.html)

What it is: A page of the Yanagawa city government (Fukuoka) on the yearly drying of the canals and the draining of the moat ringing the old castle town: how the moat is filled and emptied, and what the dredged mud was used for.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a castle-town moat that is filled from a river through a sluice and holds stored water, and canal mud spread on paddies and fields as fertilizer - the moat as reservoir and its link to the fields. The water the moat stores is named as drinking and household water, and when the moat is drained that water is let out to the Ariake Sea, not onto fields; the page never says the moat watered fields - its one tie to the fields is the mud. It is one town on a low delta whose whole ground is laced with canals, so its moat is part of a drainage and water-supply network unlike a hill castle's; the text is adapted from a local-history book (Uchiyama Kazuyuki's entry in the city's 新柳川明証図会) and dates nothing earlier than the late Meiji period besides today - that the practice is older, from the Edo period, is presumed, not shown; and the fertilizer and the after-harvest timing are said of canal-drying in general, not of the castle moat.

Used for: a castle-town moat holding stored water, and canal mud spread on the fields (cities/hinterland)
