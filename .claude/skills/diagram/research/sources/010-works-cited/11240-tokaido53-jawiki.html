What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the fifty-three post stations of the Tokaido, with the distances between them and a paragraph on how many inns the stations held.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives inn counts for 17 of one highway's 53 stations, the largest and those flanking Hakone, from Miya's 247 down to Fujisawa's 45; the other 36 stations are not given, so 45 is the smallest count listed, not the floor, and the small stations ran below it. It is a tertiary summary whose counts all cite one 2001 popular reference dictionary (Asai Kenji, 『道と路がわかる辞典』), not a period record; the page gives no year for them (they are most likely the shogunate's survey of 1843, late Edo, near the peak of travel). The total of nearly three thousand is hedged "is said", and the counts are the Tokaido's, the busiest highway (Miya's swollen by the Shichiri ferry), so they are an upper range.

Used for: the inn counts of the Tokaido's stations (towns)
