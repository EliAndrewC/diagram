kotobank-chugen

Kotobank, 中間 ("chugen", title translated), entries from several Japanese dictionaries (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%AD%E9%96%93-97157)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering dictionary entries on the chugen, the attendants of a warrior household ranked below the foot soldiers, among them the Yamakawa concise dictionary of Japanese history.

Why it applies, and its limits: The Yamakawa entry, a reference work by historians, says the Edo-period chugen lived together in large rooms in their lord's residence. It names the room (the chugen room, one of the large rooms) but gives no plan, no size and no count of the men in a room; its only number is the shogunate's own 540-560 chugen. It speaks of the residences of the domain lords (藩邸), not of a country office.

Used for: servants living together in large common rooms in a lord's residence (Estates and other compounds)
