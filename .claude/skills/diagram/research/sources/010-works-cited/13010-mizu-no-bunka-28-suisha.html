mizu-no-bunka-28-suisha

Mizkan Water Culture Center, 水の文化 no. 28, 働く水車が伝える水のポテンシャル ("The potential of water that working water wheels convey", title translated from the Japanese by this project), with Kosaka Katsunobu (https://www.mizu.gr.jp/kikanshi/no28/03.html)

What it is: A feature in the Mizkan Water Culture Center's magazine, built on an interview with Kosaka Katsunobu, a local historian of the Tama district who leads the Japan Society for the History of Industrial Technology's water-wheel group, on the history of power water wheels in Japan and a working mill in Mitaka.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest readable account of who built a village mill, why and where: rice pounding driven by brewing and castle-town demand from the mid-Edo period, mills in the villages round Edo's highway gates, a permit needed for each on shogunal land, 33 on the Musashino plateau in 1788, owners mostly wealthy farmers and village officials, and one mill 200 m below the headman's. It is a magazine piece about the Edo hinterland, the most mill-dense country in Japan, and its figures are the historian's rather than footnoted.

Used for: what a village mill was for, who owned it, how many stood on one plateau, and a mill's mortars (water)
