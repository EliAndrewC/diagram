historist-kido

Historist (Yamakawa Shuppan), 木戸(きど) ("kido", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Japanese history glossary (https://www.historist.jp/word_j_ki/entry/031353/)

What it is: A short dictionary entry, reprinted on the history publisher Yamakawa's Historist site from its Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten (revised edition, 2016), on the kido: the gate of a stockade or fort in antiquity and the middle ages, and the gate across the streets of an early-modern town, with its watch house and its keeper.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what a town's kido was for, that a watch house went with it, and that some castle towns (Sendai, Matsuyama) set them only at their entrances and at the line between the samurai and townsman quarters. It is a brief tertiary dictionary entry on Japan alone, with no sizes and no count.

Used for: the kido and its watch house, shut at night; kido at a town's entrances only in some castle towns (defenses)
