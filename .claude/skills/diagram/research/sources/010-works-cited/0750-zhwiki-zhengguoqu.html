zhwiki-zhengguoqu

zh.wikipedia 郑国渠 (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/郑国渠)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Zhengguo canal of 246 BC in Shaanxi: its head set where the Jing River leaves the northern hills, and the three scholarly views of what its head works were - a large earth-and-stone dam, stone-filled crib baskets or a low weir raising the level, or no dam at all and a weirless diversion using the river bend.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the two intake forms our maps roll between - a low weir of stone-filled cribs, or a bare intake off a river bend - are both readings of the same ancient work, and it places the head where the river leaves the hills, as the Japanese rule does. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, that the form is expressly disputed, and that the work is a great state canal rather than a village ditch.

Used for: the canal head where the river issues from the hills; the disputed head-works form - dam, stone-filled cribs or low weir, or weirless diversion off the bend
