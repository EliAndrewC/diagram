kotobank-tsujiido

Kotobank, 辻井戸 ("tsuji-ido, the roadside well", title translated from the Japanese by this project), quoting Heibonsha's Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (old edition), entry 辻 (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%BE%BB%E4%BA%95%E6%88%B8-1369863)

What it is: The Kotobank page for 辻井戸, which quotes the Heibonsha encyclopedia's entry on the tsuji - the crossroads or wayside - where it names the shared well standing by the road among the facilities passers-by used in common.

Why it applies, and its limits: A modern Japanese encyclopedia's entry (Heibonsha's Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, a tertiary summary; Kotobank shows only an excerpt, with no author's name) naming the roadside communal well as a recognized kind with its own word. It is one sentence, about Japanese wayside facilities in general; the sentence itself carries no date, and the customs cited beside it are Edo-period Kyoto (the 1670s to the 1700s), so it bears on pre-Meiji Japan and says nothing of China. It says nothing of how common such wells were, how they were built, or whether villages had them.

Used for: the communal well standing at the roadside (urban-features)
