What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Nisshinkan (日新館), the domain school of Aizu, which stood immediately west of Aizu-Wakamatsu castle on a ground of about 120 ken by 60 ken and was among the foremost such schools in the country, with a recitation hall for boys up to fifteen, a swimming ground and an observatory. The article also covers the modern reconstruction, which serves as museum and as archery and martial-arts hall.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the dimensions of a domain school's ground - about 120 by 60 ken for the original Aizu school - which sets the size band a school occupies on our capital maps, and it shows archery and martial halls standing on such a ground. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary carrying the encyclopedia's own notice that it lacks sufficient sources, which matters because we take a dimension from it; that Aizu's was one of the foremost domain schools in the country, so it bounds the top of the range; that the halls it names belong to the 1987 reconstruction rather than the original; and that it states no rule pairing a lecture hall with a drill hall.

Used for: the reconstructed school's 弓道場・武道場; the lecture-hall pairing as doctrine is not stated
