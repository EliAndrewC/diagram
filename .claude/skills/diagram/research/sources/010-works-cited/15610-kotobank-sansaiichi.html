kotobank-sansaiichi

Kotobank, 三斎市 ("sansai-ichi, the three-day market", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the entries of the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), the Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten and others (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/三斎市)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering Japanese reference works' entries on the sansai-ichi, the periodic market held three days a month from the late Heian period, which spread across the country's estates in the Kamakura period and gave way to the six-day market in the later Muromachi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where such a market was held - at the gates of temples and shrines, at landing places and road junctions, and near an estate's office or its steward's residence - and that a region's market days were set against its central market's so neighbors did not compete. It is a set of short dictionary entries, a tertiary summary: the Nipponica entry is signed by Sasaki Ginya, a historian of medieval markets, but the staggering of market days comes only from the unsigned Yamakawa entry, which names no market and no schedule, so it carries the pattern and not a spacing or a count. It describes the medieval market, before the early-modern market town; it gives no size of a market ground.

Used for: where a medieval market was held, and how market days were staggered between neighboring markets (towns)
