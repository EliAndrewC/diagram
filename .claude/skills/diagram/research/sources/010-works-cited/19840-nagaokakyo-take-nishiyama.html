nagaokakyo-take-nishiyama

Nagaokakyo City Lifelong Learning Division, Cultural Properties Section, "Exhibition corner news no. 50: the bamboo of Otokuni and life in the Nishiyama hills" (in Japanese; https://www.city.nagaokakyo.lg.jp/cmsfiles/contents/0000005/5235/hurusatofile50.pdf)

What it is: A two-page exhibition leaflet of 2012 from the city of Nagaokakyo, southwest of Kyoto, on the bamboo of the Otokuni district and village life in the hills to its west, drawn from local documents and an early Edo-period screen painting of the countryside round Kyoto.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the city's historians describing a named early-Edo screen (the Rakugai-zu in the Kobe City Museum) in which settlements are drawn ringed by bamboo groves, and the Edo-period bamboo tax the district's villages paid, so it dates a bamboo thicket round a settlement to before 1868. It speaks of one district that was a bamboo specialist, so it is weak evidence of how common such thickets were elsewhere; a screen is a picture, not a survey, and the leaflet reproduces it too small to check; it does not say who owned the groves.

Used for: bamboo groves round a settlement before modern times (vegetation)
