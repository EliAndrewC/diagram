katsushika-suijin

Katsushika City (Tokyo), "葛飾区史" (History of Katsushika Ward), chapter 5 section 2, "水への畏れ: 水神の祭り" (awe of the water: the water-god festivals) (in Japanese; https://www.city.katsushika.lg.jp/history/history/5-2-7-312.html)

What it is: A section of the ward government's published history of Katsushika, a low-lying district of rivers east of central Tokyo, on the water-god shrines of its villages and the festivals the boatmen held for them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page found that ties a shrine ashore to river boatmen by name: the boatmen of the night-soil and sand boats led the water-god festival at one village and kept a Suitengu at another. It describes the festivals as they were in the early 20th century (it names an ex-voto of 1928), in one district, and gives no plan or size for the shrines or their place relative to a landing.

Used for: the water god worshipped in nearly every village, and kept by river boatmen (river cities)
