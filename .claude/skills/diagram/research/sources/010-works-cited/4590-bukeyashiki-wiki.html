bukeyashiki-wiki

Japanese Wikipedia, 武家屋敷 ("samurai residence", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%AD%A6%E5%AE%B6%E5%B1%8B%E6%95%B7)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the buke-yashiki (武家屋敷), the samurai residence of an early modern castle town. It sets out the zoning of such a town: upper samurai in hedged and walled plots inside the middle moat near the castle, and the row houses and small plots of ashigaru and other low-ranking retainers arranged outside them, often massed at the town's entrances for defense.

Why it applies, and its limits: This is the source for the rule our samurai wards follow, that rank sets distance from the castle and that ranks of small uniform housing belong on the outer fringe of the ward. That is a rule at the scale of the town: at the scale of one compound the article itself records row houses (nagaya) within daimyo households, at home and in Edo, and nagaya gates at senior retainers' houses, so it does not rule nagaya out of a great house's fence. It is a tertiary summary and gives no plot dimensions, setbacks or counts, so it decides the pattern while the measurements come from elsewhere, and it describes the Edo-period castle town specifically rather than samurai housing in every era. Its section on retainers' houses (侍屋敷) carries a banner (May 2025) saying it cites no sources, and that section is where the finer gradient lives: that rank falls step by step with distance from the lord's seat, that ashigaru housing massed at the town's entrances for defense, where karo lived, and that senior houses had a shikidai. Only the lead's sentence, upper samurai inside the middle moat and ashigaru row houses outside them, is cited, to a page of Japan's National Institute for Land and Infrastructure Management (国土技術政策総合研究所), the closer source for that pattern.

Used for: the buke-yashiki plot program and the perimeter nagaya; setback and enclosure conventions; the shikidai of the senior retainer's house, and karo living near the lord's residence or inside the castle (buildings)
