kotobank-kashi

Kotobank, 河岸（かし） ("river landing", title translated) - 改訂新版 世界大百科事典 ("Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, revised edition", title translated), the entry by 川名登 with its etymology by 保立道久, and other reference entries (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B2%B3%E5%B2%B8-459508)

What it is: The kotobank reference page for the kashi, the Edo-period river landing, carrying the entries of Japanese reference encyclopedias: the bank where cargo was landed, the markets that formed there, its standing as a town or village, and the kashi wholesaler who ran the landing for fees.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the landing was a place of business run by its wholesalers rather than a public quay, which is how this page describes it.

Used for: the kashi wholesaler's loading, storage and fees; a landing as a town or village with its market, its boatmen and laborers, its tea houses and inns, and the word's origin in the mooring post (cities/river-cities)
