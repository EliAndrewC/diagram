rinya-satoyama-junkan

Forestry Agency of Japan, 「Using satoyama woods for fuel, charcoal and mushroom logs」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「里山林の薪炭や、きのこ生産資材としての利用」) (https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/j/tokuyou/jyunkanriyou/index.html)

What it is: A page of the national Forestry Agency describing the broadleaf woods near villages that were cut for fuel and charcoal and regrew from the stump, and the age at which each kind of wood was cut.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in one sentence what a worked village fuel wood looked like - thin, low, many-stemmed trees cut every twenty to thirty years. It is a modern government page promoting renewed use of these woods, with no figures for stems or crowns and no dates for the old practice.

Used for: the cutting cycle and the thin, low, many-stemmed look of a worked fuel wood (vegetation)
