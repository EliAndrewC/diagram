ehime-kenshi-yashikidori

愛媛県史 民俗 上 ("History of Ehime Prefecture, Folklore, vol. 1", title translated), 1983, chapter 1, section 3, "屋敷取り" ("Laying out the house lot", title translated), in the prefecture's database えひめの記憶 (in Japanese; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/49/view/6470)

What it is: The folklore volume's account of how a house lot was chosen, cut out of a slope and laid out in Ehime's farming, mountain and fishing villages, with the lots' usual areas and a 1924 survey of how fifty farm lots in one district of Imabari were used.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sizes whole house lots and says how much of one went to buildings and the drying yard, which bounds what was left for a vegetable bed, and it records a hillside house with vegetable ground all round it and no boundary. Its limits: its figures are Taisho-era and later (the 1924 survey, a lot map of 1872, recollections), from one prefecture of Shikoku; the survey's table of land uses is not in the online text, so its per-use areas cannot be read; and it never sizes the vegetable bed itself.

Used for: the area of a house lot and its share left after buildings and the drying yard; a vegetable field round a hillside house with no special boundary (Homesteads)
