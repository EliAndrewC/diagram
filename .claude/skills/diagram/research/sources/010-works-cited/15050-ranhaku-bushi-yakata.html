ranhaku-bushi-yakata

Ranzan Town, Ranzan-machi Web Hakubutsushi vol. 6, from the Ranzan-machi Shi (town history), "Things related to warriors: the warrior's residence" (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/10_158.html)

What it is: A page of Ranzan town's (Saitama) web edition of its 1968 town history (Ranzan-machi Shi), explaining the place-names left by medieval warrior residences and paraphrasing scholars' reconstruction of a powerful Kanto warrior's residence and the farms around it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes, part by part, what a rural warrior's residence held - moat and bank, a gate with a lookout, servants' row houses, a stable, the main house, a riding ground, a storehouse for rent rice - and the paddy it worked at its gate. It is a 1968 free paraphrase of unnamed scholars' reconstruction of a large Kamakura-era Kanto residence, drawn from period documents and paintings, not a survey or an excavation, and the page itself says its own village's residences, if any, were smaller. Excavation since then has found that most square moated residences still visible on the ground were built in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, so its moat, bank and 150-200 m square site are the older textbook picture and fit a later-medieval residence better than a Kamakura one.

Used for: what a medieval country warrior's residence held and how it sat among its fields (buildings)
