ranhaku-bushi-yakata

Ranzan Town, Ranzan-machi Web Hakubutsushi vol. 6, from the Ranzan-machi Shi (town history), "Things related to warriors: the warrior's residence" (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/10_158.html)

What it is: A page of Ranzan town's (Saitama) web edition of its town history, explaining the place-names left by medieval warrior residences and paraphrasing scholars' reconstruction of a powerful Kanto warrior's residence and the farms around it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes, part by part, what a rural warrior's residence held - moat and bank, a gate with a lookout, servants' row houses, a stable, the main house, a riding ground, a storehouse for rent rice - and the paddy it worked at its gate. It is a free paraphrase of unnamed scholars' reconstruction of a large Kamakura-era residence, not a survey or an excavation, and the page itself says its own village's residences, if any, were smaller.

Used for: what a medieval country warrior's residence held and how it sat among its fields (buildings)
