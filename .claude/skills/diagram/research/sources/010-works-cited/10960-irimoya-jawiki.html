ja.wikipedia 入母屋造 ("hip-and-gable roof construction", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%85%A5%E6%AF%8D%E5%B1%8B%E9%80%A0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the irimoya roof - a hipped roof with a small gable at each end of the ridge - its forms, its standing as the most formal roof, and where ordinary houses used it.

Why it applies, and its limits: One sentence says in which districts ordinary farmhouses took the irimoya roof, which is what places it on a map. Its limits: a tertiary summary flagged on the page itself for possible original research, and the sentence's own reference is Kon Wajirō's 1943 revision of his 1922 book on Japanese folk houses, so it describes the houses standing in the early twentieth century, many of them built or altered after the Edo period. It speaks of Japan only - mountain districts near Kyoto and in Kai, Sagami and Musashi, named as where such houses are many, not counted - and says nothing of Chinese farmhouses.

Used for: where ordinary folk houses were roofed irimoya (homesteads)
