furusato-ikiru-tsuijimatsu

Ie-no-Hikari Association, 『にっぽん農紀行 ふるさとに生きる』, 「The noutegori pruning that keeps the traditional tsuijimatsu landscape」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「伝統的景観の築地松をまもる陰手刈りの技」) (https://furusato-ikiru.com/chapter/shimane/detail-888/)

What it is: The page for a short documentary film, from a rural-life publisher's series on Japan's farming districts, about the clipped black-pine homestead groves (tsuijimatsu) of the Izumo plain in Shimane and the craftsmen who prune them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a Japanese homestead grove that is kept today as a clipped wall of a single species: black pines on the west and north of the house, cut to a set height every four or five years, and it gives the grove's traditional origin in pines and bamboo planted to bind an earth bank raised against floods. It is popular writing for a general audience, and the origin is given as what is said rather than as documented history, with no date. It describes the present-day grove. Some of what it says is modern: it gives protecting the pines from pine wilt, a twentieth-century pest, as one reason for the pruning. By its own account, the bank was first planted with bamboo as well as pine, so the single-species wall is the grove as it stands now, not necessarily as it was first planted. That this is the only Japanese homestead grove of one species is our reading, not the page's.

Used for: the clipped single-species pine wall as one form of the windbreak (vegetation)
