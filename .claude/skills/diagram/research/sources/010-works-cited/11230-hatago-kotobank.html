hatago-kotobank

Kotobank, s.v. 旅籠 (hatago; the Kyodo News glossary, the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha Sekai dai-hyakka jiten; in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%97%85%E7%B1%A0-114641)

What it is: The Kotobank dictionary page gathering several Japanese reference works' entries on the hatago, the inn of the Edo highways: the word's origin, the average number per post town, and the inn's building set against the lord's lodging.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the average count per post town on two highways and says what a hatago's building was. Its limits are that the average is a news agency's glossary line, hedged "is said", with no year or count behind it. It is also an average over the two main highways only, and it hides a wide spread: the same page's Mypedia entry has some stations with over a hundred inns. So it gives the order of size of inns at a main-highway station, not the count for a given town or for a town on a lesser road. The building description comes from an encyclopedia entry by the Edo-highway historian Maruyama Yasunari, not from a survey of buildings.

Used for: 55 inns a station on the Tokaido and 27 on the Nakasendo; the hatago as a reduced honjin without gate, formal entrance or raised room, in three sizes (towns)
