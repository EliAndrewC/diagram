kotobank-haka

Inokuchi Shoji, s.v. 墓 ("grave", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho (Nipponica, Shogakukan), with other dictionary entries, on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A2%93-599723)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of the entries on the grave; the passage used is the "graves of Japan" section of the Nipponica article by Inokuchi Shoji, a folklorist of funerary custom.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that formerly many households each held their own graveyard, or a branch house set its grave beside the main house's, that some set the grave within their own house land, and that from Meiji the graveyards were often merged - the household grave as a real, once-common form. It is a general encyclopedia's summary (Nipponica, first published 1984-94), resting on twentieth-century folklore fieldwork, so its "formerly" looks back from the modern village rather than being dated from period records. It speaks of Japan only; nothing in it bears on the Chinese-modelled places (for those, the same kotobank page's Heibonsha World Encyclopedia section on China, by Nishiwaki Tsuneki, says graves moved from lineage cemeteries to family graveyards from the late Warring States). It gives no share, no region within Japan and no date beyond "formerly", and nothing on where on the plot the grave stood.

Used for: the household's own grave and the house-plot grave (religion and death)
