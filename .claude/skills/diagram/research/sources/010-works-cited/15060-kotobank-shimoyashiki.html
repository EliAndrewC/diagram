kotobank-shimoyashiki

Kotobank, 下屋敷 ("lower residence", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - entries from the Digital Daijisen, the Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nihon Daihyakka Zensho, the Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten and others (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%8B%E5%B1%8B%E6%95%B7-75422)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the shimo-yashiki, the Edo-period secondary residence of a daimyo or rich merchant, carrying several dictionary entries; the Yamakawa entry says what one held.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what a lord's suburban estate was for and what it held - gardens for leisure, vegetable gardens and bamboo groves supplying the main residence - and that lords and upper retainers kept such houses in other castle towns too. The entries are short dictionary summaries, a tertiary source, and give no size for an estate; they describe daimyo estates at Edo, far larger households than a single samurai's. Outside Edo the Yamakawa entry names the castle-town case only as something found. The Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei entry gives one: a street of samurai houses at Sumoto called Shimoyashiki, three ken wide and 237 ken long, lined on both sides by the Inada house's lower residence and its retainers' houses. It does not say what those held.

Used for: what a lord's suburban estate held: gardens, vegetable plots and bamboo (buildings)
