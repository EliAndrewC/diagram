ja.wikipedia 寛永寺 ("Kan'ei-ji", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/寛永寺)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Kan'ei-ji at Ueno in Edo, the Tendai temple founded in 1625 as the Tokugawa house's prayer temple (a funerary temple of the shoguns only from Iemitsu's funeral in 1651) and the guardian of the castle's northeast, with its history, its precinct at the height of the Edo period and the buildings that stood there before the 1868 battle burned them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a great city temple's precinct, its count of sub-temples, and the size and setting of its lost main hall. It is a tertiary summary, and the passages that give these figures carry no footnote of their own (the article's sources are the 1830s Edo meisho zue, a Tokyo National Museum page and a tourist site), so the numbers are the article's and untraced; the 305,000 tsubo (about 100 ha) is the whole temple ground at its late-Edo height, with its shogunal mausolea, sub-temples and groves, not one walled court. A shogunal temple in the capital is at the top of the range, far above a provincial city's.

Used for: a precinct of 305,000 tsubo with 36 sub-temples; a main hall 45.5 by 42 m inside a square cloister, the abbot's quarters behind it (religion-and-death)
