miyazu-teramachi

Miyazu City, 宮津城下町を歩く (10) 寺町の成り立ち ("Walking the Miyazu castle town, 10: how the temple quarter came to be") (in Japanese; https://www.city.miyazu.kyoto.jp/site/citypro/5091.html)

What it is: A Miyazu city office page on the temple quarter of the former castle town of Miyazu, Kyoto prefecture, credited to the city's social education division (series 宮津城下町を歩く, part 10, 2021). It gives the twelve temples grouped in the southwest of the former town and a count by sect that comes to thirteen (six Jōdo, two Jōdo Shin, three Nichiren, one Rinzai, one Sōtō), apparently counting Chigenji on the Kyoto road with them. It also gives their place at the town's edge and the view that the quarter was laid out with the castle town, under the patronage of the daimyo Kyōgoku Takahiro.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a small castle town's temple count and where they stood. Its limits: a castle town, not a county seat. A temple quarter planned at the town's edge under a daimyo's patronage is a Japanese castle-town practice with no close counterpart in the Chinese county seat. The count is of the temples standing in the former town today, not a register from the period. It is a short local-history page with no sources cited, and it hedges the planning ("it is thought"). It gives no population, and no temple sizes or founding dates.

Used for: a small castle town's temple count and its temple quarter (religion-and-death.html).
