Akiba Masuji, "日本に於ける灌漑の一性格と歴史" ("A character and history of irrigation in Japan", title translated), reprinted in Nogyo doboku kenkyu 27(5), pp. 350-353 (first published in Nogaku 2(8), September 1948) (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjsidre1929/27/5/27_5_350/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A short survey by the late Akiba Masuji, professor at the University of Tokyo and former president of the agricultural engineering society, of the character of Japanese irrigation and its history from the earliest paddies through the land consolidation laws of 1899 and 1910 to the state irrigation programs of the 1920s to 1946, reprinted in the society's thirtieth-anniversary issue.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the proper arrangement of paddy plots and irrigation channels to the 1910 amendment of the land consolidation law and says that most irrigation before it was flow-through irrigation, which places the field without a ditch to every plot before modern times. It is a brief essay by a leading engineer, not a study of the older fields: it gives the older practice as a consequence ("accordingly") without evidence, names no region, and was written in 1948. It speaks of Japan alone, and of the fields as they stood before the Meiji laws rather than of Edo fields directly; it says "much" (多く) of the irrigation was flow-through, not all, and that it "went on changing" after 1910, so 1910 is when the ordered layout began, not when every field had it.

Used for: that a ditch to every plot came with the land consolidation of 1910, and flow-through irrigation before it (water)
