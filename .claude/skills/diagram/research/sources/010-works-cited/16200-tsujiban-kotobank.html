tsujiban-kotobank

Kotobank, 辻番 ("tsujiban, the crossing guard box", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering the entries of Shogakukan, Heibonsha, Britannica Japan and Yamakawa's Nihonshi shojiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%BE%BB%E7%95%AA-99227)

What it is: The published encyclopedias' entries on the guard boxes set at the crossings of Edo-period castle towns, above all of the samurai quarters of Edo: who set them up, how many there were, their size and staff, and their duties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Edo's count of samurai-quarter guard boxes, a box's size and staff, and the one provincial case read, Aizu-Wakamatsu, whose town-run boxes made night rounds. Its count and size are Edo's, the Aizu staffing is hedged ("it seems"), and it counts no provincial town's boxes.

Used for: the guard boxes' count, size and staff in Edo, and Aizu-Wakamatsu's night rounds (urban-features)
