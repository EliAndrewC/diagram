tsujiban-kotobank

Kotobank, 辻番 ("tsujiban, the crossing guard box", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering the entries of Shogakukan, Heibonsha, Britannica Japan and Yamakawa's Nihonshi shojiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%BE%BB%E7%95%AA-99227)

What it is: The published encyclopedias' entries on the guard boxes set at the crossings of Edo-period castle towns, above all of the samurai quarters of Edo: who set them up, how many there were, their size and staff, and their duties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Edo's count of samurai-quarter guard boxes, a box's size and staff, and the one provincial case read, Aizu-Wakamatsu, whose town-run boxes made night rounds. These are encyclopedia summaries, not period records. Its count (936 samurai-run boxes, in the Genbun years, 1736-41) is Edo's, a city of about a million, and leaves out the shogunate's boxes and the town-run jishinban. The box size and the four or five staff are a "usual" figure from one entry, by the period-drama consultant Inagaki Shisei, and Heibonsha has the staff set instead by each lord's stipend. The Aizu staffing is hedged ("it seems"), and it counts no provincial town's boxes.

Used for: the guard boxes' count, size and staff in Edo, and Aizu-Wakamatsu's night rounds (urban-features)
