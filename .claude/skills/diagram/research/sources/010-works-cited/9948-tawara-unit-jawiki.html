tawara-unit-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, "俵 (単位)" (the tawara as a unit of measure) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BF%B5_(%E5%8D%98%E4%BD%8D))

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the bale as a unit: how much a bale of rice held from the Heian period to today, how the 4-to bale became the standard in the Meiji period, and the weights at which other goods, charcoal among them, were packed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source for the bale's want of a standard size before the Meiji period, with the shogunate's and Kaga domain's figures, and for charcoal packed at weights set by grade. It is a tertiary article with footnotes to reference works, but its Edo-period figures (a bale of 2 to 5 to, the shogunate's 3 to 5 sho, Kaga's 5 to, and no standard size) carry no footnote of their own and rest on the article alone. Its charcoal figures are for the Iburi district of Hokkaido, taken from a 2014 collection of Showa-era oral histories of Hobetsu. They are twentieth-century practice on land settled only from the Meiji period, so they stand in for the fact that premodern charcoal was packed by grade, not for its premodern weights. It gives capacities and weights, not dimensions.

Used for: the bale's capacity by time and place, its want of a standard size, and charcoal packed by grade (urban features)
