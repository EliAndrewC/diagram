National Diet Library, Collaborative Reference Database, reference 1000348704, "江戸時代に、村外の寺の檀家になるということがあり得たか" ("Could a household be the parishioner of a temple outside its village in the Edo period?", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000348704&page=ref_view)

What it is: A librarian's answer, published in the National Diet Library's reference database, to a reader who found households registered at another village's temple though their own village had one; it answers by quoting the chapter on the early-modern parish system and split parishes in 『仏教民俗学大系 7 寺と地域社会』 (Buddhist folklore series, vol. 7, the temple and local society).

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries a folklorist's national judgment that a village's houses were most often divided among several temples outside the village, especially in the early part of the period - the strongest statement read on whether a village's parish temple was its own. Its limits: the judgment is quoted in two short passages from a printed book the session could not read, so its evidence and its count are not seen; and it describes where households were registered, not where temple buildings stood.

Used for: a village's houses divided among several temples outside it (religion-and-death)
