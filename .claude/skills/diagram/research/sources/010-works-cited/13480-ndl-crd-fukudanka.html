ndl-crd-fukudanka

National Diet Library, Collaborative Reference Database, reference 1000348704, "江戸時代に、村外の寺の檀家になるということがあり得たか" ("Could a household be the parishioner of a temple outside its village in the Edo period?", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000348704&page=ref_view)

What it is: A reference answer by the Tokushima Prefectural Library (case written 2020, registered 2024 in the Collaborative Reference Database that the National Diet Library runs for Japan's libraries), to a reader who found households registered at another village's temple though their own village had one; it answers by quoting the folklorist Fukuta Ajio's chapter "近世寺檀制度と複檀家" ("The early-modern temple-parish system and the multi-temple household", title translated from the Japanese by this project; a multi-temple household is one house whose members belonged to different temples) in 『仏教民俗学大系 7 寺と地域社会』 (Buddhist folklore series, vol. 7, the temple and local society; ed. Togawa Anshō, Meicho Shuppan 1992, reprinted 2016), pp. 49-66.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries a folklorist's national judgment that a village's houses were most often divided among several temples outside the village, especially in the early part of the period - the strongest statement read on whether a village's parish temple was its own. Its limits: the judgment is quoted in two short passages from a printed book the session could not read, and reaches us secondhand through a prefectural library's reference service, so its evidence is not seen; Fukuta words it as an impression from looking over temple-parish ties across Japan (「it seems it may be called the most general」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「もっとも一般的といってよいように思える」)), not as a count; and it describes where households were registered, not where temple buildings stood.

Used for: a village's houses divided among several temples outside it (religion-and-death)
