kotobank-yashiki

Britannica Kokusai Dai-hyakka Jiten, Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho (Nipponica, the entry by Takeuchi Yoshitaro) and Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha), s.v. 屋敷 ("yashiki", the house plot, title translated from the Japanese by this project), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B1%8B%E6%95%B7-145028)

What it is: Three encyclopedias' entries on the yashiki, the plot a house stands on with its outbuildings, gathered on one page: what stood inside it, how it was enclosed, how it varied from the northeast to the west of Japan, and how the Edo land registers treated it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is where the record finds, in general-reference form, that a farm plot was usually enclosed and by what, and that the enclosure changed from east to west - the grove in the east, the outbuildings themselves farther west. Its limits are that these are encyclopedia summaries, and of Japan alone, standing in for the Chinese side of the setting. The east-to-west change comes from one entry, Takeuchi's in Nipponica, which describes farmsteads as modern fieldwork found them and gives no era; only the Heibonsha entry's Edo passages rest on period survey manuals and registers. Its only figures are plot sizes (a medieval peasant's plot mostly of one to two tan, a lord's often over one chō), not counts. The Britannica entry names hedged, walled and moated plots together without saying whose was whose, and no entry says how common each enclosure was - the western one is given only as a tendency.

Used for: how a farm plot was enclosed, and how that changed from east to west (homesteads)
