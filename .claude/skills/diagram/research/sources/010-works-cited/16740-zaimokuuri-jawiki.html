ja.wikipedia 材木売 ("timber seller", title translated from the Japanese by this project; reached from 材木屋) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9D%90%E6%9C%A8%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the medieval timber seller and on timber merchants generally: the Horikawa timber merchants of Kyoto from the ninth century, the timber guilds of Kamakura, Kizu, Nara and Sakai, and the rise of the wholesalers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is used for the Horikawa, a channel about 12 m (4 jō) wide that served Kyoto's timber merchants as a log pond - a timber store on a city watercourse rather than a great river. Its limits: it is a tertiary summary; the Horikawa was not an ordinary stream but streams recut into a canal along a street of the planned grid of Heian-kyō, the imperial capital, when it was built in 794, so the width is that of a capital's engineered canal, not of a town's natural stream; the width is the Heian-era channel's (the article's 当時, "at that time"; it is mostly culverted now), while the log pond as the timber guild's monopoly is dated 1459; and the article hedges it ("it is said it could serve as a log pond") and footnotes it to a web page of a Kyoto tax accountants' cooperative relaying the Kyoto Saga Timber History (1972), a history published by a Kyoto timber firm - reported, not measured or excavated.

Used for: a city stream about 12 m wide serving as a log pond (urban-features)
