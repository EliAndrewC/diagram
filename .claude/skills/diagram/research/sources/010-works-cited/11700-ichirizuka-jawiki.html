ichirizuka-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 一里塚 ("milestone mound", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/一里塚)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the ichirizuka, the earth mound raised beside the main highways at every ri from Nihonbashi in Edo from 1604, with its size, its trees and the species counted on the five highways.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the mound's size, that it was built in pairs either side of the road, and which trees stood on it and in what shares - the great roadside tree a traveler met on the way to a village. It is a short encyclopedia article (a stub), and its numbers rest on weak sources: the size (5 ken square, 1 jō high) comes from a 2005 popular trivia paperback, and the species shares (enoki 55%, pine 27%, cedar 8%) come from a web page of a foundry-engineering society that gives no survey, date or basis. The article also converts 1 jō to 1.7 m, when it is about 3 m. The size is the prescribed standard, and surviving mounds vary. The shares are for the five main highways, and the Nikko road differed (cedar led there). The mounds stood on the shogunate's trunk highways, so a village met one only if it lay on such a road. This is Edo Japan's own institution standing in for the setting's roads; the shares are used for which trees were usual, not as exact figures.

Used for: the milestone mound's size, its pairing and its trees (vegetation)
