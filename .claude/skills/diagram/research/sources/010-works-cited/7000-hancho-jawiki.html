hancho-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 藩庁 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%97%A9%E5%BA%81)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on 藩庁 (hancho), the administrative seat of an Edo-period domain. It explains that domains ranked as castle-holders and above kept their seat in the castle itself, while domains of the lower ranks - castle-holder-in-name and castle-less lords, who were not permitted to maintain a castle - built a mansion somewhere in their territory and made that the seat of government instead.

Why it applies, and its limits: This settles a question a map has to answer before anything else: whether a domain's government sits inside a fortress or in a walled mansion in a town, and the answer turns out to follow from the lord's rank rather than from the size of the domain. It is a tertiary summary of a legal distinction, so it tells us which building held the government and nothing about what that building looked like - no plan, no size, no arrangement of offices, all of which we take from elsewhere.

Used for: 「The han of castle-holding daimyo and above had it at the castle; the han of joshu-kaku rank and below - the joshu-kaku and the castle-less daimyo - were not permitted to maintain a castle, and so placed a mansion within their territory and made it the han's administrative seat.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「城持大名以上の藩は城に、城主格以下の城主格と無城大名の藩は城を構えることが許されなかったことから、領内に屋敷を置いて藩庁とした」) - a castle-holding han's seat was AT the castle; a castle-less han's was its jin'ya
