Koshigaya City History, "The farmers' dwellings" (in Japanese; Koshigaya City Digital Archives, https://adeac.jp/koshigaya-city-digital-archives/texthtml/d000010/mp000010-000010/ht004250)

What it is: The section of the Koshigaya city history (Saitama prefecture) on the houses of its Edo-period farmers, published online in the city's digital archives. It reads a house-by-house record of 1824, the 家別双紙図面書上帳 of Hasuda village in Saitama district, for the rooms, floor coverings and buildings of its 76 households, and names the village rules that limited a farmer's house by his standing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a record made before 1868, of a whole village, read by the city's historian, and it is the record's premodern evidence that most farms had a well and a privy outside the house and many a free-standing firewood or storage shed. Its limits: it is the historian's summary of the record, which is not reproduced, and only rooms and floor coverings are tabled. The page gives numbers for rooms, floor coverings, stables and baths, but for the well and privy outside the house only "nearly every house", and for the sheds only "many", so it gives no share for either. It is one village in the low wet ground of eastern Musashi, and the historian's extension to the Koshigaya district is marked "probably". Whether the 50 stables held horses the page says is unknown.

Used for: the well and the privy outside the main house on most farms, the firewood and storage sheds standing free on many, and the stables and baths of Hasuda village in 1824 (homesteads)
