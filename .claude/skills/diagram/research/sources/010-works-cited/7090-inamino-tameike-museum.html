いなみ野ため池ミュージアム, 水生植物 (https://www.inamino-tameike-museum.com/pond-creature/aquatic-plants.html)

What it is: A page on aquatic plants from いなみ野ため池ミュージアム (the Inamino Tameike Museum), the public-education body that documents the dense cluster of irrigation reservoirs on the Inamino plateau in Hyogo prefecture. It describes the shallow embanked "dish ponds" of the lowland and the plants that ring them and cover their water.

Why it applies, and its limits: It corroborates the pond-margin planting on our maps at exactly the right scope - not wetlands in general but tameike, the built irrigation ponds themselves - with reed, Manchurian wild rice and lesser cattail forming colonies at the rim and floating-leaf plants over the open water, and it comes from an institution documenting a real, still-working pond cluster. Its limits: it is an outreach page written for visitors rather than a scientific survey, and it describes those ponds as they are now, including the eutrophication of the modern water; so it is a good guide to which plants belong at a pond's edge and a poor one to how much of them there would have been; it is in Japanese, quoted here in our own translation.

Used for: reed, wild rice and lesser cattail forming colonies around LOWLAND tameike specifically - a tameike-scoped corroboration of the zonation, from an institution documenting a real pond cluster in Hyogo.
