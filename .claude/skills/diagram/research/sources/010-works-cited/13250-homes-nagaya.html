LIFULL HOME'S PRESS, 江戸時代の長屋は誰のものだったのか？町人の暮らしが見える長屋事情 ～江戸時代の住まい事情① ("Whose were the nagaya of the Edo period? Nagaya life as a window on the townsmen - Edo-period housing, part 1", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.homes.co.jp/cont/press/rent/rent_00260/)

What it is: A popular article on a Japanese real-estate site about Edo's row tenements: the street-front row of shop-dwellings for better-off small merchants, the back-lane tenements of two kinds, and the one-story unit of nine shaku by two ken.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in plain words that the back-to-back tenement (munewari nagaya) was usually a one-story unit of nine shaku by two ken, and that the street-front row was roomier (rooms of eight and four mats) and held fairly well-off small traders, which the encyclopedia pages imply but do not state. It does not say every back tenement was one story: the other kind it names, the wari nagaya divided across the ridge, had a mezzanine loft reached by a ladder (梯子をかけた中二階), so the one-story point holds for the back-to-back kind only. It is popular writing by a general writer on a commercial site, with no references shown, and it describes Edo only; it is used for these two plain points, each consistent with the encyclopedia entries.

Used for: the one-story back tenement and the street-front row of small traders (towns)
