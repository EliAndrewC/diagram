wateraid-hand-dug-wells

WaterAid, Hand-dug wells, technical brief, January 2013 (https://www.wateraid.org/us/sites/g/files/jkxoof291/files/technical-brief-hand-dug-wells.pdf)

What it is: A short technical brief from the international water and sanitation charity WaterAid on how a hand-dug well is sited, sunk, lined and finished.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the same rule the Ethiopian manual gives, in a document of the same modern water-and-sanitation sector: an apron or concrete slab and a drain to prevent water ponding at the well, and the top three metres of the lining grouted to make a sanitary seal. The two agreeing is weaker evidence than it looks: the Ethiopian manual says in its own introduction that the series draws on the programmes of Ghana, Malawi and Tanzania, so these are one lineage written to one sanitary standard rather than two independent observations, and what they agree on is present-day doctrine rather than a property of the artifact across centuries. Its limits are the same: a modern NGO document rather than a historical source, written for concrete-ringed construction, and its stated purpose is sanitary protection rather than the practical standing our record wants - it never says the lifting gear needs dry ground, and our record marks that step as its own.

Used for: the apron and drain that stop water ponding at a hand-dug well, and the sanitary seal over its upper shaft
