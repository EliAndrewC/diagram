中国社会科学网, 水乡农谚见证桑园围发展, 2022-09-23 (https://www.cssn.cn/ztzl/jzz/rwln/wh/lnfw/202209/t20220923_5541513.shtml)

What it is: An article by 许结玲 (Xu Jieling), "水乡农谚见证桑园围发展" (Farming proverbs of the water country witness the development of Sangyuanwei), in 人文岭南, September 2022, on 中国社会科学网 (the Chinese Social Sciences Net), on the Sangyuanwei polder in Guangdong. It gathers local sayings about the polder's silk and fish economy and about the sluice that let water through its dike, and quotes 屈大均's late-seventeenth-century 廣東新語 (New Discourses on Guangdong) on the division of work between men and women in a Nanhai village.

Why it applies, and its limits: Sayings and a period observer are good evidence for the ordinary shape of a working day - who traded the fish fry, who fed the silkworms, that pond mud went onto the mulberry - which is the sort of thing that tells us what buildings and yards a polder settlement needs, and the sluice named in a place-name saying confirms the feature we draw. Its limits are that folk sayings are evocative rather than quantitative and undated - the article's own frame runs from the Ming-Qing silk trade to the machine filatures of the 1870s, so when a saying belongs is not established; that they were collected in a heritage article celebrating one polder in the Pearl River delta; and that nothing in them can be turned into a distance or a count. The one dated passage is the seventeenth-century one, which is the better evidence.
