yamamoto-2016-asuka-yashiki

山本直彦 (Yamamoto Naohiko), 平尾和洋 (Hirao Kazuhiro) and 宮内杏里 (Miyauchi Anri), 歴史的風土特別保存地区における民家の屋敷構えに関する研究 - 明日香村の奥山・飛鳥・河原・野口・岡・島庄の六大字を事例として ("Building layout inside rural house compounds in special preservation areas for historic landscape: the case of six village sections in Asuka village", the paper's own English title), 日本建築学会計画系論文集 (Journal of Architecture and Planning, AIJ) 81(721), March 2016, pp. 675-685 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aija/81/721/81_675/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: An architectural field survey of 236 farm compounds in six village sections of Asuka, in the Nara basin, made in 2012 and 2014: which way each main house faces, where its wings, outbuildings and storehouses stand, sorted by whether the section is a clustered village, a street village on an east-west street, or one on a north-south street.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one read source that sets a clustered village's farmhouses beside a street village's on the same plain and says which way each faced - the houses of its clustered Okuyama section all facing south although the section's main street runs north-south, the street villages' houses mostly turned to their street - and that the storehouse keeps the northwest corner whatever the street's direction, a corner this record reads as windward, though the paper explains its layouts partly by house-divination. Its limits: the houses are standing buildings surveyed in the 2010s, not dated to a founding; the Nara basin keeps no homestead grove, so it speaks of the house and its storehouse, never of trees; its street villages stand along temple approaches on a gridded ancient field system, not beside a paddy plain's levee; and it explains the layouts partly by the house-divination of lucky and unlucky corners, a reading of its own.

Used for: which way a farmhouse faced in a clustered village and in a street village, and the storehouse on the windward corner (homesteads)
