kotobank-nanukabon

Digital Daijisen (Shogakukan), Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (Shogakukan) and the old edition of the Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha), s.v. 七日盆 ("the seventh-day Bon", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%83%E6%97%A5%E7%9B%86-589347)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of two dictionary entries on nanuka-bon, the seventh day of the seventh month that opens the Bon observances, and short excerpts from two articles in Heibonsha's encyclopedia, on the Tanabata festival (its author not shown on this page) and on the Nebuta festival (signed by the folklorist Tanaka Sen'ichi) - modern reference works summarising folk custom, not records of any period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a standard dictionary's statement that the day was kept, region by region, by cleaning the graves and the wells, and the encyclopedia's that treating it as the start of the preparations for the returning dead is found nationwide - the calendar of grave tending this record draws on. The entries are a few lines each, undated as to period and silent on how much ground round a grave was cleared or by whom.

Used for: grave cleaning at the start of Bon (religion and death)
