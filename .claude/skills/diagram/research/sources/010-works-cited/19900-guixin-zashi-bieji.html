guixin-zashi-bieji

Zhou Mi (周密), Guixin zashi (癸辛雜識, Miscellaneous Notes from Guixin Street), the first part of the supplementary collection (別集上), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E7%99%B8%E8%BE%9B%E9%9B%9C%E8%AD%98/%E5%88%AB%E9%9B%86%E4%B8%8A)

What it is: A volume of a notebook of anecdotes, customs and events written in the late thirteenth century by a scholar of the Southern Song who lived on after its fall; one short entry describes the fry trade of Jiangzhou on the middle Yangtze.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary account, some four centuries before the Pearl River delta sources, of wild-caught fry sold far afield and then kept for a few weeks in a cloth pen hung in open water rather than in a dug pond. It is one short entry by an observer who was not a farmer, from the Yangtze valley rather than the delta; it describes the dealers' method of carrying the fry at length and the rearing in two sentences, and gives no size for the pen. The Wikisource text is a transcription of a printed edition and may carry copying slips.

Used for: that thirteenth-century fry were reared for a month or so in a cloth pen in open water before sale (archetypes)
