guixin-zashi-bieji

Zhou Mi (周密), Guixin zashi (癸辛雜識, Miscellaneous Notes from Guixin Street), the first part of the supplementary collection (別集上), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E7%99%B8%E8%BE%9B%E9%9B%9C%E8%AD%98/%E5%88%AB%E9%9B%86%E4%B8%8A)

What it is: A volume of a notebook of anecdotes, customs and events written in the late thirteenth century by a scholar of the Southern Song who lived on after its fall; one short entry describes the fry trade of Jiangzhou on the middle Yangtze.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary account, some four centuries before the Pearl River delta sources, of wild-caught fry sold far afield and then kept for half a month to a month (一月半月) in a cloth pen hung in open water rather than in a dug pond. It is one short entry, unattributed and likely written from hearsay by a scholar who was not a farmer, and not from the delta. The fry are caught at Jiangzhou's waterside, but the pen stage is at the dealers' homes, which the entry places inland in Jianchang (Jiangxi), Fujian and Quzhou and Wuzhou (Zhejiang). The pen is a dealer's holding pen before resale, not a farm's fry pond; it describes the dealers' method of carrying the fry at length and the rearing in two sentences, and gives no size for the pen. The Wikisource text is a transcription of a printed edition and may carry copying slips.

Used for: that thirteenth-century fry were reared for half a month to a month in a cloth pen in open water before sale (archetypes)
