街道の歩き方 (Kaidō no Arukikata, "How to walk the old highways", site name translated by this project), 宿場とは ("What is a post station?", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://kaido-walking.com/basic/about-syukuba.php)

What it is: An explanatory page on a walkers' guide site to the old highways, on what an Edo-period post station was, its facilities and the features at its entrances.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the mitsuke at a post town's entrances were - an earthen bank on each side of the road with a fence on top. It is one anonymous enthusiast's guide (copyright 2020). Its short reference list (two town guidebooks to the Iwami silver-mine road and a Kotobank entry) supports its notes on the inns for official visitors and on kinked roads, not the mitsuke passage, which rests on a woodblock print it shows. It names the mitsuke as one of several entrance features, alongside wooden gates and boundary posts, and says the Nagasaki highway used a gate-wall instead, so not every post town had the banks. Its note on the night lamps is hedged, and it describes the post towns of the great highways, not a market town.

Used for: the post town's entrance banks (urban-features)
