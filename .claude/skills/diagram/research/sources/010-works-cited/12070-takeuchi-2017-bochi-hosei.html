takeuchi-2017-bochi-hosei

Takeuchi Yasuhiro, "お墓事情と墓地法制 6 (最終回) 日本の墓地と墓地法制" (Graves and graveyard law, part 6: Japan's graveyards and graveyard law), 書斎の窓, Yuhikaku, March 2017 (in Japanese; https://www.yuhikaku.co.jp/static/shosai_mado/html/1703/02.html)

What it is: The last of a series of essays by a scholar of graveyard law in a law publisher's magazine, surveying Japan's graveyards from the end of the Edo period through the Meiji regulations to the present law.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in one sentence that from the end of the Edo period into the early Meiji years graveyards of several forms - an individual's, a lineage's, a settlement's and a temple's - stood across the country, governed by local custom, which is the evidence that more than one form was usual. Its limits are that it is a legal historian's overview for a general readership, not a count: it names the forms without saying how common each was or where, and it speaks only of the Bakumatsu and early Meiji, so it says nothing about how far back in the Edo period, or before it, the same mix went.

Used for: the forms of graveyard that stood side by side at the end of the Edo period (religion-and-death)
