en.wikipedia City Wall of Nanjing (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Wall_of_Nanjing)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the City Wall of Nanjing, the Ming circuit built around the first Ming capital, describing how it was laid out in a winding, free form that followed the city's broken topography, used the river as its natural moat, and so departed from the square or rectangular walls of Beijing and Xi'an.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives one clear case of a capital wall that follows the ground rather than the square - bending around lakes, hills and the river that serves as its moat - which is the form that lets our maps bend a wall where the terrain demands. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary whose history and construction sections carry the encyclopedia's own requests for citations, a sharper discount than the usual one; that it is an imperial capital, so no dimension transfers to a provincial seat; and that it gives no total length for the circuit, so the 35 km figure often quoted for this wall is on no page we could read and our text says so.

Used for: the Ming circuit built "in a winding, free style, based on the city's complicated topography" with the river as its moat; the 35.267 km length is unsupported by any readable page (aggregator sites)
