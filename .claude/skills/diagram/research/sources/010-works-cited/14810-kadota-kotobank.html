kadota-kotobank

Kotobank, 門田 (kadota, the paddy before the gate), the entries of several Japanese dictionaries, the one used by Kimura Shigemitsu in the Encyclopedia Nipponica (日本大百科全書) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/門田-45605)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering the dictionary entries for kadota, the paddy that lay around a medieval local lord's residence, typically spread before its gate and worked directly for the lord by his dependents.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the historical form of a warrior's house in the countryside: the residence set among its own paddy, mostly well-established fields, the land where the lord's hold was strongest - the nearest real thing to the setting's samurai country estate. It describes the medieval period, before the Edo-period move of samurai into castle towns, and it is a page of short dictionary and encyclopedia entries (the Nipponica one signed by the historian Kimura Shigemitsu), a tertiary summary good for the shape and not for a number. It gives no typical size for the fields and no distance from the house. The only figures it has are a planting song's praise of a two-tan kadota and one lord's seizure of over a hundred chō under the name, and neither measures the usual form. So it carries the placement and not a figure.

Used for: the medieval warrior residence with its own directly worked paddy before the gate (fields)
