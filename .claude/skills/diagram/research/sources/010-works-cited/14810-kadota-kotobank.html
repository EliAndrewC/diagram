kadota-kotobank

Kotobank, 門田 (kadota, the paddy before the gate), the entries of several Japanese dictionaries, the one used by Kimura Shigemitsu in the Encyclopedia Nipponica (日本大百科全書) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/門田-45605)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering the dictionary entries for kadota, the paddy that lay around a medieval local lord's residence, typically spread before its gate and worked directly for the lord by his dependents.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the historical form of a warrior's house in the countryside: the residence set among its own paddy, mostly well-established fields, the land where the lord's hold was strongest - the nearest real thing to the setting's samurai country estate. It describes the medieval period, before the Edo-period move of samurai into castle towns, and gives no size for the fields or distance from the house, so it carries the placement and not a figure.

Used for: the medieval warrior residence with its own directly worked paddy before the gate (fields)
