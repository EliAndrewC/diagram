rots-2015-chinju-no-mori

Aike P. Rots, "The Shinto Environmentalist Paradigm and the Rediscovery of Chinju no Mori", Japanese Journal of Religious Studies 42/2 (2015), 205-233 (open-access PDF: https://nirc.nanzan-u.ac.jp/journal/6/article/1415/pdf/download)

What it is: A peer-reviewed article in the journal of the Nanzan Institute for Religion and Culture on how shrine groves came to be presented as Japan's sacred forests in the late twentieth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the picture of a shrine grove that the literature holds up - many shrines surrounded by small woods or flanked by forested mountains, the archetype "a clearly demarcated, broccoli-shaped patch" of natural broad-leaved forest - and, in the next sentence, corrects it: many real shrine woods are conifers planted in the premodern or modern period. Its limits are that it studies a modern discourse, not the groves themselves; that the patch is the literature's archetype, which the article is critical of; and that it gives no measurements of a grove's plan.

Used for: the shape a shrine grove is thought of as having, and that real groves depart from it (religion-and-death.html).
