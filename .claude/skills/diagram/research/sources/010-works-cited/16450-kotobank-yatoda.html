kotobank-yatoda

Kotobank, 谷戸田 ("yatoda, the valley paddy", title translated from the Japanese by this project), excerpts from Heibonsha's Sekai daihyakka jiten (old edition), entries 棚田 and 谷戸, the second signed by Kuroda Hideo (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%B0%B7%E6%88%B8%E7%94%B0-1429507)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the word yatoda, which quotes excerpts from two entries of Heibonsha's encyclopedia, the second (谷戸) signed by Kuroda Hideo, a historian of medieval Japan, the first (棚田) shown unsigned: on the terrace-type paddies opened from ancient times on, and on the yato valley whose spring-fed paddies made the typical medieval farm landscape, with the regional names for such fields.

Why it applies, and its limits: A signed entry by a specialist placing the valley paddy at the center of the medieval landscape and giving its regional names, which the valley-chain form rests on. It is a pair of excerpts, not the whole entries, and it gives no dimensions. It describes the middle ages, which fall within the setting's pre-Meiji span. But it gives the regional names as present usage ("are called"), so which region used which name is the entry's modern account, not a record of the period; the only names it places in the period's documents are 棚田 and 山田, in late-Kamakura records of the Kinai. It is about Japan only, and carries nothing for the setting's Chinese side.

Used for: the valley paddy's regional names, and valley fields as the representative medieval farm landscape (archetypes)
