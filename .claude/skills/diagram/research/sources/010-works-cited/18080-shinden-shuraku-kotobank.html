shinden-shuraku-kotobank

kotobank, 新田集落 ("new-field settlement", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the entries from Heibonsha's 世界大百科事典 and Shogakukan's 日本大百科全書 (Nipponica) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%96%B0%E7%94%B0%E9%9B%86%E8%90%BD-82291)

What it is: Two Japanese encyclopedia entries, gathered on one dictionary page, on the villages founded on land opened by the new-field development of the late sixteenth to nineteenth centuries: their forms, their layout and the Musashino example.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that an Edo new-field village laid out its roads, fields and house plots on a plan, so its form was regular, and that the street villages common on the Kanto plain set each house's land behind it in strips of equal area - the premodern evidence for an evenly divided planned reclamation. It gives no geometry beyond the strips, never says the strips were paddy rather than dry field, and says nothing of a grid of paddies; Heibonsha adds that the common form was the clustered village with scattered holdings, and that new fields added onto an old village's land founded no new settlement, so the planned layout is one form among several. Encyclopedia summaries without footnotes.

Used for: the planned, regular layout of an Edo new-field village and its equal strips (fields)
