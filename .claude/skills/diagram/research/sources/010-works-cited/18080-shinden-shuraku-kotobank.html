shinden-shuraku-kotobank

kotobank, 新田集落 ("new-field settlement", title translated), the entries from Heibonsha's 世界大百科事典 and Shogakukan's 日本大百科全書 (Nipponica) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%96%B0%E7%94%B0%E9%9B%86%E8%90%BD-82291)

What it is: Japanese encyclopedia entries gathered on one dictionary page - Heibonsha's (by Kikuchi Toshio) and Nipponica's (by Nakata Eiichi), with a short Britannica Japan entry beside them - on the villages founded on land opened by the new-field development from the Sengoku period to the end of the Edo period: their forms, their layout and the Musashino example.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that an Edo new-field village laid out its roads, fields and house plots on a plan, so its form was regular, and that the street villages common on the Kanto plain set each house's land behind it in strips of equal area - the premodern evidence for an evenly divided planned reclamation. It gives no geometry beyond the strips and says nothing of a grid of paddies. Its strip villages are on the Kanto uplands (Musashino, a diluvial plateau watered by common wells and branches off the aqueducts), which was dry-field land, not paddy. In Heibonsha, the reclaimed new fields where paddy was made took another form: houses in a line along the dike, with each household's fields near the house and fairly compact; Heibonsha adds that the common form was the clustered village with scattered holdings, and that new fields added onto an old village's land founded no new settlement, so the planned layout is one form among several.

Used for: the planned, regular layout of an Edo new-field village and its equal strips (Fields)
