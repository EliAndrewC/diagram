ja.wikipedia 川越制度 ("the river-crossing system", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B7%9D%E8%B6%8A%E5%88%B6%E5%BA%A6)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Edo system of carrying travelers through a ford by porters, on foot, by litter or on horseback, with its offices, how its fares were set (posted daily, rising with the depth; it gives no amounts) and its closing depths.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests the ford as a regulated crossing where no bridge was built, and the depth at which it closed. It is a short encyclopedia article, a tertiary source: its own references are the Shimada city and Shimada tourism association pages, a dictionary entry (Kotobank) and a National Diet Library reference answer, so it carries the shape of the system, and its closing depth only as far as those pages carry it. Its details are drawn from the Oi river crossing at Shimada, the best-documented and largest, a crossing the shogunate regulated on a main highway, and may not hold for a smaller ford.

Used for: crossing by the ford (ways)
