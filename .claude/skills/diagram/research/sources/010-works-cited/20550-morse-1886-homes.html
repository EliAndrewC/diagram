morse-1886-homes

Edward S. Morse, Japanese Homes and Their Surroundings, Harper & Brothers, New York, 1889 printing, preface dated November 1885 (Project Gutenberg; https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52868)

What it is: An American zoologist's illustrated account of the Japanese house, its rooms, gardens and approaches, written from what he saw and sketched while living in Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes how the houses of small villages stood along their road and how a crowded village reached the houses behind its street. Its limits: it is one traveler's observation of the early Meiji years, after the period the setting is modeled on; its crowded case is Enoshima, a shrine island near Yokohama hemmed in by hills, not a farming village on open ground.

Used for: alleys to the houses behind a crowded village's street (Ways)
