en.wikipedia, Omamori (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omamori)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the omamori, the amulet a Japanese shrine or temple gives out - what it is, how it is made and carried, and how it is understood by the people who receive one.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the transaction rather than the object: the amulet comes from the shrine or temple itself, and what the recipient gives back is understood as a donation to it, which is why our maps show no third-party stall selling charms at a temple gate. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary; that the word the article itself uses for the exchange is "sold", so "bestowed" is our own gloss on it and not the page's; that our conclusion goes one step further than anything the page states - it never says that nobody else sold charms at a gate, and we are reading that from its silence; that the passages about factory manufacture and shop-sold versions describe MODERN commercialism, not the premodern practice our maps depict, so they cannot be read back into the setting; and that it describes Japan alone.

Used for: the amulet as a donation to the shrine or temple that issues it
