tenshu-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 天守 ("Tenshu", the castle keep, title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E5%AE%88)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the keep of a Japanese castle: its history, its plan types, the keep base it stands on, and the castles that had no keep.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sorts the Edo-period castles without a keep into four kinds - a keep lost and never rebuilt (Edo, Osaka, Kanazawa, Fukui, Saga), a keep base with no keep (Tsu, Akashi, Ako), no keep base and a three-story turret standing in for a keep (Yonezawa, Yamagata, Mito), and neither (Kagoshima, Hitoyoshi) - which makes the keep a form a castle may or may not have. It names examples, not how common each kind was; its only count is 65 keeps standing in 1854. It is a tertiary encyclopedia article, and its section on castles without a keep cites no source. It describes Japan alone, where the keep appears from the 1570s, and says nothing of Chinese walled cities, so it bears on a castle modeled on Japan and not on one modeled on China.

Used for: whether a castle had a keep, and the keep base (cities/capitals)
