nakasendoway-dosojin

Nakasendo Way, "Dosojin" (https://www.nakasendoway.com/dosojin/) - dosojin "are closely associated with roads, travelers and local boundaries", "found most often in the more isolated parts of the highway ... and near the mountain passes"

What it is: "Dosojin", a page on Nakasendo Way, an English-language guide to the history and culture of the Nakasendo highway published by Walk Japan Ltd., a walking-tour company. It describes dosojin - the small carved stone guardians of roads, travelers and local boundaries, often a paired couple - and where they are found, most often on the lonelier stretches of the highway and near the mountain passes, along with the related Jizo and the horse-headed Kannon, the guardian of horses and travelers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It settles the one thing we need, which is where such a stone stands: on the road and at a boundary rather than inside a farmyard. It is a tour operator's cultural guide written for walkers, not a scholarly work, and it describes one highway in particular, so we use it for placement and for what the stones are, never for a count or a measurement.

Used for: dosojin standing on the road and at boundaries, not inside the farmstead.
