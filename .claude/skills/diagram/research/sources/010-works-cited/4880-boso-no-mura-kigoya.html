boso-no-mura-kigoya

Chiba Prefectural Museum, Boso-no-Mura, the reconstructed farmstead's 木小屋 page (https://www.chiba-muse.or.jp/MURA/facility/page-1521181891660/page-1521294570333/)

What it is: A page from the Chiba Prefectural Museum's open-air museum Boso-no-Mura, describing one outbuilding of its reconstructed late-Edo farmstead: the kigoya (木小屋), the shed that held the charcoal and firewood a household burned. The farmstead is a village-headman-class house assembled from two real houses of the region (the Akiha house of Sago, whose roof plaques are dated 1857, and material from the Naito house of Ichihara).

Why it applies, and its limits: A museum describing its own reconstruction is good evidence for what a farmyard outbuilding was and what went in it, which is what puts a fuel shed on our farmsteads at all. It is a reconstruction of one region's farmhouse presented for visitors rather than an excavation report or a survey; it is a well-off headman's farmstead, composed from two houses, used here as evidence for the ordinary homestead's yard; and the page says nothing about where in the yard the shed stood, so the placement on our maps rests on other reasoning.

Used for: a firewood/charcoal shed on the farmstead. Its PLACEMENT is not on the page.
