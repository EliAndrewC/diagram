abele-2018-kawata

Michael Thomas Abele, Peasants, skinners, and dead cattle: the transformation of rural society in western Japan, 1600-1890, PhD dissertation, University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign (2018) (https://www.ideals.illinois.edu/items/107042)

What it is: An archive-based doctoral dissertation on the kawata communities of Kawachi and Izumi - the people who handled dead cattle and the leather trade - and on their place in Tokugawa village society, worked through village cases including Saraike and Oji.

Why it applies, and its limits: It answers what the collar around our burakumin quarter is really about: the outcast settlement was a separate residential cluster inside the same village's territory, clearly separated from the peasants' plots, standing on poor ground, and at no fixed distance out - in Saraike the peasants lived nearer to two other villages than to the kawata of their own. Its limits are that it is an unpublished dissertation about two provinces of western Japan; that its village figure is schematic and its own caption says the proportions are wrong; that its span runs to 1890, so a case from its later chapters is Meiji rather than Tokugawa; and that it gives no distance in any unit, so the 60 ft on our maps stays a calibration against the drawn sheets and not a finding. One use goes further than the work does: kawaramono, people of the riverbank, is a NAME, and a name is not evidence that a given settlement stood across a watercourse from its village.

Used for: the outcast cluster inside the village territory with a clear separation and no fixed distance; the poor ground it stood on; kawaramono, "people of the riverbank", as the older name behind the reading that it stood across a stream
