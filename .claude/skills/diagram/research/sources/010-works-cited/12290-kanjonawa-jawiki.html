kanjonawa-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 勧請縄 ("kanjo-nawa, the invocation rope", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8B%A7%E8%AB%8B%E7%B8%84)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the sacred rope strung at a village's boundary: what hangs from it, the theories of its origin, and where it is found.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places ropes, figures and sandals of straw at the village boundary across rural Japan and names the districts around Kinki where the rope is concentrated, which is what makes it a regional form rather than a universal one. It is a short encyclopedia article, a stub, and so a tertiary summary: its list of districts around Kinki rests on the distribution map in Inami Toshihide's 1993 study of boundary charms, which it cites and which is the work that carries that claim, and its account of the rope's origin is one theory among several, taken from a museum lecture. It describes the custom as it survives today and as twentieth-century folklorists recorded it; its only evidence of age is medieval picture scrolls showing a rope hung round a single house in ritual abstinence, not across a village's boundary, and it gives no count of villages and no date for how old any one rope is.

Used for: the straw rope and charms at the village boundary, and the Kinki districts where they cluster (ways)
