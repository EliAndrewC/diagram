王禎 (Wang Zhen), 《農書》 卷十八 農器圖譜十三 灌溉門 (Yuan dynasty, 1313), Wikisource text (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/王禎農書/卷十八)

What it is: The irrigation chapter of Wang Zhen's Treatise on Agriculture of 1313, an illustrated catalog of farm implements and works: entries on the water palisade (a row of timbers set across a brook upstream of high-lying fields so that the water issues sideways and runs down to them), the water gate, and the pond-and-weir pair, each with its rationale.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary premodern Chinese source, and its water palisade is the one description read of the village-scale case our maps draw - a small brook, a timber barrier placed upstream of the fields, the water turned out of the channel to reach them. Its limits are that it is a prescriptive illustrated manual - what a farmer ought to build, with idealized pictures - and not a survey of what villages had; that it gives no dimensions and does not say what share of the brook is left in its channel; that it is a Yuan compendium of practice across China rather than a record of one place; and that what we read is a Wikisource transcription of a later imperial edition, rendered into English by this project.

Used for: the water palisade - timbers set across a brook upstream of high fields, turning the water out sideways to run down to them; the water gate of coursed stone and stacked timber; the pond-and-weir pair
