kotobank-machiwari

コトバンク 町割 ("town plan", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%94%BA%E5%89%B2-634688)

What it is: A page on kotobank, a Japanese reference site that reprints the entries several published dictionaries give for one word, here 町割 (machiwari), the laying out of a town's streets, blocks and lots. Its longest entry, from Heibonsha's revised Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten, traces the Japanese town plan from the square blocks of the ancient capitals through medieval Kyoto to the castle towns of the early modern period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the sizes and shapes of real town blocks - Heian-kyo's 40-jo square, Edo's 60-ken square with its open core, and the rectangular block most castle towns used - which is what a drawn city's grid is measured against. It is a tertiary encyclopedia summary with no plans of its own, it describes Japanese planned towns rather than the Chinese walled seats our cities also draw on, and its sizes are the planned units, not surveys of any one town as built.

Used for: Heian-kyo's 40-jo blocks; Edo's 60-ken square block with a 20-ken open core, 20-ken-deep lots and the two-sided ward; square blocks rare outside Edo, Sunpu and Nagoya, most castle towns using rectangular blocks with no open core; Kyoto's squares cut into rectangles in 1591 (cities/fabric)
