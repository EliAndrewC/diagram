Hokuriku Regional Development Bureau (MLIT), Aga River office, "The Aga River and traditional river engineering" (in Japanese; https://www.hrr.mlit.go.jp/agagawa/river/history_gijyutsu05.html)

What it is: The transcript of a lecture on pre-modern Japanese river works published by a regional office of the national river administration, describing the timber frames (ushi and waku) weighted with stone baskets that were the standard traditional tools, with figures from Edo-period engineering manuals.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes what the Edo-period frames were and says weirs were built of them, of stone and of reed facing. Its weir passage is about DIVERSION weirs on large rivers built to work in flood, not a village's intake on a brook; its definition of the square frame is given under groynes; and its dimensions are for river-scale frames.

Used for: that Edo-period weirs were built of timber frames, stone and reed facing; what a square timber frame (waku) was; the Hamura intake weir built of frames (water)
