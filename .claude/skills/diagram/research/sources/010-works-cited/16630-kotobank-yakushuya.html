kotobank-yakushuya

kotobank 薬種屋 ("apothecary", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%96%AC%E7%A8%AE%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting two dictionary entries on the yakushuya, the house that compounded crude drugs and sold Chinese-style medicine, with an illustration cited from the Edo Meisho Zue.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the apothecary as a house that made up and sold its medicines itself, the kind of trade the generic shop glyph stands for. Two modern Japanese dictionary entries (a tertiary source) for a word first recorded about 1678 and still in use in the nineteenth century. They describe Edo-period Japan and say nothing of China. "A house that compounds crude drugs and deals in Chinese medicine" is the dictionary's stock wording for a trade. The same word also names the wholesale drug merchant (yakushushō): its nineteenth-century example is Osaka's Doshomachi wholesalers. So it does not show that compounding, selling and living shared one building, and it gives no premises.

Used for: the apothecary compounding and selling at home (urban-features)
