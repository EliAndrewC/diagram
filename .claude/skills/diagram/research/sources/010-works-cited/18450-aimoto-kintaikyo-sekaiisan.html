aimoto-kintaikyo-sekaiisan

"Japanese bridges and their structure before 1673", a page of the Kintai Bridge World Heritage site (in Japanese; http://www.kintaikyo-sekaiisan.jp/work3/main/truth/4.html)

What it is: A page of a site promoting the Kintai Bridge at Iwakuni for World Heritage listing, surveying the kinds of bridge Japan had before the Kintai Bridge was built in 1673 - post bridges, beam bridges, arched bridges, stone bridges and cantilever bridges - with a short history of named examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the Aimoto Bridge over the Kurobe River to 1663 and says it was built of timbers thrust out from both banks because floods took any pier within a year. Its limits: it is an advocacy site whose publisher is not named on the page read and which cites no source for this passage; it labels some bridges with a period that its own text does not support; and it gives no dimensions and says nothing about how an ordinary beam bridge's end sat on its bank.

Used for: the Aimoto Bridge of 1663, a crossing carried back into both banks (ways)
