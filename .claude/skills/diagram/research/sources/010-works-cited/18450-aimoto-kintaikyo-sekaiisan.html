aimoto-kintaikyo-sekaiisan

"Japanese bridges and their structure before 1673", a page of the Kintai Bridge World Heritage site (in Japanese; http://www.kintaikyo-sekaiisan.jp/work3/main/truth/4.html)

What it is: A page of a site promoting the Kintai Bridge at Iwakuni for World Heritage listing, surveying the kinds of bridge Japan had before the Kintai Bridge was built in 1673 - post bridges, beam bridges, arched bridges, stone bridges and cantilever bridges - with a short history of named examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the Aimoto Bridge over the Kurobe River to 1663 and says it was built of timbers thrust out from both banks because floods took any pier within a year. Its limits: it is an advocacy site whose publisher is not named on the page read and which cites no source for this passage; its Aimoto sentences are word for word the Japanese Wikipedia article on the bridge (愛本橋), so it repeats an encyclopedia rather than being a second witness to it; its year is not settled, since the Tateyama Kurobe Geopark Association gives 1662 (Kanbun 2) where this page gives 1663; it labels some bridges with a period that its own text does not support; and it gives no dimensions. It says the timbers were thrust out from both banks but not how they were set or weighted in the bank, and it says nothing about how an ordinary beam bridge's end sat on its bank.

Used for: the Aimoto Bridge of 1663, a crossing carried back into both banks (ways)
