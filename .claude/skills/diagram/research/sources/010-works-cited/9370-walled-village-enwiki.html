en.wikipedia, Walled village (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_village)

What it is: A short English Wikipedia article on the walled village of southern China and Hong Kong: a multi-family settlement ringed by thick defensive walls, usually home to an extended family or a clan of one surname, with a note on the walls built against pirates.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page read that says a Chinese clan settlement beyond a city's protection was walled for defense, which is the southern Chinese counterpart this page sets beside the moated Japanese manor. It is a stub resting on one reference, it covers southern China and Hong Kong only, its one piece of history is Ming-Qing Guangdong, where villages walled themselves against coastal pirates in country far from administrative centers (a coastal-frontier threat, not general rural insecurity), and it describes a whole village rather than one rich house's compound. Its single reference is a web page hosted on ibiblio, not a scholarly work, and it gives no size, plan or wall measure: it carries the fact that such villages were walled and why, never their form or dimensions.

Used for: the walled clan village of southern China as a fortified rural settlement (cities/hinterland)
