matsukura-2008-taito

Kitaoka Katsue and Miyawaki Masaru 2008, 台東区における寺町の道路と街区と寺院の歴史的変遷に関する研究 (A Study on the Transition of Roads and Blocks and Temples in Taito City), a study of the temple districts of Taito ward, Tokyo, and the formation of their roads, blocks and temples, 都市計画論文集 (Journal of the City Planning Institute of Japan) 43.2 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/journalcpij/43.2/0/43.2_1/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed city-planning paper that dates the roads, blocks and temples of Taito ward, which holds much of old Edo's temple quarters, by laying 88 historic maps from 1628 on, fire-insurance maps and the road register over a 2006 digital map, and dates the temples from the 1829 temple gazetteer, the 1877 temple survey, the ward's reports and a field survey; it compares the average size of the surviving blocks formed in each period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the average size of the Edo-period blocks that survive unchanged in Yanaka and Negishi-Shitaya: about 9,035 m² for the 13 laid out in 1658-1746 and about 7,327 m² for the 22 laid out in 1747-1854, against about 2,814 m² for the ward today. It also says that in Yanaka one Edo-period block still holds several Edo-period temples, which bounds how many precincts a block held. Its limits: the two figures are averages over each period, not the smallest and largest block; they are measured on a 2006 map; they cover only the 35 blocks (2% of the ward) that survived fire, earthquake and rebuilding; the paper does not say every one was a temple block, and it does not count precincts per block; it is one city, Edo, the shogun's capital; the areas are the authors' approximations for comparison; and the paper's table of each temple's precinct in tsubo is not printed.

Used for: surviving Edo-period blocks averaging about 9,000 m² (laid out 1658-1746) and 7,300 m² (1747-1854) in the old temple quarters (religion-and-death)
