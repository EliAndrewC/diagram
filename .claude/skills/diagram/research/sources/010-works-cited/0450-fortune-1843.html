fortune-1843

Robert Fortune, eyewitness account, 1843 (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/54720/54720-h/54720-h.htm)

What it is: Three Years' Wanderings in the Northern Provinces of China by Robert Fortune, the Scottish botanist and plant collector sent to China in 1843; it is his own travel narrative of the tea, silk and cotton districts, with an account of Chinese agriculture and horticulture. We quote his description of where tea was planted: always on the lower and most fertile sides of the hills, never on the low lands, in small gardens of one to five acres, every cottager having his own.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness account of tea growing before mechanization, before state terracing programs, and before the twentieth century rearranged the hillsides, which makes it about as close to a premodern observation of a tea slope as a readable source gets. The limits are that it is one traveler's narrative written for a Victorian British readership rather than a survey, that it covers the coastal and northern provinces he happened to pass through, and that it is Chinese practice standing beside our Japanese material.

Used for: tea on the lower and most fertile hillsides, never the low lands or the barren upper slope
