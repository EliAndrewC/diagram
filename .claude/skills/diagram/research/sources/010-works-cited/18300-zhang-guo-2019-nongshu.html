Zhang Sen and Guo Zhanfeng, "中国古代农业技术对现代农业发展的启示" ("What ancient Chinese farming technique suggests for the development of modern agriculture", title translated), 农业现代化研究 (Research of Agricultural Modernization) 40, no. 6 (2019): 891-899 (in Chinese; https://nyxdhyj.isa.ac.cn/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.13872/j.1000-0275.2019.0084.pdf)

What it is: A journal paper on what traditional Chinese farming practice offers modern farming, which quotes the old farm manuals, among them the Shen family's manual of the late Ming or early Qing, on manure and on keeping pigs and sheep.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the readable page on which the Shen manual's passages on pigs can be read, so it shows pig-keeping recommended to a farm before the empire's end. Its limits: it is a modern argument about sustainability, not a study of the manual, and quotes it in excerpts with omissions marked; it calls one passage a record of northern dryland farming, which the manual's own rice-and-mulberry calendar does not support; it gives a saying on pigs to "the ancients" without a source; and it gives no count of how many farms kept pigs.

Used for: that a seventeenth-century farm manual told a rice farm to keep pigs for manure (homesteads)
