Kanagawa Prefectural Museum of Cultural History, 「September 2021's featured object: the ruling map for the field boundary and mountain dispute between Nanazawa and Susugaya villages」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「七沢村と煤ヶ谷村田畑境并山論裁許絵図」) (https://ch.kanagawa-museum.jp/monthly_choice/2021_09)

What it is: A curator's page on a map of 1649 in the museum's collection, drawn when inspectors sent by the shogunate ruled on a boundary dispute between two villages of Sagami province, and on what such ruling maps were.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes one dated ruling that fixed a village boundary through fields and hill land as a drawn line, with the land on either side assigned. It is one map, which the page itself calls unusual: thin paper, plain drawing and a vermilion line, where the usual ruling map made by the shogunate's court of judgment was drawn on heavy paper with the line in ink. The page describes that usual form only in general, with a Meiji-era catalog's count of 1,539 such maps held by that court, 54 of them for Sagami, which it cautions may not be the Edo-period number. And a map was not the only form a ruling took: the page finds that every other ruling the same inspectors issued in 1649 was written only, without a map.

Used for: that a disputed village boundary was fixed as a ruled line on a map (vegetation)
