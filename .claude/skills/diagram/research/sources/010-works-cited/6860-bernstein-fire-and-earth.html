What it is: Andrew Bernstein's article "Fire and Earth: The Forging of Modern Cremation in Meiji Japan", published in the Japanese Journal of Religious Studies 27/3-4 (2000), a scholarly account of the Meiji government's short-lived national ban on cremation and its repeal. Only the abstract is on a public page; the specific episode we cite - an 1873 proposal to move the crematoria, notably those at the Senju temples, outside the city's "red line", on the argument that the stench from burning bodies injured health - is quoted from a later account of the article rather than from Bernstein's own text.

Why it applies, and its limits: It bears directly on where a city puts the burning of its dead and why, which is the rule a capital map needs when it places a crematory ground relative to the built-up area. Two limits: Meiji Japan is later than the period our settlements are drawn from, so it shows an older arrangement coming under pressure rather than the arrangement itself; and because the passage comes from a secondary retelling and not from a page carrying Bernstein's own words, we treat the 1873 proposal as reported rather than as read in the article.
