zh.wikipedia 香油錢 (incense-and-lamp-oil money) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E9%A6%99%E6%B2%B9%E9%8C%A2)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on xiangyouqian, the money given at a temple, explaining the name and the practice that lies behind it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It fixes the direction of a flow this record had backwards: in old times believers donated incense sticks, candles, lamp oil and gold paper to temples and monasteries for use in the offerings, and later gradually gave money in their place, which is why the money itself is named for the two goods. So incense and lamp oil reach a Chinese temple as a DONATION rather than leaving it as stock - the same relation the Japanese omamori already gave us, and the reason no third-party religious-goods shop is drawn at a temple's own gate. Its limits are that it is a tertiary article about a term - a stub of a few hundred characters carrying no references at all, the weakest work this page cites; that the conclusion drawn from it, no third-party shop at the gate, rests on the page's SILENCE rather than on anything it says; that it gives no temple's accounts and no annual figure, so the size of that income is still unsourced; and that it describes the practice generally rather than in a dated period.

Used for: incense, candles, lamp oil and paper donated to a Chinese temple, and money given in their place
