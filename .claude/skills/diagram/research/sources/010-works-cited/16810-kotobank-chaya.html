kotobank-chaya

kotobank 茶屋 ("teahouse", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%8C%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the chaya, the teahouse, from the tea sellers gathered at Toji's south gate in 1403 to the roadside rest houses at the edges of Edo-period post towns and the cooked-food and restaurant teahouses of the 1660s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where teahouses gathered - at temple gates, along the highways and at the ends of post towns, and in the busy quarters of the cities - which is where a map puts them. Signed reference entries on Japan; they give no counts or sizes.

Used for: teahouses at temple gates, at the ends of post towns and in busy quarters (urban-features, religion and death)
