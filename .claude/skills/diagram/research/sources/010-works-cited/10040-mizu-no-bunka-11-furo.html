Mizkan Water Culture Center, 「The bath was a festive space」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「風呂はハレ空間だった」), an interview with the architectural historian Oba Osamu, Mizu no Bunka no. 11 (in Japanese; https://www.mizu.gr.jp/kikanshi/no11/03.html)

What it is: An interview, published in 2002 in the Mizkan Water Culture Center's magazine, with Oba Osamu, an architectural historian of Kyoto Prefectural University, on the history of the Japanese bath, from temple bathhouses to the bath inside the modern home.

Why it applies, and its limits: A specialist says where the bath and the toilet stood in relation to the main house before they moved indoors. Its limits are that it is an interview rather than a study, that the passage is about the Meiji period's housing improvement rather than the Edo period, and that it gives no counts, though he describes the separate bath as the older pattern that the Meiji period inherited. The same interview also says that in the countryside a bath of one's own was the exception: most rural commoners washed with well water poured over a bamboo slat mat (gyōzui). So a separate bath building belongs on a farmstead that had a bath, not on every farmstead.

Used for: the bath and toilet kept apart from the main house, linked by a corridor or standing alone outdoors, on farmhouses in the Meiji period (homesteads)
