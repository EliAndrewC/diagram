jaanus-umaya

JAANUS (Japanese Architecture and Art Net Users System), "umaya 馬屋" (https://projects.mcah.columbia.edu/jaanus/node/392)

What it is: The entry on the stable in JAANUS, the English-language dictionary of Japanese architectural and art-historical terms: the farmhouse's inside and outside stables, and the warrior class's freestanding shoin-style stable with its stalls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, in its own English, the warrior stable as a building of its own, its length in bays from the carpenters' manual and the Ichijodani excavation, and its stalls one bay wide on raised timber floors, the figures a posting's stable is sized from. It says none of the classic warrior stables survives, so it describes them from a manual, an excavation and a shrine's miniature; it gives a stall's width and not its depth, and it never says how long a bay is. It is a dictionary's summary that cites none of its sources. Its classic stable is a great lord's: a shoin-style building from the Asakura daimyo's mansion, with a tatami-floored suite for retainers. The same entry records simpler warrior stables, such as the late 17th-century one at Hikone, whose stalls have timber slats laid on the earth. So raised timber floors are the top of the range, not the rule for a lesser posting's stable.

Used for: the warrior stable as a freestanding building three, five or seven bays long, with stalls one bay wide on raised timber floors (buildings)
