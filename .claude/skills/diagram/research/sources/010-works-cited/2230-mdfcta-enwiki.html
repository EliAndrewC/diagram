en.wikipedia "Metropolitan Drinking Fountain and Cattle Trough Association" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Drinking_Fountain_and_Cattle_Trough_Association)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the Victorian London charity that built the city's public drinking fountains and granite cattle troughs, quoting Dickens's Dictionary of London of 1879 on their use.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the throughput of one busy street trough in a day, which is the turnover figure this page's relay watering rests on. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary of a guidebook's 1879 claim, and that Victorian London's horse traffic is a far larger case than a provincial livery yard. And a Victorian street trough was fed continuously from the water mains, while a yard basin on these maps is filled by bucket from a well, so the throughput does not carry across as a bound on how many animals one basin serves.

Used for: one trough serving 1,800 horses in a day
