kominkai-genkan

古民家探訪 (Kominka Tanbō), 玄関のルーツとつくり 土間・式台・上がり框・取次 (The roots and make of the genkan: the earth floor, the shikidai, the step edge and the reception) (in Japanese; https://kominkai.net/genkan/)

What it is: A 2022 post on an enthusiasts' blog about old Japanese houses, most of those it visits in and around Fukuoka, on the genkan: why its step is high, its origin in the shikidai of Muromachi warrior houses, and the parts of a traditional entrance. Its author holds a private old-house appraiser's certificate; the post was updated in 2025. Its references are a popular illustrated book on Japanese architecture (Miyamoto Kenji, PHP 2010), an old-house association's handbook (Kawakami Yukio, 2019), and housing companies' web pages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says plainly how a palanquin met the entrance, which is what makes the arrival staged. Its limits: it is an enthusiast's blog retelling popular books and companies' web pages, with no reference tied to the palanquin sentence, it speaks of the shikidai in the Muromachi warrior house and illustrates it with a later samurai house in Kagoshima, and it is not a survey of any one building.

Used for: the palanquin brought alongside the step, and the shikidai as the entrance for formal guests (buildings)
