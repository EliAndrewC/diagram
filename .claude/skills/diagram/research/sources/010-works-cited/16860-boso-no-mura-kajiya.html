boso-no-mura-kajiya

Boso no Mura (Chiba Prefectural Museum), digital museum, "鍛冶屋" ("The smithy", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.chiba-muse.or.jp/MURA/digitalmuseum/machinami/shoka/kajiya.html)

What it is: The open-air museum's page on its reconstructed smithy, the Isumiya (named for the old Isumi district's iron sand): what a village smith (nokaji) made and how the trade was run, the building's size and floor plan, and its fittings.

Why it applies, and its limits: The page says the building's model was a village smith's (nokaji) workshop that stood at Arami in Narita, so its size and plan (3.5 by 3 ken, an earth-floored work area with bellows and hearth, a boarded room at the back) follow one real workshop's as far as the museum copied it. The page also describes the trade in general terms for village smiths across Japan: a small household trade of couples, parents and children, or master and apprentice, making tools to order for nearby farmers and local hardware dealers. Its limits are that it is one modern reconstruction, undated beyond "once", standing in a museum street of shops rather than in a village. Its fittings are not all premodern: the page itself says the upright vise came into use only in the Meiji period, and it names coke beside charcoal as the hearth's fuel. So what it shows is the trade as it continued into modern times, and its tools can be drawn for our setting only after leaving those out. It says only that such smiths were once numerous across Japan, with no count of how common they were and nothing of where in a village they stood.

Used for: the village smith's small family trade, and one smithy's size and plan (urban-features)
