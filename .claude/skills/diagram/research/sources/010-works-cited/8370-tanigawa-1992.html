tanigawa-1992

Tanigawa Akio, "Excavating Edo's Cemeteries: Graves as Indicators of Status and Class", Japanese Journal of Religious Studies 19/2-3 (1992) (https://nirc.nanzan-u.ac.jp/journal/6/article/832/pdf/download)

What it is: A peer-reviewed article in the Japanese Journal of Religious Studies, freely readable in full, translated from the author's Japanese article of 1991, reporting the archaeology of Edo-period temple graveyards excavated in Tokyo. Its grave categories and sizes come from one of them, the graveyard of Jishō-in in Shinjuku (87 graves, excavated 1984-85, their contents dated to the eighteenth century and later): it sorts those graves by construction - stone chambers, graves with charcoal or lime-plaster floors, jar burials in wooden chambers, plain jar burials and wooden coffins - and gives the major axis of each kind, from 65 cm to 2.4 m.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only measured evidence our record has for how much ground one grave takes, which is what our burial-ground sizes are built up from. Its limits must be stated wherever it is used: the paper gives the size of a GRAVE PIT and no area per burial, no density and no cemetery area - its large figures are the excavation trench and the whole temple's grounds, not a graveyard - so the step from a pit's width to a ground's acreage is this project's arithmetic; the sizes are one urban temple graveyard's in the shogun's capital, which the paper reads as a warrior family's ground, matching its plain jar burials to the graves of bannermen and retainers; only its plain wooden-coffin graves does it call the same as farmers' graves in villages, but it sizes them only together with the plain jar burials of the same category 4, which it matches to bannermen and retainers (16 jars and 18 coffins, 65 cm to 2.3 m, most under 1.4 m), so that shared range stands for a commoner's grave, with no figure for the coffins alone; most eighteenth-century bodies were buried seated, in jars and square coffins, so a pit's size is a seated burial's; the author warns that the classification rests on graves that had been moved and disturbed; and it is Edo-period Japan, so it says nothing about Chinese full-body burial.

Used for: the sizes of excavated Edo graves (one warrior-associated temple graveyard; its plain wooden-coffin graves as the commoner's)
