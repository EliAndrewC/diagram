kotobank-kataarashi-yamakawa

Yamakawa Nihonshi shojiten (revised edition), Yamakawa Shuppansha, s.v. "Kataarashi", on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%89%87%E8%8D%92-2022438)

What it is: A Japanese history dictionary's entry on kataarashi, on a Kotobank page it shares with the Nihon kokugo daijiten. It defines the word as paddy and dry field rested in alternate years, calls it nenko, and sets it against wholly unopened land, long-term waste (joko) and ground taken by a river (kawanari). It says where the resting land lay and what it was used for while it rested.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says where the resting land lay: usually (its word) mixed in among the cropped land rather than in a solid tract, and, while resting, left unmarked and grazed in common by cattle and horses. It is a short dictionary entry with no author, date or sources shown, and it describes a word found in Heian poetry and the fields of that age. The Nihon kokugo daijiten entry on the same page gives thin soil as the reason for resting the land, which marks it as an early, low-yield practice and not one of the settled village of later centuries. It says nothing of which plots of a village's paddy were the ones to rest.

Used for: that the resting land usually lay scattered among the cropped plots and was grazed in common, in the Heian and early medieval period (fields)
