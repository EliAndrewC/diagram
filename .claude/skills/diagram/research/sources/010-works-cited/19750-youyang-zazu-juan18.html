Duan Chengshi, Youyang Zazu (酉陽雜俎, "Miscellaneous Morsels from Youyang"), chapter 18, on trees (Chinese Wikisource; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E9%85%89%E9%99%BD%E9%9B%9C%E4%BF%8E/%E5%8D%B7%E5%8D%81%E5%85%AB)

What it is: A Tang-dynasty miscellany of the mid-ninth century by the scholar Duan Chengshi, whose chapter on trees records, among much else, the common saying that the persimmon tree has seven virtues. The text is in Classical Chinese; the translations quoted from it are this project's.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a premodern Chinese text, so what it says of the persimmon was said long before 1868. Its limits: it reports a saying, not an observation of any tree or farmstead; it says the persimmon lives long and gives much shade but gives no size; it is Chinese, and a Tang one, so it speaks for how the tree was thought of, not for how big a Japanese dooryard persimmon grew; and a miscellany of curiosities is not a manual of practice.

Used for: that the persimmon was known before modern times for its long life and its abundant shade (homesteads)
