FAO Training Series, Simple methods for aquaculture: Pond construction, ch. 10 "Pond outlet structures" (https://www.fao.org/fishery/docs/CDrom/FAO_Training/FAO_Training/General/x6708e/x6708e10.htm)

What it is: The "Pond outlet structures" chapter of Simple methods for aquaculture: Pond construction, part of the UN Food and Agriculture Organization's training series for small-scale fish farmers. It describes how a pond is emptied and its level controlled, principally the sluice - a protected opening in the dike closed with wooden boards and screened so the fish cannot leave - and the older monk-style outlet.

Why it applies, and its limits: A board-and-slot sluice through an earth dike is a carpentry-and-mud structure with nothing modern in it, and the chapter tells us its form and rough size, which is what a map has to place. The limits are that this is a present-day training manual for small fish farms anywhere in the world, not an East Asian or historical source; that the wooden form we quote is one option on a menu that also offers the same sluice in concrete, brick and plastic pipe; and that its recommended dimensions describe today's small ponds, so we take the shape of the structure from it, not a historical measurement.

Used for: the sluice gate, verbatim: "a protected opening in the pond dike that can be easily closed with wooden boards to regulate water level and can be screened to avoid fish losses" (inside width up to 0.8 m); the monk outlet as the older draining structure
