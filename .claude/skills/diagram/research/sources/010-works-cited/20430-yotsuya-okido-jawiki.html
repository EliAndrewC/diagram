ja.wikipedia 四谷大木戸 ("Yotsuya Okido, the great barrier gate at Yotsuya", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9B%9B%E8%B0%B7%E5%A4%A7%E6%9C%A8%E6%88%B8)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 四谷大木戸 (Yotsuya Okido), on the barrier gate the shogunate set up in 1616 where the Koshu highway entered Edo at Yotsuya - its stone paving and stone walls, the Tamagawa aqueduct's water-watch station beside it, where the aqueduct's open channel gave way to underground wooden and stone pipes into the city, the gate's removal in 1792, and one Hiroshige print of the place (from Ehon Edo miyage, 1850 on).

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the Okido was, a guarded checkpoint on a highway at the edge of the shogun's capital, beside which the aqueduct's open channel ended and its buried pipes into the city began. It is a short tertiary summary resting on one Edo-period guidebook (Edo meisho zue, in a 1927 edition) and two modern local-history books (1998, 2007), so we take from it only the gate's date and purpose. It describes one gate of one exceptional city rather than a general type, and that gate stood on Edo's west side, where the Koshu highway and the aqueduct both came in; it does not support which side of our capital the aqueduct ends on.

Used for: 「In 1616 (Genna 2), the Edo shogunate set up an Okido at Yotsuya as the way in and out of Edo on the Koshu highway.」 (translated; original: 「1616年（元和2年）、江戸幕府により四谷の地に、甲州街道における江戸への出入り口として大木戸が設けられた。」)
