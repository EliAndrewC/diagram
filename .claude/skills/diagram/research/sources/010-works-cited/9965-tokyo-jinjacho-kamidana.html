tokyo-jinjacho-kamidana

Tokyo Jinjacho (the Tokyo association of Shinto shrines), "お神札・神棚について" (on shrine talismans and the household altar) (in Japanese; http://www.tokyo-jinjacho.or.jp/ofuda_kamidana/)

What it is: Guidance from the body that administers Tokyo's Shinto shrines on keeping a household altar shelf, including how the talismans of several shrines are set out on it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the two ways several kami share one small household shrine - a shelf shrine with three doors, one talisman behind each, or one door with the talismans stacked in rank order behind it - and it is an institutional source on current practice. It describes present-day custom, including the Ise talisman that became general only in modern times, and dates neither arrangement, so it is evidence for the forms, not for their age.

Used for: the three-door and one-door household altar shelves and the rank order of the talismans on each (religion-and-death).
