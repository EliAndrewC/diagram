tokyo-jinjacho-kamidana

Tokyo Jinjacho (the Tokyo association of Shinto shrines), "お神札・神棚について" (on shrine talismans and the household altar) (in Japanese; http://www.tokyo-jinjacho.or.jp/ofuda_kamidana/)

What it is: Undated guidance (it mentions the Ise rebuilding completed in 2015) from the association that groups Tokyo's 1,398 Shinto shrines - the Tokyo office of the national Association of Shinto Shrines - on keeping a household altar shelf, including how the talismans of several shrines are set out on it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the two ways several kami share one small household shrine - a shelf shrine with three doors, one talisman behind each, or one door with the talismans stacked in rank order behind it - and it is an institutional source on current practice. It recommends present-day practice ("this order would be good") rather than recording what households did, and dates neither arrangement, so it is evidence for the forms, not for their age. Its rank order - the Ise talisman first, then the local tutelary kami, then a shrine of personal devotion - is the modern shrine establishment's prescription. The Ise talisman itself was already in most households by the late Edo period, carried out by Ise's itinerant priests, but its present name and its distribution through the local shrine date only from the early Meiji period (1871-72), and the page does not say whether the order was kept before then.

Used for: the three-door and one-door household altar shelves and the rank order of the talismans on each (religion-and-death).
