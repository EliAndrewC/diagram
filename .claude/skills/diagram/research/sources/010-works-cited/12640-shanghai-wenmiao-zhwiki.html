zh.wikipedia 上海文庙 ("the Shanghai Confucian temple", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/上海文庙)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Confucian temple of Shanghai county, moved in 1855 (finished 1856) onto a former Ming circuit office's grounds inside the county seat's west gate, with its two axes of temple and school and the size of its main hall (about 260 square metres, 14 metres high).

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a county-tier Confucian temple with a measured main hall and a stated place inside the wall. It is an encyclopedia article, a tertiary summary; its hall figures come from the temple's own former website, not a survey. The site inside the west gate is a relocation after the 1853-55 fighting and a fire on the old site, onto a vacant government plot; for five and a half centuries before, the temple stood east of the county yamen, so the present site is no guide to where a county put its temple. The building measured is a late-Qing rebuilding, restored in 1979 and 1997-99, and one county's temple is one example.

Used for: where a county's Confucian temple stood and how big its hall was (religion-and-death)
