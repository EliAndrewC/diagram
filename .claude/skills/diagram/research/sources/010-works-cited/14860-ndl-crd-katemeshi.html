National Diet Library, Collaborative Reference Database, record 福参-1203 from Fukuoka Prefectural Library, "What did commoners eat as a staple besides rice, in the Heian period and from Edo into Meiji?", quoting 『ビジュアル・ワイド江戸時代館』 (Shogakukan, 2nd edition 2013) (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000347352&page=ref_view)

What it is: A library's answer to a reader's question, recorded in the National Diet Library's reference database, which quotes illustrated reference works on everyday life, among them an encyclopedia of the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the Edo encyclopedia saying most commoners ate coarse grain while warriors and nobles ate rice, that farm meals centered on coarse grain with the rice cooked into it, and one dated menu - a village of Ina district in Shinano around 1830, whose winter midday meal was 40 percent rice - which is a dated Edo-period figure for the rice share of one meal. It is a quotation at second hand of a modern (2013) popular reference work, which as quoted names no period record for the menu, so the figure cannot be traced to a document of the time. It is one village, in an inland upland district of Shinano, and one season, not a survey of a diet and not a figure for a lowland paddy plain; another work quoted on the same page gives barley nine to rice one for rural fare, a measure of how far the share varied.

Used for: farm meals centered on coarse grain, and the Ina district menu of about 1830 (fields)
