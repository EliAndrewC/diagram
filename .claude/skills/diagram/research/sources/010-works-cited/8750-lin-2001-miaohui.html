林榮澤, "The temple fair and Chinese folk society: the Qing cases of north, northeast and northwest China" (title translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「廟會」與中國的民間社會 - 以清代的華北、東北、西北為例), 史耘 7 (July 2001), open PDF on the National Taiwan Normal University history department's site (https://www.his.ntnu.edu.tw/publish03/downloadfile.php?periodicalsPage=2&issue_id=39&paper_id=249)

What it is: A study of the Qing temple fair in north, northeast and northwest China, published in the history department's own journal at National Taiwan Normal University rather than in a major refereed venue, quoting county gazetteers and earlier accounts of the fairs at length.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the evidence for what stood on a temple's own ground: the fair was the countryside's one great trading and pleasure ground, with physicians, diviners, astrologers and physiognomists putting up sheds beside the storytellers and conjurors, and the traders who followed the fairs rented the temple's buildings and plots of ground between one fair and the next. That is the forecourt market and the diviners our town and city gates carry. Its limits are that its region is north China rather than the Jiangnan-and-Japan blend this setting draws on; that the fairs it quotes are at village temples as often as at great urban ones, so a permanent shopfront at a famous gate is not what it shows; that the renting sentence is footnoted to a Republican-era social survey while the fairs themselves are Qing; and that its scan's OCR is unreliable, so its quotations are read from the page image.

Used for: the periodic market held on a temple's own ground and the temple renting the stall space; diviners and the other fortune-telling trades at a temple fair
