takehara-2004-yashikirin

Takehara Akihide, Murata Nobito, Hirabuki Yoshihiko, Fukuoka Kōhei and Miura Osamu, 「The structure of homestead groves - how the regions differ」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷林の構造 - 地域による相違 -」), abstract of a talk at the 51st meeting of the Ecological Society of Japan (2004) (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/esj/ESJ51/0/ESJ51_0_271/_article/-char/ja/)

What it is: A conference abstract by ecologists at Iwate University and Miyagi University of Education reporting tree-by-tree surveys of homestead groves in four regions of Japan - Isawa in Iwate, Iide in Yamagata, Tonami in Toyama and Hikawa in Shimane - with the number of species per homestead and the most frequent species in each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a measured count of what grew in surviving homestead groves, including the fruit trees among them. The persimmon is among the three most frequent species in only one of the four districts, the Tonami scattered-farmstead groves. Elsewhere cedar, chestnut, pine or evergreen broadleaves lead. So it supports a persimmon in a grove of the Tonami kind, not in every homestead grove. Its species counts and its note that cedar dominated every grove of the three summer-green regions, planted in rows at Iide and Tonami and clipped like a hedge at Hikawa, are what show the planted windbreak as led by one tree yet mixed. It is a short abstract of a modern survey of a few homesteads per region (five at Tonami), not a historical record, and gives no areas or tree counts.

Used for: that a persimmon was among the commonest trees of a homestead grove; that the windbreak grove was led by one planted tree, in rows, among many kinds (vegetation)
