biodic-kusayama

「On the state of the grass mountains (kusayama) of our country」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「わが国における草山の状況について」), a briefing paper posted by the Biodiversity Center of Japan, Ministry of the Environment (in Japanese; https://www.biodic.go.jp/cbd/2006/pdf/1102_2_12.pdf)

What it is: A short government briefing paper on the history of Japan's grass mountains - the hills kept in grass for fertilizer and fodder - period by period from the middle Edo period to the 1950s, citing Mizumoto Kunihiko's history of the grass mountains.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the size of the grass ground a farming country needed against its fields, and the forms that ground took, which is what sets how much of a village's surroundings was meadow. It is a national summary for a policy audience, not a village study; its figures are hedged ("is said to have been") and rest on a secondary history; and it gives the ratio for the country as a whole, not for any village.

Used for: grass ground more than ten times the fields, and its forms - open grass or thin low pine (vegetation)
