biodic-kusayama

「On the state of the grass mountains (kusayama) of our country」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「わが国における草山の状況について」), a briefing paper posted by the Biodiversity Center of Japan, Ministry of the Environment (in Japanese; https://www.biodic.go.jp/cbd/2006/pdf/1102_2_12.pdf)

What it is: A short government briefing paper on the history of Japan's grass mountains - the hills kept in grass for fertilizer and fodder - period by period from the middle Edo period through the Shōwa 30s (1955-64) to the present day. It is one page (page 27) of a longer Ministry of the Environment document, posted among the Center's 2006 biodiversity-convention papers, and cites Mizumoto Kunihiko's history of the grass mountains, two works by Ogura Jun'ichi, one by Nakahori Kenji, and statistics.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the size of the grass ground a farming country needed against its fields, and the forms that ground took, which is what sets how much of a village's surroundings was meadow. It is a national summary for a policy audience, not a village study; its figures are hedged ("is said to have been") and rest on secondary histories; it gives the ratio for the country as a whole, not for any village. The ratio is the ground NEEDED to supply green manure and fodder, and the paper says that ground was not only grassland: cutting was also taken from woodland. So the ratio bounds a village's meadow from above rather than sizing it. And it describes Edo Japan's practice of cut-grass green manure (karishiki). That stands for the setting's Japan-modeled farming, not for its China-modeled lowlands, where fields were manured more with night soil, canal mud and bean cake than with hill grass.

Used for: grass ground more than ten times the fields, and its forms - open grass or thin low pine (vegetation)
