小畑晃男, "Studies on the Traditional Transplanting Methods of Rice in Wakayama Prefecture" (the journal's own English title; original: 「和歌山県における慣行田植法の地域性とその成立要因に関する研究」), 農作業研究 (Farm Work Research) 29: 24-29 (1977) (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsfwr1966/1977/29/1977_29_24/_pdf/-char/en)

What it is: A study of customary rice-transplanting practice across Wakayama prefecture, reporting part of a nationwide survey of such practice carried out in 1975-76. It divides the prefecture into zones and sets out, for each, when transplanting happened, how long it took, and who did the work together.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the Japanese record's own instrument for the question our uniform paddy green rests on, and it answers it in two parts that do not sit in the same place. In the mountain zones, communal transplanting was organized at the hamlet unit of twenty to thirty households and in some places the entire work schedule for the year's transplanting was settled in advance by consultation - but there valley water could be drawn at any hour and the transplanting period ran past twenty days. In the plains, the window is seven to ten days, short enough for a district's basins to stand at one growth stage, and the paper attributes that concentration to the preceding crop's calendar while noting that communal work there is comparatively little. It also cuts against the mechanism our record had assumed, describing yui as helping one another by making use of the DIFFERENCE in transplanting dates. Its limits: one prefecture, and a mountainous one whose plains are small, so the plains zone here is a modest example of its kind; the survey was made in 1975-76 and records practice remembered as customary rather than practice observed in a premodern century; and the scan's text layer is unreliable, so its passages are quoted from the page image.

Used for: a village transplanting on one agreed schedule settled in advance, at the hamlet unit of 20 to 30 households; the seven to ten day window where the preceding crop makes it tight, and the twenty-day-plus window where water is free; yui as working because transplanting dates differ
