kotobank-santome-shinden

Kotobank, 三富新田 ("Santome Shinden", title translated), the entries by Kimura Motoi and Arai Juro from Heibonsha's revised 世界大百科事典 and from Heibonsha's Hyakka jiten Mypedia (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%89%E5%AF%8C%E6%96%B0%E7%94%B0-278184)

What it is: Kotobank's page for Santome Shinden, the dry-field colony the Kawagoe domain opened on the Musashino plateau in 1694-1696; the signed Heibonsha entry gives the road width, each household's frontage and depth, the household count and holding from the 1696 survey register, and the order of house lot, field and woodland back from the road.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an encyclopedia entry signed by Kimura Motoi, a historian of Edo villages, and Arai Juro, and it names the 1696 survey register behind its total area, household count and holding (the road width and the 40 by 375 ken parcel are the entry's own account of the plan, with no record named), so it is a citing work for the Wikipedia stub's 40 ken. Its limits: it describes one planned, domain-run colony on a water-poor upland of dry fields (its first wells were few, deep and shared - the entry names one, at the Tamon-in, 25 m deep), not a paddy village, and it gives the plan, not how far apart the houses actually stood. Its 180 households are the lots of the 1696 register: the Miyoshi town page counts the same 180 as house lots (91, 40 and 49), and Kawashima (1987) counts 172 holders at the opening, the headman and the colony's leaders each holding two lots, so 180 is an upper bound on the households, not a count of them. Its figures belong to that dry-field economy: 40 by 375 ken is 5 cho a household, several times what a paddy household worked, the 40-ken frontage sets houses about 73 m apart along the road, and the woodland strip at the back served upland fields; a paddy row village can borrow the form (road first, house lot on the road, the holding in a strip behind it) but not the frontage, the depth or the woodland share.

Used for: the planned strip row: roads laid first, 40 ken of frontage by 375 ken, 180 households, house lot then field then woodland back from the road (homesteads)
