kotobank-santome-shinden

Kotobank, 三富新田 ("Santome Shinden", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the entries by Kimura Motoi and Arai Juro from Heibonsha's revised 世界大百科事典 and from Heibonsha's Hyakka jiten Mypedia (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%89%E5%AF%8C%E6%96%B0%E7%94%B0-278184)

What it is: Kotobank's page for Santome Shinden, the dry-field colony the Kawagoe domain opened on the Musashino plateau in 1694-1696; the signed Heibonsha entry gives the road width, each household's frontage and depth, the household count and holding from the 1696 survey register, and the order of house lot, field and woodland back from the road.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a signed encyclopedia entry by a historian of Edo villages that names the 1696 survey register behind its figures, so it is the citing work the Wikipedia stub's 40 ken wants. Its limits: it describes one planned colony on a waterless upland of dry fields, not a paddy village, and it gives the plan, not how far apart the houses actually stood.

Used for: the planned strip row: roads laid first, 40 ken of frontage by 375 ken, 180 households, house lot then field then woodland back from the road (homesteads)
