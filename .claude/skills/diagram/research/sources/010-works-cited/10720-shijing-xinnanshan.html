shijing-xinnanshan

Book of Songs (詩經), "Xin Nan Shan" (信南山), on Wikisource (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E8%A9%A9%E7%B6%93/%E4%BF%A1%E5%8D%97%E5%B1%B1)

What it is: The public-domain text of a harvest ode in the Book of Songs, on the fields below the Southern Mountain, whose first stanza has the line the Zuo Zhuan quotes on the direction of field rows.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the ode the envoy quotes, read at its own source so the line is not taken from the chronicle alone. A poem of the early first millennium BCE; the line says the rows run south and east and nothing more, and the reading that each tract's rows ran one way or the other is the chronicle's use of it, not the poem's statement.

Used for: the line "we bound it, we order it; south and east run its rows" (fields)
