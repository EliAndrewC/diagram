Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, Kanto Regional Development Bureau, Yokohama National Highway Office, Tokaido Q&A 17, "What is the relay of men and horses?" (title translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「人馬の「継ぎ送り」とはどういうことですか？」) (https://www.ktr.mlit.go.jp/yokohama/tokaido/02_tokaido/04_qa/index2/a0217.htm)

What it is: A question-and-answer page in the national highway office's public guide to the Tokaido, on the obligation each post station carried to keep porters and horses permanently on hand: 36 horses at first, and from 1638 a hundred post horses and a hundred porters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the documented order of magnitude for the draft animals a settlement on a trunk road had to hold standing, which is what our gate caravan cluster is sized against, from the ministry that maintains the road. Its limits are that it is a government public-information page rather than a study; that it states an OBLIGATION on a station and not the animals of any one arriving train; and that it is the Japanese post system rather than a Chinese wagon route, which is the other half of the setting's ancestry.

Used for: the standing requirement of a Tokaido post station, 36 horses rising to a hundred horses and a hundred porters from 1638
