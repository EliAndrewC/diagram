ja.wikipedia 朱雀大路 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9C%B1%E9%9B%80%E5%A4%A7%E8%B7%AF)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 朱雀大路 (Suzaku Ōji), on the great central avenue that ran from the palace gate to the southern gate of Heian-kyō and of the earlier Japanese capitals, with the corresponding avenue of Chang'an in Tang China for comparison.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the width of the ceremonial avenue of an imperial capital - 82 m for Heian-kyo, 147 m for Tang Chang'an - which is the top of the scale our widest streets are measured against. Its limits are that the Heian figure is a width prescribed in a legal code, not a measured one, and the page's own archaeological citation gives a different estimate for a different boundary, about two meters apart, so the number is a convention rather than a confirmed measurement; that the page's principal reference is a popular book rather than an excavation report, and the Chang'an figure reaches us through a Japanese tertiary page rather than a Chinese archaeological source; and that this is the grandest street of an imperial capital, which no town of ours approaches.

Used for: Heian-kyō's avenue 「width 82 meters (Engishiki)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「幅82メートル（延喜式）」); Chang'an's 「width 147 meters」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「幅147メートル」)
