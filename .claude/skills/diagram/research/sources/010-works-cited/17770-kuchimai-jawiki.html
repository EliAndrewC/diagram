kuchimai-jawiki

ja.wikipedia, 口米 ("kuchimai, the rice surcharge", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8F%A3%E7%B1%B3)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on kuchimai, the surcharge collected with the land tax from the medieval estates through the Edo period to its abolition at the Meiji land-tax reform, with what it paid for, the problems it raised and its reform in 1725.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that at intendant offices where the surcharge yielded little and could not grow, it became one cause of spending the main tax on the office and of bribery, and that income differed between offices because the rates differed by region. It is a tertiary summary without inline citations (only a list of four reference works, the Kokushi Daijiten entry among them), so this sentence cannot be traced to the work that carries it; it names no office and gives no count of how many ran short; the shortfall belongs to the years when the surcharge paid for the office, from the rules of 1616 to the reform of 1725, after which the shogunate paid the offices' costs and took the whole surcharge; and it concerns the shogunate's own intendancies - it says the domains adopted the same surcharge, but not how a daimyo's county offices were paid for.

Used for: the offices where the surcharge fell short (buildings)
