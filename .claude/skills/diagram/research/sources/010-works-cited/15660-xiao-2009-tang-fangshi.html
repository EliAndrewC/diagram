xiao-2009-tang-fangshi

Xiao Jianle, 唐代坊市制度及其历史定位 ("The Tang ward-and-market system and its place in history", title translated from the Chinese by this project), Guangming Daily, 2009, reprinted by the China Social Science Net (in Chinese; https://www.sinoss.net/c/2009-12-23/530410.shtml)

What it is: A historian's essay in the Guangming Daily on the Tang system that kept residence (the ward) and trade (the market) apart, quoting the Tang rules on where a market could be set up and when it opened and closed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the rule that no market might be set up except at a prefecture or county seat, and the drum and gong that opened and closed it, and says that even prefecture and county towns divided their market into rows by goods - which is what places a regulated market in a Tang county town. It is a newspaper essay arguing a thesis, not a study of any county town's market ground, and it does not say where its quotations come from; it gives no size and does not say whether a county town's market was walled. The drum-and-gong wording it quotes (three hundred strokes each) is the Tang Liudian's rule for the markets of the two capitals, and the essay does not itself say that rule held in a county town. The county-seat rule was also a prescription of the early and middle Tang: the essay itself says the grass markets (caoshi) that sprang up outside the seats in the late Tang broke it.

Used for: that a Tang market stood only in a county or prefecture seat, and opened and closed by the drum (towns)
