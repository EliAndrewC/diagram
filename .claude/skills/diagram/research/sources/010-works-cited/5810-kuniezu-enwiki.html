What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the kuniezu (国絵図), the series of provincial land maps that the Tokugawa shogunate ordered every province to produce during the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it only for the plain fact that a central government of this kind commissioned province-wide maps, which is what makes a surveyed boundary on our maps a thing such a state would have had. Its limits are that it is a short tertiary summary of a single national program; and that the article lists what the maps showed - mountains, rivers, roads, landmarks, village names, rice yields, castle towns - and says nothing about boundaries drawn on them, so that half of the record's claim rests on other sources or stands as a guess.

Used for: the shogunate's provincial maps (the page lists their contents - mountains, rivers, roads, landmarks, village names, rice yields, castle towns - and says nothing about boundaries drawn on them)
