Sun Meiling, Si Gaoyang, Zhang Ke and Wang Xin 2014, 「How the "sense of security" of the entrance spaces of Zhejiang's old villages was made」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「浙江古村落入口空间"安全感"的营造手法研究」), Journal of Beijing Forestry University, Social Sciences (北京林业大学学报(社会科学版)) 13(3), 55-59 (in Chinese; https://sheke.bjfu.edu.cn/cn/article/pdf/preview/9145.pdf)

What it is: A short paper by landscape architects of Zhejiang A&F University surveying the entrances of about twenty old villages in Zhejiang, with a table of what stands at each - gates, bridges, groves, water mouths, ancestral halls and temples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives named village temples standing at a village's water mouth, its head or its entrance, and reads their siting as the borrowing of the notion of an "auspicious site" from nature-deity worship. It is a 2014 field survey by landscape architects of what stands today, written to guide the design of modern residential areas: it dates none of the temples it names, and the reading of their siting as an "auspicious site" is the authors' own interpretation, not a period text's. It describes villages that survive today in one province, most of them rich lineage villages, and it names mountain-god, Guandi and local temples there; its table lists the earth-god temple at only one village's entrance, and it gives no count of how many villages sited a temple so.

Used for: temples at a Chinese village's water mouth and entrance, read as an auspicious site (religion-and-death.html).
