matsuura-2017-minuma

Matsuura Shigeki, "The establishment and development of the Minuma rice fields", Yagai Chosa Kenkyu no. 1 (2017), pp. 23-39 (in Japanese; https://sfaa371d6a73480f4.jimcontent.com/download/version/1534067772/module/8035420615/name/%E8%A6%8B%E6%B2%BC%E3%81%9F%E3%82%93%E3%81%BC%E3%81%AE%E6%88%90%E7%AB%8B%E3%81%A8%E5%B1%95%E9%96%8B.pdf)

What it is: An article by a civil engineer and historian of river works (formerly of the Ministry of Construction's Public Works Research Institute, later a professor at Toyo University) on the Minuma paddies of Saitama, from the reservoir of 1629 through the Minuma canal and reclamation of 1727-28 to the postwar land improvement and urban growth; its maps are modern drawings (the land-improvement district's of 1998, the prefectural history's of 1989, the author's sketches of the 1673 and 1701 proposals), not period maps.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the layout Izawa's canal gave the reclaimed floor - the two supply trunks on the terrace edge a step above the paddies, the drain on their lowest ground - and adds that the separation was not everywhere neat, and it says those paddies were poorly drained wet paddies until the postwar drainage works. It is one large reclaimed marsh on the Kanto plain, not a hill hamlet's fan, and the article is in a small society's journal whose scanned PDF has no text layer. Its sentence on the separated layout cites no period document: it is the author's reading of the system as it stands today, after the land improvement of 1929-1986 in its table 3. Its evidence that the floor was wet paddy is the 1910 1:50,000 topographic map, a Meiji survey read back to the Edo reclamation.

Used for: the separated layout of 1728 at the scale of the paddies, and the wet ground beside its drain (water)
