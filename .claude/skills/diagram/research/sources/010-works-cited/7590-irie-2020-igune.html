irie-2020-igune

入江彦昭ほか, "Research on the multifunctionality of the homestead grove igune as green infrastructure", 東京農業大学農学集報 65(1) 9-18 (2020) (https://agriknowledge.affrc.go.jp/RN/2010935166.pdf)

What it is: A 2020 paper in the Journal of Agricultural Science, Tokyo University of Agriculture, on what the igune - the homestead grove of the Sendai plain - does for the farmstead it stands around, argued as green infrastructure. Its full text is public on the agriculture ministry’s repository.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the siting rule this project’s homestead belts follow, with its reason: the plain’s winter winds are mainly north-northwesterly and strong, so the grove is formed on the north or the west side and often lacks the south or the east - and it records that groves built on those sides have been kept as windbreaks for several hundred years, which is what makes the rule usable for a premodern setting rather than a modern landscaping habit - though that sentence is itself footnoted to a 1993 local-history compilation rather than to a documentary study. It replaces a 1915 definition the record had been leaning on. Its limits: the rule reaches us at second hand, attributed by the paper to Nakajima’s 1963 monograph, which this project has not read; it is one region, whose north-northwesterly wind is not every map’s wind; and the paper’s own subject is the surviving modern groves and what they are worth today, its planting inventory taken at three farmsteads.
