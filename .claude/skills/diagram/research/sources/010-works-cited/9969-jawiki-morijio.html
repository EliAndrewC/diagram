jawiki-morijio

Japanese Wikipedia, "盛り塩" (morijio) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%9B%9B%E3%82%8A%E5%A1%A9)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article on morijio, its form and the stories told of its origin.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the form - salt heaped into a cone or a three-sided pyramid and set at the entrance or inside the house. The article flags itself as without verifiable sources, and its claims of an origin in the Nara or Heian period rest on nothing, so it is used for the shape alone, which the other pages do not describe. That shape is today's practice: the article has the salt pressed into a cone or pyramid with a salt-molding tool on a dish, and cites a 2025 funeral-review site and a 2025 shrine-salt shop for its details, with no date for the form. It does not show that an Edo-period or earlier heap took this shape. A molded, sharp-edged heap may be a modern refinement of a rougher hand-piled one.

Used for: the shape of a morijio heap, a cone or a three-sided pyramid (religion-and-death).
