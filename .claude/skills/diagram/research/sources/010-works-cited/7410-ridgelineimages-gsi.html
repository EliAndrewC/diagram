Ridgeline Images, "Reading GSI Topographic Maps" (https://ridgelineimages.com/maps/reading-gsi-topographic-maps/) - the bamboo-forest symbol described as its own category beside broad-leaved forest, coniferous forest and the bamboo-grass (sasa) zone

What it is: "Reading GSI Topographic Maps", a guide on Ridgeline Images, a website about hiking and mapping in Japan, which walks through the legend of the topographic map series published by the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan. It shows the bamboo grove carrying its own symbol, listed separately from broad-leaved forest, from coniferous forest, and from the dwarf bamboo (sasa) zone of the high ridges.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that Japanese topographic maps give bamboo its own symbol beside broad-leaved forest, conifers and bamboo grass, which is the convention our maps borrow when they draw a grove differently from a wood. Its limits are that it is a hiker's blog explaining the legend rather than the agency's own legend document, though that document is public and linked from the page and is the closer source; and that it describes the present-day map series, so it is evidence for how Japanese maps show bamboo now and not for any older practice - a map drawing convention, not a physical claim.

Used for: the bamboo stand drawn as its own class of ground, distinct from woodland and from dwarf bamboo.
