What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Motoyashiki kiln site at Toki (Gifu prefecture), a national historic site with the oldest multi-chamber climbing kiln in Mino, of the early 17th century: its length, width and chambers, and the workshops found beside the kilns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives an excavated climbing kiln's measured length, width and chamber count, and records that the ground of a disused kiln became the workshop of the next. It is one early kiln in the principal pottery region, summarized by an encyclopedia that cites a prefectural handbook and the city history rather than the excavation report; its builder and Keichō date are given as tradition. The 2.2 m is an average width, the chambers growing larger up the slope, and the roof has fallen, so the floor and lower walls give its plan but not its height. The disused kiln that became a workshop was one of the earlier single-chamber great kilns, not the climbing kiln; the article says only that workshops were found around the kilns. Later kilns elsewhere grew much longer.

Used for: a climbing kiln about 24 m long and averaging 2.2 m wide with fourteen chambers, and the workshop beside it (urban-features)
