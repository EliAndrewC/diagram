kiba-jawiki

ja.wikipedia, 木場 (Kiba) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/木場)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Kiba, the timber quarter of Fukagawa in Edo (now Koto ward, Tokyo), covering its founding as the city's timber yard, the timber merchants and raftsmen who worked there, and the kiba place names found elsewhere in Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page read for how Edo-period timber was handled at the water: floated in, worked and stored afloat in timber ponds, the kiba names across Japan lying on the sea or a river because logs came by sea or were floated down from upstream. It is a tertiary summary centered on one great city's specialized timber district, not on a provincial river landing, and it says nothing of piers, of stone, or of how a mixed river port was laid out. Its two claims are not equally supported: the account of timber floated and stored in ponds cites the Tokyo Metropolitan Library's note on the Edo Meisho Zue (1834-36), a source from the period, but the statement that kiba place names lie on the sea or a river because logs came by sea or were floated downstream carries no reference and is the article's own generalization.

Used for: timber floated, worked and stored in the water rather than landed at a pier (cities/river-cities)
