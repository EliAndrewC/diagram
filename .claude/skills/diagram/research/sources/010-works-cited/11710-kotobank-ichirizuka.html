kotobank-ichirizuka

Digital Daijisen, Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and other entries, s.v. 一里塚 ("milestone mound", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/一里塚-31425)

What it is: Several Japanese dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the milestone mound, among them the Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's, which says the mounds faced each other across the road and that their tree was, as a rule, an enoki. The other entries (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Nipponica, Britannica, Yamakawa) date the system to the shogunate's order of 1604, which set a pair of mounds every ri along the main highways from Nihonbashi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It confirms the pairing and the tree for the Edo period, the setting's own model, from standard dictionaries. It is a tertiary source: short dictionary summaries, not period records or a survey of the surviving mounds. The entries differ in how firmly they put it: one says "as a rule", others "mostly". Five of them name pine alongside the enoki, and one adds cedar, so the enoki is the usual tree but not the only one. The mounds were distance markers the shogunate set on the main highways, one pair per ri. The source bears on a place that sits on such a road, not on a village or town entrance as such.

Used for: the enoki as the milestone mound's usual tree (vegetation)
