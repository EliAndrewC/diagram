Sado magistracy fire history - unsupported by any readable page: search snippets across Sado tourism pages state the 奉行所, built 1603, 「It repeated destruction by fire and rebuilding five times.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「焼失と再建を5回繰り返しました」); (https://www.asoview.com/spot/15224af2172090002/)

What it is: A listing for the Sado magistracy (佐渡奉行所) on asoview, a Japanese leisure and sightseeing booking site, carrying a short history of the building: put up in 1603 at the start of the Edo period, burned down and rebuilt five times thereafter, remodeled into offices and a school after the Meiji Restoration, named a national historic site in 1929, and destroyed entirely by fire in 1942.

Why it applies, and its limits: One plain point is taken from it - that a magistracy's halls were ordinary timber buildings that burned and were rebuilt again and again, which is why our administrative compounds are drawn as wooden and replaceable rather than monumental. It stands in because nothing closer could be read on a public page: the encyclopedia, museum and tourism pages about the same building describe only its modern reconstruction, so the count of five fires rests on this page alone, and the page is a commercial listing written for visitors, with no sources of its own and its Japanese rendered into English by this project.

Used for: administrative halls as ordinary wooden buildings that burned and were rebuilt repeatedly
