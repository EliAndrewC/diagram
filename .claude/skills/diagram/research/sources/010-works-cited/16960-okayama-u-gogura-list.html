okayama-u-gogura-list

Baba (Okayama University), "郷倉" ("Gogura", title translated), a ranked list of surviving village storehouses by prefecture (in Japanese; https://www.cc.okayama-u.ac.jp/~baba/ranking/49_goukura.html)

What it is: A page of Baba Shunsuke's database of civil-engineering and industrial heritage (on an Okayama University server) listing the surviving gogura across Japan that its compiler rates C or above - with some ice houses, a salt store, tool sheds and domain or merchant relief granaries (shasō, gisō) under the same heading - each with its form, date, designation and a note from its source: how many once stood on Sado, one built per hamlet section at Hataya, eight built jointly in the Nikko shrine estate.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gathers many surviving storehouses in one place, and its notes show the unit a storehouse served varying from place to place. Its limits are that it is a secondary compilation - of municipal boards of education, site signboards and web pages, with the compiler's own ratings - rather than a study of records; that each note is one line from its source; and that it lists only the buildings that survive and rate C or above, so the number it lists for a district is not how many the district had - only a note recording the original number carries that (96 once on Sado; 288 in Himeji domain, 5 left). Nearly all were built 1780-1860 under particular bakufu or domain famine-relief orders, so the unit each served is that policy's, not a general rule.

Used for: how many storehouses a district had and what unit each served (urban-features)
