kotobank-sakaya

kotobank 酒屋 ("sake house", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%85%92%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the encyclopedia entries on the sake house, brewer and retailer, among them the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry by Wakita Haruko (medieval) and Yunoki Manabu (early modern), the Nipponica entry by Endo Motoo and the Mypedia entry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the shogunate first confined brewing to castle towns, post towns and ports, licensed every brewer, and only in the eighteenth century let landlords brew in the villages, and that brewers commonly lent money and took pledges - which is why a county town keeps a brewer and a village does not. Signed reference entries on Japan; they give no count of brewers per town and no building sizes.

Used for: brewing confined by law to towns and licensed, brewers as moneylenders and pawnbrokers (urban-features)
