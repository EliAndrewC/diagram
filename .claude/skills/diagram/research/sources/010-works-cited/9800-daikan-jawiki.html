daikan-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 代官 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/代官)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the daikan, the intendant who administered land on a lord's behalf, chiefly the Edo shogunate's intendants of its own lands, with their staff, their offices and a list of intendancies.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page read for the size of an intendant's staff: about ten shogunal retainers (tetsuki) and a few locally hired clerks (tedai), and at one late-Edo intendancy a single intendant, three tetsuki and ten tedai. It is an encyclopedia article flagged for missing citations. Its one scholarly citation (Nakata 2002) supports only its history of how the intendancy became a salaried office, from 1657 through the Kyōhō reforms (1725), and both staff figures are uncited; the Gojō count is given as hearsay ("it is said"). The counts are of officials and clerks only: the article says menial ashigaru and chūgen served beside them, so more hands worked at the office than the figures give. Its two figures do not agree on the mix (about ten tetsuki and a few tedai, against three tetsuki and ten tedai at Gojō). They are for the shogunate's intendancies, which varied in size: one staff ran a district usually of several tens of thousands of koku (Gojō's was 70,000), not a single town. Intendants near the Kantō lived in Edo and came only for surveys, inspections, tours and serious incidents.

Used for: the small staff of a daikansho, against the Chinese county's clerks (buildings)
