oamishirasato-choshi-kaoku

Oamishirasato Town History, part 1, chapter 3 (the early modern period), section 4, "(5) Houses", pp. 399-404 (in Japanese; Oamishirasato City Digital Museum, https://adeac.jp/oamishirasato-city/texthtml/d100010/mp100010-100010/ht011770)

What it is: The chapter of the Oamishirasato town history (Chiba prefecture; compiled by the town from 1980 and published in 1986) on the houses of its Edo-period farmers, published online by the city's digital museum. It prints a building-by-building table of the 16 households of Kakimochi village from a household count of 1885, with the size in ken of every main house, retirement house, stable, privy, wood shed, storage shed, nagaya and storehouse, and describes a headman's homestead plan of 1849.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one count in the record of how many farms in a village had each kind of outbuilding that its historian dates to before 1868. But the count was made in 1885, and the historian reads it back to the bakumatsu only on the ground that houses were not often rebuilt, with a hedge ("it cannot simply be taken as Edo-period house size"). It is one village of 16 households in one district of Chiba, so its shares are a calibration, not a national figure. The retirement houses on half its households are more local still: the page puts them down to a retirement custom "characteristic of the villages of the town area" (町域村々で特徴的な), in which the retiring head took a son of his own with him, so that share is this district's practice and not a rule for every farmstead. The 1849 plan is one rich headman's house, and the page says ordinary farmers' houses were small. The page does not say what its wood shed (木小屋) held; that it is a firewood shed is this project's reading, supported by the same plan's firewood barn and by the Boso-no-Mura museum's gloss.

Used for: the premodern count of a farmstead's outbuildings - privy on all 16 households, stable and retirement house on 8, wood shed on 6, storage shed and nagaya on 3 each, storehouse on 2 - and the sizes of the storehouse and the wood sheds; the headman's homestead of 1849 (homesteads)
