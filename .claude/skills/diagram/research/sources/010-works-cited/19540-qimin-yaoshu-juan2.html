賈思勰 齊民要術 卷第二 ("Essential Techniques for the Common People, Scroll Two", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (Jia Sixie, Qimin Yaoshu, c. 540), Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%8C)

What it is: Book 2 of Jia Sixie's sixth-century (Northern Wei) Chinese agricultural treatise, with its chapters on millets, pulses, hemp, wheat and barley, wet and dry rice, sesame, melons, gourds and taro, in an unsigned Wikisource transcription; the rice chapter (水稻第十一) sets out the season of a flooded rice field from sowing to harvest.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text of imperial China, about a thousand years older than the Ming, Qing and Edo practice the setting mostly draws on, giving the stages of a paddy's water: weeds cut under the water, the water let out after weeding so the roots are sunned firm, watering by the season's weather, and the water taken off again as the rice ripens. It describes North China in the sixth century, where millet and not rice was the staple, and says nothing of Japan. Its main method broadcasts sprouted seed straight into the flooded field, with seedlings transplanted only on the northern upland fields, whereas the paddies of South China and Japan were planted out from seedbeds. It gives no depth of water.

Used for: the paddy's water let out and let in again through the season (fields)
