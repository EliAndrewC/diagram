shakkanho-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 尺貫法 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/尺貫法)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the shakkanho, the traditional Japanese system of weights and measures, a tertiary entry that tabulates each unit against the metric system - among them the tsubo at 400/121 square meters, the se at thirty tsubo, and the tan at ten se.

Why it applies, and its limits: Every area a Japanese source states in tsubo, se or tan has to be turned into square meters before it can be drawn, and this is the conversion we use; the figures are exact definitions rather than measurements, so there is little for a source to be wrong about. Its limits are that it is a tertiary encyclopedia article; that the article's own values are the legally fixed modern definitions of units whose local sizes varied before they were standardized, so a premodern document's tsubo may not be exactly this tsubo; and - the one that can put a figure badly wrong rather than slightly - that the tan was redefined in the course of Hideyoshi's cadastral survey, from 360 bu to the 300 bu this page's tan assumes, so an area a pre-1590s document states in tan and we convert with this table comes out about a fifth too small. The tsubo itself is stable across that change, so an area given in tsubo is safe.

Used for: the shakkanho land measures the record converts from: the tsubo, the se and the tan in square meters
