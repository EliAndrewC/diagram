kanamaruza-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 旧金毘羅大芝居 ("Old Konpira Grand Theater", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/旧金毘羅大芝居)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Kanamaruza at Kotohira, the playhouse of 1835 in the town before the Kotohira shrine's gate, the oldest surviving playhouse in Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a permanent roofed playhouse standing in a shrine town, the case nearest to the provincial-town theater this page draws. A tertiary summary; the article does not say the house stood on the shrine's own ground. It gives the building's measures as built, the one Edo-period playhouse whose size this record has read, but it cites no source for them (it lists the 1976 restoration report, which this record has not read). The house standing now was taken down, moved from its first site nearer the approach road and rebuilt in 1972-76, then reworked in 2003-04, so the article's seat count and floor area are for the rebuilt building. It says the town, as Kotohira's vermilion-seal and shogunal land, was loosely policed, which is why shows were allowed there. It was modeled on the Ōnishi theater, one of Osaka's three Dōtonbori houses (by another account on Osaka's Chikugo-za), which is the ground for taking its size for a city house, and a pilgrimage town's playhouse may have been smaller or larger than a capital's.

Used for: the Kanamaruza of 1835 in Kotohira's gate-front town; its measures, its Osaka model and the temporary huts before it (urban-features)
