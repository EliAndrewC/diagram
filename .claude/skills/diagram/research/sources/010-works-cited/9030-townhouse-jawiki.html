What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the town house, whose historical section describes the Edo back-lane tenement: ridge-split along the lane, nine shaku by two ken, a four-and-a-half-mat room with a mat and a half of earth floor for the stove and sink, rented by the great majority of the common inhabitants, with the shared well and privy at the lane's end.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the back-to-back pair our rows are packed in - mune-wari, one roof over two ranks sharing a rear wall - as the standard form rather than as a deduction, and gives the unit its size. Its limits are that it is tertiary; that its subject is Edo, so the dimensions are the standard nine-shaku-two-ken formula rather than a measured survey; and that it describes the rented tenement and not the merchant house in front of it.

Used for: the ridge-split back-lane pair and its unit size, and the shared well and privy at the end of the lane
