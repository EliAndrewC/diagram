bicchu-sojagu-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, 備中国総社宮 ("Bitchū Province Sōja-gū") (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%82%99%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E7%B7%8F%E7%A4%BE%E5%AE%AE)

What it is: The encyclopedia article on the sōja of Bitchū province in Sōja, Okayama, explaining why provinces set up a sōja near their provincial capital and how the town grew around it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a province keeping one combined shrine, the sōja, where the gods of all its shrines were worshipped together, near its provincial seat of government, with a town later grown around it. Its limits: it is a tertiary source, an encyclopedia article whose history rests on the shrine's own leaflet and a gazetteer (Heibonsha's 岡山県の地名); the sōja is the governor's combined shrine, not the province's first-ranked shrine (the ichinomiya, which stood elsewhere), so it stands for a capital's official shrine and not the province's greatest one; the sōja is an institution of the ancient provincial government, older than the castle towns, and the town around it grew in the Edo period as a shrine-gate and post town after the 1687 rebuilding, not as a seat of government; one shrine; no precinct area is given.

Used for: a provincial capital's principal shrine and where it stood (religion-and-death.html).
