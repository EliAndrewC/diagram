ja.wikipedia 桂離宮 (Katsura Rikyu) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/桂離宮)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Katsura Imperial Villa in Kyoto, the country house of the Hachijo princely house, whose shoin halls - the Ko-shoin, the Naka-shoin, the Gakki-no-ma and the Shin-goten - were built one after another from about 1615 to about 1662 and stand in echelon; it describes each hall room by room, its verandas included, drawing on the published studies and the 1976-1982 restoration.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the best-described surviving case of shoin halls set in echelon and grown by accretion, and it gives veranda widths, in ken, for the Ko-shoin, the Naka-shoin and the Gakki-no-ma (none for the Shin-goten). Its limits are that this is a princely villa with sukiya elements, not a warrior's official residence; that its ken is the long side of a tatami mat as the article defines it, about 2 meters by the article's own figures (7.5 ken = 15.8 m), not the six-shaku ken; that the Naka-shoin's tatami veranda was at first an open veranda, enclosed later (probably when the Shin-goten was added), so it shows the hall as altered, not as first built; that its dates are scholars' estimates from the princes' diaries and the 1976-1982 restoration's findings, the Shin-goten's date and purpose disputed; and that it is a tertiary summary.

Used for: the four shoin halls in echelon, built in phases over about half a century; the Ko-shoin's one-ken veranda narrowing to half a ken round the corner; the Naka-shoin's half-ken irikawa (buildings)
