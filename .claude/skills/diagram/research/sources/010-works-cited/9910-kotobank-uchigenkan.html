kotobank-uchigenkan

Digital Daijisen and Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, s.v. 内玄関 (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%86%85%E7%8E%84%E9%96%A2-434837)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of two modern Japanese dictionaries' entries on 内玄関 (Digital Daijisen; Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, under both readings, uchigenkan and naigenkan): an entrance a house kept for its family and household in addition to its formal front entrance (表玄関), which the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten also glosses as 裏玄関, a back entrance.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the household's own entrance, kept apart from the formal one for the family's daily comings and goings. These are modern dictionary lines on a Japanese word: they give no plan, no position (the entrance is set up in addition to the front one, not placed beside it, and "back entrance" is given as a synonym), no rank, and nothing about the kitchen door. That the family used it instead of the kitchen's door is our reading, not the dictionary's. Its earliest example, under the reading naigenkan, is a joruri of 1714: the word's first recorded use, not the date of any building. The reading uchigenkan is first cited from a 1930 novel. It says nothing of a Chinese house.

Used for: the inner entrance for the family, kept in addition to the formal entrance (buildings)
