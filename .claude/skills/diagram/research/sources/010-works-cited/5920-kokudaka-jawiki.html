What it is: Japanese Wikipedia's article on 石高 (kokudaka), the rice-volume measure used to rate land and to pay retainers, including what one koku was taken to represent and what a retainer was actually issued.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use its rule of thumb - one koku as an adult's rice for a year - to turn a settlement's assessed yield into a number of mouths it could feed, and the article supplies its own hedge, that a standard stipend ran nearer 1.8 koku of brown rice, which keeps the round figure from being treated as exact. The koku equivalence is in any case a convention of assessment rather than a measured diet.

Used for: one koku as an adult's annual rice (「One koku corresponds to the quantity of rice that one adult eats in one year.」 (translated; original: 「一石は大人一人が一年に食べる米の量に相当する」)), with the page's own hedge that real stipends ran ~1.8 koku of brown rice
