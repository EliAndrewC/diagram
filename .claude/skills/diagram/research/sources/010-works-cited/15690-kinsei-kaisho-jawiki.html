ja.wikipedia 会所 (近世) ("kaisho, the early-modern meeting house", title translated from the Japanese by this project), reached from 町会所 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/町会所)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the early-modern kaisho, the building townspeople used as a guild's office, an exchange, or a ward's own office and meeting place.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what a ward's machi-kaisho was for - the ward officials posted there passed on the magistrate's notices and drew up the residents' papers - and that cities differed: Osaka had one in every ward, while in Edo the ward headman's residence served as the kaisho. It is a tertiary source, a stub article. The passage on what the ward officials did there cites nothing, and the Osaka detail rests on a single 1997 local-history newsletter, so it can show what the practice looked like but not supply a figure. It describes the great cities of the Edo period, not a place of our size, and in Edo the name 町会所 also meant the one city-wide relief-fund office set up after the Kansei reforms, which was not a ward's office. It says nothing of the building's size or where on the street it stood.

Used for: the townspeople's own office, and that it was often a house rather than a building of its own (towns)
