shirobito-castle-bridges

Kamiyu Rekishi Henshūbu, Shirobito (endorsed by the Japan Castle Foundation), 超入門！お城セミナー 第62回お城は橋を戦術的に使い分けていた？ ("Castle seminar 62: did castles use their bridges tactically?", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://shirobito.jp/article/755)

What it is: An introductory article of 15 April 2019 by Kamiyu Rekishi Henshūbu, a commercial history editorial company, on Shirobito, a castle-fan website run by Tohoku Shinsha and endorsed (not run) by the Japan Castle Foundation, on the two kinds of castle bridge - the earthen causeway and the timber bridge - and which kind of gate each tended to serve, in medieval mountain castles and early-modern castles.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where each crossing tended to go and how broadly an early-modern causeway was built, which is what a map's gate crossings are chosen from. It is a popular article with no references, hedged in its own words ("seems to have tended"), and it gives no measurements. Its bridges cross the moats between the wards of a Japanese castle, not the moat in front of a town wall's gate, which is a different scale of place. It says nothing of how Chinese walled cities crossed their moats, so for a town modeled on China it is a Japanese analog.

Used for: timber bridges at rear gates, causeways at sortie and main gates; early-modern causeways stone-faced and very wide (defenses)
