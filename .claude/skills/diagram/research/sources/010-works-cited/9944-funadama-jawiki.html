funadama-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, "船霊" (funadama, the boat's guardian spirit) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%88%B9%E9%9C%8A)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the funadama, the guardian spirit sailors and fishermen keep in a boat: what the sacred objects are, where in the boat they are placed, its taboos, and the shrines set up to it on land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the guardian is kept aboard and only sometimes given a shrine on land, which bears on whether a landing had a boatmen's shrine. It is a tertiary summary, and the passages we use (the objects kept aboard, the shrine sometimes set up on land, the fishermen and shipwrights who chiefly keep it) cite no source of their own; its references are a Japanese dictionary, a 1989 Izumo Taishakyo training manual, a shrine's website and the Shoku Nihongi. It dates a funadama festival to 758 and the name to 763 from the Shoku Nihongi, but gives no date for the customs we use, and it mixes old practice with recent (talismans placed in a boat's engine room). It speaks of seafarers and says nothing of river boatmen in particular, and its examples of land shrines (at Sumiyoshi Taisha in Osaka and in Hakodate) are not shrines at a landing.

Used for: the boat's guardian kept aboard, sometimes given a shrine on land, and worshipped chiefly by fishermen and shipwrights (river cities)
