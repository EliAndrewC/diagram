kokuga-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 国衙 ("provincial government office", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/国衙)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the kokuga, the provincial government office of the ancient imperial state (founded in the late 7th century, stable from the 8th, absorbed into the military governor's seat in the early Muromachi period; its excavated sites date from the 7th to the 10th century), with a list of excavated provincial offices and the size and plan the excavations found at each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the ancient Japanese tradition of an imperial governor's seat at the heart of a provincial town, and it gives measured plans: a main hall facing south with side halls east and west, an office compound of a few tens of meters to about 100 m square, larger whole office districts (about 300 by 200 m at Musashi, 273 by 227 m at Hoki), and at Shimotsuke an excavated road 9 m wide running south from the south gate. Its limits are that these are archaeological reconstructions of sites of the late 7th to 10th centuries, several hedged as estimates, an encyclopedia summary rather than the excavation reports, and that the article itself says the plans differ more than they agree from province to province. Its picture of the provincial town as a planned grid (an eight- or nine-chō square at Omi, a grid and a "Suzaku avenue" at Suo, the latter read from surviving place-names) follows an older model that later excavation has largely dropped: most provincial towns had their offices scattered around a center rather than laid on a grid. So the avenue south from the gate rests on Shimotsuke's excavated road, not on a general town plan.

Used for: the size and plan of a Japanese provincial office, and the avenue running south from its gate (cities/government)
