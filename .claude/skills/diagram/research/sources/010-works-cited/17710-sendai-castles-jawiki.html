What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia list article on the residences of Sendai domain's fief-holding retainers, sorted by the five forms in which the domain granted them, from a castle down to a residence the retainer set up unaided in the fief.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a large domain that paid its retainers in land and whose fief-holders kept residences, with houses for their own retainers and foot soldiers, out in their fiefs rather than only in the castle town. The article cites no sources, it counts none of the retainers, and its lists name only the principal seats held by upper retainers. It describes Sendai alone, and Sendai was the exception: most Edo domains had moved their retainers to rice stipends drawn in the castle town by the late 1600s, and only a minority of large domains, Sendai and Satsuma among them, kept paying them in land. So it shows a practice that was the exception in Edo Japan, not the rule.

Used for: Sendai retainers' residences within their own fiefs (cities/sizing)
