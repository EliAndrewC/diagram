kojima-sensoji-bettodai

Kojima Nobuyasu, 「How the acting superintendent of Sensoji was appointed in the late early-modern period」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世後期における浅草寺別当代の就任過程」), Soka University institutional repository (https://soka.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/35917/files/sokahogaku28_1_2.pdf)

What it is: A legal-history article, apparently from the Soka University law journal (the file name suggests volume 28, nos. 1-2; the extracted text names neither journal nor date), on how Sensoji in Edo, a great temple made of many sub-temples, had its acting superintendent appointed under the shogunate's temple law, with a chronology of the temple compiled from the shogunate's survey records.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its chronology records that the superintendent Chūkai, in the reign of Emperor Go-Tsuchimikado, cut the more than a hundred temples within Sensoji's precinct to thirty-three - twelve cloisters of scholar-monks and twenty-one of ritual monks - and it sets out, after Ishii Ryōsuke, the three kinds of Edo temple: the single temple, the complex of sub-temples, and the sub-temple. It counts cloisters, not monks, and describes one of the largest temples of the shogun's capital.

Used for: the great city temple as a complex of sub-temples (religion and death)
