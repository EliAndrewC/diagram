kojima-sensoji-bettodai

Kojima Nobuyasu, 「How the acting superintendent of Sensoji was appointed in the late early-modern period」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世後期における浅草寺別当代の就任過程」), Soka University institutional repository (https://soka.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/35917/files/sokahogaku28_1_2.pdf)

What it is: A legal-history article by Kojima Nobuyasu in Sōka Hōgaku (Soka Law Review, Soka University), vol. 28 no. 1, December 1998, pp. 3-36 (per the repository's record), on how Sensoji in Edo, a great temple made of many sub-temples, had its acting superintendent appointed under the shogunate's temple law, with a chronology of the temple compiled from the shogunate's survey records.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its chronology records that the superintendent Chūkai, in the reign of Emperor Go-Tsuchimikado, cut the more than a hundred temples within Sensoji's precinct to thirty-three - twelve cloisters of scholar-monks and twenty-one of ritual monks - and it sets out its own three kinds of Edo temple: the single temple, the complex of sub-temples, and the sub-temple (the legal historian Ishii Ryōsuke, whom it cites, counts only the first and third as temples). It counts cloisters, not monks, and describes one of the largest temples of the shogun's capital. The cut to thirty-three is dated to Go-Tsuchimikado's reign (1464-1500), in the Muromachi period a century and more before the Edo period the article studies, and the article carries it as the temple's own later account of its medieval past rather than a record made at the time, so the "more than a hundred" before it is the weakest figure here. The sub-temple counts and the three kinds are background to an article whose subject is how a superintendent was appointed.

Used for: the great city temple as a complex of sub-temples (religion and death)
