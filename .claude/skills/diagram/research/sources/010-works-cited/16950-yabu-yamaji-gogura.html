yabu-yamaji-gogura

Yabu City, "まちの文化財（85） 山路の郷蔵" ("The town's cultural properties (85): the Yamaji gogura", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.city.yabu.hyogo.jp/soshiki/kyoikuiinkai/shakaikyoiku/1/1/1992.html)

What it is: The city's page on a surviving village storehouse at Yamaji in Hyogo: the shogunate's order to build reserve storehouses, and the single storehouse that still stands there, its door facing the former prefectural road, with its measurements (340 cm across the front, 290 cm deep, 500 cm to the roof; two storeys under tile).

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a real storehouse where it still stands, which is the one siting observation found. Its limits are that it is one building on one road, in the one district of Tajima where the page says such storehouses survive; that it does not date this building, so what stands may be a later rebuild; that its "only one building stands" says there is a single storehouse, not how far it stands from houses; that the road its door faces is named as the former prefectural road, a modern designation not shown to follow the Edo road; and that it dates the shogunate's order (1790) a year later than the dictionaries do. It does give the building's size.

Used for: a village storehouse, a single building with its door to the road, and its size (urban-features)
