orange-hatchobori-yoriki

A personal Edo-history page, 南北町奉行所跡 ("The sites of the South and North town magistracies", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (http://orange.zero.jp/kkubota.bird/machibugyousho.htm)

What it is: A personal web page on the sites of the Edo town magistracies and the Hatchobori district where their staff lived, written about 2004 (its photographs are dated 2004/01), quoting modern reference works - chiefly the Edo-Tokyo encyclopedia (江戸東京学事典, Sanseido) and Kasama Yoshihiko's dictionary of Edo policing (江戸の司法警察事典, Kashiwa Shobo), with a Tokyo place-name dictionary (東京の地名がわかる事典) for the constables' plots and houses - for the plot sizes of the magistracies' officers and constables.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the plot sizes that show a magistracy's staff lived outside its compound on ground of their own, one of the two forms of our Imperial Magistrate's staff housing. It is a personal page quoting modern reference works without page numbers, so it is a tertiary source for these figures. It describes one city only - Edo, the shogun's capital, from about 1660 (when Hatchobori became the magistracies' group housing) to the 1860s - and the staff of that city's two town magistracies, about 25 officers and 100 to 120 constables each; it bears on a capital's magistracy, not a county seat's. The page places both officers and constables at Hatchobori, though the 200 to 300 tsubo sentence does not itself name the place. A constable's plot of about 100 tsubo was mostly let to tenants (scholars, physicians, artists), the constable living in a house of about 30 tsubo, so the plot is not one household's yard.

Used for: the magistracy officers' granted plots of about 200 to 300 tsubo, and the constables' plots of somewhat over 100 tsubo in group housing at Hatchobori (cities/capitals)
