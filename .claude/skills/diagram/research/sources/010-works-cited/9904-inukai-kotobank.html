inukai-kotobank

Kotobank, 犬飼 (inukai) - the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Nipponica entry by Kaneko Shun'ichi (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%8A%AC%E9%A3%BC-31874)

What it is: The Kotobank page for inukai, collecting two dictionary definitions of the word as the keeper who raised hunting dogs for falconry and four encyclopedia entries (Nipponica, Britannica, Mypedia, Heibonsha) on the former town of Inukai in Oita prefecture; only the Nipponica entry, whose name it traces to an Edo-period hunt held by Taketa domain.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows raising dogs for hawking as a recognized trade, and a domain lord hunting with dogs and caring for one himself. The definitions are brief tertiary entries that say nothing of where the dogs were kept, and their only dated example is a court text of 834 (the Ruiju Sandai Kyaku), so they show the trade in the Heian court, not in the Edo period. The Taketa story is a local place-name tradition from a 1978 town history, told as hearsay; it may be a folk explanation of an older name, and the hunt it describes is not said to be a hawking hunt.

Used for: the dog-keeper who raised hawking dogs; a domain lord nursing his own hunting dog (buildings)
