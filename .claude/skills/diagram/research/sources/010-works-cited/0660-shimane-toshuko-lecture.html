shimane-toshuko-lecture

Shimane University, rural-engineering lecture notes on irrigation facilities, chapter 3, head works (頭首工) (https://zoukou.life.shimane-u.ac.jp/ruraleng/shisetsuken/12kougisiryou/15.pdf)

What it is: A printed textbook chapter on the head works, hosted as course material by Shimane University's rural engineering program: their parts, a line on their history (small timber-and-stone works up to the end of the Sengoku period), the rule of one-bank against two-bank intake, where the intake mouth is set, and the intake court between the mouth and the canal.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states two rules a map needs plainly - the intake is on one bank, the side of the land it serves, on every kind of river from torrent to gentle, and it stands where the current's deep line touches that bank - and it dates the small timber-crib form to the period before the early modern age. Its limits are that it is teaching material for modern design, its velocities and depths belong to concrete works, and it gives no distance from the intake to the first division.

Used for: intake works up to the end of the Sengoku period as small timber frames packed with stone; one-bank intake on the benefited side, the rule on torrent, steep and gentle rivers; the intake where the current touches the bank; the intake court between mouth and canal
