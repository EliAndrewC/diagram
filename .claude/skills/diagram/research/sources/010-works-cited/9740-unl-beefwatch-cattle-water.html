University of Nebraska-Lincoln, BeefWatch, Meeting Water Needs of Cattle in the Feedlot (2022) (https://beef.unl.edu/beefwatch/2022/meeting-water-needs-cattle-feedlot/)

What it is: An extension article in the University of Nebraska-Lincoln's BeefWatch newsletter on getting water to feedlot cattle in hot weather: daily intake against dry matter eaten, the rate at which cattle drink, the minutes each animal spends at the trough, and how much trough edge and flow a pen needs.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the rate at which a bovine drinks from a trough, which is what sets how fast a trough's edge turns over. It is written for modern feedlot beef cattle under heat stress, not for draft oxen at a premodern yard; we take only the drinking rate, a matter of the animal rather than of the farm, and its intake figures are for heavier, grain-fed animals than a working ox.

Used for: the rate at which cattle drink at a trough (stable yards)
