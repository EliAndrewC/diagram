okayama-chikusanshi-shiyo

Okayama Prefecture Livestock Association, Okayama-ken chikusanshi (History of livestock in Okayama prefecture), vol. 1, ch. 1, sec. 1, "Keeping livestock" (in Japanese; https://okayama.lin.gr.jp/tosyo/history/1-1-1.htm)

What it is: The web edition of a prefectural history of livestock farming, in its overview of livestock up to the end of the domain era: which beasts were kept, where they were bred, how they were kept, and who owned them, with figures for Bizen in the mid-seventeenth century and the cattle-lending of the Ando headman's household of Bitchu in 1818-43.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes cattle-keeping in Edo-period villages of one western Japanese wet-rice region, where the draft beast was the ox, and it states flatly that the cattle shed was mostly inside the house. It is a compiled institutional history that gives no source for each sentence, it covers Okayama alone, and it names the lending arrangement (mayasaki) without describing where the lent beast stood, so it says nothing about a shed shared by several households either way.

Used for: that the cattle shed was mostly in the house, that most beasts were their keepers' own, and that large owners lent cattle out to tenants (homesteads)
