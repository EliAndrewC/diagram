ja.wikipedia 明倫館 ("Meirinkan", the Choshu domain school, title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%98%8E%E5%80%AB%E9%A4%A8)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Meirinkan, the domain school of Choshu (Hagi), founded in 1718 in the castle's third bailey and moved in 1849 to a far larger ground in the castle town.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives one domain school's ground twice - 940 tsubo at its founding, laid out around a Confucian hall with the martial halls as its outer ring, and 15,184 tsubo with a drill ground after the move - so it shows one school's ground at two moments and both the in-castle and the in-town siting. It is an encyclopedia article whose ground figures carry no citation. It is one large domain's school, which the article itself ranks among the three foremost academies of Japan and among the best-equipped of nearly 300 domain schools, so both grounds are an upper bound rather than a typical size. The 1849 ground, built in a late-Edo domain reform with a drill ground, is a mid-nineteenth-century form.

Used for: a domain school's ground, its Confucian hall and its siting (cities/capitals)
