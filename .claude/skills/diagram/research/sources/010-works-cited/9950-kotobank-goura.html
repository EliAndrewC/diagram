kotobank-goura

Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Yamakawa Nihonshi shojiten and Obunsha Nihonshi jiten, s.v. 郷蔵 (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%83%B7%E8%94%B5-2035847)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of the dictionary entries on the goura, the village storehouse of the Edo period for tax rice and famine grain: who built and ran it, where it stood, and where its rice went.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes a 1794 manual of rural administration on where a village storehouse was built and says where tax rice went from it, which places a granary in its chain and on its ground. Dictionary entries rather than a study; they say nothing of how the storehouse was built, and they date the shogunate's order for village-built storehouses to 1789 where other pages give 1790.

Used for: the village storehouse built within the village, mostly on dry-field ground, and the tax rice sent on from it (buildings)
