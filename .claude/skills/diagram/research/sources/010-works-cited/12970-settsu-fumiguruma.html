settsu-fumiguruma

Settsu City, 水車 ("Water wheels", section 11; title translated from the Japanese by this project), a two-page spread of the city's folk-tool catalog (https://www.city.settsu.osaka.jp/material/files/group/43/p24-25.pdf)

What it is: A spread from a local-history publication of Settsu city (Osaka), describing a treadle wheel in its collection, how it was worked, and what the farm-tool manual of 1822 says of its origin, beside a picture of the older chain pump.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures one surviving wheel (164 cm across, 17 blades), gives its lift as a few tens of centimeters, says one person could take it apart and carry it to the field, and records two-person wheels and a three-tier, six-person rig for draining floodwater. It is one low, wet district's tools, recorded from twentieth-century memory; the measured wheel is an undated survivor the page calls one of the large kind, so 164 cm is a big wheel, not a typical one, and the lift is the page's rough figure, not a measurement. By the 1822 manual it relays, the treadle wheel was made in Osaka in the Kanbun era (1661-73) and had spread through Japan by 1751-81, so it stands for Edo Japan only; before that, and in China, the page's own answer is the chain pump.

Used for: a treadle wheel's measured size, lift and portability; wheels ganged for drainage (water)
