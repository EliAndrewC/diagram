settsu-fumiguruma

Settsu City, 踏車 ("The treadle wheel", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a two-page spread of the city's folk-tool catalog (https://www.city.settsu.osaka.jp/material/files/group/43/p24-25.pdf)

What it is: A spread from a local-history publication of Settsu city (Osaka), describing a treadle wheel in its collection, how it was worked, and what the farm-tool manual of 1822 says of its origin, beside a picture of the older chain pump.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures one surviving wheel (164 cm across, 17 blades), gives its lift as a few tens of centimeters, says one person could take it apart and carry it to the field, and records two-person wheels and a three-tier, six-person rig for draining floodwater. It is one low, wet district's tools, recorded from twentieth-century memory.

Used for: a treadle wheel's measured size, lift and portability; wheels ganged for drainage (water)
