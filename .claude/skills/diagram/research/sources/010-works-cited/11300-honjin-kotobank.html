honjin-kotobank

Kotobank, s.v. 本陣 (honjin; the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), among others; in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9C%AC%E9%99%A3-133353)

What it is: The Kotobank dictionary page gathering Japanese reference works' entries on the honjin, the lodging house at a post station where daimyo and other persons of rank stayed: who used it, how many a station had, and what its building had.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the honjin of Edo-period Japan's post stations directly, and it gives the honjin's guests and the count per station. It is a set of dictionary entries, general across all highways, and gives no sizes. The count per station ("one or two on average, none at some, five or six at others") is the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's rule of thumb, and it names no survey behind it. The same page's firmer figures are Nipponica's totals from the 1843 (Tenpō 14) survey (111 honjin on the Tōkaidō, 72 on the Nakasendō, 24 on the Nikkō Dōchū) and Yamakawa's "one to a station in principle", with four at Odawara as its example. A station's count followed the sankin-kōtai traffic on its road.

Used for: who lodged at a honjin; one or two to a station on average, none at some, five or six at others (towns)
