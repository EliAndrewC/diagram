ja.wikipedia 五街道 ("the Five Highways", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BA%94%E8%A1%97%E9%81%93)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Edo shogunate's five main highways: their building from 1601, their widths, surfaces, avenues of trees and milestone mounds, and who kept them up.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a tertiary encyclopedia article, flagged by the wiki as a stub. Its footnotes lead mostly to Takebe Ken'ichi's 2015 road history 道路の日本史 and to Asai Kenji's popular road dictionaries. The late-Edo register of the highways' post towns and villages (宿村大概帳) reaches it only through those books, so a figure is carried from it only where it rests on Takebe. It describes the dressing of sand and gravel, the stone-paved exceptions and the villages' assigned stretches of upkeep. It describes the shogunate's trunk roads, the best-kept in the country, not village lanes or a domain's lesser roads. Its only specific sand dressing (Asakusa sand mixed with sea sand, the road built higher in the middle) is a 1648 order for Edo's city streets, not for the highways.

Used for: gravel and sand on the highways, the Hakone round stones, the Kyoto cart-stones, and the villages' share of the upkeep (ways)
