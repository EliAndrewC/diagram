hikone-umaya-bunka

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Online, "Hikone Castle stable" (彦根城馬屋) (in Japanese; https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/145098)

What it is: The national database entry for the stable of Hikone Castle, an Important Cultural Property: its date (mid-Edo, about 1688-1703), the length and depth of its two single-story wings, and a note that it has fittings for nineteen horses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one premodern stable read that is both dated and measured, so it is the record's calibration for a stall's width. It is a castle stable that kept the domain lord's horses (by the City of Hikone's account), not an inn's or a relay's, and the only surviving one of its kind by the entry's own account. The entry gives the wings' lengths and the horse count, not a stall's width, which is this record's division of the one by the other. That division is an upper bound: the wings also hold a small room at the east end for managing the horses (by the City of Hikone's account) and a well house. And the count is not settled: the entry says fittings for nineteen horses, while the City of Hikone counts twenty-one stalls and tethering places.

Used for: a premodern stable's length, depth and horse count, and the stall width worked from them (towns)
