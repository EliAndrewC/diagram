greatmingmilitary-fortification-gate

Great Ming Military, "Chinese fortification: an overview of parts and terminology - Part 2: Gate and moat" (https://greatmingmilitary.blogspot.com/2019/06/chinese-fortification-p2.html)

What it is: The second part of a specialist blog series on Chinese fortification, on the gate and the moat: the gate tunnel, the barbican and its neck, the wengcheng, and what each of them was for tactically, illustrated from Ming military manuals.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only readable text found that draws the comparison our gate rule rests on - the European barbican's narrow neck preventing a sortie against the wengcheng as a protected staging ground roomy enough to pass a body of troops either way. It is self-published and not peer-reviewed, so it sits below the scholarly work this record prefers; it is careful and consistent with the manuals it illustrates, but a claim resting on it alone is one specialist's synthesis rather than an attested statement, and it describes China, not Japan.

Used for: the sortie made from the wengcheng rather than through a sally port, and what a barbican's neck prevents
