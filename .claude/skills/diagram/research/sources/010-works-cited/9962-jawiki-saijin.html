jawiki-saijin

Japanese Wikipedia, "祭神" (saijin, the enshrined kami) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A5%AD%E7%A5%9E)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the kami a shrine enshrines, covering how they were named over the centuries and the division into a main kami and secondary ones.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states plainly that a shrine ordinarily enshrines several kami, one of them the main one, which is the practice this record needs. It is a tertiary summary, a stub that Japanese Wikipedia has flagged as short of sources since 2011, and it gives no figures for how many shrines kept more than one kami. Its "ordinarily several kami" describes shrines today, not a period. By its own account the formal division into a main kami and secondary kami began at the Meiji-era government shrines (only the idea is older, under names such as 相殿神). It says the Meiji shrine mergers left many shrines with more secondary kami. It also says that naming a shrine's kami after the kami of the myths reached village and unranked shrines only in the Meiji era. So for a pre-Meiji village shrine it supports only that several kami could share one hall with one of them foremost, not how common that was or what those kami were called.

Used for: that a shrine ordinarily enshrines several kami, a main one and secondary ones (religion-and-death).
