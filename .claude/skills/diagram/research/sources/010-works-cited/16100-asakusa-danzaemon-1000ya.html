asakusa-danzaemon-1000ya

Matsuoka Seigow, "1284夜 『浅草弾左衛門』 塩見鮮一郎" ("Night 1284: Shiomi Sen'ichiro, Asakusa Danzaemon", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Senya Sensatsu (in Japanese; https://1000ya.isis.ne.jp/1284.html)

What it is: A long essay (2009) by Matsuoka Seigow in his well-known Japanese book-review series, on Shiomi Sen'ichiro's historical novel Asakusa Danzaemon (Hihyosha, 1985-1987) about the last Danzaemon, head of the Edo outcastes; the essay does not retell the novel but sketches the history behind it, drawn from Shiomi's research and from Morita Yoshinori and Nakao Kenji, with the page's own captioned illustrations: his enclosure at Asakusa, its houses, its shrine and bathhouse, and how his residence was moved outward.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page found that gives the house count of the Asakusa enclosure (232 houses, a figure the page gives without a year; the 1800 record it cites just before counts Danzaemon's clerks and officials, not the houses) and names its Hakusan shrine. It is a reviewer's sketch of the history behind a novel, drawn from the novelist's research and other historians', not a history book or a primary record; it describes a special-status outcaste quarter rather than an ordinary town ward; it describes the seat of the Kanto head, which was far larger than any town's quarter, and its remark that Danzaemon spread the Hakusan shrines is the reviewer's own statement within that sketch, with no source named.

Used for: the Asakusa enclosure's house count, shrine and bathhouse, the 740-tsubo lot and pond of Danzaemon's own residence within it, and the Edo head's move outward (urban-features)
