jorokuji-hondo-bunka

Agency for Cultural Affairs, national designated cultural properties database, "丈六寺本堂（元方丈）" ("Jorokuji main hall, formerly the abbot's quarters", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://kunishitei.bunka.go.jp/heritage/detail/102/3244)

What it is: The national register's entry for the main hall of Jorokuji in Tokushima, built in 1629, with its measured length and depth, its roof and its tiling.

Why it applies, and its limits: A dated, measured early-Edo hall of a provincial temple (18.7 m by 11.8 m, 1629) is one size anchor for a temple's main hall. It was built as a Zen abbot's quarters (hōjō), a residence and reception building with a room-grid plan, and only later became the main hall; so it gives the size of that kind of building, not of a worship hall such as a Buddha hall or kondō, which in a large precinct could be bigger. It is a single Japanese building standing in for the setting's halls. The entry gives only the measurements and roof; it says nothing about the temple's rank or wealth, so it cannot show on its own whether this was a substantial temple or an ordinary one.

Used for: how big a temple's main hall was (religion-and-death)
