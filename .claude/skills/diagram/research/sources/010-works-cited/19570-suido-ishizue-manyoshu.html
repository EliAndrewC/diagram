suido-ishizue-manyoshu

"Column: the Man'yoshu and water", Suido no Ishizue (the foundations of land and water), Association of Rural Information Center (ARIC) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/04/10.html)

What it is: A short column in ARIC's popular history of Japanese land and water works, reading poems of the Man'yoshu (8th century) for what they show of water use, among them a poem on the shigarami in the Asuka River.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reads the shigarami of an 8th-century poem as stakes driven in with wood or bamboo laid across to dam the water, and likens it to the weir excavated at the Kodera site, tying the word to a built form. It is a column without footnotes; the build and the Kodera likeness are the column's own reading, not what the poem says, and it gives no size and says nothing about China.

Used for: a Man'yoshu shigarami read as a stake-and-crosspiece fence damming a river, likened to the Kodera weir (water)
