Museum Meiji-mura, 歩兵第六聯隊兵舎 ("barracks of the 6th Infantry Regiment", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.meijimura.com/sight/%E6%AD%A9%E5%85%B5%E7%AC%AC%E5%85%AD%E8%81%AF%E9%9A%8A%E5%85%B5%E8%88%8E/)

What it is: The open-air architecture museum's page on an army barracks built at Nagoya castle in 1873 and moved to the museum in 1965, a registered cultural property.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates a barracks whose squad rooms are furnished with wooden beds to the first years of the modern army, the earliest barracks with beds found. It is a museum page for visitors, about a modern Western-model army building. The wooden beds are a display that recreates the rooms "as they were at the time" without saying what time. The building was in use until 1963, so 1873 is the date of the building, not of its beds. The page does not say when beds first came into a Japanese barracks. The building was also cut to about 70% of its length and is shown with windows added after it was built, so its plan is not the 1873 form.

Used for: an 1873 army barracks with beds, marking the bunk room as modern (buildings)
