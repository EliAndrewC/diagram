satoyama-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 里山 ("village woodland", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/里山)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the satoyama, the managed woodland belt between village and mountain, its cutting cycle and its products; an encyclopedia article.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests that broadleaf coppice was cut back, leaving the roots, every 10 to 20 years for firewood and charcoal (a figure the article itself does not cite), and separately that leaf litter and undergrowth were carried off every year as fertilizer and fodder, which is the worked floor this page draws; a general tertiary article with no stand densities. For the fields its limits are two: that dry fields lie on the slopes with the satoyama rests on one region's folk classification (the Yoshino mountains of Nara, where the fields count as part of the satoyama), and that most satoyama had become red pine, grass or bare hill by the early modern period is one author's reading (Arioka 2004) of Edo-period gazetteer pictures, which the same article qualifies with sustainable use after the shogunate's forest-protection policy of 1666. It is about Japan, an analogy for China-modeled places.

Used for: the worked woodland floor; how bare the hills beyond a village's wood became - red pine, grass hill and bare hill, the 1894 estimate that 70% of the land counted as forest was bare, and the grass hill kept treeless (vegetation); where the satoyama lies against the dry plots, and that most of it had become red pine, grass or bare hill by the early modern period (fields)
