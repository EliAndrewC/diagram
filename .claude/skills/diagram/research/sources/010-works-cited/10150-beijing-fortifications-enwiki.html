en.wikipedia, Beijing city fortifications (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_city_fortifications)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the walls, gates and moats of Beijing from the Yuan through the Ming and Qing, giving the perimeter of each ring - the Inner city, the Outer city and the imperial and palace cities - and the history of their demolition in the twentieth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Ming inner city's wall perimeter, the size of the city the Beijing drum tower served. It is a tertiary summary, and Wikipedia itself marks it as needing citations and as partly translated from Chinese; its 24-kilometre perimeter carries no citation of its own. It gives the inner city as a perimeter, not as side lengths, and its one statement of shape (a roughly square city with "the east and west walls slightly longer") is wrong, since the plan is wider east-west than north-south, so only the perimeter is taken from this page and any east-west and north-south figures are read off a map. Beijing was the imperial capital and the largest walled city of its era, so its inner city is an upper bound on what one drum tower timed, not the size of a provincial town.

Used for: the extent of Beijing's inner city, as the area one drum tower timed (the bell-and-drum tower)
