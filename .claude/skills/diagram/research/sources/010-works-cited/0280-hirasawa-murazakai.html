hirasawa-murazakai

Kiyoto Hirasawa, Murazakai wa Fushigi da (Village boundaries are a curious thing), first serialized in the journal Ina 1984-85 and made a book in 1986; hosted by the Tenryugawa Upstream River Office, MLIT Chubu Regional Development Bureau (https://www.cbr.mlit.go.jp/tenjyo/jimusyo/publication/pbl_tell/pdf/08.pdf)

What it is: A local-history booklet, first serialized in a regional journal and hosted today by a government river office, on how village boundaries were drawn along the Tenryu - including a documented Edo-period lawsuit heard at the Edo high court in 1739, after the flood of the year before, between Shimada village and its neighbors over the islands in the river.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a documented premodern case of a river as the line between village territories, with the parties named by their side of the water, which is the strongest evidence read that a settlement's ground stops at a large watercourse. Its limits: it is a named author's local history for a general readership rather than peer-reviewed scholarship, and everything in it concerns a large braided RIVER - it says nothing about a brook a few feet wide, which is the water our maps draw; and it runs documented archive and local legend in one voice, the suit sitting among chapters of river-spirit tales, so a reader must take the dated record and leave the folklore.

Used for: a river as a village boundary, with villages named east and west of it
