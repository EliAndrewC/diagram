kotobank-bettoji

Horiike Shunpō, "別当寺" ("bettō-ji", the temple of a shrine's head monk), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%88%A5%E5%BD%93%E5%AF%BA-286265)

What it is: A signed encyclopedia entry on the bettō-ji, the Buddhist temple attached to a shrine whose head monk ran the shrine before 1868.

Why it applies, and its limits: Beyond the great shrine-temples it names (Iwashimizu, Kitano, Yasaka), it says there were also many bettō-ji among provincial shrines, their halls and images discarded at the separation of 1868. "Many" counts temples, not a share of shrines, and it gives no number; it also says the shrines that had a bettō-ji were shrines of rank (格式のある神社), so it cannot show that a village shrine had one. It is a modern encyclopedia's summary (2007) of a Japanese institution, and says nothing of China.

Used for: how common a shrine's own temple was (religion-and-death.html).
