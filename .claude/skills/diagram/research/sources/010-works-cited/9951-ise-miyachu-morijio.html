Ise Miyachu, "玄関へは左右一対で盛り塩を置く？" (is morijio set as a left-and-right pair at the entrance?) (in Japanese; https://www.ise-miyachu.co.jp/column/faq/%E7%9B%9B%E3%82%8A%E5%A1%A9%E3%81%AB%E3%81%A4%E3%81%84%E3%81%A6/%E7%8E%84%E9%96%A2%E3%81%B8%E3%81%AF%E5%B7%A6%E5%8F%B3%E4%B8%80%E5%AF%BE%E3%81%A7%E7%9B%9B%E3%82%8A%E5%A1%A9%E3%82%92%E7%BD%AE%E3%81%8F%EF%BC%9F/)

What it is: A customer question answered on the website of Ise Miyachu, a maker and seller of household altars and Shinto ritual goods in Ise, including the molds sold for shaping morijio.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only page read that says where the heaps stand at a doorway - usually outside it, one on each side. It is a seller's hedged answer ("we believe") about present-day household custom, posted in 2018 and updated in 2025, with no source. The page is tagged feng shui and points readers to the shop's colored dishes, whose meanings it ties to color and direction. So it shows the modern habit as it is sold today, with feng shui added on top, and nothing about the Edo period or about any residence of rank. The shop's own related answer says that a single heap at the door is also fine, so the pair is common practice and not a rule.

Used for: that morijio at an entrance is usually set outside it, left and right (religion-and-death).
