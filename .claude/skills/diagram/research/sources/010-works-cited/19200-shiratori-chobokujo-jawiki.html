ja.wikipedia 白鳥貯木場 ("Shiratori timber pond", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%99%BD%E9%B3%A5%E8%B2%AF%E6%9C%A8%E5%A0%B4)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the timber store of the Owari domain at Shiratori in Atsuta, Nagoya, from its beginnings for the building of Nagoya castle to its closing in the 20th century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives an Edo-period city timber store as a dug pond on a canal: a great pond said to have been dug about 1610, the Kiso rafts brought down the river, out to sea and up the Horikawa to it, and repeated enlargement from 1651 to 23,900 tsubo by the end of the period. It is an encyclopedia summary; the pond's origin is given as tradition, and its text puts Keicho 10 at 1610 where its own timeline says about Keicho 15 (1610), so the founding year is uncertain by a few years. Its Edo-period account carries no footnote of its own; its references (the Nagoya city history, a 1957 history of the Nagoya timber market) are listed but not cited line by line. And it is a store of the largest scale: the domain's official yard for the Kiso timber, which the article counts one of Japan's three great timber markets with Osaka and Edo, so its size (23,900 tsubo, about 7.9 ha) is a great castle city's, and it does not show how large a smaller city's or a town's timber store was.

Used for: the city timber store as a dug pond off the river, in the Edo period (Trades and services)
