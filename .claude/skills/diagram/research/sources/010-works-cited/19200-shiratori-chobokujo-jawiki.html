ja.wikipedia 白鳥貯木場 ("Shiratori timber pond", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%99%BD%E9%B3%A5%E8%B2%AF%E6%9C%A8%E5%A0%B4)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the timber store of the Owari domain at Shiratori in Atsuta, Nagoya, from its beginnings for the building of Nagoya castle to its closing in the 20th century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives an Edo-period city timber store as a dug pond on a canal: a great pond said to have been dug about 1610, the Kiso rafts brought down the river, out to sea and up the Horikawa to it, and repeated enlargement from 1651 to 23,900 tsubo by the end of the period. It is an encyclopedia summary; the pond's origin is given as tradition, and its text puts Keicho 10 at 1610 where its own timeline says about Keicho 15 (1610), so the founding year is uncertain by a few years.

Used for: the city timber store as a dug pond off the river, in the Edo period (urban-features)
