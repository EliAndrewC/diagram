The Japanese Society of Irrigation, Drainage and Rural Engineering (農業農村工学会), 「Improving farmland: land consolidation」 (translated; original: 「農地を改良する，耕地整理」) (in Japanese; https://www.jsidre.or.jp/tabata3-c/)

What it is: A page of the engineering society's popular history of Japan's farmland, on the land consolidation of the Meiji period and after: what it replaced, what it reorganized the plots into, and where in Japan it was and was not carried out.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says, in the words of the society whose members did the work, that the plots consolidation replaced were narrow and irregular and had come down from the early-modern period, and that western Japan was the exception: regular plots, the ancient jōri grid among them, were widespread there, so consolidation in the west was small and mostly drainage. It is a short, undated popular summary with no figure for the old plots' size. The one plot size it gives, 30 by 10 ken (one tan, about 54 by 18 m), is the Meiji standard that consolidation laid out, not an early-modern plot, and is not to be drawn as one. It describes the old plots from the consolidators' side, as the problem to be fixed, and it covers Japan only.

Used for: the narrow, irregular early-modern paddy plots that land consolidation replaced, and western Japan's older regular ones (towns)
