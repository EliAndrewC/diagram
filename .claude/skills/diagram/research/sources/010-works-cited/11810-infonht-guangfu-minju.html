infonht-guangfu-minju

Wang He (王河), "Wanqu shiye xia de Lingnan minju zhi er: Guangfu minju (shang)" (Lingnan vernacular houses seen from the Bay Area, part 2: the Cantonese house, first half), Nanyue Gu Yidao (the Guangdong Historic Trails site), 2021 (in Chinese; http://www.infonht.cn/ViewMessage.aspx?ColumnId=21&MessageId=10779)

What it is: An illustrated essay by an architect of Guangzhou University on the traditional Cantonese village and house: the comb-shaped village plan of parallel lanes behind a main cross-lane, the ancestral halls and the three-bay house.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the premodern Cantonese village as architectural historians reconstruct it from surviving villages, including where its drying ground lay. It is a popular essay by a specialist on the Guangdong ancient post-roads site, a secondary summary of the standard studies it cites (Lu Yuanding and Wei Yanjun's Guangdong minju of 1986, Lu Qi's of 2008, and for the village plan Feng Jiang's 2010 dissertation on Ming and Qing Guangzhou prefecture), not a survey of its own. It describes villages as they survive, mostly Qing and later, and dates the comb plan only loosely, as consolidated after the mid-Ming; its sizes are for houses, not for the village or its drying ground. It says the front lane was only sometimes widened into a drying ground, and the comb plan is the Pearl River Delta's lineage-village form, not one general to China, so for our villages it stands in as one regional pattern.

Used for: the comb-plan village's main lane widened into a drying ground at its front (homesteads)
