shirobito-612-omote-oku

Shirobito, 御殿には"表"と"奥"があるって本当？ (Is it true a palace has an "omote" and an "oku"?), Kamiyu Rekishi Henshubu, 2018 (in Japanese; https://shirobito.jp/article/612)

What it is: An introductory article on the castle palace, on the Shirobito castle site, by a history editorial team, dated 2018-12-05: the omote where the lord governed and received, the lord's own working room and bedroom behind it, and the oku where the lord's family lived.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets out, in order from front to back, the division our residences follow at a smaller scale, and it is candid that "naka-oku" is not strictly a historical term. Its limits are that it is popular writing without references, and that it describes the palaces of castle lords, far above a provincial posting.

Used for: the omote in front, the lord's working room and bedroom behind it, the family's oku beyond; naka-oku as a modern term (buildings)
