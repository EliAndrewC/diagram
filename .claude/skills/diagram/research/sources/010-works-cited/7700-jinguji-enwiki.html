Wikipedia, Jingū-ji (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jing%C5%AB-ji) - 「The main priest was tellingly called shasō (社僧) or "shrine Buddhist monk", and was both a shrine priest and a Buddhist monk.」 (the source's own English; the characters are the page's own gloss)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the jingū-ji, the shrine-temple of pre-Meiji Japan, and its destruction after 1868.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the shasō - the "shrine Buddhist monk" who was at once shrine priest and Buddhist monk - and for the scale of the 1868-1874 demolitions, which is what makes the pre-1868 fused form invisible today. A tertiary summary whose figure of 30,000 is its own estimate; it treats the great shrine-temple complexes and says nothing of how common a jingūji or a resident shasō was at an ordinary village shrine - the step this record marks as uncounted.

Used for: the shasō; 30,000 structures demolished (religion-and-death.html).
