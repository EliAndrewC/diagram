ja.wikipedia 地蔵菩薩 ("Jizō bosatsu", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9C%B0%E8%94%B5%E8%8F%A9%E8%96%A9)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the bodhisattva Jizō, including a section on the stone jizo of the roadside and the village.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the places a stone jizo stands - village boundaries, passes, crossroads, the foot of a bridge, graveyards - and says that in the Kinki region a jizo is kept for each town block, which is the town's count. Its limits: it is a tertiary article; the block-by-block jizo is a Kinki custom, described as it is kept today rather than dated; and it gives no figure for how many a village kept.

Used for: where a stone jizo stands, and a jizo for each town block in Kinki (religion-and-death)
