fao-y1187e

FAO, Integrated agriculture-aquaculture: a primer (FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 407) (https://www.fao.org/4/y1187e/y1187e34.htm)

What it is: A primer by Ruben Sevilleja, Jose Torres, John Sollows and David Little for the UN Food and Agriculture Organization (Fisheries Technical Paper 407, c. 2001) on farming crops, livestock and fish together, written as a general introduction rather than as a study of one country. We cite the section on what animal waste actually does in a pond, and its table of where a pen may stand.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the mechanism in the cleanest form found anywhere - waste feeds the pond along three pathways - and, unusually, it gives the REASONS a pen is put where it is put, which turn out to be the labor of carrying waste and the comfort of the animals rather than anything about contamination. That is directly useful to a map that has to say why a shed stands where it does. Its limits: it is a modern general-purpose manual with no historical claim at all, it is written for present-day smallholders in many countries rather than for any one tradition, and its figures are recommendations for building a pond today, so none of them is evidence about a premodern place. And the use the record makes of its silence - that contamination is nowhere given as a reason for a pen's position - is an argument from what a MODERN manual does not say, which bounds the question rather than settling it.

Used for: wastes acting by stimulating phytoplankton, as substrate for bacterial production, and as feed for zooplankton; pen siting given as a trade-off of labor and animal comfort
