okeihan-kyomachiya

Keihan Holdings, "Kyoto tsu no susume, no. 45: the Kyoto machiya, the interior" (in Japanese; https://www.okeihan.net/navi/kyoto_tsu/tsu201201.php)

What it is: A short illustrated feature (December 2011) on the Keihan railway company's visitor site, written by Satō Rinako of Rakutabi, a Kyoto tour and lecture company, explaining the rooms of a Kyoto townhouse - the earth-floored passage, the shop room, the kitchen, the back room and the small light garden.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the usual one-row, three-room plan of a Kyoto townhouse and what each room was for. It is popular writing by a tour guide for a railway company's visitor site, citing nothing; it describes surviving Kyoto houses (the two it sends readers to are one of early Shōwa and one over a century old), not provincial towns or a dated era, and says itself that the plan changed over time, and gives no sizes beyond the light garden of about one tsubo.

Used for: the one-row, three-room plan of a townhouse and its small light garden (buildings)
