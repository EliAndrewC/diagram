The Paper (澎湃新闻), 私家历史 column, 城市里的田园：都市为何离不开农村 ("Fields in the city: why cities could not do without the countryside", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (in Chinese; https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_1477443)

What it is: A popular-history essay by the pen-named writer 维舟 (Wei Zhou), published 16 July 2016, on the farmland and vegetable gardens inside and around Chinese cities. It quotes the prefatory 杂说 (Miscellaneous Remarks) of the farm manual Qimin yaoshu, the twelfth-century memoir of Kaifeng, and nineteenth- and twentieth-century observers of Nanjing, Beijing, Shanghai, Wuhan and Chengdu.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries, in their original words, two old passages on the vegetable ground round a Chinese city: the advice in the 杂说 at the head of Qimin yaoshu to grow melons and vegetables on land near a city wall, and the memoir's Kaifeng ringed with gardens. The essay quotes that advice as the manual's, but the 杂说 is generally held to be a later addition rather than Jia Sixie's sixth-century text, so it witnesses the practice from a later hand - already in the Song edition, by the Siku editors' account - not from the sixth century. The Kaifeng passage, with gardens and no idle land for a hundred li, describes an imperial capital of perhaps a million people, in a memoir's exaggeration: it bounds the gardens round a great city and does not give the ring round a county town. It is a newspaper essay, not a study; most of it concerns farmland inside the walls and in the twentieth century, and the two old passages are quoted from it rather than read in their own editions.

Used for: the advice in the Qimin yaoshu's later prefatory 杂说 to grow vegetables near a city wall and sell the surplus, and the gardens round Kaifeng at the end of the Northern Song (towns)
