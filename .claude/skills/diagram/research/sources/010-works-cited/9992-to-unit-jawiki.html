Japanese Wikipedia, 斗 (the to, a unit of volume) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%96%97)

What it is: A short Japanese encyclopedia article on the to, a traditional East Asian measure of volume. It gives the unit's size in Japan and in China and its place in the chain of units, ten sho to a to and ten to to a koku.

Why it applies, and its limits: The ratio between the units is the defining one in the Japanese system, the one in use when rice bales were counted in to, so it holds for the premodern setting. The article is a brief modern summary, and Wikipedia has flagged it since December 2021 as citing no sources, so it stands only for a ratio that is basic to the system and not for any number of its own. Its liter figures are modern: about 18.039 liters in Japan is the Meiji-era legal definition, and 10 liters in China is the metric one. It traces the Chinese to through the dynasties, from about 2 liters under the Zhou to about 10.7 under the Ming, but says nothing of the Japanese unit's size before Meiji. We take from it only the ratio.

Used for: the to as a tenth of a koku, in the size of a rice bale
