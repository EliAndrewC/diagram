ja.wikipedia 雁木 (広島市) ("stone landing-steps", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/雁木_(広島市))

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the gangi of Hiroshima, the stone stepped landings and revetments of the delta city's rivers; an encyclopedia article.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests the stepped landing as itself a revetment, in timber and then granite, built where boats berthed and cargo was landed, and the step against the floating pier for a moving water level; a delta port on a 4 m tide, not a river's annual stage. It is a tertiary summary leaning on a local preservation society's survey (雁木組 2007) and city and press pages, and its period claims are hedged: the change from timber to stone in the early Edo period, and the samurai residence with no landing, are what "is thought" (考えられている), not what a period record shows. Most of the landings it describes standing are later: the private and back-gate landings of the Kyōbashi River were built by single households from the Meiji period on, after the domain's boat licences (舟株) ended and river boats multiplied, and many landings were relaid in revetment works from the 1960s to 1985. So the merchant's back-gate landing is a modern survival read back into the Edo town, and the count of 300 to 400 landings is today's, not the castle town's.

Used for: the stepped quay as revetment; public and private stepped landings, the merchant's back-gate landing, and the samurai residence set back behind its wall with none (river cities)
