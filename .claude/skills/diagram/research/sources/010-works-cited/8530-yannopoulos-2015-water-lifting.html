yannopoulos-2015-water-lifting

Stavros Yannopoulos and others, "Evolution of Water Lifting Devices (Pumps) over the Centuries Worldwide", Water 7(9) (2015) 5031-5060 (https://res.mdpi.com/d_attachment/water/water-07-05031/article_deploy/water-07-05031.pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed review article in the open-access journal Water surveying the devices used to lift water from antiquity to the present across the world, with sections on the Chinese shadoof (jiegao) and the Chinese windlass (lulu) and on what became of each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is what makes a north-China field well a piece of machinery on our maps rather than a hole in the ground, and it dates the windlass's rise to the Ming and Qing with the three changes that made it: animal power in place of human, several containers at once, and deeper wells. Its limits are that it is a review compiled from other people's work rather than fieldwork or excavation; that it gives no well depth in meters, so any figure in meters is ours and is labeled a guess; that it does not say where the operator stood, so our record does not either; and that it is a modern engineering survey using archaeological finds as illustrations of a long practice.

Used for: the jiegao as the Chinese shadoof (upright post, hinged beam, counterweight) and the lulu as a windlass on a wooden stand with wheel, axle, crank and ropes; the Ming and Qing rise of the lulu and its three innovations
