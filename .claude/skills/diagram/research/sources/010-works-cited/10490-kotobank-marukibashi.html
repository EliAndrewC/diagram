kotobank-marukibashi

Dictionary entries s.v. 丸木橋 (maruki-bashi), aggregated at kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/丸木橋)

What it is: The entries for the log bridge in two standard Japanese dictionaries, the Daijisen and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, as kotobank reproduces them, with the dictionary's earliest attestations of the word in medieval poetry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It fixes what the Japanese word for a log bridge meant: a log laid across and nothing more (the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten says one log, and gives a second sense, any bridge built of logs). That this was the simplest crossing is our reading of "nothing more"; the dictionaries compare it with no other crossing, and a ford or stepping stones are simpler still. It is a definition only, from modern dictionaries, and Japanese only - it gives no size, no place, no date beyond the word's first appearance in writing (1190), and nothing on who laid one; the only water it names is a narrow valley stream (細谷川) in the earliest quoted poem.

Used for: that a log laid across the water, and nothing more, was a crossing of its own (water)
