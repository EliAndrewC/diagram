FAO, Irrigation Manual (planning, development, monitoring and evaluation of irrigated agriculture with farmer participation), Module 7, chapter 6 "Hydraulic structures" (https://www.fao.org/4/ai596e/ai596e03.pdf)

What it is: A chapter of the FAO's irrigation manual for small schemes in southern and eastern Africa, on hydraulic structures: what a head work is and its parts (a side offtake, a regulating structure across the river or part of it, a sediment flushing arrangement), when a river can be tapped without a weir and when a temporary or permanent weir is needed, where to place the offtake and at what angle, and how an intake takes the cleaner upper flow and returns the sediment-laden lower flow to the river.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the physics of the intake in plain English at the scale of a small gravity scheme built with local labor - gabion and masonry weirs - which is the scale of a hamlet's field, and it is the clearest statement read that the river continues below the works. Its limits are that it is modern engineering guidance for a different continent, so we take from it the mechanics that do not depend on period or place - the weir raising the level, the surplus passing over, the offtake on the outside of a bend - and never a number for what a premodern village built.

Used for: the parts of a head work; run-of-river offtake without a weir when the level suffices, a temporary or permanent weir when it does not; the intake taking the upper part of the flow and disposing of the lower part down the river; the offtake in a straight reach or on the outside of a bend, at an angle when silt-free and at a right angle when silt-laden
