token-edo-shokunin

東京都建設業協会 (Tokyo Construction Industry Association), 江戸の職人発展史 ("A history of Edo's craftsmen", title translated from the Japanese by this project), in its magazine (in Japanese; https://www.token.or.jp/magazine/g200310/g200310_5.htm)

What it is: An installment of a serial history of the Edo building trades in the magazine of Tokyo's construction-industry association, on the spread of carpentry manuals and on how Edo's craftsmen were ranked and sorted, including the split between those who worked out on a site and those who worked at home.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states plainly that the carpenter and the plasterer worked away from home while the cabinetmaker worked in his own workshop, from which we take it that a carpenter needs no workshop of his own on a town map. That conclusion is ours, not the page's: the same page puts the independent master carpenter (棟梁) in a street-front house (表店), so the trade still has a frontage on the street even though its work is done on site. It is an unsigned popular history of Edo by a trade body, published in its magazine in 2003, not a study, and it cites nothing; it is written about Edo, but the split between work out on site (出職) and work at home (居職) was a general classification of the period's crafts, not only Edo's.

Used for: the carpenter as a trade that works out on site, the cabinetmaker as one that works at home (urban-features)
