hotes-wetland-diversity

Hotes, wetland ecosystem diversity, Global Environmental Research 12(1) (https://www.airies.or.jp/attach.php/6a6f75726e616c5f31322d316a706e/save/0/0/12_1-04.pdf)

What it is: A 2007 review article by the wetland ecologist Stefan Hotes, then in the conservation ecology laboratory of the University of Tokyo's Graduate School of Agricultural and Life Sciences, published in Japanese in 地球環境 (Global Environmental Research, volume 12) by the Association of International Research Initiatives for Environmental Studies. It surveys the ways wetlands are classified and what those classifications are worth for conserving and restoring them, and along the way sets out the Japanese wetland types and the plants that characterize each.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for what grows where along a wetland's edge - the large sedges, reed, cattail and alder stands of a nutrient-rich lowland mire - which is the plant zonation our marsh and pond margins are drawn from, and a scientific description of which plants take which ground holds as well for a premodern marsh as for a modern one. Its limits: it is a present-day scientific review, written about Japan's surviving wetlands and about the problem of classifying them rather than about any historical landscape or about how villagers cut and used those margins; it is in Japanese, and we quote it in our own English translation.

Used for: Japanese wetland zonation
