What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the time bell of Iwatsuki, a castle town north of Edo: when the domain placed it, who rang it and how often.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a domain's own time bell described as the castle's bell, placed at one spot in the town, rung twelve times a day in the late Edo period. In 1687 four ringers held the duty, whom Saitama City's own page only supposes were ashigaru and chūgen; by 1828 the duty had passed to the headman (nanushi) of Shibue-machi, a townsman. It is a Wikipedia article, a tertiary summary whose facts all come from Saitama City's cultural-property pages, the closer source. Neither says the town had no other bell: that this was the town's single time bell is our reading of their silence, not their claim. It is one small domain, and the tower that survives is a rebuilding of 1852-1866 after a fire in the Tenpō era.
