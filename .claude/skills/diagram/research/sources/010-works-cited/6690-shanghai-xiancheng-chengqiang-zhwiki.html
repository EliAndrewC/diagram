zh.wikipedia 上海县城墙 (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%8A%E6%B5%B7%E5%8E%BF%E5%9F%8E%E5%A2%99)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the wall of Shanghai's county town: its building in 1553 against the wokou, its gates and three water gates, and its moat - some 1,500 zhang long and six zhang wide - which communicated with the water gates and with the Huangpu's tides and was dredged by the magistrate in 1753 so that boats could pass.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for a Chinese county seat's moat as a worked waterway: tied to its river for navigation, fresh water and drainage, and dredged when a magistrate had it done. It is a tertiary summary of one Yangtze-delta town whose moat was tidal rather than fed from upstream, so it speaks to a moat kept full by its river and to dredging as an official's act, not to the upstream-intake and downstream-return arrangement these maps draw; the 1753 dredging is a single dated event with no interval or cycle behind it, so it attests that dredging happened rather than a maintenance rhythm; and the article gives its moat two different lengths (1,620 zhang in its lead, some 1,500 in its moat section), so its own measurements are not consistent.

Used for: the moat communicating with the water gates and the Huangpu's tides (river-cities fn-20); the 1753 dredging (river-cities fn-37)
