ainoshuku-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 間の宿 ("ai-no-shuku, the in-between station", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%96%93%E3%81%AE%E5%AE%BF)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the ai-no-shuku, the unofficial station that grew up between two post towns, with a list of them by highway.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains why an in-between station was forbidden to lodge travelers - the post towns bore the official relay and the shogunate protected their inns - and that its street was lined with shops. It is a secondary summary, a stub encyclopedia article that has carried a banner since April 2013 saying it lacks sources: the ban on lodging itself is footnoted only to two Japanese dictionaries (Daijirin and Daijisen), and its reasons - the relay burden, the shogunate's protection of the post towns' inns - are unsourced, so they carry the general picture and not a firm rule. The article itself says the absence of inns, porters and a relay office was nominal, in name only (名目上・表向き), so the ban is the official rule, not always what a traveler found; and it notes exceptions, such as a former station like Kikugawa, where lodging was allowed when the Ōi River was closed.

Used for: the in-between station and its ban on lodging (ways)
