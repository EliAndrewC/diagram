nihonminkaen-kanagawa

Kawasaki Municipal Nihon Minka-en (open-air museum of Japanese folk houses), "Kanagawa village" (in Japanese; https://www.nihonminkaen.jp/kanagawa.html)

What it is: The museum's page on the buildings of its Kanagawa section - four farmhouses (three of them headmen's), a woodshed of about 1924, a shrine hall of 1863 and a boatman's hut of 1929 - giving for each its former site, its type, its roof form and material, its length and depth in meters, and its date.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured dimensions and roof forms of four thatched farmhouses of Sagami and Musashi built in the late seventeenth or early eighteenth century - two hipped, two hip-and-gable, 13.6 to 16.4 m long and 7.3 to 9.1 m deep - beside the Kanto page. Its only gabled roofs are a woodshed of about 1924 and a boatman's hut of 1929, twentieth-century buildings that do not show an Edo farmhouse's roof. The same survivor bias holds: most of these houses were headmen's, so they show the upper stratum of a village; the houses were moved to the museum and restored, so each dimension is of the house as restored; there are only four of them; and it is one region.

Used for: the measured length, depth and roof form of Kanagawa farmhouses (homesteads)
