en.wikipedia Pan Gate (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Gate)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Pan Gate (盘门), the surviving gate at the southwest corner of Suzhou's old city wall, which describes it as two separate gates in one structure, a land gate for road traffic and a water gate for boats, connecting the city's inner canals and its moat to the Grand Canal.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a real case of a city gate built as a pair - one opening for the road and one for the canal, the water gate joining the inner canals and moat to the Grand Canal - which is what a canal town's gates on our maps are drawn from. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary whose whole article rests on two references, a city page about a different gate and a travel guide; that it is one gate of one exceptionally canal-dense southern city; that its dating is loose, a Yuan rebuild on an ancient foundation; and that it shows a water gate existed and says nothing about how one was closed.

Used for: "two separate gates, one opening to a road ... and another opening to a canal", Suzhou's Land and Water Gate; the sluiced arch is unsupported by any readable page (chinahighlights)
