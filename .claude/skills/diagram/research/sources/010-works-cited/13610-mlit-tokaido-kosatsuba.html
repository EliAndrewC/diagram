mlit-tokaido-kosatsuba

Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, Kanto Regional Development Bureau, Yokohama National Highway Office, "The Tokaido and the facilities of the post towns: the kosatsuba 1" (title translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「高札場1」) (in Japanese; https://www.ktr.mlit.go.jp/yokohama/tokaido/02_tokaido/03_sisetu/0501.htm)

What it is: A page in the national highway office's public guide to the Tokaido in Kanagawa prefecture, tabulating the height, frontage and depth of each post town's notice board as the shogunate's own post-town survey (the Tokaido shukuson taigaicho, compiled in the 1840s) recorded them, with the size of the reconstructed board at Kanagawa.

Why it applies, and its limits: The figures are measured boards of the Edo period, taken from an official survey, and they are the only dimensions of real boards the record has. They are the boards of only the nine post towns on the Kanagawa stretch of the busiest highway in the country, and so likely among the largest a town would have (a reading of this project; the page says only that frontage varied from town to town, perhaps with the number of boards posted); a farming village's board may have been smaller, and the page, which says every one of Japan's 60,000-odd villages had a notice board, usually at its center or a busy crossroads, gives no village board's size. The size it gives for Kanagawa's board (about 5 m frontage, 3.5 m high, 1.5 m deep) is that of a modern reconstruction, not a survey figure; its metric figures are its own conversions of the survey's jo, ken and shaku; and that the boards faced the highway is the page's own hedged reading ("it seems") of the locations the survey records.

Used for: the height, frontage and depth of a town's notice board, and that it faced the highway (urban-features)
