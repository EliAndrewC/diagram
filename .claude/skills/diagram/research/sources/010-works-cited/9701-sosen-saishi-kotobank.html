Kotobank, 祖先祭祀 (ancestor veneration) - Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (old edition), from its article 家筋 (house line) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%A5%96%E5%85%88%E7%A5%AD%E7%A5%80-1356674)

What it is: The Kotobank page for ancestor veneration, carrying excerpts of the Heibonsha world encyclopedia, among them one from its article on the house line that sorts Japanese tablet veneration into two types by whose tablets a household keeps.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says whose tablets a Japanese house kept - its successive heads and their spouses, along the line that inherited - which bears on whose tablets stand in a residence's altar. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary with no author named for this excerpt, and it speaks of households, not of offices. Its typology comes from modern folklore fieldwork in twentieth-century Japan, not from Edo-period records, so it does not date the practice to our setting's era. The line of heads and spouses is only one of two types, and the excerpt calls it the rule only "in principle." The excerpt breaks off before the second type, so an altar holding only the line's heads is one attested pattern, not the rule. A scholarly treatment of the same typology, Ueno Kazuo's 1985 paper in the Bulletin of the National Museum of Japanese History (vol. 6), has not been read.

Used for: the tablets of the successive household heads and their spouses, following the line of inheritance (buildings)
