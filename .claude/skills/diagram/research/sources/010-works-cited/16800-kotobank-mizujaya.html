kotobank-mizujaya

kotobank 水茶屋 ("tea stall", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%B4%E8%8C%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the dictionary entries, the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry by Nishimura Kiyoshi and the Nipponica entry by Harashima Yoichi on the mizujaya, the Edo tea stall that served tea to passers-by at the roadside and in temple and shrine precincts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where the tea stalls stood - the roadside, the temple and shrine precincts and gates, the crowded amusement quarters - and that most were stalls whose keepers lived elsewhere, some open only in blossom season, which is why a tea stall is placed by where the crowd is and may be drawn as a light stall. Signed reference entries centered on Edo.

Used for: tea stalls at the roadside, at temple gates and in precincts, most of them stalls with the keeper living elsewhere (urban-features)
