kotobank-mizujaya

kotobank 水茶屋 ("tea stall", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%B4%E8%8C%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the dictionary entries, the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entry by Nishimura Kiyoshi and the Nipponica entry by Harashima Yoichi on the mizujaya, the Edo-period tea stall that served tea to passers-by at the roadside and in temple and shrine precincts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where the tea stalls stood - the roadside, the temple and shrine precincts and gates, the crowded amusement quarters - and that most were stalls whose keepers lived elsewhere, some open only in blossom season, which is why a tea stall is placed by where the crowd is and may be drawn as a light stall. Signed reference entries, tertiary summaries rather than period records. Their examples are all from Japan's great cities in the mid-to-late Edo period: Edo's Ryogoku, Asakusa and Ueno from about 1740-1770, and Kyoto's Gion and Shijo riverbed. They show where tea stalls stood in a metropolis, not how many a town or village had. They are Japanese only and say nothing about China's roadside tea stalls.

Used for: tea stalls at the roadside, at temple gates and in precincts, most of them stalls with the keeper living elsewhere (urban-features)
