kotobank-zaimokuya

kotobank 材木屋 ("timber dealer", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9D%90%E6%9C%A8%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the dictionary entries and the Nipponica encyclopedia's entry by Endo Motoo on the timber dealer, from the timber merchants of twelfth-century Kyoto to the wholesalers, brokers and retailers of the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the timber trade arose where consumers and good water transport met, that the timber merchants' quarters were called kiba, and that the retail timber dealer kept sawyers and raftsmen and was found scattered everywhere - which is why a big timber yard stands on water. That a small retail yard need not is our inference: the entry has even the retail dealer keeping raftsmen to move his timber. A signed encyclopedia summary on Japan (tertiary), whose one listed reference is a history of Edo's Kiba, so its timber quarter and its retail dealer with sawyers and raftsmen are the great Edo-period city's, not a small town's; it gives no yard size and says nothing of China.

Used for: timber quarters on water, retail timber dealers scattered everywhere (urban-features)
