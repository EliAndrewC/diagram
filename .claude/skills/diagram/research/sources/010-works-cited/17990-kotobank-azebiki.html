kotobank-azebiki

Kotobank, 畔引 (azebiki, "the bund deduction"), the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%94%E5%BC%95)

What it is: The Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's entry for the land-survey practice of taking the bund out of a field's surveyed area, reckoning the bund at one shaku wide and a further one-shaku strip along the bund on each side (the azegiwa), with its earliest example from a shogunate order of 1726 printed in the Tokugawa Kinreiko, the Meiji-era collection of Edo shogunate law.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that an Edo land survey reckoned a paddy bund at one shaku (about 30 cm), a premodern figure for the bund's width. It is a survey convention for tax, not a measurement of real bunds, and the survey took out more than the bund: a one-shaku strip beside it on each side as well, so one shaku is the bund's reckoned width, not the width of the whole deduction (about three shaku across a bund line). The quoted order of 1726 reckons each holding's deduction at one shaku five sun, which reads as its one-shaku edge strip plus half the shared one-shaku bund, consistent with the definition. The entry is a dictionary definition; the figure rests on the order it quotes. It is about Japan only.

Used for: the Edo land survey's one-shaku reckoning of a bund (water); the same reckoning as a premodern width for the bund between two paddies (fields)
