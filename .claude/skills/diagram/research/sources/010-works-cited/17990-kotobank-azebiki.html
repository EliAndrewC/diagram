kotobank-azebiki

Kotobank, 畔引 (azebiki, "the bund deduction"), the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%94%E5%BC%95)

What it is: The Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's entry for the land-survey practice of taking the bund out of a field's surveyed area, reckoning the bund at one shaku wide, with its earliest example from a shogunate order of 1726 printed in the Tokugawa Kinreiko, the Meiji-era collection of Edo shogunate law.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that an Edo land survey reckoned a paddy bund at one shaku (about 30 cm), a premodern figure for the bund's width. It is a survey convention for tax, not a measurement of real bunds, and the quoted order of 1726 reckons the deduction at one shaku five sun with the adjoining land, which differs from the definition's own figures; it is about Japan only.

Used for: the Edo land survey's one-shaku reckoning of a bund (water)
