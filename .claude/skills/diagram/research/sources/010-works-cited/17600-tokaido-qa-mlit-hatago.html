Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, Kanto Regional Development Bureau, Yokohama office, "東海道への誘い: Q15 旅籠屋と木賃宿はどう違うのでしょうか？" ("An invitation to the Tokaido: Q15, how did the hatago and the kichin-yado differ?", title translated) (https://www.ktr.mlit.go.jp/yokohama/tokaido/02_tokaido/04_qa/index2/a0215.htm)

What it is: One answer in a question-and-answer series on the old Tokaido highway published by a regional office of Japan's Ministry of Land, explaining the difference between the inn with meals and the cheap firewood-fee inn, how many times the one cost the other in the Tenpo era (1830-1844) - a ratio, not a price for either - and where the cheap inns stood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the cheap inn at the edge of the post town and prices it against the inn with meals, which is what decides where a map puts its cheapest lodging. It is a short popular answer by a road agency, with no sources given, and it speaks of the Tokaido's post-stations, not of a provincial seat. Its five-times price is a late-Edo figure, from after the inn with meals had spread; the page itself says the firewood-fee inn was the usual lodging before the Edo period, so the ratio does not carry to earlier eras. And it puts the cheap inns at the edge of the post town "and such places" (はずれなど), which is where they usually stood, not a rule.

Used for: the cheap inn at the edge of the post town; a hatago costing more than five times a kichin-yado in the Tenpo era (cities/fabric)
