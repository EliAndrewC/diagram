ifc-307-bonfire

International Fire Code section 307.4, open burning - read in a state adoption (New York State Fire Code 2010, 307.4.1) at UpCodes (https://up.codes/s/open-burning-and-recreational-fires) - "A bonfire shall not be conducted within 50 feet (15 240 mm) of a structure or combustible material unless the fire is contained in a barbecue pit"

What it is: Section 307.4 of the International Fire Code, the model fire-safety code published by the International Code Council and adopted, with local amendments, across much of the United States. We read it in one such adoption - the 2010 New York State Fire Code, section 307.4.1 - reproduced on the code-hosting site UpCodes, the publisher's own site being closed to us; the clause forbids a bonfire within fifty feet of a structure or combustible material unless the fire is contained in a pit.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies one number, the working clearance a modern regulator requires around a large open fire, from which the cleared ground around a capital's pyre is derived. Its limits: it is a present-day American code, not a historical or East Asian source, and it governs a recreational bonfire rather than a funeral pyre; the fifty feet is a regulator's safety margin keyed to a code-defined fire, not a measurement of anything; and the record's own finding is that Edo's crematoria were moved for their smell rather than their sparks and that the real premodern practice was containment - a stone hearth inside a walled ground - so this distance stands in for a containment practice the record documents, as a reasoned choice on our part and not as evidence about any real settlement.

Used for: the 50 ft bonfire setback the pyre-ground clearance is derived from (cities/capitals.html).
