jokamachi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 城下町 ("castle town", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9F%8E%E4%B8%8B%E7%94%BA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on 城下町 (jokamachi, the castle town). It describes how such a town was zoned around its castle, with samurai quarters ranked by their distance from it, merchant and artisan quarters beyond them and a temple district on the outer edge, and how streets were deliberately bent into right-angle dog-legs and stopped with dead ends to lengthen an attacker's approach.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source of the zoning and the street tricks that give our towns and cities their shape, and it states in one place what scattered town histories show one town at a time. It is a summary describing the castle town as a general type, so it gives us the pattern rather than any single town's plan, and Japanese castle towns took shape in the Sengoku period (the late 1500s) and matured under the Edo shogunate, which is later than parts of the setting our maps also draw on. It describes a Japanese form only: the article itself notes that early castle towns, unlike most walled cities elsewhere, moated and walled only the lord's castle and not the town, so its zoning does not carry over to our cities modeled on Chinese walled cities. It is also thinly sourced: it carries a 2015 notice asking for inline citations, the sentence we quote on bent streets and dead ends cites nothing, and the ranking of samurai quarters by distance cites only a popular paperback (Ōoka Toshiaki's 2014 Bushi no enikki), so the defensive purpose of the street plan is the article's claim, not a cited finding.

Used for: samurai quarters ranked by distance from the castle, merchant and artisan quarters outside them, the temple district on the perimeter as part of the defense, the trunk road lined on both sides with houses and no gaps (Urban fabric), streets kinked and dead-ended to lengthen the approach, and merchants and artisans moved in by trade, the trade names of the wards, and the narrow deep lots along the highway (Trades and services) (「By such means as bending the roads into key shapes (right-angle dog-legs) and providing dead-end alleys, the distance one had to travel to reach the castle was lengthened.」 (translated; original: 「道を鍵形に曲げたり袋小路を設けるなどすることで、城への到達距離を延長した」))
