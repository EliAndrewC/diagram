kyoto-city-takasegawa

Kyoto City, 都市史22 高瀬川 ("Urban history 22: the Takase river", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www2.city.kyoto.lg.jp/somu/rekishi/fm/nenpyou/htmlsheet/toshi22.html)

What it is: A page of Kyoto city's own urban-history series on the Takase canal: its route and length, the takasebune (their average size and the number in service between Fushimi and Nijō around 1710), and the count of boat-entry basins along it over the Edo period, from the old maps.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the city's own popular heritage page (c. 2003-2009) describing a real Edo city canal, the best-grounded account of the canal read; but it is a summary, not the records: only its basin counts are said to come from the old maps, its boat figures are given without a source, and it refers the reader to Ishida Takayoshi's Takasegawa (2005) for the detail. It is one canal in the imperial capital and gives the boats' size rather than the channel's width; and its seven to nine basins lined only the stretch between Nijō and Shijō, not the canal's whole ten kilometers to Fushimi.

Used for: the canal's length, the boats that worked it, and how many boat-entry basins lined its city stretch (cities/river-cities)
