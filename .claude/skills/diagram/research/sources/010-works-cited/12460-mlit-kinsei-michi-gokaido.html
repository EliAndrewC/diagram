mlit-kinsei-michi-gokaido

Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (Japan), "道の歴史: 近世の道 - 五街道の道路整備と維持管理" (the history of roads: early modern roads - the building and upkeep of the Five Highways) (in Japanese; https://www.mlit.go.jp/road/michi-re/3-2.htm)

What it is: A panel in the national road ministry's online history of Japanese roads, listing in bullet points how the Five Highways were laid out, surfaced and maintained, with quotations from an account of about 1630 and a summary of the shogunate's order of 1612.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says plainly that the sand-and-gravel dressing was never carried through everywhere (people walked the muddy roads in high geta when it rained, and an account of about 1630 it quotes has Edo's roads deep in mud after a little rain), and that the day-to-day upkeep fell on the villages near the road, those within about 20 km. It is a short government summary without references beyond the period texts it quotes, written of the main highways and of Edo's streets; its wording of the 1612 order differs from the ministry's regional Tokaido page. Its 9 m is the width it gives for Hidetada's directive of 1605 (not the 1612 order), not one measured on the road, and it cites nothing for it: the Japanese Wikipedia article on the Five Highways dates the order to 1604 from the Tōdaiki chronicle, and gives the late-Edo surveys of the post towns (the Shukuson taigaichō) as putting most of the Five Highways at about 3 to 4 ken (5.4 to 7.2 m) outside the mountains and at 5 ken (about 9 m) in many places near Edo. The same article gives the mountain passes at about 2 ken (3.6 m), narrower than the 4 to 7 m this page gives for the mountains without a source, and says it is unclear whether the 5 ken included the rows of roadside trees, and that an answer of 1789 held 2 ken (3.6 m) or more of roadway enough once tree strips of at least 9 shaku (2.7 m) were kept on each side - so the 9 m may be the whole road with its tree verges, not the surface walked on. It dates none of its upkeep rules: it places the push onto the villages' own work and road associations after the shogunate's finances worsened, without saying when, and gives the 20 km reach without a date or a period source.

Used for: the highways' standard width of about 9 m set in 1605 and its narrowing to about 4 to 7 m in the mountains, the highway dressing that was never carried through thoroughly, the 1612 order, and the villages' upkeep within about 20 km (Ways)
