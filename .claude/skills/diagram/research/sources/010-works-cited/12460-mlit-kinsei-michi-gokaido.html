mlit-kinsei-michi-gokaido

Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (Japan), "道の歴史: 近世の道 - 五街道の道路整備と維持管理" (the history of roads: early modern roads - the building and upkeep of the Five Highways) (in Japanese; https://www.mlit.go.jp/road/michi-re/3-2.htm)

What it is: A panel in the national road ministry's online history of Japanese roads, listing in bullet points how the Five Highways were laid out, surfaced and maintained, with quotations from an account of about 1630 and a summary of the shogunate's order of 1612.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says plainly that the sand-and-gravel dressing was never carried through everywhere (the account of about 1630 has people in high geta in the mud of Edo's roads when it rained), and that the day-to-day upkeep fell on the villages near the road, those within about 20 km. It is a short government summary without references beyond the period texts it quotes, written of the main highways and of Edo's streets; its wording of the 1612 order differs from the ministry's regional Tokaido page. It dates none of its upkeep rules: it places the push onto the villages' own work and road associations after the shogunate's finances worsened, later in the period than the orders of 1605 and 1612, and gives the 20 km reach without a date or a period source.

Used for: the highway dressing that could not be kept up, the 1612 order, and the villages' upkeep within about 20 km (ways)
