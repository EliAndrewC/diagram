nihonminkaen-kanto

Kawasaki Municipal Nihon Minka-en (open-air museum of Japanese folk houses), "Kanto village" (in Japanese; https://www.nihonminkaen.jp/kantou.html)

What it is: The museum's page on the three houses of its Kanto section (from Chiba, Yamanashi and Ibaraki prefectures), giving for each house moved there its former site, its type (a farmhouse, a headman's farmhouse, and a sardine-fishing net-owner's house), its roof form and material, its length and depth in meters, and its date.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured dimensions and roof forms of three real houses of eastern Japan, each main house built in the late seventeenth century (two with a separate earth-floored work wing added in the late eighteenth), the kind these maps draw. Only two of the three are farmhouses (the Ota and Hirose houses). The third, the Sakuta house, is a coastal sardine-fishing net-owner's house, and its size is not a farmhouse's. Its limit is who survives: a house is preserved because it was old, large and well built, so the houses here lean to headmen and well-off households and do not show an ordinary smallholder's house; it covers one region only, and three houses are a handful of examples, not a sample. The Hirose house comes from a windy mountain slope in Yamanashi (Kai), west of the Kanto plain proper.

Used for: the measured length, depth and roof form of Kanto farmhouses, and the two-roof (bunto) houses (homesteads)
