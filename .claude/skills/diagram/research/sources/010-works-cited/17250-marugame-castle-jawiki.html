ja.wikipedia 丸亀城 ("Marugame Castle", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%B8%E4%BA%80%E5%9F%8E)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Marugame Castle in Kagawa, a hill-and-plain castle of a small domain, known for its tiered stone walls and for the smallest surviving keep.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a small domain's castle at both ends - stone walls stacked in four tiers to 60 m in all, the third-bailey wall alone 22 m (found on repair to reach 31 m), and a keep only 15 m high. Its walls are famous because they are exceptional, and the 60 m is four walls climbing a 66 m hill, not one wall - the article itself gives Osaka's 33 m as the tallest single wall. Its keep is the smallest of the twelve that survive, not of all that were built: a three-storey turret of 1660, altered in 1877, which some scholars count as a turret rather than a keep, because no new keeps were built after 1615. So it is a floor only among survivors. It is a Japanese stone castle of 1597-1673, and it bears on a Japanese-style castle town, not on a Chinese walled city. And it is an encyclopedia article, a secondary summary: the 60 m rests on a Shogakukan castle guide, the 31 m on the castle office's newsletter, and the 15 m has no citation of its own.

Used for: a stone wall's height and the smallest keep (cities/capitals)
