Hong Kong Herbarium (AFCD), "An Overview of Fung Shui Woods in Hong Kong" (https://www.herbarium.gov.hk/en/special-topics/fung-shui-woods/an-overview-of-fung-shui-woods-in-hong-kong/index.html)

What it is: "An Overview of Fung Shui Woods in Hong Kong", a page published by the Hong Kong Herbarium of the government's Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department. Fung shui woods are the patches of trees traditionally kept standing behind a village for the good fortune they were held to bring, and the page summarizes a field survey begun in 2002: about 116 such woods, averaging a hectare, ranging from 600 square meters at the smallest to 6 hectares at the largest, with 80% of them lying below 100 m elevation.

Why it applies, and its limits: The village grove is a feature we draw on nearly every map, and this gives the one thing hardest to guess - how big it actually is, and how much the sizes vary. We use a Hong Kong survey because no comparable measured survey of Japanese homestead or village groves could be read on a public page; it is southern China rather than Japan, and subtropical, so the tree species differ, and that the siting custom is the same is our assumption rather than the page's finding. It is a modern survey of the woods that have survived to be counted, not of what once stood; and the page rounds its figures and cites no study of its own, so where it disagrees with the underlying 2006 paper we follow the paper.

Used for: the survey summary - 116 woods, average 1 ha, 600 m² to 6 ha, 80% below 100 m; the 2006 paper's GIS table is primary where the two differ
