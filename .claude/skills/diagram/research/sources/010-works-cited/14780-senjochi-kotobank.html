senjochi-kotobank

Kotobank, 扇状地 (senjōchi, an alluvial fan), the entries of several Japanese dictionaries, the one used by Juen Shingo in the Encyclopedia Nipponica (日本大百科全書) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/扇状地-88386)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering the dictionary entries for the alluvial fan; the entry used is the Encyclopedia Nipponica's, which describes where on a fan settlement and paddy came early and where development came late.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the land use of the fan's zones for Japan - the spring-fed toe and the fan head settled early with paddy near the toe, the dry fan middle long left as wild ground and brought into dry fields and orchards only from the end of the Edo period into Meiji - which tells a map what a fan's middle looked like before then. It is an encyclopedia summary without a named fan or a survey, and its crop list (cotton, mulberry, tea, wheat) describes the late development, not the older state.

Used for: the fan toe settled early with paddy, the dry fan middle left wild until late Edo and then dry-farmed (fields)
