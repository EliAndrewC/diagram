nawata-sangiin-2006

Nawata Yasumitsu, "Rekishi-teki ni mita Nihon no jinko to kazoku" ("Japan's population and family seen historically", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Rippo to Chosa no. 260, House of Councillors, October 2006 (in Japanese; https://www.sangiin.go.jp/japanese/annai/chousa/rippou_chousa/backnumber/2006pdf/20061006090.pdf)

What it is: A briefing paper by a research officer of Japan's House of Councillors, surveying the country's population and family from the early modern period to the present, with a section on the Edo village drawn from the historical demographers' register studies.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states in one place how the farm household shrank over the Edo period and why - the early large households of a powerful farmer with his dependents and kin breaking up into small stem-family farms - and gives one region's figures for it. It is a secondary summary for legislators, not the research itself: its Suwa figures come from Hayami Akira's book, it gives only round numbers ("about eight", "about four"), and its "stem family of about four" is a one-line summing-up; it speaks of Japan alone.

Used for: the farm household halving in size from the seventeenth century to the nineteenth, and the stem family's rise as dependents became small farmers of their own (settlements)
