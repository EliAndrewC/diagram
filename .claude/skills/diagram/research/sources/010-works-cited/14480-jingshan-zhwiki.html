zh.wikipedia 径山寺 ("Jingshan Temple", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%BE%84%E5%B1%B1%E5%AF%BA)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Jingshan Temple near Hangzhou, the Southern Song Chan monastery ranked first among the empire's great public monasteries, with its abbots, buildings and rebuildings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates Dahui Zonggao's abbacy from 1137, gives the monks under him as over 1,700, and has a Thousand-Monk Pavilion built in 1140 as the monks' hall to hold them, so it is a dated figure for one of the largest Song houses - but a round one handed down in the temple's own tradition, not a register count: it counts the monks who flocked to a famous master, the article gives the same 1,700 for his successor Qingliao, and its own buildings section says only that the monks rose from 300 to over a thousand, so a resident body of about a thousand is the safer reading. It is an encyclopedia article on the single most famous monastery of its day, a mountain house rather than a city temple, and an upper bound rather than a norm.

Used for: the monk population of a great Song public monastery (religion and death)
