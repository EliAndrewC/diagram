ja.wikipedia 同心 ("doshin", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/同心)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the doshin, the low-ranking constables of the Edo shogunate, the town magistracies' among them: their numbers, their duties and where they lived.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the constables' housing - a residence plot granted with the post, about 300 tsubo for a yoriki and 100 for a doshin, in a group residence (組屋敷) at Hatchobori - which is the answer to where an urban magistracy's staff lived; that the compound stood apart from the magistracy offices is our reading of where Hatchobori lay, not the article's statement. It is an encyclopedia stub that its own editors flag as insufficiently sourced, and the plot sizes carry no footnote of their own (the nearest cites a family-history website); other accounts give a yoriki 300 to 500 tsubo, so the yoriki figure is approximate, while about 100 tsubo for a doshin agrees across accounts. It describes Edo, the largest case; a smaller city's staff are reached by scaling down.

Used for: the constables' residence plots and where they stood (cities/government)
