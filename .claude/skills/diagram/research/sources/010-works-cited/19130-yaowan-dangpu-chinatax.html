Jiangsu Provincial Tax Service (Xuzhou), reprinting China Taxation News, "走进古镇当铺 闲话清代当税" ("Into an old town's pawnshop: on the Qing pawn tax", title translated), 2024-01-15 (in Chinese; https://jiangsu.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2024/1/15/art_8586_447948.html)

What it is: A short article on the Baofengcui pawnshop in Yaowan, an old canal town in Xinyi, Jiangsu, describing the compound's courts and their uses, and the Qing pawn tax, with the county gazetteer's record that the town had two pawnshops in the early Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a Qing pawnshop of a market town as a four-court compound whose front court held the silver vault and the pledge store, behind a frontage on the town's main street, and ties it to the gazetteer's early-Qing record. Its limits: it is a popular article by a tax office, not an architectural survey; it describes the compound as it was, while only 23 rooms of it now stand, most of it having become a grain store after 1949; it gives no building heights or dimensions beyond a total site of over 10,000 square meters; it does not say when the Cao family of Taigu founded the pawnshop or date its buildings, so the gazetteer's early-Qing tax record dates the business, not the four-court compound it describes, which may be of any date in the Qing; and it is a large pawnshop, not a typical one.

Used for: a Qing market-town pawnshop's courts and pledge store (urban-features)
