fao-drainage-systems

FAO, "Drainage systems", chapter 3 of Drainage of irrigated lands (Irrigation Water Management training manual) (https://www.fao.org/4/ai587e/ai587e01.pdf)

What it is: The chapter of an FAO training manual that lays out the kinds of drainage system a field can be given - bedding, parallel grid, herringbone - and how the drains in each relate to one another and to the watercourse they empty into.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the drainage geometry our cross-slope collector is drawn to, and states it as ordinary practice rather than as a special case: the field drains run in the direction of the greatest land slope, the collector is constructed at the LOWER end of the field and takes them at right angles, it is laid along the lesser slope - across the fall - and the main drain it feeds is often a canalized stream running through the lowest parts of the agricultural area. Its limits: it is a modern extension manual for machine-cut drainage rather than a premodern source, though the layout it states follows from where water runs downhill and holds for a hand-dug ditch exactly as for a cut one, and nothing in the passages we take depends on machinery; and it never states the angle at which the collector meets the stream, nor uses the phrase valley stream at all, so that junction angle remains this record's own inference and is labeled as one.

Used for: the field drains running down the fall into a collector at the low end of the field, meeting it at right angles; the collector laid along the lesser slope; the main drain as a canalized stream in the lowest ground
