tfd-hongou-fire-history

Tokyo Fire Department, 防火対策の歴史 (Hongo station; https://www.tfd.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/fs/hongou/page_00029.html)

What it is: A page on the history of fire precautions (防火対策の歴史) published by the Hongo station of the Tokyo Fire Department, a short public-education account of how the inhabitants and authorities of Edo guarded against fire, including the water kept ready in ordinary households.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives two things a town map draws - the fire-water kept at the doorway and on the roof of a commoner's house, and the permission for tile, plastered storehouses and plastered houses in the merchant quarter - from the city that invented the regime. Its limits are that it is a fire service's public-education history with no author and no bibliography; that the passage we quote gives the practice without dates or quantities, though the page carries dated ordinances elsewhere; and that it describes Edo, the densest wooden city in the country under a shogunal fire regime, so the habit travels further than the regulation does.

Used for: commoners keeping fire-water routinely at the doorway and on the roof (「The names varied - yosui-oke (water bucket), tensui-oke (rainwater butt), chinka-sui (fire-quenching water) - but commoners kept water ready as a matter of course and set it at the doorway and on the roof.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「用水桶、天水桶、鎮火水と名称はいろいろあるが、庶民は平素から水を用意して、玄関先や、屋根上に設置した」))
