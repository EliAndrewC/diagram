kotobank-yaba

Kotobank, 矢場 ("archery ground", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering entries from Shogakukan's Nipponica encyclopedia and dictionaries and Heibonsha's MyPedia and World Encyclopedia (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%9F%A2%E5%A0%B4-648895)

What it is: A dictionary aggregator's page for the word; the Nipponica encyclopedia's entry, signed by Inagaki Shisei (1912-1996, a consultant on period dramas and a novelist), gives the samurai archery ground's fixed length (33 bow-lengths, about 76 m) and breadth (one bow-length, about 2.3 m), its target mound, and where such grounds were set (inside a castle or residence, or in thinly settled outskirts). The page's other entries are mostly about the Edo-period paid archery parlour, which is a different thing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that sizes a samurai archery ground and says where it stood. It is a tertiary encyclopedia entry that cites no source and names no period. Its figures are the standard it says warrior houses set, not a survey of real ranges, and they are Japanese only: no Chinese archery ground is sized here. A breadth of one bow-length is at most a single shooting lane; the entry does not size the shooting hall or the ground around it. The entry is short and names no town. The page's second sense is the Edo paid archery parlour: from about 1789 it shot at 7.5 ken (about 13.5 m) in temple precincts and amusement quarters, and it was a front for unlicensed prostitution. That parlour is another institution, and none of its figures or places size the samurai ground.

Used for: the archery ground's size and its places (cities/government)
