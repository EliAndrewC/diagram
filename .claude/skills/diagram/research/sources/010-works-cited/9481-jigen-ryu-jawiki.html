ja.wikipedia 示現流 (Jigen-ryū) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A4%BA%E7%8F%BE%E6%B5%81)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Jigen-ryū, the school of swordsmanship of the Satsuma domain: its founder, its lines and its practice, chief among them striking a standing timber.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the one practice found that trains against a post, in the words a reader can check. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, flagged by Wikipedia itself since 2021 for thin sourcing, whose one footnote for this practice is a modern popular book (Tsumoto Yō's 勝者の極意); that it describes the practice as the school teaches it today and does not itself date the standing timber to the Edo period; that it describes one regional family of schools (Jigen-ryū and offshoots such as Yakumaru Jigen-ryū), barred from being taught outside the Satsuma domain, not the practice of samurai at large; and that it gives the post no size, count or setting.

Used for: striking a standing timber left and right with a wooden sword and a shout, repeated (buildings)
