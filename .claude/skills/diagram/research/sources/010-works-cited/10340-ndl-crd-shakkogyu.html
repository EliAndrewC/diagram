ndl-crd-shakkogyu

National Diet Library Collaborative Reference Database, "About shakkogyu (borrowed plow cattle)", answered by Kagawa Prefectural Library from Sanuki monoshiri jiten (1982) (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000003223&page=ref_view)

What it is: A librarian's answer to a reader's question on the shakkogyu, the draft cattle that Sanuki (Kagawa) farmers borrowed from the mountain villages of neighboring Awa (Tokushima) for the plowing seasons, drawn from a published encyclopedia of Kagawa lore and naming its further reading.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests a wet-rice region that met its shortage of draft cattle by hiring whole animals for the season, at times as many as several hundred head, rather than by a shed kept in common. The cattle were hired, not lent between neighbors: they came from the grass-rich mountain villages of another province, and the lender took one koku of rice in return (hence the name kometori-ushi, "rice-taking cattle"), so it attests a paid seasonal hire across a provincial border, not villages sharing beasts. It is a short reference answer about one region, the custom it describes lasted until the power tiller and is undated before that, and it does not say where a borrowed beast was stabled.

Used for: that a region short of draft cattle hired them, for rice, from the mountain villages of a neighboring province for the plowing season (homesteads)
