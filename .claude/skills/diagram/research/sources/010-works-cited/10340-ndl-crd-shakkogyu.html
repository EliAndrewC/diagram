ndl-crd-shakkogyu

National Diet Library Collaborative Reference Database, "About shakkogyu (borrowed plow cattle)", answered by Kagawa Prefectural Library from Sanuki monoshiri jiten (1982) (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000003223&page=ref_view)

What it is: A librarian's answer to a reader's question on the shakkogyu, the draft cattle that Sanuki (Kagawa) farmers borrowed from the mountain villages of neighboring Awa (Tokushima) for the plowing seasons, drawn from a published encyclopedia of Kagawa lore and naming its further reading.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests a wet-rice region that met its shortage of draft cattle by borrowing whole animals for the season, several hundred at a time, rather than by a shed kept in common. It is a short reference answer about one region, the custom it describes lasted until the power tiller and is undated before that, and it does not say where a borrowed beast was stabled.

Used for: that a village short of draft cattle borrowed them from other villages for the plowing season (homesteads)
