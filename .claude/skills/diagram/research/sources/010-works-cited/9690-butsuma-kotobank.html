butsuma-kotobank

Kotobank, 仏間 (butsuma) - Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten; Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), Hirai Kiyoshi (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BB%8F%E9%96%93)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the butsuma, the room of the household altar, carrying the Shogakukan unabridged Japanese dictionary's definition and the Nipponica entry by the architectural historian Hirai Kiyoshi on the butsuma in Zen temple quarters, in the imperial palace and in farmhouses, with where it stood in each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the several places a butsuma was set in a house, which is the choice our residence plans make. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, an encyclopedia entry that cites no sources on the page; that its placements in a house are drawn from farmhouses and the imperial palace, and its one warrior example, the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimasa's Tōgu-dō, is a separate chapel hall rather than a room of a residence, so nothing in it places a butsuma in a samurai house; that it gives no era for the farmhouse placements, which it describes in the present tense of surviving houses, so they may be Edo-period or later; and that the six-mat butsuma behind the oku-no-ma or zashiki is a practice of districts where the Shin sect was strong, not a general one.

Used for: the butsuma as the room of images and tablets; beside the zashiki's tokonoma, behind the oku-no-ma or zashiki, or projecting from the room; the palace's butsuma as a separate building beside the everyday hall (buildings)
