butsuma-kotobank

Kotobank, 仏間 (butsuma) - Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten; Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), Hirai Kiyoshi (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BB%8F%E9%96%93)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the butsuma, the room of the household altar, carrying the Shogakukan unabridged Japanese dictionary's definition and the Nipponica entry by the architectural historian Hirai Kiyoshi on the butsuma in Zen temple quarters, in the imperial palace and in farmhouses, with where it stood in each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the several places a butsuma was set in a house, which is the choice our residence plans make. Its limits are that its placements are drawn from farmhouses and the palace, not from warrior residences, and that it is a tertiary summary.

Used for: the butsuma as the room of images and tablets; beside the zashiki's tokonoma, behind the oku-no-ma or zashiki, or projecting from the room; the palace's butsuma as a separate building beside the everyday hall (buildings)
