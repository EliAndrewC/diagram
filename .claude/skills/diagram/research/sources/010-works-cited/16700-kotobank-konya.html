kotobank-konya

kotobank 紺屋 ("indigo dyer", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%B4%BA%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the kon'ya, the indigo dyer who became the dyer in general, among them the Nipponica and the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten entries by Endo Motoo and the Mypedia entry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that cloth was woven at home but dyed by the dyer, that dyers gathered in dyers' streets in the towns and settled a few to a village in the country, that they worked at home to order, and that their indigo vats were sunk in the ground four together and warmed by fire pots - which is why a town keeps a dyer and why the dyer's vats fit his house. Signed modern reference entries (tertiary summaries, not period records) on Japan alone, nothing on China, and on the early modern (Edo) dyer: the entries date the four sunk vats and their fire pots to the early modern period, and say dyers settled in villages only as cotton spread in the Edo period (before that, travelling dyers visited), while the medieval dyer grew his own indigo and did every step himself; the Nipponica adds that rural households making their own cloth sometimes did the whole dyeing themselves, their vats warmed only by the weather. They give no yard size.

Used for: dyers in towns and villages, working at home to order, their vats sunk four together (urban-features)
