コトバンク 御用部屋 (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BE%A1%E7%94%A8%E9%83%A8%E5%B1%8B-66403)

What it is: The Kotobank entry for 御用部屋, reprinted from published Japanese reference works. It defines the term as the room inside Edo Castle where the senior councillors and their deputies sat and conducted the business of government, and adds that the room where a domain's senior retainers did their work - both in the domain office at home and in the domain's Edo mansion - went by the same name.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for a single idea: that the seat of a government contained a dedicated room in which its executive council actually met, rather than the ruler simply holding court wherever they were. Its subject is the shogun's own castle, so for a provincial seat it is an analogy carried down a tier rather than direct evidence, and a dictionary definition gives no size, no position within the building and no plan - only that the room existed and what it was for.

Used for: the executive council room inside the seat of government
