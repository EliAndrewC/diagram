Tonami Kainyo Club (砺波かいにょ倶楽部), 「What is a kainyo」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「カイニョとは」) (https://tonami-kainyoclub.com/%E3%82%AB%E3%82%A4%E3%83%8B%E3%83%A7%E3%81%A8%E3%81%AF-2/)

What it is: The explainer page of a Tonami citizens' society that works to preserve the homestead groves (kainyo) of the Tonami plain's dispersed villages, covering the word, how the groves arose, and what they were good for, with a quotation from a local Edo-period farm manual, Miyanaga Shoun's Shika nogyo dan of 1789.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its quotation of the 1789 manual is a premodern account of the Tonami grove's uses - shelter, a guard against fire, firewood, trees thinned and cut for use, leaves for fuel and manure. The page is a preservation society's, written for the public and undated; it quotes the manual without a page or edition, and its account of how the groves began is the society's own view, so only the quoted manual passage is used here.

Used for: the uses of the Tonami homestead grove in the 1789 farm manual Shika nogyo dan (homesteads)
