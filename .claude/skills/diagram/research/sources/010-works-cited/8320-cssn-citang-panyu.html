cssn-citang-panyu

中国社会科学网, 漫谈番禺广府祠堂 (https://www.cssn.cn/ztzl/jzz/rwln/wh/whcy/202209/t20220923_5541419.shtml)

What it is: An article by 朱光文 (Zhu Guangwen), published in the magazine 人文岭南 (Renwen Lingnan) number 98 and dated 2019-11-27 where it is reposted on the website of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, about the Cantonese (Guangfu) ancestral halls of Panyu, in Guangdong: how such a hall is laid out on its axis, with a pond or pan-pool and an open plaza before its front gate, and what the plaza is used for.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is our evidence that a hall of this kind fronts a hard open plaza where the lineage gathers and holds its ceremonies, which is why our maps keep swept open ground before a religious building instead of packing houses to its door. Its limits are that it describes the Cantonese ancestral HALL of one southern county, not the earth-god shrine we pair it with and not northern practice; that it is a popular article on an institutional site rather than a survey; that it is written about halls that are still standing, and where it does sort them by period it is dating the BUILDINGS, while every sentence it gives about the plaza carries no period at all - so it says what such a forecourt is for and nothing about when the arrangement began or how old the one we draw would be; and that it says nothing about grain-drying, markets or opera on that ground.

Used for: the pool and the open plaza in front of a Cantonese ancestral hall, and the reasons given for them - fengshui, drainage and fire
