kotobank-nagayamon

Digital Daijisen, Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho / Nipponica (Shogakukan, the entry by Kudo Yoshiaki), s.v. 長屋門 ("row-house gate", title translated from the Japanese by this project), aggregated at kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%95%B7%E5%B1%8B%E9%96%80-108447)

What it is: The kotobank page for the nagayamon, the gate set in a long range of rooms, carrying two dictionary definitions, a signed Nipponica entry (Kudo Yoshiaki) on its origin, its rooms and its surviving examples, a short Britannica International entry, and excerpts on the nagaya and the gate from Heibonsha's older World Encyclopedia.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the range stood at the front of a samurai residence and housed retainers and menservants, that its roof ran on unbroken from the range, that a samurai gate had a guard room projecting at each side with its form fixed by the house's rank, and that one surviving domain gate once ran to a 120 m frontage. Reference entries without plans or measured sections: they give no range's depth or height, and the rank rules they mention are the daimyo's and the shogun's direct retainers' in general, not any one domain's. The 120 m frontage is a daimyo house's gate, the largest scale of samurai residence, and cannot size a lesser retainer's gate.

Used for: who lived in a samurai residence's gate range, where it stood, and its guard rooms (government)
