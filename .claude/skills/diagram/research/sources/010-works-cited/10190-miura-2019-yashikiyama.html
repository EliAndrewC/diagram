miura-2019-yashikiyama

Miura Osamu (三浦 修), 「Invention Process of a Technical Term Yashikirin "Farmstead Groves or Forest" and Its Diffusion Process in Geography」 (the journal's own English title; original: 「用語「屋敷林」の造語過程と地理学への普及」), Quarterly Journal of Geography (季刊地理学) 71:3 (2019), pp. 120-127, short report (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tga/71/3/71_120/_pdf)

What it is: A short report in the journal of the Tohoku Geographical Association tracing how the word yashikirin (homestead grove) was coined by the forester Honda Seiroku (in a 1904 lecture booklet, then his 1915 book on protection forests) and taken up by geographers from 1918, and quoting the older words for the same thing - among them three entries from a 1684 Mito-domain register of one village's woods (大里村山御改帳), quoted from a 1932 ministry compilation of forestry records, and the author's paraphrase of a 1910 forestry paper's description of the Musashino landscape.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes a seventeenth-century register that gives three named households' homestead woods with their dimensions and areas, the only period measurement of such a wood this record has read, and a 1910 description of how the woods and fields were arranged around a group of farmhouses. It is a study of a word, not a survey of groves: the three register entries are examples the author picked, from one Mito-domain village, and two of them do not multiply out as printed (9 by 9 ken is 81 bu, stated as 5 se 21 bu; 40 by 20 ken is 800 bu, stated as 2 tan 6 se), only the 30 by 10 ken, 1 tan entry agreeing. The register is quoted from a 1932 printed compilation, not the manuscript, and the register does not itself say its yashiki-yama stood inside the homestead: that is the author's inference from the areas. The 1910 landscape is the Musashino upland near Tokyo, paraphrased by the author from a forester's paper on the Yotsuya-log trade; its cedar woods were intensively managed for the Meiji capital's market in scaffold poles, polished logs and utility poles, so the order of woods and fields it gives is that of a commercial suburb of 1910, standing in for an older village's.

Used for: the size of a homestead's own wood in a 1684 register, and the order of woods and fields around a farmhouse group (vegetation)
