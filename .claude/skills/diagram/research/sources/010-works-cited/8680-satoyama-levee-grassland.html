satoyama-levee-grassland

"Earthworm species and density in semi-natural grasslands on rice paddy levees in Japanese satoyama", open access through PubMed Central (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7538448/)

What it is: Kishimoto-Yamada and Minamiya, in the Biodiversity Data Journal (2020): a peer-reviewed open-access study of the earthworms living in the semi-natural grasslands that grow on rice-paddy levees, sampled in five areas of Sado Island, Niigata, which describes along the way how those levee grasslands are kept.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its whole frame is the thing our bund entry needed a source for and had none: a paddy levee carries a GRASSLAND, one that farmers maintain by periodic mowing or herbicide, and in the paddies it studied by mowing two to five times a year with no herbicide at all - which is the measure of how hard the grass grows and therefore of how completely a bund greens over in the season our maps depict. Its limits: it is a modern ecological survey of present-day Japanese paddies, so the mowing regime is today's - though not all of it is post-consolidation ground, since two of its five areas had never been through farmland consolidation, which makes the sward it describes a closer analogue than a blanket disclaimer would allow; its subject is the soil fauna rather than the vegetation, so the grass is described only as the habitat; and it says nothing at all about how a green bund READS against a green field, which is the visual step our record takes in its own voice and labels as its own.

Used for: the paddy levee as a semi-natural grassland, mown two to five times a year to keep it down
