susquehanna-boom

Wikipedia, Susquehanna Boom; PA Conservation Heritage; Lumber Heritage Region (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susquehanna_Boom)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the Susquehanna Boom at Williamsport, Pennsylvania, the seven-mile log boom that ran from 1851 to 1909: some 350 to 400 stone-filled timber cribs, 22 feet high, stretched diagonally across the river, holding as much as 300 million board feet of logs at once.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the anatomy of the largest kind of boom - fixed cribs of stone-filled timber, the enclosure they make, the volume it held - from which the physics of anchoring and enclosure on our timber rivers is taken. Its limits are that it is industrial North America, not premodern East Asia, so the arrangement transfers and the scale does not; that it is a tertiary summary whose figures are only as good as its own references; and that a lifetime total of logs is not on the page at all and is not used.

Used for: seven miles of boom stretched diagonally across the river; ~350-400 stone-filled cribs 22 ft high; 300 million board feet held at once (the lifetime log total is not on the page)
