henriot-shanghai-graves

Christian Henriot, "When the Dead Go Marching In: Cemetery Relocation and Grave Migration in Modern Shanghai", in The Chinese Deathscape: Grave Reform in Modern China (Stanford University Press digital edition, 2019; https://chinesedeathscape.supdigital.org/read/when-the-dead-go-marching-in)

What it is: An essay by a historian of modern Shanghai (Aix-Marseille University) in a peer-reviewed digital volume on grave reform in China, tracing how the city's burial grounds were moved, leveled and regrouped from the mid-nineteenth century to the People's Republic, and opening with the countryside's graves before the city swallowed them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in so many words that villagers around Shanghai buried their dead in individual graves in the middle of open fields and quotes an 1842 eyewitness who found graves in every field, which is the in-field grave our hamlet maps draw. Its limits: it is about one region, the Yangzi delta around Shanghai, seen mostly from the nineteenth and twentieth centuries; it never names what the fields grew, so it does not show a grave standing in a flooded paddy as such; and it says itself that nobody can now know how many such graves there were.

Used for: that Chinese villagers buried their dead in individual graves out in the open fields, and that no count of them survives (fields)
