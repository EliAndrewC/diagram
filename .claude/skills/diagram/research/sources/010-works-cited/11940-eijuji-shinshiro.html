eijuji-shinshiro

Shinshiro City, 永住寺本堂・開山堂及び位牌堂・庫裡及び書院・禅堂・衆寮・経蔵 ("Eijuji's main hall ... dormitory and sutra store") (in Japanese; https://www.city.shinshiro.lg.jp/kanko/minzokugeino/eijuji-hondo.html)

What it is: The Shinshiro city office's page on the six registered buildings of Eijuji, a Sōtō temple in Aichi prefecture, with their dates, the main hall's dimensions and the layout of the halls about the forecourt.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives an early-modern temple's main hall size, its halls' layout and the rule that temples of its rank keep a full set of halls. Its limits: a leading Sōtō Zen temple of the rank that had to hold the ninety-day monks' retreat, rebuilt and supported by the local lords, and standing in Shinshiro, a small lord's seat, not the countryside - larger than an ordinary parish temple; its halls about the forecourt are the Sōtō pattern, not every sect's; its meditation hall and monks' hall were kept for that retreat, and the page never says who lived in which hall; the buildings date from 1697 to 1893, and their tile roofs replace the main hall's first shingles and the two monks' halls' first thatch; a heritage description of one temple, not a survey of temples.

Used for: a temple's main hall, its layout and who lived in its halls (religion-and-death.html).
