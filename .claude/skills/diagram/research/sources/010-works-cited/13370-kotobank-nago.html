kotobank-nago

Kotobank, 名子 ("nago, the dependent farmer", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, revised edition, entries by Oishi Naomasa and Abiko Rin, among others (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%90%8D%E5%AD%90)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the nago, the dependent farmer attached to a powerful household, carrying several published encyclopedias on the status from its medieval origins to its end in the twentieth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It distinguishes the ordinary tenant, who rented fields, from the nago, who rented the house plot and house as well - which is why some poor households had no house of their own. Its limits: tertiary entries spanning several centuries, in which the nago's standing changes. The house-renting distinction is Abiko's account of the stage from the mid-Edo period on, after the lords' policies to free the nago had weakened their bondage. It runs into the twentieth century, when nago survived mostly in backward and mountain districts (the former Morioka domain's system lasted until the postwar land reform). Before that stage, the unfree nago lived within the master's household and was not counted as a household of its own. The entries give no house size or siting.

Used for: the nago renting house and plot as well as fields, against the tenant who rented fields only (homesteads)
