kiba-koto

the tokyo files, "the kiba of Koto-ku"; Hiroshige print notes (Adachi / Brooklyn Museum) (https://thetokyofiles.com/2023/03/20/the-floating-world-of-floating-logs-the-kiba-of-koto-ku/)

What it is: A March 2023 post by Clark Parker on the tokyo files, a personal blog about the geography, design and history of Tokyo, tracing the kiba (木場) of Koto ward - the timber quarter where logs rafted down from upstream were held afloat in a maze of canals and storage ponds and worked by the sawmills around them - from the Edo period to its late twentieth-century conversion into parks, and read here together with museum notes on Hiroshige's prints of the same district.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the shape of a timber quarter - an off-river district of canals and storage ponds, logs kept afloat, rafts poled to the yards - which is what our timber towns' storage grounds are drawn from. Its limits are that it is a personal, well-researched but non-scholarly site with no citations a reader can follow; that much of its detail is twentieth-century, the Edo arrangement inferred from its survival and from the prints; and that Edo's timber quarter served the national market of the largest city in the country, so a provincial river town's timber ground is a scaled-down reading of it, not the same thing.

Used for: Edo's off-river timber district of canals and storage ponds; floating storage preserving timber; rafts poled in canals
