kotobank-ujiko

Sasaki Masaru, s.v. 氏子 ("parishioners of a tutelary deity", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho (Nipponica, Shogakukan), with other dictionary entries, on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%8F%E5%AD%90-34483)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of the dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the ujiko, the households belonging to a shrine's deity; the passage used is the Nipponica article by a folklorist.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says who kept a village shrine: representatives chosen from among its own households, sometimes in rotation for a fixed term, answering for the shrine's cleaning, the priest's care and the festival, and that the shrine's offices were once filled from those households rather than by a professional priest. It describes the institution as folklore records it, largely in modern practice, and names the cleaning without saying what ground it covered or how often.

Used for: who cleaned a village shrine (religion and death)
