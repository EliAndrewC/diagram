"『お盆』前に草刈りをするって知ってた！？" ("Did you know the grass is cut before Bon?", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Mama Yell column, Yakult (2020, updated 2026) (https://yakult-lady.jp/mama-yell/4247)

What it is: A lifestyle column, no author named, on the recruiting site Tokyo Yakult Sales Co. runs for its Yakult Lady staff, on Bon customs, among them cutting the grass round graves and houses before the festival.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says the grass round the graves was cut before Bon, and that in some places it was cut so the spirits could pass - the clearing of a grave's surroundings as a seasonal act. It is a present-day popular column describing Japanese regional custom as it survives today. It compiles four other web pages (a temple's page, a travel news site, a trivia site and a Buddhist-altar retailer's page) and does not report any research of its own. Its history is loose: it calls the 8th century the Heian period. So it attests that the practice exists and nothing of its extent or antiquity.

Used for: cutting the grass round graves before Bon (religion and death)
