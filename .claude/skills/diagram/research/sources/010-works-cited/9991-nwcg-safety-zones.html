nwcg-safety-zones

National Wildfire Coordinating Group, 6 Minutes for Safety: Safety Zones 1 (LCES) (https://www.nwcg.gov/6mfs/operational-engagement/safety-zones-1-lces)

What it is: A short training page from the United States body that sets common standards for wildland firefighting, on choosing a safety zone where a crew can shelter from a fire. It states the least separation a firefighter should keep from the flames when radiant heat is the only threat: four times the maximum flame height, on every side the fire can reach. The page was last reviewed in 2025.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is modern guidance for protecting a human body from radiant heat, not a rule about when nearby timber catches fire, and nothing like it was written in premodern Japan or China. The figure is a least distance for radiant heat alone, written for open wildland fires in the United States, and the page itself says wind and slope push it further. We take from it only that published figure, to show that the charcoal yard's own separation rule for timber is a different and smaller thing.

Used for: the four-flame-heights safety-zone rule, named beside the charcoal yard's separation
