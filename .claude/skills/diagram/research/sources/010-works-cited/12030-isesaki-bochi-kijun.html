isesaki-bochi-kijun

Isesaki City, "墓地等を経営するときの許可の基準や申請について" (The permit standards and application for running a graveyard) (in Japanese; https://www.city.isesaki.lg.jp/soshiki/kankyobu/kankyo/kikaku/boti/2993.html)

What it is: A present-day page of Isesaki city (Gunma prefecture) setting out its ordinance's standards for permitting a graveyard or crematorium, among them the distances it must keep from houses and public facilities and from rivers and lakes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a measured set-back of a graveyard from open water, which no historical source read gives, and its figure is of a kind many Japanese municipalities share. Its limits are that it is a modern hygiene and siting ordinance under the 1948 national burial law, and one city's: its 20-metre river distance is common, but other cities set the house distance differently (about 100 metres in some). It also binds only a graveyard permitted today. The older temple, hamlet and household graveyards across Japan came before it and often sit far nearer houses and water than it allows. So it describes neither where graveyards stand now nor what a premodern village did: it is a modern bound to compare the map's reasoning with, never a distance the map must keep.

Used for: the modern set-back of a graveyard from a river or lake, and from houses (religion-and-death)
