daikan-jawiki

ja.wikipedia, 代官 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/代官)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the daikan, the intendant who ran a territory's affairs in place of its lord, from the medieval estates through the Edo shogunate's intendants of its own lands, with their duties and how they governed from a distance.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the Edo intendants of the Kanto lived in Edo and went out to their villages only for land surveys, harvest inspections, tours of inspection and serious incidents, which is the historical form of an official seen rarely in the countryside. It is a tertiary summary, the sentence concerns the Kanto intendants in particular, and it gives no count of visits a year, so any frequency is our estimate.

Used for: how seldom an official came out to a village, as the reason the notice board stands in for the state (the notice board)
