daikan-jawiki

ja.wikipedia, 代官 ("daikan, a local magistrate", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/代官)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the daikan, the intendant who ran a territory's affairs in place of its lord, from the medieval estates through the Edo shogunate's intendants of its own lands, with their duties and how they governed from a distance.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the Edo intendants of the Kanto lived in Edo and went out to their villages only for land surveys, harvest inspections, tours of inspection and serious incidents, which is the historical form of an official seen rarely in the countryside. It is a tertiary summary, and the article carries Wikipedia's banner saying it cites no or too few sources, with no citation on this sentence. The sentence concerns the Kanto intendants of the shogunate's own lands in particular, not the daimyo domains. It says those intendants governed through contact with their tetsuke, the staff officers under them, so what was rare was a visit from the intendant himself, not all contact with the state. It gives no count of visits a year, so any frequency is our estimate.

Used for: how seldom an official came out to a village, as the reason the notice board stands in for the state (the notice board)
