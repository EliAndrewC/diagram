ja.wikipedia 稲架 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/稲架) - 「They are most often built in the paddies and dry fields, mainly after the harvest.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「主に収穫後の田畑に作られることが多い」); the regional names (稲掛け, 稲機, はさ/はざ/はせ/はぜ/はで)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the 稲架 (hasa), the wooden rack of poles and crossbars on which cut rice is hung head-down to dry after the harvest. It describes where the racks are put up, notes that some districts leave a rack standing in place rather than building one each year, and lists the many regional names for it (稲掛け, 稲機, はさ, はざ, はせ, はで and others).

Why it applies, and its limits: Drying racks are one of the few farm fixtures that change the look of a settlement's fields for a few weeks a year, and this is a plain description of where they usually stand - mainly in the paddies and dry fields after the harvest - which is what we needed in order to place them. The article says "mainly", not "only": it supports the field as the usual place and is silent about the farmyard, so keeping racks out of our yards is our reading, not its rule. It is a tertiary encyclopedia summary, and it describes Japanese practice as recorded in the modern era; hand-harvested rice was dried this way for centuries, but the article does not date any of its statements, so we take from it only the general placement and not any figure.

Used for: the rice-drying rack as a post-harvest fixture of the fields - the record's "or the yard" is not on the page.
