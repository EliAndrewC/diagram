ja.wikipedia 国分寺 ("kokubunji, the provincial temples", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/国分寺)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the kokubunji, the monastery and nunnery Emperor Shomu ordered set up in every province in the 740s, each monastery with a seven-story pagoda.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Japanese case of a pagoda required by rule in every province: the edicts of 740 and 741 ordered a seven-story pagoda in each province, and the page says the kokubunji stood in or near the provincial capital and were, with the provincial office, the largest buildings of the province. It is not the only such case: the page names Chinese precedents in the state temples of the Sui and Tang, and Emperor Wen of Sui had relic pagodas built in more than a hundred prefectures in 601-604, which this page does not describe. It is an encyclopedia article, used for the shape of the rule; its own citations (the Shoku Nihongi and the Ruiju Sandai Kyaku, through a Kokubunji city museum sheet) carry the edict. The order is of the eighth century and held while the state paid for the temples: the page says most kokubunji decayed once that support ended and some survived as temples of other sects, and it says nothing of whether they kept their pagodas.
