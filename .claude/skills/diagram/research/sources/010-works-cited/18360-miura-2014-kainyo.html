miura-2014-kainyo

Miura Osamu (三浦修), 「On the origin of kainyo, the Tonami plain's name for the homestead grove: the geographers' kaito and the historians' kakine」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「砺波平野における屋敷林の呼称「カイニョ」の語源について ── 地理学の垣内と歴史学の垣根 ──」) (Kikan Chirigaku (Quarterly Journal of Geography) 65(4), 2014, pp. 222-227), J-STAGE (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tga/65/4/65_222/_pdf)

What it is: A short paper by a geographer in the Tohoku Geographical Association's journal arguing that the Tonami word kainyo comes from 垣根, the word Edo-period documents of the Kaga (Kanazawa) and Toyama domains use for the homestead grove. To make the case it quotes, in the original, village and domain documents of 1658 to 1782 on the groves, taken from local historians' published work.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page found that quotes Edo documents on the Tonami homestead grove itself: the domain's permit rule for cutting the reserved trees and bamboo around homesteads, a grove kept thick as a fire- and windbreak, and large sugi sold from a grove to pay rice tax. Its subject is the word, not the grove, so it quotes only the passages that bear on the name, gives no count, height or layout of any grove, and takes its documents at second hand; it says itself that the reading of 垣根 in the Kanazawa domain's official documents is unknown, the kaine reading resting on private village papers.

Used for: the Edo documents on the Tonami homestead grove - the 1658 cutting permit, the 1750 fire- and windbreak, the 1782 sale of four large sugi (homesteads)
