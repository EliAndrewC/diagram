miura-2014-kainyo

Miura Osamu (三浦修), 「On the origin of kainyo, the Tonami plain's name for the homestead grove: the geographers' kaito and the historians' kakine」 (translated; original: 「砺波平野における屋敷林の呼称「カイニョ」の語源について ── 地理学の垣内と歴史学の垣根 ──」) (Kikan Chirigaku (Quarterly Journal of Geography) 65(4), 2014, pp. 222-227), J-STAGE (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tga/65/4/65_222/_pdf)

What it is: A short paper by a geographer in the Tohoku Geographical Association's journal arguing that the Tonami word kainyo comes from 垣根, the word Edo-period documents of the Kaga (Kanazawa) and Toyama domains use for the homestead grove. It appeared in the journal's Forum section, not as a research article. To make the case it quotes, in the original, village and domain documents of 1701 to 1782 on the groves (a 1701 settlement of a land dispute, the 1750 Tobe entry, and the 1782 seven-trees notice and sugi sale). It gives others, from 1615 to 1828, only as one-line summaries, among them the 1658 Tonami notice on cutting permits. All of them come from local historians' published work (Imamura 2006, Saeki 1988, Takase 1986, Yamaguchi 2003).

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page found that quotes Edo documents on the Tonami homestead grove itself: the domain's permit rule for cutting the reserved trees and bamboo around homesteads, a grove kept thick as a fire- and windbreak, and large sugi sold from a grove to pay rice tax. Its subject is the word, not the grove, so it quotes only the passages that bear on the name, gives no count, height or layout of any grove, and takes its documents at second hand; it says itself that the reading of 垣根 in the Kanazawa domain's official documents is unknown, the kaine reading resting on private village papers. The rules it reports belong to one domain: Kaga's seven-trees (shichiboku) reserve on valuable timber, not a practice shared across Japan. The grove-ringed scattered homestead those rules govern is a form of the Etchū plains, and the paper says it is absent even from the neighbouring Kanazawa plain. The 1658 permit appears only as a one-line summary of Imamura 2006, not in the document's own words.

Used for: the Edo documents on the Tonami homestead grove - the 1658 cutting permit, the 1750 fire- and windbreak, the 1782 sale of four large sugi (homesteads)
