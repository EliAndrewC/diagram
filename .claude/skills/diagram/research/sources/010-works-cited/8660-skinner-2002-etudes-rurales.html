G. William Skinner, "Marketing and social structure in rural China", Etudes rurales 161-162 (2002), open access on OpenEdition Journals (https://journals.openedition.org/etudesrurales/7952)

What it is: The open-access publication, in a French rural-studies journal, of the analysis G. William Skinner first set out in the Journal of Asian Studies in 1964: the standard marketing community, a market town with the ring of villages that trade at it, and the spacing that pattern produces across a countryside.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source of the figure our town entries rest on, in the author's own words on a page a reader can open: in the modal case a marketing area is just over 50 square kilometers, market towns are less than eight kilometers apart, the longest walk in to the town is about 4.5 km, and the typical community is some 1,500 households in eighteen or so villages. That is what makes a market town the center of a farmed hinterland a person walks out into, and at the corrected area the argument our maps make holds better rather than worse, since fifty square kilometers is a morning's walk across. Its limits: it describes late imperial and Republican-era China, so it is the closer of the two reference cultures and not the other; the figures are a MODAL case from a model in which the area varies with population density, so they are the shape of a settlement pattern rather than a measurement of any one county, and the table they come from should be read before any other cell of it is quoted; and the analysis is a mid-twentieth-century social-science model, influential and much argued with, rather than a primary record.

Used for: the standard marketing community - about eighteen villages over just over fifty square kilometers, market towns under eight kilometers apart, the longest walk in about 4.5 km. This supersedes the ~300-500 km2 the record carried under skinner-marketing
