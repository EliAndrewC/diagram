maruyanagi-daizu-hatake

Maruyanagi, 大豆を収穫するまで：北海道の大豆畑の成長記録 ("Until the soybeans are harvested: a growth record of a Hokkaido soybean field", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.maruyanagi.co.jp/mushimame/daizu-hatake/)

What it is: An undated column on the steamed-soybean promotional site of Maruyanagi, a Japanese bean-foods company, following one cooperating farmer's soybean field of about 3.5 hectares in the Sorachi-Ishikari district of Hokkaido through a year - from the snow of February through May sowing to October harvest - in photographs month by month.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records what a soybean field looks like month by month - how tall the plants stand and when the field turns from green to yellow-brown. It is a company's promotional page, not a study, and it describes one modern, machine-sown, lightly sprayed field of soybeans alone, about 3.5 hectares - far larger than a premodern household's plot - in Hokkaido, a cold northern region opened to Japanese farming only from the Meiji era, so it has no premodern counterpart in the setting's model and its months run late. Its one height - the top leaves near 80 cm in late August - is that of a modern variety and is carried only as a rough bound on how tall a field stands; the turn from green to yellow-brown in the weeks before harvest is what the maps take from it as it stands.

Used for: the height a soybean field reaches and its turn from green to yellow-brown before harvest (fields)
