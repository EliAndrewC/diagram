kochi-hantei-jstage

Uematsu Kiyoshi, Nakajima Setsuko and Tani Naoki, 高知藩屋敷の建築構成について: 大坂・京都・伏見屋敷を中心に ("On the architectural composition of the Kochi domain's residences, centering on the Osaka, Kyoto and Fushimi residences", title translated), 建築史学 (the journal of the Society of Architectural Historians of Japan) 35 (2000), pp. 30-54 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsahj/35/0/35_30/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A paper by three architectural historians reading five plans of the Kochi (Tosa) domain's residences at Osaka, Kyoto, Fushimi and Edo, kept among the Goto family documents at the Aki City history and folklore museum; all but the Fushimi plan (undated) are dated to the Kansei era (1789-1801), the Osaka and Edo Azabu plans by the authors' estimate. It goes through the residences' rowhouses dwelling by dwelling, with who lived in each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is scholarship on period plans, and for each class of a lord's staff, down to the gatekeepers and the permanent servants, it says how many rooms a dwelling had and whether it had an earth floor, a yard and a privy. Its limits: these are a great lord's residences in Osaka and Kyoto, not a country office; the plans do not tell walls from doors and sliding fittings, so the authors worked each dwelling out from its entrance, earth floor and privy - a separate dwelling with its own way in is their reading, and no door position can be read off the plans; the earth floor was not universal - at Kyoto the gatekeepers' one-room dwellings had none, and the paper's table of the Fushimi rowhouse gives none to one of its two gatekeepers' dwellings; most Kyoto dwellings had no yard or privy, and one of the permanent servants' two-room dwellings held a kitchen that the authors take for shared cooking; these are official quarters (役宅) for the domain's posted staff, not family households: at Osaka none of the dwellings had a kitchen, and the authors suppose the residence's provisioning office cooked and the staff ate together; the paper does not say whether families lived in them; the reader meets it as a scan of vertical type.

Used for: the rowhouse dwellings of a lord's lower staff and servants, each with its own earth floor, all but the gatekeepers' at Kyoto and one at Fushimi (buildings)
