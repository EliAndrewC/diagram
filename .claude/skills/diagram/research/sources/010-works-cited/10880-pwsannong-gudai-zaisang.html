pwsannong-gudai-zaisang

"Gudai zaisang jishu" (古代栽桑技术, ancient mulberry-growing technique), an entry in the history-of-agricultural-science section of the encyclopedia on Zhihui Sannong (智汇三农), a Chinese agricultural knowledge database published by China Agriculture Press (in Chinese; https://www.pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/560280.shtml)

What it is: A Chinese encyclopedia entry on how mulberry was bred, propagated, planted, manured and pruned in China before the modern period, drawing on agricultural books from the Qimin yaoshu and the Yuan-era northern manuals to the Ming and Qing books of the Yangtze delta, with notes on Guangdong's ground mulberry and dike-ponds.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that gives a pre-modern planting density for leaf mulberry, and it dates it: about 300 mid-trunk trees a mu in a district south of the Qiantang River in the late Qing, with planting said to have been sparser in Jiangsu and Zhejiang before the mid-Qing. It is a modern secondary summary posted in 2016 that does not cite the text behind its figure, the figure is for field plantations in the Yangtze delta rather than pond dikes, and it gives no crown width. The entry itself says spacing varied widely with climate, terrain and the form the tree was trained to, and that many farms simply set mulberries singly at field heads and house corners; its figure comes from a commercial silk district at the late-Qing turn toward denser planting, in an unstated local mu, so it describes a specialised plantation, not the mulberries of an ordinary hamlet.

Used for: how thickly leaf mulberry was planted before the modern period, and that mid-trunk and low-trunk forms spread in the Taihu district from the Ming (archetypes)
