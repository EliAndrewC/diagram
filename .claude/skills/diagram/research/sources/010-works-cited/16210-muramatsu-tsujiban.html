muramatsu-tsujiban

Watanabe Yoshiaki, 村松藩の辻番所 ("The Muramatsu domain's guard box", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on the author's site (in Japanese; http://www.ywatanabe.net/ronbun16.html)

What it is: An article written for the author's own website in 2019 by a member of a local history society of Muramatsu (Niigata) on the Edo guard box kept by the Muramatsu domain at a crossing in Kanda, with the institution's history, numbers, plan and staffing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a guard box's usual plan and roof and a count of the boxes in Edo. It is a local historian's self-published article, and it names no source for the usual plan or for the count, though it cites period records and reference works elsewhere. Its plan is hedged ("it seems"). Its hut areas are the author's reading of an 1883-84 survey map, drawn after the boxes had ceased (by its account about 1871), and the 13.5 tsubo it reads for Muramatsu's box is by its own account far larger than the roughly 5 tsubo of the others. Its count of 899 does not match its own sub-counts, which add to 910. The guard box was an institution of Edo's warrior quarters; the townsmen's quarters kept their own watch houses (jishinban), which it does not describe.

Used for: the guard box's plan and size (urban-features)
