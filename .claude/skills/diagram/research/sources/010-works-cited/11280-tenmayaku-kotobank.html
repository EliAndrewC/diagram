tenmayaku-kotobank

Kotobank, s.v. 伝馬役 (tenmayaku, the relay-horse duty; the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), among others; in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BC%9D%E9%A6%AC%E5%BD%B9-102802)

What it is: The Kotobank dictionary page gathering Japanese reference works' entries on the relay-horse duty that a post station and its residents owed: how many men and horses a station was made to keep, and from whom they were taken.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the standing numbers on each class of highway and says the horses and men were requisitioned from the station's residents. Its limits are that the 1638 date and the "many stations" qualification come from one of its entries only, and that the fixed numbers are what was ordered, which the same entries say fell short as traffic grew.

Used for: 36 horses a station from 1601; 100 men and 100 horses on the Tokaido, 50 and 50 on the Nakasendo, 25 and 25 elsewhere; horses and men taken from the station's residents (towns)
