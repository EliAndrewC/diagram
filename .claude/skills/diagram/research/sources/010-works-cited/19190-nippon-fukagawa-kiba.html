nippon-fukagawa-kiba

nippon.com, 深川木場：浮世写真家 喜千也の名所江戸百景 第103回 ("Fukagawa Kiba: the ukiyo photographer Kichiya's One Hundred Famous Views of Edo, no. 103", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 2021 (https://www.nippon.com/ja/guide-to-japan/gu004103/)

What it is: A magazine article on Hiroshige's print of the snow-covered lumber yards at Fukagawa (1856), set beside the 1858 map of Edo 『安政改正御江戸大絵図』 and a modern photograph of the site.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reads the 1858 map for the form of a lumber dealer's lot at Kiba: a large pond at the center as the timber store, dry ground only round its edge, and the pond joined to the canals. It is a popular article, not a study; the map reading is the writer's, and it gives as hearsay ("it is said") both that dry ground lay only round each lot's edge and that rafts were brought in and out through the ponds. It gives no size for a pond. And Kiba was the shogun's capital's one purpose-built timber district, laid out in 1701 when the city's demand for wood outgrew its older yards: a pond to each dealer is the form at that scale, not evidence that a lumber dealer in a provincial town kept a pond of their own.

Used for: the Edo lumber lot as a pond with a rim of dry ground, joined to the canals (urban-features)
