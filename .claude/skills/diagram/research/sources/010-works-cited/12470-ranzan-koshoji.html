ranzan-koshoji

Ranzan Town web museum (嵐山町web博物誌), 比企郡寺院明細帳, 曹洞宗 広正寺 七郷村大字広野 (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/09ter07.html)

What it is: The Ranzan town office's transcription of the Hiki district temple register entry for Kōshōji, a Sōtō temple of Hirono, Saitama, founded in 1606, listing its main hall, two dormitories, kuri, treasure house and four gates with their dimensions, and a precinct of 1,944 tsubo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the largest of the nineteen temples the town transcribes from the district register (those of present Ranzan town, not the whole of Hiki district) and the fullest list of what a precinct held. Its limits: one rural temple, the top of the band, founded by a Tokugawa bannerman as his father's memorial temple rather than by a village; a Meiji-era register, amended as late as the 1931 earthquake and filed in 1963, so its buildings and its 1,944 tsubo are the precinct as the Meiji state recorded it after the temple-land confiscations of the 1870s, not as the Edo period laid it out.

Used for: the largest temple precinct in the band, its main hall, dormitories and gates (religion-and-death.html).
