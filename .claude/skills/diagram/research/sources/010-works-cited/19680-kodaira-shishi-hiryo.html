kodaira-shishi-hiryo

Kodaira city history, "Crops and fertilizer in the Edo period" (in Japanese; Kodaira City Library digital archive, https://adeac.jp/kodaira-lib/texthtml/d100010/mp100010-100010/ht002580)

What it is: A section of a local history of Kodaira (western Tokyo), published by the city library, on the crops and manures of its Edo-period villages. It quotes and glosses the village detail records (村明細帳) of Ogawa village and its neighbors from 1713 to 1870.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes a village's own record of 1754 on what went into the dry fields' manure - tree leaves, barley straw and turf with night soil, bran and ash - so the composting it describes is attested before 1868. Its limits: it is one upland village on the Musashino terrace, a dry-field district, not a paddy one; it says nothing of where on the farm the compost was made or piled; and its point on stable manure is the historian's conjecture ("they will largely have met the need"), made from the village's horse counts.

Used for: the composting of leaves, straw and turf with night soil in a village record of 1754 (homesteads)
