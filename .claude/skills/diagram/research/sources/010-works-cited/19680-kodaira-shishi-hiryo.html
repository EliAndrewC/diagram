kodaira-shishi-hiryo

Kodaira city history, "Crops and fertilizer in the Edo period" (in Japanese; Kodaira City Library digital archive, https://adeac.jp/kodaira-lib/texthtml/d100010/mp100010-100010/ht002580)

What it is: A section of the folklore volume of Kodaira's city history (小平市史 地理・考古・民俗編; western Tokyo), read in the city library's digital archive, on the crops and manures of its Edo-period villages. It quotes and glosses the village detail records (村明細帳) of Ogawa village and its neighbors from 1713 to 1870.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes a village's own record of 1754 on what went into the dry fields' manure - tree leaves, barley straw and turf with night soil, bran and ash - so using those materials together is attested before 1868. Its limits: the record says only that they were used (相用); that they were heaped with the night soil, bran and ash and left to ferment is the historian's gloss (かけて発酵させて), and its line that fallen leaves were made into compost (落ち葉を堆肥にした) is undated, in a folklore volume that says the same manures were still used after the war; the night soil was bought and carted from Edo, a great city's trade that a village far from one would not have had; it is one upland village on the Musashino terrace, a dry-field district with little paddy (the record gives the paddy a separate manure, bought bran and night soil); it says nothing of where on the farm the compost was made or piled; and its point on stable manure is the historian's conjecture ("they will largely have met the need"), made from the village's horse counts.

Used for: the composting of leaves, straw and turf with night soil in a village record of 1754 (homesteads)
