zh.wikipedia 东水关 (南京) ("The East Water Gate, Nanjing", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/东水关_(南京))

What it is: A short Chinese Wikipedia article on the East Water Gate of the Ming wall of Nanjing, the sluice where the Qinhuai river enters the city, built in the Five Dynasties and rebuilt in the early Ming: three tiers of eleven vaulted openings, the lowest carrying the water.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a surviving Chinese water gate where a whole river, not a canal, passes a city wall, so it shows the form a water gate takes when the watercourse is wide. It is a tertiary stub article on one gate of a Ming imperial capital, and its only reference is a 2013 Nanjing Daily feature, so its account of the upper tiers holding troops and grain is a modern newspaper's claim, not a period record; the gate standing today is a 2001 rebuild, with the upper tier surviving only as ruins and one arch restored. It is one example, not a survey of water gates; it gives the arch count but no widths, and its sluice is described as built in the late Qing, so the gate's earlier closing gear is not described.

Used for: a river-wide water gate as a row of arches with a sluice in the lowest tier, and the upper tiers used to store troops and grain (cities/river-cities)
