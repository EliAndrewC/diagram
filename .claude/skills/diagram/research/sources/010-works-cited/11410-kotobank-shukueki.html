kotobank-shukueki

Kotobank, 宿駅 ("relay station", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AE%BF%E9%A7%85-78488)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the relay station, whose article in Heibonsha's revised World Encyclopedia, signed by the transport historian Kodama Kota, traces the station system from the ancient relay stations of the ritsuryo codes through the medieval inns to Tokugawa Ieyasu's organization of the Tokaido in 1601, and describes how stations were laid out.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the Tokugawa stations were given plots along the highway with a fixed frontage division, so that the lots came out even, with entrances bent at a right angle (masugata) and a water channel along both sides or down the middle of the street - the planned side of a road town. It also says most stations were built on existing villages, some joined from several, so the even lots were a re-division along the road rather than a town laid out on open ground, and it gives the bent entrances and channels as things commonly done, not the plan of every station. It is a tertiary summary, though by the leading historian of the subject, of the Japanese system only, and gives no measurements.

Used for: the even lots and the squared entrance of a post town (towns)
