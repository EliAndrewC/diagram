kotobank-shukueki

Kotobank, 宿駅 ("relay station", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AE%BF%E9%A7%85-78488)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the relay station, whose encyclopedia article traces the station system from its medieval roots to Tokugawa Ieyasu's organization of the Tokaido in 1601, and describes how stations were laid out.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the Tokugawa stations were given plots along the highway with a fixed frontage division, so that the lots came out even, with squared entrances and a channel along the street - the planned side of a road town. It is a tertiary summary of the Japanese system only, and gives no measurements.

Used for: the even lots and the squared entrance of a post town (towns)
