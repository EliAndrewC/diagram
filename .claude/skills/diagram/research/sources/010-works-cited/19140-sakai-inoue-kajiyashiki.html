sakai-inoue-kajiyashiki

Sakai City, "井上関右衛門家住宅 主屋・座敷棟・道具蔵・俵倉・附属棟" ("The Inoue Sekiemon house: main house, parlor wing, tool store, bale store and annex", title translated), updated 2024-08-27 (in Japanese; https://www.city.sakai.lg.jp/kanko/rekishi/bunkazai/bunkazai/shokai/bunya/kenzobutu/inouesekiemonke.html)

What it is: The city's description of a designated Tangible Cultural Property: the workshop and dwelling of a Sakai gunsmith family, on its site by the map of 1689, with the frontage and room plan of each of the main house's three blocks (only the central one dated, to the early Edo period), the purchase and conversion dates of the parlor wing, and a table of each building's overall dimensions.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the core of a town smith's house to the early Edo period and gives its frontage, three and a half ken, with a through earth floor and a board-floored strip where bellows and gun-making tools are now shown, so it is a period anchor for how wide a town smith's house stood on its street. Its limits: the smith was a gunsmith in a great commercial city, not a tool smith of a county town; the three and a half ken is the central block only - the main house as it stands adds blocks of two and three ken on either side, which the page does not date, and the site today runs through from one street to the next; the page gives the main house's overall size, 18.116 by 15.846 m for all three blocks together, without saying which side is the street, and no depth for the central block alone; and it says where the tools are shown today, not where the forge stood in the period.

Used for: an early-Edo town smith's house frontage (urban-features)
