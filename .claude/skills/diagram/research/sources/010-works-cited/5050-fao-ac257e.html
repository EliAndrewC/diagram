fao-ac257e

FAO, Report of Consultancy at the Regional Lead Centre in China for Integrated Fish Farming (https://www.fao.org/4/ac257e/AC257E05.htm)

What it is: A working paper by Peter Edwards of the Asian Institute of Technology, Bangkok - NACA/WP/82/6, Network of Aquaculture Centres in Asia, October 1982, hosted in the UN Food and Agriculture Organization's document repository - on the Chinese regional lead center for integrated fish farming, recording what was observed at the ponds and what was recommended. Its subject here is what happens in the water directly below a livestock shed built on the dike.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the ONE source read that addresses where manure enters a pond rather than how much of it does, which is the nearest anything comes to the question this map raises - and the answer it gives is that the problem is concentration at the point of entry, cured by spreading the manure, while the shed stays on the dike. That is why it is cited: it bounds what the record does and does not say about a shed's position. Its limits are that it is one consultant's 1982 report on modern farms rather than a survey, and the anaerobic patch it watched formed under a modern manuring rate, that its remedies (pumps, sprinklers, concrete distribution channels) are modern equipment we do not draw, and that it says nothing whatever about a pond's inlet or its sluice - the specific question asked of it.

Used for: manure distribution techniques needed to avoid anaerobic conditions in the pond next to the livestock quarters; fish losing appetite at the manuring and feeding sites; the shed itself sanctioned on the dike in the same passage
