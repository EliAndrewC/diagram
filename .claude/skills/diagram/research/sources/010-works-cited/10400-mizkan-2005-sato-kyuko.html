Mizkan Mizu no Bunka Center, "Ine no 'joshiki' wa kinsei shoki ni tsukurareta" (Rice's "common sense" was made in the early modern period), an interview with Sato Yoichiro, 2005-01-12 (in Japanese; https://www.mizu.gr.jp/fudoki/people/012_satoh.html)

What it is: An interview published by a food company's water-culture foundation with Sato Yoichiro, a rice geneticist and historian, then a professor at the Research Institute for Humanity and Nature. He argues that rice farming rested its land until the medieval period and turned to cropping the same paddy every year at the start of the early modern period, once the land surveys taxed by area.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that dates the end of resting paddy and gives a reason for it. It is an interview, not a study. Sato gives it as his own view ("I think"), cites no documents for it, and pictures the medieval practice as moving on to other land, not a set rotation.

Used for: that resting ended in the early modern period, as the land surveys taxed paddy by area - one scholar's view (fields)
