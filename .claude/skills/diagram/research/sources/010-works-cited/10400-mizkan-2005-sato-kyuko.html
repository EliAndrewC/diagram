mizkan-2005-sato-kyuko

Mizkan Mizu no Bunka Center, "Ine no 'joshiki' wa kinsei shoki ni tsukurareta" (Rice's "common sense" was made in the early modern period), an interview with Sato Yoichiro, 2005-01-12 (in Japanese; https://www.mizu.gr.jp/fudoki/people/012_satoh.html)

What it is: An interview published by Mizkan Mizu no Bunka Center, a food company's water-culture center (closed at the end of 2025; its pages stand as an archive), with Sato Yoichiro, then a professor at the Research Institute for Humanity and Nature, a plant geneticist who studies rice by DNA analysis and writes popular histories of rice. He argues that rice farming rested its land through the medieval period - alongside newly opened paddy that was not rested - and turned to cropping the same paddy every year at the start of the early modern period, once the land surveys taxed by area.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the end of resting paddy and gives a reason for it, but it is an interview, not a study, and speaks for Japan alone. Its dating runs against the historians of medieval Japan: a specialist's review of the field (Nishitani 2023, Shakai Keizai Shigaku 89(3)) reads the period's land records as showing fields left out for a year (nen-ara, kata-arashi) as common in the early medieval period, and made stable and cropped every year - in western Japan double-cropped - in the Kamakura and Muromachi periods, the 13th to 15th centuries by region, long before the early modern surveys. It is one scholar's minority view, used as such. Sato is a geneticist speaking outside his field. Sato gives it as his own view ("I think"), cites no documents for it, and pictures the medieval practice as moving on to other land, not a set rotation.

Used for: that resting ended in the early modern period, as the land surveys taxed paddy by area - one scholar's view (fields)
