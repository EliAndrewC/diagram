jinya-kotobank

Kotobank, 陣屋 - 世界大百科事典 (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%99%A3%E5%B1%8B-82659)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the jin'ya, carrying the Heibonsha world encyclopedia on the several kinds of seat the word covers, on the intendant's official residence also being called the daikan-jin'ya, on what stood inside such a compound - the residence, the office for public business, the staff houses, the rice and levy stores, the judgment yard and the gaol - and on the jin'ya-machi that grew around one.

Why it applies, and its limits: It makes the identification our unwalled tax seat rests on a published one rather than this record's own guess, and it names the rice store inside the compound, which is the thing the fire posture says was fireproofed. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary and that it describes Tokugawa Japan only, so the Chinese county seat our maps also draw on is not covered by it.

Used for: the daikansho as the daikan-jin'ya, what its grounds held, and the jin'ya-machi that grew around one
