ja.wikipedia 五重塔 ("five-story pagoda", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/五重塔)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on five-story pagodas, written by anonymous editors (read in its 2026-09-16 revision). It lists Japan's national-treasure and important-cultural-property pagodas, most with a height, alongside Meiji-and-later pagodas (some of them concrete) and pagodas that were lost. The article carries a banner saying it has too few sources, and none of its heights is footnoted.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the range of heights of real premodern five-story pagodas that still stand outdoors, from about 16 m (Murō-ji) to about 55 m (Tō-ji, 1644); the miniature pagodas kept indoors and the modern concrete ones on the same page are not evidence for this. It is a tertiary summary whose heights carry no citations, so it is used for the range and not as the authority for any one number. It covers Japan only: the article itself says few wooden pagodas survive in China or Korea, and China's pagodas were often masonry and could be climbed. It lists only the pagodas that survived, and taller ones burned (it gives Gangō-ji's lost pagoda as about 73 m, uncited), so the top of the range understates the tallest ever built. Every pagoda on the list stands at a great or famous temple, and the list says nothing of how common pagodas were.

Used for: how tall a five-story pagoda was (religion-and-death)
