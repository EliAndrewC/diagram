What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the cultivated chrysanthemum: its arrival from China, the court's Choyo festival, the Edo-period breeding and its regional lines, and its use as an altar and offering flower.

Why it applies, and its limits: It establishes that the chrysanthemum was grown in Japan as a medicine, an ornament and a flower for the altar, which is what a planted field of it could be for. A tertiary encyclopedia article, which Wikipedia itself flags as insufficiently sourced. Its history (the arrival from China, the court festival, the Edo breeding) is footnoted to a 1994 Asahi encyclopedia essay by the horticulturist Tsukamoto Yotaro, but its statement that the flower has been used on altars "since long ago" carries no date and no footnote. It describes breeding and display, not fields, and gives no plot sizes; the only commercial growing it describes is modern (electric lighting, greenhouse cut flowers supplied year-round, dwarfing chemicals) and none of that belongs in the setting.

Used for: the chrysanthemum's arrival from China, the court festival, Edo-period breeding and its use as an offering flower (fields)
