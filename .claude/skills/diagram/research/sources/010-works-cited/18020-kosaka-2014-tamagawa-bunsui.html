kosaka-2014-tamagawa-bunsui

Kosaka Katsunobu, "玉川上水の分水の利用 ─江戸時代から近代─" ("The use of the Tamagawa aqueduct's branches, from the Edo period to modern times", title translated from the Japanese by this project), lecture 1 of the 18th Tama Exchange Center series "武蔵野台地と水", 26 September 2014, Tama Exchange Center (in Japanese; https://www.tama-100.or.jp/cmsfiles/contents/0000000/676/18.pdf)

What it is: The printed outline of a lecture by a director of the Japan Society for Industrial Archaeology, with a table of the Tamagawa aqueduct's thirty branch channels taken from the Josuiki, the shogunate's record of the aqueduct: each branch's year of grant, its intake's width and height, the villages it served and its length.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Edo-period sizes at the small end of the channel ladder: a branch serving one village taken off through an intake of seven sun to one shaku square, one serving fourteen villages through an intake of three shaku. These are the sizes of the intake openings, not of the channels below them, and the branches of an aqueduct on a dry upland carried drinking and household water as well as field water; the table records the original's red-ink corrections beside some figures, and the lecture does not date the Josuiki.

Used for: the intake sizes of Edo-period branch channels serving one village or many (water)
