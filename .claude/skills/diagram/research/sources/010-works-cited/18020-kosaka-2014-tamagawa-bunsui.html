kosaka-2014-tamagawa-bunsui

Kosaka Katsunobu, "玉川上水の分水の利用 ─江戸時代から近代─" ("The use of the Tamagawa aqueduct's branches, from the Edo period to modern times", title translated), lecture 1 of the 18th Tama Exchange Center series "武蔵野台地と水", 26 September 2014, Tama Exchange Center (in Japanese; https://www.tama-100.or.jp/cmsfiles/contents/0000000/676/18.pdf)

What it is: The printed outline of a lecture by a director of the Japan Society for Industrial Archaeology, with a table of thirty of the Tamagawa aqueduct's branch channels taken from the Josuiki, the shogunate's record of the aqueduct (three branches to daimyo estates are left out, and the lecturer has corrected some entries from the Josuiki's other volumes and from 1870 records): each branch's year of grant, its intake's width and height, the villages it served and its length.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Edo-period sizes at the small end of the channel ladder: a branch serving one village taken off through an intake of two or three sun to one shaku square, most of them six sun to one shaku, one serving fourteen villages through an intake of three shaku. These are the sizes of the intake openings, not of the channels below them, and the branches of an aqueduct on a dry upland carried drinking and household water as well as field water, and were grants off a supply the shogunate rationed for Edo (the lecture lists limits on branches as a topic), so an intake may be smaller than a village's own river-fed channel would be; the table records the original's red-ink corrections beside some figures, and the lecture does not date the Josuiki.

Used for: the intake sizes of Edo-period branch channels serving one village or many (water)
