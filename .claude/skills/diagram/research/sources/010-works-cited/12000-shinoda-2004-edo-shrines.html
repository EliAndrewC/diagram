shinoda-2004-edo-shrines

Shinoda Akie, Fukui Tsuneaki, Nakai Yū and Shinohara Osamu, 「Locations and Movements of Shinto-shrines in Edo City」 (the paper's own English title; original: 「江戸城下町における神社の配置とその傾向」), 土木史研究論文集 (Papers on Historical Studies in Civil Engineering) 23 (2004), pp. 157-164 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/journalhs2004/23/0/23_0_157/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed study of where Edo's shrines stood and how their sites changed from before the castle town to the end of the Edo period, drawn from 110 shrines (108 listed in the shogunate's 1829 survey 『御府内寺社備考』, plus two), plotting each on maps period by period and classing its site in five steps of ground - flat land, foot of a plateau edge, mid-slope, top of a plateau edge or hilltop, flat top of a plateau - and tracing the moves of the Sannō shrine, Edo's principal shrine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a castle town's principal shrine kept on raised ground as it was moved outward, and the drift of the town's shrines toward flat ground. Its limits: Edo was the shogun's capital, far larger than the setting's cities, and it is one city - the authors name other castle towns as work not yet done, so the pattern is not shown to hold elsewhere; Edo's ground, plateau edges above river and bay lowland, offered raised sites that a town on a level plain would lack; the sites are placed from modern maps (1657-1895 street maps, a 1995 1:10,000 survey) and field visits, and the foundings and early moves rest on the shrines' own legends, which the authors warn are not proven history; the paper's own count finds many shrines on flat ground from the start; the PDF is set in two columns and its text layer interleaves them.
