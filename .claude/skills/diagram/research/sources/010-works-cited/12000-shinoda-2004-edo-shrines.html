shinoda-2004-edo-shrines

Shinoda Akie, Fukui Tsuneaki, Nakai Yū and Shinohara Osamu, 「The placement of shrines in the Edo castle town and its tendencies」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸城下町における神社の配置とその傾向」), 土木史研究論文集 (Papers on Historical Studies in Civil Engineering) 23 (2004) (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/journalhs2004/23/0/23_0_157/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed study of where Edo's shrines stood and how their sites changed from before the castle town to the end of the Edo period, classing each site by its ground - plateau, plateau edge, hilltop, flat land - and tracing the moves of the Sannō shrine, Edo's principal shrine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a castle town's principal shrine kept on raised ground as it was moved outward, and the drift of the town's shrines toward flat ground. Its limits: Edo was the shogun's capital, far larger than the setting's cities; the paper's own count finds many shrines on flat ground from the start; the PDF is set in two columns and its text layer interleaves them.

Used for: a city's principal shrine and where it stood (religion-and-death.html).
