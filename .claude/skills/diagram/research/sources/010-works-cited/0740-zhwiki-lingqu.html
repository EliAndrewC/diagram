zhwiki-lingqu

zh.wikipedia 灵渠 (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/灵渠)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Lingqu canal of 214 BC in Guangxi: the plowshare-snout that divides the Xiang River so that seven parts return to the Xiang over the Great Balance and three pass by the Little Balance into the canal, the two balances being overflow crests that let floods spill back into the old river bed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a second premodern division of a river in stated proportions, with the barrier an overflow crest rather than a closure, corroborating the rule that the stream continues below a diversion. Its limits are the same as Dujiangyan's: a tertiary summary of a river-scale state work, not a village brook.

Used for: the Xiang divided seven to the river and three to the canal; the balances as overflow crests spilling floods into the old bed
