What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the 町火消 (machi-bikeshi), the townsman fire companies of Edo: the ordinance establishing them, issued in 1718 by the South Town Magistrate Ooka Tadasuke on the advice of the block headmen and of Ogyu Sorai; the 1720 revision into forty-seven iroha companies west of the Sumida and sixteen more for Honjo and Fukagawa, one company to about twenty blocks; and the arrangement by which the companies stood under the town magistrate's command while each block bore their cost.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells us who actually manned, paid for and commanded an urban fire watch, which is the point our setting departs from deliberately, and it shows that the departure is narrower than it looks, since command sat with the magistrate historically as well. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary; that it is about Edo alone, the largest city in the country; and that it describes an institution of the long Tokugawa peace, so its scale and its politics do not transfer to a provincial city of the earlier, more martial period these maps are pitched in.

Used for: who manned, paid for and commanded a townsman fire company, and the block-by-block scale of the arrangement - which is what our setting's fire watch is measured against
