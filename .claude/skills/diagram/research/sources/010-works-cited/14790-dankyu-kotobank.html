dankyu-kotobank

Kotobank, 段丘 (dankyū, a terrace), the entries of several Japanese dictionaries, the one used by Yonekura Nobuyuki in the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%AE%B5%E4%B8%98-94939)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering the dictionary entries for the terrace, the flat step left above a river, lake or sea when the water cut down below its old floor; the entry used is the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia's, signed by a geomorphologist.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says terraces, being flat, high and dry, were used early for settlements, dry fields and roads, that ground short of water was left undeveloped, and that irrigation works later turned much of it to paddy - the terrace's place in the fields page's order of ground. It is an encyclopedia summary, written of terraces generally and of Japan's by implication, and it gives no proportions and no date for the conversion to paddy. Its closing sentence on irrigation works sits in a present-day overview, so those works may be modern as readily as Edo-period and the paddy step cannot be taken as premodern; it carries the order of use and not how much of a terrace was dry field or paddy at any one time.

Used for: terraces as early settlement and dry-field ground, left undeveloped where short of water, later partly irrigated to paddy (fields)
