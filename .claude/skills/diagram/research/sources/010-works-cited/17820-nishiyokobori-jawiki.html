ja.wikipedia 西横堀川 ("Nishi-Yokobori canal", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E6%A8%AA%E5%A0%80%E5%B7%9D)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Nishi-Yokobori, an Osaka canal dug in 1600 by a timber merchant and town elder, which branched from the Tosabori River and ran south to the Dotonbori until it was filled in in 1962; it lists the bridges that crossed it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a city canal cut straight off a river at its own mouth, dug by a townsman rather than as part of a defense, which is the second of the two forms a river city's cargo canal takes. Its history rests on one general reference, the 1983 Kadokawa place-name dictionary of Osaka, with no inline citation for the 1600 date or the digger. It says where the canal branched off the Tosabori but not what its mouth looked like; Osaka was an unwalled commercial castle town on a delta laced with rivers, so it says nothing about how a walled city's canal met a moat, and a canal that no longer exists is described from records rather than as seen.

Used for: a city canal opening its own mouth on a river (cities/river-cities)
