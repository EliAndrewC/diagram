en.wikipedia, Fujian tulou (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujian_tulou)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the Fujian tulou, the great earthen communal houses of the Hakka and Minnan mountain districts inscribed as World Heritage: their round and rectangular forms, the single clan under one roof, and the equal, unranked rooms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a famous East Asian fortress form built round, which this page uses to show that even there the square was as common, so that a curved wall is not the East Asian answer to the corner. A tertiary summary used with the Chinese article beside it, which states which form is the more widespread; and a tulou is a rural clan dwelling, one building, recruited here into an argument about castle baileys and city walls of a far larger order.

Used for: the clan under one roof, the equal rooms, and the round and rectangular forms
