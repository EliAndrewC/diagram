What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the 堀川 (Horikawa) of Nagoya, a canal running from Ise Bay to the neighborhood of Nagoya Castle. It records the canal's traditional origin: cut at the founding of Nagoya in the early Edo period, commonly said to have been dug in 1610 under Fukushima Masanori, to carry building materials up to the castle then under construction.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is our type case for the transport canal a city digs for itself - the reason a straight channel runs from a harbor to a construction site and then keeps carrying freight long after the works are done. It is a tertiary summary, the attribution of the digging is one the article itself reports as what "is often said" rather than as established, and the Horikawa today is a modern urban waterway, so we take the purpose and the moment of cutting rather than any measurement of it.

Used for: dug in 1610 under Fukushima Masanori for the castle's construction materials; the 舟入堀 count of nine in Edo is unsupported by any readable page (ja.wikipedia 舟入 is a disambiguation page)
