kotobank-bomori

Kotobank, 坊守 (ぼうもり), entries from the Digital Daijisen, the Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (the last by Kitanishi Hiroshi) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9D%8A%E5%AE%88-628589)

What it is: Dictionary and encyclopedia entries on bōmori, in Shin Buddhism the head priest's wife; the encyclopedia article dates the usage from a list of 1343 and quotes Rennyo's letters on the wives of the priests of the lodging houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: A Shin historian's article in a standard encyclopedia that dates the Shin priest's wife from dated documents: a list of 1343 naming the wife of the head of a dōjō, and Rennyo's fifteenth-century letters on the wives of the priests of the lodging houses. Its limits: it is a tertiary summary, and we read the article, not the documents it cites; the 1343 instance is the wife of the head of a dōjō, a meeting hall, not of a temple; it attests the wife and her role in running temples and dōjō, not the rest of the household, and says nothing of where the household lived or of other sects.

Used for: that a Shin priest's wife and household are attested before modern times (religion-and-death.html).
