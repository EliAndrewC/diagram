kotobank-ressen

Kotobank, 列村 ("linear village", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - entries from Heibonsha's Hyakka jiten Mypedia and Shogakukan's Nihon dai-hyakka zensho (Nipponica) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%88%97%E6%9D%91-878864)

What it is: Two Japanese encyclopedia entries on the linear settlement, houses strung along one or both sides of a road, stream or hill foot; the Nipponica entry (by Nakata Eiichi) separates the road village of farm households from the street village facing a highway with transport and commercial functions.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the geographer's name for a settlement that grows as a strip along a highway because it lives by the traffic, which is the form a town's edge takes when houses run out along the road. It is a pair of short tertiary definitions: it gives no lengths, no dates and no example, and it defines a settlement form rather than saying how any one town ended.

Used for: the street village along a highway, as against the road village of farm households (towns)
