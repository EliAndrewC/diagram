kotobank-negari

Kotobank, "Negari" (根刈, root-cutting), the entry in the Britannica kokusai daihyakka jiten shokomoku jiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%A0%B9%E5%88%88-111166)

What it is: A short encyclopedia entry on root-cut mulberry, the training form in which the trunk is kept within half a meter of the ground and the bushes are small.

Why it applies, and its limits: It confirms the planting number for root-cut mulberry and that the bushes are kept small, and says the form made up 60 percent of Japan's mulberry fields. It is a modern encyclopedia describing twentieth-century practice in dedicated mulberry fields rather than on dikes, undated beyond that. The thousand bushes to ten ares and the 60 percent share are modern figures, and the entry does not say the root-cut form was used before modern sericulture, so for our setting it is a modern practice standing in. It gives no crown width.

Used for: about a thousand root-cut bushes to ten ares, kept small (archetypes)
