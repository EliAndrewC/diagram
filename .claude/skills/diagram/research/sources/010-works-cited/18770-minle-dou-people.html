Liu Taishan and Jiang Xiaodan, "Sangyuan polder: a water-works legend of Lingnan (World Heritage Irrigation Structure)," People's Daily, 2021-02-16 (in Chinese; http://ent.people.com.cn/n1/2021/0216/c1012-32029812.html)

What it is: A People's Daily feature on the Sangyuan polder of Foshan, in the Pearl River delta, on its listing as a heritage irrigation structure; its reporters visit the Minle dou, one of the polder's sluices, and quote the city's water bureau.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a newspaper piece about a heritage site as it survives, written for a general reader. Its dated evidence is the inscription it reports on the Minle dou, carved in the fourth year of the Guangxu era (1878, late Qing), and its sketch of the polder's history: that its dikes, creeks and sluices made one irrigation system by the end of the eighteenth century, and a line it quotes from the polder's gazetteer of 1794, that in drought the people within the polder already had water for their fields. That history is the reporters' own, sourced only by that quotation. How the sluices work (opening to drain the polder when the water within stands high, closing against the tide or flood without) it gives in the present tense, in the words of today's city water bureau director, so that the dou sits in the dike is implied rather than stated; and it says nothing of a sluice for each fish pond.

Used for: that a dou is a sluice through the polder dike, one of them inscribed in the Guangxu era, through which the polder's land and ponds were watered and drained (archetypes)
