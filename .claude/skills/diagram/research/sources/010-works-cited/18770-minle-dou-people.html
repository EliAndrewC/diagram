Liu Taishan and Jiang Xiaodan, "Sangyuan polder: a water-works legend of Lingnan (World Heritage Irrigation Structure)," People's Daily, 2021-02-16 (in Chinese; http://ent.people.com.cn/n1/2021/0216/c1012-32029812.html)

What it is: A People's Daily feature on the Sangyuan polder of Foshan, in the Pearl River delta, on its listing as a heritage irrigation structure; its reporters visit the Minle dou, one of the polder's sluices, and quote the city's water bureau.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its dated evidence is the inscription it reports on the Minle dou, carved in the fourth year of the Guangxu era (1878, late Qing), and its sketch of the polder's history: that its dikes, creeks and sluices made one irrigation system by the end of the eighteenth century, and a line it quotes from the polder's gazetteer, the Sangyuanwei zhi, under the year 1794 (the piece does not date the gazetteer itself), that in drought the people within the polder already had water for their fields. That history is the reporters' own, sourced only by that quotation. How the sluices work it gives in the present tense, in the words of today's city water bureau director, and for some sluices only: those hung with mitre ('人'-shaped) wooden gates open of themselves to drain the polder when the water within stands high, and close against the tide or flood without. It does not say the Minle dou is one of these, though it shows the dou's two heavy wooden gates; that the dou sits in the dike is implied, from its 'within' and 'without' the polder, rather than stated; and it says nothing of a sluice for each fish pond.

Used for: that a dou is a sluice through the polder dike, one of them inscribed in the Guangxu era, through which the polder's land and ponds were watered and drained (archetypes)
