nishitani-2023-chusei-nogyo

Nishitani Masahiro, "Chusei no nogyo to tochi seido" (Medieval agriculture and the land system), in "Nihon chusei keizaishi no kaiko to tenbo", Shakai keizai shigaku (Socio-Economic History) 89(3), November 2023, pp. 217ff. (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sehs/89/3/89_217/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A review article by an economic historian at Fukuoka University, surveying research on medieval Japanese agriculture and land tenure for the Socio-Economic History Society's journal. Among much else it gives the cropped share of Heian paddy, the worked and resting paddy of Oyama estate in 1102, the yearly re-choosing of plots on unstable land, and the stabilizing of land and the nucleation of villages from the Kamakura period on.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a recent survey of the field, written by a specialist for the journal's review section (kaiko to tenbo) rather than a refereed study, and it carries the figures and the sequence behind this record's two forms of paddy, the unstable and the settled. It is a review, so most of what it gives is another scholar's and is read here at second hand: the cropped share is Takashima 2017's reconstructed average for late Heian, not any one place's; the yearly re-choosing is Toda 1967's reading; the stabilizing is Kinda 1985's. The Oyama figures it takes from the estate's own document (Kyo-o-gokokuji monjo no. 24). It says nothing of which plots in a village rested. The one estate figure is a whole estate's in 1102, not a hamlet's, and Oyama lay in Tanba, near the capital. The article dates the stabilizing of land to the advanced regions first. It puts the nucleation of villages at the late 13th to 14th century in the Kinai but not until the late 14th to 15th in the east and Kyushu.

Used for: how much paddy lay out of crop in the early medieval period, the yearly re-choosing of plots, and how the unstable land was overcome (fields)
