kotobank-edo

Kotobank, 江戸 ("Edo", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - entries from Shogakukan's Nihon dai-hyakka zensho (Nipponica), Heibonsha's Sekai dai-hyakka jiten and the Britannica International Encyclopedia, among others (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B1%9F%E6%88%B8-36887)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering the Japanese reference encyclopedias' long entries on Edo, the shogun's city, including how its town ground grew: the towns that spread along the suburban highways, the dates at which the built-up ground around the old core was brought under the town magistrate, and the temples' own gate-front townhouses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the plainest readable statement that a Japanese city's built ground ran on out along its highways past the planned core, and that the government later caught up with it by taking the new strips into the town. Its limits: Edo was the largest city in the country, a capital rather than a town, so its highway strips are an upper case; the entries are tertiary summaries; and the dating is hedged in places ("around the Kan'ei era").

Used for: town ground growing along Edo's suburban highways after the fire of 1657, and the built-up ground around the core taken under the town magistrate in 1662 (towns)
