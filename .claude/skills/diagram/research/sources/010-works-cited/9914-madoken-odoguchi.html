madoken-odoguchi

Window Research Institute (Madoken), glossary of window terms, "大戸口" (ōdo-guchi, the big-door entrance) (in Japanese; https://madoken.jp/terms/40048/)

What it is: A glossary entry by the Window Research Institute, a foundation studying windows and openings in architecture, defining the big-door entrance of a Japanese folk house and the big door itself, with photographs of two preserved houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets the big door against the ordinary one and dates the word, which is the one width relation found in plain words: the big door was one wider than half a ken. It is a short, unsigned glossary entry, a modern tertiary summary that cites no source: it gives the half-ken sense of the word, and its appearance in writings from about the Nanboku-chō period (the fourteenth century), as what is said, not from a document it names, and "wider than half a ken" is where the word's sense begins, not a measured width of any door, so it bounds the big door from below only. It gives no width for an ordinary door and speaks of farmhouses and townhouses, not warrior residences.

Used for: the big door as wider than an ordinary door and, originally, wider than half a ken (buildings)
