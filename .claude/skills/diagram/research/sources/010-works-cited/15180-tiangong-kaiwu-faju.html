tiangong-kaiwu-faju

Song Yingxing (宋應星), Tiangong kaiwu (天工開物, The Works of Heaven and the Inception of Things), 1637, chapter 12 "Gaoye" (膏液, Oils), section "Faju" (法具, Methods and tools), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E5%B7%A5%E9%96%8B%E7%89%A9/%E6%B3%95%E5%85%B7)

What it is: The section on oil pressing from a late-Ming technical encyclopedia: the wedge press made from a hollowed log, its wood and its slot, the roasting pan, the seed mill worked by two men or by an ox, the steaming and wrapping of the meal, and the boiling method for some seeds.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the standard Chinese account of the press and its tools, written by a scholar who described working practice, and it sizes the log, the slot and the pan - but as rules of thumb, not measurements: the log is "an arm-span round" (合抱), its hollow holds from "under five dou" to "a little over one shi", and the slot is "about three or four chi long, three or four cun wide", which the text says is made to suit the log and has "no fixed form" (無定式); its chi and cun are Ming measures (a Ming chi is about 32 cm, not the Japanese shaku). Its limits are that it is of the late Ming, not the Song; that it describes the equipment, not the building it stood in or how many pressers a town kept; and that the transcription is marked as unproofread.

Used for: the hollowed press log an arm-span round, its slot, the roasting pan, and the hand and ox mills (urban-features)
