Niu Runzhen and Song Yu, 「The development of Buddhism in the Siming region at the turn of the Song and Yuan, centered on the Yanyou Siming Gazetteer」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「宋元之际四明地区佛教的发展 - 以延祐《四明志》为中心的考察」), first published in 中国地方志 2018 no. 1, reposted by the Jilin Provincial Local Gazetteer Compilation Committee (https://dfz.jl.gov.cn/fzlt/201809/t20180911_5218848.html)

What it is: A journal article by two historians of Renmin University of China on what the 1320 gazetteer of Siming (the old Ningbo prefecture) records of its Buddhist temples and eminent monks, quoting the gazetteer's biographies and citing modern histories of the Yuan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the Yuan count of 42,318 temples and 213,148 monks and nuns across the realm - which it dates to Shizu's twenty-seventh year, though the Yuan shi's annals enter it as the Xuanzheng yuan's report at the close of Zhiyuan 28 (1291) - at second hand from Zhou Liangxiao's modern history of the Yuan rather than from the Yuan shi itself, and the gazetteer's account of Tiantong's monks growing from under two hundred to twelve hundred under the abbot Zhengjue in the Southern Song. The realm-wide count is an official tally of registered clergy and gives no spread, so the average it yields hides the great houses and the one-monk temples alike; the Tiantong figures describe one famous Chan monastery in the mountains sixty li east of the prefectural city - neither an ordinary house nor a city temple - and come from a biography written some 160 years after Zhengjue's death, in a passage that also tells a miracle. The 1,200 is a round number for the monks he took in after the Jin raid burned Mingzhou's other temples, and was more than the house could feed without a donor's gift of a thousand hu of grain; the under-two-hundred before him is the likelier ordinary strength of a great monastery.

Used for: the Yuan census of temples and clergy, and the monk population of a great Song monastery (religion and death)
