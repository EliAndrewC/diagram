daqing-luli-yejin

《大清律例》兵律, article 夜禁 (the Great Qing Code, the night prohibition), Chinese Wikisource transcription (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E5%A4%A7%E6%B8%85%E5%BE%8B%E4%BE%8B/%E5%85%B5%E5%BE%8B)

What it is: The military-law chapter of the Great Qing Code, the penal code in force across Qing China, in a Wikisource transcription of the primary text; its night-prohibition article sets the hours of the curfew by the watch bells and the punishment for breaking it, in the capital and in the provinces.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the law itself, the best kind of source for what a curfew was and what it cost to break, and it applies directly to the Chinese half of the two-traditions argument our block gates rest on. Its limits are that a statute records the rule and not how often it was enforced, and that the hours are given in watches, which this page converts to clock time only roughly.

Used for: the curfew's hours by the watch bells and its penalties, graded between the capital and the provinces
