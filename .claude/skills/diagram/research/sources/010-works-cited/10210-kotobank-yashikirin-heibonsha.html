kotobank-yashikirin-heibonsha

Tsutsumi Toshio (堤 利夫), 「Homestead grove」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋敷林」), in the Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha, revised edition), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%B1%8B%E6%95%B7%E6%9E%97-145113)

What it is: The Heibonsha world encyclopedia's signed entry on the yashikirin, the grove kept inside a farm homestead, by a forest ecologist, reproduced on the kotobank dictionary site beside shorter dictionary definitions.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a general account by a specialist of what homestead groves were for and how they varied, which bears on how big a village's groves were only so far as to say that their form, size and species varied. It is an encyclopedia entry for all of Japan, citing no sources and giving no figures; it names regions only as examples (the tsuiji-matsu of the Hikawa plain in Shimane, the zelkova and oak groves of the Kantō plain). It describes the groves as they stood in twentieth-century Japan, already declining as farm households fell; only its note on the related village woods, whose grown trees needed the domain's leave to fell, reaches back to the Edo period. So it shows the form lasted into living memory, and dates no grove's form or size to the era we draw.

Used for: that a homestead grove's form, size and species varied (vegetation)
