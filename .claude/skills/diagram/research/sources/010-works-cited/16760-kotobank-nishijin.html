kotobank-nishijin

kotobank 西陣織 ("Nishijin weaving", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%A5%BF%E9%99%A3%E7%B9%94)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting the dictionary and encyclopedia entries on Nishijin, the weaving quarter of Kyoto that grew up on the site of the western army's camp after the Onin war and wove the country's finest figured silks on the high loom (takabata).

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the example of a city quarter of weaving houses bound in a guild of high-loom (takabata) weavers, and in the Edo period it was the country's leading silk-weaving district. It was not the only one: by the page's own account Sakai and Hakata also wove silk, and after the 1730 fire Tango, Nagahama and Kiryu grew into rival weaving districts. The page collects modern dictionary and encyclopedia entries, so it is a tertiary summary. It is good for the quarter's history and its guilds, and says nothing of the quarter's extent, street plan or houses. It describes the imperial capital's luxury trade, a ceiling rather than a provincial norm, and gives no house or loom counts. Its Meiji and later material (Jacquard looms from 1873, power looms from about 1887, synthetic dyes) is modern and does not apply to the setting.

Used for: Kyoto's weaving quarter and its guild of high-loom (takabata) weavers (urban-features)
