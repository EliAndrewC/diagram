bushijyoshi-daikan-jinya

Bushi Joshi Nikki (a blog), "Daikan no jin'ya gurashi" ("An intendant's life in the jin'ya", translated from the Japanese by this project), 2024 (https://bushijyoshi.com/%E4%BB%A3%E5%AE%98%E3%81%AE%E9%99%A3%E5%B1%8B%E6%9A%AE%E3%82%89%E3%81%97/)

What it is: A popular blog post on how the shogunate's intendants lived in their jin'ya - what the jin'ya was, who worked there, how the office was paid for and what an intendant's family made of the posting - listing as its references Nishizawa Atsuo's Daikan no nichijo seikatsu (Kodansha, 2014) and a 2018 book by Yamamoto Hirofumi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page found carrying the nine-in-ten figure for intendants in debt and the Takayama gundai's letter about snow on his veranda. It is a secondary retelling by an unnamed author with no page references, and it hedges the money sentences itself ("it seems", "it is said"); the two books behind it were not read, so the figure rests on the blog's report of them. It concerns the shogunate's own intendants of the late Edo period, not a daimyo's county magistrates.

Used for: the jin'ya as residence, office, court and tax store (towns); the allowance still short after 1725, the intendants' hikioi debts, and Toyoda Tomonao's letter from Takayama (buildings)
