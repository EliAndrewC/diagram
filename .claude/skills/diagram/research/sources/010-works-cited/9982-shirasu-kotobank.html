shirasu-kotobank

Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha; Jinbo Fumio), Hyakka Jiten Mypedia (Heibonsha), Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten and the Britannica concise encyclopedia, s.v. 白洲, aggregated at kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%99%BD%E6%B4%B2-80840)

What it is: The Kotobank page for shirasu, carrying four dictionary entries on the Edo-period court: its tatami upper veranda, board lower veranda and gravel, who sat on each by status, and the upper tier where examiners and scribes sat.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the scribes at a hearing and the commoners on straw mats on the gravel, which is where our clerks' seats and kneeling places come from, and one of its entries (the World Encyclopedia's) is signed by the legal historian Jinbo Fumio. The scribes' place rests on one entry alone, Mypedia's unsigned summary: the signed entry says only that some magistracies set apart an upper tier and names no one on it, and the Yamakawa entry puts the examining officers between the upper and lower verandas instead. Its limits are that the entries are short, describe the great magistracies of the cities rather than a country office, differ among themselves on some points (which ronin sat where, whether every court had the upper tier), and give no sizes.

Used for: the examiners' and scribes' tier, and the commoners on straw mats in the open (buildings)
