zh.wikipedia 宏村村 (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/宏村村)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Hongcun, a Huizhou village on the World Heritage list, describing its siting and the water channel its villagers dug through it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the rivers pass on one side of the village while a dug channel about a meter wide runs through it to every household - the same pairing the record already shows at Harie, now in a Chinese village. It is a tertiary summary, flagged by Wikipedia as needing more sources, and it cites nothing for its water passages (its only references are a Michelin travel-guide page and a county conservation plan whose link is dead). It dates the bringing of water into the village to the Ming Yongle reign (1403-1424), when a geomancer re-planned the layout, but it does not date the channel itself. Hongcun is a wealthy lineage village, planned by a geomancer and rebuilt with salt-merchant money, and what stands today is a restored tourist site. It is not a small farming hamlet, and its channel carries household water, not irrigation.

Used for: the rivers passing west of Hongcun; the channel nearly 1 m wide dug through the village to every household (water)
