kudamatsu-stone-torii

Kudamatsu City, 下松市史異説 (Kudamatsu city history, variant accounts), section 「Stone torii」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「石鳥居」), on the ADEAC local-history archive (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/kudamatsu-city/texthtml/d100060/mp100060-100060/ht000720)

What it is: A section of 下松市史異説, a local-history volume of "variant accounts" of Kudamatsu (Yamaguchi prefecture) published on the city's archive, in its chapter on the city's stone monuments (the page shows no author or date; it records damage in 1991, so it was written after that). It lists the city's stone torii by their inscribed dates, transcribes the inscriptions, and gives the measurements of the six oldest.

Why it applies, and its limits: It counts 64 stone arches standing in the city today and lists the 26 dated up to the Kansei era, year by year from 1679 to 1801. A temple record of 1807 shows the earliest (1679) to have been raised by the parishioners (氏子), at the solicitation of the Buddhist priest of the Myōken shrine-temple whose main approach it marked. The rest stand one to a place at village shrines, Konpira shrines and a Benzaiten hall, and several were given by a single named donor rather than a parish. The 64 is a present-day count of arches of every era, not an Edo-period number. The prefecture's oldest dated stone torii is from 1629, so stone arches here are a seventeenth-century-and-later form and stand for nothing earlier. It is one provincial city's inventory, it lists the arches rather than their spacing, and it describes no row.

Used for: dated single stone arches, one built by a parish (religion-and-death.html).
