kotobank-hasa-nipponica

Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho (Nipponica, Shogakukan), s.v. 稲架 ("hasa", the rice-drying rack, title translated from the Japanese by this project), with the Digital Daijisen and Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entries, on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/稲架)

What it is: The general encyclopedia's entry, by the agronomist Hoshikawa Kiyochika, on how cut rice was dried in the sheaf before threshing in Japan, setting the rack beside the other methods - laid on the paddy floor, stacked in a heap, hung on a stake - with where each was used and how the racks were built.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page that sets out the drying methods side by side with their regions, which is what turns "the drying rack" into a choice among several forms, and it says where a rack stood and how long it could run. It is a modern reference entry, for Japan alone, and its regions and rack sizes are the twentieth-century pattern from before combines replaced the rack; it says nothing of China, which the setting also draws on. Its only dates are that ground drying and stacking were in use by the Nara period (eighth century) and that a record of 841 urged rack drying, so no form's regional spread can be dated earlier than the twentieth century. Its twenty to thirty days of drying is reported as what is said, not measured, and it gives no count of racks per household.

Used for: the regional drying methods, where a rack stood and how long one ran (homesteads)
