kotobank-hasa-nipponica

Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho (Nipponica, Shogakukan), s.v. 稲架 ("hasa", the rice-drying rack, title translated from the Japanese by this project), with the Digital Daijisen and Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entries, on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/稲架)

What it is: The general encyclopedia's entry on how cut rice was dried in the sheaf before threshing, setting the rack beside the other methods - laid on the paddy floor, stacked in a heap, hung on a stake - with where each was used and how the racks were built.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable page that sets out the drying methods side by side with their regions, which is what turns "the drying rack" into a choice among several forms, and it says where a rack stood and how long it could run. It is a modern reference entry, describing practice as folklorists recorded it in the twentieth century, and it gives no dates for any form and no count of racks per household.

Used for: the regional drying methods, where a rack stood and how long one ran (homesteads)
