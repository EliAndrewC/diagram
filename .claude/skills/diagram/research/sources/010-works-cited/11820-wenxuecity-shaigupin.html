Banshizhai zhuren (半是斋主人), "Shaiguping de qianshi jinsheng" (The drying floor, past and present), a blog post on Wenxuecity, 2023 (in Chinese; https://www.wenxuecity.com/blog/202304/80401/6455.html)

What it is: A personal memoir by a writer from a terraced rice valley at the foot of Mount Heng, in Hunan, of the drying floors of the village of their childhood: each household's floor in the yard before its house, how it was made (tamped earth washed with cow dung, or, for the rich, lime and sand), and the cement floors of today.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness account of a south-central Chinese rice village in which the drying floor belonged to the household, which is one of the two forms the record finds. It is a memoir of the twentieth century, of one village, without references, and what it says of earlier centuries is the writer's own belief; it is used only for the household floor and how it was surfaced. By its own account the lime-and-sand floor was for the rich (barely one household in a hundred, in the big tiled houses and ancestral halls), and the ordinary floor was tamped earth washed with cow dung before each harvest; the cement it describes today is modern and has no place in the setting.

Used for: the household drying floor before the house, and its surface (homesteads)
