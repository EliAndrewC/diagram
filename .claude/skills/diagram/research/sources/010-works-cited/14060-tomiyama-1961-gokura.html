tomiyama-1961-gokura

Tomiyama Hiroshi (富山博), 近世における郷蔵建築の型について - 上州勢多・利根地方の場合 ("On the types of village storehouse building in the early modern period: the case of the Seta and Tone districts of Joshu", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 日本建築学会論文報告集 (Transactions of the Architectural Institute of Japan) no. 69, October 1961, pp. 797-800 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijsaxx/69.2/0/69.2_KJ00004445653/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A short architectural-history paper surveying the surviving village storehouses (gokura) of two districts of Gunma, sorting them by purpose - the store that received the tax rice and the store that kept grain against famine - and by construction.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says, as a historian of the buildings, where the tax rice went after the village store: to the lord's rice storehouses in the castle town or to a river landing for shipment, which is the chain a county's granary sits in. Its limits: it covers two districts of one province, it is about the village end of the chain, and the passage is a general statement without a count of how much went which way.

Used for: that the village tax store held rice only briefly before it went on to the castle town's storehouses or a river landing (buildings)
