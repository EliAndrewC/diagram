ahmed-soliman-2022

Ahmed, S.T. and M. Soliman, "Using Experimental and Artificial Intelligence Techniques for Prediction of the Sedimentation at Lateral Intakes", SCIREA Journal of Civil Engineering and Building Construction 7(1), February 2022, doi:10.54647/cebc56057 (https://article.scirea.org/pdf/56057.pdf)

What it is: A study from Cairo University run in a laboratory flume at Egypt's Hydraulics Research Institute - a straight rectangular channel 0.6 m wide with a side intake set at several angles over a movable sand bed - with statistical and neural-network models fitted to the runs to predict how much of the channel's sediment the intake draws.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page read for the finding that a right-angle intake draws a large share of the sediment and that turning the intake toward the main flow draws less, and for the older studies it reviews, which disagree on where the best angle lies. It is a modern flume at laboratory scale on a straight channel with no weir and no bend; its least-sediment angle of 164 degrees comes from the fitted models rather than a tested run (the flume's best was 160), the angle is measured from the main channel upstream of the intake so that 164 is an intake only about 16 degrees off the flow, and the earlier works are reported through it rather than read. It says nothing of premodern intakes or of moats. Its own runs cover only angles from 110 to 170 degrees, so the statement that a right-angle intake draws a large share of the sediment is the paper's framing of what earlier workers noticed, not one of its tested results. It is published by SCIREA, a publisher that was on Beall's list of potentially predatory open-access publishers, so it is relied on for the direction of the effect, which the older studies it reviews share, and not for its exact figures.

Used for: how an intake's angle changes the sediment it draws (cities/river-cities)
