ja.wikipedia 水路橋 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/水路橋)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 水路橋 ("aqueduct bridge"), on the structures that carry a watercourse over a river or a valley, with Japanese examples such as the Tsujun bridge, the Meisho irrigation works and the Suirokaku arcade in Kyoto.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it to establish the form itself - that when a canal has to cross a valley the answer is a bridge carrying the water, and what such a bridge looks like - which is a piece of furniture our irrigated landscapes need and which readers rarely expect. It is a tertiary summary and does not date its examples, so it tells us the type exists and how it is built and nothing about when any given one was; the named examples are also nineteenth-century or later works, so we draw the form and not their engineering.

Used for: the aqueduct bridge carrying water over rivers and valleys (通潤橋, 明正井路, 水路閣 - the page does not date them)
