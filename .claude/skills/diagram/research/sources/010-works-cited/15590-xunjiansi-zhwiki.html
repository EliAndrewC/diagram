xunjiansi-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 巡檢司 ("xunjiansi", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B7%A1%E6%A3%80%E5%8F%B8)

What it is: A stub Chinese Wikipedia article on the xunjiansi, the patrol and inspection office that began under the Later Tang, spread under the Song and Jin, and under the Yuan, Ming and Qing sat below the county yamen. It gives the rank of its officer (by the Qing example, ninth rank), the Ming's use of it against pirates, and its growth in the late Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the Chinese state's own post below the county seat, which a town map might otherwise be tempted to draw at a town gate. It is a tertiary encyclopedia stub. Its two footnotes (the Wenxian tongkao and Gu Yanwu's Rizhilu) support only its opening sentence, and it lists two modern works as sources. Its figure of forty-two posts set up in Fujian in 1387 carries no citation, and the Mingshi's biography of Zhou Dexing (juan 132) gives forty-five, so the number is not to be taken from here. Its ninth rank is stated by the Qing example. It says the posts answered to local officials but that the Ministry of War controlled their setting up, closing and evaluation. The office changed by dynasty: under the Yuan it was non-permanent and military, and it multiplied in the late Qing. It says nothing of where a post stood or what it looked like.

Used for: the xunjiansi's low-ranking officer, the Ming's forty-two in Fujian, and county posts under the magistrate (defenses)
