Nakajima Gakushō (中島楽章), "清代徽州の山林経営・紛争・宗族形成" ("Forest management, disputes and lineage formation in Qing Huizhou", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 社会経済史学 72-1 (2006), pp. 3-25 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sehs/72/1/72_KJ00005697845/_article/-char/ja)

What it is: A socio-economic history paper on one lineage of Qimen county, Huizhou, read from its own contracts and records of the eighteenth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the Chinese side: grave land was among what a family left undivided, and one lineage kept its founding ancestor's grave-hill on its own land, its ridge in the ancestral hall's name and its slopes shared among members. Its limits are that the undivided-land point is what scholars are "often" said to argue, which the paper then qualifies for Huizhou; that it is one mountain lineage's case, not a village's burial ground; and that it gives no distance from the houses.

Used for: a Chinese lineage's grave-hill held on its own land, and grave land left undivided
