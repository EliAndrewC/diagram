izakaya-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 居酒屋 ("izakaya, drinking house", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B1%85%E9%85%92%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the drinking house, mostly on the modern trade, with a section on how the Edo drinking house grew out of the sake retailer who let customers drink on the spot.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Edo more than 1,800 drinking houses in the 1820s and says the countryside was slow to get them, not until the later eighteenth century - the contrast between a great city and a village. A tertiary summary. Both claims rest on one popular book, Koizumi Takeo's 酒と日本人 (Chūō Kōron Shinsha), and the page gives the Edo count as hearsay ("is said to have been"), not from a named survey. The count is also for Edo, a city of about a million people and the largest of its day, so it is a different scale of place from our towns and cities and cannot be carried over one-to-one.

Used for: Edo's drinking houses in the 1820s and their late arrival in the villages (urban-features)
