What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the jishinban, the Edo-period neighborhood watch house: its patrol and reporting duties, the ladder on the roof - which the article itself calls a small-scale fire tower - and the bell beside it, and the arrest and firefighting tools kept inside, down to the standard, the hook, the hand pump, the buckets and the ladders.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source that puts the bell, the ladder and the gear at the TOWN's watch post, which is where our unwalled-seat fire posture places them and which no page had previously supported. Its limits are that it is tertiary and specific to Edo-period Japanese towns, and that it attaches all of it to the neighborhood watch house and nothing at all to an intendant's office, which is the half of our posture that stays unsupported.

Used for: the roof ladder and bell on the watch house, and the firefighting gear kept inside it
