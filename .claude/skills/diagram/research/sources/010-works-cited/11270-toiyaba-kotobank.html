toiyaba-kotobank

Kotobank, s.v. 問屋場 (toiyaba; the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha Sekai dai-hyakka jiten article by Kodama Kota, and the Yamakawa Nihonshi shojiten, among others; in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%95%8F%E5%B1%8B%E5%A0%B4-107230)

What it is: The Kotobank dictionary page gathering several Japanese reference works' entries on the toiyaba, the office at an Edo-period post station that ran the relay of porters and horses: its staff, where it stood in the station, how many a station had, and what a great passage was like there.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gathers several dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the office, one of them signed by Kodama Kota, a historian of the Edo post-station system, and it says where the office stood and that it could be part of the house of the toiya, the station's chief official (not the village headman). Its limits are that these are short tertiary entries that give no evidence, so "one or two to a station" is a generalization, not a count; that it describes an office, not a yard: it names no stable and no waiting ground; that its crowd of hundreds of horses and thousands of men is the extreme of a great passage on the shogunate's main highways, with relief levies called in, not an ordinary day at a small station; and that it describes the Tokugawa post-station system of Edo Japan, one of the setting's two models, not China's relay stations.

Used for: the office's staff and siting, one or two to a station, the separate offices at Okazaki, and the crowd of support horses and men at a great passage (towns)
