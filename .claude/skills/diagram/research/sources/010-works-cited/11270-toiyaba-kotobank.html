toiyaba-kotobank

Kotobank, s.v. 問屋場 (toiyaba; the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha Sekai dai-hyakka jiten article by Kodama Kota, and the Yamakawa Nihonshi shojiten, among others; in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%95%8F%E5%B1%8B%E5%A0%B4-107230)

What it is: The Kotobank dictionary page gathering several Japanese reference works' entries on the toiyaba, the office at an Edo-period post station that ran the relay of porters and horses: its staff, where it stood in the station, how many a station had, and what a great passage was like there.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest readable account of the office, written by historians of the Edo road system, and it says where the office stood and that it could be part of the headman's house. Its limits are that it describes an office, not a yard: it names no stable and no waiting ground, and it is about Japanese post stations, not relay stations of the setting's kind.

Used for: the office's staff and siting, one or two to a station, the separate offices at Okazaki, and the crowd of support horses and men at a great passage (towns)
