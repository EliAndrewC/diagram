Mirage News, "China's Water Heritage: Significance of Polders" (XJTLU; https://www.miragenews.com/chinas-water-heritage-significance-of-polders-955573/)

What it is: A news item on Mirage News reporting research from Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University on the history of China's polders, the embanked field islands of the lower Yangtze, quoting the researcher directly.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is our warrant for the shape of a polder landscape - by the early twentieth century the large enclosures had been subdivided into small islet-like "fish-scale" polders, and it was only after 1949 that their organic outlines were replaced by standardized rectangles for industrialized farming - which is why our maps draw irregular polder blocks instead of a grid. It is a press release republished as news rather than the study itself, so it is a summary in the researcher's own words that a reader cannot check against data; and its dates run later than our setting, so we read it as the end point of a long development rather than as a picture of any one earlier century.

Used for: "By the early 20th century, they were further compartmented into even smaller islet-like polders, dubbed as fish-scale polders or yulin wei (鱼鳞圩), for their shapes as seen on aerial photos"; after 1949 "the organic forms of polders were replaced by standardised rectangular patterns for industrialised agricultural production"
