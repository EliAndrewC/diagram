ja.wikipedia 金剛組 ("Kongo Gumi", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%87%91%E5%89%9B%E7%B5%84)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Kongo Gumi, the Osaka temple-building firm that traces itself to the building of Shitennoji, with a chronology of the firm.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the house served as Shitennoji's retained temple carpenters up to the early modern period and lost the temple's stipend in 1868, when the temple lost its lands - a craft household kept on a stipend by one temple. It is a tertiary encyclopedia article that carries a banner saying it lacks sources, and its chronology - the stipend and its loss in 1868 included - cites nothing; the firm's early history is its own tradition as the article relays it. It describes one house at Shitennoji, one of Japan's oldest and greatest temples, in a major city, so it shows the arrangement at a great temple and not that an ordinary town or village temple kept carpenters; it gives neither the stipend's size nor how many carpenters there were, and says nothing of where the carpenters lived.

Used for: a temple's retained carpenters (religion and death)
