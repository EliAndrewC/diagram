fukui-kuzuryu-edo-dikes

Fukui River and National Highway Office (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism), 4．江戸時代の治水 ("4. Flood control in the Edo period", title translated from the Japanese by this project), from the Kuzuryu river basin history (in Japanese; https://www-2.kkr.mlit.go.jp/fukui/kasen/siryou/hen2/2syou2/4edo1.html)

What it is: A short page of the river office's history of the Kuzuryu basin in Echizen, on the river dikes built in Echizen in the early seventeenth century (the continuous Gankaku dike and local banks raised by the domain's chief retainer Honda Tomimasa, and other dikes of the time), giving their rough usual base, crown and height in ken and meters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that gives an Edo-period earth dike's cross-section in figures, from a government office drawing on local histories. The dikes are river dikes guarding a castle town, a retainer's seat and the villages of the plain, not a village's ring dike round its own fields (the page names the ring dike as an older, different form), so they bound a village polder's dike from above rather than measure it. The page gives the figures as rough, says the size varied from place to place, and cites no source for them (its one citation, a village history, is for another dike). Taken together they do not describe a bank that could stand: a 3-ken base under a 1-ken crown and a 2-ken height leaves each side a run of 1 ken for a rise of 2, a slope of 1 horizontal to 2 vertical, far steeper than an earth bank usually stands, so at least one figure (most likely the base) is a rounding or a slip, and the height and crown are the safer figures to use.

Used for: an early Edo earth dike's base, crown and height (archetypes)
