吳自牧 (Wu Zimu), 夢粱錄 (Mengliang lu) ("Record of the Millet Dream", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 卷十三, Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A2%E7%B2%B1%E9%8C%84/%E5%8D%B713)

What it is: The thirteenth chapter of the memoir of Southern Song Hangzhou of about 1274, on the city's trades, guilds and markets, including the names the trades went by.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness account of the Song capital and names the bathhouse keepers' guild, the "fragrant water guild", which attests commercial bathhouses as an organized trade of a Song city. Its limits are that it describes the capital, the largest city of its day, and says nothing of how many bathhouses there were or of a smaller town.

Used for: the Song bathhouse keepers' guild (urban-features)
