吳自牧 (Wu Zimu), 夢粱錄 (Mengliang lu) ("Record of the Millet Dream", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 卷十三, Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A2%E7%B2%B1%E9%8C%84/%E5%8D%B713)

What it is: The thirteenth chapter of the memoir of Southern Song Hangzhou of about 1274, on the city's trades, guilds and markets, including the names the trades went by.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness account of the Song capital and names the bathhouse keepers' guild, the "fragrant water guild", which attests commercial bathhouse-keeping as a trade of a Song city with a name of its own; the same chapter's early-market section also has hawkers selling washing water at bathhouse doors (浴堂門賣面湯者). Its limits are that it describes the capital, the largest city of its day, which the chapter itself says was unlike other prefectures (非他郡比也), and says nothing of how many bathhouses there were or of a smaller town; and it gives the name in its list of trades with unusual guild names, after saying that some trades only borrowed the name 行 without being answerable for the government's purchases, so it attests the trade and its name, not how the bathhouse keepers were organized.
