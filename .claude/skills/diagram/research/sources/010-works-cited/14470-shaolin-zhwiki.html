zh.wikipedia 少林寺 ("Shaolin Temple", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B0%91%E6%9E%97%E5%AF%BA)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Shaolin Temple on Mount Song in Henan, the Chan monastery famous for its martial arts, with a history of its growth and its destructions.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the monastery at its Tang-Song height a population of over 2,000 monks on 540 mu of temple ground, which is one of the great houses at the top of the range, not a typical temple. The sentence carries no footnote of its own, and the paragraph it sits in is cited only to the temple's own official website (its modern introduction to itself, archived 2014), so the number is the monastery's own promotional claim rather than a count from a period record; the same paragraph repeats legend as fact (a Tang grant of a thousand qing of land). The figure is also a round one for a span of centuries, and Shaolin was a mountain monastery rather than a city temple.

Used for: the monk population of a great Chinese monastery at its height (religion and death)
