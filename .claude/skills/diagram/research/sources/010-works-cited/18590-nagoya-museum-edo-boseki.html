nagoya-museum-edo-boseki

Nagoya City Museum, "江戸時代の墓石" ("Gravestones of the Edo period", title translated), an exhibition page of 2020-2021 (in Japanese; https://www.museum.city.nagoya.jp/exhibition/owari_joyubi_news/edijidai_boseki/index.html)

What it is: The page of a small permanent-gallery exhibition on Edo-period gravestones and graveyard finds from within Nagoya, including a panel on the stone monuments set up in graveyards.

Why it applies, and its limits: A city museum's curators name the six jizo at the entrances of graveyards and temples as a representative stone monument of the Edo-period graveyard. Its limits: an exhibition summary with no site list or dates for the six jizo themselves; its sentence that such monuments, carved with dedications such as "all spirits of the three worlds" and "safety of the whole village", were set up in numbers "from the mid-Edo period" by individuals, lay associations or villages follows straight on from its naming of both the six jizo and the three-worlds stones, and may cover either or both; it names no dated six jizo, so it is not used for the six jizo's date; it calls the six jizo a representative monument, not one every graveyard had; it describes the Owari region.

Used for: the six jizo at graveyard entrances as an Edo-period graveyard monument (religion-and-death)
