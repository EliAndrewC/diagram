Packer, Meyerson, Skálová, Pyšek and Kueffer, "Biological Flora of the British Isles: Phragmites australis", Journal of Ecology 105 (2017) 1123-1162 (https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1365-2745.12797; DOI 10.1111/1365-2745.12797) - the Wiley page shows the full text to a reader with no subscription

What it is: A peer-reviewed review article by Jasmin G. Packer, Laura A. Meyerson, Hana Skálová, Petr Pyšek and Christoph Kueffer in the Journal of Ecology (2017), part of the long running "Biological Flora of the British Isles" series, which gathers what is known about the biology, ecology and habitats of the common reed, Phragmites australis.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use its account of the hydrosere, the succession of vegetation running from open water through the littoral zone to reed, marsh and alluvial forest, to decide which bands of plants a map paints around still water and in what order. The limit is place: it is a flora of the British Isles, so while the reed itself is the same plant that grows across Eurasia, the communities it names are European, and what we take from it is the depth-driven ordering of the zones rather than a species list for an East Asian pond.

Used for: the hydrosere as the paper states it - open water, littoral, reed, marsh, alluvial forest (section IV); it names no sedge or wet-meadow band and never mentions Calamagrostis
