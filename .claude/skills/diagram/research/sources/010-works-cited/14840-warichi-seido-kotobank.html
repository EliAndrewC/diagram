warichi-seido-kotobank

Kotobank, 割地制度 (warichi seido, the land re-allotment system), Yoshitake Kaichirō's entry in the Encyclopedia Nipponica (日本大百科全書) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/割地制度-880122)

What it is: The Encyclopedia Nipponica's entry on the Edo-period land re-allotment system, describing step by step how a village sorted its land into groups, made up equal shares and drew lots for them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says why a re-allotted holding was scattered: every group of land - paddy or dry field, good or poor soil, safe or flood-prone - was split into as many parts as there were shares, and each share took one part from every group, so a household's lot lay in pieces across the whole village. It describes only the villages that practiced re-allotment, which the entry calls a special custom found mostly where harvests were unstable - newly reclaimed land on the low, wet ground of great rivers' lower reaches, and landslide country - while in ordinary villages each farmer's plots stayed fixed, so it is no evidence that holdings were scattered this way everywhere. It is a short encyclopedia summary resting on a single study (Aono Shunsui, 1982), and the lots were drawn by the landholding farmers alone, so it carries the scatter's mechanism and no count of pieces.

Used for: why a re-allotted holding lay in pieces across every kind of ground (fields)
