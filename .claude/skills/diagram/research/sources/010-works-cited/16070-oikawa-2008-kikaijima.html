oikawa-2008-kikaijima

Oikawa Takashi (及川高), "先祖へと収束する力―喜界島における墓制とその語りを貫くもの―" ("The force converging on the ancestors: what runs through the burial customs of Kikaijima and their telling", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 文化人類学研究 9 (2008), pp. 79-100 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/wsca/9/0/9_79/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: An anthropology paper on the burial customs of the settlements of Kikaijima, in the Amami islands, from the late Edo period to the present, built from the remembered accounts of four informants from four of the island's settlements, and aimed at how the living speak for the dead rather than at a survey of graves.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for one case: after the Meiji ban on exposure burial, where to bury was settled settlement by settlement, and graveyards of each settlement began to form outside the old burial caves. Its limits are large: Kikaijima's cave-burial culture is unlike the mainland's, the settlement graveyards it records are post-1868, the paper itself assumes (after Gamō 1959) that the island's settlements differed widely, its table, where the settlement graveyard is named, reads only through a garbled text layer; and it rests on four informants' memories from four settlements, not a count of the island's graveyards.

Used for: settlement graveyards beginning to form on one island after the Meiji ban on exposure burial
