ichigami-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 市神 ("ichigami, the market god", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/市神)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the market god, the deity worshipped at a market site to keep its trade safe and fair, drawing on the folklorist Kitami Toshio's studies of markets and peddlers and Nakajima Giichi's study of market settlements.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that most market sites were on old highways and that the god was worshipped at the roadside, sometimes at the market's boundary, and that its body was usually a round natural stone - which is what a market leaves on the ground on a day with no market. It is a short article resting on mid-twentieth-century folklore studies; it gives no date for the stones it describes and no count of them. It describes Japanese folk practice only, recorded by folklorists in the 1950s-70s; it says nothing of Chinese periodic markets, so for a town modeled on China it is a Japanese practice standing in. Its one sign of age is its own contrast: the roadside god is the older arrangement, and markets and gods moved into shrine grounds after modernization.

Used for: where a market was held on the road, and the market-god stone that marks it (towns)
