Japan Greenery Research and Development Center, 「Pine groves then and now: the Byobuzan windbreak」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「松原の今昔物語 屏風山防風林」) (in Japanese; https://www.jpgreen.or.jp/pinerescue/jiten/matsu/boufurin/03.html)

What it is: A page of a greenery foundation's series on Japan's coastal pine groves, giving the history of the Byobuzan windbreak on the west coast of the Tsugaru peninsula in an old documentary style, from its founding in 1682 down to the early twentieth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates to 1682 a domain's planting of the hills and the coastal dunes on the side the sea wind came from, to shield the fields being opened behind them, and records that when the belt was felled in the famines of the 1780s the new-field paddies nearly went back to waste - a planted windbreak for a whole district's farmland, before the modern period. It is a domain-scale coastal belt tens of kilometers long, not the belt of one village or town; the page's narrative is a transcribed older history with no author or source named and at least one slip in its dates, and its closing dimensions (40 km by 4 km, and an area of 4.1 ha that cannot square with them) are undated.

Used for: the Tsugaru domain's windbreak of 1682, planted on the windward side of the fields it sheltered (towns)
