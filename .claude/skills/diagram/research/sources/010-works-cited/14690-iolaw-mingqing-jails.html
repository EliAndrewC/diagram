iolaw-mingqing-jails

Bai Hua (柏桦), Institute of Law, Chinese Academy of Social Sciences (中国法学网), 明清州县的监狱 ("The jails of Ming and Qing departments and counties", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (http://iolaw.cssn.cn/gdfls/200501/t20050112_4594856.shtml)

What it is: A legal-history paper by Bai Hua (柏桦), professor of law at Nankai University, on the jails of Ming and Qing departments and counties, posted on the website of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences' Institute of Law in January 2005: a reading of the Ming and Qing codes, local gazetteers and a Qing magistrate's handbook on the statutes, the staff and the parts of a jail, and on the informal lock-ups (post stations, granaries, runners' lodgings) counties kept beside it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the jail's internal parts, which is what a drawn jail compound divides into. By Qing statute there was an inner jail for the condemned, an outer one for lesser convicts, and a separate room for women; the Ming rule it cites asks only that heavy and light offenders, and men and women, be held apart. That the women's jail stood beside the outer one behind its own wall comes from one magistrate's handbook (Huang Liuhong's 福惠全书, 1694), which says how a county jail should be run, not what statute required. The one gazetteer it quotes shows a county jail of only two rooms. It is about the county and department tier, and says the four-layer form held in some counties, not all.

Used for: the parts of a Chinese jail (cities/government)
