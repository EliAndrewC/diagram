Suzuki Ryoji, Kozai Isao, Ogawa Hatsue, Yuda Kazuo, Inoue Tatsuya, Mogi Shigeo and Matsuoka Isamu, 「On the position and structure of privies in farm villages」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農村における便所の位置および構造について」), Nōson Igaku (Journal of the Japanese Association of Rural Medicine) 8(2-3): 333-339, 1959 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjrm1952/8/2-3/8_2-3_333/_pdf)

What it is: A short public-health survey by parasitologists and physicians of where the privy stood and how it was built on 154 farm households in one village each of Saitama, Tokyo and Miyagi, made alongside a field trial of an egg-killing agent and compared with an earlier survey of Gunma. It counts outdoor and indoor privies, their pits and storage tanks, and the field pits (nodame) where night soil was kept out by the fields.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a household count of the privy as it still stood in 1959 farm villages that manured with night soil, the nearest readable measure of how usual the outdoor privy was before flush toilets. Its limits are that it covers three villages the authors themselves say cannot stand for their prefectures, that it is postwar and so a late survival of the practice rather than an Edo figure, taken after decades of government privy-improvement and anti-parasite campaigns and with chemical fertilizer already in use, either of which may have changed how pits and tanks were built and how much night soil was stored, and that its history of the storage tank moving out to the fields is the authors' conjecture, not an observation.

Used for: the share of farm households with an outdoor privy; the privy inside the barn; night soil kept beside the privy or in a field pit (homesteads)
