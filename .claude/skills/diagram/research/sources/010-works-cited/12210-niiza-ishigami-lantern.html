niiza-ishigami-lantern

Niiza City, 「Niiza notes, no. 24: the stone lanterns left behind」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「にいざ見聞録（第24回　残された石燈籠）」) (in Japanese; https://www.city.niiza.lg.jp/site/niiza-kenbunroku/bunkazai-kenbun-024.html)

What it is: A local-history column of Niiza city, Saitama, first printed in the city's newsletter in 1998 to 2000, on a pair of stone lanterns from the vanished tutelary shrine of Ishigami village.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a village tutelary shrine, its precinct with its grove at over 2,000 tsubo while the shrine stood (it stood until the end of Meiji), and a pair of lanterns given to it and inscribed in 1799 by the village's parishioners. The lanterns now flank the hall of the Inari shrine on the site, and the column does not say where they stood at the old shrine; the column does not date the precinct figure beyond "at that time", so it may describe the Meiji ground rather than the Edo one. It is one village's pair, reported in a popular municipal column, not a survey of village lanterns.

Used for: a village's dated lanterns at its shrine (religion-and-death.html).
