tokyo-archives-jiin

Tokyo Metropolitan Archives, 「Visiting the temples of Edo-Tokyo: researching Tokyo's temples - the road to the family temple」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸東京寺院探訪 - 東京のお寺を調べる - 旦那寺への道」) (https://www.soumu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/01soumu/archives/0713jiin.htm)

What it is: An unsigned guide by the Tokyo Metropolitan Archives, apparently of 2024, to the sources for a Tokyo temple's history: the shogunate's survey of Edo's temples and the Meiji government's temple registers of 1872 and 1877, which set down each temple's history, parish households, precinct and clergy.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the 1872 registers carry the careers of every temple's head priest, retired priest and disciples, and that in the Edo period, when celibacy was the rule outside some sects, many monks were younger sons of farming or samurai households, ordained in their early teens and moving among their sect's temples before rising to head one. It describes who the clergy were and gives no count of monks for any temple; it is a guide to the records, not an analysis of them.

Used for: who staffed an ordinary Edo temple (religion and death)
