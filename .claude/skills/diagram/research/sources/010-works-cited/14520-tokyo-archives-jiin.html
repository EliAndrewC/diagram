tokyo-archives-jiin

Tokyo Metropolitan Archives, 「Visiting the temples of Edo-Tokyo: researching Tokyo's temples - the road to the family temple」 (translated; original: 「江戸東京寺院探訪 - 東京のお寺を調べる - 旦那寺への道」) (https://www.soumu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/01soumu/archives/0713jiin.htm)

What it is: An unsigned guide by the Tokyo Metropolitan Archives, dated 2024 on the current site though its latest reference is of 2001, to the sources for a Tokyo temple's history: the shogunate's survey of Edo's temples of the 1820s, the temple registers of 1872 ordered by the Meiji Ministry of Doctrine, and those of 1877 ordered by Tokyo Prefecture, which set down each temple's history, parish households, precinct and clergy.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the 1872 registers carry the careers of every temple's head priest, retired priest and disciples, and that in the Edo period, when celibacy was the rule outside some sects, many monks were younger sons of farming or samurai households, ordained in their early teens and moving among their sect's temples before rising to head one. It describes who the clergy were and gives no count of monks for any temple; it is a guide to the records, not an analysis of them, and its "many" is the archive's own summary with no figures behind it. The careers it summarizes are those of the clergy in office in 1872, the last Edo generation rather than the whole period, and of the temples of Edo, whose prefecture counted 2,486 temples in 1872. A village temple may have been staffed otherwise, though the page notes that the monks' birthplaces and postings often lay far beyond Edo.

Used for: who staffed an ordinary Edo temple (Religion and the dead)
