oamishirasato-kaoku

Oamishirasato city, 大網白里町史 ("History of Oamishirasato town", title translated from the Japanese by this project), part 1, chapter 3 (the early modern period), section 4, (5) 家屋 ("Houses", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on the city's digital museum (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/oamishirasato-city/texthtml/d100010/mp100010-100010/ht011770)

What it is: A section of a Chiba town's official history describing the houses its Edo-period farmers lived in, with the 1849 plan of the Ohara family's residence (the headman of Seinakoya village) and a table of the buildings and main-house sizes of the sixteen households of another village of the town, Kakimochi, in 1885.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives both ends of the ladder in one district's terms: why a headman's house was large (it lodged the lord's officials on their rounds), what stood on the headman's plot, and that an ordinary farmer's house was small, about 18 tsubo in the 1885 table against 40 to 45 for the leading households. Its limits: it is one coastal district of Kazusa; the house-size table is from 1885, which the page argues roughly reflects the late Edo period, and from a different village (Kakimochi) than the headman's plan (Seinakoya), so the 18-tsubo ordinary house is the most common size among sixteen households of one village, not a measured neighbor of the Ohara house; and the Ohara were also rich landholders with manufacturing businesses, so their house is not the office's alone.

Used for: why a headman's house was large, what stood on its plot, and how big an ordinary farmer's house was beside it (homesteads)
