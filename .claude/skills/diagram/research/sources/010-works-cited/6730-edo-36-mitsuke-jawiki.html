What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 江戸城三十六見附 (the thirty-six mitsuke of Edo castle), listing the guarded gate posts around the castle, many of them built as a single unit with a bridge thrown across the moat. The article notes that the figure of thirty-six is a popular saying and that other counts, such as sixty-six or ninety, are argued.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for how a great castle's approaches were watched - a guardhouse paired with a bridge at each crossing of the moat - and for the conventional round number a place of that size was said to have. Its limits are that an encyclopedia is a tertiary summary, and that the article's own hedge is the point: thirty-six is a traditional figure rather than a count of gates, so we treat it as a convention and not as a total to reproduce.

Used for: 「It is popularly said that Edo Castle had 36 mitsuke (gate guardposts),」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「俗に江戸城には36の見付があったといわれ」) - and the page's own hedge that the real count was higher (「there are various theories, such as 66 or 90」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「66、90など諸説あり」))
