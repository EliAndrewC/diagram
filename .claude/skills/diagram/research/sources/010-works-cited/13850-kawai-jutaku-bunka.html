kawai-jutaku-bunka

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Online, 河合家住宅主屋 ("The main house of the Kawai residence", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/181523)

What it is: The national heritage database's entry for a registered tangible cultural property in Gujo, Gifu prefecture: the main house, built in 1805, of a family that served as the district's shoya, with its dimensions and building area.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one measured headman's house of the Edo period - about 12.5 by 6.5 ken, 291 sq m of building area, two stories - which gives a real size to set against the one this record draws. Its limits are that it is a single house, in a rural mountain district of central Japan (Ichijima, in Hachiman-cho), two-storied and tile-roofed, described as it stood when registered in 2000, and it gives no ordinary farmhouse of the same place to compare it with. Its size is the footprint of the main building alone, not of the homestead's other buildings. That footprint includes an earthen work floor (doma) across its eastern part and a stable projecting to the south, and its upper floor was built open for silkworm-raising. So 291 sq m is the size of a working farm building, not of the family's living rooms.

Used for: the measured size of one Edo-period headman's house (homesteads)
