en.wikipedia Kaifeng (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaifeng) - "Five major floods devastated the city between 1375 and 1416, and flooding occurred more than 300 times from the Southern Song Dynasty to the late Qing dynasty"

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Kaifeng, the Chinese city on the Yellow River that served as an imperial capital and has been repeatedly buried and rebuilt by floods. The passage we use counts the disasters: five major floods between 1375 and 1416, and more than three hundred flood events from the Southern Song to the late Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the shape of Kaifeng's history under the Yellow River - the repeated floods that buried the city layer on layer - which is the extreme case a river-city map is measured against. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary, and the flood count we quote from it carries no citation on the page, so it is an uncited encyclopedia statement, with the peer-reviewed frequency in the peer-reviewed flood study cited beside it the better anchor; that Kaifeng under a perched, silt-choked river is the worst case of river-city danger and not the average; and that it is the Chinese half of a setting also modeled on Japan.
