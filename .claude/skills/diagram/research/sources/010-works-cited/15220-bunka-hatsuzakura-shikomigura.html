bunka-hatsuzakura-shikomigura

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Online, "初桜酒造仕込蔵" ("Hatsuzakura Brewery fermenting store", title translated from the Japanese by this project), registered tangible cultural property, Katsuragi, Wakayama (in Japanese; https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/196649)

What it is: The national database's entry for a sake brewery's fermenting store of the Meiji era (1868-1911): a tiled two-story timber building of 16 ken by 6 ken, with a building area of 376 square meters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an official record of a surviving brewery hall in a country town, the large end of the brewery glyph's range. Its limits are that it is of the Meiji era, after the period the setting draws on, when brewing had grown; that it gives one building, not the compound or its tanks; and that it gives the plan in ken, whose length the record does not state. At a 6-shaku ken (about 1.82 m) 16 by 6 ken is about 96 by 36 ft, or about 320 square meters, but the record's own building area is 376 square meters. That fits the longer Kansai ken of 6.5 shaku (about 1.97 m; about 103 by 39 ft), or an attached lean-to, and the record does not say which.

Used for: a brewery hall of about 96-103 by 36-39 ft (urban-features)
