jawiki-toshuko

ja.wikipedia 頭首工 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/頭首工)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the toshuko, the head works: the collective name in agricultural engineering for the structures that draw irrigation water from a river or lake into a canal - the intake weir, the intake mouth and the ancillary works such as the settling basin, fishway and boat passage - and what damming the river to raise its upstream level is for.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the parts of the transition from stream to canal in the order a map draws them, which is what the entry on the intake rests on. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary of modern engineering practice, silent on village-built works before modern times and on whether the river continues below the works, so both of those rest on other sources.

Used for: the parts of the head works - intake weir, intake mouth, settling basin, division works; raising the upstream level to ease intake and divide the flow
