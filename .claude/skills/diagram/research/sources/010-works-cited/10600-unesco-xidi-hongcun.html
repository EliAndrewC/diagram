unesco-xidi-hongcun

UNESCO, World Heritage List, "Ancient Villages in Southern Anhui - Xidi and Hongcun" (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1002/)

What it is: The World Heritage listing, inscribed in 2000, of two villages in the Huizhou region of Anhui whose Ming and Qing layout survives, with the committee's description of how each sits on its water.

Why it applies, and its limits: In one authoritative page it shows both answers to our question side by side: Xidi built along and between its streams, and Hongcun next to its stream. Its limits: both are wealthy merchant-lineage villages in one region of China, larger and richer than a farming hamlet, and the page is a heritage listing, not a survey: it describes them as they survive and dates their layout only broadly (features of Anhui villages "between the 14th and 20th centuries", waterways "dating from the 14th century"). Hongcun "next to a stream" is not a village set apart from its water: the page says a canal was dug in 1405 to bring the stream's water into the village, with a south lake (Nanhu) added about two centuries later and the Moon Pond at its center. So it is the beside-the-stream form with water led through it. That dating comes from the page's Dutch summary, which also wrongly calls Hongcun's 1131 founder a Han-dynasty general.

Used for: that Xidi was built along and between three streams and Hongcun next to a stream (water)
