kasuga-mura-no-tera

Terasaki Naotoshi (Kasuga Local History Society), "村の寺" ("The village temple", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Kasuga city, Fukuoka, reprinted from the city bulletin of 15 July 2020 (in Japanese; https://www.city.kasuga.fukuoka.jp/miryoku/history/1010879/1011757/1012437.html)

What it is: A short local-history essay on the city's website, by a member of the local history society, on how the old temples of Kasuga's villages came to be founded.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives one measured medieval village - Sugaura on Lake Biwa, about 70 households with three temples and twelve hermitages in the fifteenth century - and the general point that temples came to many villages as the self-governing village formed. Its limits: it is a popular essay with no references given, Sugaura is a famous and well-documented exception rather than a typical village, and the figures are fifteenth-century, before the Edo parish system.

Used for: a village of about 70 households keeping several temples; temples founded in many villages from the fifteenth century (religion-and-death)
