kasuga-mura-no-tera

Terasaki Naotoshi (Kasuga Local History Society), "村の寺" ("The village temple", title translated from the Japanese by this project), Kasuga city, Fukuoka, reprinted from the city bulletin of 15 July 2020 (in Japanese; https://www.city.kasuga.fukuoka.jp/miryoku/history/1010879/1011757/1012437.html)

What it is: A short local-history essay on the city's website, by a member of the local history society, on how the old temples of Kasuga's villages came to be founded.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives one medieval village - Sugaura on Lake Biwa, about 70 households with three temples and twelve hermitages and cloisters in the fifteenth century, the count given without its source - and the author's view that temples probably came to many villages as the self-governing village formed. Its limits: it is a popular essay with no references given, and it states the spread of village temples as a supposition ("it is thought"), not a finding. Sugaura, in Ōmi near the capital where self-governing villages were strongest, is a famous and well-documented exception rather than a typical village, and it stands in here for villages in general and for Kasuga in Kyushu, which the essay is about. Only three of its fifteen were temples; the twelve hermitages and cloisters housed monks who, the essay says, lived as villagers, supported themselves and fought beside them, not endowed temples. The figures are fifteenth-century, before the Edo parish system.

Used for: a village of about 70 households keeping several temples; temples founded in many villages from the fifteenth century (religion-and-death)
