Himeji Castle (Wikipedia) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himeji_Castle)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on Himeji Castle, the largest and best-preserved of Japan's surviving castles and a World Heritage site. It gives the complex's overall measurements - a circumference of 4,200 m and an area of 233 hectares (576 acres), 107 hectares inside the middle moat, roughly 950 to 1,600 m east to west and 900 to 1,700 m north to south - along with moat dimensions averaging 20 m wide, 34.5 m at the widest and about 2.7 m deep.

Why it applies, and its limits: These are the numbers that fix the grand end of our castle range, and the moat widths in particular set how broad a defensive water line should read on a map. It is a tertiary encyclopedia summary and we take its figures only where its own references support them; Himeji is exceptional rather than typical, so it is an upper bound and never a median, and its measurements are of the modern site as preserved and restored, including ground whose Edo-period use is no longer visible.

Used for: the grand end of the castle band - 233 ha / 576 acres total enceinte, 4,200 m circumference, 107 ha inside the middle moat; moat dimensions (avg 20 m, max 34.5 m, depth ~2.7 m)
