Wang Shuanghuai, "The practice and wisdom of water control in ancient China", Renmin Luntan (People's Tribune), 16 July 2025, p. 7 (in Chinese; http://paper.people.com.cn/rmlt/pc/content/202507/16/content_30093293.html)

What it is: An essay by Wang Shuanghuai, professor of history at Shaanxi Normal University, in a magazine of the People's Daily group, summarizing Chinese irrigation engineering from the legendary Yu to the Qing, including the standard division of canal head works into those built with a dam and those without, and the gate that stood on a canal's intake mouth.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the same two intake forms the record already holds for Japan, from the Chinese side, and says how the damless form found its mouth. It is a modern general summary by a historian, not a primary text, and cites only one work of its own; its two-form division is modern hydraulic-engineering classification, given with no dynasty or region attached, so it cannot say which form any period or place favored; it speaks of canals in general, and the works it names are large state and county ones, rather than of a village's brook, and it gives no proportion between the two forms.

Used for: the two forms of intake head works, with a dam and without; the damless mouth opened at the concave side of a bend; the gate on the intake mouth (water)
