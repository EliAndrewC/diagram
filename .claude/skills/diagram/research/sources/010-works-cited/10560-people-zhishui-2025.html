"The practice and wisdom of water control in ancient China", Renmin Luntan (People's Tribune), July 2025 (in Chinese; http://paper.people.com.cn/rmlt/pc/content/202507/16/content_30093293.html)

What it is: An essay in a magazine of the People's Daily group summarizing ancient Chinese irrigation engineering, including the standard division of canal head works into those built with a dam and those without, and the gate that stood on a canal's intake mouth.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the same two intake forms the record already holds for Japan, from the Chinese side, and says how the damless form found its mouth. It is a modern general summary, not a primary text; it speaks of canals in general rather than of a village's brook, and it gives no proportion between the two forms.

Used for: the two forms of intake head works, with a dam and without; the damless mouth opened at the concave side of a bend; the gate on the intake mouth (water)
