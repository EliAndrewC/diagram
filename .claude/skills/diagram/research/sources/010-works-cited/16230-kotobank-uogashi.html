kotobank-uogashi

Kotobank, 魚河岸 ("Uogashi, the fish landing", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the 日本大百科全書 ("Encyclopedia Nipponica", title translated from the Japanese by this project) entry by 佐藤農人 and other reference entries (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%AD%9A%E6%B2%B3%E5%B2%B8-34099)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the uogashi, gathering dictionary and encyclopedia entries: the Nipponica defines it as a river landing holding a fish market that wholesales to the trade, and says such landings are found all over the country, before turning to Edo's Nihonbashi market.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one read statement that the fish-market landing is a general form rather than a great city's alone, but it is a 2019 definition in the present tense: it says such landings are found across Japan now, gives no date for when they spread, and names no premodern provincial one, so for the setting it is a hint that the form was widespread, not evidence of it. It is a one-line encyclopedia definition that names no provincial example and describes no layout; the fuller Edo and Osaka accounts are the ones the form is drawn from.

Used for: that landings with a fish market are found all over the country today, as a hint that the form was general (cities/river-cities)
