jfs-inagawa-daiba-kunugi

The Japanese Forest Society, Forestry Heritage No. 10, 猪名川上流域の里山（台場クヌギ林） ("The satoyama of the upper Ina River (the daiba kunugi woods)", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://www.forestry.jp/forestryheritage/2013-3082/)

What it is: The Japanese Forest Society's register entry, made in 2013, for the kunugi coppice woods on the Hyogo-Osaka border that supplied Ikeda charcoal, giving their date of origin, their history and how they are cut today.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates a short-rotation coppice to before modernity: the area produced Ikeda and Hitokura charcoal back to at least the Muromachi period, Edo-period documents record the charcoal's use for the tea ceremony, and its date of origin is given as the Muromachi period on the strength of old documents and tradition. It is a short heritage register by a learned society, not a study; the cutting cycle it gives (eight to nine years) is today's practice, and it describes one charcoal district famous for a luxury charcoal, which it itself calls rare, not the ordinary fuel wood near a town.

Used for: a short-rotation kunugi coppice dated to the Muromachi period (towns)
