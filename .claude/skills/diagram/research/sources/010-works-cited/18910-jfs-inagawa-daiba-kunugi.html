jfs-inagawa-daiba-kunugi

The Japanese Forest Society, Forestry Heritage No. 10, 猪名川上流域の里山（台場クヌギ林） ("The satoyama of the upper Ina River (the daiba kunugi woods)", title translated) (https://www.forestry.jp/forestryheritage/2013-3082/)

What it is: The Japanese Forest Society's register entry, made in 2013, for the kunugi woods of the upper Ina River on the Hyogo-Osaka border that supplied Ikeda charcoal, grown as daiba pollards, giving their date of origin, their history and how they are cut today.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates a short-rotation coppice to before modernity: the area produced Ikeda and Hitokura charcoal back to at least the Muromachi period, Edo-period documents record the charcoal's use for the tea ceremony, and its date of origin is given as the Muromachi period on the strength of old documents and tradition. It is a short heritage register by a learned society, not a study; the cutting cycle it gives (eight to nine years) is today's practice, and it describes one charcoal district whose charcoal it calls the finest, kept for the tea ceremony, not the ordinary fuel wood near a town. The rarity it names is today's: it calls the district one of very few places where the patchwork of rotational cutting can still be seen, and it calls that patchwork the original form of a satoyama cut for fuel, which is the society's general statement, not a record. Its kunugi are grown as daiba, a special pollard cut back to a raised head so that the trunk grows thick, not cut at the ground, and it gives no date for that form.

Used for: a short-rotation kunugi coppice dated to the Muromachi period (towns)
