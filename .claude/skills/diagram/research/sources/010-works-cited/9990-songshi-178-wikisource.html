宋史 (History of Song), 卷一七八 ("Chapter 178", title translated from the Chinese by this project), the treatise on food and money (食貨志), Chinese Wikisource transcription (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/宋史/卷178)

What it is: The Yuan-compiled official history of the Song; this chapter of its economic treatise records first the labor-service law and then the state's relief institutions, the poorhouses, relief clinics and public pauper cemeteries set up under Cai Jing from 1102 to 1104.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the official history's record of where the Song ordered its pauper cemeteries placed - in each county, and in any lesser town of a thousand households with an official - which is the one-to-a-seat rule this page draws. It is a Yuan compilation that abridges the Song edicts, some 240 years after them; the fuller text of the edicts is in the Song huiyao jigao. It is a Song Chinese institution with no counterpart in Japan, so the rule serves only places modeled on China. It records the order, not how fully it was carried out: the same chapter says it was carried out to excess and cut back in 1120. And it is read from a crowd-transcribed wiki copy with character-conversion errors.

Used for: the louzeyuan set up in 1104 and ordered for every county and every sizable town; the three to five qing of land ordered for each county of the capital district (religion and death)
