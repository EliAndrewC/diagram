zhenhai-wall-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 镇海城墙 ("the Zhenhai city wall", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/%E9%95%87%E6%B5%B7%E5%9F%8E%E5%A2%99)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the wall of Zhenhai, a county town at the mouth of the Yong river in Zhejiang: a wooden stockade in 1368, a stone wall from 1374, enlarged after a garrison was set there in 1387, with its height, its thickness at base and top, its gates, their barbicans and drawbridges, and its moat side by side, quoting for the figures the Republican-era county gazetteer (民国镇海县志), which carries them from older gazetteers - the moat's from the Ming Jiajing-era prefectural gazetteer.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one of the few public pages that measures a county wall in section and its moat on each side, and says every gate had a barbican and a drawbridge. It is a tertiary summary of one coastal town that was also a garrison seat, so its wall and gates may be heavier than an inland county's; its metric figures are the article's own conversions of the gazetteer's zhang and chi. Its moat is uneven - about 16 m wide on the east, 14 m on the south, 42 m on the west - and joins rivers on the west and east, so the widest figure is not a typical moat. It has no moat on the north because that wall stood on the seawall itself, a form few towns shared.

Used for: a county wall's height and its thickness at base and top; a barbican and a drawbridge at every gate; the moat's width side by side and its absence on the sea side (defenses)
