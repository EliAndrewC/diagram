ushijima-2020-manchu

Ushijima, Akira; Yang, Dan; Nakazono, Mahito; and Mishima, Sachiko, "Spatial composition and premise arrangement of traditional Manchu village in Northeast China", Japan Architectural Review 3(3) (2020) 346-358 (https://doi.org/10.1002/2475-8876.12146)

What it is: A measured field survey of one Manchu village in Liaoning, by an architectural team that worked there between 2013 and 2017: sixteen days of survey and interviews, nineteen buildings and twenty houses drawn, and all 296 housing lots in the village classified by the street they front, where their gate stands, what they measure and how their outbuildings sit. It is the authorized English of a paper first published in Japanese by the Architectural Institute of Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is honest evidence that a nucleated farm village in East Asia lays deep walled lots in rows along an east-west street with subordinate lanes crossing it, and honest evidence of what such lots measure - about one to two, mostly 30 m or less wide by 50 m or less deep, commonly 300 to 500 square metres, with the working yard in front of the house rarely deeper than 20 m. What it cannot be used for is anything that depends on the village not having been rebuilt: its own central finding is that the plan was remade by a Chinese state resettlement in about 1968, which is when the ground behind the lots was opened and given streets. Everything it reports about the older form comes at second hand from two mid-twentieth-century works. The building culture is Manchu and Han northeastern, shaped by the kang and by a cold-climate demand for south-facing rooms, and the authors note the Manchu form had become hard to tell from the Han by 1933. It is not evidence about Japan and not directly evidence about the premodern period.

Used for: the hierarchy of ways in a nucleated village and the shape and size of a housing lot
