Park Mee Jeong, Kim Sang Bum, Jang Choul Soon, Shin Min Ji and Kim Eun Ja, 「A study on the construction of a GIS database of traditional village groves and on the analysis of their distribution characteristics」 (translated from the Korean by this project; original: 「전통마을 숲의 GIS-DB구축 및 분포 특성 분석에 관한 연구」), 농촌계획 (Journal of Korean Society of Rural Planning) 19(1) (2013) 149-164 (https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201311637857073.pdf) - Korean analogue

What it is: A peer-reviewed Korean study by Park Mee Jeong, Kim Sang Bum, Jang Choul Soon, Shin Min Ji and Kim Eun Ja in the Journal of the Korean Society of Rural Planning (2013), which assembles a mapped database of 462 traditional village groves and field-surveys 100 of them, reporting each grove's area, its distance from the village and the kind of place it stands in.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only publicly readable survey we found that actually measures the planted grove at a village's mouth, and its numbers are what our maps size that belt of trees by - most groves under a hectare, and the village-entrance grove, the sugumagi that blocks the water mouth, averaging around seven tenths of a hectare and accounting for 42 of the 100 surveyed. Two limits stated plainly: it is Korean rather than Japanese or Chinese, cited because no comparable public survey of Japanese village groves could be read, and Korea is offered as a fair analogue because it shares the region's geomantic siting tradition and its village scale; and the groves measured are the ones that survive today, so a present-day area is only an approximation of the original planting.

Used for: the 462-grove GIS census (national mean the paper's own national mean of 10,375 m² - which is the unweighted mean of its eight PROVINCIAL means and not the per-grove mean of the census, that being about 10,223 m²; 356 of the 462 under 10,000 m²) and the 100-grove field survey by location type: the village-entrance grove (동구숲, the sugumagi water-mouth blocker) mean 7,149 m², 42 of 100
