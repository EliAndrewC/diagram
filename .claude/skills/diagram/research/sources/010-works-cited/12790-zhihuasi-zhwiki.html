zh.wikipedia 智化寺 ("Zhihua Temple", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/智化寺)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Zhihua Temple inside the walls of Beijing, a Ming foundation, with the halls on its axis, its measured length, width and area, and its bell and drum towers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a walled-city temple that survives with its axis and its own bell tower, measured. It is a capital temple founded by a powerful eunuch as his family temple and named by the emperor, so larger and richer than a provincial one: its black glazed-tile roofs are the mark of a Ming imperial or imperially commissioned temple, which a provincial temple would not have. Only its middle range of three survives, and the length, width and area it gives are of that range today, not of the whole original compound. It is an encyclopedia article whose halls and measurements come from the temple's own visitor guide, and the buildings were restored in 1958 and 1986, with new side gates added.

Used for: what a Chinese city temple held, and its own bell tower (religion-and-death)
