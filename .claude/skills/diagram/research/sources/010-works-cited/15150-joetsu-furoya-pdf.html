joetsu-furoya-pdf

Joetsu City Public Records Center, exhibition sheet for "資料から探る江戸時代の風呂事情" ("Bathing in the Edo period, explored from the documents", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 2017 (in Japanese; https://www.city.joetsu.niigata.jp/uploaded/attachment/135605.pdf)

What it is: The printed explanation sheet of the Joetsu archive's exhibition on Edo-period bathing: the bathhouse guilds of Takada castle town (19 houses from 1769) and Naoetsu-imamachi (8), mixed bathing and the domain's orders against it, the closing hour (about 8 p.m., from a domain order of 1776 - the sheet says no record gives the opening hour) and the strong-wind closure, bath fees, and the home baths shared in turn in the countryside.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is written from named domain and family documents held in the Takada library, so its counts and the orders it quotes are primary evidence for one provincial castle town and its neighbor. Its limits are that it is one domain, in snowy Echigo; that the 19 and the 8 are guild caps - Takada's bathhouses fixed their shares in 1769 and Naoetsu-imamachi's later, and both numbers held to the end of the period - so they count licenses, not demand, and the sheet gives neither town's population to scale them by; that its reasons (firewood and water too hard for a townsman to get for a bath of his own, separate tubs costing more space, firewood and water) are the archivists' explanation, and its statement that provincial towns barred home baths as Edo did is their presumption, not a quoted order - while the strong-wind closure is a quoted order; that it gives no building size; and that it is an exhibition sheet, not a study.

Used for: the castle town's and Naoetsu-imamachi's bathhouse counts, home baths shared in the countryside, firewood and water as a bathhouse's costs, and the strong-wind closure (urban-features)
