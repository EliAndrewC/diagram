beitang-studies

Nature Communications 2023; Jiang-Huai pond irrigation (PMC6695888) (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-39454-w)

What it is: A peer-reviewed article in Nature Communications (June 2023) on reactivating small water bodies for irrigation and drainage in Chinese rice farming. It describes the beitang (陂塘) pond systems of the Jiang-Huai region as a pond-based irrigation paradigm with more than 2,500 years of history, and records that in the 1950s some 8.3 million such decentralized systems served 39 percent of China's irrigated area.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source for our assumption that a village in rice country waters itself from ponds it holds locally, rather than only from a canal running past it, which changes what a settlement map has to show. It is a modern paper whose real subject is present-day sustainability, and its one big number is a 1950s count rather than a premodern one, so we take the system's antiquity and its village scale from it and treat the share as an indication that the pond mode was ordinary rather than marginal.

Used for: Chinese beitang pond systems as the dominant village-scale irrigation mode in rice China
