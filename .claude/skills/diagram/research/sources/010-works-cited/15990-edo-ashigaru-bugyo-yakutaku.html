edo-ashigaru-bugyo-yakutaku

江戸雑記録 ("Edo miscellany", a blog on the officials of late-Edo Edo), 町奉行役宅 ("The town magistrate's official residence", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 2018-03-22 (http://edo.ashigaru.net/?eid=23)

What it is: A blog post walking through the plan of the Edo North Town Magistracy as drawn in 1841: the office half and the residence half, the rooms of each, the row housing of the magistrate's own retainers, and the gates.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes how a magistracy's one compound was divided between court and office on one side and the magistrate's household on the other, which is what our Imperial Magistrate's compound holds. It is an enthusiast's reading of a period plan that it reproduces but does not cite by archive, and the magistracy was Edo's. The office-east, residence-west split is that one compound's layout, set by where its gates and entrances stood (the post says only that the South Magistracy was built almost the same). It supports putting office and household in one compound, divided into two halves, but not a rule about which compass side each half takes.

Used for: the magistracy as office and residence in one compound, the office east and the residence west, with a wing of row housing for the magistrate's own retainers (cities/capitals)
