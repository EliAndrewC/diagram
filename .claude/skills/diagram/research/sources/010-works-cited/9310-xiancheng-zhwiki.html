xiancheng-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 县城 (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/县城)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the xiancheng, the town that was a county's seat, with a short history, dynasty by dynasty, of how often county seats were walled; an encyclopedia article resting on modern Chinese studies of city walls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says how usual a wall was at a county seat in each age: walls ordered under the Han, most seats reusing old walls or having none from the Sui to the middle Tang, walling discouraged away from the borders under the Song, and walls general again under the Ming and Qing. It is a tertiary summary, its late-Ming figure is itself an estimate, and it counts walls rather than describing what stood inside them.

Used for: that a Chinese county seat of the later dynasties was usually walled, behind where China stands on the walled town's side of the fire-tower line (cities/fabric)
