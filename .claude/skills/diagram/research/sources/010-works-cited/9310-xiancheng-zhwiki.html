xiancheng-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 县城 ("county seat", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/县城)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the xiancheng, the town that was a county's seat, with a short history, dynasty by dynasty, of how often county seats were walled (the page is titled 县治, the seat of county government). An encyclopedia article whose wall history rests almost entirely on one paper, Lu Xiqi and Ma Jian, "A city within the walls? Rethinking the form of the ancient Chinese seat-of-government city" (2009), and which Wikipedia itself tags as needing more sources.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says how usual a wall was at a county seat in each age: walls ordered under the Han, most seats reusing old walls or having none from the Sui to the middle Tang, walling discouraged away from the borders under the Song, and walls general again under the Ming and Qing. It is a tertiary summary with one real source behind it, and its late-Ming figure is that paper's estimate, so this record uses it for the trend and not as a count. It counts walls rather than describing what stood inside them, and the paper it rests on argues that a walled seat was not a town contained by its wall: its inhabitants lived inside the wall or settled against it outside. So "walled" here does not mean the whole town stood within the wall.

Used for: that a Chinese county seat of the later dynasties was usually walled, behind where China stands on the walled town's side of the fire-tower line (cities/fabric)
