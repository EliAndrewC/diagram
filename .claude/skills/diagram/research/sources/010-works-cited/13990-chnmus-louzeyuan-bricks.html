Henan Museum, "4-33 漏泽园墓砖" ("4-33: grave bricks from a louzeyuan", title translated from the Chinese by this project), the museum's online collection notes (https://www.chnmus.net/sitesources/hnsbwy/page_pc/dzjp/mzyp/sdmz/list1.html)

What it is: A short article of about 2016 in the Henan Museum website's "Weekly Featured Item" series, written by Wang Xiaobin, an undergraduate archaeology student at Henan University. It covers fifteen inscribed Song grave bricks from a public pauper cemetery in Hua county, which are held by Henan University's museum. It quotes the Song huiyao's edicts and summarizes the excavated cemeteries, with a reference list.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the edict's allotment of ground to each burial and the choice of high, barren land, and gives the archaeology: about a thousand excavated graves at nineteen sites, the largest cemetery 849 graves near Sanmenxia, shallow pits in neat rows. It is a student's secondary summary: its edict quotations come from the Song huiyao and its grave counts from the Sanmenxia excavation report (the Northern Song Shaanzhou louzeyuan report of 1999), which is the closer source for those figures. It quotes the edicts rather than reproducing them whole, gives no area for any excavated cemetery, and describes Song China, which serves only the Chinese-modeled places of the setting.

Used for: the ground per burial and the excavated pauper cemeteries (religion and death)
