chnmus-louzeyuan-bricks

Henan Museum, "4-33 漏泽园墓砖" ("4-33: grave bricks from a louzeyuan", title translated from the Chinese by this project), the museum's online collection notes (https://www.chnmus.net/sitesources/hnsbwy/page_pc/dzjp/mzyp/sdmz/list1.html)

What it is: A provincial museum's note on inscribed grave bricks from a Song public pauper cemetery, quoting the Song huiyao's edicts and summarizing the excavated cemeteries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the edict's allotment of ground to each burial and the choice of high, barren land, and gives the archaeology: about a thousand excavated graves at nineteen sites, the largest cemetery 849 graves near Sanmenxia, shallow pits in neat rows. It quotes the edicts rather than reproducing them whole, gives no area for any excavated cemetery, and describes Song China, which serves only the Chinese-modeled places of the setting.

Used for: the ground per burial and the excavated pauper cemeteries (religion and death)
