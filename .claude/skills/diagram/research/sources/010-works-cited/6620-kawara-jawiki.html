kawara-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 瓦 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/瓦)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on kawara (瓦), fired roof tile, covering its manufacture, its types and the course of its spread through Japanese building from the temples of antiquity to ordinary houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: This settles a decision every map makes hundreds of times - what an ordinary townsman's roof looked like - by stating that commoners' houses were board-roofed as a general rule throughout the Edo period and that tile only became widespread at its very end, which is why our roofs are boards and thatch and our tile is reserved for temples, gates and the wealthy. It is a tertiary summary, so we take the direction of the trend rather than any proportion from it, and "the end of the Edo period" is later than the eras our setting draws on, which makes this a reason to draw less tile rather than a license to draw some.

Used for: 「Even once the Edo period had come, board roofing was the general rule for the houses of ordinary commoners, and it was at the end of the Edo period that tile roofing spread.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代になっても一般庶民の家屋は板葺きが一般的で瓦葺きが普及したのは江戸時代末期のこと」) - commoners' houses board-roofed; tile widespread only at the end of Edo
