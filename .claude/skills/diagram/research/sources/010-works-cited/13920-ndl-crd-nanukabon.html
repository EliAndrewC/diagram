Shiojiri City Library, answer to the reference question "Every year we clean the graves on 7 August - does it mean anything?" (in Japanese), Collaborative Reference Database, National Diet Library (https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000329772&page=ref_view)

What it is: A public library's reference answer, registered 2023, that gives, in one line each and mostly in paraphrase, what seven books say of the seventh day of Bon: dictionaries and popular books on Japan's annual observances, a folklore study, and the Zenkoku nenchu gyoji jiten and a Shiojiri ward history.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the statement that on nanuka-bon the graves AND the paths to the graves were cleaned, and a local record of the kin of one surname gathering to clean their ancestors' graves. It is a librarian's digest of printed books, one line each, and the books are modern works (1995-2021) on custom as still observed. The paths to the graves come from one of them alone, a 2021 popular book from a moral-education foundation (Ubukata, Nihon no shukujitsu to saiji no yurai); the three dictionaries of observances say only that graves are cleaned "and the like" on nanuka-bon. The one folklore study it cites (Takaya 1995) says the seventh-day grave visit is a recent start in parts of Shinshu, the region of the Shiojiri record, so that record is no evidence of an old practice. Its August dates are the modern calendar's, standing for the seventh day of the seventh lunar month. How far from the grave the cleaning reached it does not say.

Used for: the cleaning of graves and of the paths to them at the start of Bon (religion and death)
