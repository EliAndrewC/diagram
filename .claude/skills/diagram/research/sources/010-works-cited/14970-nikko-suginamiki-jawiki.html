ja.wikipedia 日光杉並木 ("the Nikko cedar avenue", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日光杉並木)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the cedar avenue lining three highways to Nikko for about 35 km, planted by one retainer of the Tokugawa over some twenty years from 1625, and the best known of the surviving avenues of pine and cedar the shogunate kept along its main highways; the article notes that pine, not cedar, was the tree those avenues were usually planted with.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that the shogunate established and kept tree avenues along the Five Highways and other main roads, which bears on what lined a trunk road near a town. The Nikko avenue itself is an exceptional memorial planting; the article does not say how continuous the ordinary highway avenues were, or how far from a town they ran. It is a tertiary source, and its one sentence on the shogunate's avenues cites a popular trivia paperback (ロム・インターナショナル編『道路地図 びっくり!博学知識』, 2005), so it carries only that such avenues existed, not their extent or when they were ordered; a dictionary entry on 並木 (Kotobank) that dates the order to 1604 would be the closer source. It describes Edo Japan only, standing in for the setting's Chinese side.

Used for: that avenues of pine and cedar lined Japan's main highways (ways)
