Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department (Hong Kong), "Fung Shui Woods in Hong Kong", Advisory Council on the Environment, Nature Conservation Subcommittee paper NCSC 9/06, December 2006 - with an Annex listing all 115 surveyed woods by species richness and GIS-measured area in m² (https://www.epd.gov.hk/epd/sites/default/files/epd/english/boards/advisory_council/files/ncsc-paper-06-09.pdf)

What it is: A December 2006 paper by Hong Kong's Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department to the Nature Conservation Subcommittee of the Advisory Council on the Environment, reporting a 2002 to 2004 survey of 115 fung shui woods - the native forest patches behind villages that villagers preserved, with fruit trees and useful plants at their edges - each wood's boundary drawn on a survey map and its area computed by GIS, listed in an annex.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one large measured set of village grove areas the record could read - the annex over which our median, mean and spread of grove sizes are computed - and so it is what sizes the groves on our maps. Its limits are that the 115 woods were chosen as the most representative sites and the paper says the survey was not exhaustive, so our figures are statistics over a selected sample that plausibly drops the smallest and most degraded woods; that the paper scopes the feature to southern China, so its standing in for the wider East Asian practice is our assumption rather than its claim, made because no comparable Japanese survey could be read; that it is a modern survey of the woods that survived; and that the boundaries were drawn by judgment where a wood merged into secondary forest, so each area carries a convention.

Used for: the one large measured area distribution of village fengshui groves - the record's arithmetic over the Annex: median 9,866 m², mean 11,026 m², 58 of 115 under 1 ha, 45 between 1 and 2 ha, 12 at 2 ha or more, range 606-53,170 m²
