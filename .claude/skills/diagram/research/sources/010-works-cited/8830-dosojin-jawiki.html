ja.wikipedia 道祖神 (dosojin, the deity of the way) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%81%93%E7%A5%96%E7%A5%9E)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the dosojin, the stone deity of roads and boundaries: where it is enshrined, in what form, and what it is there to keep out.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the rule our wayside shrines are placed by, and states it as an organizing idea rather than a list: the deity is enshrined at spatial and social boundaries - a village's entrance and exit, a mountain pass, a crossroads, the foot of a bridge - standing by the roadside as a stone monument or image to ward off epidemics and evil spirits coming from outside. That is the landing, the crossing and the gate our city shrines mark. Its limits are that it is a tertiary encyclopedia which carries a sourcing-deficiency notice over the very sections the placement sentence sits in; that it is a Japanese folk-religion form rather than a general one; and that it does not put such a shrine at a wellhead, which this record had also claimed. It is a different work from the walking-tour company's English page on the same deity, which the record also cites.

Used for: the wayside deity enshrined at a village's entrance and exit, a pass, a crossroads and the foot of a bridge
