ogura-kyoto-ezu-shokusei

Ogura Junichi, "The vegetation landscape near Kyoto from the late Muromachi to the late Edo period, studied chiefly from pictorial maps", Kyoto Prefecture Red Data Book 2015 (in Japanese; https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/kankyo/rdb/eco/rs/2015rs02.html)

What it is: An essay by a landscape historian of Kyoto Seika University, published in the prefecture's Red Data Book, reading paintings, illustrated guidebooks and pictorial maps from the 16th to the 19th century for the vegetation of the hills round Kyoto, with a section on how far such pictures can be trusted.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates what it describes: from a screen of about 1658-1661 it finds the hill woods mostly pine, with bamboo groves not rare right beside shrines and temples, and it warns that the pictorial maps of the period more often than not carry no legend for their colors. It is about the hills round one great city, and about woods near shrines and temples, not a village's own thicket.

Used for: bamboo groves beside shrines and temples in the 17th century, and the old maps' want of a legend (Vegetation and terrain)
