higashiyamato-nariki-lime

東大和の歴史 ("The history of Higashiyamato"), 江戸へ石灰を運ぶ道（最初の江戸街道・青梅街道） ("The road that carried lime to Edo: the first Edo road, the Ome road", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 2019 (in Japanese; https://higashiyamato.net/higashiyamatonorekishi/2272)

What it is: An article by Ajima Kiichi (安島喜一) on a local-history site (東大和の歴史, on the Higashiyamato community portal) on the road cut across the Musashino moor to carry lime to Edo, quoting in modern Japanese the shogunate's 1606 order for lime from the hill villages of Kami-Nariki and Kita-Osoki for the castle works, which the intendant passed to the lime-burning kiln-masters there, and a road survey of 1649-50 giving the distances stage by stage.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows where one great city's lime came from for its castle in 1606 - burned in kilns in the limestone hills and carried in by relays of horses along a road made for it. It records a single urgent order for the castle works, not the city's lime supply over the period, and says nothing either way about lime burned at the city's edge. It is a local enthusiasts' site, though it quotes its documents - in modern Japanese, without saying where they are held, and with slips in its own era dates (Keichō 16 given as 1620); the date of the order differs by a year from the Ome city page, and it concerns Edo alone. The 1649-50 stage distances run from Edo to Ome and on to the Kai border, not to the lime villages beyond Ome.

Used for: lime for Edo's castle fetched from the hill villages by relay horses, and the stage distances of the road (towns)
