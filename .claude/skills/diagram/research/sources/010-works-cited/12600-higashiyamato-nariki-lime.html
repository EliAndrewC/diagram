東大和の歴史 ("The history of Higashiyamato"), 江戸へ石灰を運ぶ道（最初の江戸街道・青梅街道） ("The road that carried lime to Edo: the first Edo road, the Ome road", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 2019 (in Japanese; https://higashiyamato.net/higashiyamatonorekishi/2272)

What it is: A local-history site's article on the road cut across the Musashino moor to carry lime to Edo, quoting the shogunate's 1606 order for lime from the hill villages of Kami-Nariki and Kita-Osoki for the castle works, and a road survey of 1649-50 giving the distances stage by stage.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows where a great city's lime came from - the limestone hills, carried in by relays of horses along a road made for it - rather than from kilns at its edge. It is a local enthusiasts' site, though it quotes its documents; the date of the order differs by a year from the Ome city page, and it concerns Edo alone.

Used for: lime for Edo's castle fetched from the hill villages by relay horses, and the stage distances of the road (towns)
