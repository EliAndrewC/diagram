ja.wikipedia 茶園 ("tea garden", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/茶園)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the tea garden, which describes, beside the planted tea field, the bund tea (keihan-cha): tea set in one row along the banks between fields.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the form and what it was for - a boundary mark, a windbreak, a check on soil washing off - and says a household sold what it did not drink. It is an encyclopedia article, marked a stub, whose sentence on bund tea rests on one footnote: a page on what appears to be the old website of the Miyoshi town history and folk museum (Saitama, on the Musashino upland), which the article also lists under its external links. It describes Japan only, not China, and the form it cites is one region's dry-field practice. It dates bund tea only as one of the old forms common before the change after the Second World War to cuttings and bred cultivars. So it shows the form in the modern era, with surplus shipped to wholesalers, not that it existed in the Edo period or earlier. It gives no count, spacing or row length. It carries the form and its purposes, not a date or a number.

Used for: tea planted in a row along the field bunds as a boundary and windbreak (vegetation)
