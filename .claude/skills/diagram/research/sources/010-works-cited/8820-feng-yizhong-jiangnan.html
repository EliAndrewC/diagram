冯贤亮, "Graves and charity graveyards: popular life and environmental protection in Ming-Qing Jiangnan" (title translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「坟茔义冢：明清江南的民众生活与环境保护」), 华东师范大学历史系, open PDF (https://www.sinoss.net/uploadfile/2010/1130/6012.pdf)

What it is: A scholarly paper by a historian at East China Normal University on graves and charity graveyards in Jiangnan under the Ming and Qing, quoting county gazetteers on individual endowments and tabulating the charity grounds of Zhenze county one by one.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the measured Chinese burial ground this record had been reasoning about without one: a Kangxi 9 endowment of 4 mu of field took 573 coffins, about 4.7 m2 and some 50 sq ft for a full-body grave - ground bought after Lake Tai burst its banks and the floating dead ran into the tens of thousands, so that figure is a mass burial's packing and a hard floor under the charity band, and individual charity graveyards are recorded from 3 fen to 10 mu apiece with county totals beside them. That turns the four-to-six-times multiple for Chinese full-body burial from arithmetic into a measurement. Its limits are that these are charity grounds for the unclaimed and the drowned, packed more tightly than a family grave sited by fengshui would be; that they are Jiangnan, where the paper itself says land pressure was acute; and that the mu is taken at the standard 666.7 m2, which the gazetteers do not state.

Used for: 4 mu holding 573 coffins, about 50 sq ft a full-body grave; charity graveyards ranging from 3 fen to 10 mu
