chinesepen-shuzhai

Independent Chinese PEN Center, old-site articles, "书斋——中国文人的精神家园" (the study: the spiritual home of the Chinese literati), posted 2008 (in Chinese; https://www.chinesepen.org/old-posts/?p=6959)

What it is: An unsigned essay for general readers, posted in 2008 by a site account on the old site of the Independent Chinese PEN Center (a writers' association, not an architectural body), describing the traditional Chinese scholar's study: its history from the Han to the Qing, how it was furnished (largely paraphrasing the Ming writer Gao Lian's essay on the study), and where in the house compound it stood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that over time the study separated from the living rooms into a room with its own function and layout, and that it generally stood in the secluded part of the house compound, adjoining the rear garden where the house had one. It does not say the study was a detached building; a detached garden study is our reading of it. It describes the traditional house of a literate household of some means in general, naming no dynasty or region for this placement. It is an unsigned general essay for readers, not a scholarly or dated architectural account, and it cites no sources for these statements. A closer period source says the same: Ji Cheng's garden treatise Yuanye (1631), whose section on the study's site places it in a secluded spot of the garden, connected to it.

Used for: the Chinese study set apart from the living rooms, beside the rear garden (buildings)
