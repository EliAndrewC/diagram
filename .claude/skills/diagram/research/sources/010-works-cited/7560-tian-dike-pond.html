tian-dike-pond

Tian, M., "Seeing from Above: Observation of Contemporary Dike-Pond Landscape", Landscape Architecture Frontiers 7(4) (2019) 130-138 (https://doi.org/10.15302/J-LAF-1-050004)

What it is: A short bilingual essay in a Chinese landscape-architecture journal, looking at the Pearl River Delta’s dike-pond country from the air and arguing that its character is being lost. It is written in Chinese and English on facing text, the journal supplying the English and the author being one of its two translators.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source of the description of the delta as a mosaic of built ponds threaded by natural rivers. Two limits matter. It describes the landscape as it IS, and its argument is that the mosaic is going: from the 1960s a state improvement program squared the irregular ponds into rectangles, so the mosaic it praises is read off 1960s aerial imagery rather than surveyed in a premodern century. And it is an essay rather than a study - it measures nothing, counts nothing and cites no survey, so it can carry the FORM of the landscape and must not be asked for a number.

Used for: the Pearl delta read as a mosaic of constructed ponds among meandering natural rivers
