kotobank-bukeyashiki

Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha) and Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho / Nipponica (Shogakukan), s.v. 武家屋敷 ("samurai residence", title translated), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/武家屋敷)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of two signed encyclopedia entries, by Suzuki Mitsuru (Heibonsha) and Fujino Tamotsu (Nipponica), on the buke-yashiki, the samurai residence in Edo and in the provincial castle towns: how it was granted, how it was enclosed and who lived in it, rank by rank.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the provincial plot's enclosures - earthen wall, board fence or hedge - which is where this page's rank ladder takes its materials, though it attaches none of them to a rank; that a plot was granted by status and stipend, and changed with promotion; that a daimyo's Edo residence was ringed with row-house ranges for its retainers and servants while a smaller samurai house, with few live-in servants, had a wall instead; and that foot soldiers and the lowest retainers had no plot of their own but lived in shared row-houses, which the Nipponica places on the town's edge in many cases, not all, and whose size it says varied by domain. Without plans or counts; the Edo passages describe the shogun's capital, which the entry itself sets apart from the provincial castle town.

Used for: the enclosures of a samurai plot, plots granted by rank, where a household's servants lived, and the foot soldiers' row-houses (government)
