nishikori-tsunaba

Kotobank, Nishikori tsunaba site entry; Yaotsu town history pages (yaotsu-mall.com) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%8C%A6%E7%B9%94%E7%B6%B1%E5%A0%B4%E8%B7%A1-3061429)

What it is: A Kotobank reprint of the entry on the Nishikori tsunaba from the Heibonsha historical place-name gazetteer 日本歴史地名大系, a scholarly reference compiled from local records. It describes the rope catch across the Kiso river at Nishikori, where the logs floated down from the Kiso forests were stopped at the mouth of the gorge and made up into rafts, and says that when the practice began is not known.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells us that a full-river catch stood at the gorge mouth where the loose-log drive ended and the rafting began, which is what puts a boom and a timber yard at that point on our river maps. Its limits are that it is a gazetteer entry reprinted by an aggregator, read in extract, so it supports the site and its position and nothing more; that a yearly log count, the season, the size of the timber magistracy and an origin in the 1340s are on no page we could read, and the entry itself says the origin is unknown, so they stand as guesses; and that one exceptional domain-run river stands in for the ordinary timber trade.

Used for: the full-river rope catch at the gorge mouth (the entry says when it began is unknown; a yearly log count, the season, a 138-official magistracy and a 1340s origin are on no readable page and stand as guesses)
