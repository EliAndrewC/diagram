kashi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 河岸 and 雁木 (港湾) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/河岸)

What it is: Two Japanese Wikipedia articles: 河岸 (kashi), on the river landing that grew into a warehouse and merchant district from the medieval period to its Edo peak, and 雁木 (gangi), a short stub on the stair-shaped landing structure that lets people and cargo go up and down as the water level changes, in whatever material it was built.

Why it applies, and its limits: They give the form of a working river landing - the stepped bank where cargo is worked, the warehouses and merchants behind it - which is what our river towns' waterfronts are drawn from. Their limits are that both are tertiary and below the usual encyclopedia bar - one carries a notice that its sources are insufficient, the other is a stub - so neither has references of its own to carry a claim; and that the form is taken from them and no date or dimension.

Used for: the kashi as a river port and landing that grew into a warehouse and merchant district; gangi as the stairs at a landing used to load and unload cargo
