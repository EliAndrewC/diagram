kotobank-umabune

Kotobank, 馬槽 ("umabune, the horse trough", title translated), quoting Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen and the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A6%AC%E6%A7%BD-441396)

What it is: Two modern Japanese dictionary entries (Shogakukan) for the old Japanese word for a horse trough, defined as the container for a horse's feed, a fodder tub. They come with the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's earliest attestations: the Wamyōshō, a glossary of about 934, for the word; the Iroha-ji word-list of 1559 for its variant form mabune; and the Warambe-gusa of 1660 for its second sense, a large tub shaped like a fodder tub.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the trough in a Japanese stable to the tenth century, which is the premodern attestation a map's trough needs. It is a dictionary: it defines a word and dates its first use, and it gives no size, no material, no place in the yard, and no sign that the trough held water rather than feed.

Used for: that the horse trough of premodern Japan was a fodder trough (urban-features)
