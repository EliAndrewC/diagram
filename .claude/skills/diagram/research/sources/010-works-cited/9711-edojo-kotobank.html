edojo-kotobank

Kotobank, 江戸城 (Edo-jo) - Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Murai Masuo; Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), Owada Tetsuo (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B1%9F%E6%88%B8%E5%9F%8E-36923)

What it is: The Kotobank page for Edo castle, carrying the Heibonsha world encyclopedia entry by the historian Murai Masuo and the Nipponica entry by the historian Owada Tetsuo, both describing the palace in the main bailey and its division by use into the omote, the naka-oku and the Ooku (the great oku, the shogun's women's quarters).

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the order of the three parts of the shogun's palace, and where the family's quarters stood against the lord's own rooms, which is the room order our residence plans take. Its limits are that it describes the shogun's palace, the largest in the country and not a provincial posting: the three-part division, the Ooku walled off and reached by the one Osuzu corridor, and the rule that no man but the shogun entered it are the shogun's arrangements, while the page's only word on lesser houses (the older Heibonsha Ooku entry quoted beneath) says daimyo and hatamoto houses divided a front, the omote, from a back, the oku - two parts, not three. It is also a tertiary summary, though both entries are by historians and the Nipponica entry names its sources (the Tokyo Shishiko and Naito Akira's Edo to Edo-jo).

Used for: the honmaru palace divided by use into the omote, the naka-oku and the oku; the oku on the north side of the naka-oku, walled off and joined to it only by the Osuzu corridor (buildings)
