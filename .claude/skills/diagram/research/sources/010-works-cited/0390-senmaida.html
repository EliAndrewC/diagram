senmaida

Shiroyone Senmaida (Wajima, Ishikawa) - the Japanese government's Highlighting Japan feature (gov-online.go.jp, June 2025), the Wajima city cultural-property listing (wajima.hiddenheritage.jp), and JNTO's site page (japan.travel); (https://www.gov-online.go.jp/hlj/en/june_2025/june_2025-01.html)

What it is: The website of the Shiroyone Senmaida Scenic Preservation Council, the body that keeps the terraced paddies at Wajima on the Noto peninsula, whose office sits in the city's tourism division. Its "about" page describes the terrace as it is worked today, by hand, because machines cannot enter: 1,004 basins on about four hectares, an average of about 18 square meters each, the smallest about 50 centimeters square.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the small end of a really worked paddy - basins of a few square meters, the smallest a quarter of one - which is what tells us that no absolute minimum paddy area exists and lets our steepest fields be drawn tiny. Its limits are that it is a preservation body's own promotional page rather than a survey; that it is an extreme case, basins made tiny by a steep slope, so it is a floor and never a typical size; and that its counts describe the terrace's present state, not a documented historical layout - and the page gives the average and the minimum only, so how many basins run about a square meter is our reading, not its figure.

Used for: the SMALL end of a real worked paddy - 1,004 basins on ~4 ha, average ~18 m2, the smallest about half a meter square, and the straw-raincoat anecdote (the readable page is the preservation council's own site, wajima-senmaida.jp; it gives the average and the smallest only).
