ja.wikipedia 日本橋 (東京都中央区の橋) ("Nihonbashi, the bridge in Chuo ward, Tokyo", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/日本橋_(東京都中央区の橋))

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Nihonbashi, the bridge in Edo from which the Five Highways were measured, with its history from the first timber bridge of 1603 to the present stone arch, quoting Miura Jōshin's Kenmonshū, written in the 1630s-40s and so probably describing the bridge as rebuilt in 1618 rather than the first, for the timber bridge's length and width (37 ken 4 shaku 5 sun by 4 ken 2 shaku 5 sun; the conversions to about 69 m by 8 m are the article's).

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the dimensions of the most important timber bridge of the shogun's capital, which bounds the width of a town's chief bridge. It does not bound length, which follows the river and not the rank of the place: Edo's Ryōgoku bridge was 94 ken (about 170 m), and the Tōkaidō's Yahagi bridge at the castle town of Okazaki was 208 ken (about 380 m). It is the exceptional bridge of the largest city, not a town's. The article is a tertiary encyclopedia summary; the figure rests on the one period account it quotes, which is what carries the number.

Used for: the length and width of Edo's chief timber bridge, the width as an upper bound (ways)
