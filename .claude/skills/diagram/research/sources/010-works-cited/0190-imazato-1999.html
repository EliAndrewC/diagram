imazato-1999

Satoshi Imazato, "A classification system of village space and its integrated examination: the case of Hagikura, Shimosuwa, Nagano", Japanese Journal of Human Geography 51(5): 433-452 (1999) (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjhg1948/51/5/51_5_433/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed settlement-geography study of one Japanese hamlet, Hagikura in Shimosuwa, Nagano - 124 households in 1998 - which classifies the village's space from its inhabitants' own terminology and land use, and maps the result: the house lots, the back mountain, the mountain across the river, the through-lanes and the three nested village boundaries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a hamlet read as its own inhabitants divide it, which is what makes its folk names evidence rather than description - the far bank being named "the facing mountain" is the villagers' own account of where their settlement stops. Its limits are large and must travel with it: it is ONE settlement, a loose-cluster village on a river TERRACE in a mountain valley, where the terrace itself constrains where houses can stand; the paper states no general rule about banks; and its own footnote records that the settlement's original site may have been along the river rather than on the terrace, which the pictorial map cannot settle - and the author finds that riverbank theory somewhat doubtful. It is 1998 fieldwork on a place with a long history, not a premodern survey - and the 124 households are a modern figure: the paper's own table counts 12 households in 1708, 34 in 1872 and 65 as late as 1965, the rise to 124 coming from housing estates since the 1970s, so the village at the scale these maps draw is the smaller, earlier one.

Used for: a hamlet standing on one bank of its river, with the far bank named as the other side
