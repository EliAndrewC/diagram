woods-westbrook-2006

Woods, Westbrook et al., "Hydrologic interactions between an alluvial fan and a slope wetland in the central Rocky Mountains, USA", Wetlands 2006 (https://www.cheriewestbrook.ca/publications/16523-hydrologic-interactions-between-an-alluvial-fan-and-a-slope-wetland-in-the-central-rocky-mountains-usa) - "Continuing ground-water discharge into the upper part of the wetland limited the water level declines"; stream seepage "is the primary source of ground-water recharge for the alluvial fan"

What it is: A peer-reviewed hydrology paper by Woods, Westbrook and colleagues, "Hydrologic interactions between an alluvial fan and a slope wetland in the central Rocky Mountains, USA", published in the journal Wetlands in 2006, which traces where the water in a wetland at the foot of an alluvial fan actually comes from.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the mechanism behind a marsh drawn at the edge of a fan - groundwater discharging out of the fan keeps the wetland wet, and seepage from the stream is what recharges that groundwater - so the chain from stream to fan to marsh holds together on the map instead of each piece being placed by eye. Its limits are place and era: it measures one site in the Rocky Mountains in the 2000s, not an East Asian one, so we cite it for the physics, which does not change by country, and never for a measurement of a particular place. Only the abstract and the summary sections are on a public page, the full paper being behind a subscription.

Used for: slope wetlands at a fan's perimeter fed by the fan's groundwater - which the stream recharges; the chain is kept whole.
