班固 漢書 卷二十四上 食貨志 ("Book of Han, Scroll 24A, the Treatise on Food and Money", title translated from the Chinese by this project), Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%BC%A2%E6%9B%B8/%E5%8D%B7024%E4%B8%8A)

What it is: The first half of the Book of Han's treatise on the economy, compiled by Ban Gu in the first century, in a Wikisource transcription; its passage on Zhao Guo's "alternating field" (代田) describes grain sown in furrows a chi wide and deep, three to a mu, with the ridges hoed down onto the roots as the crop grows and furrow and ridge changing places each year.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text from well before the setting's period describing dry grain sown in lines in furrows between ridges, which is the texture the maps draw on a dry plot. It describes a method one official promoted on the North China plain in the first century BCE, calling its dimensions an ancient method; it does not say how widely it was followed, and it says nothing of Japan.

Used for: grain sown in rows in furrows between ridges, in the Han (fields)
