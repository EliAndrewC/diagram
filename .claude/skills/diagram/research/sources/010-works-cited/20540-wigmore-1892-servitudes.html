wigmore-1892-servitudes

John Henry Wigmore (ed.), Materials for the Study of Private Law in Old Japan, Part V, "Property: Civil Customs", chapter II, section 3, "Sundry Servitudes" (pp. 39-43), Asiatic Society of Japan, Tokyo, 1892 (https://archive.org/details/materialsforstu00japagoog)

What it is: An English edition of Japan's local civil customs, reported district by district under each province, published in Tokyo in 1892; its section on sundry servitudes records who might cross whose land to reach a highway, a well or a drain, and on what terms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says what a household did when its land had no way of its own to the road: passage over a neighbor's land, owed by custom, sometimes for a rent. Its limits: this part of the volume does not date the reports; each entry is one district's custom, several marked as holding in towns; most of its reports of passage speak of plots of land, and only one names a house; and the scan's text is machine-read, so it is quoted only where its words stand clean.

Used for: passage over a neighbor's land for land with no way of its own to the road (Ways)
