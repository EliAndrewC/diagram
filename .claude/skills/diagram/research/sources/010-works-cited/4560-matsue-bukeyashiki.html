matsue-bukeyashiki

Matsue Buke Yashiki (Shiomi Nawate), museum building documentation (https://www.matsue-bukeyashiki.jp/about/equipment.html)

What it is: The visitor page on the building, published by the operator of the Matsue Buke Yashiki (the San-in Chuo Shimpo newspaper company). The house is a Matsue City designated cultural property: a residence of the Matsue domain's retainers on the Shiomi Nawate street, rebuilt after the fire of 1733 and, by the site's overview page, lived in by retainers of about 500 to 1,000 koku in turn. The page gives a plan of the house, short captions on the master's living room, the zashiki and the nagayamon on the street front, and a list naming the other rooms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a surviving building described by the people who keep it, which is why we use it for the nagayamon standing on the street and doubling as the quarters of the gatekeeper and the household servants, and for a room program that includes the servants' service rooms - both things our residence plans have to place. It is one house in one castle town, presented for visitors rather than as a study. It is also not the house as first built: the main house was altered and extended several times after it was rebuilt in 1733, and the 2016-2019 conservation work restored it to a plan drawn in the Meiji period. So the room program shown is the house's late state, not a plan dated to the Edo period. The page names the service rooms (the miso room, the kitchen hearth, the bath) without saying who worked in them; that servants did is our reading. It tells us what one middle-rank residence in Matsue came to be, not what a typical residence anywhere was.

Used for: a middle-rank residence with its nagayamon on the street; room program including servants' service rooms; the master's living room set apart from the zashiki where he received guests, with a family room, the wife's living room, a tea room and a butsuma among the rest (buildings)
