guangdong-xinyu-27

Qu Dajun (屈大均), Guangdong xinyu (廣東新語, New Sayings of Guangdong), chapter 27, "Grass talk" (草語), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E5%BB%A3%E6%9D%B1%E6%96%B0%E8%AA%9E/%E5%8D%B727)

What it is: The chapter on grasses and herbaceous plants of a late-seventeenth-century description of Guangdong, with entries on the banana and on sugar cane: where they were grown, and how banana and white cane were rotated on the same ground.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the primary account nearest the dike-pond hamlet's period of where banana and cane grew in the delta, and it places both off the ponds - bananas in fields at Xizhou in Zengcheng, in hill ground (the fibre bananas) and in the author's own garden; cane in cane fields and, for the pressing canes, over hills and mounds - and never on a pond dike. That is an absence in two entries of a scholar's miscellany of observation and lore, not a survey and not proof that no pond dike carried either; the same book's own account of dike planting (chapter 22, "養魚種") names lychee, tea, mulberry, citrus and longan and neither banana nor cane, and is the more direct witness to that absence. Its rotation is described for one locality, Xizhou in Zengcheng on the delta's eastern edge, not for the delta as a whole.

Used for: that banana and cane were grown off the ponds in the late seventeenth century (in fields, hill ground and gardens), rotated on the same ground at Xizhou in Zengcheng, with nothing placing either on a pond dike (archetypes)
