ja.wikipedia 奈良井宿 ("Narai-juku", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A5%88%E8%89%AF%E4%BA%95%E5%AE%BF)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Narai, a post town of the Nakasendo in the Kiso valley and a preservation district, giving the extent of its townscape (about 1 km along the river and 200 m across) and its houses, inns and population in the 1843 survey.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives both a length and a breadth for a surviving road town beside its house count, the one pairing that lets a density per acre be worked out. It is an encyclopedia article on one mountain station, a long thin town hemmed in by its valley, and its extent is the preserved townscape of today rather than a surveyed Edo boundary. The article takes that length and breadth from the Nagano prefectural tourism site, round figures and not a survey. It gives the 1843 counts from the shogunate's Nakasendo station survey (Nakasendo Shukuson Daigaichō) without a footnote. So a density worked from them sets an 1843 house count against an approximate present-day extent, whose 200 m breadth likely takes in hillside and temple ground beyond the house lots. It is a gross figure, not a density of the built street.

Used for: Narai's length, breadth and 409 houses (towns); the Kiso valley's traditional stone-weighted board roof (towns)
