宿白 (Su Bai), "A preliminary investigation of ancient city sites within modern cities" (title translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「現代城市中古代城址的初步考查」), published by the Peking University Institute of Humanities and Social Sciences and reproduced with attribution by the Institute of Archaeology and Cultural Heritage, Lanzhou University, 2024-04-22 (https://whyc.lzu.edu.cn/index.php/portal/article/index.html?cid=22&id=259)

What it is: Su Bai's own method for reading a Chinese city's older plan out of its modern street grid, illustrated across north China: the square city with one gate to a side and a great cross-street with a lesser cross in each quarter, the yamen and the market laid out in the middle of it, what a Ming prince's city had to demolish to stand on that ground, and the fixed corners that ritual buildings came to take. Su Bai (1922-2018) founded historical-period archaeology at Peking University.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is scholarship by the field's founder on exactly the question our government quarter answers, where a planned seat's compound stands, and it settles it with named cities rather than by assertion. Its limits are that it is a survey method illustrated from examples rather than a measured census; that the cities are mostly northern and mostly larger than a provincial seat; and that it supports the compound at the crossing without supporting the bureaus lining the avenues around it, which our record now says in so many words.
