ja.wikipedia 仲見世通り ("Nakamise street", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BB%B2%E8%A6%8B%E4%B8%96%E9%80%9A%E3%82%8A)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the shopping street on the approach to Sensoji in Asakusa, drawing on the street's own shopkeepers' association and two local guides.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the street's Edo-period origin - a right to trade on the approach granted to local residents who owed the temple the sweeping of its precinct - and its two Edo-period halves, twenty water teahouses nearer the inner gate and shops selling toys, sweets and souvenirs nearer the outer one. Its count of about 90 shops on about 250 m is the present street, rebuilt in 1885 and 1925 and restored after its interiors burned in the Second World War, not an Edo count, and Sensoji was the greatest pilgrimage temple of the shogun's capital. It is an encyclopedia article, and its Edo-period account comes from the shopkeepers' association's own page and a tourist site, not from period records. It gives the founding only as sometime between Genroku and Kyoho, or by one account about 1685. So it is good for the shape of the street, not for exact dates.

Used for: the Edo-period shops on a great temple's approach (religion and death)
