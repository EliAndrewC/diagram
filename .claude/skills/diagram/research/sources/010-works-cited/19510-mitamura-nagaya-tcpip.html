江戸時代の大名生活: 百間長屋と盲長屋 ("daimyo life in the Edo period: the hundred-ken rowhouse and the blind rowhouse", title translated from the Japanese by this project), a personal site quoting 三田村鳶魚全集 第2巻, お大名の話, 2015 (in Japanese; https://www.tcp-ip.or.jp/~ask/history/history14/nagaya/index.html)

What it is: A personal web page on the history of townscapes that transcribes a chapter of Mitamura Engyo, a historian of Edo life, on the rowhouses of the lords' residences, with the page owner's own captions and notes.

Why it applies, and its limits: Mitamura gives the warrior's standard rowhouse unit, nine shaku by two ken, its earth and plank floors, its origin in the soldiers' lodging huts of the Warring States castles, and a war chronicle's servants' room. It is a modern writer's summary of Edo lore, transcribed on a private site whose headings may be the owner's; it speaks of the lords' Edo residences, and gives the unit's size but not how many lived in one.

Used for: the warrior's rowhouse unit, its floors, and the servants' room at Okuwa castle (buildings)
