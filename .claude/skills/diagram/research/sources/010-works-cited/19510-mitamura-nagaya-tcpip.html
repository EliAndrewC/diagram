江戸時代の大名生活: 百間長屋と盲長屋 ("daimyo life in the Edo period: the hundred-ken rowhouse and the blind rowhouse", title translated), a personal site quoting 三田村鳶魚全集 第2巻, お大名の話, 2015 (in Japanese; https://www.tcp-ip.or.jp/~ask/history/history14/nagaya/index.html)

What it is: A personal web page on the history of townscapes that transcribes a chapter of Mitamura Engyo (1870-1952), a historian of Edo life writing in the early twentieth century, on the rowhouses of the lords' residences, with the page owner's own captions and notes.

Why it applies, and its limits: Mitamura gives the standard rowhouse unit of a lord's lower retainers, nine shaku by two ken, its earth and plank floors, its origin in the soldiers' lodging huts of the Warring States castles, and a war chronicle's servants' room. It is a modern writer's summary of Edo lore, transcribed on a private site whose headings may be the owner's; it speaks of the lords' Edo residences, and gives the unit's size but not how many lived in one. The unit is the middle rowhouse of men below full samurai rank, down to foot soldiers; he says a samurai proper needed at least five rooms. Its descent from the Warring States lodging huts is Mitamura's own reading, and the servants' room comes second-hand from the Dobora Gunki, a war tale compiled about 1700 on the fighting of 1564-65.

Used for: the lower retainers' rowhouse unit, its floors, and the servants' room at Okuwa castle (buildings)
