thepaper-surviving-walls

通城旅游 (Tongcheng Tourism), 盘点：细数中国的古城墙，最后一座堪称奇迹 ("A roundup of China's old city walls - the last one is a marvel", title translated), 2019, on The Paper's account platform (https://m.thepaper.cn/baijiahao_3860060)

What it is: A county tourism office's repost, on the news site The Paper's platform for outside accounts, of an unsigned list of 36 surviving Chinese walled sites - mostly city walls, but also the Shenyang and Beijing palaces, the Great Wall, the Penglai water fort and a modern memorial park at Kashgar - giving for most some of length, height, gates, towers and moat, and for a few no measures at all.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that measures the moats of several cities side by side - Qufu, Kaifeng, Xi'an, Jingzhou, Xiangyang and Guide - and it counts the enemy platforms of Yuci and Guide. It is a compilation marked as taken from the internet with no author and no sources, some of its figures disagree with other pages (Xi'an's moat, 20 by 10 m against 18 by 6 m elsewhere; Pingyao's towers every 60 m against 50 m). Two of its six moats are outliers, not the ordinary city moat: the page itself calls Xiangyang's 130-250 m moat rare in the whole country, and Guide's runs from 25 to 500 m wide, more lake than ditch; they bound the broad end of the range only. Kaifeng's 16.66 by 3.33 m and Qufu's 3.3 m read as round old measures (5 zhang by 1 zhang, and 1 zhang) converted at the modern one-third-meter chi, so a few percent larger than the Ming-Qing measure they came from.

Used for: the widths and depths of six cities' moats, and the enemy-platform counts of Yuci and Guide (defenses)
