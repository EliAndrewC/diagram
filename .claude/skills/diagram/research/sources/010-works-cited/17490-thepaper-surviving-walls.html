thepaper-surviving-walls

通城旅游 (Tongcheng Tourism), 盘点：细数中国的古城墙，最后一座堪称奇迹 ("A roundup of China's old city walls - the last one is a marvel", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 2019, on The Paper's account platform (https://m.thepaper.cn/baijiahao_3860060)

What it is: A county tourism office's repost, on the news site The Paper's platform for outside accounts, of a survey of 36 surviving Chinese city walls, each with its length, height, gates, towers and moat as the compiler found them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that measures the moats of several cities side by side - Qufu, Kaifeng, Xi'an, Jingzhou, Xiangyang and Guide - and it counts the enemy platforms of Yuci and Guide. It is a compilation marked as taken from the internet with no author and no sources, some of its figures disagree with other pages (Xi'an's moat, 20 by 10 m against 18 by 6 m elsewhere; Pingyao's towers every 60 m against 50 m), and it describes the walls as they survive or were restored.

Used for: the widths and depths of six cities' moats, and the enemy-platform counts of Yuci and Guide (defenses)
