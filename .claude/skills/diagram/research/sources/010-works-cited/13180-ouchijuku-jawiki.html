ja.wikipedia 大内宿 ("Ouchi-juku", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A7%E5%86%85%E5%AE%BF)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Ouchi, a post station of the Aizu Nishi Kaido in Fukushima and a preservation district, describing it as a "half farming, half station" stop whose thatched houses still line the old road amid the fields.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a surviving instance of the quiet road town the record sets against the busy highway's, thatched and farmhouse-like. It is one mountain station in the snowy north-east, described by an encyclopedia article that is itself a stub; its "half farming" claim rests on a paper it cites (Nakao Chiaki, 2010, Nara Women's University, hdl:10935/1629), and the page hedges it as what the station is thought to have been. That character was not how the station began: it opened about 1643 with a honjin and waki-honjin on the Aizu lord's procession route, and turned half-farming after the processions were stopped in 1680 and the road was cut by the 1683 Nikko earthquake. What stands today is partly a Meiji change and partly a modern restoration: the water channel ran down the middle of the road until 1886, when it was split to both sides and the road widened; roofs went to tin from the 1950s-70s and were re-thatched under preservation after 1981; the asphalt was taken up to restore a dirt road; and the honjin is a 1984 rebuilding. It does not date the houses, or say how common such a town was.

Used for: a half-farming station's thatched houses along its road (towns)
