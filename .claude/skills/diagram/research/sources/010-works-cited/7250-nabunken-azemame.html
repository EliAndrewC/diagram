nabunken-azemame

Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties, Asuka Historical Museum, "畦豆" (2023-06) (https://www.nabunken.go.jp/asuka/info/2023/06/post-444.html)

What it is: A short explanatory article of June 2023 from the Asuka Historical Museum of the Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties on azemame (畦豆), the soybeans sown along the bund of a paddy field.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, from a national research institute's museum, the practice of sowing beans along the paddy bund after transplanting and harvesting them with the rice - once done across Japan, now rare - which is what puts bund beans on our paddies. Its limits are that it is a short piece for a general audience with no proportion of bunds sown and no household count; that its "once across Japan" carries no date, so it fixes the practice in no particular century; and that the post itself says these bund plants are seen especially in the terraced hill country, where the bund is a larger share of the farmland than on the flat - so our flat-valley paddies are the case the source calls the less bund-intensive one.

Used for: azemame - soybeans sown on the paddy bund after transplanting and harvested with the rice, grown in a row along the bund; once nationwide, now rare
