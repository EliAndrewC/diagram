kotobank-gogura

Kotobank, 郷蔵 ("Gogura, the village storehouse", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%83%B7%E8%94%B5-2035847)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the gogura, carrying the Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten (with a passage on its siting quoted from the rural officials' manual Jikata hanreiroku of 1794), the Yamakawa Nihonshi shojiten and the Obunsha Nihonshi jiten: what the storehouse held, who kept it, that not every village had one, and the shogunate's order of 1789.

Why it applies, and its limits: Three standard reference dictionaries agree on what the storehouse was and who ran it, and one quotes a period manual on where it stood. Their limits are that they are short tertiary entries; that the siting passage is one manual's rule; and that they give no size and no count of how many villages shared one.

Used for: the village storehouse's purpose, keepers and siting, and that some villages shared one or used the headman's storehouse (urban-features)
