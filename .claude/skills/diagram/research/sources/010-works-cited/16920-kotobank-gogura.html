kotobank-gogura

Kotobank, 郷蔵 ("Gogura, the village storehouse", title translated) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%83%B7%E8%94%B5-2035847)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the gogura, carrying the Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten (with a passage on its siting quoted from the rural officials' manual Jikata hanreiroku of 1794), the Yamakawa Nihonshi shojiten and the Obunsha Nihonshi jiten: what the storehouse held, who kept it, that not every village had one, and the shogunate's order of 1789.

Why it applies, and its limits: Three standard reference dictionaries agree on what the storehouse was and who ran it, and one quotes a period manual on where it stood. Their limits are that they are short tertiary entries; that the siting passage is one manual's rule; that they give no size and no count of how many villages shared one; and that what they describe changed within the Edo period and says nothing of earlier times. The storehouse first held tax rice for a short time before it was shipped. It became a famine-reserve granary only after the Tenmei famine of the 1780s. Until the shogunate's 1789 order that villages build their own, the village officials' private storehouses were often used instead.

Used for: the village storehouse's purpose, keepers and siting, and that some villages shared one or used the headman's storehouse (urban-features)
