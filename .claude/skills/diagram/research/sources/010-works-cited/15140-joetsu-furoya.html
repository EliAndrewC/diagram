joetsu-furoya

Joetsu City Public Records Center, "資料から探る江戸時代の風呂事情" ("Bathing in the Edo period, explored from the documents", title translated from the Japanese by this project), 2017 (in Japanese; https://www.city.joetsu.niigata.jp/soshiki/koubunsho/tenji14.html)

What it is: The Joetsu city archive's page for a traveling exhibition of its documents on bathing in the Edo period: the bathhouses of Takada castle town and Naoetsu-imamachi, their counts, and the hot springs and cures of the countryside.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a municipal archive writing from documents in its own and the Takada library's keeping - the domain's compendium of records (記録便覧, whose bathhouse section runs 1743-1801) and the papers of town and village families - and it gives a provincial castle town's and a port town's bathhouse counts and says who used them. Its limits are that it is one domain on the Japan Sea coast, that it is a short exhibition notice rather than a study, and that it gives no population to set the counts against. Its statement that ordinary townspeople had no bath of their own is the archive's own summary, not tied to any document it shows, and its counts are dated only "late Edo" (the port town's eight match the eight-house bathhouse guild that petitioned in 1868).

Used for: that ordinary townspeople had no bath of their own and used the bathhouse (urban-features)
