ihns-bunongshu

Zhang Lüxiang, 补农书 (Bu nongshu, "Supplement to the Farm Manual", title translated), completed 1658 by its author's preface, full text on the agricultural-history site of the Institute for the History of Natural Sciences (in Chinese; http://agri-history.ihns.ac.cn/books/bns.htm)

What it is: A full transcription of the farm book of Zhang Lüxiang (1611-1674), a schoolteacher who also farmed in Tongxiang county, Zhejiang, with the farm manual of a Mr. Shen of Huzhou (his given name lost), written in the last years of the Ming, printed before it and a modern editor's notes after each part.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the household garden of a rice-and-silk farm of the Lower Yangzi in south China: fenced with a thorny hedge, beside the house, and a mu of it feeding ten people their vegetables all year; and it has farmers of the author's own county growing the sweet chrysanthemum, a tea flower, by the mu to sell. Its limits: it is one scholar's advice on how a farm should be kept, not a survey of how farms were - Zhang was a schoolteacher of reduced fortune who farmed ten-odd mu with his household, in the Jiaxing-Huzhou country, among the most heavily manured and commercial farmland in China, so its mu of garden feeding ten may be smaller than a garden elsewhere would need; it names the mu without giving its size; and the online text is a modern collation, on an 1897 print, of a book that went around in manuscript copies that differ, with no page references and characters it could not type written out as their component parts.

Used for: the fenced garden beside a south Chinese farmhouse, its thorny hedge, and one mu of garden to a household of ten (Homesteads); the sweet chrysanthemum grown for market in Tongxiang county in 1658 (Fields)
