chinanews-ancient-firefighting

China News Service, "Firefighting in the past: the Tang put fires out with the squirt, and the Qing had many popular firefighting organizations" (title translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「古代消防：唐代用溅筒灭火 清代民间消防组织多」), 2015-08-19 (https://www.chinanews.com.cn/m/cul/2015/08-19/7477344.shtml)

What it is: A news-agency cultural feature on firefighting in the Chinese past, which quotes the Ming Wanshu zaji for the ward-and-post organization of the county town of Wanping - a headman responsible for fire and theft, each post with a head and three to five firemen, the night watch with clappers and bells, and the patrol post where the gear was kept - and records the 1743 order of the governor of Hunan that every prefecture and county organize registered volunteer fire companies.

Why it applies, and its limits: It answers whether a place below the capital kept an organized fire watch, which our fire-tower rule turned on, and it does so with a named primary source for the Ming case and a dated edict for the Qing one. Its limits are that it is journalism rather than scholarship, that the Ming detail reaches us through the article's paraphrase of the Wanshu zaji rather than from the text itself, and that it establishes a WATCH and not a standing lookout tower.

Used for: the night watch and the patrol post of a Ming county town, and the registered volunteer fire companies ordered in 1743
