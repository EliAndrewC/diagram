kotobank-aidono

Kotobank, "相殿" (aidono), entries from Digital Daijisen, Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nipponica and MyPedia (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%9B%B8%E6%AE%BF-421671)

What it is: Kotobank's page gathering the entries of several published Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias on aidono, the enshrining of two or more kami in one shrine hall; the Nipponica entry is signed by a scholar of Shinto rites, Numabe Harutomo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the practice, cites its use in a shrine record of 804, and names the two-bay, two-door hall built for it, so it is sound for what the form was and what it was called. It is a set of short reference entries: it gives no count of how common each form was, no dates for the two-door hall, and nothing about a shrine inside a residence or an office.

Used for: that several kami could share one hall, the lesser ones ranked below the main kami, and that a two-bay hall with a door for each was built for it (religion-and-death).
