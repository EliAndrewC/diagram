ja.wikipedia 井戸 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/井戸) - the shaft's lining: 「If the ground is firm a bare dug shaft will do, but where it is liable to collapse a well lining (idogawa, 井戸側) becomes necessary.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「地盤が堅固であれば素掘りでもよいが、崩れやすい場合には井戸側が必要となる」)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 井戸 (ido, "well"), covering how wells in Japan are dug, lined and owned.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use it for the construction of the shaft that the well glyph in a yard stands for - firm ground will hold a bare dug shaft, while ground liable to collapse needs a lining (idogawa) - and for the related point that wells were often held jointly rather than by one household, because digging one was expensive. Its limits: a tertiary summary, taken only for plain description of the thing itself; it describes Japanese wells in general, modern ones included, and dates nothing it says, so it does not establish that the lined shaft or the shared well is the Edo-period form rather than a general Japanese one; it says nothing about where in a yard a well is placed, so that decision on our maps rests on other sources; and it is in Japanese, quoted here in our own translation.
