ja.wikipedia 舟橋 ("pontoon bridge", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%88%9F%E6%A9%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the pontoon bridge, with Japanese examples: the temporary bridges laid over the Kiso rivers only when the shogun or a Korean embassy crossed, and the permanent bridges kept at Toyama, Morioka and Fukui in place of a ferry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that on the Kiso rivers a bridge of boats was the exception - laid only for the shogun and the Korean embassies, from boats requisitioned from the villages around, while the rivers were ordinarily ferried - and that a few castle towns (Toyama, Morioka, Fukui) kept one standing in place of a ferry, taking it up in floods. It is a tertiary encyclopedia article with a banner saying its footnotes are insufficient: its boat counts and river widths carry no footnote, though a Toyama museum catalogue and books on the Korean embassies are among its listed references. It is about Japan; China has one paragraph, on the bridge's origin, so it cannot say how common the bridge was on Chinese rivers. Its lead says permanent pontoon bridges were many, so it supports calling the bridge rare only for the Kiso crossings.
