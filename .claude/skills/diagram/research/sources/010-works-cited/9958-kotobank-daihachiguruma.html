kotobank-daihachiguruma

kotobank, "大八車" (daihachiguruma, the Edo hand cart) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A4%A7%E5%85%AB%E8%BB%8A-558136)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting several Japanese reference works' entries on the daihachiguruma, the large two-wheeled hand cart of Edo: its origin after the fire of 1657, its size, where it was used, and the regulation of it.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its entries are short Japanese dictionary and encyclopedia articles - tertiary summaries, only two of them signed (Matsuzaki Kin'ichi, Kumai Tamotsu) - one of them quoting an 1815 report by the headman of Shiba Kurumamachi in Edo, and they give the cart's size and its spread through the towns. They disagree with each other on where it spread - the 1815 report says Kyoto, Osaka and the castle towns of other provinces, while three other entries say Osaka (one says Kyoto and Osaka) used a different cart, the beka-guruma, and two say the daihachiguruma was mainly a cart of Edo and the Kanto castle towns. The bed of 8 by 2.5 shaku (about 8 by 2.5 ft) comes from one unsigned passage of an older encyclopedia edition; another entry says beds ran from 6 to 10 shaku long, the 8-shaku one being the size that named the cart. They say nothing of carts on the highways or of the width of the lanes a cart used.

Used for: the Edo hand cart's spread through the castle towns and its bed of about 8 by 2.5 ft (ways)
