kotobank-magusaba

Digital Daijisen, Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and Nihon Dai-hyakka Zensho, s.v. 秣場 ("fodder ground", title translated from the Japanese by this project), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/秣場-633669)

What it is: Three Japanese reference entries on the magusaba, the grass ground where fodder was cut; the encyclopedia entry (by Iioka Masaki) describes it as an Edo-period moor for fodder and fertilizer, usually held in common by a village or hamlet.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the ground's names, its double use for fodder and for green manure, its tenure as a village commons, and why it kept its fodder use where pack horses and farm animals were kept - the evidence for both the village meadow and the town's. It is a dictionary summary: it gives no sizes and no layout, and it describes the Japanese institution in general rather than any one village. It names the village or hamlet as the holder of the ground and never names a town or post station; the town's meadow rests on our reading of its clause about places that raised pack horses for highway carriage and farm animals, not on anything it says about towns.

Used for: the village's fodder and green-manure commons, and fodder ground where pack horses were kept (vegetation)
