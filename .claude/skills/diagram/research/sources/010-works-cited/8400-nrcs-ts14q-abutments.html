nrcs-ts14q-abutments

United States Department of Agriculture, Natural Resources Conservation Service, "Technical Supplement 14Q: Abutment Design for Small Bridges", National Engineering Handbook Part 654 (2007) (https://directives.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files2/1712931168/7375.pdf)

What it is: A design supplement in the United States Department of Agriculture's engineering handbook covering the abutments of small rural bridges - the single-span, single-lane kind carried on timber stringers and simple strip footings - with the loads, the footing, the slope and what to do about scour.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the bridge our maps draw, and it states the mechanism that sets an abutment back from the water: a shallow strip footing cannot be sunk below the depth the current will scour, so the bank is armored instead and the footing kept clear of it. Its limits are that it is modern engineering guidance written for modern materials, loads and construction; that the only distance in it is an illustrative worked example rather than a rule, and is measured from the crest of the bank slope rather than from the water; and that it is American, standing in for a practice no premodern East Asian source we could read describes.

Used for: the scour reason for setting a bridge's bearing back from the channel edge, and the description of the small single-span timber bridge our plank glyph stands for. NOT for a landing distance
