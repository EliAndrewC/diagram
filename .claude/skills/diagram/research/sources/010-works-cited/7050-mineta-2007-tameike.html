嶺田拓也, 「Basic knowledge for ecosystem consideration (part 3): the flora of paddy fields and irrigation ponds」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「生態系配慮の基礎知識（その3）― 水田とため池の植物相 ―」), 農業農村工学会誌 (Journal of the JSIDRE) 75(8), 2007, pp. 745-748 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjsidre2007/75/8/75_8_745/_pdf)

What it is: An article by Mineta Takuya in the Journal of the Japanese Society of Irrigation, Drainage and Rural Engineering (Japanese: 農業農村工学会誌) 75(8), 2007, part of a series on the ecological basics of farmland, dealing with the plants of paddy fields and of irrigation ponds.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source with numbers behind our rule that a farm pond's shallow shore is reeded, giving the zoning of reed and cattail by water depth and bank slope, and it is what shows that a working reservoir does not have a bare margin: a Kagawa study it reports found more emergent and floating-leaved species where ponds are dredged and their algae cut, and it is the ponds that have fallen out of use that lose their variety, so a working reservoir does not have a bare margin. It is a professional-society review reporting recent surveys of ponds still in use rather than primary evidence of its own or evidence about any earlier century, and the correlation it reports comes from a single prefecture, so we take a pattern of where plants stand from it and not a figure about how a premodern pond looked.

Used for: the finding that CONTRADICTS "a managed reservoir has a bare margin" - a Kagawa Prefecture study found a statistically significant POSITIVE correlation between emergent- and floating-leaf plant species counts and the practice of dredging silt and cutting algae, and it is the ponds whose water use has stopped that lose diversity; and for the zonation of reed and cattail (ヨシやガマなどの大型抽水植物) by water depth and slope. The one number-bearing source behind the rule that a tameike's shallow shore is reeded.
