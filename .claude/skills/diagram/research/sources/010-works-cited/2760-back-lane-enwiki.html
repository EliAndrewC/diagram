en.wikipedia "Back lane" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_lane)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the back lane, the service way behind houses, with its origin in the planned medieval village running parallel to the main street at the far end of the plots.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that the back lane and the main street together framed the planned village and that the lane divided the village from its fields, which is the field-ward edge this page draws. Its case is the planned medieval village of England, resting on one paper on English medieval street towns (Slater 2004, the Hertfordshire evidence), in a three-sentence stub; it says nothing of East Asian villages, so the field-ward lane is a European device this page carries over, not one attested in the setting's models.

Used for: the back lane as a planned-village device and the village's edge toward the fields
