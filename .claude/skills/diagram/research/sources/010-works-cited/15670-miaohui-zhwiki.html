miaohui-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 庙会 ("miaohui, the temple fair", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E5%BB%9F%E6%9C%83)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the temple fair, the gathering at a temple for offerings, contests and performances that took on market trading, from its early history to modern Beijing and Taiwan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the fair gathered near the temple, first on its religious festivals and later on fixed dates, took on trade, and so became a form of the Chinese market, citing in a footnote a Qing note that a capital temple held one on the ninth of each month. It is an encyclopedia article, a tertiary summary whose references are mostly popular web pages, and it mixes premodern and modern practice. Its only dated evidence is two period texts quoted in its footnotes, and both are about the capital, Beijing: the Qing note above, and a Ming description of the City God temple's fair, held on the first, fifteenth and twenty-fifth of each month with stalls three li long. So it describes a capital's fairs, not a county town's. It gives no area for a fair's ground, and that stall length is a capital's.

Used for: the temple fair as a place of trade and of festival, held on a temple's ground (towns)
