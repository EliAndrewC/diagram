hoppou-shibata-ashigaru

Northern Culture Museum (北方文化博物館), 足軽長屋 ("the foot soldiers' rowhouse", title translated), a page of its Shimizuen garden at Shibata (in Japanese; https://hoppou-bunka.com/shimizuen/ashigaru.html)

What it is: The page of the museum that keeps the former Shibata domain's foot soldiers' rowhouse, which stands across the Shibata River from its Shimizuen garden at Shibata, Niigata: a single row of eight households side by side under one thatched hipped roof (24 ken by 3.5 ken; each household 3 ken wide and 3 ken deep, with a kitchen and an earth floor in a lean-to at the back), whose ridge tag names the domain's commissioner of works and the year 1842; it gives one household's plan, with two room layouts, as the pattern for all eight.

Why it applies, and its limits: The building's keeper describes a surviving Edo-period rowhouse of a domain's lowest retainers, gives the plan of each household, its own entrance earth floor included, and says who lived there: by post, servants of the gate-guard and flag-bearer companies and rope men, not foot soldiers. It is one building in one northern domain, lived in until 1969 and dismantled and restored in 1971-72, so the plan it gives is the restored one. It was one of four rowhouses the domain kept at Shimizudani until the end of the Edo period, behind a street at an entry road into the castle town, not inside a residence's compound, so a domain's group of rows stands in for a servants' row within a compound. The page calls it a 棟割長屋, but its own figures (eight households 3 ken wide in a 24-ken row, 3.5 ken deep) make it a single row, not back to back. It is a short visitor page of the garden that names no source beyond "the records", with no drawn plan, and it does not say how many people a household held.

Used for: a servants' rowhouse of eight households in a single row, each entered by its own entrance earth floor (buildings)
