zh.wikipedia 西安滿城 ("the Manchu city of Xi'an", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/%E8%A5%BF%E5%AE%89%E6%BB%A1%E5%9F%8E)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the banner garrison quarter built from 1645 in the northeast corner of Xi'an, on the site of a Ming prince's palace: its size, its gates, its banners and its drill ground.

Why it applies, and its limits: An encyclopedia article (a tertiary source), whose figures are only as good as the works it cites. It measures one garrison quarter against its city: about 2.05 by 1.55 km, some 3 square km, a little over a quarter of the walled city. That is a modern measurement from the Xi'an city gazetteer (西安市志); the only Qing measure it gives is 1,028 by 1,200 paces. It says the drill ground lay inside the quarter, on the old Ming palace site, and was used on fixed days each month (it does not say how many). The quarter was the Qing conquest dynasty's banner garrison, walled off from the townspeople, and it stands in here for garrison quarters in general. Its share changed: from 1683 to about 1780 a southern annex gave the garrison nearly the whole east of the city. It is not an upper bound, since Jingzhou's banner quarter took the eastern half of its city and drilled outside the south gate.

Used for: a garrison quarter's share of its city and its drill ground (cities/government)
