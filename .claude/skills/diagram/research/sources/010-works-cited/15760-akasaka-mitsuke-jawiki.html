ja.wikipedia 赤坂見附 ("Akasaka mitsuke", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%B5%A4%E5%9D%82%E8%A6%8B%E9%99%84)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Akasaka mitsuke, one of the watched gates on the outer moat of Edo castle, with a short account of what a mitsuke was and how many Edo castle had.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that a masugata gate stood on the outer works of the shogun's own castle, not only at its core. It is a short tertiary article with no references at all (the page carries Wikipedia's banner for an unsourced article), so its claims are only as good as a check against a sourced work. It gives the 36 mitsuke as a traditional figure ("there are said to have been 36"), not a count of gates, so the number sets the scale and is not an exact tally. It is about Edo, the largest castle in Japan, so it says nothing about the gates of a provincial castle town.

Used for: Edo castle's 36 mitsuke along its moats, and Akasaka mitsuke as a masugata gate on the outer moat (defenses)
