yoshiwara-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 吉原遊廓 ("Yoshiwara pleasure district", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%90%89%E5%8E%9F%E9%81%8A%E5%BB%93)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Yoshiwara, Edo's licensed pleasure quarter, its founding, its removal - ordered in 1656, delayed by the Meireki fire and completed in 1657 - to Nihon-zutsumi behind Senso-ji in Asakusa, and its later history.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records where the quarter was moved to, which is one of the three places this page aligns to Edo's northeast. A tertiary summary used for the relocation; it says nothing of the kimon or of direction. The article carries the encyclopedia's own notice that it lacks sufficient sources. And the article gives its own reason for the move - the city had grown and daimyo residences had come up against the quarter, and it infers that the competition from bathhouses was a further reason - which competes with the northeast reading this page draws from the places. It also names the two sites the shogunate weighed, Nihon-zutsumi or Honjo, and Honjo lies east across the Sumida rather than northeast, so by the article's account direction did not fix the choice. For the quarter's own form it gives the first site's size, and, in the uncited caption of a late-Edo plan of the second site, the high wall, the ditch (ohaguro-dobu) and the single approach by Emon-zaka and the Gojikken road - the new quarter's form at the end of the period, not evidence for the first site, and a caption that warns the plan's proportions are distorted (the real quarter was longer top to bottom and narrower side to side than drawn); and it gives the second site's size twice in figures that do not agree (3 cho square and a little over 20,000 tsubo); it describes one quarter, the most isolated of the three great ones, not the usual form.

Used for: the Yoshiwara's removal to Asakusa; the quarter's wall, ditch, single approach and the size of its two sites (urban-features)
