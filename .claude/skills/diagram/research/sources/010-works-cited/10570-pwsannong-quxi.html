Zhihui Sannong (an agricultural knowledge service), "Irrigation and drainage canal systems" (in Chinese; https://www.pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/560500.shtml)

What it is: An encyclopedia-style article, apparently from a Chinese reference work on the history of water conservancy, on the parts of a traditional canal system, the head works first, divided into intakes with a dam and without, with regional examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It corroborates the two-form division of intakes and gives the one regional statement read about which form an area used: the ancient Ningxia intakes on the Yellow River were all damless. Ningxia's are large canals off a great river, not a hamlet's brook, and the page names no source of its own.

Used for: the two forms of intake head works; the ancient Ningxia intakes all damless; the Qin canal's wooden intake gate rebuilt in stone (water)
