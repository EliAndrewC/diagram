pwsannong-quxi

Zhihui Sannong (an agricultural knowledge service), "Irrigation and drainage canal systems" (in Chinese; https://www.pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/560500.shtml)

What it is: An encyclopedia entry with no author named, on China Agriculture Press's knowledge service, filed under the history of farmland water conservancy in its agricultural-history encyclopedia (apparently the Agricultural History volume of the Encyclopedia of Chinese Agriculture), on the parts of a traditional canal system, the head works first, divided into intakes with a dam and without, with regional examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It corroborates the two-form division of intakes and gives the one regional statement read about which form an area used: the ancient Ningxia intakes on the Yellow River were all damless. Every example on the page is a large state or prefectural canal (the Zhibo and Zhang canals, Dujiangyan, the Ningxia canals), so the two-form division is a modern engineering classification of great works, carried to a hamlet's brook only by analogy; Ningxia's are large canals off a great river, not a hamlet's brook. "Damless" there means no dam across the river, not no work in it: the same page says the Qing built stone-and-brushwood guide dikes out into the Yellow River at those mouths, one 450 zhang long. It is a tertiary summary that names no source of its own, and it hedges its dates (the Qin River canal was built "about" the Qin; its wooden gate was rebuilt in stone under the Cao Wei).

Used for: the two forms of intake head works; the ancient Ningxia intakes all damless; the Qin canal's wooden intake gate rebuilt in stone (water)
