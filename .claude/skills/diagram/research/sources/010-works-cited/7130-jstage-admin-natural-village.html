"The Relation between Administrative Village and Natural Village in the Edo Era", Japanese Journal of Human Geography 19(2) (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjhg1948/19/2/19_2_117/_article)

What it is: Tadashi Kondo, "The Relation between Administrative Village and Natural Village in the Edo Era", a research article in the Japanese Journal of Human Geography, volume 19 issue 2, 1967. It examines how the villages the Edo tax system recognized related to the settlements that actually existed on the ground, and finds that a taxed village on a plain was usually a single settlement while one in the mountains gathered several, more of them the deeper into the hills it lay.

Why it applies, and its limits: It answers a question the record asks about terrain rather than rank - that a taxed village on a plain was usually one natural village while one in the mountains gathered several, and more the deeper into the hills. Its limits are that it is one district's pattern rather than a national rate, as the paper's own abstract says; that it is scholarship of 1967, which later Japanese historical geography may have refined; and that we have read and quoted its English abstract only, not the Japanese full text.

Used for: that multi-hamlet administrative villages were commoner in mountains than on plains - a terrain pattern, and the closest thing found to a frequency statement. Cited as evidence that the record does NOT rank settlement forms
