kotobank-suitengu

Kotobank, "水天宮" (Suitengu), the entries of several Japanese encyclopedias and dictionaries on one page, among them Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia and the Britannica International Encyclopedia (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%B4%E5%A4%A9%E5%AE%AE-83092)

What it is: Kotobank's page for Suitengu, the water-god shrines whose head shrine is at Kurume in Fukuoka prefecture, reprinting the entries of modern Japanese reference works on the head shrine and on its branch in Tokyo, and (from Heibonsha's historical place-name dictionary, only partly shown) on two other branches founded in the Edo period: at Tsuchiura in 1840 and at Otaru harbour in 1859-61.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the Suitengu faith before the modern period: the lords of Kurume revered the shrine for its powers against water disasters and for safe birth and in 1818 enshrined it at their Edo residence, where its faith spread to the neighbors. It is a set of modern encyclopedia entries, not a period record, and the one entry that calls the shrine a guardian of boats says so in the present tense with no date; the entries also differ on what was set up in Edo in 1818, a place to worship from afar or the enshrined god itself.

Used for: the Suitengu faith dated before the modern period, and its boats-guardian role undated (river cities)
