Kotobank, 火の見櫓 - 世界大百科事典 and 日本大百科全書, with the 火の番 passage on the same page (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%81%AB%E3%81%AE%E8%A6%8B%E6%AB%93-120594)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the fire-watch tower, carrying two published encyclopedias which agree independently that in Kyoho-era Edo one tower served about every ten machi, with a ladder and a bell on the watch hut in the machi between, and a third passage on the shogunal standing fire watch, its brigade mansions at several places in the castle town, and the watch house set in every machi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies the tower-to-neighborhood ratio our dispersal rule rests on, from two works that state it separately, and it separates what was per-machi (the watch house) from what was not (the tower). Its limits are that these are tertiary summaries; that the ratio is Edo's, the largest wooden city there was, in one era, so a provincial seat two orders of magnitude smaller is being reasoned to rather than described; and that the Japanese arrangement is only half of the setting's ancestry.

Used for: one fire tower to about ten machi with a ladder and bell between, the shogunal brigade mansions, and the watch house in every machi
