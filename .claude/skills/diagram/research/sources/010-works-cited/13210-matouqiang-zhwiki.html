zh.wikipedia 馬頭牆 ("horse-head wall", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A6%AC%E9%A0%AD%E7%89%86)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the horse-head wall, the stepped fire gable of Hui-style building, saying it was raised between one house and the next because houses were of wood and the lanes between them narrow to save land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is evidence that traditional Chinese houses stood close enough to need a fire wall between them. It is a short encyclopedia article on one regional style, strongest for Huizhou in the south-east and its neighbors (Hubei, Hunan, Jiangxi). It gives no date, no town and no measure of how close the houses stood, and it speaks of traditional buildings and their lanes generally, not of towns in particular: Hui-style walls are best known from villages. Its own sentence on stopping fire rests on a 2018 Epoch Times culture-site feature, and the one on narrow lanes on a popular book, so the claim is a secondary summary at best. It does not speak for the north.

Used for: the fire gable between house and house in a close-built Chinese town (towns)
