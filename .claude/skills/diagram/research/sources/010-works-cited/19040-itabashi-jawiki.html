ja.wikipedia 板橋宿 ("Itabashi-juku", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%9D%BF%E6%A9%8B%E5%AE%BF)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article (an encyclopedia article, a tertiary source) on Itabashi, the first post town on the Nakasendo out of Edo, giving - through a 2006 walking guide to the Nakasendo that it cites (Kamei Chihoko et al., Yama-kei) - from the shogunate's road commissioners' survey (the Nakasendo Shukuson Daigaicho) of about 1841-1844 the length of its road, the length of its built street within that, its houses and its population.

Why it applies, and its limits: The survey separates the built street from the station's whole road, so it measures how long a post town's built strip was in the Edo period. Itabashi was one of the four busy stations at the edge of Edo, so it sits at the dense end rather than the typical; the page itself says the survey's date is uncertain, from the 1840s into the 1850s, and the house count does not say how many houses fronted the road. Itabashi was three sub-stations run as one (Kami-juku, Naka-juku and Hirao-juku, each with its own village headman), so its built street is three settlements joined end to end rather than one village's street. The figures are second-hand: an encyclopedia article repeating a modern walking guide's reading of the survey, not a reading of the survey itself.

Used for: one busy post town's built street length and houses in the 1840s (towns)
