What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the 1642 Yellow River flood, in which the dikes above Kaifeng were cut during a siege and the city was destroyed. It gives the traditional casualty figure, some three hundred thousand of the city's three hundred and seventy eight thousand inhabitants lost to the flood and to the famine and disease that followed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the scale of what a river can do to a walled city in one year - the dikes cut during a siege, and most of the population dead - which is the reason a river city on our maps keeps its distance from the bank. Its limits are that it is a tertiary summary of a single catastrophic year, not the ordinary risk of the bank; that its death toll is cited to one modern book on an adjacent subject rather than to the chronicles, and the encyclopedia contradicts itself between this article and its Kaifeng article on the numbers, as the footnote records; that the page gives three accounts of who breached the dikes, so we say only that they were cut during the siege; and that it is imperial China.
