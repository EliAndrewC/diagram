ehime-kenshi-joka-shokunin

Ehime Prefecture, Ehime kenshi: kinsei ge (1987), chapter 6, section 3, part 2, "城下と職人町の形成" ("The castle town and the forming of the craftsmen's streets", title translated), in the database Ehime no kioku (in Japanese; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/63/view/7977)

What it is: The prefectural history's account of how the castle towns of Iyo gathered craftsmen, gave them house plots and set them in streets by trade, with the domains' registers of their retained craftsmen.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a scholarly prefectural history drawing on the domains' own registers, so its account of how craftsmen were settled in a castle town is well grounded. Its limits are that it describes the castle towns of one province, Iyo, all of middling or small domains, and that the streets-by-trade pattern it describes is the early one: it says that later in the period the streets became mixed, with merchants holding the main streets and craftsmen renting row houses in the back lanes, that even in Matsuyama the streets named for a craft lay only in the old town built first, and that in the smaller towns of Ōzu and Yoshida there were no craftsmen's streets at all, the craftsmen being spread through the town with only dyers, smiths, coopers and carpenters tending to cluster. Its counts are of two kinds, the domains' registers of the craftsmen the lord retained and Matsuyama's counts of all the town's craftsmen in 1694 and 1784, which it warns are hard to compare because merchant and craftsman were not always told apart.
