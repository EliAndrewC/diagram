matsui-2014-edo-religious-space

Matsui Keisuke, 「Discussions on the Religious Space of Edo City : The Landscape and Functions of Temples and Shrines」 (the paper's own English title; original: 「寺社分布と機能からみた江戸の宗教空間」), Journal of Geography (Chigaku Zasshi) 123(4): 451-471, 2014 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jgeography/123/4/123_123.451/_pdf)

What it is: A peer-reviewed geography article on how Edo's temples and shrines were distributed and what they did for the city, drawing on the shogunate's surveys and on earlier studies of Edo's temple gate towns (Nishiyama 1983, Kaneyuki 2000, Ito 1995).

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Edo's new gate towns had rest teahouses and souvenir shops from the first half of the seventeenth century, counts 440 gate-town wards in the mid-eighteenth, and prints the 22 households on the gate-town land of an average temple of about 1,000 tsubo in 1843, with their trades, in houses about 3 ken deep. It is Edo only - it says the form of townhouse built on a temple's own land was not generally found in Kyoto or Osaka - and the one temple's list is one ward in one year.

Used for: the trades and the size of an ordinary Edo temple's gate town (religion and death)
