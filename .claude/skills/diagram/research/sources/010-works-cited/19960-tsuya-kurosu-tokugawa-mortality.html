tsuya-kurosu-tokugawa-mortality

Tsuya and Kurosu, "Household Socioeconomic Status and Mortality in Tokugawa Japan: Evidence from Three Northeastern Villages 1708-1870", paper for the International Population Conference 2021 (https://ipc2021.popconf.org/uploads/210715)

What it is: A conference paper by two historical demographers analyzing deaths in the population registers (ninbetsu aratame cho) of three farming villages of the Nihonmatsu domain, in present Fukushima prefecture - Shimomoriya, Niita and Hidenoyama - from 1708 to 1870.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured crude death rates for Japanese farming villages before 1868: about 30 per 1,000 in the decade of the Tenmei famine and 29 in that of the Tenpo famine, about 18 in the decades after them (its text dates these 1780-1789 and 1840-1849, the first plainly a slip, since 1780-1789 is the famine decade itself). It is three villages of one northeastern domain, a region the famines struck hard; its text gives the rates of those decades only (the rest are in a chart), its registers leave out infants who died before their first annual registration, which the authors call their data's most serious shortcoming, so its rates run low; the authors also judge these villages' mortality one of the highest known for Tokugawa villages over the whole period; and it is a conference paper, still a draft, not a reviewed article.

Used for: the Edo death rate behind the burial-ground sizing (religion and death)
