hannan-terakoya

Hannan City, "寺子屋 ～江戸時代の教育～" ("Terakoya: education in the Edo period", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.city.hannan.lg.jp/kakuka/syogai/syogai_s/bunkazai_shokai/bunkazai_arekore/1494826741043.html)

What it is: The city's cultural-properties page on the terakoya of its villages at the end of the Edo period, counted from the Nihon kyoikushi shiryo, with two more known from their masters' gravestones.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one village-by-village count found, and it shows that a village with a school kept one (one village had two). Its limits are these. It is a modern city page summarizing the Meiji-published Nihon kyoikushi shiryo (1890-92), not that compilation itself, which is public and tabulates every province. It covers a few villages in one Kinai district, and it names only the villages that had a school, not those that had none, so it cannot say what share of villages kept one. Most of its schools taught writing only, or reading and writing, and only one taught arithmetic too. The survey it counts from also missed some schools, as its own gravestone finds show.
