hu-shangantang

Li Bohua et al., "Research on the landscape features of traditional villages from the perspective of the landscape gene information chain: the case of Shanggantang village", Human Geography 35(4) (2020) (https://html.rhhz.net/RWDL/html/20200406.htm)

What it is: A Chinese human-geography paper analyzing one traditional village in Hunan - Shanggantang - by its landscape "genes", including its siting: mountains on three sides and the Xiemu River in front.

Why it applies, and its limits: "Mountains on three sides, water on one side" is the cleanest statement read that the ideal village has its water BESIDE it rather than through it. Its limits: it is a Chinese village rather than a Japanese one, it describes an ideal configuration in fengshui terms rather than a surveyed distribution, and it does not say which bank the houses occupy in fact - it names the village's old stone arch bridge as its main entrance without saying what water it crosses; the phrase itself is the 2020 authors' summary of the site rather than a period text, so it dates the reading and not the village.

Used for: mountains on three sides, water on one side
