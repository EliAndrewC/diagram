kotobank-minakuchi-matsuri

Kotobank, 水口祭 (minakuchi matsuri, "the water-inlet festival"), entries from the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten and the Nipponica encyclopedia (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%B4%E5%8F%A3%E7%A5%AD-139085)

What it is: A dictionary aggregator's page gathering several reference works' entries on the rite held at a seedbed's water inlet (mizuguchi) when the seed rice is sown; the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten gives its earliest example from a poem of about 1134.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the mizuguchi as the inlet through which water enters a paddy and dates the rite held at it, and so the inlet itself, to a twelfth-century poem, which places the form long before the modern period. The date carries the dictionary's own question mark, the rite is described as a folk custom collected in modern times, and nothing here gives the inlet's size.

Used for: that a paddy's water inlet (mizuguchi) is a premodern form (Water)
