jawiki-goshi

Japanese Wikipedia, "合祀" (gōshi, joint enshrinement) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%90%88%E7%A5%80)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article on gōshi, the moving of one shrine's kami into another shrine, and the three ways it was done.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the choice this record needs - a second kami taken into the main hall, or given its own small shrine in the precinct - but it is a stub flagged as short of sources, its three-way division resting on a library reference-desk answer, and its one historical passage concerns the forced shrine mergers of the Meiji and Taishō eras, long after the period the maps draw. Nothing in it dates either form to before Meiji, and both of its references are modern (a 2024 library answer and a 2014 city-planning paper on shrines restored after those mergers), so it carries the names and shapes of the forms, not evidence that the setting's era used them.

Used for: that a second kami could be enshrined in the main hall or given its own shrine in the precinct (religion-and-death).
