kotobank-bansui

コトバンク 番水 ("Kotobank: bansui", title translated) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%AA%E6%B0%B4-118160)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering Japanese dictionary and encyclopedia entries on bansui, the delivery of irrigation water in turns: the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, signed articles of the revised Sekai Daihyakka encyclopedia (by Miura Keiichi and Kitamura Toshio), the Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten and the Britannica Kokusai Daihyakka short-entry edition, with excerpts from the old Sekai Daihyakka's entries on irrigation and on water rights.

Why it applies, and its limits: The dictionaries date the practice from the medieval period, as a drought regime, and the encyclopedia says its rules were being written down by the late medieval period, so it places turn-by-turn delivery before 1868. They are reference summaries, not the village documents themselves, and the examples they name are from Yamato and the dry, advanced districts of western Japan. They give no calendar season, only a time of drought or shortage, and no hours for a turn, only its patterns: time allotted in proportion to the area planted, every other day, or day and night alternating. Its devices are named but not described: a dividing timber or stone at the point where the water divides, a weir left open by a set gap, and the right to pull a pond's sluice.

Used for: that delivering irrigation water in turns in a drought is medieval and early modern Japanese practice (cities/capitals)
