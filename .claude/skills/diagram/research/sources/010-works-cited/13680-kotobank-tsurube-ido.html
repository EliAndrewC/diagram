kotobank-tsurube-ido

Kotobank, 釣瓶井戸 ("tsurube-ido, the bucket well", title translated), quoting Shogakukan's Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), entry by Amano Takeshi (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%87%A3%E7%93%B6%E4%BA%95%E6%88%B8-1565067)

What it is: The Nipponica encyclopedia's entry on the bucket well, by a folklorist: the older well frames, the ways the bucket was raised, "such as" a balance-beam sweep or a rope over a pulley (the entry's list is open), and its note that a well shared by a village or a group within it was "not uncommon".

Why it applies, and its limits: A signed encyclopedia entry covering Japan in general and the period before the hand pump, which is the period the maps draw. It names the forms without saying where or when each prevailed, cites no period record, and gives no dimensions. The bucket well lasted until the hand pump and piped water, which is modern, so the forms it describes were probably recorded by folklorists in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries; that they are Edo or older is our inference, not the entry's statement.

Used for: the two lifting gears of a well, the old wooden frames, and the village's common use of a well (urban-features)
