kotobank-tsurube-ido

Kotobank, 釣瓶井戸 ("tsurube-ido, the bucket well", title translated from the Japanese by this project), quoting Shogakukan's Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), entry by Amano Takeshi (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%87%A3%E7%93%B6%E4%BA%95%E6%88%B8-1565067)

What it is: The Nipponica encyclopedia's entry on the bucket well, by a folklorist: the older well frames, the two ways the bucket was raised (a balance-beam lever or a rope over a pulley), and the wells' common use by a village or a group within it.

Why it applies, and its limits: A signed encyclopedia entry covering Japan in general and the period before the hand pump, which is the period the maps draw. It names the forms without saying where or when each prevailed and gives no dimensions.

Used for: the two lifting gears of a well, the old wooden frames, and the village's common use of a well (urban-features)
