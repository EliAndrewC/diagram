ranzan-soshinji

Ranzan Town web museum (嵐山町web博物誌), 比企郡寺院明細帳, 曹洞宗 宗心寺 七郷村大字吉田 (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/09ter03.html)

What it is: The Ranzan town office's transcription of the Hiki district temple register entry for Sōshinji, a Sōtō temple of Yoshida, Saitama, founded in 1600, listing its main hall, entrance, bell tower, kuri, earthen storehouse, monks' dormitory (shuryō), retired abbot's quarters (inryō), woodshed and a founder's hall of 1781 with their dimensions, and its precinct of 1,665 tsubo; the register is kept in a Saitama prefecture file of 1963.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives what stood in a rural temple's precinct beside the main hall, with sizes. Its limits: one rural temple; a Meiji-era register (the village still under its pre-1889 name, with notes added up to 1942) that was filed again in 1963, so the buildings are those registered in the 1880s at a temple founded in 1600. That is close to the Edo period's end, but not necessarily an Edo set. And it is one sect (Sōtō Zen) at a temple of 554 parishioners with a monks' dormitory, larger than a hamlet's temple.

Used for: a temple's bell tower and storehouse (religion-and-death.html).
