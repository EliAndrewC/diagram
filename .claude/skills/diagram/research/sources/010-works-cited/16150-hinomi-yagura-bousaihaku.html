hinomi-yagura-bousaihaku

Fire and Disaster Management Museum (消防防災博物館), (3)消防資機材等 ("(3) Firefighting equipment and materials", title translated from the Japanese by this project), in its history of firefighting (in Japanese; https://www.bousaihaku.com/ffhistory/11305/)

What it is: A page of the online museum of the Institute of Scientific Approaches for Fire and Disaster, on the fire-watch tower from the Edo period and the hand and steam pumps of the Meiji era.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the first tower's height (5 jo, about 15 m, against the 3 jo of the encyclopedias), the rule that every other tower was lower and a townsmen's tower open-legged, and the liter figures of the Meiji pumps from which a koku's volume is read. It is an institute's popular history about Edo and Tokyo. Its text has no citations (its illustrations are credited to 東京の消防百年の歩み, Tokyo no shobo hyakunen no ayumi, "a hundred years of Tokyo fire service"), and it gives no footprint. The tower rules are the shogunate's rules for its capital, which the page itself hedges ("it seems"), so a provincial town's tower need not have followed them. The liters are the page's own modern conversions at about 180 liters to the koku (the steam pump's 1.6 kiloliters works out to about 183), so the koku read from them is the standardized Edo and Meiji koku, not a measurement, and not the Chinese shi.

Used for: the first tower's height, the open legs of a townsmen's tower, and a koku in liters (urban-features, cities/fabric)
