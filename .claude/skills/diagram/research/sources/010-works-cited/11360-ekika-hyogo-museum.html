Hyogo Prefectural Museum of Archaeology, "The ancient relay stations (umaya)" (in Japanese; https://www.hyogo-koukohaku.jp/modules/guidance/index.php?action=PageView&parent_category_id=3&language=ja&number=28)

What it is: A short explanatory page by a prefectural archaeology museum on the relay stations of the ancient highways and what excavations at two Hyogo sites found of their buildings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a museum's gallery text summarizing excavated evidence for the relay stations of Japan's ritsuryo state (from the late seventh century, modeled on Tang China's system), about a thousand years before the Edo post towns. The page itself calls the tiled, white-walled, red-pillared buildings rare for their time and luxurious, fit to receive foreign guests: they show the showpiece stations of a major road, not what an ordinary relay station looked like. It is brief and popular, gives no dimensions, and its "first in the country" refers to the buildings found, at two sites together.

Used for: the tile-roofed, white-walled, red-pillared buildings found at Koinumaru and Ochiji (towns)
