Nihon Nogyo Shimbun, "Under one roof with the cow: the one maya left in the Mikata area of Hyogo" (in Japanese; https://www.agrinews.co.jp/news/index/174809), 5 August 2023

What it is: A farm newspaper's feature on the last farmhouse in the Mikata district of Hyogo that still keeps its cow in a maya, a stall inside the house, written when the district's traditional Tajima cattle system was named a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records, on the word of the Tajima Cattle Museum, that the in-house stall was the usual form in one mountain district of western Japan, some 4,000 houses at the 1950s peak, and why - partly that there was too little flat land for a separate shed. It is a newspaper article about one district in the twentieth century, not a study of the Edo period, and its reason is one museum official's hedged account.

Used for: the in-house cattle stall, and too little flat land for a separate cattle shed as one reason for it (homesteads)
