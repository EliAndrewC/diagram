Tarama Village Agricultural Heritage Promotion Council, 「The sustainable island farming system of Tarama island, nurtured by the hogo (pogu) of the Ryukyu kingdom's time」 (translated; original: 「琉球王国時代の「抱護（ポーグ）」が育む多良間島の持続的島嶼農業システム」), application dossier for Japan's nationally important agricultural heritage systems, published by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (in Japanese; https://www.maff.go.jp/j/nousin/kantai/attach/pdf/giahs_7_tarama-1.pdf)

What it is: A long dossier, for the island's designation as Japanese Agricultural Heritage in January 2025, on Tarama's farming system and its tree belts: the village belt's founding under Sai On, its sides, width and trees, its upkeep by the island's wards, and the yamadome closed season, drawing on the Shirakawa genealogy, local histories and recent vegetation surveys.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest account read of a premodern belt sheltering a whole village, with its width when planted. But the belt is a Ryukyuan form: the kingdom's fengshui planning laid it out for a small, flat island open to winter north winds and summer typhoons, and the dossier itself calls it unlike anything else in East Asia's fengshui tradition, surviving close to its original form only on Tarama. So it stands for a village belt in country as exposed as Tarama's, not for the grove of a Chinese or Japanese village. It was written to support a heritage application, so it speaks well of the system; its founding story rests on one family's genealogy, which it reports with a hedge in one place and without in others; its tree ages are an estimate by a local formula; and its upkeep is described as it is today, the patrols before 1972 from a ward head's recollection.

Used for: who ordered Tarama's belt and when, its sides, its width when planted and today, its trees and their height, the yamadome season and the wards' upkeep (The countryside)
