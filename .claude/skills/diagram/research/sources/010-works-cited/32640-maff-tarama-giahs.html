Tarama Village, 「The sustainable island farming system of Tarama island, nurtured by the hogo of the Ryukyu kingdom's time」 (translated; original: 「琉球王朝時代の「抱護（ポーグ）」が育む多良間島の持続的島嶼農業システム」), application dossier for Japan's nationally important agricultural heritage systems, published by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (in Japanese; https://www.maff.go.jp/j/nousin/kantai/attach/pdf/giahs_7_tarama-1.pdf)

What it is: A long dossier on Tarama's farming system and its tree belts: the village belt's founding under Sai On, its sides, width and trees, its upkeep by the island's wards, and the yamadome closed season, drawing on the Shirakawa genealogy, local histories and recent vegetation surveys.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest account read of a premodern belt sheltering a whole village, with its width when planted. It was written to support a heritage application, so it speaks well of the system; its founding story rests on one family's genealogy as the dossier reports it ("it is thought" that the belt was made then), its tree ages are an estimate by a local formula, and its account of upkeep and patrols is of the twentieth century and today, from a ward head's recollection.

Used for: who ordered Tarama's belt and when, its sides, its width when planted and today, its trees and their height, the yamadome season and the wards' upkeep (The countryside)
