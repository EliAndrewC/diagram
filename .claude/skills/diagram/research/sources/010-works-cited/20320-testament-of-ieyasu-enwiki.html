Wikipedia (English), "Testament of Ieyasu" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testament_of_Ieyasu)

What it is: A short English Wikipedia article on the maxim handed down as Tokugawa Ieyasu's testament. It separates that maxim from the longer text in a hundred articles, and lists the three English translations of the longer text.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the hundred articles are: a longer text on how the shogunate was to be run, at least partly drafted by Ieyasu's successors. It is a tertiary summary whose sentence on authorship has no footnote of its own, and a weak witness on authorship, since it presents the short maxim as Ieyasu's words of 1604 where Japanese Wikipedia holds it a later composition, first credited to him in print in 1835. Gubbins's 1919 paper, one of the translations it cites and readable as a scan, dates the compilation to the third shogun's reign from evidence in the text itself (the junior council, set up in 1633; Nagasaki as the only port, from 1641) and is the closer source.

Used for: the hundred articles as a text at least partly drafted after Ieyasu (ways)
