kotobank-miyage

Kotobank, 土産 - 改訂新版 世界大百科事典, the entry signed 岩本通弥 (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%9F%E7%94%A3-583333)

What it is: The Kotobank page for miyage, the souvenir brought back from a journey, carrying the signed entry from the revised Heibonsha world encyclopedia on how the custom grew, what was sold, and who sold it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates a souvenir trade at a temple gate to the early modern period and names its mechanism: at Kumano and elsewhere the shugenja became half-monk and half-layman and came to sell souvenir goods, and later, as demand expanded, specialist souvenir shops came into being and formed a gate town - the goods being talismans and amulets above all, with lucky dolls, toys, picture books and sacred medicines beside them - the custom spreading from the middle of the early modern period as the highways were made up and commoner pilgrimage spread through the pilgrimage guides and oshi. That is the souvenir row our gate quarters draw, on a dated statement rather than on inference. Its limits are that it is a signed encyclopedia entry rather than a primary source; that it dates the flourishing to mid-Edo, so an earlier gate carries fewer such shops; and that it speaks of Japan, while this setting's temple economy also draws on China.

Used for: specialist souvenir shops at a premodern temple gate, forming the gate town, and the goods they sold
