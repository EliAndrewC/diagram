jimingyi-visitbeijing

Visit Beijing (visitbeijing.com.cn), "风雨沧桑鸡鸣驿" ("Jimingyi through wind and rain", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://www.visitbeijing.com.cn/article/47QrmalSb31)

What it is: A feature by Yang Xilai from the Beijing Suburbs Daily (京郊日报), 6 July 2017, reprinted on the Beijing tourism site, about Jimingyi in Huailai county, Hebei, the largest surviving Ming-Qing post station of China, describing its wall, gates, streets and buildings and quoting the county gazetteer on its garrison and horses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a readable description of a real Chinese post station and its layout - offices, granary, horse compound and official lodging court inside a wall. Its limits are that it is tourism writing; that another page of the same site gives a different perimeter and area; that its placement of the lodging court is inconsistent within the page; that its own figures disagree, since a 2,330 m perimeter around a roughly square wall would enclose about 0.34 km², not the 0.2 km² it gives; that its garrison of more than 300 and its 82 horses are the Qing peak ("at most"), quoted secondhand from an unnamed edition of the county gazetteer; and that Jimingyi was a fortified frontier station with a garrison, far larger than an ordinary relay.

Used for: the walled station's 2,330 m perimeter, its offices, granary, horse compound and lodging court, and its Qing garrison of more than 300 and 82 horses (towns)
