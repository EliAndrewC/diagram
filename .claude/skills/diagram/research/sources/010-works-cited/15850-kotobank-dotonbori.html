kotobank-dotonbori

Kotobank, 道頓堀川 ("Dotonbori river", title translated from the Japanese by this project), the Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei (Heibonsha) entry, with the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Daijisen and Britannica entries beside it (https://kotobank.jp/word/道頓堀川-103981)

What it is: The dictionary entries on the Dotonbori canal in Osaka (dug 1612-1615) gathered on Kotobank. The one we use is Heibonsha's Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei, a scholarly historical place-name dictionary that Kotobank shows only in part. It gives the canal's Edo-period east-west length (22 cho 11.5 ken) and its north-south breadth at the east end (20 ken) and the west end (34 ken), quoting a 1787 (Tenmei 7) document held at Kyoto University.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one read figure for a city canal's width in the Edo period itself, cited to a period document. It is a reference work's one-line quotation of that document, not the document itself. The entry does not say whether the breadth is open water or bank to bank, landing strips included. It is one canal, a trunk canal joining the Higashi-Yokobori to the Kizu River, in Osaka, one of the three great cities of the realm. So it marks the wide end for a great city's canal and says nothing about a town's canal or a village's ditch.

Used for: a city canal's Edo-period width (cities/river-cities, water)
