mizu-no-bunka-60

"The inhabitants who keep the kabata life", Mizu no Bunka (Water Culture) no. 60, Mizkan Water Culture Center (https://www.mizu.gr.jp/kikanshi/no60/06.html)

What it is: A magazine feature by the editorial department of a corporate water-culture research center, reporting from a visit to Harie on the shore of Lake Biwa in July 2018, on how its households live with the channels and their kabata.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one cited source that puts a channel physically against the dwellings - the channel that flows alongside the house - which is the relation our questions about a settlement and its water turn on. It is REPORTAGE, not scholarship: a visit, undated as to the arrangement's age, and it should carry no weight beyond describing what a visitor sees today. It says nothing about where a household's garden, yard, storehouse or privy stands relative to that channel, so for that question it is suggestive at most. Harie's channels are spring-fed, carrying water up from artesian springs through the settlement, not a natural brook passing a hamlet, and Harie is a close-built lakeside village of some 170 households, larger than a hamlet.

Used for: the channel running alongside the house; the picture of a channel along the houses behind the guess that a farmstead stands whole on one bank
