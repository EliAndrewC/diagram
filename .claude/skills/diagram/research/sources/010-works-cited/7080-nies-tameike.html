nies-tameike

国立環境研究所, ため池の生物とその環境 (https://www.nies.go.jp/biology/data/tameike.html)

What it is: A page from Japan's National Institute for Environmental Studies, "The life of farm ponds and their environment" (ため池の生物とその環境), comparing the wildlife of irrigation ponds in different condition - about six dragonfly species in a pond swallowed by the town, walled in concrete and choked with nutrients, against twenty-nine or thirty over the year in a pond ringed by worked woodland and full of water plants.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the check on an easy mistake, that a pond a village works must be the barren one: the impoverished pond in this comparison is the urban concrete one, while the farmed pond in its living landscape is the rich one. It is a modern ecological account of present-day ponds, so we take the direction of the contrast - managed is not the same as bare - and none of its counts appear on any map.

Used for: the low-diversity end of the spectrum being the urbanized, concrete-armored, eutrophic pond (six dragonfly species) rather than the farmed one - the check that "managed" and "bare" are not the same axis.
