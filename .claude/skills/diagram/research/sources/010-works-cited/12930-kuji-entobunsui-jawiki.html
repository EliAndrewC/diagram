ja.wikipedia 久地円筒分水 ("The Kuji cylinder divider", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B9%85%E5%9C%B0%E5%86%86%E7%AD%92%E5%88%86%E6%B0%B4)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the 1941 cylinder divider on the Nikaryo canal in Kawasaki, with a short section, citing no source, on the proportional flume (the Kuji bunryo-hi) that stood there from the mid-Edo period until 1941.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the Edo-period precedent for a built structure at a split: a flume dividing one canal four ways in proportion to each district's cultivated area, which did not end the disputes. It is an encyclopedia article, and its Edo-period section cites no source, so it carries the flume's existence and purpose and nothing more. Every number on the page (the 16 m diameter, the four arc ratios) is the 1941 concrete cylinder's, not the flume's. It says nothing of the flume's size or form, and nothing of it survives. The Kawasaki Board of Education's page on the site (https://www.city.kawasaki.jp/880/page/0000000312.html) is a closer source for the form: wooden gates, with inlet widths and downstream spreads set in proportion to irrigated area.

Used for: the proportional flume, a built division work of the Edo period (water)
