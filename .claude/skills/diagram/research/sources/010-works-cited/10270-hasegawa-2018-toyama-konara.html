hasegawa-2018-toyama-konara

Hasegawa Mikio, Nakajima Haruki, Ōmiya Tōru and Zushi Kōtarō, 「Species composition and stratification of secondary forest dominated by Quercus serrata in Toyama prefecture」 (the paper's own English title; original: 「富山県におけるコナラを主とする二次林の種組成と階層構造」), Bulletin of the Toyama Prefectural Agricultural, Forestry and Fisheries Research Center, Forestry Research Institute 10 (2018), pp. 1-10 (https://agriknowledge.affrc.go.jp/RN/2010925171.pdf)

What it is: A prefectural research bulletin reporting 72 plots in konara-dominated woods across Toyama prefecture - woods cut on a cycle for fuel until the mid-twentieth century and left uncut since - with their standing timber and the species in each layer.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets the timber in today's uncut woods (about 265 cubic meters of stem a hectare) against what a twenty-year-old stand holds (about 100), and says the woods now hold two and a half times or more what they held when cut on a twenty-year cycle, which bounds how much thinner a worked fuel wood was. The twenty-year figure is not measured in these woods: it is one sentence quoting a prefectural institute's conference abstract (Nakajima 2012) on present-day yields. It is the stock of a stand at the moment of cutting, the most a worked wood ever held, so a wood cut parcel by parcel on that cycle held much less on average and the two and a half is a floor. The cycle is the twentieth-century practice before these woods were abandoned, standing in for an Edo-period one, and the woods measured are abandoned ones, 50 to 60 years old, in Toyama's low hills.

Used for: how much less timber a worked fuel wood held than the same wood left uncut (vegetation)
