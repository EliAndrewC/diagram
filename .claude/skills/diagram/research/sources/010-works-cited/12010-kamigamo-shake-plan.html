kamigamo-shake-plan

Kyoto City, 上賀茂伝統的建造物群保存地区保存計画 ("Preservation plan for the Kamigamo preservation district for groups of traditional buildings") (in Japanese; https://www.city.kyoto.lg.jp/tokei/page/0000281306.html)

What it is: Kyoto City's preservation plan for the Kamigamo preservation district, a municipal notice of 26 May 1988 (Kyoto City Notice No. 65, with the Board of Education) made under the city's preservation-district ordinance. It covers the shake-machi of Kamigamo, the quarter along the Myōjin river just outside the Kamigamo shrine's precinct, where from the Muromachi period until the Meiji Restoration the shrine's priest families lived together with farmers. It gives two short paragraphs on the quarter's history and character; the rest is the city's standards for repairing the district's houses, walls and gates and for keeping other buildings in harmony with them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows where a shrine's priest families lived and that their houses were a style of their own, walled and gated, distinct from the townhouses. Its limits: a shrine's priests, not a temple's clergy; one exceptional quarter, which the plan itself calls a settlement of a special character whose like has died out elsewhere, and where the priests lived among farmers rather than apart from them; a modern (1988) municipal plan, not a historical study: its history is two paragraphs with no sources given, and its building descriptions are today's repair standards (some naming modern materials such as glass lattice doors and copper-sheet roofing), so they show the style's form, not a period record; the buildings are those standing today, in a townscape the plan dates to the Edo period.

Used for: where clergy families lived outside a precinct and what their houses looked like (religion-and-death.html).
