kaibara-jinya-tamba

Tamba City, 柏原藩陣屋跡 ("The site of the Kaibara domain's jin'ya", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://www.city.tamba.lg.jp/soshiki/shakaikyoikubunkazaika/gyomuannai/7/1/2441.html)

What it is: The Tamba city education board's page on the jin'ya of the Kaibara domain, a 20,000-koku domain without a castle, quoting the text of its 1971 designation as a national historic site (the gate is also, separately, a prefectural cultural property) and describing the surviving buildings, among them the rowhouse gate of the 1710s with its rooms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures a rowhouse gate of a small lord's seat and lists its rooms, which is the narrow rowhouse form a staff range is drawn in. Its limits: it gives two figures for the gate's length (thirteen and a half ken in the designation text, about thirteen ken one shaku in the city's own description) and two different room layouts. The city also judges the gate altered: its north end is not original, and the range once ran further north (the domain's drawing shows the rear gate in the same building), so both lengths are of a shortened gate. Its horse-viewing room, now plank-floored, was probably once matted. And a domain lord's seat is not a county magistracy.

Used for: the depth and length of a jin'ya's rowhouse gate and the rooms in it (buildings)
