Touken World, 間取りで見る武家屋敷 (Samurai residences seen through their floor plans) (in Japanese; https://www.touken-world.jp/tips/105023/)

What it is: An unsigned, undated article on Touken World, a sword-culture website the page credits jointly to Token Corporation (a land-use and construction company), the Japanese-history office of the Touken World Foundation (which runs the Nagoya Touken Museum) and the Totsu Agency, walking through the plans of several samurai residences: the Maeda domain's Edo mansion, the Nomura house at Kanazawa and the Kuchiba house at Hagi, room by room.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the room sequence of two surviving upper-samurai houses of about 1,000 to 1,200 koku, closer in scale to a provincial posting than a daimyo palace. Its limits are that it is unsigned popular writing on a company-produced site, without references; that the Nomura house as it stands is, by the page's own account, smaller than the period house, and its audience room and raised jodan-no-ma were moved in from the house of a Kitamae-ship owner at Daishoji - a merchant's house, not a samurai's - so the order of its rooms, the butsuma beyond the audience room included, is a later assembly and not that house's own period sequence; and that the Kuchiba main house's date (late 18th to early 19th century) is hedged.

Used for: the Kuchiba house's six-room plan, the zashiki and oku-zashiki at its far end; the Nomura house's butsuma beyond its audience room (buildings)
