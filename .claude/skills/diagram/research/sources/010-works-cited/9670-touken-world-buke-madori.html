Touken World, 間取りで見る武家屋敷 (Samurai residences seen through their floor plans) (in Japanese; https://www.touken-world.jp/tips/105023/)

What it is: An article on the Touken World site of the Nagoya Touken Museum walking through the plans of several samurai residences: the Maeda domain's Edo mansion, the Nomura house at Kanazawa and the Kuchiba house at Hagi, room by room.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the room sequence of two surviving upper-samurai houses of about 1,000 to 1,200 koku, closer in scale to a provincial posting than a daimyo palace. Its limits are that it is popular writing by a museum's web team without references; that part of the Nomura house as shown was moved in from another building, so its sequence is not that house's own; and that the Kuchiba main house's date is hedged.

Used for: the Kuchiba house's six-room plan, the zashiki and oku-zashiki at its far end; the Nomura house's butsuma beyond its audience room (buildings)
