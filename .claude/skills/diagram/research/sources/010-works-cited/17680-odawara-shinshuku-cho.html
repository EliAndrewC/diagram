odawara-shinshuku-cho

Odawara City, 城下町・宿場町 おだわらの町名・地名 ("Castle town and post town: Odawara's ward names and place names", title translated), the entry 新宿町 ("Shinshuku-chō") (in Japanese; https://www.city.odawara.kanagawa.jp/global-image/units/526688/1-20230815145543_b64db135fb6657.pdf)

What it is: An undated 23-page booklet of the Odawara city government summarizing, from period records, the historic ward names of the castle town along the Tōkaidō; its first entry is Shinshuku-chō, the ward that adjoined the castle town's Sannō-guchi entrance.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a counted ward at a castle-town entrance on a trunk road: made in the early Edo period when the highway was re-routed, lined with lodging inns for villagers on official business and with tea shops, and holding 124 houses in the early 1830s. Odawara was also a post town, which swelled its roadside trade; the count is of houses, not shops; the leaflet does not say whether the ward lay inside or outside the town's outer enclosure; the booklet is a modern secondary summary: for this ward it names the domain lord's 1645 diary for the ward's first mention but does not name the record behind the 124-house count, which is also about two centuries later than the ward's founding; and the PDF is an image with no text layer, so its words can be read but not searched.

Used for: the count of houses in one ward at a castle-town entrance (cities/hinterland)
