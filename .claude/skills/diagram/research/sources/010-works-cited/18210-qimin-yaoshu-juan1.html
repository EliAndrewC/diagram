賈思勰 齊民要術 卷第一 ("Essential Techniques for the Common People, Scroll One", title translated) (Jia Sixie, Qimin Yaoshu, c. 540), Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E4%B8%80)

What it is: Book 1 of the sixth-century Chinese agricultural treatise, with the chapters on plowing (耕田第一), keeping seed (收種第二) and growing grain (種穀第三), in a Wikisource transcription; the author's notes describe the seed drills still used in his day, and the grain chapter sets the spacing of plants and how the sower treads the row.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text from before the setting's period showing grain sown with drills in rows and thinned to a set spacing, which grounds the rows the maps draw on a dry plot. It describes North China's dry farming in the sixth century, largely from the Yellow River plain; the drill (耬) is named only in the author's notes (on a quoted Han memorial, and in the seed chapter), though his main text has the ox walk slowly so the seed falls evenly while the sower treads the row bottom. The spacing is "one stand (科) to the chi" and for good land only: a stand may be more than one stalk, a Han author he quotes has poor land sown thinner, and his chi is the Northern Wei measure of about 28 to 30 cm, near but not the same as a foot or the Japanese shaku.

Used for: grain sown with a drill in rows and thinned to one stand a chi (about a foot) on good land (fields)
