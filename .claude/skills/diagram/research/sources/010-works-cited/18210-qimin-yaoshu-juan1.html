賈思勰 齊民要術 卷第一 ("Essential Techniques for the Common People, Scroll One", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (Jia Sixie, Qimin Yaoshu, c. 540), Chinese Wikisource (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E9%BD%8A%E6%B0%91%E8%A6%81%E8%A1%93/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E4%B8%80)

What it is: Book 1 of the sixth-century Chinese agricultural treatise, with the chapters on plowing (耕田第一), keeping seed (收種第二) and growing grain (種穀第三), in a Wikisource transcription; the author's notes describe the seed drills still used in his day, and the grain chapter sets the spacing of plants and how the sower treads the row.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a primary text from before the setting's period showing grain sown with drills in rows and thinned to a set spacing, which grounds the rows the maps draw on a dry plot. It describes North China's dry farming in the sixth century, largely from the Yellow River plain, and says nothing of Japan; the drill sentences are the author's notes on a quoted Han source, not his main text.

Used for: grain sown with a drill in rows and thinned to one plant a foot (fields)
