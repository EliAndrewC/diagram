OPAL (Otsu, Shiga), environmental-education sheet 2-2, "About reeds 2: what the reed bed does" (ヨシについて② ヨシ原の役割), 2023 (https://www.o-pal.com/common/pdf/education/download/pdf_03_08.pdf)

What it is: A short illustrated slide set for schoolchildren on Lake Biwa's reed beds, dated 2023 in its file, from an outdoor-education provider on the lake, on what the reed bed gives the lake, its creatures and the people round it, and on the cutting and burning that keep it a reed bed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the mechanism by which the reed beds were kept open - cutting the reed stops old reed from building up into soil on which willow and other trees then grow, and burning the bed makes it harder for trees to take - as a practice people have long carried out on the lake to get good reed for blinds and roofs. It is a modern teaching sheet for children, not a study: it gives no date for the practice, no measurement and no frequency (it never says the reed was cut or burned every year), it describes Lake Biwa's shore rather than a village's marsh toe, and so it supports that cutting keeps trees out of a reed bed, not how any particular settlement managed its margin.

Used for: reed cutting and burning keeping the reed bed open and willow out (vegetation, the marsh margin)
