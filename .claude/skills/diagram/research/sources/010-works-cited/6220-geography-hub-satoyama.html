What it is: "Japan's Satoyama Landscapes", an article on The Geography Hub, a general-audience educational geography site that publishes explainers under headings like cultural, historical and biological geography. It carries no named author and no date. The piece surveys satoyama, the managed mosaic of coppiced woodland, paddy, grassland and water that sits between a Japanese village and the mountain behind it, how centuries of cutting and gathering produced it, and how it has fared since.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its subject is exactly ours - the managed coppice country between village and mountain - and the three things we take from it, leaf litter gathered for fertilizer, oak and chestnut cut on a cycle, and the light let onto the floor by cutting to the stump, are ordinary and well attested. Its limits are severe for a source: the page is unsigned, undated and cites no sources at all, so a reader who follows the link can trace nothing further; it mixes the historical practice with its present-day conservation; and closer public sources for the same three claims exist in the scholarly literature on satoyama. Nothing quantitative is taken from it.
