fushimi-inari-senbon

伏見稲荷大社, the shrine's visitors' questions page (https://inari.jp/faq/)

What it is: The official website of Fushimi Inari Taisha in Kyoto, the head shrine of the Inari cult - its visitors' questions page, which answers what the vermilion gates on its mountain paths are and how many of them there now are. The site gives the shrine's own account of the gates as votive offerings, a custom it dates from the Edo period onward, and its present count of about ten thousand across the whole mountain approach.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells us that the gates are donations and roughly how thickly the most heavily endowed shrine in the country came to be lined with them, which is the upper bound a map needs when it draws a shrine approach. Its limits are that it is the institution describing itself, not a survey; that the count is a present-day round number accumulated since the Edo period, not a period census, and only the whole-mountain figure is readable on the site, so any density per stretch of path is derived by us; and that this is the most famous shrine of its kind, so it is a ceiling and never a typical case.

Used for: 「At present about 10,000 torii stand along the whole of the mountain's approach.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「現在は約1万基の鳥居がお山の参道全体に並んで立っています」); gates donated by worshippers from the Edo period onward (the Senbon Torii sub-count is on neither page)
