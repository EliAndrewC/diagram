kawazoe-2010-ryobosei

Kawazoe Yoshiyuki, "両墓制集落における祭祀と埋葬の空間論" (A spatial theory of worship and burial in two-grave settlements), 景観・デザイン研究講演集 (Proceedings of Landscape and Design Research) no. 6, Japan Society of Civil Engineers, December 2010 (in Japanese; https://www.jsce.or.jp/library/open/proc/maglist2/00897/2010/pdf/A32D.pdf)

What it is: A paper by a University of Tokyo civil-engineering researcher who surveyed and mapped 22 settlements keeping the two-grave custom, in the Shiwaku islands of the Inland Sea, the Shima region of Mie and the Wakasa region of Fukui, and measured where each settlement's burial ground and visiting-grave ground lie relative to its houses, its watercourse, its temple and its shrine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source read that MEASURES how far from the houses a village's graves lie and on which side of them, which is exactly what a map must place. Its limits are that it is about Japanese villages only and cannot stand for a place modeled on China; that the settlements are two-grave ones, a custom concentrated in the Kinki region and its neighbors, mostly coastal fishing and farming villages; that they were surveyed as they stand in the twenty-first century, some after the custom had lapsed, and the paper does not date the layouts - its own note says only that the custom is agreed to go back at least to the modern (Meiji) era - so that a pre-Meiji arrangement is assumed, not shown; that its distances are from the center of the residential area and are read off its graphs as averages; and that its reading of why the burial ground lies downstream is the author's own suggestion, not a finding.

Used for: how far from its houses a village's burial ground and visiting graves lie, and on which side of them (religion-and-death)
