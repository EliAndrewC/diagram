What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the water wheel: an unsigned, crowd-edited encyclopedia article (a tertiary source) covering water wheels worldwide, with a section on their history in Japan. Since February 2015 it has carried a banner saying it lacks verifiable sources.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the full use of power water wheels is held to date from the Edo period, for polishing rice and milling grain as white rice spread, which dates the village mill. It hedges the claim ("is said to") and cites no source for it, and the article as a whole is flagged as undersourced, so it carries the general shape of the history and not a firm date. The date is for Japan only: the same article puts water-driven machinery in China in the Han and, through its one cited footnote, widespread water mills from the Tang, and in Japan it records a water-driven mill in 610 and irrigation wheels from 829. So in the parts of the setting modeled on China, the village mill is far older than the Edo period.

Used for: the power water wheel dated to the Edo period (water)
