kotobank-yoriai

Kotobank, 寄合 ("yoriai, the meeting", title translated from the Japanese by this project) - the Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, revised edition, entry by Fukuta Ajio among others (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AF%84%E5%90%88-146512)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the yoriai, the meeting, carrying several published encyclopedias on the village meeting and on the meetings of officials in the shogunate and the towns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a village meeting was often held at a village official's house, though temple halls and, in some regions, buildings in a shrine's or temple's grounds were used too, and that in the Kinki region villages set up meeting halls, often inside the tutelary shrine's precinct. Its limits: a tertiary entry, and a folklorist's undated summary whose hosts include the ward head (区長), a post from the Meiji period onward, so what it describes reaches past the Edo period into modern village practice; it gives no dates for the Kinki halls beyond "from early times", no size for them and no count of how many villages had one; the rest of the page is about meetings that are not a village's.

Used for: the village meeting held at an official's house, and the Kinki meeting hall in the shrine precinct (homesteads)
