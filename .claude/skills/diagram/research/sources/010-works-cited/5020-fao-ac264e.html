fao-ac264e

FAO/NACA, Integrated Fish Farming in China, ch. 7 "Introduction of Chinese integrated fish farming and some major models" (https://www.fao.org/4/ac264e/ac264e09.htm)

What it is: Integrated Fish Farming in China, a report from the UN Food and Agriculture Organization and the Network of Aquaculture Centres in Asia-Pacific; the chapter we cite introduces the major Chinese models of combining fish ponds with other livestock, including pig sheds built on the pond dike or out over the water and duck ponds with a fenced dry run on the bank.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives us the physical arrangement of a pond that also carries animals - where the shed stands, how the duck run meets the water - which is what a map has to draw. It describes practice as observed on the collective and state farms of the 1980s, so we use it for the form of the thing and never as evidence of how common such a pond was in a premodern village, and the stocking rate it gives (one to five pigs per mu of pond) is a modern intensity we do not draw from.

Used for: the pig shed "constructed on the pond dyke or over the water surface" (ponds under 8 mu), 1-5 pigs per mu; fish-cum-duck ponds with a fenced dry run on the dike and a wet run in a corner of the water. Modern practice (1980s), cited as the FORM, not as premodern prevalence
