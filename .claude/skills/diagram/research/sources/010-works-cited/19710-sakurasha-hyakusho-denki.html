sakurasha-hyakusho-denki

Sakura-sha, "Hyakusho Denki is interesting!" (in Japanese; blog post, 5 March 2013, https://www.sakura-sha.jp/blog/hyakusyou_dennki/)

What it is: An unsigned post on the blog of a Tokyo publisher of books for teachers, by a reader of the first volume of a modern edition of the Hyakusho Denki, the farm manual of the later seventeenth century, reporting what it says about where a farm should put its privy and how a farm without a bath should wash.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only readable page found that reports what the manual itself says on the privy and the bath, and the manual was written before 1868. Its limits: it is a lay reader's paraphrase, not the manual's text, and it does not name the edition; some of its framing (that the privy stood away from the house) is the blogger's own rather than something he attributes to the manual, and he hedges the bath being a luxury with "it seems". The manual describes the Tokai region.

Used for: the privy southeast of the house in the sun, and the bath as a luxury for a farm, in the Hyakusho Denki (homesteads)
