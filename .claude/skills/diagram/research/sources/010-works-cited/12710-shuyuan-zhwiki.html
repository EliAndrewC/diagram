zh.wikipedia 书院 ("academy", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/书院)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the shuyuan, the academies of China and its neighbors, and how they stood apart from the official schools.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines an academy as a privately founded school, sometimes under official direction, set apart from the official schools, with its own school lands, a head called the shanzhang, and three functions: lecturing, offerings at its shrine, and examining students. It is a tertiary summary, thinly footnoted (most of its history cites one popular book on the academies of Changhua), with no sizes, plans or counts of academies per city. What an academy was changed by era: private lecture halls under the Song and Yuan, suppressed more than once under the Ming, and under state supervision after the Qing ordered academies in every province in 1733. A claim taken from it should name the dynasty it holds for. It says nothing of Japan; its Korean and Taiwanese sections stand in as the wider region.
