koshigaya-nishikata-kyuki

越谷市デジタルアーカイブ (Koshigaya City Digital Archive), [西方村旧記五] ("Old records of Nishikata village, part 5", title translated), a transcription of a village's own record book of the Bunsei era (1818-1830), from the Koshigaya city history's source volumes (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/koshigaya-city-digital-archives/texthtml/d000040/mp000080-100010/ht200020)

What it is: The village officials' copies of their dealings with the shogunate's intendant over the rice tax from 1818 to the late 1820s, among them the fixed-rate tax requested and granted in 1825, which states the village's assessed yield and area split into paddy and dry field.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, in the village's own figures, how much of a Saitama lowland village's land was paddy and how much dry field - about 118 cho of paddy against 30 of dry field - and the same book counts some 44 cho of its paddy as waterlogged ground, so the village stood on low, wet ground. Its limits: the figures are the shogunate's part of the village only - the same book has the intendant set the village's private-domain and temple lands apart from it - so they are not the whole village's land; it is one village, and a large one (the book calls it a big village, of some 1,350 koku and 148 cho), in one assessment, so it is a bigger place than a hamlet; the 30 cho of dry field is the registered dry-field area and counts the house plots, and the same 1825 petition says that once the house plots are taken out only a little of it is cropped, so the dry field actually farmed was less than the 30 cho; the page describes its paddy only as partly high ground like dry field and partly marsh, lying where a reservoir's overflow flooded it, and does not name the landform, say whether the village stood in a row, or say where the dry fields lay; and the areas are tax areas, the registered measure of the fields, not a survey of the ground.

Used for: the share of dry field in a lowland paddy village (homesteads)
