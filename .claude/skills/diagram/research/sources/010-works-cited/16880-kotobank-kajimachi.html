kotobank-kajimachi

Kotobank, 鍛冶町 ("Kajimachi, the smiths' ward", title translated) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%8D%9B%E5%86%B6%E7%94%BA-1040397)

What it is: Kotobank's page for the place name Kajimachi, carrying the Nihon rekishi chimei taikei's entries for the smiths' wards of many castle towns, among them Kofu's, with counts of its smith households: 28 of the town's 33 duty smithies stood in this ward while Kofu was a domain, according to a 1724 handover register, and 32 remained, 27 of them still smithing, in a register of 1780, when Kofu was under direct shogunate rule.

Why it applies, and its limits: The historical gazetteer cites the registers it counts from, so Kofu's figures are near-primary counts of one castle town's smiths and of how many lived on their own street. Its limits are that Kofu is one large castle town, that the counts are of smith households owing the lord smith's work rather than of every smith, and that it does not separate blade smiths from tool smiths.

Used for: how many smiths a castle town kept and that they lived together on one street (Trades and services)
