nippon-com-terakoya

nippon.com, "寺子屋：驚くべき江戸時代の教育力" ("Terakoya: the remarkable educational power of the Edo period", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.nippon.com/ja/japan-topics/g01005/)

What it is: A 2021 feature article on the terakoya by Takahashi Satoshi, professor emeritus at the National Museum of Japanese History and a historian of Edo commoner education, with a worked example of one village school's curriculum in Kozuke, saying a school needed only a master and one room.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states plainly that the school was a room, not a building, which is the point the map turns on. Its limits are that it is a general-audience essay arguing for Edo education against modern schooling; that "a master and one room" is the author's generalization about terakoya, not a description of the one school it works through (whose curriculum, not premises, it gives); that it describes Edo Japan only, so for a Chinese-modeled place it stands in for the village schools of China, which it does not treat; and that its "about as many as villages" is a saying it reports, not a count.
