taixi-shuifa-quanlan

熊三拔 (Sabatino de Ursis), 泰西水法 ("Western Methods of Water", title translated), 1612, the Siku Quanshu text on Chinese Wikisource, all books on one page (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E6%B3%B0%E8%A5%BF%E6%B0%B4%E6%B3%95_(%E5%9B%9B%E5%BA%AB%E5%85%A8%E6%9B%B8%E6%9C%AC)/%E5%85%A8%E8%A6%BD)

What it is: A late Ming book of water-lifting machines, cisterns and wells, headed on every book as written by the Jesuit Xiong Sanba (Sabatino de Ursis), its catalog summary dating it to 1612; its well-digging method has five parts, the first on choosing the site.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a premodern Chinese text on where to dig a well: the mountain foot first, then sunny ground where gardens and houses stand, then open country; and it says that in low-lying marsh country a well finds water anywhere. It is a Jesuit's book written from European practice for Chinese readers, not a record of Chinese village custom; it never forbids a well in wet ground, and what it says of marsh country favors wells there; and it says nothing about Japan.

Used for: where a well was sited, and that nothing in it keeps a well off wet ground (water)
