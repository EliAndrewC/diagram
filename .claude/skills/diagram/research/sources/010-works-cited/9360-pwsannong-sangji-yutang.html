pwsannong-sangji-yutang

"Sangji yutang" (桑基鱼塘, the mulberry-dike fishpond), an entry in the sericulture section of the encyclopedia on Zhihui Sannong (智汇三农), a Chinese agricultural knowledge database published by China Agriculture Press (in Chinese; https://www.pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/564807.shtml)

What it is: A short Chinese agricultural encyclopedia entry on the mulberry-dike fishpond: how the dikes are built from pond mud, how the mulberry on them is planted and trained in the Pearl River delta and in Jiangsu and Zhejiang, and how the system arose in Guangdong from the fruit-dike ponds after the sixteenth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that gives how thickly the dike mulberry of the Pearl River delta was planted and how it was trained, and it describes the system the map's dike-pond hamlet is drawn from. It is a modern reference work, posted in 2016, citing no sources of its own. It dates the system's history (fruit-dike ponds before the ninth century, mulberry dikes after the sixteenth) but gives its planting figure only as the practice "to this day" (至今, translated by this project), with no date; that the figure describes the twentieth-century system is this project's inference. So its planting density is evidence for the later form of the system, not a measured pre-modern figure, and its mu is presumably the modern one of about 667 square meters, not the Qing mu; it names no crown width. A peer-reviewed study of the same delta system, Zhong Gongfu's 1980 paper in Acta Geographica Sinica, was not read here and may give a better figure.

Used for: how densely dike mulberry was planted in the Pearl River delta, and that it was trained as root-cut ground mulberry (archetypes)
