Huang Yang 黄洋 and Du Yao 杜瑶, 古代"一尺"有多长？有哪些常见的长度计量单位？ ("How long was an ancient chi? What were the common units of length?", title translated from the Chinese by this project), 2021, republished by the Chinese Academy of History (http://hrczh.cass.cn/lszg/lszg_sgwx/202502/t20250218_5846894.shtml)

What it is: A museum-written popular article, first published by the WeChat account of Museums of China and republished on the Chinese Academy of History's site, on the ancient units of length, illustrated with excavated rulers from the Han to the Tang.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Tang foot at 30.7 cm, from a Tang half-foot ruler excavated at Shanzhou, and the pace of the old reckoning at five of the builder's feet, which turns paces into meters. It is a short popular piece; the five-foot pace is stated for the old reckoning in general, not for the Tang or the Song in particular, and a foot's length varied from dynasty to dynasty.

Used for: the Tang foot, 30.7 cm, and the five-foot pace (defenses)
