kotobank-kosatsu

Kotobank, 高札 ("kosatsu, the notice board", title translated from the Japanese by this project), quoting Heibonsha's Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (revised new edition; entry signed by Katō Hideaki) among other dictionaries (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%AB%98%E6%9C%AD-64337)

What it is: The Kotobank dictionary page for 高札, which gathers the entries of several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias; the Heibonsha encyclopedia's entry says where the boards were hung and how their sites were enclosed and guarded.

Why it applies, and its limits: A signed entry (Katō Hideaki) in a modern general encyclopedia - a tertiary summary that gives no period record for what it says. Its sentence on enclosure is worded generally, but it follows directly on the list of Edo's six great notice-board sites and its 35 others, so it may describe the shogun's capital and its major sites rather than a village's or a small town's board; none of the other dictionaries on the page mentions an enclosure at all. It names the enclosures - a palisade, a stone wall, a turf bank - as alternatives without saying which was usual or giving any size.

Used for: the enclosure of a notice-board site (urban-features)
