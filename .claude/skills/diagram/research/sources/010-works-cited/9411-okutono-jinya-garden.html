okutono-jinya-garden

Oniwa-san, 奥殿陣屋"蓬莱の庭" (the Hōrai garden of the Okutono Jin'ya), Ito Masatoshi (in Japanese; https://oniwa.garden/okutono-jinya-okazaki/)

What it is: A page on a garden-visiting site by its author describing the Okutono Jin'ya in Okazaki, Aichi, the seat of the small Okutono domain of Mikawa: its history, its shoin, and the two-tier pond garden laid out there after an Edo-period drawing of the jin'ya's garden.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one of the few readable accounts of the garden of a jin'ya, the kind of walled office-residence our compounds draw, and it says the garden was a pond garden seen from the shoin. Its limits are that it is a garden-media site written by its editor, not the keeper's own account; that the jin'ya was dismantled after the domains were abolished and what stands there now is a park opened in 1985, the shoin moved back in the Shōwa era, the page not saying to its old place, and the garden made then from the drawing, which the page does not reproduce, so that the site itself files the garden as Shōwa work, its Kyoto-style planting of Kitayama cedar is the restorers' choice, and the page does not say whether the two tiers, the upper one reaching toward a restaurant built with the park, are the drawing's or the restorers'; and that it was the seat of a domain, grander than a county post, and is one example rather than a pattern.

Used for: a pond garden in two tiers, seen from the shoin, at a small domain's jin'ya (buildings)
