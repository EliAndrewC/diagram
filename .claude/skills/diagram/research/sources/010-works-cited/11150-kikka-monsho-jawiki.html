ja.wikipedia 菊花紋章 ("Chrysanthemum crest", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8F%8A%E8%8A%B1%E7%B4%8B%E7%AB%A0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the chrysanthemum crest: how the retired emperor Go-Toba took the flower as his mark, how it became the imperial crest by custom, and how widely the design was used.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is why a chrysanthemum reads as the Emperor's flower in a setting drawn on Japan. A tertiary encyclopedia article on an emblem, and its sentence on Go-Toba and the Kamakura emperors cites no source of its own. It says the crest was the Emperor's by custom only. In the Edo period the design was used freely on sweets and Buddhist altar goods, and the bans on others using it are Meiji decrees of 1868-1871, later than the eras the setting is drawn on. So it supports the flower reading as the Emperor's, not the flower being reserved to him. It says nothing about growing the flower or about any planted field.

Used for: the chrysanthemum as the imperial emblem since the Kamakura period (fields)
