suido-ishizue-yayoi-suiden

Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), Suido no Ishizue, "水田の誕生、弥生水田" ("The birth of the paddy: Yayoi paddies", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/01/06.html)

What it is: A page of an agricultural-engineering association's illustrated history of Japanese land and water works, on the earliest paddies found by archaeology across Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sums up the excavated paddies as divided by bunds and channels, with intakes and outlets fitted with weirs and level-setting boards, a basic form it says was handed down to the present, so the bund, the channel and the inlet are all premodern forms. It is a general statement by an industry body without footnotes; its "reinforced with sheet boards and stakes" is not true of every site (the Hattori and early Sena paddies were not), and it gives no sizes.

Used for: that the earliest excavated paddies had bunds, channels, intakes and outlets (water)
