suido-ishizue-yayoi-suiden

Agricultural and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), Suido no Ishizue, "水田の誕生、弥生水田" ("The birth of the paddy: Yayoi paddies", title translated) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part2/01/06.html)

What it is: A page of an agricultural-engineering association's illustrated history of Japanese land and water works, on the earliest paddies found by archaeology across Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sums up the excavated paddies as divided by bunds and channels, with intakes and outlets fitted with weirs and structures for regulating the amount of water, found from the Late Jomon onward, a basic form it says was handed down to the present, so the bund, the channel and the inlet are all premodern forms. It is a general statement by an industry body without footnotes; its "reinforced with sheet boards and stakes" is not true of every site (the Hattori and early Sena paddies were not). Its only sizes are for plots (the Kofun-period Mifuro plots of 3-5 m², about one or two tatami); it gives none for a bund, a channel or an intake. The paddies it describes are Jomon to Kofun, far earlier than the Edo and imperial Chinese eras the setting is modeled on, and that the form lasted is the page's own claim.

Used for: that the earliest excavated paddies had bunds, channels, intakes and outlets (water)
