Okubo Jun, Nihon Keizai Shimbun, "Oniku to ieba nishi wa ushi, higashi wa buta: noko-yo no dobutsu ga chigatta" (In the west meat means beef, in the east pork: the farm animals were different), 17 February 2017 (in Japanese; https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXKZO12714730Z00C17A2W02001/)

What it is: A newspaper feature explaining Japan's east-west split in the meat people eat by the draft animals each half of the country once plowed with, citing the national livestock survey of 1877.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, from an 1877 count, the same east-west split of cattle and horses that the historical geography finds, which makes the split a second-sourced finding rather than one author's map. It is journalism summarizing surveys it does not reproduce, written to explain a modern taste, and it describes the Meiji count, not the Edo village; it is used only for where the cattle and the horses were.

Used for: cattle in western Japan and horses in the east and in southern Kyushu (homesteads)
