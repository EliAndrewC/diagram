kotobank-shinboku

Kamata Hisako, "神木" ("shinboku", the sacred tree), in the Nipponica encyclopedia (日本大百科全書), on Kotobank with other dictionaries' entries (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%A5%9E%E6%9C%A8-82559)

What it is: A signed entry in Shogakukan's general encyclopedia, reproduced on Kotobank, on the tree held sacred at a shrine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the sacred tree as generally a tree in a shrine's precinct, roped with a shimenawa and revered, which is the tree the country-shrine sheet draws. It is a definition, not a count: it does not say how many shrines, or which ranks of shrine, kept one. It is also a modern folklorist's summary of the custom as it is now, and it does not date the roped tree; its only historical example is the medieval Kasuga sacred tree. It speaks for Japan only. The shimenawa is a Japanese form, and the entry says nothing of China.

Used for: the roped sacred tree in a shrine's precinct (religion-and-death.html).
