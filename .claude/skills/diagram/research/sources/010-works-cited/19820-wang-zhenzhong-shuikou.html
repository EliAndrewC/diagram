wang-zhenzhong-shuikou

王振忠 里至源头，外至水口：明清以来徽州村落空间的建构 ("'From the Source Within to the Water Mouth Without': How Huizhou Village Space Was Built from the Ming and Qing On", title translated) (Wang Zhenzhong, in 徽学 Huixue no. 14), the chapter as published on QQ Reading (https://m.read.qq.com/read/1043461189/7)

What it is: A chapter by a historian of Huizhou (Anhui University and Fudan University) on how the villages of Huizhou, in the hills of southern Anhui, set their bounds between the stream's source above the village and its water mouth below, read from lineage genealogies, lawsuits, gazetteers, verse and fieldwork; it quotes a 1568 preface on the source-and-water-mouth plan, and Qing writing on the water-mouth grove itself: early-Qing village rules, a 1762 county stele and Qianlong-era verse.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the water-mouth grove as a form: the source-and-water-mouth plan was fixed by the mid-sixteenth century at the latest, and eighteenth-century verse describes the dense shade of the trees screening the water mouth. The Ming date is for the plan, not the grove: every dated text it quotes on the trees themselves is Qing (village rules fining their cutting, a 1762 county stele, Qianlong verse), and its only Ming claim for them is a Qing stele's statement that the pines had been kept "since the former Ming". It is about one region, Huizhou, whose lineage villages are unusually well documented; it gives no area, width or tree count for any grove, and its one description of a grove's shape (a planted dam at Fangkeng) comes from a villager's account the chapter does not date.

Used for: that the water-mouth grove stood in the Ming and Qing, before any modern survey measured one (vegetation)
