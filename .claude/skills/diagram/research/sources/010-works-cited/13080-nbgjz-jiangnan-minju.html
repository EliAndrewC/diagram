汉匠 (Hanjiang, a traditional-architecture firm), 江南水乡民居有哪些特点？原因是什么？ ("What are the features of Jiangnan water-town houses, and why?", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://www.nbgjz.com/2080.html)

What it is: A short article on the website of a Chinese firm that designs temples and traditional buildings, summarizing the features of the riverside houses of the Jiangnan water towns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that nearly every riverside house had a small landing at its door where clothes, vegetables and rice were washed and boats moored - the Jiangnan form of the washing place, one per house rather than one per village. That form belongs to the canal country of the lower Yangtze, where houses open straight onto the water; it is not the form for all of China, and a map of a drier region should not take one landing per house from it. It is a firm's blog, with no author, no date and no sources, describing the water towns as they survive, so it is weak evidence for a date.

Used for: the Jiangnan house landing for washing (water)
