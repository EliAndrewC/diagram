ja.wikipedia 高瀬川 (京都府) ("The Takase river, Kyoto", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高瀬川_(京都府))

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Takase river, the transport canal Suminokura Ryoi and his son cut through Kyoto between 1611 and 1614 and used for some three hundred years: its width and depth, the flat takasebune that worked it, its list of boat-entry basins along the city stretch, and the boat basin at Shichijo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a real Edo-period canal cut through a city to bring freight to the townspeople's doors, the case a river city's in-city canal is. It is one canal, and a small one - a shallow barge cut, not a harbor channel - so its width is the narrow end of the range; the article is tertiary and cites a few secondary works.

Used for: a city canal's width and depth at the narrow end, and the boat-entry basins cut off its side (cities/river-cities, water)
