ja.wikipedia 在郷町 ("rural town", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9C%A8%E9%83%B7%E7%94%BA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the zaigo-machi, the Edo-period town that belonged legally to the countryside while functioning as a town - post towns, temple-gate towns and ports among them - with a section on its plan: usually one or two rows of townhouses on strip lots along a road or river, without the castle town's quarters, and some planned, moated exceptions.

Why it applies, and its limits: The rural town is the Japanese town nearest in rank to a county seat, a local market and administrative center below the castle town, and the article describes its plan in words. It is an encyclopedia article resting on cited Japanese histories, describing Japan only; its plan section speaks of most cases, not all.

Used for: the rural town's plan along a road without quarters, and its planned exceptions (towns)
