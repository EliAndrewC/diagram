ja.wikipedia 在郷町 ("rural town", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%9C%A8%E9%83%B7%E7%94%BA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the zaigo-machi, the Edo-period town that belonged legally to the countryside while functioning as a town - post towns, temple-gate towns and ports among them - with a section on its plan: usually one or two rows of townhouses on strip lots along a road or river, without the castle town's quarters, and some planned, moated exceptions.

Why it applies, and its limits: The rural town is a Japanese market town below the castle town, and the article describes its plan in words. Its standing in for a county seat is this project's analogy, not the article's: the rural town was legally a village and a center of trade rather than of government, where the Chinese county seat was a magistrate's walled seat. It is an encyclopedia article (marked a stub) resting on cited Japanese reference works, describing Japan only. Its plan sentence rests on a modern national town-planning guide rather than a history and speaks of most cases, not all; its planned, moated exceptions are temple-precinct towns and medieval cities, mostly in the Kinai.

Used for: the rural town's plan along a road without quarters, and its planned exceptions (towns)
