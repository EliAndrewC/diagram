udworks-igune

Urban Design Works (都市デザインワークス), 「What is an igune?」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「居久根とは？」) (https://www.udworks.net/igune/about-igune)

What it is: An explanatory page (2013) by a Sendai non-profit, written for its project to rebuild the coastal Minami-Gamō district after the 2011 tsunami, on the homestead groves (igune) of northern Kanto and the Pacific side of Tohoku, centred on Miyagi, with residents' recollections of the groves from the early Showa period (the late 1920s) to the present.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the four tall trees that form an igune's frame - sugi, zelkova, alder and black pine, reaching over 20 m - on the homestead's north-west, with many middle and low species brought in by birds, which is a statement of how mixed the grove was. It is a local group writing for the public about the Sendai area, with no survey method or counts, and its recollections go back only to the early Showa period. The four-tree frame and its heights of over 20 m describe groves standing in the twentieth century and today; the page does not show that Edo-period groves had the same frame. Black pine, a tree of the coast, suggests the frame is that of the Sendai coastal plain rather than of every igune.

Used for: the igune's tall frame of four species and its many lesser ones (vegetation)
