ja.wikipedia 厩肥 ("stable manure") (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/厩肥)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 厩肥 (kyuhi, "stable manure"), a short encyclopedia entry defining the manure made by mixing livestock dung with straw and fallen leaves and letting the cattle and horses tread it until it rots, with a paragraph on its history in Japan - named in the tenth-century Engishiki, widespread by the late middle ages, and declining in the early modern period as purchased fertilizers spread.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what stable manure was made of and how, for the Japanese farm our farmsteads are drawn from, and it places the practice in the premodern centuries our setting takes its models from. Its limits are that it is a tertiary source whose own banner asks for better footnoting, resting on three historical dictionary entries it lists but does not quote; that it gives no quantities and says nothing about where on a farmstead the manure was heaped; and that by its own account the practice was declining in the early modern period outside the smaller lowland farms, so it describes a common practice rather than a universal one.

Used for: stable litter composted into stable manure on the farmstead
