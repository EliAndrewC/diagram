Chi, Plieninger, Lin and Chowdhury, "Governing Landscape Simplification in Rapid Commercialization Contexts - the Case of the Dike-Pond System in the Pearl River Delta, China", International Journal of the Commons 18(1) 260-275 (2024), DOI 10.5334/ijc.1300 (https://thecommonsjournal.org/articles/1300/files/66151cd3a6d55.pdf)

What it is: An open-access article on how the mulberry-dike fish-pond landscape of the Pearl River delta has been simplified as it commercialized, worked through the case of one village in Shunde.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the one statement this record has of what commercialization did to the dikes - the water bodies expanded and arable dikes that used to be 20 meters wide were eroded to less than 4 - and names the mechanism, fish cultivation narrowing the dike, which is what tells the map that the thin uniform dike is the modern look and the broad planted one the older. It supplies a figure the record had been carrying as unsourced. Its limits are period, sample and method. Its frame is the decades since the reform and opening-up - it compares the 1980s with the 2020s - not any premodern century, and its case is one village rather than the delta at large, so the 20 m figure is where a modern erosion STARTED and not a measurement of a Ming or Qing dike. And it is a qualitative study: its data are seventeen interviews, and the 20 m is a figure its informants report rather than one anybody measured, which is a weaker thing to rest a drawn width on than the sentence sounds.

Used for: the dike eroding from about 20 m to under 4 m, and the mechanism behind it
