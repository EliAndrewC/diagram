"Zhejiang Huzhou mulberry-dike fishpond system: the wisdom of water control that makes farming thrive," People's Daily Online (人民网), Zhejiang channel, by Zhang Fan (张帆), 2023-11-26 (in Chinese; http://zj.people.com.cn/n2/2023/1126/c186327-40655074.html)

What it is: A news feature by reporter Zhang Fan on People's Daily Online's Zhejiang channel, the fourth in a series on Globally Important Agricultural Heritage Systems. It reports from Digang village in Huzhou, on the south shore of Lake Tai, the core protected area of the Huzhou mulberry-dike fishpond system, listed by China in 2014 and by the FAO in 2017.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the fish ponds as a reporter sees them today, from the air ("a vast blue-and-green chessboard") and on foot. Its history is heritage promotion. Its one source from the period is an opening couplet by the Tang poet Lu Guimeng (ditches and banks, silkworm trays and fish traps round Lake Tai). The couplet shows mulberry and fish farmed together but says nothing of how the ponds were laid out, and the article cites nothing else for its history. It claims the system began in the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods, over 2,500 years ago. It says only that the low ground was drained by a water works of "lengthwise channels and crosswise embankments" (纵浦横塘), with the ponds dug out and their mud heaped up as dikes. It gives no layout of the ponds themselves before the present day. The landscape it shows is also a managed one: it says many ponds were abandoned or shrank before 2013, and that the system has since been protected by regulation and developed for tourism.

Used for: that the chessboard look of the ponds is the present-day view from the air (archetypes)
