ja.wikipedia 閑谷学校 ("the Shizutani school", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/閑谷学校)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Shizutani school, opened by the Okayama domain in the 1670s for commoners, with its lecture hall, Confucian shrine and other buildings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows what a domain-built school held besides its Confucian hall. It was a school for commoners in the countryside, not the domain's school for samurai in the castle town, and the article itself calls it unmatched in its care and scale, so it is the finest case, not a typical one. The article is a tertiary summary, most of it cited to the school museum's display boards and a pamphlet handed out at the site. Its list of protected buildings is what survives, not all the school held: the student dormitories, the writing hall and the teachers' lodgings were taken down after the school closed in 1870. (Its counts of 24 and 25 protected structures agree: the 25 protected in 1938 included the lecture hall, which was made a National Treasure on its own in 1953.)
