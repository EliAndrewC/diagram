banta-kotobank

Kotobank, 番太 ("banta, the hired watchman", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering the entries of Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen, Nihon kokugo daijiten and Nipponica, Britannica Japan and Heibonsha (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%AA%E5%A4%AA-118241)

What it is: The published dictionaries' and encyclopedias' entries on the banta, the watchmen whom Edo-period towns and villages hired for the night watch, the fire watch and other guard work, and on their status.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says who kept a town's or village's night watch and that outside Edo many were of hinin status (the entries say "many", not "most"), which ties the watch to the outcaste status; in the city of Edo itself the watchmen were commoners. The entries speak generally, note that the watchman's character differed between town and country and from region to region, and name no town. They are short dictionary and encyclopedia entries, a tertiary summary that cites no period record, good for who kept the watch and what it did but not for how many watchmen a place kept or how one particular town arranged its watch.

Used for: who kept the night watch in towns and villages, and their status (urban-features)
