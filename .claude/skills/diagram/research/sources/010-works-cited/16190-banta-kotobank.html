banta-kotobank

Kotobank, 番太 ("banta, the hired watchman", title translated from the Japanese by this project), gathering the entries of Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen, Nihon kokugo daijiten and Nipponica, Britannica Japan and Heibonsha (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%AA%E5%A4%AA-118241)

What it is: The published dictionaries' and encyclopedias' entries on the banta, the watchmen whom Edo-period towns and villages hired for the night watch, the fire watch and other guard work, and on their status.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says who kept a town's or village's night watch and that outside Edo most were of hinin status, which ties the watch to the lowest caste. The entries speak generally, note that the watchman's character differed between town and country and from region to region, and name no town.

Used for: who kept the night watch in towns and villages, and their status (urban-features)
