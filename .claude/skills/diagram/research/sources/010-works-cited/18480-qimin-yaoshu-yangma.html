qimin-yaoshu-yangma

齊民要術 ("Essential Techniques for the Common People", title translated from the Chinese by this project) 卷六 ("Chapter 6", translated from the Chinese by this project), the chapter on raising oxen, horses, donkeys and mules, on Gushiwen with an editor's collation notes (in Chinese; https://m.gushiwen.cn/guwen/bookv_94788c42af6a.aspx)

What it is: The livestock chapters of Jia Sixie's sixth-century Chinese farming manual, in a modern collated text: how often a horse is watered and how much, how to keep many stallions apart in a yard of their own with many troughs, and how to condition a campaign horse by feeding it from a trough set out in the open.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a premodern manual from the tradition the setting is modeled on, so it can date a practice rather than describe a modern one. It prescribes good practice rather than recording what an ordinary stable did; it is northern China of the sixth century, far from Edo Japan; its troughs are for chopped fodder and grain and beans, and it says nothing of watering troughs, of their size, or of where they stood relative to a well. The page's editor notes that one character of the watering passage may be a copying error, so the midday measure is uncertain. The page names no editor or date.

Used for: that horses were watered at set times, rationed, and that a yard's troughs stood in the open (urban-features)
