qimin-yaoshu-yangma

齊民要術 ("Essential Techniques for the Common People", title translated) 卷六 ("Chapter 6", translated), the chapter on raising oxen, horses, donkeys and mules, on Gushiwen with an editor's collation notes (in Chinese; https://m.gushiwen.cn/guwen/bookv_94788c42af6a.aspx)

What it is: The livestock chapters of Jia Sixie's sixth-century Chinese farming manual, in a modern collated text: how often a horse is watered and how much, how to keep many stallions apart in a yard of their own with many troughs, and how to harden a riding horse (征馬, which the page's annotator glosses as a horse ridden on journeys) by setting its trough out in the open, on ground away from the stable.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a premodern manual from the tradition the setting is modeled on, so it can date a practice rather than describe a modern one. It prescribes good practice rather than recording what an ordinary stable did; it is northern China of the sixth century, far from Edo Japan; its troughs are for chopped fodder and grain and beans, and it says nothing of watering troughs, of their size, or of where they stood relative to a well. Its one trough in the open is an exception it prescribes to harden a riding horse: set on ground away from the stable (迥地, as the page's annotator glosses it) and not under the shed even in snow. So it implies that an ordinary trough stood under a shed, and it cannot show that a yard's troughs usually stood in the open. The page's editor judges the midday phrase corrupt in every copy and notes that later horse manuals read it as "a moderate amount" (酌其中), so the midday measure is uncertain. The page names no editor or date.

Used for: that horses were watered at set times, rationed, and that a stallion yard had many troughs, an ordinary one seemingly under a shed (urban-features)
