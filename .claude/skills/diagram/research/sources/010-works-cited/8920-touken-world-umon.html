刀剣ワールド 城, castle-terms glossary, 埋門 (umon) (https://www.homemate-research-castle.com/useful/glossary/castle/2122201/)

What it is: A castle-terminology glossary entry for the umon, the gate cut through the lower part of a castle's stone wall or moat face: concealed so that an enemy does not read it as an entrance, built plainly and so that it can be filled in easily, kept impassable except when wanted, and intended among other things as the route by which the lord of the castle escapes. It names surviving examples at Takamatsu and Nagoya.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the closest thing the East Asian record offers to a sally port's FUNCTION - a small concealed opening kept shut and opened when needed - which is the term our postern comparison leans on hardest. Its limits are that it is a curated commercial glossary with no citations of its own, tertiary and written for enthusiasts; that it describes Japanese castles rather than a Chinese city wall; and that the better sources for it, a cultural-property survey or the city that holds the famous surviving example, were not reached.

Used for: the umon as a small concealed postern, built to be filled in and used as an escape route
