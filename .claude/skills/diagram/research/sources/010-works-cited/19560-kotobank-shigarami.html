kotobank-shigarami

Kotobank, 柵み (shigarami, "the woven stake fence"), the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%9F%B5%E3%81%BF-3137780)

What it is: The Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's entry for the shigarami, a row of stakes driven into a river with brushwood or bamboo twined onto them to hold back the flow, with its earliest written examples, a poem in the Man'yoshu (the anthology compiled in the late 8th century, which holds poems older than its compiling) and the Heike monogatari of the early 13th.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the word and the thing it names, a woven stake fence across running water, to the 8th century in Japan, and names brushwood and bamboo as what was woven. It is a dictionary definition: its examples show the word in use, not a working irrigation weir - the Man'yoshu fence is imagined in a poem and the Heike one is a work of war - and it gives no size, no date for any weir, and nothing about China.

Used for: the stake fence woven with brushwood or bamboo across a river, and its 8th-century and 13th-century written examples (water)
