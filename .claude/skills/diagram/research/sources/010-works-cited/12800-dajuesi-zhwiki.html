zh.wikipedia 大觉寺 (北京) ("Dajue Temple, Beijing", title translated from the Chinese by this project) (https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/大觉寺_(北京))

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the Dajue Temple in Beijing's western hills, with the buildings of its middle axis and its total site area.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the order of one Chinese temple's axis with its bell and drum towers, as rebuilt under the Ming (1428) and Qing (1720, 1747), the era our setting models. It is a tertiary source: the axis and the site area cite nothing, and its one reference, a Haidian district chronicle, is cited only for events of 1986 and 1992. It is an imperially sponsored temple with a Qing travel palace on its south side, and a hillside temple outside the city, not a town temple. It faces east, which the article puts down to the Khitan custom of its Liao founding, where most Chinese temples face south, so the order of its halls is the usual one but the direction of its axis is not, and the article's site area of 6,000 m² disagrees with the far larger figure the Beijing cultural heritage bureau is reported to give, which could not be read.

Used for: the order of buildings on a Chinese temple's axis (religion-and-death)
