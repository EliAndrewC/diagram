kotobank-watashiba

「Ferry landing」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「渡し場」), in the Digital Daijisen (Shogakukan), with an excerpt from the Heibonsha Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten entry on the crossing, on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B8%A1%E3%81%97%E5%A0%B4-665240)

What it is: A modern dictionary's one-line definition of the ferry landing, beside a short excerpt from the entry on the ferry (watashi, 渡し) in the old edition of Heibonsha's encyclopedia. The excerpt names the best-known river crossings of the Edo highways, and says floods often closed them, which filled the nearby post towns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the landing and names which Tokaido rivers were forded and which ferried. Both are tertiary works with no dates and no references on the page, so they carry the shape of the practice, not a date. The encyclopedia names only the crossings it calls well known, not every one. Its split between forded and ferried rivers is given once for the whole Edo period, though how a river was crossed changed over the period (the Rokugo was bridged until 1688 and ferried after).

Used for: the ferry landing, and which Tokaido rivers were forded and which ferried (ways)
