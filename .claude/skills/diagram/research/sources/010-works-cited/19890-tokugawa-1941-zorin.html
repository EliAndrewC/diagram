徳川宗敬 江戸時代に於ける造林技術の史的研究 ("A historical study of forest-planting technique in the Edo period", title translated from the Japanese by this project) (Tokugawa Muneyoshi, Tokyo: Chikyū Shuppan, 1941), chapter 3 (植立, planting), pp. 191 and 197-205, a scanned excerpt posted by the tree-shelter maker GCJ (https://treeshelter.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/tokugawa-edo.pdf)

What it is: A forestry historian's survey of how trees were planted for timber in the Edo period, domain by domain, drawn from the period's own documents (domain orders, village records and forestry manuals, each footnoted); the excerpt is its section on planting density, which gives the counts per tsubo or per chōbu, and the spacings in shaku and ken, that the documents set for sugi, hinoki, pine and a few other trees.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a modern historian who dates each figure to an Edo era and names the document, so it places counted planting densities in Japan before 1868. Its limits: it is about timber plantations of conifers, seedlings at the day they were set out, not about a grown wood, a village's fuel wood or a windbreak round a house; the excerpt is ten scanned pages of a longer book, read as images; and a few figures are from surveys of the early Meiji, which the author marks as such.

Used for: how thickly timber was planted in the Edo period, from one tree to the ken up to nine to the tsubo, and thicker against sea wind (vegetation)
