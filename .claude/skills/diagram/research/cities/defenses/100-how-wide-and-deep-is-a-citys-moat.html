How wide and deep is a city's moat?

Sources: pingyao-chengqiang-zhwiki (Pingyao's moat, measured), thepaper-surviving-walls (the moats of Qufu, Kaifeng, Xi'an, Jingzhou, Xiangyang and Guide), xian-fortifications-enwiki (Xi'an's, measured), xiangyang-cheng-zhwiki (the widest moat read)

A Chinese walled city's moat took one of two forms. The common one is a dug ditch, and its width follows the size of the city it guards. Pingyao has a moat 4 m wide and 4 m deep, about 13 ft each way; Qufu's is about 3.3 m wide and deep and stands 7 to 10 m out from the wall. At the great cities the ditch is five to ten times as wide: Kaifeng's 16.66 m wide and 3.33 m deep, Xi'an's 18 m wide and 6 m deep, and Jingzhou's 30 m wide with 4 m of water. So the lesser ditches read are about 11 to 13 ft across, and the great cities' about 55 to 100 ft; the depth is 3 to 6 m, 10 to 20 ft, whatever the width. The two pages read disagree on Xi'an, the one giving 18 m by 6 m and the other 20 m by 10 m, and neither says which survey it follows.

The second form is a broad sheet of water rather than a ditch. Xiangyang's moat reaches 250 m at its widest, 130 to 250 m wide and only 2 to 3 m deep; Guide's - the old prefectural city at Shangqiu - lies 3.5 m outside the wall and runs from 25 m wide at its narrowest to 500 m at its widest, 1 to 5 m deep. These are lakes round a city more than trenches, and shallower than the dug ditch. What made these two so wide is on no page read; that it follows the ground a city stands on, low and wet beside a great river, is this page's GUESS.

The rule the map follows: the moat's width is a DEGREE set by the city's standing, within the band the measured ditches give - about 13 ft at the lesser end, 55 to 100 ft at the great cities. That a county seat takes the lesser end and a provincial seat the greater is this page's calibration from the seven cities read, whose ranks no page read states. A provincial seat's moat drawn about 66 ft across therefore sits inside the band the great cities give. Its depth, 10 to 20 ft, is not drawn. Beside the ditch there is a second FORM, the broad water moat, shallow and anywhere from about 80 ft to more than 1,600 ft across (Xiangyang's about 430 to 820 ft), and a city's moat is rolled between the two from its seed. How often the broad form is rolled is a GUESS - two of the seven cities read carry one - and so is where it may be rolled; this page keeps it to a city on low ground beside a river.
