• wengcheng-zhwiki - 「In the Wujing zongyao written by Zeng Gongliang, an account of the barbican appears for the first time」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「its barbican outside the wall is round or square, made to suit the lie of the ground, its height and thickness equal to the wall's, with only one gate opened off to one side, left or right as is convenient」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (quoting the Wujing zongyao) ... 「The barbican's gate is usually not in a straight line with the city gate it protects, so that the attackers must turn their siege engines another way rather than drive straight in」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • wengcheng-zhwiki - 「Among the walled cities of ancient China, the building of barbicans flourished in the Five Dynasties and the Northern Song.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「In the Yuan, to keep the Chinese from holding cities in revolt, orders were given everywhere to tear down city walls」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「and so neither Yuan Dadu nor Yuan Shangdu had barbicans」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「Only after the peasant risings of the late Yuan did Emperor Shun hastily build barbicans at Dadu.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「The Ming gave weight to the defense of cities: at Yingtian prefecture in Nanjing, at the Ming middle capital, Fengyang prefecture, at Shuntian prefecture in Beijing, and at local cities of prefecture, department and county rank such as Xi'an, Guide (today's Shangqiu in Henan) and Pingyao」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「all set up barbicans」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ); and 「there is no record of barbicans being set at Southern Song Lin'an or at the Upper Capitals of the Liao and Jin」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • pingyao-chengqiang-zhwiki - 「Of the six gates, there is one each on the south and north, and two each on the east and west; outside the east and west gates are built attached barbicans」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • zhenhai-wall-zhwiki - 「In the early Ming six land gates were set; in the thirteenth year of Yongle (1415) the north gate was blocked, leaving five」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「outside each were set a barbican and a drawbridge (the Xiaonan gate's barbican being added in the twenty-ninth year of Hongwu, 1396)」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • shouxian-wall-zhwiki - 「The Shou county wall has four gates」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) ... 「and the barbicans of the four gates are still preserved.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • sohu-xian-wall-archive - 「Taking the south gate's barbican as the example: 70 meters east to west, 50 meters north to south, 3,500 square meters in area; in ancient times it could hold three thousand soldiers」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; )
  • koguchi-jawiki - 「a square space is set in front of the koguchi, with gates or openings built there two deep, so that even if the attackers get inside the masugata they are stopped from entering the castle by the second gate, and inside the masugata take the defenders' attack from every side」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「Masugata come as the inner masugata (uchi-masugata) and the outer masugata (soto-masugata). The inner masugata makes a small square space on the inner side of the enclosure's koguchi and builds a second gate.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「The outer masugata thrusts a small square space, continuous with the ground, out beyond the koguchi of the main enclosure, and opens one more gate at its very front.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「the basic form is thought to be the front gate a koraimon and the gate behind it a yaguramon, but there are also those with only the rear gate and no front gate, and those with only the front gate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • otemon-jawiki - 「it was built heavily for defense, and often has a large yaguramon and forms a masugata with stone walls and the like」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • akasaka-mitsuke-jawiki - 「A mitsuke is a watched castle gate set mainly on a castle's outer enclosure to spot an enemy's advance or entry; Edo castle is said to have had 36 mitsuke along its outer and inner moats.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「Akasaka mitsuke took the form of a masugata gate, the same as the Tayasu gate, the Shimizu gate and others.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ... 「Akasaka mitsuke is a castle gate set on the outer moat」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
