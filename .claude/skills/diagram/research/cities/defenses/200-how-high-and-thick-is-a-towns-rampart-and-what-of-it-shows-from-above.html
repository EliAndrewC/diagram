How high and thick is a town's rampart, and what of it shows from above?

Sources: pingyao-chengqiang-zhwiki (a county wall's height, width, facing, towers and gates), shanghai-xiancheng-chengqiang-zhwiki (a county wall raised in a hurry, then heightened and partly refaced in stone), zhenhai-wall-zhwiki (a county wall's height and its thickness at base and top), shouxian-wall-zhwiki (a four-gated town), sohu-xian-wall-archive (the parapets on a wall's top), dorui-jawiki (the Japanese earth bank: height, parts, planting), sogamae-jawiki (Kyoto's great bank and its moat)

A county wall on the Chinese model. Three county walls read on public pages span the range. Pingyao's, rebuilt at scale in 1370, stands about 12 m (39 ft) high, averages 3.5 m (11.5 ft) wide and runs 6.4 km round. Zhenhai's Ming wall was 2 zhang 4 chi, about 7.8 m (26 ft), high, about 3.2 m (10.5 ft) thick at the base and about 2.56 m (8.4 ft) on top. Shanghai's, thrown up in 1553 against pirates, was first only 1 zhang 5 chi high - about 16 ft at the 3.2 m zhang the Zhenhai page itself uses (this page's conversion) - and stood at 2 zhang 4 chi once later magistrates had raised it. That is the band the record already gives for most county walls, 4 to 10 m, with Pingyao a little above it (see was a county seat walled). How high and thick a provincial city's wall stands is under gate structures - real footprints, from Xi'an's.

Earth inside, and sometimes a skin. Pingyao's wall is earth inside, faced on the outside with lime-set masonry and paved on top with gray brick, with a ramp for horses and drains on its inner side. Shanghai's was rammed earth in wooden forms, and only later did two magistrates replace one long stretch of it with large stone. So a county wall may be bare rammed earth or earth with a masonry face, and both are attested.

What shows from above. The flat top, with a crenellated parapet along its outer edge and a low parapet without crenels along its inner one - so named at Xi'an; Pingyao counts over 3,000 crenels. Pingyao's wall also carries a projecting platform (a mamian) with a tower on it every 50 m (about 165 ft), 72 in all, a corner tower at each of its four corners, and six gates, each with its gate tower. The count of gates varies: Pingyao and Shanghai six (Shanghai with three water gates besides), Shou county four. Whether an ordinary county wall carried towers at Pingyao's spacing is on no page read; Pingyao is a famous and well-kept example.

The Japanese earth bank. A Japanese town that enclosed itself did it with a dorui, an earth bank. On level ground a bank is paired with a ditch and often built of the ditch's own spoil; it stands about 2 m (6.5 ft) high at the least and about 3 m (10 ft) at the most, measured from inside; its flat top is wide enough for men and horses, and a wall or fence set on it leaves a walk inside and a ledge outside. Its faces were held with turf, bamboo grass or bamboo, and sometimes a low course of stone at the foot. Kyoto's great bank is the outsize case: bank and moat each about 20 m (66 ft) wide. How wide a small town's bank was at the base is on no page read.

The rule the map follows: a walled county town on the Chinese model rolls its wall's height between about 16 and 33 ft - a continuum from Shanghai's first wall to the top of the band most county walls kept, Pingyao's 39 ft standing above it - and draws its footprint about 10 to 12 ft thick, the two measured county walls. Whether its face is bare rammed earth or masonry over earth is a knob rolled per town; from above both read as a flat walk with a toothed outer edge and a plain inner one, and a masonry face may be drawn a shade lighter than bare earth (a drawing convention). It has four to six gates. It may carry projecting towers at about 165 ft, as Pingyao does, and a tower at each corner; the tower spacing is Pingyao's and how general it was is a GUESS. A town on the Japanese model that encloses itself draws an earth bank 6.5 to 10 ft high with a fence or wall along its top and its ditch outside it; the bank's width at the base is drawn about 20 ft, a GUESS.
