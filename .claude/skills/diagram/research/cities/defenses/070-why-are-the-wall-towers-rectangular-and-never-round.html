Why are the wall towers rectangular and never round?

Sources: sinoss-cao-city-defense (what the wall's projections are for), jah-song-military-cities (the Song corner tower, curved on purpose), xian-wall-zhwiki (three square corners and one round one at Xi'an), qq-xian-southwest-corner (the other dating of that round corner), fortified-tower-enwiki, castle-enwiki, mengxi-bitan-wikisource

Along the curtain, rectangular - and the reason is the defender's volume of fire. A mamian is a platform built one after another against the outer face of the wall, so placed that a besieger at the foot of it is struck from two sides at once, and the spurs stand about a bowshot apart so that no stretch of the wall's base is out of reach. A long straight-sided spur lets archers shoot straight down along the wall's foot - the sources attribute the effect to the spur's length and projection, its two sides looking straight down on the foot of the wall - and that a curved face does worse is this page's reading.

The corner is the other case, and it goes the other way. A study of the Northern Song military compendium finds the dituan - the tower standing where the wall turns, or at the angle of a barbican - deliberately arc-shaped, made so "in order to increase the difficulty of the enemy's attack and to reduce the area exposed to attack", a form its author finds in no Han or Tang text and takes to be a Song creation. That is the reason Europe gives for its own round towers, reached separately, so a curved tower is not a European signature, and a round corner on a Chinese wall does not by itself say "an older piece of wall". Xi'an is the case that looked as though it did: three of its four corner towers are square and the southwest one is round, following the building that stood on the spot before it. Even there the century is disputed - the encyclopedia account puts the inherited round platform in the Yuan, a popular one in the imperial city of Sui-Tang Chang'an, and the two agree only that a later rebuild went around an older corner.

Japanese yagura and tenshu are square or rectangular timber on stone bases, and the one thing Japan curves is the batter of the ishigaki wall rather than the towers standing on it. No readable page says so.

The rule the map follows: every wall and gate tower draws as a RECTANGLE. The mamian spur is wider along the wall than deep, about 1.7 to 1; the tower building set on it, and the gate towers, run nearer square, so no ratio is forced there. The CORNER is where the drawn map and the record part company: the record finds a Song city's corner towers arc-shaped on purpose, at the turn of the wall and at a barbican's angle, and these maps square every corner along with the rest of the ring. No map draws a round tower.
