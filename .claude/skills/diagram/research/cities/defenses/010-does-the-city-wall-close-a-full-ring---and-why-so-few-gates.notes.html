  • wengcheng-zhwiki - 「A wengcheng is a small gate-protecting walled enclosure, semicircular or square, built outside (...) a city gate in order to strengthen the defense of a fortress or of a strategic pass」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; , the elision a parenthetical on Nanjing's Zhonghua gate as the exception built inside the gate) (the barbican court outside the gate)
  • wujing-zongyao-wikisource - 「Make four gates, two open and two shut.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ); chengchi-zhwiki - 「Ordinarily a prefectural city had 8 gates and a county city opened 4 gates. They were usually set on the four quarters: east, west, south and north.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (few, counted openings, the encyclopedia beside it giving 「Ordinarily a prefectural city had 8 gates and a county city opened 4 gates」; 「held twice over」 is this page's reasoning)
  • greatmingmilitary-fortification-gate - 「The neck prevents enemy troops from storming the gate en masse, as moving a large body of troops across such a narrow passage in a short time is all but impossible, although this also prevents the defenders from sortie out to meet their enemies head on.」 and 「On the other hand, the Weng Cheng is an enclosing wall designed to delay enemy assault and, whenever a chance presents itself, lure a portion of enemy troops inside, separate them from the main body of the army, and annihilate them. As such, it is much more spacious than barbican, allowing more troops to get in or out at once. It can also serve as a protected staging ground for the defenders that want to engage their enemies outside the walls.」 (English. The first passage is about the European barbican's neck, the second about the wengcheng; between them they make the contrast the entry draws. A specialist blog on Ming military technology, self-published rather than peer-reviewed.)
  • suzhou-panmen-gov - 「The land gate of Panmen likewise has two layers, inner and outer, and between them is a wengcheng roughly square in plan, about 177 meters in inner perimeter, the wall 8.1 meters high, with dressed stone as its base and city brick laid above.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (177 m of inner perimeter is about 580 ft and 8.1 m about 27 ft. 平面略成方形 is "roughly square in plan": the urn is in the name and not in the shape.)
  • karamete-kotobank - 「The rear gate of a castle. The rear side of the enemy. Opposed to ote, oute.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the dictionary defines the karamete as the rear gate and marks it the express opposite of the main front; its earliest attestation there is of 1235. It says nothing of the umon, the shuimen or the European sally port.)
  • touken-world-umon - 「An umon is, among the gates of a Japanese castle, a gate quietly provided so that the enemy will not notice that it is an entrance. It is set up for such purposes as being the route by which the lord of the castle escapes.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「in the case of an umon it is of a plain form, made as if hollowed out under the stone wall or the moat. So that it could be kept impassable except when needed, it was built to be easy to bury.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (a curated commercial glossary of castle terms - publicly readable, and consistent with the surviving examples it names at Takamatsu and Nagoya, but tertiary.)
  • suzhou-panmen-gov - 「Panmen is easy to defend and hard to attack; its water gate and land gate stand side by side, and it is an important military defensive structure of the old city. The water gate of Panmen is composed of two layers of city gate, inner and outer, 4.6 meters apart, with a depth of 24.5 meters.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (24.5 m is about 80 ft. The water gate is doubled inner and outer like a land gate, which is what makes it a grand gate rather than a postern.)
  • wengcheng-zhwiki - 「shaped like a "weng" (a large jar with a small mouth and a wide belly), hence the name」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (the name is the jar's; the same sentence gives the court's plan as semicircular or square)
