How far does an archer on the wall reach?

Sources: tongdian-157-wikisource (the ranges at which Tang archers and crossbowmen loosed), cass-ancient-chi (the length of the pace and the Tang foot), shoucheng-lu-juan2-wikisource (the old rule of a mamian every sixty paces)

The one range read comes from the Tongdian's drill for a Tang army in the field: the crossbowmen loose at 150 paces from the enemy, the archers at 60, and within 20 paces both put down bow and crossbow for the sword and the staff. A pace was five of the builder's feet in the old reckoning, and a Tang foot about 30.7 cm, so the archers' sixty paces is about 92 m (302 ft) and the crossbowmen's 150 about 230 m (755 ft) - the arithmetic this page's, and the five-foot pace is given for "the old reckoning" in the builder's foot, not for the Tang (the same passage gives six feet to the pace under the Qin dynasty), so applying it to a Tang drill is this page's assumption. Those are the ranges at which the drill has men begin to shoot, not the reach at which an arrow is sure to kill, and no page read gives that.

The same sixty paces turns up on the wall. The Song Shoucheng Lu gives the old rule for the projecting towers: a mamian every sixty paces, standing out no less than two zhang from the wall, its two sides looking straight down at the foot of the wall. A wall towered to that rule has its towers the archers' opening range apart, so every point of the curtain lies within that range of a tower on either side. Neither text names the other, and that the one number was chosen for the other is this page's reading.

The rule the map follows: no change to what is drawn. Of the three defense settings at Wall towers - the mamian system and bowshot ranges, the GARRISON setting's reach of 328 ft (100 m) sits within a tenth of the Tang archers' 302 ft and is taken as attested. The SIEGE setting's 197 ft (60 m) is shorter than any range read; it stays, labeled as a calibration, because the spacing it produces is Pingyao's measured 50 m and not a figure inferred from a bow.
