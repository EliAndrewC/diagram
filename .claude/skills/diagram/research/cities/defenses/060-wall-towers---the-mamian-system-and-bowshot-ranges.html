Wall towers - the mamian system and bowshot ranges

Sources: xian-wall-zhwiki (the ~120 m mamian spacing, tied to bow and firearm range), ditai-zhwiki, mengxi-bitan-wikisource, pingyao-chengqiang-zhwiki, jah-song-military-cities, tang-changan-zhwiki (the guarded gate of a walled ward), panmen-zhwiki (the water gate's grating)

Flanking fire from both sides is the design purpose the sources give the projecting wall tower, and at Xi'an the towers stand within bow and firearm range of one another. Adjacent mamian bastions are spaced so their fields of flanking fire overlap - an attacker at the curtain base between two towers is shot at from BOTH (Shen Kuo's eleventh-century Mengxi Bitan describes it as 「the arrows and stones reach one another」 (translated; original: 「矢石相及」)). The spacing that puts two towers within reach of every point therefore equals the effective arrow range: put towers a bowshot apart and every curtain point has two within a bowshot. Real spacings bracket the effective ranges: Pingyao 50 m as the encyclopedia article states, though its 72 towers on a 6.4 km ring average more, and Xi'an ~120 m, the sparser ring. How far a bow reached from the wall is its own question, How far does an archer on the wall reach? - about 90 m, sixty paces, by the one figure read.

The rule the map follows: how densely a city towers its wall is a per-city choice, because fortification followed THREAT rather than a universal constant: the barbican was at first a frontier city's device, and reached the interior cities only when the Northern Song set out to strengthen their defenses. That a border city was towered as hard as it could afford and a city long at peace was not is this page's reading. That a region's raids drove its cities to add towers is attested, and is weighed at Did a city that had seen war carry more towers?; that the count rose in proportion to the wars is this page's calibration. Three settings, each naming how far a tower's fire reaches and how many towers must cover every point of the curtain. A SIEGE city: two towers within 197 ft (60 m, this page's calibration), so bastions stand about 60 m apart and the wall wears a dense necklace of them, which is the correct read for a border fortress. A GARRISON city, the default: two towers within 328 ft (100 m, calibrated against the archers' range at How far does an archer on the wall reach?), a looser ring. A PEACEFUL city: one flanking tower within 197 ft everywhere, the midpoints between towers still covered by two, which comes out at roughly 394 ft (120 m) between towers. The spacings all fall inside those the sources record, from 50 m at Pingyao to 120 m at Xi'an. Of the ranges they are calibrated against, the GARRISON's 100 m sits about a tenth above the Tang archers' sixty paces; the SIEGE setting's 60 m is shorter than any range read, and stands as a calibration against Pingyao's 50 m spacing rather than as a range.

The rule the map follows: coverage is judged along the whole curtain, about every 54 ft, and a tower counts as reaching 36 ft past its recorded center, because an archer shoots from the bastion's parapet SPAN and not from a point. Four stretches are left out of that reckoning, each because it is already the most defended ground on the wall and no tower is seated there: within 390 ft of a gate, within 165 ft of a gate's guard buildings, within 135 ft of a water gate's opening - a grated passage flanked by its own two towers - and within 186 ft of the gate of a walled ward where the ward's fence meets the rampart, because a ward gate was a guarded one. That a ward's fence ends on the rampart at all is the map's form, a guess against the Tang one, weighed at Where does a neighborhood fence stop when it reaches the rampart? Those exemptions MIRROR where towers are seated, deliberately: ground that will structurally never be towered must not be ground the coverage rule demands, or no map can ever satisfy it. Beneath all of it sits a coarse floor - at least six towers, and no bare stretch spanning more than about 75 degrees of the ring. The towers also keep a RHYTHM: no gap between two open-curtain bastions falls below seven tenths of that map's own median gap, the flanking pairs at gates and water gates excepted, because mamian stand at regular intervals and a doubled-up pair reads as a mistake. And each tower is turned SQUARE to the wall where it stands, within about 15 degrees: a tower on a 30 degree stretch slants 30 degrees rather than sitting as an axis-aligned box.
