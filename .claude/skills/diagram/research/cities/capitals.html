Domain capitals: the research behind the castle-town tier

The findings behind the castle, the government avenue, the wards, the water and the wharf of a domain capital on these maps, the decisions they drove, and - where no generator draws the feature yet - the rules a map follows.

At the capital tier Japan leads and China is the tiebreaker

Sources: yamen-enwiki, columbia-qing-state, jokamachi-jawiki

The standing rule is China first, Japan as tiebreaker. The capital tier inverts it, deliberately.

The reason is that the subject is a daimyo's castle town, which is a Japanese institution.234 The Chinese prefectural seat has a yamen, and sometimes a walled inner citadel, but it has no tenshu, no rank-graded concentric samurai rings, and no teramachi rim - because imperial China had no resident hereditary military aristocracy to house.116 Its local officials were rotating appointees who lived in the yamen.117

Where China does have something to say the finding still uses it, and the two agree more often than the inversion suggests - see the nested-citadel and ministry-siting entries below, both of which the two traditions agree on. What Japan supplies is the FORM of the tier.

This disturbs nothing below it. A village, a county town and a provincial city are all administrative and agrarian settlements where the Chinese reality genuinely is the better guide, and they keep the standing order.

Both traditions nest a walled citadel in the seat, so a CENTERED castle is the median form

Sources: jokamachi-wiki-corpus, baidu-yangzhou-city-site, mnwhstq-quanzhou-walls, sogamae-jawiki

Japan. A jokamachi was established4 "with the lord's castle in the center," and the town was zoned in concentric rings by status, closeness to the castle tracking rank:5

  • samurai innermost6, "retainers with higher position had a closer location to the castle" (surviving place names Sange, Kamiyashiki-cho)
  • ashigaru outside the townsmen district (Ban-cho, Teppo-cho)100
  • merchants and artisans outside the samurai town, segregated by trade (Gofuku-machi drapers, Kaji-machi smiths)7
  • teramachi at the outer rim, where8 "spacious temples formed part of the city defenses"

Two further details drive rules of their own: main roads were deliberately routed past the castle's front rather than its rear,9 "to indicate the glory of the ruler"; and total enclosure of the whole town (sogamae - moat plus earth mounds) became increasingly common, Odawara and Osaka being the exemplars.10 The sogamae is what makes our single-rampart capital honest: a castle town that walls its whole population is an attested form, not a convenience.233

China. The prefectural seat nested the same way - yacheng (the administrative city) inside zicheng (the inner city) inside luocheng (the outer city).145 Tang Yangzhou's luocheng was the residential and commercial city, its zicheng the seat of the offices.118

The decision. The centered castle is not an import we are choosing; it is what both traditions do, so the centered castle is the DEFAULT and the edge castle is the variant.

The EDGE castle is real, and it comes with water

Sources: okayama-castle, jokamachi-jawiki, kitsuki-castle-jawiki, kyu-shokyoto-kitsuki

Where the castle sits on a flank rather than in the middle, a river or the sea does the defensive work on that side in each case below:194

  • Okayama - Ukita Hideie diverted a branch of the Asahi River to serve as the moat along the castle's northeastern flank.11
  • Kitsuki - the castle stands on a promontory between the mouths of the Yasaka and Takayama rivers where they open into Morie Bay, with the samurai quarters on the high ground to north and south and the merchants' town in the valley between them.119

The decision. The edge mode is COUPLED to water. A dry inland capital takes the ring; a river or coastal capital may take either, and if it takes the edge, the castle's own outer moat becomes that stretch of the city's perimeter rather than a second line inside it. A castle on a dry edge is refused - it is not a variant, it is a weak wall.

A median castle is ~85% of an entire provincial city

Anchor Daimyo Total enceinte Notes
Hirosaki Tsugaru, 47,000 koku13 ~50 ha / 123 acres includes the moats; the keep itself ~0.6 ha. The hectare figures are the park's own and are not on the linked article, which gives the outline as 612 by 947 m (about 58 ha); they are a GUESS checked against that outline
Himeji Ikeda, ~520,000 koku193 233 ha / 576 acres, 4,200 m circumference14 107 ha inside the middle moat;15 950-1,600 m E-W by 900-1,700 m N-S16

Himeji also anchors the moat glyph: average width 20 m (66 ft), max 34.5 m, depth ~2.7 m.17 Our provincial-city moat default is 78 ft, so the same glyph carries to the castle's own moat unchanged.

The decision. Take Hirosaki's ~50 ha (123.5 acres, 5.38M sq ft) as the MEDIAN capital's castle.

Tango's entire walled interior is about 59 ha, so a median castle is ~85% of a whole provincial city. This is the single most consequential number in the capital budget, and it is why a capital's wall cannot be sized by population alone: the ring encloses roughly four provincial cities of inhabitants plus most of a fifth in castle.

The castle's area is therefore declared per city, defaulting to ~50 ha with the 50-230 ha band documented - so a grand old Clan seat can declare a Himeji and a poor frontier house can declare less.

THE KEEP IS NOT THE CASTLE. The ~50 ha is the park's own figure for the whole enceinte, every bailey plus all three moats (the original castle was 38.5 ha). Hirosaki's tenshu, a three-story turret standing in for a keep, has a first-floor plan of 5 by 6 ken - about 0.01 ha, a fraction of a percent of the works.192 Any reasoning that treats "the keep" as the large thing is measuring the wrong object - which is exactly the error the ministry-siting question below turned on.

Our capital is a Hikone-scale market town carrying a quarter of Hikone's samurai

Sources: hikone-castle-town

Hikone (Ii, 290,000 koku after 1615101) counted 15,371 townspeople in 53 separate wards in the 1695 census - chonin only, not the samurai.18 Our capital holds ~10,800 non-samurai inhabitants against ~1,560 samurai. So the commercial town is ~70% of Hikone's, while the samurai burden is far lighter.

That is exactly the "deliberately restrained echo" the setting's canon claims - Hikone counted 15,371 townspeople in 1695 against an estimated 20,000 warriors, the warriors being exempt from the census,19 and Rokugan's capitals at ~13% "are far more commercial." The consequence for the map is that we cannot copy a jokamachi's proportions: ours is a merchant city with a castle in it, not a garrison with a market attached. The samurai rings are real but thin; the machi are the bulk of the fabric.

Hikone's 53 wards is also the anchor for how many named machi a capital carries.20

NEITHER tradition walls its wards: the answer is a MESH of night-barred gates

Sources: edo-machi-kido, qing-zhalan, jokamachi-zoning, lifang-zhwiki, wugou-lifang-rujia, worksinprogress-samurai-city, daqing-luli-yejin, visitbeijing-zhalan, roji-jawiki, kotobank-kido, bjd-zhonggulou

The GM's instinct (2026-08-08) was that one large sealed samurai neighborhood is probably wrong and many smaller wards more likely, and asked for real research before deciding. The research says the instinct is right about the first half and the second half is still not quite the shape of it. What both traditions actually have is not smaller enclosures but a dense mesh of small gates barred at night, hung across street and lane mouths, with no continuous fence at all.191

Japan (Edo). Every machi (町) block was closed by a kido, open from about 4 in the morning until about 10 at night.21 Below that, every nagaya tenement row and every roji lane had its own roji-kido / nagaya-kido, locked at roughly 6 pm and reopened around 6 am, with the keys held by the nagaya owner or by the month's duty-holder.143 The system was self-governing - the block was collectively responsible for its own gate - and it is credited with much of Edo's night-time public order.144

China (Ming/Qing). The Tang walled-ward (fang) system was torn down by the Song, and the ward walls were not rebuilt - the system was gone after the Song and Yuan.120 What replaced them is the same answer Edo reached: Qing Beijing built zhalan (栅栏, palings) closing each street at night against thieves - the commercial street Dashilan (大栅栏,22 "Big Palings") is named for its gate and still carries the name. It was backed by a real curfew: the Great Qing Code's night-prohibition article sets thirty blows of the light bamboo for being abroad after the first watch's bell falls silent and before the fifth watch's stirs, fifty for the second to fourth watches (roughly 9 pm to 3 am), a degree less in the provincial cities and towns, and the palings were manned every night.142

Two traditions, no contact, same institution. That is the strongest corroboration on this page.

And the samurai quarter is not ONE quarter. The jokamachi pattern was for chonin to live in wards forming narrow strips that SEPARATE different groups of samurai, those strips running along the major thoroughfares where their trades were most useful to everyone.23 Within the buke-chi, the separation that exists is per-compound - each estate ringed by its own wall with one or two gates - not a district palisade.141 So a castle town has several samurai districts interleaved with commercial strips, each samurai plot walled in its own right.

What this implies for the tier.

  1. No continuous ward fence at capital scale. Replace it with kido at the block and lane mouths - a glyph the maps already draw, at a far higher count and a different placement rule.
  2. Several samurai districts, separated by commoner strips on the thoroughfares - not one contiguous sealed quarter.
  3. The walled compound does the sealing, which dovetails with the inverted no-manor-inside-the-wall rule: a capital's senior retainers live in walled yashiki, and those walls ARE the boundary that a fence was standing in for.
  4. The bell-and-drum tower acquires a documented job. We already draw one; the curfew above is what it is FOR - it sounds the hour the gates close and the hour they open.190

The honest tension, flagged rather than buried. The provincial tier currently seals its samurai/government quarter with a continuous palisade, justified by the same observation that the Tang fang system does not apply. This research says the continuous fence is more than history supports even there.

The capital's samurai are SENIOR-heavy, which INVERTS the provincial housing mix

Sources: kanra-ashigaru-nagaya; the setting's own budget notes, "Samurai rank distribution", ashigaru-jawiki, bukeyashiki-wiki

Working the capital column of the rank table in the setting's own budget notes, against the provincial column:

R5+ (senior) R1-4 (junior)
Capital (800 working) 563 - 70% 237 - 30%
Provincial city (225 working) 61 - 27% 164 - 73%

The mix does not scale, it inverts. The setting's own budget notes say so in words too - the capital "is staffed with the senior cohort," and "low-rank samurai compose a lower proportion of retainers in the capital than in the provinces" - because a capital posting is prestigious even when the job is menial, and because the capital absorbs the rank-by-association cohort (the Doctrine of Three Steps puts the daimyo's kin at Rank 9+ holding no office at all).

The consequence for the map. The provincial housing rule wants a MINORITY of large senior houses among many small junior ones, which is right for a provincial city at 27% senior. At capital scale that flips: roughly ~218 senior against ~94 junior households of the ~312 total, so large houses and walled yashiki are the MAJORITY of the samurai fabric and the small houses are the minority.

Why there is no ashigaru quarter. The historical kumi-yashiki pattern - dense uniform rows of low-rank warrior housing, "ashigaru terraces" - would seem to belong in a capital.203 It does not, twice over:

  • Wrong caste. The setting's own budget notes are explicit that in Rokugan ashigaru are PEASANTS, not samurai - "the original L5R usage, which departs from historical Japan, where ashigaru formed the lowest stratum of the samurai class." The historical kumi-yashiki housed a domain's lowest-ranking retainers, the ashigaru among them standing below full samurai status, so the institution has no Rokugani counterpart under that name.202
  • Wrong place, and wrong quantity. The setting's canon puts ashigaru in the villages - about 10% of farmers, licensed or unlicensed, trained by the county magistrate. They are rural peasant militia, not a resident urban caste, so a capital has no ashigaru quarter to draw. And the slot they would have filled is the one the rank table shows is smallest here, not largest.

What genuinely occupies that structural position is a retainer terrace: modest housing for the capital's ~94 junior (Rank 1-4) samurai households - castle guards, household retainers of the daimyo's retinue, and junior officials in training. A real feature, correctly named, and a minority texture rather than a dominant one.

The aqueduct is OPEN outside the wall and BURIED inside it - and the boundary is the gate

Sources: edo-josui, kanda-kakehi, mizu-no-bunka-mokuhi, ktjplus-josui-ido, nippon-com-suidobashi, tamagawa-aqueduct-enwiki, edo-tokyo-museum-tamagawa, tokyo-waterworks-tamagawa-rekishi

The GM's preference (2026-08-08) is for an above-ground aqueduct where the history allows one, because it makes a better map - but asked for the possibility space first. The space is real and generous, and it has a sharp boundary in a surprising place.

The Edo josui is two systems with the city gate between them:

  • OUTSIDE, an open channel. The Tamagawa Josui runs ~43 km from Hamura to the Yotsuya Gate (Yotsuya Okido24) as an open earth cut - an earth canal, largely above ground, terminating AT a gate of the city.140
  • CROSSING a watercourse, an open flume on a bridge - the kakehi (懸樋). Where the Kanda Josui had to cross the Kanda River at Ochanomizu it was carried over on a flume;25 the bridge downstream is called Suidōbashi, "aqueduct bridge," for exactly that reason, because the bridge once carried the conduit - and Hiroshige drew the bridge with the Kanda Jōsui beneath it in his One Hundred Famous Views of Edo.139 This is the single most visually striking element in the whole system and it is fully attested.
  • INSIDE, buried conduit. Within the city the water runs in stone (sekihi) and wooden (mokuhi) pipes laid underground26 - 67 km of them for the Kanda system alone and a further 84.7 km for the Tamagawa - feeding thousands of draw-wells (jōsui ido; one count for all of Edo-Tokyo runs to 8,000) and cisterns from which residents drew as they needed.138 The wooden pipe work was specialist enough that ship's carpenters were the trade that laid it.27

No arcades. There is no East Asian equivalent of the Roman arcaded aqueduct.137 The vocabulary is: gravity canal at grade, buried pipe, and a flume bridge only where a watercourse must be crossed.201 Do not draw arches - that is the one form the possibility space excludes.

Why the inside is buried (our inference; no source says it): a dense wooden city needs its street surface, and open water threaded through the fabric is both an obstruction and a contamination problem. The boundary is functional, not arbitrary.

The decision. Draw it above ground wherever history put it above ground - which is most of its interesting length, and all of its drama:

  1. the intake works on the river,
  2. the open approach canal running to the wall,
  3. a kakehi flume where it crosses a watercourse, if the geography gives us one,
  4. its terminus at a gate.

Inside the wall the system is honestly buried, and it is represented by what a resident would actually see: its draw-points. Those are wells, which we already draw - so the recommendation is a distinguishable aqueduct-fed draw-basin rather than a fake surface channel. The existing well network does not go away; the aqueduct is laid on top of it.

The wharf is the COLLECTING end, and "kurayashiki" is the wrong word for it

Sources: osaka-kurayashiki, asakusa-kuramae, kurayashiki-jawiki, kotobank-horokusei

The word first. A kurayashiki (蔵屋敷) is a daimyo's warehouse-and-residence at the market - Osaka, around Nakanoshima,28 more than 110 of them at the early-1800s peak (a figure the exchange's page as quoted does not carry - a GUESS until a page does) - where tax rice was auctioned to brokers against tradeable rice bills. By the 1670s eighty domains kept kurayashiki warehouse compounds there, and by the 1840s 125.136 That is the SELLING end, and a domain capital is not it. Calling Shiro Daika's waterfront a kurayashiki district would be wrong twice: wrong word, and wrong economic role.

What a domain capital actually holds. Peasants pay tax rice up to the domain granary, and the domain pays its samurai their stipends out of it (kuramai, the rice stipend).121 So the capital is the collecting-and-disbursing end: rice arrives from the provinces, most of it goes straight back out as stipends, and the surplus ships downriver to the market.

The model to copy is Asakusa Okura / Kuramae. The shogunate's own rice granaries stood on the Sumida, and the district in front of them is literally named Kuramae (蔵前),30 "before the storehouses." There sat the fudasashi - brokers who warehoused stipend rice for a fee, converted it to cash, and lent against it,31 becoming rich enough that the theaters and geisha houses of neighboring Asakusa grew up on their money.32

That gives the capital a chain of three features linked by one mechanism, which is the sort of thing that makes a map read as a real place:

river wharf -> the domain granary -> the brokers' row in front of it -> the entertainment district next door

The divergence from Edo, which was the GM's call. In Edo the arbitrage between rice and coin made chonin brokers rich. In L7R, the setting's own budget notes hand that income to the Ministry of Retainers - "~1,600 (rice/coin arbitrage on capital stipend throughput of ~28,000)" - a samurai ministry, not a merchant street. A brokers' row that is a ministry annex rather than a commercial quarter weakens the Edo causal chain from broker wealth to the entertainment district; the two can coexist (the ministry takes the denomination cut; merchant brokers still trade), but which one the map depicts changes what is drawn there. The GM ruled it merchant - see "The brokers' row is MERCHANT" below.

The Emperor's granaries are separate, and their siting is not in the sources. The setting's own budget notes give the Imperial Magistrate a ~450-koku line for "local Imperial granary supervision," with "separate granary staff, materials, and operations," so they are distinct stores under a foreign authority - but nothing in the sources says where a capital would put them. Adjacent to the Imperial Magistrate's compound, or with their own water access, are both plausible, and the two became a knob - see "The Emperor's granaries are separate" below.

Per-household ground costs for the two housing types the budget model has never seen

Sources: fukui-bushi-jutaku, shibata-ashigaru-nagaya - both already cited by government.html, matsue-bukeyashiki-about

The walled samurai compound inside the wall. The anchor is already in our own research: the Fukui archive's Suganuma plan (a 1,000-koku retainer, 1839) is a 28 x 32.5 ken plot33 = ~167 x 194 ft, about 0.30 ha. That is a PLOT, so it already contains its own yard - the walled perimeter IS the boundary, so it carries far less shared-margin overhead than a detached house. Applying only a modest ~1.15x for street margin gives a walled samurai compound of about 0.35 ha (0.86 acre).

Sanity checks against things we already draw: 1.7x an ordinary in-wall samurai house's gross (right for a chancellor against a bushi), 1.3x the drawn merchant-estate court (0.59 acre - right for a chancellor against a very rich merchant), and comfortably under the extramural country manor (~1 acre), which has land a city plot does not.

The retainer terrace. The anchor is likewise ours already: Shibata's Important Cultural Property (ICP) ashigaru-nagaya is 8 households under one roof,34 143 x 24 ft (a depth of 7.3 m), 18 ft of frontage each35 = 378 sq ft per household. But that is the lowest stratum, and our retainer terrace houses Rank 1-4 samurai - poor, but a band reaching 16 koku at the top. It should therefore sit above the historical ashigaru unit and below the detached samurai house (2,322 sq ft drawn, which matches the Matsue residence's 67 tsubo (~220 m^2) almost exactly, though its households were retainers of 500 to 1,000 koku, well above our terrace's band).240 Taking ~990 sq ft drawn (just above our laborer row house at 891) at a row-housing gross-up gives a retainer terrace of about 5,900 sq ft gross (0.14 acre).

Confidence, stated honestly. Both figures are settled and in use as the tier's ground costs; both SIZE anchors behind them are attested and measured. The two derived figures are interpolations, and the terrace figure is the softer of the two - it is bracketed by real numbers at both ends but its position between them is a judgment. The compound figure rests directly on a measured plan and is firmer.

What it makes the wall. Pricing the capital inventory with these rows - 2,160 packed households, a samurai cohort split by rank into ~60 walled yashiki / ~160 detached / ~94 terraced, the ~50 ha castle, ~15 ha of expanded civic program, and the 7% circulation fraction - gives a required interior of ~270 ha and a derived ring of roughly 1.2 x 1.1 miles across, a ~3.6 mile circuit.

And two of the provincial figures do not survive the tier. A capital's packed quarter is not a provincial city's packed quarter, and the difference is a decision as much as a measurement. A capital's drawn fabric comes out at about 0.28 acre of gross ground per packed family - estates, works and dojos embedded in the quarters, generous courts - so pricing its rampart from the provincial 0.14 acre under-built the wall by some 40% and spilled more than half the cohort into suburbs the extramural ruling does not allow. The capital's packed household is therefore priced at about 0.20 acre gross, a packed-tight target between the provincial figure and that first loose draw, and the drawn fabric has to MEET it rather than merely be measured against it. Circulation is the other one: a capital carries a street mesh, band lanes, block alleys, the ceremonial and rear approach systems and forty-odd compound halos, measured at about a fifth of the interior on that first draw and circulation declared at 15% for the packed-tight fabric, against the provincial 7%. The two corrections nearly cancel, which is why the as-built rampart lands inside the wall check's tolerance of the corrected minimum.

One knob this exposes. The share of samurai housed inside the wall is 2/3 for a provincial city, the rest holding extramural estates they commute in from. A capital should run HIGHER - proximity to the daimyo's court is the whole point of the posting, and the walled yashiki is what makes staying in-wall attractive to exactly the senior cohort that would otherwise build outside. The tier is settled at 0.85 - an estimate rather than a measurement, but a settled figure the budget spends: it moves about 40 households of the priciest housing type.

The brokers' row is MERCHANT, and the ministry's cut is narrower than it looks

Sources: asakusa-kuramae, the setting's own budget notes "Domain ministry budgets"

GM ruling, 2026-08-08. The brokers' row is a merchant quarter, not a Ministry of Retainers annex - and the reason is a distinction the budget line does not spell out on its face.

The setting's own budget notes credit the Ministry of Retainers with "~1,600 (rice/coin arbitrage on capital stipend throughput of ~28,000)." That figure covers the PAYING of stipends only - the denomination decisions, the rice-versus-coin split, the payment-day logistics that the ministry administers. It is not a general franchise over rice finance. Everything else - the contracts, the clearinghouse business, the lending against next year's stipend, the arbitrage a samurai household does on its own account - sits outside it, and the merchant class has grown rich on that in Rokugan exactly as the fudasashi did in Edo.36

So the Edo causal chain survives intact, and with it the reason the entertainment district belongs beside the granary: the brokers' money is what builds the theaters. Draw the row as merchant frontage - shops and merchant houses, with the wealth band skewed high - not as state violet.

Why this matters beyond one row of buildings. It resolves an apparent conflict between setting canon and the historical model without weakening either, and the resolution is a general one: a ministry line item in the setting's own budget notes prices the ministry's own administrative function, not the whole economic activity that function touches. The same reading applies to any other ministry's "informal income" line.

The Emperor's granaries are separate, because they face a different THREAT

Sources: kotobank-kurayashiki; the setting's own budget notes "Imperial magistrates and their staff"

GM ruling, 2026-08-08, and the reasoning is a threat model rather than a plan:

The Emperor's granaries do not need to be as guarded as the daimyo's, because an invading neighbor would be unlikely to attack the Emperor's granaries. They need protection from brigands, not from besiegers.

That single distinction settles what was otherwise an arbitrary placement. The daimyo's siege stock has to be inside the castle because a siege is exactly what it is for; the Emperor's stores face theft, not investment, so a stout wall and a watch is sufficient and there is no reason to spend castle ground on them. They therefore sit outside the castle, as their own modest walled compound.

Where that compound goes is a tunable knob, because both plausible sitings are real answers and different cities will have made different choices:

  • beside the Imperial Magistrate's compound - the official who oversees them is right there, which is the administratively obvious arrangement; or
  • on the water - granaries want the wharf, since grain arrives and leaves by boat.200

It is a per-map choice between those two values, with neither as a strong default. This is the same shape as the castle-seat choice: a genuine either/or that gives two capitals different skeletons for a documented reason rather than a die roll.

A river gets a TOWPATH, not a road - and they are not the same feature

Sources: shaoxing-towpath, jining-museum-qiandao, zhedong-qiandao-zhwiki, shiue-2002, kobayashi-2003-kasen-shuun, matsumura-1994-oigawa, oigawa-jawiki, kotobank-kashi

The GM asked (2026-08-08) whether a road would run along the river, or whether the river replaces it. Three different features get confused under "riverside road", and only two of them belong.

1. A trunk road paralleling the river: NO. Water carried bulk far more cheaply than any cart, so a highway shadowing a navigable river is redundant - the river IS that route, and the road network exists to reach what the water does not.155 Japan makes the point at its sharpest: roads there did not follow rivers, and at the Oi-kawa on the Tokaido bridges and ferries were deliberately PROHIBITED - the defense of Edo is the usual explanation, though the river's own volume and the crossing trade's profits are argued as well.156 A road meets a river to cross it, not to accompany it.

2. A TOWPATH on one bank: YES - and this is the real riverside way. The Chinese qiandao (纤道) is ancient and substantial: Shaoxing's, on the Eastern Zhejiang Canal, dates to 815 CE, when the commissioner Meng Jian raised its embankment, and runs close to a hundred li in all, some fifty kilometers.153 Its form is worth knowing because it is not road-shaped - it comes in two kinds, one on the37 bank as expected, and one built out in the water parallel to the bank, slab stones laid on stone piers about half a meter above the surface.154

What determines it: upstream haulage. A towpath exists because of the boats, not instead of them - downstream traffic drifts, upstream traffic is pulled. So it supplements water transport, which is exactly the GM's intuition, and it is narrow, single-bank, and goes only where the boats go.

3. A quay street inside the city (河岸, kashi): YES, but it is wharf frontage, not a route.199 It serves the dock, the granary and the brokers' row; it belongs to the wharf district and is already in that program.

The decision. A river capital draws a towpath on the wharf's own bank - a narrow hauling path, drawn distinctly from a road (no roadbed, no dashed lane centerline), running to the wharf and no further - plus the quay frontage inside the wall. It does not draw a trunk road paralleling the river. The domain's overland roads leave in the directions the water does not serve, which for Shiro Daika is exactly what the GM's road list already says: east to the Fox lands and southwest into the domain, with the Imperial road running north-south.

A drawable option held in reserve: the piers-in-the-water towpath is a striking, genuinely attested form. It is not the default - the bank-side path is the common case - but it is available for a capital whose bank is too steep or too built-up to carry one.

The ministries sit OUTSIDE the castle, flanking the approach avenue

Sources: beijing-imperial-city, nagoya-castle, matsumoto-goten, sina-neixiang-xianya, sina-pingyao-xianya, travelchinaguide-pingyao-yamen

Both traditions answer the same way, and the split is by SCALE, not by culture. The rule in both is: the ruler's own hall stays inside; the bureaucracy moves out as it grows.

  • China, county scale - there are no separate ministry buildings at all. The six offices (六房, the six 'fang') are rooms122: the six boards' fang as side halls flanking the courtyard before the great hall, three to the east and three to the west. Pingyao's county yamen runs over two hundred rooms across its courts.123
  • China, capital scale - the Six Ministries become their own compounds, and they line the Corridor of a Thousand Steps (千步廊) outside Chengtianmen: flanking the ceremonial approach avenue, OUTSIDE the palace's own walls.40
  • Japan, castle town - the goten (honmaru or ninomaru palace) is where the daimyo lived and conducted official business, inside the works.41 But the offices around it spilled out as they grew: Nagoya's third bailey housed its senior retainers and elders,42 and at Matsumoto, when the ninomaru proved too small, the county office and the town office were moved out into the town (to Rokku town) and the daimyo's conference hall to another.43

The decision. A government ward outside the castle's outermost gate, the six ministries flanking the ote-suji approach avenue. Three reinforcing reasons:

  1. It is what both traditions do at exactly this tier - the tier at which the bureaucracy is finally large enough to warrant its own compounds. Given how much of this tier is extrapolation, the answer both anchors converge on is the one to take.

Wall geometry: rectangles and terrain loops - the circle is the form BOTH anchors decline

Sources: nanjing-wall-enwiki, shanghai-xiancheng-zhwiki, changan-enwiki, pingjiang-tu, xian-wall-zhwiki (the rectangular cities), keep-enwiki, odawara-castle-jawiki, kuruwa-jawiki, shirobito-615-ore, chen-2016-song-yamen, bjd-qing-yamen, kaogongji-wikisource, thepaper-city-walls, chinese-city-wall-enwiki, fujian-tulou-enwiki, fujian-tulou-zhwiki, nagoyajo-nawabari

The question that prompted this (GM 2026-08-09): should the castle enceinte - and by extension the governor's mansion - be rectangular or rounder? The working assumption was: small compounds (magistrate manors, governor mansions, country estates) rectangular; large enclosures (provincial-city walls, capital walls, castle walls) more circular.

The finding: the assumption holds for small compounds and INVERTS for large walls. In both anchor traditions, enclosures stay rectilinear or go terrain-irregular as they grow. They never become circular BY DESIGN - a bigger wall in East Asia is a bigger rectangle or a longer terrain loop, not a rounder ring.

  • China: square at EVERY tier, by formwork more than by cosmology. The Kaogongji's ideal capital is a square, three gates a side, nine crossing streets, the court in front and the market behind - and the cosmology usually invoked for it, round heaven, SQUARE earth, has no direct evidence behind it: the plainer reading is that a square was the easiest wall to build.152 The built record follows the ideal from county seat to imperial capital - Ming Beijing,103104 Xi'an,73 Suzhou106 are all rectangles - so the shape is RANK-INDEPENDENT: a bigger administrative seat gets a bigger rectangle, never a rounder one. Deviation comes from TERRAIN, not from size: Nanjing's Ming circuit (its often-quoted 35 km is on no page we could read) bends around its lakes, hills and river frontage, and is45 "circular" only in the closed-circuit sense of the word. The yamen and the government compounds inside are axial and symmetrical, built as rectangular courtyards.151
  • Japan: angular masonry inside, a terrain loop outside. A castle's nawabari adapts its baileys to the ground (the rinkaku, teikaku and renkaku layouts classify how the baileys are arranged, and the renkaku form follows a ridge or a terrace), the ishigaki runs in straight revetted segments with flanking angles at its corners, and a tenshu's base is rectangular.149150 The plains castles our capital resembles approach clean rectangles - Nagoya's own guide calls its enclosures rectangular and straight-lined, and Osaka and Nijo read the same way on their plans.198 The town's own enclosure, the sogamae, is the OTHER shape: Odawara's 9 km circuit runs from Mount Hachiman to the sea in a horseshoe - an organic terrain-following loop, neither square nor circle.148
  • The military logic, and why East Asia never needed the circle. A corner is the weak point of a curtain (dead ground at the angle, a miner's favorite target), and Europe's round flanking towers and shell keeps are often read as the cure - though the record calls the corner's weakness theoretical before the trebuchet and the crossbow.147 East Asia cured the same problem by FLANKING instead: Chinese walls carry mamian ("horse-face") bastions and corner towers on straight curtains, Japanese walls carry corner yagura and masugata gate courts - because hangtu rammed-earth formwork and ishigaki masonry both want straight runs, and the cosmology wanted the square anyway.157158 Same problem, different cure, and the cure is why big East Asian walls stay angular.
  • The attested circles, and why neither is our case. Shanghai's 1553 county wall was genuinely round - thrown up in one season against the wokou pirate raids,46 and a circle encloses the most area per foot of wall, so the round wall is the SPEED-AND-ECONOMY form, not a prestige form. The Hakka tulou are round communal fortress-HOUSES (a clan under one roof, wedge apartments with no hierarchy and no weak corner) - and even tulou are as commonly rectangular.159 Both are communal, defensive forms of the mountain districts; neither is how a daimyo's seat or a governor's city announces itself.197

Decisions:

  1. The castle enceinte stays RECTANGULAR - the drawn form is confirmed, not merely kept. A round keep would be reading European castle grammar into an East Asian map.
  2. Every government compound stays rectangular - yamen, magistrate manors, governor mansions, lineage yashiki, country estates. The GM's working assumption for small compounds was correct, and nothing changes.
  3. Our city walls draw as ~elliptical polygon rings (Tango, Nagahara, Minami, Shiro Daika) - recorded as a disclosed HOUSE STYLE, not a researched form. Of the attested shapes it sits closest to the Japanese sogamae terrain loop (an organic closed circuit), and furthest from the Chinese square. If a future map wants the China-first form, the grounded options are the rectangle (flat open ground) or an irregular terrain-following loop (river or hill ground) - not a truer circle. Reshaping the four shipped cities is a GM decision to make deliberately, not a drift to make silently.

Moat water: drawn connections outside, standing water inside - and yes, it scums

Sources: xian-moat-zhwiki (a permanently watered moat 20 x 10 m);74, hori-jawiki, kojodan-mizubori, shirobito-906-mizubori, takamatsu-my-kagawa, imabarijo-blog, roving-bridge-enwiki, kotobank-kawazarae

The questions (GM 2026-08-09): does the inner (castle) moat need to connect to anything? How is the water kept in, and kept from stagnating? And why was the CITY moat not simply connected to the river, as Minami's and Nagahara's are?

The city moat: river-fed flow-through, and the connection is drawn. Japanese wet moats filled from whatever water stood nearest - rivers and streams, lakes, even the sea (Takamatsu's moat is seawater and its fish are ocean fish; Imabari's exchanges tidewater with the sea) - and the water came from wherever it could be had: welling up from below, let out of the castle, or deliberately drawn in.167 Minami and Nagahara back onto their rivers, so their moat FEET are the connection and nothing extra needs drawing. Shiro Daika's ring stands ~600 ft off its bank, so the connection is a pair of engineered channels. Two things decide their form. The tap is not at the river's CLOSEST approach (southeast) but upstream: with the land falling NE -> SW, water entering at the low southeast corner could not climb the east arc, and the whole northern ring would be a dead arm. And they are moat-width, as TANGO's are - the other stand-off closed ring takes 66 ft feeder and outfall channels, so the moat reads as flowing through. The form is river-to-river, the loop the sibling cities close at their moat feet: a 66 ft sluiced feeder taps the river's upper reach (downstream of the aqueduct's intake) and runs monotonically down-map - every segment west and south, the declared NE -> SW bearing - into the ring's east arc, and the 66 ft drain leaves the southeast arc and rejoins the river just below its bend, approaching swept DOWNSTREAM, onto ground the planar fall genuinely puts below the arc. The towpath crosses the drain's mouth on a plank deck, as a real towpath was bridged where a side channel interrupted it.195

The castle's inner moat: standing water, and that is period-accurate. An inner moat dug below the water table holds groundwater and rain; water wells up where the moat is dug, and castles sectioned their moats with earthen weirs into pools at different levels (mitochigai).165 There is no underground aqueduct feeding it - a besieged castle wants water that cannot be cut off, which is exactly what groundwater is.166 Keeping it IN is the easy half (the moat floor sits below the table; earthen moats hold water the way any pond does). Keeping it CLEAN mostly did not happen: duckweed, algae and pond scum on a quiet moat are the historical look - Edo's surviving moats bloom green to this day135 - managed only by occasional dredging (the same sarae a canal got) and (in this page's reading, on no page read) by the carp kept in them.239 So the inner moat connects to NOTHING, and a GM describing it as still, green-skinned water with fat carp under the lilies is being accurate, not unflattering.

Why the temples belt the wall instead of clustering in a temple quarter

Sources: bodaiji-jawiki (the lineage temple)77

The question (GM 2026-08-09): why seven temples strewn through the city rather than two main temples plus smaller ones in a temple neighborhood?

The structure IS "two main + the rest" - the two sovereign temples (Benten, Jurojin) are the capital's great complexes, flanking the government axis at equal offsets, and the five modest halls are the "everything else." What differs from the provincial pattern is WHERE the rest stand: belted along the rampart's inner face as a teramachi rim, not gathered into one neighborhood. How a josui actually ran

Sources: tamagawa-josui-jawiki (92.3 m over 42.74 km; the Yotsuya Ōkido terminus), edo-josui, kanda-kakehi. The "~67 km of buried mokuhi" total and the row-house wells as the aqueduct's taps are on no page read; the 玉川上水 page says the mains ran underground below the 水番所 without giving a length

The questions: why does the aqueduct have a bridge? Why is it blue - is it open-topped? It leaves the river at the wrong angle, then wanders. Where did real ones start, what direction did they run, where did they end, and what did they feed into at the city - basins? wells? Above or below ground inside?

The Edo system, which is our anchor, answers every one of them:

  • Where it starts: at an intake WEIR on a river, far upstream - the Tamagawa Josui's nagewatashi weir at Hamura is 43 km from the city58 - or at an upland spring pond (the Kanda Josui rises from Inokashira).59 The take-off peels away at a SHALLOW DOWNSTREAM angle: a diversion swallows moving water; a perpendicular tee fights it.218
  • How it runs: as an OPEN EARTH CUT - open-topped,60 which is why water-blue between spoil banks is the correct rendering - falling gently and CONTINUOUSLY: 92 m of drop over 43 km, about 21 cm per 100 m, and never a climb.79 The famous winding of real josui is GRADIENT-HOLDING across long country,80 not wandering; where the source is near, the cut runs direct. Enclosed masonry appears nowhere outside the city, so there is no stone-colored run to draw.217
  • The bridge: a kakehi flume exists ONLY where the channel must cross another watercourse111 - Edo's Suidobashi ("aqueduct bridge") is named for the Kanda's crossing of its river.127 A route that crosses nothing carries no bridge, which is why this map carries none.

The moat RING and the river-flank moat are both real - and the wall's distance to the bank decides

Sources: xian-moat-zhwiki (the complete ring), osaka-castle-jawiki (the Yodo as a natural moat feeding the castle's own), pingyao-enwiki, pingyao-chengqiang-zhwiki, beijing-chengchi-zhwiki, hori-jawiki

The question (GM 2026-08-09): Minami and Nagahara's moats do not fully encircle - they are open arcs whose ends meet the river, the river itself completing the circuit. The capital's moat fully encircles AND connects to the river through leats. Is that intentional? Are both valid? Which one is the researched form?

Both are researched forms, from the two anchor traditions, and neither city type is wrong:

  • The complete ring is the CHINESE standard. Xi'an's moat is a complete artificial ring112 - 14.6 km encircling the wall entirely - fed from the region's water system by engineered connections;128 Pingyao's moat runs outside its walls while the city stands on the Fen's east bank and its south wall follows the Zhongdu river;164 Beijing's moats ringed each wall circuit, fed from the Jade Spring hills to the northwest and draining to the Tonghui.129 The Chinese city rings itself with engineered water, and where it stands on a river the wall still carries its own moat.
  • The river-as-flank is the JAPANESE riverside form. A jokamachi ON a bank spent the river as one side of its defenses (Edo's Sumidagawa, Osaka's northern rivers),83 and its moat runs as an arc closing onto the river at both ends - which is exactly the Minami/Nagahara form, researched when those maps were drawn.
  • What decides between them is whether the RAMPART reaches the bank. A wall on the bank gets the river for free and digs only the landward arc; a wall standing back must close its own circuit, and connects to the river (if at all) by cut channels.215

The decision: Shiro Daika's rampart deliberately stands ~600 ft off the bank - the wharf suburb, the two granary complexes and the towpath OWN the waterfront, and a rampart on the bank would have consumed the working shore the capital exists to use. So the capital takes the Chinese complete-ring form with sluiced feeder and drain leats, and the provincial river cities correctly keep their Japanese river-flank form: the difference is the two traditions showing through two different sitings, not an inconsistency. A future capital drawn ON its bank (the edge-castle knob already anticipates one) would take the river-flank form instead.

Which districts get INTERNAL walls - and the fang ward as the Lion variant

The question (GM 2026-08-09): at capital scale, are the temple neighborhoods walled, or just neighborhoods? Which district types take internal walls at all - and can this be a tunable knob, given the Lion's canonical taste for walling their districts?

The period default has ONE answer, already half-recorded in the ward-mesh research above: NOTHING is district-walled. Both anchors, at our period, run open streets barred at night - Edo's kido113 and roji-kido, Qing Beijing's zhalan palings114 - and the security architecture is:

  • Samurai districts (several, interleaved with commoner strips - the GM's memory is right): NOT walled as districts. The sealing is done by each yashiki's OWN wall - a samurai quarter at this tier is a street of private fortifications, not a fortified street.212
  • Temple neighborhoods: the PRECINCT is walled - every temple compound is its own enclosure,163 and a teramachi row reads as a defensive line because the temples were placed continuously at the perimeter's key points for their military value - but the monzen neighborhood around a great temple is ordinary open machi: shops, inns and stalls on the approach, barred at night by kido like any block, never by a district wall.169162 A walled monzen would strangle the commerce it exists for.
  • The castle and the walled compounds (yashiki, magistracy, merchant estates) are the only interior walls the period draws, plus the rampart itself.168

The walled ward IS real - one dynasty earlier, and that is the knob. Tang Chang'an ran 108 lifang: residential wards behind their own perimeter walls, gates opened and closed by appointed officials to morning and evening drumrolls, markets confined and curfews enforced.24286 The Song ended it from below - residents knocked down the ward walls to open street-front shops, and the open commercial city (Kaifeng, Hangzhou) replaced the compartmented one.87 So the two forms are not invention vs history; they are TWO PERIODS of history, and a clan that keeps its cities compartmented is running the OLDER imperial system on purpose.

The decision - a tunable knob, defaults per the research:

  • Mesh (DEFAULT, all clans): the kido/zhalan mesh the research recommends - open streets, night-barred blocks, yashiki walls doing the sealing. This is the Song/Ming + Edo consensus and what Shiro Daika draws.
  • Fang (the LION variant, and any deliberately archaic/martial administration): district perimeter walls with gated openings and a curfew drum - the Tang lifang kept alive as martial philosophy and administrative practice, which is exactly the Lion's canonical character. A fang-style city compartmentalizes samurai districts AND great-temple monzen alike; it trades commerce for control, visibly.

Variation within researched bounds is this project's standing practice (the village/town/farm archetypes exist for the same reason); this knob extends it to urban administration. The capital implements the mesh.

The internal dock and the bank quay: the WATER decides which a city gets

Sources: horikawa-nagoya-jawiki (the Horikawa cut from the castle to the sea); "Edo dug nine funairi-bori" is this page's reading, on no page read, kawagoe-gokashi-jawiki, smtrc-kawagoe-shuun, kashi-jawiki, kotobank-kashi, chuoku-nihonbashi-kashi, mizu-no-bunka-32-yanagawa, nippon-com-matsue-horikawa, hanzheng-jie-zhwiki, ndl-crd-hikifune, coastalwiki-siltation

The question (GM 2026-08-09): Nagahara and Minami let barges float INTO the city - water gate, dock basin. Shiro Daika unloads everything at a riverbank quay, and so far only grain. Would a city of this size have an internal dock? Is not drawing one unusual, or did some cities have them and some not?

Both forms are THE standard form - of two different kinds of water. Which one a city gets reads off its river, not off its size or rank.

China first. Canal-country cities let the boats in: Suzhou's Pan Gate is the paired land-and-water gate - one arch for the road, one for the barges89 - opening the wall between the Grand Canal, the moat, and the internal canal grid. That is exactly the form the two sibling cities already draw (the Suzhou pattern: canal communicates with the moat, the moat's river junction is the single navigation mouth). But it belongs to FLAT DELTA ground where the water stands at grade and near-still - in canal country the canal is the street grid.184 A Chinese city on a live river keeps its wharf ON the bank instead: boats moor along the shore and the goods go up the landing into a bankside commercial district.183

Japan drew the same split and named both halves. The funairi-bori (boat-entry canal) is the internal-dock form - Edo dug nine of them,65 Nagoya cut the Horikawa from the castle to the sea,66 Matsue and Yanagawa are laced with them - and each sits on delta or lakeside ground: Yanagawa on the Chikugo's silt beside a tidal sea, its channels drawn from above a tide barrier, Matsue's channels spreading out from its castle moat into the town.182 The river towns' form is the kashi (bank landing): a quay on the riverbank worked by its wholesalers, often specialized by cargo (rice kashi, salt kashi, the small-boat kashi), with the warehouses, wholesalers and markets forming a commercial settlement along the bank.181 Kawagoe's trade ran through its Shingashi-river landings, the outer port that replaced the older Irima-river landings, which a belt of low wet ground had set off from the castle town.180

Why a live river refuses the basin. (1) Current and seasonal stage: a still-water basin cut into a flowing bank silts up and must be dredged, and stands open to the river's rise.210 (2) A towpath river is a HAULED river130 - barges are dragged upstream along the bank, which is bank work; a basin serves boats that arrive under pole or sail on still water. (3) At a siege-grade wall the moat is a military work: Nagahara and Minami's moats communicate with their rivers at grade, so a barge slips river -> moat -> canal through the water gate, but Shiro Daika's ring stands off the bank behind two SLUICED leats whose whole job is to control the level. Opening that plumbing to navigation would surrender the control that makes the wet moat work.

Verdict: not an omission, and not a size threshold. Still, at-grade water (delta, lake, canal country) gets the water gate + dock basin; a live current past the wall gets the kashi quay + towpath. Shiro Daika's diagonal towpath reach takes the second, correctly.

What the quay still owes. A kashi was never only grain - the landing IS the commercial hub, and the warehouse rows, brokers, and markets belong on the bank top.209 The wharf-chain doctrine already plans exactly this (wharf -> domain granary -> brokers' row -> entertainment district), so the grain-only look is not the design.

How often is a sluice OPEN? The duty cycle, and why one glyph suffices

Sources: jsidre-bansui-2009 (the gate's duty cycle itself is this page's reading, on no page read), kiyosu-haisui-schedule, guangdong-tongzhi-14

The question (GM 2026-08-09): were sluices closed by default and opened only when more water was needed, or did something else set the schedule? This decides whether the map needs separate open and closed glyphs.

The moat pair runs THROTTLED, not open-or-shut. A wet moat loses water continuously to seepage and evaporation, and standing water goes foul - which is why this map declares through-flow at all (feeder in at the east leat, around the ring, out at the southwest). So the resting state of BOTH boards is partly raised: a small steady trickle in, the same trickle out, and the operator's normal work is TRIMMING the opening a few inches, not opening or closing the gate.133 Neither board sits fully open or fully shut in ordinary times.

What moves a board is an EVENT, not a schedule:

  • River in spate - the intake winds DOWN hard: storm water carries the silt load that chokes a leat, and the moat must not surge.
  • Drought - the outfall shuts to hold what the ring has.
  • Dredging and repair - the one time the pair works fully opposed: intake shut, outfall full open, and the ring draws down for the spade men. Moats silt and were periodically dredged; this is the maintenance drawdown.208
  • Siege - both shut, holding maximum water.

Irrigation sluices are the scheduled kind - the field gates on the village and city maps DO run a calendar: open from spring to the end of the growing season, delivered by block on assigned days, with a rotation imposed when drought tightens the supply; shut when the season ends.179 The distinction is the JOB: a supply gate holds a LEVEL (trim continuously), a field gate delivers an ALLOTMENT (open on your day, shut on your neighbor's).

Consequence for the glyph: one closed-board form, with the word, is the honest drawing. At rest neither moat gate is "open" or "closed" - both are throttled a few inches, and a partly-raised board is sub-pixel at 3 ft/px. Separate open/closed glyphs would assert a binary state the real duty cycle does not have; the state worth showing on a map is an EVENT (a drawdown, a siege), which is a caption's job on the day it matters, not a permanent glyph.

Do a capital's trades and funerary program scale from a provincial city's?

Sources: edo-sanza-jawiki (exactly three licensed playhouses), sento-jawiki (600+ sento in the Bunka era), shichiya-kotobank (the Edo pawnshop count, twice over), kasoba-jawiki (temple-precinct cremation), dongjing-menghualu-rujia (Kaifeng's patrol stations). zuihoden-jawiki, jiangning-zhizao-zhwiki, nippon-com-kanda-konyacho, yk-kankou-konya, kozukappara-jawiki, imado-yaki-jawiki, kyoyaki-jawiki, imari-okawachiyama, liulichang-zhwiki, visitbeijing-wudachang, wugou-song-cremation-rujia, hinomi-yagura-kotobank, japanknowledge-jishibai, kanamaruza-jawiki, wulin-jiushi-wikisource, dongjing-menghualu-juan2, mengliang-lu-wikisource

The GM asked it plainly: "would there be the same number and size of kiln works in a capital city as in a provincial city? Same question for dye works, the common burial ground, etc." The answer, from the record: almost nothing is "same number and size," and the four features scale in four different ways. What follows is the WHY.

The four scaling classes, and why each feature lands where it does.

  1. LINEAR - per-capita services multiply, they do not grow. Bathhouses are the cleanest case in the whole record: Edo in the Bunka era ran over 600 sento for ~1.1M residents69, one per ~2,000, and each was an ordinary shophouse with a fuel yard.206 A capital of 12,400 therefore gets six, all the same size - the bath did not become grander, there were simply more of them. Pawnbroking is the same shape at a startling ratio (Edo: 2,700+ pawnshops, one per ~400 residents - pawnbroking was the commoner's bank; the count is the recorded one and the ratio is this record's own arithmetic against an Edo population taken from elsewhere),70 which our maps DISCLOSE as a representative draw: we draw two or three with their pledge-kura courts and imply the rest in the shop rows. Fire towers are linear in built AREA rather than population, because the rule was always a fixed radius: Song Kaifeng from 1023 posted a patrol station with a brick watchtower every 300 paces91; Edo ran roughly one per ten blocks.205

  2. SUBLINEAR - works CONSOLIDATE. A kiln is a quarter, not a shop: some fifty kiln households at Imado outside Edo, kiln houses concentrated at Awataguchi on Kyoto's eastern side, with others scattered to the east and north - firing clustered where the clay and the fuel came in, and only the lesser kilns scattered.178 So a capital's second works belongs BESIDE the first, sharing the pit. Cremation splits the two traditions: Southern Song Lin'an ran its cremation through Buddhist crematoria by the dozen - several dozen at the least, sixteen of them once demolished and fourteen ordered restored - while Edo concentrated a million residents' cremation at a handful of temple kasoba92 - our capital follows the Japanese form: ONE cremation ground, sized up, not four.204 Burial grounds likewise accreted AREA faster than SITES.230

  3. SUPERLINEAR / capital-only - the concentration IS the capital. Permanent theater is the sharpest signal: Northern Song Kaifeng held 50+ goulan inside its wazi pleasure precincts, the largest of them seating several thousand, and Hangzhou 17 to 23 wazi by its two memoirs' counts, while provincial cities made do with touring players on temple ground (this last on no page read).237 Japan reaches the same place by the opposite mechanism - Edo licensed exactly three permanent kabuki theaters71 and provincial towns got shibai-goya on shrine ground.229 Either way: a permanent roofed theater house is capital grammar. The domain school is the other capital-only institution - 200+ hanko were built by domains, in their castle towns, each with a bugeijo martial wing93 (Hagi's Meirinkan, Aizu's Nisshinkan).

  4. FIXED - one per seat, whatever the seat's size. The pauper's ground is the best-attested fixed feature in the list, and it is an EDICT: from 1104 Huizong ordered a louzeyuan (public pauper cemetery) in every prefecture and county of the Song,94 one per administrative seat regardless of size. Japan corroborates with the muenzuka - Kozukappara alone took more than 200,000 dead, left under a covering of earth and, from the early nineteenth century, cremated, and became a great burial ground for the north of Edo.177 So: one pauper mound, 10-30 ft, at every tier. The drum tower is fixed for a different reason (it keeps the time, and a city has one time).

The two features whose FORM changes rather than their count.

  • Dye works become a STREET. Castle towns laid out an occupational Konya-machi (dyers' ward): Yamatokoriyama's stands on a channel led off the castle moat in which the dyed cloth was rinsed, and Edo concentrated the trade in Kanda Konya-cho, named for the indigo dyers standing eave to eave there from the early Edo period, whose visual signature was the DRYING: the bolts bleaching in the sun, ranged like banners.176 Cloth consumption is linear, so vat count scales - but a capital draws three to five contiguous dyer lots on one downstream bank, not one bigger yard. (True court dyeing is a tier above us: the Qing imperial weaving-and-dyeing offices existed in exactly three cities - Jiangning, Suzhou and Hangzhou - each with its assigned wares.175 A clan capital gets at most a purveyor-to-the-castle within the row.)
  • The mausoleum gains a lineage TIER and may leave the walls. The daimyo pattern is an ancestral mortuary precinct: Sendai's Zuihoden - Date Masamune's mausoleum with his son's and grandson's beside it and lesser family tombs around - stands on a forested hill across a meander of the Hirose river from the castle's honmaru.174 So a capital carries the ruling family's precinct (in-town bodaiji form or the wooded-rise form) plus two to four small karo-lineage crypts in the teramachi temples. A provincial governor is an APPOINTEE, not a dynasty, which is exactly why their city's single mausoleum belongs to the local great lineage and does not multiply.

Official kilns: two different attested forms, at two different distances. A capital may host an official tile works at the fringe - Beijing's Liulichang was one of the Ming Ministry of Works' five great works, a glazed-tile kiln enlarged for the palace building of the inner city and standing between the inner-city moat and the ground of today's Beijing Normal University, because structural ceramic is bulky and follows the construction site.189 Or the clan's fine-ware domain kiln sits far away and guarded: the Nabeshima moved theirs to Okawachiyama in 1675 and set a barrier at its entrance to protect the secret.188 Both are clean; the on-map choice is the tile works, and a prestige-ware kiln is better recorded as off-map.

Where the record is thinnest - each of these is a guess: the oil-press count (Osaka's guild concentration says it is sublinear, but no count survives), the capital kiln count of exactly two, the dyers'-row lot count, the roofed theater's footprint, and the karo-crypt count.

The one place our canon and the research disagree, and the reconciliation. Our maps want 2-4 graveyards, and the research says a capital carries 3-5 - but our temple doctrine gives EVERY temple a parish ground, and a capital has seven or eight temples.228 The two rules are counting different things: the research's "burial grounds" are the community's grounds (parish + extramural common), while our cemetery records also hold each temple's own precinct plot. A capital therefore shows more cemeteries than the band, legitimately.

How far outside the wall does the funerary ground sit?

Sources: kasoba-jawiki (Edo's cremation in temple precincts), bernstein-fire-and-earth (the May 1873 shubiki proposal and the stench), louzeyuan-zhwiki (the 1104 edict), suzugamori-jawiki, kozukappara-jawiki, rokudo-chinnoji-jawiki, fuso-jawiki, toribeno-jawiki, injoji-jawiki, souda-kyoto-enmado, jokanji-jawiki, manzello-2019-firebrand, nwcg-spotting, senbon-dori-jawiki, kyototuu-rendaino, yoritomo-rokudo

The GM's objection: a common burial ground, cremation ground and pauper's ossuary drawn 900-1,260 ft outside the wall "makes the map harder to read," and they asked for the distance only if research supports it. It does not: the record says the constraints are satisfied far closer.

What actually set the distance was LAND QUALITY and the ROAD, not a setback. The only explicit siting criterion anywhere in the record is Chinese and says nothing about distance: Cai Jing's edict of 1104, which established a louzeyuan (public pauper cemetery) in every prefecture and county, directs officials to "choose high, open, barren ground" (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「選在高曠不毛之地」)95. A spread of one to ten li (~1,800-18,100 ft) is given for Zhejiang county gazetteers and rests on no page read, and a spread that wide would not be a rule being obeyed: it is where each county's first patch of worthless ground happened to lie.236 A capital whose bad ground begins 300 ft past the moat is fully compliant. The second driver is that a corpse is carried on foot: every attested site straddles a highway at a gate - Kozukappara on the Ōshū/Nikkō Kaidō, Suzugamori on the Tōkaidō,96 Rendaino at the head of Senbon-dōri, Toribeno at the Rokudō crossroads.227

Ritual pollution is a BINARY, not a gradient. The requirement is outside, full stop, and Kyoto proves it: Injō-ji (Senbon Enmadō), the gate-temple "at the entrance of Rendaino," stands at the ground's mouth and is described as the place that leads through to the other world.187 The Rokudō crossroads does the same job for Toribeno at ~1,334 ft.235 Pollution buys zero extra feet past the wall.

Fire buys almost nothing either, and this is the finding that settles it. Edo cremated its dead on open pyres inside the city, in temple precincts - a cluster of kasō-dera at Senju - for 250 years,97 in the most flammable city on earth, through the Meireki fire that killed 100,000. The crematoria were never moved for fire. They were finally proposed for relocation past the shubiki line in May 1873, and the reason on the record is the stench, not sparks.115 The real practice was CONTAINMENT, not distance: a stone hearth inside a walled kasoba (Varanasi burns its dead on open pyres in the heart of its old city every day, on a stone-stepped ghat at the river).226 Modern code for exactly this fire size - a bonfire, not a wildfire front - is 50 ft (IFC §307.4.1102 / NFPA 1). Wildfire ember-spotting figures do not apply: those come from crown fires lofting brands in a convection column, and even there only a burning firebrand starts a new fire more than ~60 m from the main front.225 A wet moat ends the fire argument at its outer face.

The three features belong together, and tighter than we drew them. At Edo's north gate one patch astride one road holds the Kozukappara execution and burial ground,72 Enmei-ji at 287 ft, Ekō-in at 258 ft98 (which maintains the site's muen-zuka - the literal pauper's mound), and Jōkan-ji the "throw-in temple" at 1,527 ft.132 Kyoto's Toribeno was ONE field doing all three jobs - exposure, cremation and burial - entered through one gate-temple at the Rokudō crossroads, and Rendaino likewise a single ground entered at Injō-ji.186 The Chinese louzeyuan is a fenced compound with a resident caretaker monk, and Chinese cremation was institutionally a Buddhist-temple business,99 so the crematory sat at the temple that ran the pauper ground.224

In each case this page could read, the funerary complex's ENTRANCE is at or just past the wall and the field extends outward for another 1,000-4,000 ft.185 A small compact feature drawn at 900-1,260 ft puts at the NEAR end what history puts at the FAR end - which is why such a feature reads unmotivated.

Why is the castle drawn BLANK inside?

Sources: japanese-castle-enwiki; the GM's ruling of 2026-08-08 and the two renders made under it; nothing historical is asserted here, hori-jawiki

The castle is drawn as an enceinte and nothing else: its rampart, its own moat and its gates, with bare ground inside. No tenshu, no goten, no granary, no armory, no barracks. That is the ruling rather than an omission, and the GM's reasoning for it generalizes past the castle to every compound this project draws as an implied interior - a governor's mansion, a magistrate's manor:

I plan to also show a separate diagram for them. And if we fill in any details at all on the map, then the diagram will need to match and be kept in sync with the map, which will add a layer of difficulty... I'd rather nothing be shown than the WRONG thing be shown.

Any interior detail on a settlement map becomes a CONSTRAINT on the compound's own sheet, which must then be drawn to match it - and a constraint nothing enforces, so the two drift apart quietly and the map ends up asserting something the compound's plan contradicts. An empty compound asserts nothing and can never be wrong. The cost of the blank is legibility; the cost of the detail is a contradiction nobody catches, and the blank wins until a real sync exists. A future cross-check - a compound sheet's own footprints projected onto the settlement map's compound - would let it relax, and the GM wants that eventually; until then, walls only.

The ground is spent either way. A castle's ground is a declared program line of about 50 ha, inside a documented band of 50 to 230 ha, and the budget prices the WORKS rather than their contents - so a blank enceinte costs its city exactly what a fully drawn one would.

The one attempt the GM authorized, and what it settled. The GM allowed a single try at drawing the internal wards - "let's make one attempt and if it doesn't work then we'll just remove them" - and two renders were made. The first drew the wards concentric and sharing the castle's axes: it read as a bullseye, a target symbol rather than a fortress. The second fixed every obvious fault - the wards offset toward the far side from the main gate, so that an attacker crosses the whole works under fire; the wall weights graded; the gate court enlarged until it was actually visible - and it was a real improvement, and still nested rectangles. The finding, which is the part that generalizes: rectangles inside rectangles read as ABSTRACTION however they are arranged. What makes a castle read as a castle is irregular ward outlines, substantial water BETWEEN the wards, and corner yagura - and none of those survives being drawn walls-only at three feet to the pixel.222 So the internal works bought nothing and cost a second sheet to keep in step, and the blank rule holds on its own terms rather than by argument. The knob that would draw them stays, switched off: the finding is about this drawing vocabulary rather than a law, and a castle with genuinely irregular wards could reopen it.

One thing did survive the experiment: the ishigaki doubling on the outer enceinte - the battered stone rampart drawn as a doubled line, which reads as mass where a single stroke reads as a fence. That is the OUTER wall, so it adds no interior to keep in sync, and it is kept.

A caveat on how strong the verdict is. The blank castle was judged inside a blank city, where everything read as empty because nothing else had been drawn yet. Once the housing lands and the castle is the one large walled thing at the heart of a dense map, it will read considerably better than it did. If it still reads as a void THEN, that is the moment to reopen this - and the castle is deliberately the FIRST thing a capital draws, ahead of the city fabric, so that the call can be judged off an early render instead of at the end of a long build.

The rule the map follows: a capital's castle is drawn as its outer enceinte only - the rampart as a doubled line for its battered stone face, its own moat, and its two gates - with nothing inside it. No keep, no residence, no granary, no armory, no barracks, no well, no garden: not one structure stands on castle ground on a city map, and the same rule governs every compound whose interior is implied rather than drawn, the governor's mansion and the magistrate's manor included. The castle's ground is budgeted in full regardless.

What does a capital have that a provincial city has no version of?

Nine things at this tier have no smaller version to scale down from. Four of them have entries of their own above - the domain school, the Emperor's granaries, the House Chancellery and the wharf granary complex - and the rest are here:

  • The Imperial Magistrate's compound. About 56 staff and a dozen of their families, funded in the setting's own budget notes at 700 koku a year for "manor maintenance, grounds, stable, fortified walls, ceremonial halls".   • Cosmopolitan lineage compounds. Named, labeled walled yashiki in the samurai wards: the setting's canon has cosmopolitan lineages based in their domain's capital. This is the tier's best flavor feature - eight named compounds are what make a map read as a SPECIFIC domain's seat rather than as a generic large city.

Why is a chancellor's compound smaller than his rank?

Because the compound reads how many of his lineage live in the capital, not how important he is. The house whose capital was worked out here carries nine lineages, more than most, and their weights are published numbers rather than inventions: a lineage holding a tenth or more of the domain's samurai usually takes a Chancellery seat, which gives six chancellors and leaves three houses below the line. One of the six is provincial rather than cosmopolitan - its seat is out in the province its name is attached to - and the GM ruled on 2026-08-08 that the chancellor still lives in the capital and still holds a lineage estate there, because a Chancellery seat is held in person. His compound is smaller only because most of his lineage lives out in that province.

So the rule is that a compound's footprint tracks HOUSEHOLDS HOUSED, not rank: a provincial lineage in the capital is small-but-grand rather than absent. The result is four visibly different compound sizes, each tracking a published figure - the ruling lineage's seat IS the castle and needs no yashiki of its own; the cosmopolitan chancellors take the largest walled compounds after it; the provincial chancellor a markedly smaller estate; the houses below the Chancellery line modest ones. A full chancellor on a visibly smaller plot because his people are elsewhere is the kind of specific a generic city cannot fake.

The rule the map follows: a capital draws one labeled walled compound for each lineage of its ruling house except the ruling lineage itself, whose seat is the castle, and every one of them is labeled with its name. Their sizes are graded by the number of that lineage's households resident in the capital - not by the rank of its head - so a lineage seated out in the provinces still holds a compound here, and holds a smaller one.

The rule the map follows: a capital also draws, and a provincial city never does: the Imperial Magistrate's compound in its own ink as foreign sovereign ground; the Emperor's granaries outside the castle, beside that compound or at the wharf; the domain school on the government avenue; retirement housing for the relocated elders beside a temple; the two sovereign temples as head houses of the domain's Order, with an abbot's residence, administration and library rather than an enlarged precinct; a rolled witch hunter's office; and trade-named commoner wards, each named for the trade that fills it. It draws no chancellery compound at all.

Does a Scorpion capital look different from a Crane one?

Sources: the GM's ruling of 2026-08-08; the setting's own campaign notes

Not in its bones. The GM ruled on 2026-08-08 that clan identity changes labels only: "all clans fundamentally have the same needs and the same shared material reality", so the tier's layout, program and proportions are clan-independent. What varies is dedications, names and the character of an individual city - a Scorpion seat's two sovereign temples are dedicated to that clan's patron fortunes, its lineage compounds carry that house's names, its trade wards are named for their trades - while the ring, the castle, the avenue, the wards and the caste proportions are the ones any other clan's capital would build. The map gains nothing from clan-specific layout rules, and the honest reason to say so out loud is that inventing them is the obvious thing to do and it would be wrong.

The rule the map follows: a capital's clan changes only names, dedications and labels - the patron fortunes of the two sovereign temples, the lineage names on the compounds, the flavor of the individual city. No layout rule, no count and no proportion varies by clan.

Which provincial rules turn upside down in a capital, and what simply multiplies?

Sources: the setting's own budget notes (the capital caste and rank tables); the housing-mix inversion is worked out in the entry on the senior-heavy cohort

Four provincial rules reverse. A provincial city's only walled samurai compound inside the wall is the governor's; here the karo, the councilors and the chancellors live in walled yashiki INSIDE the ring, which is both the defining texture of a castle town and the mechanism the lineage compounds need. The housing MIX reverses with the rank table: the capital is senior-heavy, so large houses and walled compounds are the majority of the samurai fabric and the retainer terraces are the minority texture rather than the dominant one. There is no governor at all - the ruler is the daimyo, in the castle - so the provincial rules that want a governor's mansion, and want it large, have no subject. And the wall holds no farmland: a capital walls its farms out, so the in-wall agricultural district a provincial city may declare is not available to it.
