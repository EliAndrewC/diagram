• bodaiji-jawiki - 「A bodaiji (菩提寺) is a temple whose sect a household has been devoted to for generations and at which it holds memorial services for the enlightenment (bodai) of its ancestors.」 (translated; )
  • teramachi-jawiki - 「In the Edo period and earlier, with the intent of making them a line of defense against an enemy attack, temples and graveyards were gathered at the outer edge of the town in many castle towns.」 (translated; )
  • jokamachi-jawiki - 「Furthermore, the teramachi were placed on the outer ring of the castle town, lined with broad temples, and took a part in strengthening the town's defense.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-teramachi - (日本歴史地名大系) of Fukuchiyama, 「temples were concentrated as the northern guard when the castle town was built」 (translated; ); of Shibata, 「a quarter formed by placing temples on the southeast outer ring of the Shibata castle town, outside the Shibata river and the moat that surround the town. It lay at the place corresponding to the town's entrance, and also had a role in the town's defense.」 (translated; ); and of Yonago, 「a block of the sand-hill country northwest of the castle was set aside for temples and formed the castle town's defense line」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-teramachi - (日本歴史地名大系) of Tokushima, 「it is generally held that placing a teramachi within a castle town was for its use in massing troops in wartime, and for its carrying an important defensive function」 (translated; ); and of Matsue, 「temples run in a long line from south to north, and when the castle town was built they took the role of a defensive position against enemies entering from the east or the south, or of a gathering ground for the army when it went out on campaign」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-teramachi - (日本歴史地名大系) of Hikone, 「though it is called Teramachi, the mid-Edo map of samurai residences and the Tenpo map of the castle town both show only samurai houses, with just three temples, Raigoji and others, to the north of it」 (translated; )
  • wakayama-teramachi-nwn - 「Temples, whose tile-roofed buildings within the precinct were sturdy and which were moreover enclosed by stone walls and earthen walls, became forts in an emergency. What is more, stone walls and the like could be built and repaired without obtaining the shogunate's permission」 (translated; ) and 「by gathering many temples to form a "teramachi", a defensive wall for the castle town was completed」 (translated; )
  • miyazu-teramachi - 「At Kanazawa, which places three teramachi, a function of watching the Ikko sect was stressed, and because the broad precincts of temples suit military operations, defensiveness against outside enemies has been assumed as the general function of the teramachi. However, looking at the teramachi of various parts of Japan, there are examples that include the domain lord's family temple (bodaiji) and examples close to pleasure quarters or ports, so their function seems to have changed by region and in the course of history.」 (translated; )
  • miyazu-teramachi - 「In the southwest of the former Miyazu castle town, as many as twelve temples are concentrated.」 (translated; )
  • mano-2016-takada-teramachi - 「What is held at present to be almost the settled view is that they were defensive works for the castle town and for the castle itself ... In recent years, however, an objection to this settled view has also come to be attempted」 (translated; ), 「the notion that "the placement of temples in a castle town was decided with defense in mind" is very possibly a product of the peaceful era after the Tokugawa」 (translated; ) and 「I cannot help thinking that a town plan nearly undefended as seen from the highway had little practical effect for the idea of stressing defense」 (translated; ) (the second passage is the author's report of the view of 中西和子 1998, not his own claim; the author adds that his own reading of Takada is far too little to make a general rule)
