Time bells in castle towns and capitals (toki no kane)

A Japanese castle town told the time by one great bell. The domain's lord set it up, a few low-ranking men rang it, and it was struck every toki - about two hours, though the hour of the Edo period stretched and shrank with the season - day and night, so that retainers and townspeople alike knew the time by it. It also gave the alarm for fire. Edo, the shogun's own city, kept a relay of many bells, and a Chinese city a drum tower and a bell tower apart.

  • A castle town kept one time bell, and it was the domain's.
    At Iwatsuki the fourth lord set up a bronze bell in 1671 "as Iwatsuki castle's bell", at one spot in the town. In 1687 four low-ranking men rang it, and it was struck twelve times a day.
  • At Matsushiro the domain's bell rang every two hours, day and night, and gave the fire alarm.
    The domain's rule was a great bell struck every toki, by which retainers and townspeople alike told the time, and which also gave the alarm when fire broke out in the town. The first lord set it up in 1624, together with a fire lookout.
  • The list of Japan's surviving time bells gives one to a place, and several only to Edo.
    It has one each for Morioka, Hanamaki, Iwatsuki, Kawagoe and Osaka's town bell, and several for Tokyo; it says itself that it does not list every time bell.
  • Edo rang its hours through a relay of many bells.
    Its first time bell hung inside the castle. In 1626 it was moved out into the town, because it disturbed the shogun's quarters; the town bell rang by the castle's drum, and the city's other bells rang after it. Edo was the shogun's own city, not a domain's seat, and no page we read gives a second bell to any domain's castle town.
  • Once its bell had moved out, Edo castle told its own time by a drum.
    When the bell left the castle, a drum took its place for the time signal within the castle.

A Chinese city marked its hours from two towers, not two of the same. The combined tower a walled seat kept is at the bell-and-drum tower.

  • Xi'an built a drum tower and a bell tower, one reporting the hours by drum and the other by bell.
    The drum tower was built in 1380, west of the bell tower, and the bell tower in 1384, first standing opposite the drum tower.
