How big is the Imperial Magistrate's compound, and who lives inside it?

Sources: machi-bugyo-jawiki (the Edo town magistracy: residence inside, staff numbers), edo-ashigaru-bugyo-yakutaku (how a magistracy's ground was divided), kazusa-bugyosho (the size of the Edo magistracies), tabiiro-takayama-jinya (the size of a shogunal intendant's office), takayama-jinya-jawiki (what that office was), orange-hatchobori-yoriki (where a magistracy's staff lived), edo-hantei-jawiki (an outside lord's retainers housed inside the estate); the setting's own campaign and budget notes for what the office is

What it is. In the setting's own notes, every domain has one Imperial Magistrate, an Imperial samurai appointed from outside the domain under the Emerald Charter and stationed at its capital, with roughly 25 assistants at the office there, oversight of the Emperor's local granaries among its duties, and a budget line of about 700 koku a year for "manor maintenance, gardens, stable, fortified walls, ceremonial halls". It is a court, an office and a residence in one walled compound, and it is an outside power's ground inside the daimyo's city.

The closest historical case is the Edo town magistracy: an official of the ruling power, with its own court and staff, set down in a city. Its compound was office and residence at once - the magistrate's official residence stood inside the magistracy, the office on one side of the ground and the residence on the other, with a projecting wing of row housing for the magistrate's own retainers. Each of the two Edo magistracies stood on about 2,500 to 2,600 tsubo, with a mansion of over 1,000 tsubo on it. The shogunate's intendant's office for the province of Hida, at Takayama, stood on grounds of over 3,000 tsubo - and those grounds held its rice storehouse too.

How big, then. The two anchors are about 89,000 to 92,000 square feet for a magistracy (0.83 to 0.86 ha) and over 107,000 square feet (1 ha) for an intendant's office with its granary. The compound as priced today is 72,000 square feet, about 2,000 tsubo, a fifth short of the smaller anchor. The size is a degree, not a choice of form, so it is calibrated rather than rolled: the compound takes the Edo magistracy's 90,000 square feet. With the Emperor's granaries beside it (one value of their siting knob), the two together come to about 117,000 square feet, a little over the Takayama grounds that held office and granary together - which is the check that the two figures agree.

Who lives inside it: a knob. The Edo magistracy housed its magistrate's family and the magistrate's own retainers inside the compound, and nobody else: its 25 assistant officers and 100 constables lived outside it, on granted plots of their own - about 200 to 300 tsubo for an officer, somewhat over 100 for a constable, the constables in a group quarter. But a lord's establishment in another lord's city did the opposite: a domain's estate in Edo housed its lower retainers in row housing inside its own walls. The Imperial Magistrate is both things at once - an official like the town magistrate, and an outside power's household like a domain's estate - so both forms are real answers:

  • staff inside - the dozen staff households the setting's notes count live within the compound's own walls, in row housing along its perimeter, as a domain's estate housed its people; or
  • staff beside - the compound holds the magistrate's household and office only, and the staff households take their own walled plots in a group quarter against its wall, as the Edo magistracy's officers and constables did.

It is rolled per map from the seed, with neither value privileged. Under the second, the group quarter is the Emperor's ground too and is drawn in the compound's own ink - a convention, so that the reader sees one foreign presence rather than a magistracy and an unrelated samurai block.

The rule the map follows: the Imperial Magistrate's compound is one walled compound of about 90,000 square feet, drawn in its own ink as the Emperor's ground, holding the magistrate's court, office and residence. Whether its dozen staff households live inside its walls or in a walled group quarter against them is rolled per map; in the second case the quarter's households each take a samurai plot of the capital's own sizing, and the quarter is drawn in the compound's ink.
