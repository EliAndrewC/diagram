The chancellery meets IN the castle - executive out, council in

Sources: hancho-jawiki, honmaru-jawiki, kakegawa-castle-jawiki, jinya-jawiki, karo-jawiki (the karō council); goyobeya-kotobank (analogy only); hyojosho-jawiki (the Edo Hyōjōsho, outside the castle at Tatsunokuchi), ming-neige-zhwiki, kawagoe-castle-jawiki

The question (GM 2026-08-09): would the House Chancellery actually have its own meeting building outside the castle, on the government avenue? The GM's working assumption was that chancellery meetings happen in the castle itself.

The GM's assumption is right, and both anchors agree. In Japan a daimyo's karo council met in the goten's audience halls; the shogunate's own Hyōjōsho (the supreme judicial council of the Roju and commissioners) sat outside Edo castle, at Tatsunokuchi (present Marunouchi) - across the moat from the Ōte gate, in the daimyo-mansion belt - but Edo is an unusual capital, and the median domain below is the anchor. In China the Grand Secretariat (neige), the emperor's inner secretariat, sat INSIDE the palace precinct, while the Six Ministries stood outside on the approach. The split both traditions converge on is clean: the EXECUTIVE bureaus (clerks, archives, public business) stand outside the works; the ruler's COUNCIL meets beside the ruler, inside. A deliberative body of 5-10 lineage representatives generates no street traffic and holds no archives of its own - it is a chamber, not a compound.

Decision: no House Chancellery compound on the map; the council chamber is part of the castle's implied goten, never drawn.

A median domain's council met in its castle (GM question, 2026-08-28). The GM asked whether a TYPICAL domain's council met inside or outside its castle, Edo being an unusual capital. A castle-holding han's 藩庁 (its domain office) stood "城に" - at the castle - and a castle-less han's at its jin'ya (hancho-jawiki); the goten's public half is the 政庁 (the government hall) (honmaru-jawiki); at Kakegawa, ~51-59,000 koku - a median domain - the \"政務所である二ノ丸御殿\" - the second-bailey palace that was the seat of business - sat in the ninomaru (kakegawa-castle-jawiki), and Kawagoe likewise used its ninomaru; the karō decided in council and lived near the castle (karo-jawiki); a small domain's jin'ya held 居館 and 役所 in one compound (jinya-jawiki). No page read puts a domain's administration in the town. So the capital's chancellery meeting IN the castle is the attested median form - in the ninomaru or honmaru goten, which ring varying by castle; Edo's Hyōjōsho, a bakufu judicial council outside the castle, is not the anchor.
