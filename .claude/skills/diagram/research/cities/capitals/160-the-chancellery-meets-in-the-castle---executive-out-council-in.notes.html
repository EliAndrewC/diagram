• goyobeya-kotobank - 「The room inside Edo Castle where the tairo, roju and wakadoshiyori were in attendance and conducted the business of government.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸城内で、大老・老中・若年寄が詰めて政務を執った部屋。」) and 「Note also that in the domain office (hancho) of each domain and in their Edo mansions as well, the room where the karo carried out their duties was called the goyobeya.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「なお各藩の藩庁や江戸屋敷でも家老が執務する部屋を御用部屋とよんだ。」)
  • hyojosho-jawiki - 「It stood at Tatsunokuchi outside Edo Castle (the present southwestern part of number 4, Marunouchi 1-chome, Chiyoda ward, the position where the Marunouchi Eiraku Building stands ...), and was the body that tried important matters of bakufu government, suits by daimyo and hatamoto, and problems spanning the jurisdictions of several magistrates; it was composed of the town magistrate, the temple-and-shrine magistrate, the finance magistrate, and one roju.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸城外の辰ノ口（現在の千代田区丸の内一丁目4番南西部で丸の内永楽ビルディングがある位置。...）にあり、幕政の重要事項や大名・旗本の訴訟、複数の奉行の管轄にまたがる問題の裁判を行なった機関で、町奉行、寺社奉行、勘定奉行と老中1名で構成された。」)
  • ming-neige-zhwiki - 「The Ming Wenyuan Ge was one of the Grand Secretariat's principal offices; but the Wenyuan Ge of that time did not stand behind today's Wenhua Hall - it stood east of the Meridian Gate, near the Qing-dynasty Grand Secretariat archive.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「明朝文渊阁为内阁主要办公场所之一，然当时文渊阁的位置并不在今天的文华殿后方，而是在午门之东，清代内阁大库附近。」) (the Six Ministries on the approach are the Corridor of a Thousand Steps note above; the article also says the Secretariat was never a first-rank organ of state, which is why it is called a secretariat here and not a council)
  • hancho-jawiki - 「The hanchō of an Edo-period han was the daimyo's residence castle or dwelling; set in the castle or in a mansion, it carried out the administrative business of the territory the daimyo ruled. Han of castle-holding daimyo rank and above had it in the castle; han of castle-lord grade and below - the castle-lord grade and the castle-less daimyo - were not permitted to maintain a castle, and so placed a mansion in their territory and made it the hanchō.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の藩の藩庁は、大名の居城や居所のことで、城や屋敷に設置して大名が支配する領内の行政業務を執り行う。城持大名以上の藩は城に、城主格以下の城主格と無城大名の藩は城を構えることが許されなかったことから、領内に屋敷を置いて藩庁とした。」)
  • honmaru-jawiki - 「The honmaru of the early modern period held the oku-goten, the inner palace where the castle lord and his family lived, and the omote-goten, the outer palace that was the seat of government, with a multi-storied keep and turrets built up alongside them as required ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「近世における本丸は、城主とその家族が暮らす奥御殿と政庁である表御殿を置き、必要に応じて重層な天守や櫓を建て並べ」)
  • kakegawa-castle-jawiki - 「Yamauchi Kazutoyo, who had been a direct vassal of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, entered Kakegawa castle at 51,000 koku (later 59,000 koku).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「掛川城には豊臣秀吉の直臣であった山内一豊が5万1千石（のち5万9千石）で入った。」) and 「At this time the Ninomaru goten, which was the administrative office, the drum turret, the Fuki gate and the Ōte second gate were rebuilt by 1861 (Bunkyū 1), but ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この際、政務所である二ノ丸御殿、太鼓櫓、蕗の門、大手二の門は1861年（文久元年）までに再建されたが」)
  • kawagoe-castle-jawiki - 「The castellan's residence was in the ninomaru until it burned down, and the ninomaru also held the armaments office.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「城主の住居は焼失するまで二の丸にあり、二の丸には武具方役所もあった。」) (the residence and a domain office stood in the ninomaru until the 1846 fire; the article does not itself say the domain's business was done there)
  • karo-jawiki - 「As for important matters, they assembled at the hyōjōsho or the like on fixed days and settled them after joint deliberation.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「重要事項については定例日に評定所などに集合して合議の上で決裁する。」) and 「There were also cases in which a karō held a lower mansion (shimo-yashiki) separate from his own residence; basically they kept their mansions near the castle, which was a defensive facility.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「家老が自分の屋敷とは別に下屋敷を有する場合もあり、基本的に防衛施設である城の近くに屋敷を構えていた。」) ("Important matters were decided by assembling at the hyōjōsho or the like on fixed days and deliberating in council." / "A karō sometimes had a lower mansion besides his own; basically they kept their mansions near the castle, a defensive facility.")
  • jinya-jawiki - 「Moreover, in the jin'ya of a small domain one sees an arrangement in which, as a facility ranking next to a castle, earthen ramparts and a moat are run around the perimeter while no high stone walls are provided, and the residence, the offices, the retainers' official quarters, storehouses, a drill ground and the like are laid out within.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また、小藩の陣屋では、城に準ずる施設として周囲に土塁や堀をめぐらせつつも高い石垣は備えず、居館・役所・家臣の役宅や土蔵、調練場などを配置する構成がみられる」)
