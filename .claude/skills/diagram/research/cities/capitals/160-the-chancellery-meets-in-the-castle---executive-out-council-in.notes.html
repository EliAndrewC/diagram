• goyobeya-kotobank - 「The room inside Edo Castle where the tairo, roju and wakadoshiyori were in attendance and conducted the business of government.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Note also that in the domain office (hancho) of each domain and in their Edo mansions as well, the room where the karo carried out their duties was called the goyobeya.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • hyojosho-jawiki - 「It stood at Tatsunokuchi outside Edo Castle (the present southwestern part of number 4, Marunouchi 1-chome, Chiyoda ward, the position where the Marunouchi Eiraku Building stands ...), and was the body that tried important matters of bakufu government, suits by daimyo and hatamoto, and problems spanning the jurisdictions of several magistrates; it was composed of the town magistrate, the temple-and-shrine magistrate, the finance magistrate, and one roju.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • ming-neige-zhwiki - 「The Ming Wenyuan Ge was one of the Grand Secretariat's principal offices; but the Wenyuan Ge of that time did not stand behind today's Wenhua Hall - it stood east of the Meridian Gate, near the Qing-dynasty Grand Secretariat archive.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (the Six Ministries on the approach are cited in the question on the ministries outside the castle; the article also says the Secretariat was never a first-rank organ of state, which is why it is called a secretariat here and not a council)
  • hancho-jawiki - 「The hanchō of an Edo-period han was the daimyo's residence castle or dwelling; set in the castle or in a mansion, it carried out the administrative business of the territory the daimyo ruled. Han of castle-holding daimyo rank and above had it in the castle; han of castle-lord grade and below - the castle-lord grade and the castle-less daimyo - were not permitted to maintain a castle, and so placed a mansion in their territory and made it the hanchō.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • honmaru-jawiki - 「The honmaru of the early modern period held the oku-goten, the inner palace where the castle lord and his family lived, and the omote-goten, the outer palace that was the seat of government, with a multi-storied keep and turrets built up alongside them as required ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kakegawa-castle-jawiki - 「Yamauchi Kazutoyo, who had been a direct vassal of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, entered Kakegawa castle at 51,000 koku (later 59,000 koku).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「At this time the Ninomaru goten, which was the administrative office, the drum turret, the Fuki gate and the Ōte second gate were rebuilt by 1861 (Bunkyū 1), but ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kawagoe-castle-jawiki - 「The castellan's residence was in the ninomaru until it burned down, and the ninomaru also held the armaments office.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the residence and a domain office stood in the ninomaru until the 1846 fire; the article does not itself say the domain's business was done there)
  • karo-jawiki - 「As for important matters, they assembled at the hyōjōsho or the like on fixed days and settled them after joint deliberation.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「There were also cases in which a karō held a lower mansion (shimo-yashiki) separate from his own residence; basically they kept their mansions near the castle, which was a defensive facility.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kakegawa-goten-bunka - the entry's room list: 「hall-and-shoin part: shikidai, great hall (with alcove), middle guard room, consultation room, ... shoin upper room (with alcove and shelves), goyobeya ...; offices part: steward's room, chief inspector's room, provisions office, ... foot inspector's room ...」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「the palace of the second bailey of Kakegawa castle, the Ota family's 50,000 koku」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「As a palace building attached to a castle it is one of the few surviving examples in the whole country, and it is rare that not only the main parts, the great hall and the shoin, but almost the whole, down to the small shoin and the offices, remains.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the goyobeya as the karō's room is the kotobank note above)
  • jinya-jawiki - 「Moreover, in the jin'ya of a small domain one sees an arrangement in which, as a facility ranking next to a castle, earthen ramparts and a moat are run around the perimeter while no high stone walls are provided, and the residence, the offices, the retainers' official quarters, storehouses, a drill ground and the like are laid out within.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
