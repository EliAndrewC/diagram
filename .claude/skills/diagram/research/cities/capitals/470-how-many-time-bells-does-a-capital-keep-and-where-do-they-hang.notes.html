• iwatsuki-tokinokane-jawiki - 「In Kanbun 11 (1671) of the Edo period, Abe Masaharu, the fourth lord of the Iwatsuki domain, placed the first bronze bell at Shibue-guchi as the bell of Iwatsuki castle」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代の寛文11年（1671年）、第4代岩槻藩主の阿部正春は岩槻城の鐘として、渋江口に初代の銅鐘を置いた」), 「as of Jokyo 4 (1687), four men such as ashigaru and chugen served as bell-ringers」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「貞享4年（1687年）時点では4人の足軽や中間などが鐘搗役を担っていた」) and 「in the Edo period the bell was struck 12 times a day」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代には一日に12回鐘を撞いていた」)
  • matsushiro-shoro - 「the domain had a rule that a great bell be struck every toki (about two hours), making no distinction between day and night. The retainers and the people of the castle town told the time by this "time bell"」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「藩では昼夜の区別なく一時(いっとき=約2時間)ごとに大鐘をつかせる決まりがありました。藩士や城下の人々は、この“時の鐘”で時刻を知り」), 「it also served to give the alarm when a fire broke out in the castle town」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「城下に火事が起きた際などには非常を知らせる役割も果たしました」) and 「it was set up in 1624 (Kan'ei 1) together with a fire lookout」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「1624(寛永元)年に火の見櫓とともに設置されました」)
  • tokinokane-list-jawiki - the list's entries include 「Time bell (Morioka) - Uchimaru, Morioka, Iwate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「時鐘 (盛岡市) - 岩手県盛岡市内丸」), 「Time bell (Hanamaki) - Jonai, Hanamaki, Iwate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「時鐘 (花巻市) - 岩手県花巻市城内」), 「Time bell (Saitama) - Honcho, Iwatsuki ward, Saitama」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「時の鐘 (さいたま市) - 埼玉県さいたま市岩槻区本町」), 「Time bell (Kawagoe) - Saiwaicho, Kawagoe, Saitama」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「時の鐘 (川越市) - 埼玉県川越市幸町」) and 「the Osaka town time bell - Tsurigane-cho, Chuo ward, Osaka」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大坂町中時報鐘 - 大阪府大阪市中央区釣鐘町」) (the list has several Tokyo entries and one for each other place; it says itself that not every time bell is listed)
  • kokucho-tokinokane-jawiki - 「In the Edo period, around the time of Tokugawa Hidetada, the time bell was placed in the west bailey of Edo castle. Because the bell tower was close to the lord's quarters and a nuisance, in 1626 Tsuji Genshichi moved the time bell to Honkokucho 3-chome ... and built a bell tower, and a drum came to be used for the time signal within the castle. The other time bells rang on hearing the sound of the bell that began here.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、徳川秀忠の頃、時の鐘は江戸城西の丸に置かれていた。鐘楼堂が御座所に近く差し障りがあったため、1626年に時の鐘を辻源七が本石町三丁目（今の日本橋本町四丁目）に移して鐘楼堂を建て、城内の時報には太鼓を使うことになった。他の時の鐘はここから始まった鐘の音を聞いて鳴らした。」) and 「it was rung every toki, taking the sound of the drum from Edo castle as the standard」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸城からの太鼓の音を基準にして、いっとき毎に鳴らしていた」) (the article says the shogunate recognized nine time bells in all and then lists nine besides this one, so whether Edo had nine or ten is unclear on the page; either way it had many)
  • xian-zhonglou-zhwiki - 「first built in the seventeenth year of Hongwu of the Ming (1384)」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「始建于明洪武十七年（1384年）」), 「it first stood at the Yingxiang monastery ..., opposite the Xi'an drum tower, about 1,000 m from its present position」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「它最初位于…迎祥观，与西安鼓楼对峙，距目前所在位置约1000米。」) and 「the two buildings reported the hours by the sound of the drum and of the bell respectively」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「两座建筑分别以鼓声和钟声报告时辰」)
  • xian-gulou-zhwiki - 「the drum tower stands west of the Xi'an bell tower」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「鼓楼位于西安钟楼以西」) and 「first built in the thirteenth year of Hongwu of the Ming (1380)」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「始建于明洪武十三年（1380年）」)
