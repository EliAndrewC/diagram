Himeji also anchors the moat glyph: average width 20 m (66 ft), max 34.5 m, depth ~2.7 m. Our provincial-city moat default is 78 ft, so the same glyph carries to the castle's own moat unchanged.

The decision. Take Hirosaki's ~50 ha (123.5 acres, 5.38M sq ft) as the MEDIAN capital's castle.

Tango - the provincial city this project has drawn, which the comparison here is made against - has an entire walled interior of about 59 ha, so a median castle is ~85% of a whole provincial city. This is the single most consequential number in the capital budget, and it is why a capital's wall cannot be sized by population alone: the ring encloses roughly four provincial cities of inhabitants plus most of a fifth in castle.

The castle's area is therefore declared per city, defaulting to ~50 ha with the 50-230 ha band documented - so a grand old Clan seat can declare a Himeji and a poor frontier house can declare less.

THE KEEP IS NOT THE CASTLE. The ~50 ha is the park's own figure for the whole castle ground (the castle as first built was 38.5 ha). Hirosaki's tenshu, three stories, built in 1810 and said to have been called a turret (the Gosankai-yagura) at the time, has a first-floor plan of 5 by 6 ken - about 0.01 ha, a fraction of a percent of the works. Any reasoning that treats "the keep" as the large thing is measuring the wrong object - which is exactly the error the ministry-siting question below turned on.
