• asakusa-kuramae - 「A very profitable business, fudasashi acted both as usurers and as middlemen organizing the logistics of daimyō tax payments to the shogunate.」
  • asakusa-kuramae - 「The rice brokers, like other elements of the chōnin (townspeople) society in Edo, were frequent patrons of the kabuki theatre, Yoshiwara pleasure district, and other aspects of the urban culture of the time.」
  • kotobank-asakusa-okura - 「The town land on the west side was called Kuramae, and there great merchants - the fudasashi, who received and sold the stipend rice of the hatamoto and gokenin, and the rice wholesalers - stood eaves to eaves.」 (translated; ) (the granary stood on the right bank of the Sumida River, so its west side is the landward, town side; that reading of the compass is this page's)
  • kotobank-asakusa-okura - 「The block in front of the granary was specially called Kuramae; besides a firebreak open ground being set there, the fudasashi, who sold the stipend rice on commission, set up kurayado (fudasashi houses) and did business there.」 (translated; ) (the page puts the firebreak and the brokers' houses in the same block; it does not say where in the block the firebreak lay)
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「The hatamoto and gokenin who drew stipend rice, until the granary office called them for the issue of their rice, rested at such places as the tea stalls selling tea and dumplings in front of the Asakusa granary and the shopfronts of the rice wholesalers to whom they always sold off their rice. These shops coming to act as their proxies in receiving and cashing the stipend rice is the origin of the fudasashi.」 (translated; )
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「They opened shops at Kuramae in Asakusa and, besides taking commissions for receiving, carrying and selling the rice, lent at high interest against the stipend rice and made large profits.」 (translated; )
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「The 109-member fudasashi guild formed here had its shops mainly in Kayacho, Kawaracho, Fukuicho, Hatagocho, Tennocho, Kuramae Katamachi, Moritacho, before the Daigoin gate and Saruyacho. These were divided into three groups, the Okuramae Katamachi, Moritacho and Tennocho groups, called the "three fudasashi groups", with 31, 47 and 31 members.」 (translated; ) and, for the year, 「in the fifth month of the following year, Kyoho 9 (1724), ... submitted a petition under the joint signatures of the 109」 (translated; )
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「Each group sent five monthly officers (gyoji), who were obliged to check the Kuramae rice price and post it publicly in front of the shops.」 (translated; )
