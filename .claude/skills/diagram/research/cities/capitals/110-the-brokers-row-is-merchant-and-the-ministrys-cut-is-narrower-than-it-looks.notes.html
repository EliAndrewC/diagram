• asakusa-kuramae - 「A very profitable business, fudasashi acted both as usurers and as middlemen organizing the logistics of daimyō tax payments to the shogunate.」
  • kotobank-asakusa-okura - 「The town land on the west side was called Kuramae, and there great merchants - the fudasashi, who received and sold the stipend rice of the hatamoto and gokenin, and the rice wholesalers - stood eaves to eaves.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「西側の町地を蔵前と呼び，旗本，御家人の俸禄米を受領，売却する札差や米問屋ら大商人が軒を連ねていた。」) (the granary stood on the Sumida's right bank, so its west side is the landward, town side; that reading of the compass is this page's)
  • kotobank-asakusa-okura - 「The block in front of the granary was specially called Kuramae; besides a firebreak open ground being set there, the fudasashi, who sold the stipend rice on commission, set up kurayado (fudasashi houses) and did business there.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「御蔵の前の1区画をとくに蔵前とよび，火除明地(ひよけあきち)が設けられたほか，切米の委託販売を行う札差が蔵宿(札差宿)を設けて営業した。」) (the page puts the firebreak and the brokers' houses in the same block; it does not say where in the block the firebreak lay)
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「[The samurai] rested at the tea stalls selling tea and dumplings in front of the Asakusa granary and at the shopfronts of the rice wholesalers who always sold their rice. These shops coming to act as their proxies in receiving and cashing the stipend rice is the origin of the fudasashi.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「浅草御蔵の前で茶や団子を売っていた水茶屋や、いつも米を売り払っている米問屋の店先などで休んでいた。蔵米の受け取りや換金といった作業の代理をこれらの店が行うようになったのが札差の起源である。」)
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「They opened shops at Kuramae in Asakusa and, besides taking commissions for receiving, carrying and selling the rice, lent at high interest against the stipend rice and made large profits.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「浅草の蔵前に店を出し、米の受け取り・運搬・売却による手数料を取るほか、蔵米を担保に高利貸しを行い大きな利益を得た。」)
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「The 109-member fudasashi guild formed here had its shops mainly in Kayacho, Kawaracho, Fukuicho, Hatagocho, Tennocho, Kuramae Katamachi, Moritacho, before the Daigoin gate and Saruyacho. These were divided into three groups, the Okuramae Katamachi, Moritacho and Tennocho groups, called the "three fudasashi groups", with 31, 47 and 31 members.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「ここに成立した109名の札差株仲間は、主として茅町・瓦町・福井町・旅籠町・天王町・蔵前片町・森田町・大護院門前・猿屋町に店があった。これらを御蔵前片町組・森田町組・天王町組の三組に分け「札差三組」と呼び、それぞれ31名・47名・31名が属した。」) and, for the year, 「in the fifth month of the following year, Kyoho 9 (1724), ... submitted a petition under the joint signatures of the 109」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「翌享保9年（1724年）5月…願書を109人の連署をもって提出した」)
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「Each group sent five monthly officers (gyoji), who were obliged to check the Kuramae rice price and post it publicly in front of the shops.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「各組から月番で行事（行司とも書く）5名ずつが出て、…蔵前米相場を調べて店の前に張り出して公示することが義務づけられた。」)
  • fudasashi-jawiki - 「They agreed to limit the transfer of shares to trusted insiders, such as members' brothers and children or those who had long served in a fudasashi house, and moreover that the address be at Kuramae.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「株を譲渡する先を、仲間内の兄弟や子供、長年札差の家に奉公してきた者など信頼できる身内に限り、しかも住所を蔵前にするよう申合わせていた。」) (the rule was attached to the transfer of a guild share)
