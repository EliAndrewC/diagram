• sando-jawiki - 「In the narrow sense it denotes only the passage inside the sacred bounds, such as within the torii or the temple gate; in the broad sense it means the whole of the road running to the shrine or temple from a place of heavy foot traffic such as a highway.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「狭義には、鳥居や山門などの結界内の通路のみを示すが、広義には街道筋など人通りの多いところから社寺に至る道の全てを意味する。」), 「Generally, there are also many places where the sando is recognized together with the torii-front town or gate-front town that arose of its own accord around an influential shrine or temple,」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「参道は、一般的に、影響力がある神社や寺の周辺で、自然発生した鳥居前町・門前町と合わせて認識されている場所も多く」) and 「Also, places where the line of a highway was bent so that it came close to a shrine or temple for the sake of pilgrimage were not rare either,」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また、参詣のために街道筋が捻じ曲げられて社寺に近づいていたところなども、珍しくはなく」) ("Narrowly it means only the path inside the sacred bounds of the torii or gate; broadly it means the whole way from a busy place such as a highway to the shrine or temple." / "Sandō are often recognized together with the torii-front and gate-front towns that grew up around influential shrines and temples." / "It was not rare for a highway to be bent toward a shrine or temple for the convenience of pilgrims.")
  • bodaiji-jawiki - 「A bodaiji (菩提寺) is a temple whose sect a household has been devoted to for generations and at which it holds memorial services for the enlightenment (bodai) of its ancestors.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「菩提寺（ぼだいじ）とは、一家が代々その寺の宗旨に帰依して、先祖の菩提を弔う寺院のこと[1]。」) and 「For example, the Tokugawa house's Kan'eiji and Zojoji are generally called bodaiji, while the Date house's Zuihoji is called a kogein, and so on.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「例えば、徳川家の寛永寺や増上寺は一般に菩提寺と呼び、伊達家の瑞鳳寺は香華院と呼ぶなど。」)

  • ujidera-jawiki - 「An ujidera is a Buddhist temple built and venerated, from the Asuka period onward, by a particular clan, to pray for the repose of its house and for benefit in this world」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「氏寺（うじでら）とは、飛鳥時代以降、特定の氏族により、一門の冥福と現世の利益とを祈るために建立され、信仰された仏教寺院」) (the Asuka period is nine centuries before the castle towns; the comparison is this page's)
