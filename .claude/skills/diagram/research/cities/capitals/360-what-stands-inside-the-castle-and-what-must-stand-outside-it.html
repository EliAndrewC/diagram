What the history puts inside. A castle of the Edo period kept the lord's household quarters and the seat of government - the two halves of the goten - in its innermost bailey, with the keep and the turrets as the castle needed them. Its outer bailey held senior retainers' residences, places for keeping horses, and storehouses. The reserve rice was kept inside the castle, and it stayed with the castle when the lord changed, as the castle's own arms did. So the keep, the residence, the council's room in it, the siege stock, the armory and the stables in the left-hand column are attested. The treasury, the guard's barracks, the wells and the private gardens are not in anything read for this entry: they stand in the column as a GUESS about a working castle, and since none of them is drawn, the guess costs the map nothing.

Two findings that complicate the right-hand column. First, when a castle's government outgrew the innermost bailey, some castles moved its offices out to the second or third bailey - still inside the castle's walls. The source read here shows offices moving within the castle, not out of it; the ministries' place outside is argued on other evidence in their own entry. Second, the great retainers did not always live in the town: at Fukuoka, estates of the great retainers stood in part of the castle's third bailey from the early Edo period to its end. That bears on the lineage compounds, whose siting has its own entry: both forms are attested, and the one inside the castle collides with the blank castle, so which the map may draw is a question for the GM. Until it is answered, the lineage compounds stay in the right-hand column.

The one that matters most: the domain's rice appears TWICE, in two places, for two reasons - the siege stock inside the castle, the tribute rice collected at the granary down at the wharf, from which, on this page's reading, the stipends are paid. Drawing only one of them would misrepresent how the domain actually holds its grain.

The rule the map follows: nothing in the left-hand column above appears on a capital's map, and everything in the right-hand column does. The two granaries are drawn as two separate features, the siege stock implied inside the castle and the stipend stores standing at the wharf; a chancellery drawn as a compound anywhere in the city is an error, because the council meets in the daimyo's residence.
