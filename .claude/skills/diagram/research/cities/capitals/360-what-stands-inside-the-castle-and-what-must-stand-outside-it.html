What stands inside the castle, and what must stand outside it?

Sources: jomai-jawiki; the setting's own campaign and budget notes for the domain's institutions; the siting of each is argued in the entries above, bunka-kawashiri-komegura, kotobank-horokusei

The point of the blank castle is that we still know what it holds - so the inventory is the record of that, and it doubles as the list of what a reader should expect NOT to find drawn on the map.

INSIDE the castle: implied, drawn only on its own sheet OUTSIDE: drawn in the city like any other feature
the tenshu, the keep the six domain ministries and their government ward, on the ote-suji approach avenue
the goten - the daimyo's residence and audience halls the domain school, the hanko, with its martial wing
the House Chancellery's council chamber, a room in the goten the domain granary holding the STIPEND and transshipment rice, at the wharf
the domain granary's SIEGE stock the brokers' row beside that granary, and the entertainment district next to it
the armory and the treasury the Imperial Magistrate's compound, foreign sovereign ground
the castle guard's barracks, the daimyo's own retinue the Emperor's granaries, beside that compound or on the water
the daimyo's stables and private gardens the rolled private dojos, and the lineage compounds of every lineage but the ruling one
the castle's own wells and cisterns the two sovereign temples and the teramachi rim
the wharf with its dock, jetties, quay frontage and towpath
the aqueduct: intake, open canal and flume outside, draw-basins inside
all samurai districts - walled yashiki, detached houses, retainer terraces
all commoner machi, the markets, the road ribbon, the entertainment district
the bell-and-drum tower, the fire towers, the public wells
burakumin quarters, tanning yards, the execution ground
gate furniture, gate markets, flophouses, caravan clusters

The one that matters most: the domain's rice appears TWICE, in two places, for two reasons - the siege stock inside the castle, the working stipend-and-transshipment rice down at the wharf.221 Drawing only one of them would misrepresent how the domain actually holds its grain.

The rule the map follows: nothing in the left-hand column above appears on a capital's map, and everything in the right-hand column does. The two granaries are drawn as two separate features, the siege stock implied inside the castle and the stipend stores standing at the wharf; a chancellery drawn as a compound anywhere in the city is an error, because the council meets in the daimyo's residence.
