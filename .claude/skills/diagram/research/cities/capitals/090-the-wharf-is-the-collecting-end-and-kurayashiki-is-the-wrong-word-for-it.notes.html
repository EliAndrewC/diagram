• osaka-kurayashiki - 「Feudal domains sold tax rice stored at kurayashiki (combined warehouses and residences) around Nakanoshima to rice brokers through auctions and issued rice bills (notes promising to exchange for rice) to successful bidders. The rice bills, including those to exchange for rice to be delivered to Osaka, were actively traded.」
  • kurayashiki-jawiki - 「Osaka is said to have had 80 domain kurayashiki in the Enpo era (1670s) and 125 in the Tenpo era (1840s)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大坂には延宝年間（1670年代）には80、天保年間（1840年代）には125の藩の蔵屋敷があったとされ」)
  • kotobank-horokusei - 「The fief village is kept nominally, while the retainer receives, from the domain's granary, the tax rice the domain has collected at the domain's official tax rate」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「知行村を名目的には残しながら藩公定の年貢率で藩が収納した物成米を藩庫からうけとる」); kotobank-horokusei - 「A system under which retainers were paid kuramai (stipend rice) by their lord」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「家臣が領主から蔵米(俸禄米)を給与された制度」)
  • asakusa-kuramae - 「The rice brokers in Edo were called fudasashi (札差, "note/bill exchange"), and were located in the kuramae (蔵前, "before the storehouses") section of Asakusa.」 (the source's own English)
  • asakusa-kuramae - 「Merchants in Osaka and Edo thus began to organize storehouses where they would store a daimyō's rice in exchange for a fee, trading it for either coin or a form of receipt; essentially a precursor to paper money. Many if not all of these rice brokers also made loans, and would become quite wealthy and powerful.」 and 「A very profitable business, fudasashi acted both as usurers and as middlemen organizing the logistics of daimyō tax payments to the shogunate.」
  • asakusa-kuramae - 「The rice brokers, like other elements of the chōnin (townspeople) society in Edo, were frequent patrons of the kabuki theatre, Yoshiwara pleasure district, and other aspects of the urban culture of the time.」
