What does a capital have that a provincial city has no version of?

Sources: jokamachi-jawiki; the setting's own campaign notes (the cosmopolitan lineages, the sovereign temples and their grand abbots, the witch hunters) and its budget notes (the Imperial Magistrate's line); the school, the Emperor's granaries and the chancellery are argued in their own entries above

Nine things at this tier have no smaller version to scale down from. Four of them have entries of their own above - the domain school, the Emperor's granaries, the House Chancellery and the wharf granary complex - and the rest are here:

  • The Imperial Magistrate's compound. About 56 staff and a dozen of their families, funded in the setting's own budget notes at 700 koku a year for "manor maintenance, grounds, stable, fortified walls, ceremonial halls". It is FOREIGN SOVEREIGN GROUND and should read as such, in its own ink, the way state violet marks a martial hall.
  • Cosmopolitan lineage compounds. Named, labeled walled yashiki in the samurai wards: the setting's canon has cosmopolitan lineages based in their domain's capital. This is the tier's best flavor feature - eight named compounds are what make a map read as a SPECIFIC domain's seat rather than as a generic large city.
  • Retirement housing for the relocated elders, temple-adjacent - the inkyo households the tier's population figure includes and the provincial tier has no line for.
  • Sovereign temples with grand abbots. Not a scaled-up precinct but a different program: the head house of the domain's whole Order, with the abbot's residence, the Order's administration and its library.
  • A witch hunter's office - rolled rather than given, because the office was ordered for every capital and some of those orders were later rescinded.
  • Trade-named machi - the smiths' street, the drapers' street - from the occupational segregation the castle-town zoning attests. Cheap to draw and a high payoff in character.

Why is a chancellor's compound smaller than his rank?

Because the compound reads how many of his lineage live in the capital, not how important he is. The house whose capital was worked out here carries nine lineages, more than most, and their weights are published numbers rather than inventions: a lineage holding a tenth or more of the domain's samurai usually takes a Chancellery seat, which gives six chancellors and leaves three houses below the line. One of the six is provincial rather than cosmopolitan - its seat is out in the province its name is attached to - and the GM ruled on 2026-08-08 that the chancellor still lives in the capital and still holds a lineage estate there, because a Chancellery seat is held in person. His compound is smaller only because most of his lineage lives out in that province.

So the rule is that a compound's footprint tracks HOUSEHOLDS HOUSED, not rank: a provincial lineage in the capital is small-but-grand rather than absent. The result is four visibly different compound sizes, each tracking a published figure - the ruling lineage's seat IS the castle and needs no yashiki of its own; the cosmopolitan chancellors take the largest walled compounds after it; the provincial chancellor a markedly smaller estate; the houses below the Chancellery line modest ones. A full chancellor on a visibly smaller plot because his people are elsewhere is the kind of specific a generic city cannot fake.

The rule the map follows: a capital draws one labeled walled compound for each lineage of its ruling house except the ruling lineage itself, whose seat is the castle, and every one of them is labeled with its name. Their sizes are graded by the number of that lineage's households resident in the capital - not by the rank of its head - so a lineage seated out in the provinces still holds a compound here, and holds a smaller one.

The rule the map follows: a capital also draws, and a provincial city never does: the Imperial Magistrate's compound in its own ink as foreign sovereign ground; the Emperor's granaries outside the castle, beside that compound or at the wharf; the domain school on the government avenue; retirement housing for the relocated elders beside a temple; the two sovereign temples as head houses of the domain's Order, with an abbot's residence, administration and library rather than an enlarged precinct; a rolled witch hunter's office; and trade-named commoner wards, each named for the trade that fills it. It draws no chancellery compound at all.
