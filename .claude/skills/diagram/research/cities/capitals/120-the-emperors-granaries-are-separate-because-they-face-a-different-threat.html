The Emperor's granaries are separate, because they face a different THREAT

Sources: kotobank-jomai (the shogunate's reserve rice kept inside castles), kotobank-asakusa-okura (the shogunate's working granary on the river), jtb-takayama-jinya (a granary inside an intendant's office), kotobank-kurayashiki (the domains' warehouses on the rivers); the setting's own budget notes "Imperial magistrates and their staff"

GM ruling, 2026-08-08, and the reasoning is a threat model rather than a plan:

The Emperor's granaries do not need to be as guarded as the daimyo's, because an invading neighbor would be unlikely to attack the Emperor's granaries. They need protection from brigands, not from besiegers.

That single distinction settles what was otherwise an arbitrary placement. The daimyo's siege stock has to be inside the castle because a siege is exactly what it is for; the Emperor's stores face theft, not siege, so a stout wall and a watch is sufficient and there is no reason to spend castle ground on them. They therefore sit outside the castle, as their own modest walled compound.

What the history says, and why it agrees. Edo had both kinds of overlord's rice, and kept them in different places for different work. The shogunate's RESERVE rice, held against war and famine, was stored inside castles - its own and those of its hereditary vassal lords - and it went with the castle to the next lord, the way the castle's arms did. Its WORKING rice, the tax rice of its own lands paid out as stipends to its retainers, went to the Asakusa granary instead, built on filled ground on the bank of the Sumida with eight boat channels cut into it: outside the castle, on the water.

The setting's budget notes make the Emperor's granaries the second kind. Most Imperial tax grain never travels to the Imperial capital; it is stored in the Imperial granaries of each domain capital and spent there, paying the stipends of the Emperor's own staff among other things. That is a working store that pays stipends - the Asakusa granary's job, not the castle reserve's - so the history and the GM's threat model point the same way. A store of the Emperor's rice kept inside the daimyo's castle, which is how the shogunate held its war reserve, is a form the setting's notes do not describe, and the map draws none.

Where that compound goes is a tunable knob, because both sitings are attested and different cities will have made different choices:

  • beside the Imperial Magistrate's compound - the official who oversees them is right there. The shogun's intendant's office at Takayama kept its rice storehouse inside its own grounds; or
  • on the water - granaries want the wharf, since grain arrives and leaves by boat, as the Asakusa granary did and as the domains' warehouses at Osaka did.

It is a per-map choice between those two values, with neither as a strong default. This is the same shape as the choice between a castle at the center of the city and one at its edge: a genuine either/or that gives two capitals different skeletons for a documented reason rather than a die roll.
