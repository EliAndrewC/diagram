The Imperial Magistrate's compound in a capital

In the setting, every domain has one Imperial Magistrate, an Imperial samurai whose duties the Emerald Charter defines, stationed at the domain's capital with a staff of roughly 25 assistants (yoriki) at the office there, handling its judicial work and disputes between the clans, and it oversees the Emperor's granaries there. Its budget pays for a residence and office that reflect the magistrate's rank: manor, gardens, stable, fortified walls and ceremonial halls. It is a court, an office and a residence in one walled compound - the Emperor's official set down in the daimyo's city.

The office is the setting's own. The record measures it against two offices of Japan's shogunate, which are analogues, not ancestors: the town magistracies of Edo, each an official of the ruling power with staff inside a city, and the shogun's intendant's office at Takayama, which ran a province from one compound; and, for how its staff were housed, a lord's estate in Edo, the shogun's city - an outside household inside another's. An Edo magistracy seems to have stood on about 2 acres.

  • Edo's town magistracy was an office and a residence in one compound.
    The magistrate's official residence stood inside the magistracy. The ground was divided in two, the office on the east and the residence on the west, with a projecting wing of row houses for the magistrate's own retainers.
  • How big was an Edo town magistracy?
    Each of the two, North and South, seems to have stood on about 2,500 to 2,600 tsubo, some 89,000 to 92,500 square feet (~2 acres), with a mansion of over 1,000 tsubo, some 35,600 square feet, on it. The page hedges its figure with "it seems".
  • The shogun's intendant's office at Takayama stood on over 3,000 tsubo, with its rice storehouse inside the grounds.
    Takayama's office was set up by the shogunate to run the province of Hida as its own territory. Its grounds exceed 3,000 tsubo, over 106,700 square feet (~2.4 acres), and hold its rice storehouse.
  • Each Edo magistracy had 25 assistant officers and 100 constables.
    There were 25 assistant officers (yoriki) and 100 constables (doshin) in each of the two, North and South - the same 25 assistants the setting gives the Imperial Magistrate.
  • The Edo magistracy's officers and constables lived outside it, on plots of their own.
    An officer's granted estate was about 200 to 300 tsubo (~7,100-10,700 square feet), and a constable's plot in the Hatchobori group quarter somewhat over 100 tsubo (~3,600 square feet).
  • A lord's estate in Edo, the shogun's city, housed its lower retainers inside its walls.
    In a domain's estate in Edo, the lower-ranking samurai lived in row houses built inside the estate.
  • Is the Imperial Magistrate's compound more like a magistracy or a lord's estate?
    In this project's reading, it is both: an official of the ruling power set down in a city, as the town magistrate was, and an outside power's household in another lord's city, as a domain's estate in Edo was. The record therefore supports both ways of housing its staff - outside the compound, as the magistracy's officers were, and inside it, as a domain's estate housed its people.
