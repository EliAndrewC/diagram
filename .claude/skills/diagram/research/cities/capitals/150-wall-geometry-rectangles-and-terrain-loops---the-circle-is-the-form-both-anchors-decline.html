Wall geometry: rectangles and terrain loops - the circle is the form BOTH anchors decline

Sources: nanjing-wall-enwiki, shanghai-xiancheng-zhwiki, changan-enwiki, pingjiang-tu, xian-wall-zhwiki (the rectangular cities), keep-enwiki, odawara-castle-jawiki, kuruwa-jawiki, shirobito-615-ore, chen-2016-song-yamen, bjd-qing-yamen, kaogongji-wikisource, thepaper-city-walls, chinese-city-wall-enwiki, fujian-tulou-enwiki, fujian-tulou-zhwiki, nagoyajo-nawabari

The question that prompted this (GM 2026-08-09): should the castle enceinte - and by extension the governor's mansion - be rectangular or rounder? The working assumption was: small compounds (magistrate manors, governor mansions, country estates) rectangular; large enclosures (provincial-city walls, capital walls, castle walls) more circular.

The finding: the assumption holds for small compounds and INVERTS for large walls. In both anchor traditions, enclosures stay rectilinear or go terrain-irregular as they grow. They never become circular BY DESIGN - a bigger wall in East Asia is a bigger rectangle or a longer terrain loop, not a rounder ring.

  • China: square at EVERY tier, by formwork more than by cosmology. The Kaogongji's ideal capital is a square, three gates a side, nine crossing streets, the court in front and the market behind - and the cosmology usually invoked for it, round heaven, SQUARE earth, has no direct evidence behind it: the plainer reading is that a square was the easiest wall to build.152 The built record follows the ideal from county seat to imperial capital - Ming Beijing,103104 Xi'an,73 Suzhou106 are all rectangles - so the shape is RANK-INDEPENDENT: a bigger administrative seat gets a bigger rectangle, never a rounder one. Deviation comes from TERRAIN, not from size: Nanjing's Ming circuit (its often-quoted 35 km is on no page we could read) bends around its lakes, hills and river frontage, and is45 "circular" only in the closed-circuit sense of the word. The yamen and the government compounds inside are axial and symmetrical, built as rectangular courtyards.151
  • Japan: angular masonry inside, a terrain loop outside. A castle's nawabari adapts its baileys to the ground (the rinkaku, teikaku and renkaku layouts classify how the baileys are arranged, and the renkaku form follows a ridge or a terrace), the ishigaki runs in straight revetted segments with flanking angles at its corners, and a tenshu's base is rectangular.149150 The plains castles our capital resembles approach clean rectangles - Nagoya's own guide calls its enclosures rectangular and straight-lined, and Osaka and Nijo read the same way on their plans.198 The town's own enclosure, the sogamae, is the OTHER shape: Odawara's 9 km circuit runs from Mount Hachiman to the sea in a horseshoe - an organic terrain-following loop, neither square nor circle.148
  • The military logic, and why East Asia never needed the circle. A corner is the weak point of a curtain (dead ground at the angle, a miner's favorite target), and Europe's round flanking towers and shell keeps are often read as the cure - though the record calls the corner's weakness theoretical before the trebuchet and the crossbow.147 East Asia cured the same problem by FLANKING instead: Chinese walls carry mamian ("horse-face") bastions and corner towers on straight curtains, Japanese walls carry corner yagura and masugata gate courts - because hangtu rammed-earth formwork and ishigaki masonry both want straight runs, and the cosmology wanted the square anyway.157158 Same problem, different cure, and the cure is why big East Asian walls stay angular.
  • The attested circles, and why neither is our case. Shanghai's 1553 county wall was genuinely round - thrown up in one season against the wokou pirate raids,46 and a circle encloses the most area per foot of wall, so the round wall is the SPEED-AND-ECONOMY form, not a prestige form. The Hakka tulou are round communal fortress-HOUSES (a clan under one roof, wedge apartments with no hierarchy and no weak corner) - and even tulou are as commonly rectangular.159 Both are communal, defensive forms of the mountain districts; neither is how a daimyo's seat or a governor's city announces itself.197

Decisions:

  1. The castle enceinte stays RECTANGULAR - the drawn form is confirmed, not merely kept. A round keep would be reading European castle grammar into an East Asian map.
  2. Every government compound stays rectangular - yamen, magistrate manors, governor mansions, lineage yashiki, country estates. The GM's working assumption for small compounds was correct, and nothing changes.
  3. Our city walls draw as ~elliptical polygon rings (Tango, Nagahara, Minami, Shiro Daika) - recorded as a disclosed HOUSE STYLE, not a researched form. Of the attested shapes it sits closest to the Japanese sogamae terrain loop (an organic closed circuit), and furthest from the Chinese square. If a future map wants the China-first form, the grounded options are the rectangle (flat open ground) or an irregular terrain-following loop (river or hill ground) - not a truer circle. Reshaping the four shipped cities is a GM decision to make deliberately, not a drift to make silently.
