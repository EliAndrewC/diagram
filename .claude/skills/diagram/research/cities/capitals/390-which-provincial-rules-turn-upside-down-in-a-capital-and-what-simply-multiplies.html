Which provincial rules turn upside down in a capital, and what simply multiplies?

Sources: l7r-budgets (who staffs a capital and a provincial city, and where a city's farmers live); karo-jawiki (the senior retainers' houses near the castle); jokamachi-jawiki (rank by distance from the castle, and wards named for their trades); the housing-mix inversion is worked out in the entry on the senior-heavy cohort

Four provincial rules reverse. A provincial city's only walled samurai compound inside the wall is the governor's; here the councilors, the domain ministers and their deputies live in walled yashiki INSIDE the ring, which is both the defining texture of a castle town and the mechanism the lineage compounds need. In a castle town, basically, the higher a retainer's status the closer to the castle their house stood, and the karo, a daimyo's senior retainers, basically kept their houses near the castle. The housing MIX reverses with the rank table: the capital is senior-heavy, so large houses and walled compounds are the majority of the samurai fabric and the retainer terraces are the minority texture rather than the dominant one. There is no governor at all - the ruler is the daimyo, in the castle - so the provincial rules that want a governor's mansion, and want it large, have no subject. And the wall holds no farmland: the setting counts a city's farmers in the villages around it, and a capital walls its farms out, so the in-wall agricultural district a provincial city may declare is not available to it.

Everything that hangs on a GATE - the gate market, the flophouse outside it, the caravan cluster, the notice board, the inspection station - multiplies for free the moment gates are a count rather than a pair.

The rule the map follows: inside a capital's wall, walled samurai compounds are ordinary and expected rather than forbidden, and senior housing is the MAJORITY of the samurai fabric with the retainer terraces the minority texture. A capital draws no governor's mansion and no in-wall agricultural district. The Imperial road always runs through, so the commercial ribbon that follows an Imperial road is never optional here.

The rule the map follows: a capital draws four gates or more, about 10-15 fire towers, 160-240 public wells at one per 10-20 households, several theater stages with an entertainment district, two burakumin quarters with two tanning yards, about 20 merchant kura, 4-8 walled merchant estates and dozens of named wards; and it repeats the full gate program - market, flophouse, caravan ground, notice board, inspection station - at every one of its gates. The counts are scaled from the provincial figures and from the areas and populations involved, not researched one by one.
