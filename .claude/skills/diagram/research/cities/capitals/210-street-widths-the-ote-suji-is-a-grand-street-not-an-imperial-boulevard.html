Street widths: the ote-suji is a grand street, not an imperial boulevard

Sources: suzaku-oji-jawiki (82 m Heian-kyō, 147 m Chang'an - the entry's 84 / ~150 are rounded), tokaido-jawiki (5 ken in 1604), ginza-machidukuri-width

The question (GM 2026-08-09): is the width of the avenue into the castle realistic? "It looks huge."

The researched band: Edo's post-Meireki street plan - the SHOGUN'S million-person capital - made Honcho-dori 13.8 m (~45 ft) and Nihonbashi-dori 18.2 m (~60 ft), and those were its grandest ordinary avenues; the wider hirokoji were firebreaks, not streets. The truly vast processional ways (Heian-kyo's 84 m Suzaku-oji, Chang'an's ~150 m Suzaku Avenue)55 are the ancient CONTINENTAL-capital form - the Chinese cosmological city - and the grandest through-street of the shogun's own capital ran 7-8 ken (13-15 m), an order of magnitude below them.232 So a domain capital's ceremonial approach sits at the Honcho class: 45 ft, half again the 30 ft highway it meets - grander by proportion, not by absurdity. The map draws exactly that, with the ministry files pulled in to a ~21 ft setback so they front the avenue like a corridor instead of floating beside a runway.

The highway's width - GM ruling, 2026-08-28. The 1604 standard for the Tōkaidō was 5 ken ≈ 9 m ≈ 29.5 ft78 (tokaido-jawiki), so the trunk-road default is 30 ft on every settlement map, rounded to the "about thirty feet" the GM asked for; the ōte-suji's 45 ft is exactly half again the highway (45 / 30). No page read carries the 13.8 m Honchō figure (9.9-11.8 m are the figures seen), so the 45 ft stands as a proportion of the 30 ft highway.
