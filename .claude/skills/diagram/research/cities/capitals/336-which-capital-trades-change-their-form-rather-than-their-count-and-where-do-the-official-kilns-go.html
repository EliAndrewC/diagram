Which capital trades change their form rather than their count, and where do the official kilns go?

Sources: nippon-com-kanda-konyacho, yk-kankou-konya, jiangning-zhizao-zhwiki, zuihoden-jawiki, liulichang-zhwiki, visitbeijing-wudachang, imari-okawachiyama

The scaling question asks how many of each trade and funerary ground a capital carries. For two features the answer is not a count but a change of FORM, and the official kilns take one of two forms at two different distances.

The two features whose FORM changes rather than their count.

  • Dye works become a STREET. Castle towns had a Konya-machi (dyers' ward): Yamatokoriyama's stands on a channel led off the castle moat in which the dyed cloth was rinsed, and Edo's was Kanda Konya-cho, named for the indigo dyers standing eave to eave there from the early Edo period, whose visual signature was the DRYING: the bolts bleaching in the sun, ranged like banners. Cloth consumption is linear, so vat count scales - but a capital draws three to five contiguous dyer lots on one downstream bank, not one bigger yard. (True court dyeing is a tier above us: the Qing's Three Jiangnan Weaving Offices stood in three cities - Jiangning, Suzhou and Hangzhou - each with its assigned wares. A clan capital gets at most a purveyor-to-the-castle within the row.)
  • The mausoleum gains a lineage TIER and may leave the walls. The daimyo pattern is an ancestral mortuary precinct: Sendai's Zuihoden - Date Masamune's mausoleum with those of the Date lords Tadamune and Tsunamune beside it and lesser family tombs around - stands on a forested hill across a meander of the Hirose river from the castle's honmaru. So a capital carries the ruling family's precinct (in-town bodaiji form or the wooded-rise form) plus two to four small karo-lineage crypts in the teramachi temples. A provincial governor is an APPOINTEE, not a dynasty, which is exactly why their city's single mausoleum belongs to the local great lineage and does not multiply.

Official kilns: two different attested forms, at two different distances. A capital may host an official tile works at the fringe - Beijing's Liulichang was one of the Ming Ministry of Works' five great works, a glazed-tile kiln enlarged for the palace building of the inner city and standing between the inner-city moat and the ground of today's Beijing Normal University - this page's reading is that structural ceramic is bulky and follows the construction site. Or the clan's fine-ware domain kiln sits far away and guarded: the Nabeshima moved theirs to Okawachiyama in 1675 and set a barrier to keep its techniques and craftsmen from leaving. Both forms are attested; the on-map choice is the tile works, and a prestige-ware kiln is better recorded as off-map.

Where the record is thinnest here - each a guess: the dyers'-row lot count and the karo-crypt count.
