Which capital features grow faster than the city, and which stay one to a seat?

Sources: dongjing-menghualu-juan2, wulin-jiushi-wikisource, mengliang-lu-wikisource, edo-sanza-jawiki (exactly three licensed playhouses), japanknowledge-jishibai, kanamaruza-jawiki, hanko-jawiki, louzeyuan-zhwiki, kozukappara-jawiki

The scaling question sorts a capital's trades and funerary grounds into four classes and works the first two - the per-capita services that simply multiply, and the works that consolidate. These are the other two: what grows FASTER than the city, and what stays one to a seat.

  1. SUPERLINEAR / capital-only - the concentration IS the capital. Permanent theater is the sharpest signal: Northern Song Kaifeng held 50+ goulan inside its wazi pleasure precincts, the largest of them seating several thousand, and Hangzhou 17 to 23 wazi by its two memoirs' counts, while provincial cities made do with touring players on temple ground (this last on no page read). Japan reaches the same place by the opposite mechanism - Edo licensed exactly three permanent kabuki theaters and provincial towns got shibai-goya on shrine ground. Either way: a permanent roofed theater house is capital grammar. The domain school is the other capital-only institution - 200+ hanko were built by domains, in their castle towns, each with a bugeijo martial wing (Hagi's Meirinkan, Aizu's Nisshinkan).

  2. FIXED - one per seat, whatever the seat's size. The pauper's ground is the best-attested fixed feature in the list, and it is an EDICT: from 1104 Huizong ordered a louzeyuan (public pauper cemetery) in every prefecture and county of the Song, one per administrative seat regardless of size. Japan corroborates with the muenzuka - Kozukappara alone took more than 200,000 dead, left under a covering of earth and, from the early nineteenth century, cremated, and became a great burial ground for the north of Edo. So: one pauper mound, 10-30 ft, at every tier. The drum tower is fixed for a different reason (it keeps the time, and a city has one time).

Where the record is thinnest here - a guess: the roofed theater's footprint.
