Which capital features grow faster than the city, and which stay one to a seat?

Sources: dongjing-menghualu-juan2, wulin-jiushi-wikisource, mengliang-lu-wikisource, edo-sanza-jawiki (exactly three licensed playhouses), japanknowledge-jishibai, kanamaruza-jawiki, hanko-jawiki, louzeyuan-zhwiki, kozukappara-jawiki, songshi-178-wikisource (where the pauper cemeteries were ordered)

The scaling question sorts a capital's trades and funerary grounds into four classes and works the first two - the per-capita services that simply multiply, and the works that consolidate. These are the other two: what grows FASTER than the city, and what stays one to a seat.

  1. SUPERLINEAR / capital-only - the concentration IS the capital. Permanent theater is the sharpest signal: Northern Song Kaifeng held 50+ goulan inside its wazi pleasure precincts, the largest of them seating several thousand; Hangzhou had no fewer than 17 wazi by one memoir's count and 23 named in the other's list, the North Wa alone holding thirteen goulan. That provincial cities made do with touring players on temple ground is a guess. Japan reaches the same place by the opposite mechanism - Edo licensed exactly three permanent kabuki theaters, while villages kept permanent stages in their shrine precincts, and the oldest surviving playhouse in Japan, the Kanamaruza, stands in the town before the gate of the Kotohira shrine in Kagawa. Either way the concentration is what marks a capital - many roofed playhouses in one town, where the provinces had a village stage or a single house - and that is this page's reading of the sources, not a rule any of them states. The domain school is the other capital-only institution - 200+ hanko were built by domains, on domain ground, with martial arts taught beside letters (Choshu's Meirinkan, Aizu's Nisshinkan).

  2. FIXED - one per seat, whatever the seat's size. The pauper's ground is the best-attested fixed feature in the list, and it was set by the throne: in 1104 the emperor Huizong, on his minister Cai Jing's proposal, set up the louzeyuan (public pauper cemetery) as a system every later dynasty kept, and the Song's own history orders one in each county and in every walled town, stockade, garrison post or market of a thousand households or more - one to a seat, and one to any sizable place below it. Japan offers no attested match: no page read describes a Japanese pauper ground set one to a seat. The nearest read is an execution ground - Kozukappara, Edo's northern execution ground, alone took more than 200,000 dead, left under a covering of earth, and from the early nineteenth century it became a great cremation and burial ground for the north of Edo. The map draws one pauper mound at every tier; its 10-30 ft size is a guess. The drum tower is fixed too, on this page's reasoning alone (it keeps the time, and a city has one time) - a guess.

Where the record is thinnest here - a guess: the roofed theater's footprint.
