How big is a lineage's compound in the capital, and where does it stand?

Sources: edo-hantei-jawiki (the shogunate's scale of estates by holding; retainers housed inside), fukui-bushi-jutaku (a domain's plots fixed by stipend; a 1,000-koku house's plot), kanazawa-honda-kamiyashiki (a great retainer house's estate and what went on in it), kanazawa-kaga-hakka (the holdings of those houses), bukeyashiki-wiki (the wall and gate of a senior retainer's estate), jokamachi-jawiki (rank and nearness to the castle), homemate-sannomaru, fukuokajyo-sannomaru (senior retainers inside the castle's outer bailey); the GM's ruling of 2026-08-08 (households, not rank)

How the history sized a great house's estate: by its holding. The shogunate granted daimyo estates in Edo on a scale by the lord's holding in koku - 2,500 tsubo for 10,000 to 20,000 koku, 5,000 for 50,000 to 60,000, 7,000 for 100,000 to 150,000 - though many estates were larger and the scale was not strictly applied. A domain did the same for its own middle and lower retainers, the same stipend getting the same plot, and at Fukui a house of 1,000 koku held a plot of about 1,000 tsubo, its street front lined with row housing for its servants. At the top of a domain, the Honda estate at Kanazawa - the seat of one of the eight elder houses that each held 10,000 koku or more - covered over 10,000 tsubo, and it was where the house's own retainers came to serve and where the house ran its affairs. A senior retainer's estate was walled, with a gate built into a row house and a formal entrance.

Why the GM's households rule is the history's own logic. The history grades by holding; the GM ruled that a lineage's compound is graded by how many of its households live in the capital. In the Edo estates the two come to the same thing, because the ground held the lord's people - the lower retainers lived in row housing inside the estate's walls - so a larger holding bought ground for a larger household. Where the two part is exactly the case the GM ruled on: a provincial chancellor, whose rank alone would buy a large estate but most of whose lineage lives out in its province. Households decide, and that chancellor's compound is the smaller one.

The size, in feet. The anchors run from about 36,000 square feet (0.33 ha) for the 1,000-koku house's plot, through about 89,000 (0.83 ha) for the smallest lords' estates in Edo, to 356,000 (3.3 ha) for the Honda estate. The Honda estate belonged to an elder of the largest domain in Japan, and our capital is a Hikone-scale town, so it bounds the range from above and no lineage compound approaches it. Within the range, a compound's households place it, and the rungs are read as: a house below the Chancellery line starts near the 1,000-koku plot; a cosmopolitan chancellor's compound reaches the smallest lords' estates in Edo, or somewhat more. That reading of where the Chancellery line falls against the anchors is a GUESS; no source read gives the estates of a mid-sized domain's elder houses.

Where it stands: a knob the blank castle blocks. Two sitings are attested. In the castle town, the higher a retainer's standing, the nearer the castle their estate stood. But the castle's own outer bailey also held senior retainers' residences, and at Fukuoka estates of the great retainers stood inside the third bailey from the early Edo period to its end. Research that supports two forms makes a knob. The second form, though, puts the compounds behind the castle's wall, where nothing is drawn - and the labeled lineage compounds would vanish from the map with it. Whether the knob may take that value is a question for the GM; until it is answered, the map draws the first form only.

The rule the map follows: each lineage compound is a walled estate with a row-house gate, sized by its households resident in the capital: from about 36,000 square feet for the smallest house below the Chancellery line to about 90,000 square feet, or somewhat more, for the largest cosmopolitan chancellor's (a guess at where the line falls), and never approaching 356,000. The compounds take the samurai ground nearest the castle, the chancellors' nearest of all.
