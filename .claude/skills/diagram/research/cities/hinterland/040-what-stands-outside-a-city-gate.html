What stands outside a city gate?

Sources: chang-morphology-walled-capitals (what grew outside a gate: markets and businesses serving the countryside, inns on the gates that carried the long-distance routes, and the suburb filling before the walled ground did), madian-mosque-zhwiki (the horse market and the forty-eight horse inns outside one Beijing gate), zhangjiakou-fort-zhwiki (a gate suburb established as overflow, and streeted); the GM's ruling of 2026-07-24

A built-up strip outside the gate was normal enough to have its own name, guanxiang: the streets immediately outside a city gate and the ground adjoining them.23 It is a road-driven suburb - it forms along the approach road at a gate that carries traffic, which is why, on these maps, a city's MAIN gates each earn one while the scale differs from gate to gate.24 In the drawn cities the wider southern approach carries the bigger market and the northern gate a stall row; a river city's water-side gate carries the whole wharf instead. What GREW in that strip is attested in general terms for the Chinese walled city: because a gate funneled all the traffic of one sector of the countryside, the ground immediately outside it was a favored site for markets and for businesses serving the rural inhabitants, the gates that carried the long-distance routes drew inns and other services for travelers and traders, and at the busiest gates the suburb commonly filled before the walled ground inside it had.7 One described strip fits that pattern item by item: outside Beijing's Deshengmen, on the trunk road north, the tribute horse trains sold their culled animals on the spot, the merchants who dealt in horses, cattle, sheep and camels lodged there, and a market formed of itself.15 One runs the other way, and is worth knowing: the guanxiang of the Zhangjiakou fort was set up in 1480 because the ground inside the fort had filled and the added inhabitants had to be housed somewhere, and it grew streets of its own.14 Its SIZE is a guess: the figure the maps were calibrated against is ten to forty structures at a busy gate, and the nearest counted thing is the same Deshengmen strip, which at its height held forty-eight horse inns - one trade, at the busiest gate of a capital, so a provincial seat's belt sits sensibly below it.8 The GM set the drawn floor at six structures on 2026-07-24 on the strength of that figure, on the reasoning that the belt on the sheet is a slice of a longer suburb.

The rule the map follows: a gate market stands outside EVERY main-road gate of a city, not merely the busiest one: at least six extramural shops within about 1,560 ft of the gate , strung along the approach road with the outermost running off the frame, so that the truncation says the suburb continues. A flophouse belongs in the same belt outside each gate, and a second inside the walls. A purely military sally gate - one that opens onto empty field for sorties, with no road - carries no market, no flophouse and no caravan cluster; no city on the sheet has one today.
