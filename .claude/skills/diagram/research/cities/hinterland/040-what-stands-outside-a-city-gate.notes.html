  • chang-morphology-walled-capitals - 「Since gates channeled all traffic to and from a sector of the city's hinterland, the areas immediately outside them were favored sites for markets and businesses serving the rural populace.   • madian-mosque-zhwiki - 「After the Ming built the city of Beijing, Madian was the trunk road running north from Deshengmen to Qinghe, Shahe, Changping, the Thirteen Tombs, Gubeikou, Yanqing and Zhangjiakou. In the Ming and Qing, the horse trains that came each year from the north and northwest to render tribute to the court had their best horses chosen as imperial horses and offered to the imperial house, their second grade distributed among the civil and military officials, and the horses culled from them sold on the spot for common use. Traveling merchants trading in horses, cattle, sheep and camels also commonly lodged at Madian. Madian naturally formed a market.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) and 「Madian lies north of the guanxiang outside Deshengmen」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (Madian lies beyond the gate suburb, not in it.)
  • zhangjiakou-fort-zhwiki - 「The guanxiang came about because the population inside Zhangjiakou Fort increased and it could not but expand outward to solve the living space of the added population.」 and 「Within the guanxiang there gradually formed crossroads such as Wucheng Street and Dongguan Street, Xiaonanguan Street and Dongguan Street, and Xiguan Street, as well as roads such as Beiguan Street.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (the same article dates the establishment: 「In the sixteenth year of Chenghua (1480) Zhangjiakou Fort was enlarged and a guanxiang was set up」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ). This one suburb formed for the opposite reason to the pattern quoted above - the walled ground filled first - which is why the entry names it.)
  • madian-mosque-zhwiki - 「the horse market moved to the guanxiang outside Deshengmen (at its height there were 48 horse inns)」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (the one counted figure found for a gate suburb, and it counts ONE trade's premises at a capital's gate on its trunk road north, so the drawn ten to forty for a provincial seat is a calibration below it and not a measurement of anything.)
  • odawara-shinshuku-cho - 「In the early Edo period this town was a new town made when the Tōkaidō was re-routed to the north because of a change to the castle's main-gate entrance. Because the town adjoined the 'Sannō-guchi', the castle town's entrance, it was the place where the lord was met on returning to the castle; besides that, gōyado (inns where villagers going to the domain offices on official business stayed) and tea shops lined it, and it had one of the castle town's two Odawara-chōchin lantern makers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「In the early Tenpō era (1830s), the town had 124 houses.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (houses, not shops; Odawara was a post town as well as a castle town; the booklet does not say whether the ward lay inside or outside the town's outer enclosure.)
  • nanguanxiang-haining-zhwiki - 「about 350 m long north-south and about 50 m wide east-west, covering about 1.7 ha; the main street, Nanguanxiang Street, runs north-south, parallel to the Luotang River.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) and 「Nanguanxiang is named for the guanxiang set up within it at the end of the Ming by Zhou Zongyi to resist the Qing (usually at key water and land routes, opened early and closed late to guard against bandits)」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) and 「As it stands today, the district as a whole preserves fairly well the Ming-Qing Jiangnan market-town historic look and traditional pattern of shop and home combined, shop in front and river behind, small bridges and flowing water.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) and 「a historic district in the southeast of Xiashi Subdistrict, Haining, Jiaxing, Zhejiang, China」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (a market town and a checkpoint, not a walled seat's gate; the extent is today's protected district.
