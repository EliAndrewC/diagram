  • madian-mosque-zhwiki - 「After the Ming built the city of Beijing, Madian was the trunk road running north from Deshengmen to Qinghe, Shahe, Changping, the Thirteen Tombs, Gubeikou, Yanqing and Zhangjiakou. In the Ming and Qing, the horse trains that came each year from the north and northwest to render tribute to the court had their best horses chosen as imperial horses and offered to the imperial house, their second grade distributed among the civil and military officials, and the horses culled from them sold on the spot for common use. Traveling merchants trading in horses, cattle, sheep and camels also commonly lodged at Madian. Madian naturally formed a market.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「明朝建北京城后，马甸便是从德胜门北通清河、沙河、昌平、十三陵、古北口、延庆、张家口的要道。明清时期，每年从北方和西北来为朝廷进贡的马队，将马中的上等选为御马进贡皇家，次等分赠文武百官，淘汰下来的马匹作为民用就地出售。贩运马、牛、羊、骆驼的客商也常在马甸住宿。马甸自然形成交易市场。」) and 「Madian lies north of the guanxiang outside Deshengmen」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「马甸位于德胜门外关厢以北」) (Madian lies beyond the gate suburb, not in it.)

  • odawara-shinshuku-cho - 「In the early Edo period this town was a new town made when the Tōkaidō was re-routed to the north because of a change to the castle's main-gate entrance. Because the town adjoined the 'Sannō-guchi', the castle town's entrance, it was the place where the lord was met on returning to the castle; besides that, gōyado (inns where villagers going to the domain offices on official business stayed) and tea shops lined it, and it had one of the castle town's two Odawara-chōchin lantern makers.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代前期、この町は、城の大手口の変更によって東海道が北寄りに付けかえられた時にできた新町です。町は城下の出入り口である「山王口」に接していたので、藩主帰城の時の出迎え場所であったほか、郷宿（ごうやど：公用で藩御所などへ出向く村人が泊まる宿屋）や茶店が並び、城下に2軒あった小田原町ちょうちんづくりの家のうち1軒がありました。」) and 「In the early Tenpō era (1830s), the town had 124 houses.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「天保初期（1830年代）、町内の戸数は124軒でした。」) (houses, not shops; Odawara was a post town as well as a castle town; the booklet does not say whether the ward lay inside or outside the town's outer enclosure.)
  • nanguanxiang-haining-zhwiki - 「about 350 m long north-south and about 50 m wide east-west, covering about 1.7 ha; the main street, Nanguanxiang Street, runs north-south, parallel to the Luotang River.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「南北长约350米，东西宽约50米，占地面积约1.7公顷，主街南关厢街呈南北走向、与洛塘河平行。」) and 「Nanguanxiang is named for the guanxiang set up within it at the end of the Ming by Zhou Zongyi to resist the Qing (usually at key water and land routes, opened early and closed late to guard against bandits)」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「南关厢因其中有明末周宗彝为抗清而设置的关厢（多位于水陆要道，早开晚关，以防盗贼）而得名」) and 「As it stands today, the district as a whole preserves fairly well the Ming-Qing Jiangnan market-town historic look and traditional pattern of shop and home combined, shop in front and river behind, small bridges and flowing water.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「现状整个街区较好地保存了明清时期江南市镇商住合一、前店后河、小桥流水的历史风貌和传统格局」) and 「a historic district in the southeast of Xiashi Subdistrict, Haining, Jiaxing, Zhejiang, China」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「南关厢为位于中国浙江省嘉兴海宁市硖石街道东南部的一处历史街区」) (a market town and a checkpoint, not a walled seat's gate; the extent is today's protected district.
