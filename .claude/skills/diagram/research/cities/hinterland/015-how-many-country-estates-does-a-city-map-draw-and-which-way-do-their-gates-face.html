How many country estates does a city map draw, and which way do their gates face?

Sources: the GM's rulings of 2026-07-19 and 2026-07-22; siheyuan-zhwiki (the gate at the auspicious southeast). Why the real estates lie dispersed and miles out is the question before this one.

What the map can honestly show (the GM asked on 2026-07-22 whether the tight belt of estates just outside the wall was accurate). A city view is under a mile across - about 3,800 ft at this tier - so the country estates proper, miles out, are entirely off the sheet. The map draws only the NEAREST one to three, spread apart on the open, capital-facing approaches, each on its own ground with its own drive out to the district road, and leaves the rest implied. That is a deviation, and a deliberate one: the near estate is on the sheet so that a reader learns the type exists, not because the ground within half a mile of a gate held several.

The belt is a slice, and the count is free (the GM's ruling of 2026-07-19). The GM's own domain figures support roughly eight to fifteen estate households in a provincial city's orbit, but a map never claims a census: it draws at least two, at the frame edge, some of them cut by the frame - exactly like the farm fields the frame cuts, where the truncation itself says that this continues beyond the sheet. Any count of two or more is right. The households who live in them are counted in the surrounding village districts, not in the city's own three thousand.

Where the full pattern would show, and why it is deferred. The genuinely dispersed arrangement - estates strewn across the whole countryside, each beside its own fields and its ancestral village, out to perhaps fifteen miles, most of them nowhere near the city (a reach no source read sets: see the note on it in the question before this one) - only becomes visible on a map of the district rather than of the city. That is a separate mode nobody has built. At city scale the honest depiction stays the nearest few at the frame edge with the rest off-map.

The rule the map follows: a provincial city draws one to three walled country estates outside its wall, at least two of them falling at least partly inside the cropped window. They stand at least 600 ft apart , each its own compound on its own land and never a tight cluster, and they vary in size, the largest at least half again the smallest. They lie toward the capital, whichever direction that is from this city, on the open approaches the fields leave free; they overlap neither each other nor the wall and moat, and they keep off the highway out of the gate. Their gates do not all face the same way: the formal gate favors the auspicious south side, at the southeast as in the northern courtyard house, while others face the approach from the city, because each estate fronts its own lane that the map is cropped too tight to draw. The spacing and the size spread are calibrated against the three drawn cities, not derived from a source.
