• chinese-city-wall-enwiki - 「Long-term strategic considerations meant that the walls of important cities often enclosed an area much larger than existing urban areas in order to ensure excess capacity for growth, and to secure resources such as timber and farmland in times of war. The city wall of Quanzhou in Fujian still contained one quarter vacant land by 1945. The city wall of Suzhou by the Republic of China era still enclosed large tracts of farmland.」 (English. The article attributes the Quanzhou and Suzhou statements to Chen Zhengxiang, Chinese Cultural Geography, Joint Publishing, Beijing 1983, pp. 68, 74 - a printed book with no copy this project could find, which is why the encyclopedia is the readable witness.)
  • king-forty-centuries - 「Among the most common sights on our rides from Yokohama to Tokyo, both within the city and along the roads leading to the fields, starting early in the morning, were the loads of night soil carried on the shoulders of men and on the backs of animals, but most commonly on strong carts drawn by men, bearing six to ten tightly covered wooden containers holding forty, sixty or more pounds each.」 and 「Generally the liquid manures must be diluted with water to a greater or less extent before they are "fed", as the Chinese say, to their plants, hence there is need of an abundant and convenient water supply.」 (English, from an American agronomist's 1911 account of what he saw in China, Korea and Japan. It carries the night soil and the water, on garden ground reached from a city; that the surplus ground INSIDE a wall was the ground worked this way is the record's own join, and the wall's own enclosure of farmland is quoted at the note below.)

  • jawiki-yosuiro - 「When water is drawn, the level in the canal is raised by putting in weir boards and the like, the sluice is opened, and the water is let into the field by natural inflow.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「引水時は、用水路に堰板を入れるなどして水位を上げ、樋を開けて自然流入により田へ水を流し込む。」) (gravity feed; the pond at the pocket's HIGH corner and water entering the city from high ground are this page's, and the same page puts a canal at nearly paddy level)
  • qimin-yaoshu-zhongkui - 「In spring one must plant in beds and water them. [author's note: In spring there is much wind and drought, so without beds it cannot be done. Moreover with beds the ground is economized and the vegetables are many; one bed supplies one mouth.] The bed is two paces long and one pace wide. [author's note: If it is larger the water is hard to spread evenly, and also one does not want a person's foot to go into it.]」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「春必畦種、水澆。春多風、旱，非畦不得。且畦者地省而菜多，一畦供一口。畦長兩步，廣一步。大則水難均，又不用人足入。」) (from the mallow-planting chapter; the same chapter generalizes it - 「For all things planted in beds, the making of the bed is in every case as in the method for planting kui」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「凡畦種之物，治畦皆如種葵法」). The bracketed passages are Jia Sixie's own interlinear notes, set small in the text.)
  • qimin-yaoshu-zhongkui - 「Also, the method for winter-planting kui: near a prefectural or commandery town or city where there is a market, thirty mu of good land backing onto the city wall」 and 「In the middle of it, sink ten wells along its length. [author's note: The wells must correspond to one another; set at a skew angle they waste the ground. If the shape of the ground is narrow and long, the wells must be made in a single row; if the shape of the ground is a true square, making two or three rows is not objectionable either.] For each well make a well-sweep and a windlass.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「又冬種葵法：近州郡都邑有市之處，負郭良田三十畝」 and original: 「於中逐長穿井十口。井必相當，斜角則妨地。地形狹長者，井必作一行；地形正方者，作兩三行亦不嫌也。井別作桔橰、轆轤。」) (負郭, "backing onto the city wall", is the siting; thirty mu is the extent, in a measure this record has not converted from a source.)
