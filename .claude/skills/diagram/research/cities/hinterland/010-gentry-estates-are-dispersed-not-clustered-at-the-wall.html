Gentry estates are DISPERSED, not clustered at the wall

Sources: cdlib-local-elites (the urbanized gentry living in town as absentee landlords, land gathered in scattered plots, the end of the resident-landlord estate, and how far out Ningbo's gentry villages lay), walled-village-enwiki (the walled clan village), heino-bunri-jawiki (samurai gathered into the castle town, and its limits); the fortified medieval lord's seat and the guanxiang (guanxiang-zdic, madian-mosque-zhwiki), chinese-kinship-enwiki, zaichi-ryoshu-jawiki, bukeyashiki-wiki, horinouchi-jawiki

Three forces all push AGAINST a wall-cluster. (1) Land: elite holdings were usually built up slowly, in small pieces and scattered plots, and lineages were rooted in specific ancestral villages, so the rural big-house sits among its OWN fields / at the home village, dispersed across the district (the large estate under a single resident landlord was the earlier form, which in the Lower Yangzi gave way at the Ming-Qing transition). (2) Security: a row of rich, undefended compounds outside the wall is a bandit magnet that negates the wall - the rural estates that existed were each individually fortified (the moated seat of the medieval Japanese resident lord, and in southern China the walled village of a single clan) precisely because they stood ALONE beyond the city's protection, which spreads them apart. (3) Amenity: the gentry moved INWARD through the gate to live in town as absentee landlords (that the seat's exam/office/social life was the pull is this record's GUESS), working their land through agents (in an Edo reading they don't commute - most are resident in the castle town, though no policy ever moved the whole warrior class there and some domains kept many retainers in farming districts or the townsmen's quarters to the end). And the belt hugging the gates was the COMMERCIAL commoner suburb (guanxiang), not a genteel quarter - "close to the gate" describes the wrong land use. So: the bulk of well-off samurai live INSIDE the walls (the samurai ward); the immediate wall-shadow is the commercial suburb (gate markets and flophouses); the country estates proper are dispersed ~2-15 miles out - around one prefectural city, eleven gentry lineages had their home villages within ten kilometers (about six miles) of the wall, and the reach beyond that is this record's own - each an isolated fortified compound by its own land; at most a few near-city retreats sit within a mile or few, spread apart, never clustered (the near-city retreats are this record's GUESS).

What a city map draws of this - the nearest few estates, the rule they follow and which way their gates face - is the next question.
