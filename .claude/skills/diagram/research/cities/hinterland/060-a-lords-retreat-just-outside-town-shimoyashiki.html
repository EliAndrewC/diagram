A lord's retreat just outside town (shimoyashiki)

A daimyo of the Edo period kept, besides his main residence, a lower mansion on the outskirts of Edo: mostly a second house with gardens, placed in the suburbs away from the castle. It held gardens for the lord's rest and amusement, and vegetable plots and bamboo groves that supplied his main house. Lords and senior retainers kept them in other castle towns too. China had the same thing in the official's private garden outside a city gate. The sources say what such a retreat was far better than how far out it lay.

  • At Edo, a lower mansion held gardens, kitchen plots and, by the water, a landing.
    A dictionary of Japanese history says that at Edo the lower mansion held gardens for the lord's amusement and rest, and vegetable plots and bamboo groves to supply the upper residence, and that one on a shore or a riverbank also served as a landing place for cargo.
  • Some lower mansions stood on farmland a daimyo bought.
    A house a daimyo built on land bought from its private owners, farmland among it, was called a kakae-yashiki; these lay generally in Edo's suburbs and served some domains as lower mansions.
  • Castle towns other than Edo had them too.
    The same dictionary says that in castle towns other than Edo there are cases of domain lords and senior retainers keeping one besides the castle or their main house. It gives cases, not a count, and no sizes.
  • At Sumoto, a lower mansion lined a street of the outer town.
    A gazetteer entry on the same dictionary page describes a street running east to west through the south of the castle town's outer town, three ken wide and 237 ken long (about 18 ft by 1,400 ft ), with the Inada family's lower mansion and its retainers' houses on both sides. It is a second form of the retreat: a street within the town's outer edge, not a house on its own ground.
  • In China, a Ming official kept a private garden outside a Suzhou gate.
    Suzhou's Lingering Garden stands outside the Changmen gate, northwest of the old city, and was begun in 1593 as the private garden of an official who had been removed from office. The page does not say whether he lived there or in town.
  • How far out did a retreat lie?
    No source we read gives a distance from a castle or a city wall to a retreat: the pages say "the outskirts" and "the suburbs", and name Edo's by district. That a retreat lay within a mile or a few of its town is this record's GUESS.
