• jin-zhongdu-water-gate-bjd - 「The water system rose at Lianhuachi and ringed the whole city. In flood season the water collected in the city ran into the channel and, through the water gate, drained into the moat south of the city, effectively relieving urban waterlogging.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「水系发源于莲花池，环绕全城。汛期城内积水汇入河道，经由水关排入城南护城河，有效缓解城市内涝。」) (said of the Jin capital Zhongdu, from its excavated water gate; a contributor's article on the Beijing Daily's platform, which carries the platform's notice that it speaks only for its author.)
  • yanagawa-jogo-mizuochi - 「The canal ringing Yanagawa's old castle town is generally called the jōgō. Water of the Futatsugawa, of the Yabe River system, flows into it through the jōgō sluice. At the draining, the sluice is closed, the Nichō-ibi at Okinohata is opened, and the jōgō's stored water is let out to the Ariake Sea.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一般に柳川の旧城下町を囲む掘割を城濠と呼びます。ここに城濠水門を経て矢部川水系の二ツ川の水が流れ込みます。そこで水落としの時、城濠水門を閉じ、沖端に設けられている二挺井樋を開放し、城濠の貯水を有明海に流します。」) (one castle town; the page adapts a local-history book and describes the draining as it was done up to the late Meiji period and today.)
  • mizu-no-bunka-32-yanagawa - 「The canals were made to drain the land; the earth dug from them was used for farmland, house land and embankments (doi, levees); and they also serve as a 'reservoir' storing fresh water.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「掘割は、土地の水はけのためにつくられ、そこから掘り上げられた土は耕地や住宅地や土居（堤防）に利用され、さらに真水を蓄える「溜池」機能も果たすのである。」) and 「Done this way, even when rain falls at high tide and cannot drain, storing it in the canals prevents flooding.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「こうしておけば、満潮時に雨が降って排水できないときにも、掘割に貯留することで氾濫を防ぐことができる。」) ("done this way" is the narrowings the article describes just before, which slow the water; the canals are the town's whole network, of which the moat is one ring.)
  • zaichi-ryoshu-jawiki - 「The moat enclosing the residence served for defense and at the same time had the function of irrigating the gate paddy and dry field.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「宅を囲う堀は防御とともに門田畠への灌漑機能も合わせて有し」) (a medieval lord's seat in the countryside, not a city's moat.)
  • masuami-yosui-ueda - 「The Masuami channel is the farm-irrigation water of Suwabe, Akiwa and Kamishiojiri, drawn from Amagafuchi at the Ueda castle town in the time of the Sengoku house's rule of Ueda domain.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「桝網用水(ますあみようすい)は、上田藩仙石時代(うえだはんせんごくじだい)、上田城下尼(うえだじょうかあま)が淵(ふち)から水(みず)を引(ひ)き込(こ)んだ、諏訪部(すわべ)・秋和(あきわ)・上塩尻(かみしおじり)の農業用水(のうぎょうようすい)です。」) and 「It also serves as a river, as a channel for handling the urban area's rainwater.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また、市街地(しがいち)の雨水処理(うすいしょり)のための水路(すいろ)として、河川(かせん)の役目(やくめ)もはたしている」) (the Sengoku house is the family that held the domain, not the age of civil wars; the page does not say the channel was drawn from the castle moat itself, and the rainwater role is today's.)
  • yanagawa-jogo-mizuochi - 「Because the mud, 'gata', that had settled in the canals made a fertilizer rich in nutrients. So they dried the gata, broke it small with a tool called a 'kurewari', and spread it on paddies and fields.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「堀に滞留していた泥、「ガタ」は養分を多分に含む肥料となるからです。そのため、ガタを乾燥させ、「くれ割」という道具で小さく砕いて田畑に撒いていました。」) (said of the yearly drying of the canals in general, which the page says mattered to rural inhabitants, rather than of the castle moat's draining.)
