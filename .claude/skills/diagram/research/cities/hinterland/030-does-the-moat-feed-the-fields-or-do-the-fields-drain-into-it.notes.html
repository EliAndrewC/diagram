• jin-zhongdu-water-gate-bjd - 「The water system rose at Lianhuachi and ringed the whole city. In flood season the water collected in the city ran into the channel and, through the water gate, drained into the moat south of the city, effectively relieving urban waterlogging.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (said of the Jin capital Zhongdu, from its excavated water gate; a contributor's article on the Beijing Daily's platform, which carries the platform's notice that it speaks only for its author.)
  • yanagawa-jogo-mizuochi - 「The canal ringing Yanagawa's old castle town is generally called the jōgō. Water of the Futatsugawa, of the Yabe River system, flows into it through the jōgō sluice. At the draining, the sluice is closed, the Nichō-ibi at Okinohata is opened, and the jōgō's stored water is let out to the Ariake Sea.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (one castle town; the page adapts a local-history book and describes the draining as it was done up to the late Meiji period and today.)
  • mizu-no-bunka-32-yanagawa - 「The canals were made to drain the land; the earth dug from them was used for farmland, house land and embankments (doi, levees); and they also serve as a 'reservoir' storing fresh water.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「Done this way, even when rain falls at high tide and cannot drain, storing it in the canals prevents flooding.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) ("done this way" is the narrowings the article describes just before, which slow the water; the canals are the town's whole network, of which the moat is one ring.)
  • zaichi-ryoshu-jawiki - 「The moat enclosing the residence served for defense and at the same time had the function of irrigating the gate paddy and dry field.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (a medieval lord's seat in the countryside, not a city's moat.)
  • masuami-yosui-ueda - 「The Masuami channel is the farm-irrigation water of Suwabe, Akiwa and Kamishiojiri, drawn from Amagafuchi at the Ueda castle town in the time of the Sengoku house's rule of Ueda domain.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「It also serves as a river, as a channel for handling the urban area's rainwater.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Sengoku house is the family that held the domain, not the age of civil wars; the page does not say the channel was drawn from the castle moat itself, and the rainwater role is today's.)
  • yanagawa-jogo-mizuochi - 「Because the mud, 'gata', that had settled in the canals made a fertilizer rich in nutrients. So they dried the gata, broke it small with a tool called a 'kurewari', and spread it on paddies and fields.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (said of the yearly drying of the canals in general, which the page says mattered to rural inhabitants, rather than of the castle moat's draining.)
