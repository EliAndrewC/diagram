• kotobank-shimoyashiki - 「In the Edo period, a daimyo residence set up in the outskirts of Edo apart from the main residence.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、本邸以外に江戸近郊に設けられた大名屋敷。」) (the Digital Daijisen's entry; the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry on the same page is broader, a secondary house of a daimyo or a great merchant, with a Muromachi example.)
  • edo-hantei-jawiki - 「The lower residences among the Edo domain residences mainly had a large role as secondary residences with gardens and the like, and most were placed in the suburbs away from Edo Castle.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸藩邸の下屋敷は主に庭園など別邸としての役割が大きく、大半は江戸城から離れた郊外に置かれた。」) (Edo only, and a great lord's house.)
  • kotobank-shimoyashiki - 「In Edo's case, gardens for the lord's amusement and rest, and vegetable plots and bamboo groves to supply necessary goods to the upper residence and others, were made; in addition, lower residences along the seashore or riverbanks were also used as landing places for cargo.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸の場合，藩主の遊興・保養のための庭園，上屋敷などに必要物資を補給するための菜園や竹林などが造られたほか，海岸・河岸沿いの下屋敷は荷揚げ場としても用いられた。」) (the Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten's entry; it says this of Edo only.)
  • edo-hantei-jawiki - 「Residences a daimyo built on land he bought from private owners, such as farmland, are called kakae-yashiki. Kakae-yashiki were generally in Edo's suburbs and were used for various purposes depending on the domain, such as lower residences.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大名が民間の所有する農地などの土地を購入し建築した屋敷は、抱屋敷（かかえやしき）と呼ばれる。抱屋敷は総じて江戸の郊外にあり、下屋敷など藩により様々な用途に使用された。」) (the page's words are privately owned farmland "and the like", so not every such house stood on farmland.)
  • kotobank-shimoyashiki - 「In castle towns other than Edo too, there are cases of domain lords and upper-ranking retainers setting them up besides the castle or main residence.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸以外の城下町でも，藩主や上級藩士が城館や本邸のほかに設ける例がある。」) (the Yamakawa entry; "there are cases" is its own hedge, and it gives no count and no place.)
  • kotobank-shimoyashiki - 「An east-west street in the southern part of the castle town's outer town (soto-machi), below Mt. Magata.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「城下外（そと）町南部、間形（まがた）山下の東西の通り。」) and 「A samurai district also called Soto-no-Shimo-yashiki-chō; street width 3 ken, total length 237 ken. Both sides were the Inada family's lower residence and their retainers' residences (per 'Michigusa' and others).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「外ノ下屋敷丁ともよばれる武家地で、通幅三間、惣長二三七間。両側とも稲田氏の下屋敷および家臣の屋敷となっていた（「味地草」など）。」) (the Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei's entry for Sumoto, of which Kotobank prints only a part; it does not say what the houses held. The feet are this page's arithmetic at about six feet to the ken.)
  • liuyuan-zhwiki - 「Liuyuan lies outside Changmen, northwest of the old city of Suzhou. It was begun in the 21st year of Wanli in the Ming (1593) as a private garden, named Dongyuan, designed and built by the noted rockwork master Zhou Shichen at the invitation of Xu Taishi, Vice Minister of the Court of the Imperial Stud, who had by then been removed from office.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「留园位于苏州古城西北的阊门外，始建于明万历二十一年（1593年），为当时已罢官的太仆寺少卿徐泰时邀请叠石名人周时臣设计建造的私家园林，名东园。」) (one garden, in Suzhou; the page does not say whether its owner lived there or in town, nor how far it lay from the wall.)
