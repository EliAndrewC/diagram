  • edo-hantei-jawiki - 「The lower residences among the Edo domain residences mainly had a large role as secondary residences with gardens and the like, and most were placed in the suburbs away from Edo Castle.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (Edo only, and a great lord's house.)
  • kotobank-shimoyashiki - 「In Edo's case, gardens for the lord's amusement and rest, and vegetable plots, bamboo groves and the like to supply necessary goods to the upper residence and others, were made; in addition, lower residences along the seashore or riverbanks were also used as landing places for cargo.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Yamakawa Nihonshi Shojiten's entry; it says this of Edo only.)
  • edo-hantei-jawiki - 「Residences a daimyo built on land he bought from private owners, such as farmland, are called kakae-yashiki. Kakae-yashiki were generally in Edo's suburbs and were used for various purposes depending on the domain, such as lower residences.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the page's words are privately owned farmland "and the like", so not every such house stood on farmland.)
  • kotobank-shimoyashiki - 「In castle towns other than Edo too, there are cases of domain lords and upper-ranking retainers setting them up besides the castle or main residence.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Yamakawa entry; "there are cases" is its own hedge, and it gives no count and no place.)
  • kotobank-shimoyashiki - 「An east-west street in the southern part of the castle town's outer town (soto-machi), below Mt. Magata.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) and 「A samurai district also called Soto-no-Shimo-yashiki-chō; street width 3 ken, total length 237 ken. Both sides were the Inada family's lower residence and their retainers' residences (per 'Michigusa' and others).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei's entry, of which Kotobank prints only a part; the quoted words do not name the town - that it is Sumoto rests on where the page files the entry, under Sumoto's castle town, which is not quoted here. It does not say what the houses held. The feet are this page's arithmetic at about six feet to the ken.)
  • liuyuan-zhwiki - 「Liuyuan lies outside Changmen, northwest of the old city of Suzhou. It was begun in the 21st year of Wanli in the Ming (1593) as a private garden, named Dongyuan, designed and built by the noted rockwork master Zhou Shichen at the invitation of Xu Taishi, Vice Minister of the Court of the Imperial Stud, who had by then been removed from office.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (one garden, in Suzhou; the page does not say whether its owner lived there or in town, nor how far it lay from the wall.)
