Sources: kotobank-bukeyashiki (warriors gathered into the castle towns; country samurai with residences scattered through the villages; the low ranks on the town's edge), jokamachi-jawiki (foot soldiers placed outside the townsmen's district), sogamae-jawiki (enclosures that took in the samurai residences with the town: Funai and Hagi), satsuma-gaijo-datamax (Satsuma's retainers housed in outer districts, and the 1639 count), reimeikan-satsuma-gaijo (Satsuma's outer districts and their farming warriors), sendai-castles-jawiki (Sendai retainers' residences in their own fiefs), l7r-median-domain (county towns as the lowest level samurai live at, and the country estate), l7r-budgets (ashigaru as peasants).

The space budget above houses about two thirds of a city's samurai households inside the wall and puts the rest on estates outside it. This question asks what history says about that split.

Where the town was enclosed, the samurai were inside it. Once warriors were separated from farming, they were gathered to live in the castle towns. Inside a castle town, upper and middle retainers often lived in or next to the castle's enclosures, and the lower ranks, such as foot soldiers, at the town's edge. The foot soldiers were often placed outside the townsmen's district. Where a castle town had a sogamae, the enclosure could take in the samurai residences along with the townsmen's district. Funai's did, and at Hagi the high-ranking retainers lived inside one enclosure while the middle and lower retainers lived with the townsmen inside an outer one.

Samurai kept outside a town, foot soldiers aside, lived in the country. Some domains kept many of their retainers on the land. Satsuma could not house all its retainers in the Kagoshima castle town, so it settled them in 113 outer districts. In 1639 it counted 1,015 castle-town samurai against 8,202 rural samurai, so only about one in nine lived in the castle town. Those rural samurai, the gōshi, farmed to support themselves in peacetime. Sendai paid its retainers in land. Some of them built their own residences, with houses for their own retainers and foot soldiers, out in the fiefs they held. Generally, samurai who lived in the countryside had residences scattered through the farming villages.

What no source gives is a share: how many of one town's resident samurai households stood inside its enclosure and how many outside it.

How this lands in the setting. Satsuma's outer-district samurai and Sendai's fief-holders do not correspond to a city's out-of-wall estates. In the setting, county towns are the lowest level at which samurai live, and a samurai family may at most keep a country estate near some farmland. The domain's rural samurai are therefore already counted in the county towns. The historical foot-soldier quarters at the town's edge have no samurai counterpart either, because in the setting ashigaru are peasants. What does carry over is the enclosed castle town: where the wall took in the town, it took in the samurai quarters too. Leaving aside the foot soldiers, whom the setting makes peasants, the samurai these sources place outside a castle town lived out in the country, not in a ring just beyond its wall.

The rule the map follows: the budget keeps about two thirds of a city's samurai households inside the wall and the rest on country estates outside it. The two thirds is a guess. No readable source gives a share, and on this evidence the true figure leans higher: the history puts a town's samurai inside its enclosure and its country samurai in the villages. A samurai household drawn outside the wall is a country estate set among the fields, not a quarter against the wall.
