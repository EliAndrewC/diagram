Does a city's canal open its own mouth on the river?

Sources: pan-gate-enwiki (Suzhou's water gate joining its canals to the moat); sohu-pingjiang-suzhou (the moat joined to the city's canals through the water gates); nishiyokobori-jawiki and higashiyokobori-jawiki (Osaka canals opening straight off a river); water-gate-enwiki (a wall's gate onto a river side); the GM's ruling of 2026-07-23; the Suzhou water gate described in Most provincial cities sit on a river

Not beside the moat's own mouth, it does not: two mouths that close together spoil each other's reading, drawn as a doubled channel with a sliver of bank forking between them. History offers two ways out, and both are attested.

The first MERGES the two mouths. In the Suzhou pattern a city's canals communicate with the moat through its water gates, and the 1229 plan shows the broad moat outside the wall joined to the canals inside it that way, its western stretch being part of the Grand Canal. On these maps the moat's downstream river junction is then the single navigation entrance. Separating them would be the worse drawing for a second reason as well: a distinct mouth further downstream would have to cross the moat's southern arc at grade. So the canal ends ON the corner of the moat's outfall arm, and boats run river, then moat arm, then water gate, then dock basin.

The second gives the canal its own mouth on open bank. Osaka's canals were cut straight off its rivers: the Nishi-Yokobori, dug in 1600 by a timber merchant, branched from the Tosabori River just above a bridge and ran south to the Dotonbori, and the older Higashi-Yokobori, dug as the castle's outer moat, likewise leaves the upper Tosabori southward. Osaka's older canal was dug as a Japanese castle's outer moat, not beside a walled Chinese city's gate, but a walled city could also open onto its river directly, since a water gate is by definition a gate from the wall straight onto a quay or river side. That the second form's mouth must stand well away from the moat's junction is this project's drawing rule, from the reading problem above, not a finding.

With both forms attested, neither is the one right answer, so which a city draws is rolled per city.

The rule the map follows: a river city has ONE navigation mouth on the river, in one of two forms rolled per city: either the cargo canal ends on the moat's downstream corner and the moat arm carries the traffic in, or the canal opens its own mouth on open bank well away from the moat's junction. What the map must not draw is a canal tapping the river inside the moat's junction corridor - two mouths side by side, each spoiling the other's reading.
