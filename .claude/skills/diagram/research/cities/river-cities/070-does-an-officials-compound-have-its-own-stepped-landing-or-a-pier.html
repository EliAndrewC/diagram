Does an official's compound have its own stepped landing, or a pier?

Sources: gangi-hiroshima-jawiki (Hiroshima's public and private stepped landings, and why the samurai quarters had none with a gate)

Steps, as a rule. Why a river landing is a flight of steps cut into a faced bank, and a pier the exception for a bank that shelves too gently, is answered at "The wharf's working face". The same is recorded where the water rises and falls most: in coastal ports with a large tidal range, floating piers are used now, but in old times the bank was made into steps, the gangi.

Whose steps. On Hiroshima's six tidal rivers there are said to be some 300 to 400 stepped landings, the public ones used for landing river cargo and for river traffic, and small private ones besides. A private landing reached through a back gate belonged to the townsmen's and merchants' quarters; in the samurai quarters the residences were built back from the revetment and walled, so it is thought there were no stepped landings with a gate there. Some of the private landings standing today are modern, built after the boat licenses were abolished. No page read describes an intendant's or other official's compound with a landing of its own.

What the map draws. An official's compound on a river keeps its wall back from the bank, with the bank street between, and its landing is a flight of steps cut into the revetment across that street, reached from a gate in the wall - never a back gate opening onto the steps, the merchant's form. That the landing is the compound's own rather than a public one is a guess. A pier springs from the revetment only where the bank shelves.
