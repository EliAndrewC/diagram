Fish and produce markets at the landing (uoichiba)

A great city sold its fresh food where the boats brought it in. Edo's fish market grew up at the landing on the Nihonbashi river, because that was the handiest place to land the catch, and an 1842 plan of it shows a dozen piers, fifty sheds and over a hundred stalls along the bank.   • Edo's fish market centered on the landing on the north bank of the Nihonbashi river.
    The ground facing the landing was the core of the market, because it was the handiest place to land goods.
  • A plan of Edo's fish market from late in the Edo period shows piers, sheds, wells and stalls along the landing.
    The plan, of 1842, records along the landing from the Nihonbashi bridge to the Edobashi twelve piers at the bank's edge; behind them, back sheds along the bank for holding the catch and front sheds for selling, fifty in all; twenty-five aqueduct wells; and 126 display stalls and shopfronts pushing out into the street, many of the stalls only 3 shaku (about 3 ft) across.
  • How were the fish laid out for sale?
    On flat boards, ita-bune, about 70 cm (~2 ft) wide and 150 cm (~5 ft) long. The plan does not show the boards; that the narrow stalls were crowded with them is the ward's own inference.
  • Osaka's fish market, the Zakoba, stood on a canal bank near the river mouth.
    It stood on the east bank of the upper Hyakkenbori canal, near the river mouth where the city's two great waterways divide, which favored bringing the fish in.
  • The Zakoba met three times a day.
    Morning, noon and evening.
  • Osaka kept its fish, rice and produce at three separate markets.
    The Zakoba was one of Osaka's three great markets, with the Dojima rice market and the Tenma produce market, and the river fish had a market of their own again at the Kyobashi bridgehead.
  • Osaka's Tenma produce market stood along the Okawa river.
    The Japanese encyclopedia article on the market places it there.
  • A Japanese dictionary says fish landings are found all over the country.
    It defines a fish landing as a landing holding a fish market that wholesales to the trade, though it gives no date.
