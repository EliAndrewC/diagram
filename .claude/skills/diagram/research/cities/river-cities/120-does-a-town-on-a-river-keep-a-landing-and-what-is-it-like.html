Does a town on a river keep a landing, and what is it like?

Sources: kashi-jawiki (where landings grew, and what a landing held), kotobank-kashi (a landing as a town in its own right, and who lived there), zhouzhuang-zhwiki (a Chinese market town's stone landings)

Yes: a town on navigable water keeps a landing, and in Japan the landing was often what made the town. The Edo-period river landing, the kashi, flourished at river junctions and forks, at crossings with the main highways, and in castle towns and temple-gate towns. In its administration a landing was a town or a village, with the same town or village officers as any other, and the landing's wholesaler often held that office.

What it was. A landing had its boat moorings and its unloading ground, together sometimes called the kashiba, and at its center the wholesaler or shipping agent who ran it; wholesalers and their storehouses gathered there into a commercial settlement. Because goods lay over there in quantity, a market formed and merchants gathered. Its residents were boat owners, the boatmen and sailors they hired, and the porters and day laborers who loaded and unloaded, with tea houses and inns for shippers, their agents and travelers. The word itself names the landing's oldest fixture: it first meant a pole carried on the boat, then the mooring post fixed at a landing, and from that the landing where boats unloaded.

A Chinese market town on the water keeps the same thing in stone: Zhouzhuang, a canal market town near Suzhou, still has its stone landing steps beside its stone arch bridges. The landing's working face - a faced bank with stepped landings, a pier where the water is shallow - is the one the wharf's working face describes, at a town's smaller length.

The rule the map follows: a town on navigable water gets a landing as its freight link: a stretch of faced bank with one or two stepped landings and mooring posts, an open unloading ground behind it, the landing wholesaler's house and a few storehouses at its head, and a tea house or inn near it. The landing is placed where the highway or the town's main street meets the water, this page's reading of the highway crossings above. How long the faced stretch runs for a town is a guess scaled down from the city wharf; no page read gives a town landing's length.
