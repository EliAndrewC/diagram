Urban fabric: the research behind the frontage and row-packing rules

Urban commoners built in continuous street walls

Sources: kyomachiya-jawiki, kyototuu-unagi, nagaya-jawiki

  • What the history says. Urban commoners in both reference cultures did not build detached-with-yard. Machiya street frontage was continuous - party walls or touching eaves (unsourced), because street frontage was taxed and precious;1 back-lot3 nagaya were literally ONE ROOF over a row of family units;2 Chinese county-seat courtyard housing shared rammed-earth or brick party walls in continuous street walls (unsourced). Detached-with-yard was a samurai (and rural) form (unsourced).

The frontage tax is disputed. "street frontage was taxed" is the popular explanation of the narrow lot (kyototuu-unagi carries it), and ja.wikipedia 京町家 disputes it outright - Edo-period Kyoto assessed a lump sum per town, not per frontage.4 The continuous built edge is read either way; the tax is not the reason to cite.

Machiya row density: a commercial street is a continuous built edge

Sources: kyototuu-unagi (frontage ~2 ken, depth 10-12 ken),5 kyomachiya-jawiki, hiyokechi-jawiki (breaks at block scale after 1657).6 See the frontage-tax note in the entry above

The question (GM, 2026-08-11), looking at Shiro Daika's north gate market: "Is that the correct amount of space between gate market buildings? No objection, they just look more spaced out than I expected."

What the research says. The Japanese urban shophouse (machiya) was built to the street and to its neighbors. Frontage was the taxed and traded dimension, so lots were narrow and deep:10 a Kyoto kyo-machiya typically fronted 2-3 ken12 (about 12-18 ft) on a lot running 60 ft or more back,11 and the fronts abutted, sharing party walls and a continuous eave line down the block (unsourced). The street read as a WALL of shopfronts pierced by gateways and alley mouths, not as a row of detached boxes (unsourced). Post-fire policy in Edo cut firebreaks (hiyokechi) and broad avenues THROUGH the fabric13 and pushed tile roofing (unsourced), but it never spaced individual machiya apart - the breaks were at block scale, and the block itself stayed solid.14

The decision. A dense frontage row measures its row mates edge to edge along the row's own axis, keeps its own street clear alongside whatever else it is told to avoid, and accepts a road that bends inside the stretch it fronts.

The deliberate departure from literal reality. True machiya shared party walls - a gap of ZERO. We land at a median of 18 ft on the capital's north and south gate markets (9 ft on the crowded southwest strip, 48 ft on the loose roadside market at the east gate, which is a different thing and should stay loose). An 18 ft gap is enough that a reader can still count individual shops and see which way each one faces, which is the whole job of the drawing. The relative sizes stay honest; only the mortar joint is drawn wider than life.

A county seat's street share, open reserve and civic share

Sources: chang-morphology-walled-capitals (the street grids, the intramural farmland, and Jinan's lake at a fifth of the walled area;7 the further anchors - Quanzhou a quarter vacant in 1945, Suzhou's intramural farmland, Pingyao's "4 big streets, 8 small streets, 72 lanes" - are unsourced)

A Chinese county seat ran a sparse street net - one or two gate-to-gate trunks in a cross or T with the drum tower at the crossing,8 everything else lanes - at roughly 10-20% of the walled ground, and normally enclosed 25-30% (the figure unsourced) deliberately unbuilt ground:16 walls were sized to administrative rank and growth,15 and intramural fields, gardens and ponds were siege insurance9 and flood refuge (unsourced). Civic buildings alone took only a few percent (unsourced); the big open consumer was the drill ground (unsourced) and the under-built remainder. The maps draw streets at ~7% (deep blocks, alley warrens) and admit open ground only as a declared, drawn line (agricultural district, drill ground, gardens), never as ambient slack.
