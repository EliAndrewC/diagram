  • chinanews-ancient-firefighting - 「Ming local government at the base level had a zongjia responsible for fire and theft. According to the Wanshu zaji, the county town of Wanping in Beijing then had resident organizations such as fang and pu, each pu with a head and three to five firemen, all managed by the zongjia. They kept watch by night in turn, striking clappers and ringing bells to remind people to beware of fire and theft. In some places a patrol post (watch post) was also set up, where the zongjia and the firemen could rest and shelter from rain and snow. In the patrol post were kept knives, spears and other weapons, and fire-hooks, buckets and other firefighting equipment.」 and 「In the eighth year of Qianlong (1743) the governor of Hunan, Jiang Bo, instructed every department and county to organize volunteer fire companies specifically responsible for putting out fires. These companies were all to be registered on rolls and reported to the authorities, which issued them a fixed sum of silver each year.」 (translated; ) (a news-agency feature on firefighting in the Chinese past, quoting the Ming Wanshu zaji for the county town. It carries an organized Ming night watch, described for Wanping in Beijing, patrol posts set up in some other places, and fire companies ordered for every department and county of Hunan in 1743; it does not carry a standing lookout tower outside the capital.)
