• jinya-kotobank - 「In the Edo period the name jin'ya was given to the residence of a small castle-less daimyo or of a kotaiyoriai on his granted land, to the out-of-castle-town mansion of a great domain's senior retainer, to the post of the waterworks construction officer, and to the offices of the local officials who administered a domain's detached holdings or a high-stipend bannerman's fief; and the daikansho, the official residence in a district administered by a shogunal district governor or intendant, also went by the name daikan-jin'ya.」 and 「Generally in an intendant's jin'ya there were set up, within the grounds, the honjin that was the intendant's official residence, the office for public business, the official residences of the subordinate staff, the rice store and the levy store, and the judgment yard and the gaol. Where a settlement grew up around a jin'ya as its core and became a town, it was also called a jin'ya-machi, and often flourished as the central town of the territory administered.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代には無城の小大名や交替寄合の拝領地における居館，大藩重臣の城下町外の屋敷，用水方普請役の詰所，藩飛地領や高禄旗本の知行地の支配にあたった地方役人の役所などを陣屋とよび，幕府郡代・代官の支配地における役宅である代官所も代官陣屋の別称があった。」 and original: 「一般に代官陣屋では敷地内に代官の官舎である本陣，公用執務の役所，属僚の役宅，米蔵・運上蔵や白洲・牢屋などが設けられていた。陣屋を核に集落が発達し，町場化した場合には陣屋町ともよばれ，支配領域の中核都市として活況を呈する場合が多かった。」) (the published encyclopedia makes the identification the entry needs: the intendant's office IS the daikan-jin'ya, and the town that grows round one is the jin'ya-machi. It also names the rice store inside the compound, which is the thing the paragraph says was fireproofed.)
  • jishinban-jawiki - 「The jishinban patrolled the neighborhood and, if there was a suspicious person, seized them and reported them to the magistrate's office. Fire watch was also an important duty, and many watch houses were fitted with a ladder on the roof (a small-scale fire-watch tower) and a hansho bell. For this reason, arrest tools and firefighting tools - the matoi standard, the tobiguchi hook, the ryudosui pump, the genba bucket, ladders and well-buckets - were kept ready inside the watch house, and when the hansho was struck the town officers and firefighting hands rushed to the jishinban, took out the tools, assembled, and then went to the fire.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「自身番は町内を見回り、不審者がいれば捕らえて奉行所に訴えた。また、火の番も重要な役割であり、自身番屋の多くには、屋根に梯子（小規模な火の見櫓）や半鐘が備えられていた。このため、捕り物道具や纏・鳶口・竜吐水・玄蕃桶（げんばおけ）・梯子・釣瓶といった火消道具が番屋内に用意され、半鐘が鳴らされると町役人・火消人足が自身番にかけつけて道具を持ち出し、勢揃いしてから火事場に赴いた。」) (the ladder on the roof, called a small-scale fire tower by the page itself, the bell, and the stored gear - all of it at the TOWN's watch house.)
  • hinomi-yagura-jawiki - 「It was usual for a watch house (also called bansho or jishinban) to be set up in each such town and a watchman (called hiban, bantaro or banta) stationed there permanently, keeping guard around the clock. A frame was then raised on the watch house so that the watchman could look over the whole town, and a lookout platform was placed one level up; this is what is called a hinomiyagura.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この町ごとに番屋（番所、自身番とも）を設置し番人（火番、番太郎・番太と呼ばれていた）を常駐させて24時間態勢で警戒にあたるのが一般的であった。このとき番人が町全体を見渡せるよう番屋に櫓を組んで一段高いところに見張台を置いたが、それが火の見櫓と呼ばれる。」)
  • hyoshigi-jawiki - 「When a keibodan or a fire brigade and the like make their rounds at night, they walk calling out "mind your locks, mind your fire" while striking the clappers together, clack-clack.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「警防団や消防団などが夜、見回る時に、 「戸締り用心、火の用心」と声をあげながら、拍子木をカチカチッと打ち鳴らして歩く。」) (the practice and its call. The bodies the page names for it - keibodan, shobodan - are modern, and the page does not date the round to the Edo period.)
  • tensuioke-jawiki - 「The tensuioke is a traditional Japanese firefighting water cistern. It is a vessel for storing rainwater, and in the Edo period it was used mainly as firefighting water in urban areas.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「天水桶（てんすいおけ）とは、日本の伝統的な防火水槽である。雨水を貯めるための容器で、江戸時代には主に都市部の防火用水として利用された。」)
  • dozo-jawiki - 「Storehouses are divided according to what they hold - rice store, miso store, unhulled-rice store, gunpowder store, money store, sutra store and so on - and have the function of protecting these from fire, damp, theft and the like.」 and 「The wall thickness is often about 300 mm or more, and the outer doors of the openings are sometimes made as earth doors (doors whose outer surface is covered with earth and plaster). In the great fires of the Edo period and, in modern times, in the great fires caused by air raids, there were many cases in which the fire did not get through to the interior.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「蔵は収納物によって、米蔵、味噌蔵、籾蔵、硝煙蔵、金蔵、経蔵などに分けられ、これらを火災、湿気、盗難等から守る機能をもつ。」 and original: 「壁厚は約300mm以上あることが多く、開口部の外戸なども土戸（土と漆喰で戸の外部表面を覆ったもの）とすることがある。古くは江戸時代の大火、近代では空襲による大火でも、内部に火が回らない事例が多かった。」)
  • gogura-jawiki - 「The gogura was a storehouse established in various places in the Edo period. It was placed one per village, or one per several villages, as a temporary storage place for carrying out tax rice and other farm produce, and was kept under strict guard.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「郷倉（ごうぐら、郷蔵）は江戸時代、各地に設けられた倉庫である。年貢米を始めとする農作物を運び出す際の一時保管場所として、1か村ないし数か村ごとに1か所置かれ、厳重な警備体制を敷いていた。」) (reached through the article on the charitable granary, which carries the gogura's own description.)

  • hinomi-yagura-jawiki - 「Beginning with Edo in the Edo period, fire-watch towers were developed together with the firefighting system, and by the early Showa period they had been installed in almost every region of the country.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「火の見櫓は江戸時代の江戸を皮切りに火消体制とともに整備されてゆき、昭和初期には全国ほぼ全ての地域に整備されていった。」) (the tower is an Edo-period institution that spread outward; the page dates the reach, not the origin, to Showa.)
