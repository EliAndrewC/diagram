  • hiyokechi-jawiki - 「Hiyokechi are fire-prevention open lots that the Edo bakufu established in Edo, prompted by the Great Meireki Fire of Meireki 3 (1657).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「火除地（ひよけち）とは、江戸幕府が明暦3年（1657年）の明暦の大火をきっかけに江戸に設置した防火用の空地」) / 「After that fire, five burned-over sites were assigned as hiyokechi, and thereafter, mainly to keep fire from spreading to Edo Castle, they were added little by little, centered on the castle's northwest side, growing to as many as 13 sites by the Kyoho era.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「同大火後に焼け跡5ヶ所を火除地に充てた他、以後も主として江戸城への類焼を防止する観点から江戸城の北西側を中心に少しずつ増やされて享保年間には13ヶ所にも増大された。」) (the page never discusses the spacing of individual houses.)
  • kyototuu-unagi - 「because tax was levied according to the width of a house's frontage, it is said that frontages were narrowed and deep 'eel's bed' style machiya were built to save tax.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「家の間口の広さに応じて課税をしたことで、間口は狭くして、奥行きのある「鰻の寝床」様式の町家を建てて節税をしたといわれる」) (The "traded dimension" half is on no page read.)
  • kyomachiya-jawiki - 「There is also a theory that the shape was a reaction to taxation that treated a three-ken (about 5.4 m) frontage as one household's share.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また三間（約5.4m）の間口を一軒役として課税したことへの反発した形状とする説もある。」) (the page does not itself state a typical frontage of 3 ken. Together with the 京町家 page's 「the frontage is narrow and the depth is deep」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「間口が狭く奥行きが深い」) this is adequate for "narrow", weak for "3 ken typical".)

  • udatsu-jawiki - 「Where machiya stood adjoining one another and were built continuously, they came to be built as firewalls to prevent a fire from the neighboring house from spreading across.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町屋が隣り合い連続して建てられている場合に隣家からの火事が燃え移るのを防ぐための防火壁として造られていった」) (abutting, continuous fronts; 「sharing party walls」 is the nagaya's form on the pages read, and the eave line follows from the adjoining roof)
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「Entry to and exit from the back nagaya was through a wooden gate and an alley set between two front shops.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「裏長屋へは、2軒の表店の間に設けられた木戸と路地から出入りした。」) (the frontage pierced by gate and alley; the same page captions a post station's merchant houses 「Sekijuku, a post station on the Tokaido, whose merchant houses stand eave to eave.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「商家が軒を連ねる東海道の宿場関宿。」))
  • hiyokechi-jawiki - 「fire-prevention open ground that the Edo bakufu established in Edo, prompted by the Great Meireki Fire of Meireki 3 (1657)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸幕府が明暦3年（1657年）の明暦の大火をきっかけに江戸に設置した防火用の空地」) / 「In the broad sense it also includes hirokoji and the like - streets made with the same purpose.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「広義では、同様の趣旨を持った街路である広小路なども含まれる。」) (the page also lists named ones (両国広小路, 湯島広小路, 御茶水広小路, 中橋広小路 ...).)
  • edo-no-kaji-jawiki - 「From Kyoho 7 (1722) the use of tile roofing, storehouse construction and nuriya (buildings plastered with earth on the outside) began to be ordered in various places throughout the city of Edo.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「享保7年（1722年）からは江戸市中の各所で瓦葺・土蔵造り・塗り屋（外部に土を塗った建物）の使用を命じるようになった。」) (the 1720s push; the same page has tile PROHIBITED after Meireki and the mandate only under Yoshimune, so this policy is sixty-five years after the firebreaks)
  • hiyokechi-jawiki - 「After that fire, five burned-over sites were assigned as hiyokechi, and thereafter, mainly to keep fire from spreading to Edo Castle, they were added little by little, centered on the castle's northwest side, growing to as many as 13 sites by the Kyoho era.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「同大火後に焼け跡5ヶ所を火除地に充てた他、以後も主として江戸城への類焼を防止する観点から江戸城の北西側を中心に少しずつ増やされて享保年間には13ヶ所にも増大された。」) (it contains no rule about spacing between houses. "Never spaced individual machiya apart" is therefore an inference from what the page does NOT say, not a statement it makes.)
  • udatsu-jawiki - 「The wall of the keraba portion (the two ends of a gable roof) where it meets the neighboring house was raised slightly and given an independent small roof; this came to be called an udatsu (hon-udatsu).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「隣家と接するケラバ（切妻屋根の両端）部分の壁を少し持ち上げ、独立した小屋根を乗せたものを「うだつ」と呼ぶようになった（本うだつ）。」)
