  • kyoto-city-jobosei - 「The size of one machi was fixed at forty jo (about 120 meters) a side, excluding the roads.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kyoto-city-jobosei - 「One bo was further divided into sixteen by three lanes (about 12 meters wide) each way, lengthwise and crosswise, and one of those divisions was made a cho.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-machiwari - 「[Edo's town plan] takes the form of a block 60 ken (39 jo) square in Kyoto measure, with an open ground 20 ken square called the kaishochi taken at its center, lots 20 ken deep laid along the streets, and a town made of the two sides facing each other across a street.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • ginza-machidukuri-machiwari - 「The town plan of the commoners' quarter took as its unit a square sixty ken (about 120 m) on a side in Kyoto measure.」 and 「Inside the square, an open ground called the kaishochi was taken at the very middle, and town lots 20 ken deep were laid out along the streets.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-machiwari - 「However, square blocks in the early modern castle towns were limited, outside Edo, to a few such as Sunpu (Shizuoka) and Nagoya; the great majority are rectangular blocks that take no open ground.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-machiwari - 「In Kyoto too, a rebuilding of the town plan was carried out by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1591 (Tensho 19): in the blocks around the central district a north-south lane was run through the middle, and the square blocks were remade into rectangular blocks.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • ginza-machidukuri-machiwari - 「After that, almost all of it burned in the Great Meireki Fire (1657), and the town of Edo was laid out again.」 and 「At that time, as new lanes (shinmichi), the two between Ginza-dori and Namiki-dori (the streets that are now Konparu-dori and Suzuran-dori, the street corresponding to Gasutou-dori, Nishi-gobangai and Ginza Renga-dori, and the street corresponding to Kenban-dori) were made.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; The page speaks of Ginza only.)
  • jokamachi-jawiki - 「On entering the castle town, by setting houses along both sides of the trunk road with no gaps, the castle was made hard to see, and by such means as bending the roads into key shapes [right-angle dog-legs] and providing dead-end alleys, the distance to reach the castle was lengthened.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • roji-jawiki - 「The roji of Edo, and especially the roji of Edo's commoners' quarter, were not places that created such bustle; it was the main streets alone that carried that function.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
