• kotobank-machiwari - 「The blocks 40 jo (about 120 m) square, cut off by the checkerboard roads of Heian-kyo's avenues and lanes, were called machi」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平安京の大路・小路の碁盤目状の道路によって区切られる，40丈（約120m）四方の街区は〈町（まち）〉と呼ばれ」)
  • kyoto-city-jobosei - 「The size of one machi was fixed at forty jo (about 120 meters) a side, excluding the roads.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一町の規模は道路を除いて一辺四十丈(約120メートル)と固定されていました。」)
  • kyoto-city-jobosei - 「One bo was further divided into sixteen by three lanes (about 12 meters wide) each way, lengthwise and crosswise, and one of those divisions was made a cho.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「一つの坊をさらにたてよこ各3本の小路(幅約12メートル)で16区分し，その一区画を町(ちょう)としました。」)
  • kotobank-machiwari - 「[Edo's town plan] takes the form of a block 60 ken (39 jo) square in Kyoto measure, with an open ground 20 ken square called the kaishochi taken at its center, lots 20 ken deep laid along the streets, and a town made of the two sides facing each other across a street.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「京間で60間（39丈）四方の街区中央に，20間四方の会所地（かいしよち）と呼ぶ空地をとり，街路沿いに奥行き20間の敷地を配し，街路をはさんだ両側で町を作る形態をとっている。」)
  • ginza-machidukuri-machiwari - 「The town plan of the commoners' quarter took as its unit a square sixty ken (about 120 m) on a side in Kyoto measure.」 and 「Inside the square, an open ground called the kaishochi was taken at the very middle, and town lots 20 ken deep were laid out along the streets.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町人地の町割は、京間六十間（約120m）四方の正方形が一単位。」; original: 「正方形の中は、まんなかに会所地とよばれる空地をとって、通り沿いに奥行き20間の町屋敷が配置されていました。」)
  • kotobank-machiwari - 「However, square blocks in the early modern castle towns were limited, outside Edo, to a few such as Sunpu (Shizuoka) and Nagoya; the great majority are rectangular blocks that take no open ground.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「ただ近世城下町における正方形街区は，江戸以外では駿府（静岡），名古屋など少数に限られ，大部分は空地をとらない長方形街区である。」)
  • kotobank-machiwari - 「In Kyoto too, a rebuilding of the town plan was carried out by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1591 (Tensho 19): in the blocks around the central district a north-south lane was run through the middle, and the square blocks were remade into rectangular blocks.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「なお京都でも，1591年（天正19）に豊臣秀吉によって町割改造が行われ，中心部の周辺の街区で中央に南北の小路を通し，正方形街区を長方形街区に作り変えている。」)
  • ginza-machidukuri-machiwari - 「After that, almost all of it burned in the Great Meireki Fire (1657), and the town of Edo was laid out again.」 and 「At that time, as new lanes (shinmichi), the two between Ginza-dori and Namiki-dori (the streets that are now Konparu-dori and Suzuran-dori, the street corresponding to Gasutou-dori, Nishi-gobangai and Ginza Renga-dori, and the street corresponding to Kenban-dori) were made.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「その後、明暦の大火（1657）によってほとんどが焼失し、江戸の町は再整備されました。」; original: 「そのときに、新道（しんみち）として、銀座通りと並木通りの間の２本（今の金春通り・すずらん通り、ガス灯通りにあたる通り、西五番街や銀座レンガ通り、見番通りにあたる通り）がつくられたのです。」 The page speaks of Ginza only.)
  • jokamachi-jawiki - 「On entering the castle town, by setting houses along both sides of the trunk road with no gaps, the castle was made hard to see, and by such means as bending the roads into key shapes [right-angle dog-legs] and providing dead-end alleys, the distance to reach the castle was lengthened.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「城下に入ると幹線道路の両脇に家屋を隙間なく配置させることで城を見え難くして、道を鍵形に曲げたり袋小路を設けるなどすることで、城への到達距離を延長した。」)
  • roji-jawiki - 「The roji of Edo, and especially the roji of Edo's commoners' quarter, were not places that created such bustle; it was the main streets alone that carried that function.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸の路地、特に江戸町人地の路地はそのような賑わいを作り出す場ではなく、その機能を担ったのは表通りだけだった」)
