What was a back alley like underfoot?

Sources: roji-jawiki (where an alley began, its gate, and the drain boards down its middle), ginza-machidukuri-machiwari (the shops on the street, the tenements up the alley, and the shared privy and well)

An Edo back alley was a narrow lane, not a street. It began at a gap between two merchant houses in the row facing the main street, and ran in past the shops, which a Ginza column pictures as some three to five ken (about 18 to 30 ft in the Edo ken, or 19 to 32 ft in the longer Kyoto ken the column lays its blocks out in; it does not say which it means here) wide at the front, to the tenement rows standing on both sides of it. Its mouth was closed by a gate with a door, the alley gate, opened at dawn and shut at dusk, about six in the evening. How narrow it was between the rows is on the question of how deep the rows stack.

Underfoot, the middle of the alley was a line of drain boards laid over a small ditch six or seven sun (about 7 to 8 inches) wide. The privy and the well were shared. What the ground either side of the boards was - beaten earth, gravel or anything else - no page read says.

So a back alley on these maps is a narrow lane with a line of boards down its middle. The boards are accurate; the gravel the maps have given it rests on nothing read, and the ground either side of the boards is a GUESS, best drawn as plain earth.
