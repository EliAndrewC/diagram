Where does the Imperial road run through a city, and where is it labeled?

Sources: the GM's setting notes for the Imperial roads and for who maintains a city street; chang-morphology-walled-capitals (what most surviving Chinese city plans do with the street between two opposite gates)

An Imperial road is Imperial property and an Imperial responsibility. The same roadway inside a city's walls is a city street, which the city maintains. That is the whole reason the label sits outside the gate: the words name a jurisdiction, and at the gate the jurisdiction changes.

The straight gate-to-gate run is the setting's form rather than the usual historical one, and the entry below on a county seat's street net says why. The regular Chinese models put a cross or a T of trunk streets between the gates, but most surviving plans deliberately deflect the trunk so that no street runs uninterrupted from one gate to the opposite one.38 A road the Empire drove through the country, with the city grown against it, runs straight through: a deviation, taken knowingly.

The rule the map follows: where a city stands on the Imperial road, the road runs straight north to south through it, gate to gate, and off both edges of the sheet; with the implied east-west axis it quarters the interior. The label "Imperial Road" is placed beyond a gate, outside the walls, never on the stretch between them. No other road on a settlement map is labeled at all.
