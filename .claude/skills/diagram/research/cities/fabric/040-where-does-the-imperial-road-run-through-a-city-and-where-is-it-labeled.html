Where does the Imperial road run through a city, and where is it labeled?

Sources: l7r-budgets (the GM's setting notes: who pays for an Imperial road's upkeep, and who keeps up a domain's own roads); chang-morphology-walled-capitals (what most surviving Chinese city plans do with the street between two opposite gates)

In the setting, keeping an Imperial road in repair is a permanent Imperial commitment funded from the central treasury, while a domain's everyday roads are built and kept up by its own daimyo, provincial governors and county magistrates. This record reads the roadway inside a city's walls as one of those local works - a city street, kept by the city's own government rather than by the Empire; the notes do not say so in as many words. That is the whole reason the label sits outside the gate: the words name a jurisdiction, and at the gate the jurisdiction changes.

The straight gate-to-gate run is the setting's form rather than the usual historical one, and the entry below on a county seat's street net says why. The regular Chinese models put a cross or a T of trunk streets between the gates, but most surviving plans deliberately deflect the trunk so that no street runs uninterrupted from one gate to the opposite one. A road the Empire drove through the country, with the city grown against it, runs straight through: a deviation, taken knowingly.

The rule the map follows: where a city stands on the Imperial road, the road runs straight north to south through it, gate to gate, and off both edges of the sheet; with the implied east-west axis it quarters the interior. The label "Imperial Road" is placed beyond a gate, outside the walls, never on the stretch between them. No other road on a settlement map is labeled at all.
