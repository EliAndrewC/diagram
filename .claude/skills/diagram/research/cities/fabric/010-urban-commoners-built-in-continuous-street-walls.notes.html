• jokamachi-jawiki - 「The townsman district had a smaller area per household than the samurai district, and stood built up along the highway with no gaps between the buildings.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町人地は侍町よりも世帯当たりの面積が狭く、街道沿いに隙間なく建ち並んだ。」); machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「Compared with a farmhouse, whose main building stands deep inside a gated lot, the characteristic of the machiya is that they stand built in a relatively even row facing the street.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「農家が、門を構えた敷地の奥に主屋が建つのに比べ、通りに面して比較的均等に建ち並ぶ点に特徴がある」) (the Japanese half, the townhouse page setting the street-facing row against the gated farmhouse plot; the Chinese half is on no page read - zh.wikipedia 四合院 gives the blank street wall and nothing on neighbors sharing one)
  • udatsu-jawiki - 「Where machiya stood adjoining one another and were built continuously, they came to be built as firewalls to prevent a fire from the neighboring house from spreading across.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町屋が隣り合い連続して建てられている場合に隣家からの火事が燃え移るのを防ぐための防火壁として造られていった」) (adjoining, continuous machiya with the roof between one house and its neighbor; 「touching eaves」 is what the page carries, the shared wall being the nagaya's)
  • kyototuu-unagi - 「Toyotomi Hideyoshi taxed houses according to the width of their frontage, under the name jiguchi-sen, so it is said that people narrowed the frontage and built deep 'eel's bed' style machiya to save on tax.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「豊臣秀吉は、「地口銭（じぐちせん）」と称される、家の間口の広さに応じて課税をしたことで、間口は狭くして、奥行きのある「鰻の寝床」様式の町家を建てて節税をしたといわれる」) (Note the page's own hedge といわれる ("it is said").)
  • nagaya-jawiki - 「In the Edo period in urban areas, especially dense ones like Edo, merchant houses of the middle level and above kept independent shops on the main street, but the rest of the townsfolk and craftsmen almost all lived as tenants in nagaya in the back streets.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代において都市部、とくに稠密な江戸などでは中層以上の商家などは表通りに独立した店を構えていたが、それ以外の町人、職人などはほとんどが裏町の長屋に借家住まいだった。」)
  • nagaya-jawiki - 「A thing in which several dwelling units are joined in a row horizontally and share walls. Put another way, a single building divided horizontally, each part made an independent dwelling.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「複数の住戸が水平方向に連なり、壁を共有する物。別の言い方をするなら、1棟の建物を水平方向に区分し、それぞれ独立した住戸とした物。」)

  • bukeyashiki-wiki - 「A buke-yashiki is a residence, enclosed by a hedge, a wall, a gate and the like, in which a warrior household lived.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「武家屋敷（ぶけやしき）は、武家が居住した生垣や塀、門等に囲まれた屋敷」) (the enclosed plot of the UPPER samurai; the same page puts ashigaru in row barracks, and 「(and rural)」 is on no page read)
  • kyomachiya-jawiki - 「There is also a theory that the shape was a reaction to taxation that treated a three-ken (about 5.4 m) frontage as one household's share. In fact, however, the Edo-period Kyoto tax system, regardless of frontage width, first assessed a total on the town, which the house-owning townsmen making up that town then divided among themselves - that is, a system of so much per house.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また三間（約5.4m）の間口を一軒役として課税したことへの反発した形状とする説もある。しかし実際は、江戸時代の京都の税制は、間口幅に関係なく、まず町に対し総額が賦課され、それを、その町を構成する家持町人が分担する、つまり１軒いくらで負担する制度であった。」)
  • udatsu-jawiki - 「The part where the roof between one's own house and the neighboring house is raised a little came to be called the udatsu.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「自家と隣家との間の屋根を少し持ち上げた部分を「うだつ」と呼ぶようになった。」) (separate houses built adjoining; the drawn hairline seam is a map drawing convention)
  • roji-jawiki - 「The width of the roji between one back-tenement building and the next was about three to six shaku (about 0.9 m to 1.8 m).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「裏長屋の建物と建物の間の路地の幅は3尺から6尺ほど（0.9メートルから1.8メートルほど）だった」) (the lane between one tenement building and the next, with a drain down its middle; a ridge-split pair shares its rear wall at zero, so the gap is between terraces, and 「eave」 is this page's)
  • nagaya-jawiki - 「A thing in which multiple dwelling units run together horizontally and share walls.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「複数の住戸が水平方向に連なり、壁を共有する物。」) (the shared wall; the 18 ft drawn frontage gap is this project's own measurement)
