What lines the Imperial road where it runs through the city? A ribbon of shops

Sources: jokamachi-jawiki; l7r-budgets (the GM's setting notes: a provincial city's merchant share against the other tiers'); this project's calibration against the three drawn provincial cities; shukuba-jawiki

A Japanese castle town built up both sides of its trunk road with houses leaving no gaps, and a post station lined its road with inns, cheap lodgings, teahouses and shops; this project reads the through-road as a city's prime frontage, because the caravans and the travelers pass along it, so both its sides carry shops and traveler lodging rather than the backs of houses or bare ground. Where no Imperial road runs through a city, this project keeps its commerce in the market district instead. The stretch that matters is the part running through the built city - bounded by the wall where there is one, by the footprint of the buildings where there is not - and the fault to watch for is a dead stretch between two quarters that both turn their backs on the road.

A provincial city's merchant households are about a quarter of its inhabitants, the same share as a domain capital's and two and a half times a town's, which is what makes a ribbon of that density affordable in households as well as in ground.

The rule the map follows: a city on the Imperial road, walled or not, lines it with shops, merchant houses and inns at roughly one commercial building per 390 ft of the stretch running through the city, and lays them as a true two-sided market street rather than a single row. That figure is a floor that catches a bare spine, calibrated against the three drawn cities and not derived from a source. Across the whole city this project takes a healthy commercial face to be about one storefront per 25 to 30 inhabitants, an estimate of its own rather than a figure from a source - about 105 shop, merchant and inn frontages for a city of 3,000 - and a road city sits at the denser end of that band, with the difference concentrated on the ribbon. A domain capital, which always has an Imperial road, always carries one.
