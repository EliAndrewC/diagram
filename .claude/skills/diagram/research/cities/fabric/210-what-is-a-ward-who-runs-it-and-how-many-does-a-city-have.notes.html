• kotobank-machikido - 「A wooden gate set up for security in the various machi of Edo. It was closed at night.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-ryogawacho - 「The side split off from the machi joined the side facing it across the street to form one cho as a both-sides ward.」 and 「this both-sides ward became the unit of the townspeople's life and was called a cho.」 and 「This appeared in Kyoto from the civil war of the Northern and Southern Courts onward, and took form everywhere out of the ruins of the Onin war.」 (translated; ) (the war of the Northern and Southern Courts began in 1336, so the fourteenth century is the record's own dating)
  • kotobank-machiyakunin - 「The monthly duty: the house-owning townsmen of the machi, or the landlords' managers, took charge of the machi's administrative business in turn every month.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-machiyakunin - 「The ward levy: the costs of running the machi were the ward levy collected from the landowners and house-owners, and the expenditure ranged widely - gold and silver paid to the shogunate such as the provincial levy, festival costs, first offerings to the gods and buddhas, the end-of-month money to the town elders, the headman's fee, the upkeep of the time bell, the wages for dredging the canals, the share of the water-supply works, the costs of the fire brigade's standard, and so on.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-machiyakunin - 「However, Edo's town elders were hereditary in the three houses of Naraya, Taruya and Kitamura, and were not chosen by the townsmen.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-edo-machinanushi - 「For reasons of cost they were not placed in each machi; it was usual for one nanushi to govern from two or three to a dozen and more machi, and there are said to have been two hundred and some in all of Edo.」 and 「Under the direction of the town elders, he handled the business of the machi he governed at his own house.」 and 「The post of machi-nanushi was hereditary, and he was not allowed to take up another trade; instead he received a salary from the machi.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-edo-machinanushi - 「It was the rule that he lived in a machi he governed, and he was not permitted another occupation.」 (translated; ) (a second encyclopedia on the same page)
  • kotobank-jobosei - 「Under the ritsuryo system, by the provisions of the household code, under the capital office, one ward head was appointed for each ward, and one ward superior for every 4 wards of each row, and they saw to the checking of households, the supervision of wrongdoing, and the pressing of taxes and labor levies.」 and 「The source of the jobo system lies, after all, in China」 (translated; )
  • lijia-zhwiki - 「Lijia: a Chinese household-registration unit or administrative division created in the 14th century, later turned into an organization or system of labor service, used in the Ming and Qing periods.」 and 「110 neighboring households in a Chinese area were organized as one li; a li governed ten jia, and one jia was ten households in all.」 and 「Within one li there are ten li-head households, and the jia-head households belonging to the ten jia, a hundred in all.」 (translated; ) (the count for a city of 600 households is the record's arithmetic)
