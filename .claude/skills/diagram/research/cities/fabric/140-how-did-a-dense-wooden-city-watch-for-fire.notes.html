  • hinomi-yagura-kotobank - 「In the Kyoho era (1716-36) one fire tower was provided to every ten machi, and in a machi without a tower a fire ladder was provided on top of the jishinban hut.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「享保年間（1716-36）には10町に一つずつ火の見櫓が設けられ，櫓のない町には自身番屋の上に火の見梯子が設けられた。」) and, from the second published encyclopedia reprinted on the same page, 「In Edo one was built for about every ten machi, and in the other machi there were places where only a ladder and a bell were fitted on the roof of the jishinban hut.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸では、およそ十か町に一か所建てられ、それ以外の町では、自身番小屋の屋上に、梯子(はしご)と半鐘を取り付けただけの所もあった。」) and, for the official stations and for what WAS per-machi, 「After the Meireki fire (1657), besides the daimyo fire brigades, which lacked quickness, the shogunate's own standing fire watch of Edo - the jobikeshi - was organized, and fire-brigade mansions were established at several places in the castle town. Fire towers were set up there, with fire watchmen standing lookout day and night, who signaled with a drum when they spotted an outbreak. The townsmen's side also formed a fire organization for its own protection, and for security a jishinban was established in every machi and a kidoban at the machi boundaries.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「明暦の大火(1657)以後，機敏性を欠く大名火消のほかに幕府直属の江戸中定火之番つまり定火消が組織され，城下数ヵ所に火消屋敷が設置された。そこには火の見櫓を設けて昼夜火の番が見張りに立ち，出火を発見すると太鼓で合図を出した。町方でも自衛の火消組織ができ，警備のために各町内に自身番，町境には木戸番が設置された。」) (two published encyclopedias on the same page agree on the ratio independently.)
