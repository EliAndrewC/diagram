Where may a merchant's compound wall stand? On dry, private ground

Sources: hinomi-yagura-jawiki; bunka-sugimoto-omoya (a wall run round a great merchant's estate), matsusaka-hasegawa-kanko (the back edge of a merchant's lot); the GM's doctrine of 2026-07-19

The temptation when placing a rich merchant's compound is to run its wall out to the water the merchant's money comes from, or across whatever public ground happens to be free. The GM's doctrine of 2026-07-19 is that it may do neither. A wall footed in water is undermined, and a canal or a dock edge is working waterfront - the boats, the porters, the towpath that makes the merchant rich - so a private wall may come up to the quay but never stand in it. The fire watch is municipal: its tower needs its own footing, daylight around the braced frame and a public way for the watchman to reach it, so it can be neither built into a private wall nor shut inside a private court. And a street, an alley or a road is the public way: a compound wall may line one - that is what a walled compound on a block looks like - but the cleared band stays open.

The lot's own edges are attested. A great merchant's house in Kyoto has a high wall run round its whole estate, and a castle-town merchant's lot runs back to the block's back-to-back drain, which is also the ward's boundary - the wall stands on the merchant's own lot, inside that line. How the house and its storehouses sit on the lot is How big is a rich merchant's walled house?.

The rule the map follows: the whole perimeter of a merchant compound, the gate gap included, stands clear of canal, dock basin, moat, river and pond; clear of every fire tower, and never closed around one; and out of the cleared band of every street, alley, road and the ring road, though it may run alongside any of them. The compound's gate opens onto open ground and never into the face of the building next door - a wall that abuts a temple turns its gate to the free side. Water and fire towers are therefore drawn before the compounds. Town and city tiers both.
