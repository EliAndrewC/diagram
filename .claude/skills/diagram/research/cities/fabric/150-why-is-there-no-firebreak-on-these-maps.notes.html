• hiyokechi-jawiki - 「A hiyokechi is an open ground for fire prevention that the Edo shogunate established in Edo in the wake of the Meireki great fire of Meireki 3 (1657).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「火除地（ひよけち）とは、江戸幕府が明暦3年（1657年）の明暦の大火をきっかけに江戸に設置した防火用の空地」) (with 「In the broad sense it also includes the hirokoji and the like, streets having the same purpose」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「広義では、同様の趣旨を持った街路である広小路なども含まれる」); the page says the ground was not simply vacant, and 「a blaze could not jump」 was this page's)
  • tokyo-archives-hirokoji - 「In Edo there were streets called hirokoji, built wider than an ordinary street. Most were established at places about the city as "fire-clearance open ground" (hiyoke-akichi) to stop a fire spreading.」 and 「Because these open grounds fundamentally had the function of fire-clearance ground, no permanent building could be put up on them. But on the hirokoji established at busy bridgeheads and before the gates of temples and shrines that drew many worshippers, rows of movable and removable stall-shops were put up and goods were traded, markets formed, and besides street performance, entertainments were staged in booths screened with reed matting - so many of them developed into pleasure quarters.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸には広小路とよばれる一般の街路より幅広く作られた街路がありました。その多くは火災の延焼防止のため「火除明地（ひよけあきち）」として市中の各所に設置されたものです。」 and original: 「これらの広場は、本来的には火除地としての機能をもちますから、恒久的な建造物は建てることができませんでした。しかし、往来の激しい橋詰や、多くの参拝者を集める寺社門前に設置された広小路には、移動や撤去の可能な床店が建ち並んで商品の取引が行われたり、市場が形成され、また大道芸のほか、葭簀張で囲われた小屋での芸能興行が行われるなど、盛り場として発展した場所も多かったのです。」) (the Tokyo Metropolitan Archives' own page for its published study of the Edo hirokoji.)
