A county seat's street share, open reserve and civic share

Sources: chang-morphology-walled-capitals (the street grids, the intramural farmland, and Jinan's lake at a fifth of the walled area; the further anchors - Quanzhou a quarter vacant in 1945, Suzhou's intramural farmland - are on no page read), pingyao-mct-streets (Pingyao's streets and lanes counted), kyoto-city-jobosei (the Heian block and lane widths the street share is checked against)

A Chinese county seat ran a sparse street net - at its most regular one or two gate-to-gate trunks in a cross or T with the drum tower at the crossing, though most plans deflected the trunks so that no street ran straight from gate to gate, everything else lanes (Pingyao's old city counts four main streets, eight lesser streets and seventy-two lanes, tying together some 4,000 house compounds) - at roughly 10-20% of the walled ground, and often enclosed a large share of unbuilt ground - this record guesses 25-30%, a figure no page read gives: walls were sized to administrative rank and growth, and intramural fields and an assured water supply were siege insurance (the flood function the chapter gives belongs to the walls and ramparts, not to the fields). Civic buildings alone took only a few percent; the big open consumers were cultivation, ponds and parks in the chapter's account, and on these maps the drill ground and the under-built remainder. No page read gives any city's street share as a figure. The one check on the band is arithmetic on a grid whose sizes are known: Heian-kyo's blocks were fixed at about 120 m a side and its lanes were about 12 m wide, so a field of blocks with only lanes between them gives about 17% of its ground to streets, inside the band - and that is a lane on every side of every block, with the capital's wider avenues left out. The maps draw streets at ~7% (deep blocks, alley warrens) and admit open ground only as a declared, drawn line (agricultural district, drill ground, gardens), never as ambient slack.
