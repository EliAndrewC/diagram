A county seat's street share, open reserve and civic share

Sources: chang-morphology-walled-capitals (the street grids, the intramural farmland, and Jinan's lake at a fifth of the walled area; the further anchors - Quanzhou a quarter vacant in 1945, Suzhou's intramural farmland, Pingyao's "4 big streets, 8 small streets, 72 lanes" - are on no page read)

A Chinese county seat ran a sparse street net - one or two gate-to-gate trunks in a cross or T with the drum tower at the crossing, everything else lanes - at roughly 10-20% of the walled ground, and normally enclosed 25-30% (the figure unsourced) deliberately unbuilt ground: walls were sized to administrative rank and growth, and intramural fields, gardens and ponds were siege insurance (the flood function the chapter gives belongs to the walls and ramparts, not to the fields). Civic buildings alone took only a few percent; the big open consumers were cultivation, ponds and parks in the chapter's account, and on these maps the drill ground and the under-built remainder. The maps draw streets at ~7% (deep blocks, alley warrens) and admit open ground only as a declared, drawn line (agricultural district, drill ground, gardens), never as ambient slack.
