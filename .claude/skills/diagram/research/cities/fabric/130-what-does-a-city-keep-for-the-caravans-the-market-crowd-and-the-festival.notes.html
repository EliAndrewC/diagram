• sukego-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, a corvee imposed on the villages near a post station to make up the shortfall when the men and horses kept standing at the station were insufficient.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、宿駅常備の人馬が不足する場合、その補充のために宿駅近隣の村々に課された夫役」) (the station's complement made up from a ring of villages, 「2 men and 2 horses per 100 koku」 and later past ten ri; 「oxen」 appear on no page read)
  • tokaido-qa-mlit - 「For this reason each post station was placed under an obligation to keep permanently on hand porters and horses for carrying the baggage. At first each station on the Tokaido was made to keep 36 horses, but from Kan'ei 15 (1638) onward the stationing and relaying of 100 post horses and 100 post porters was made obligatory.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「このため各宿では、荷物を運ぶための人足と馬を常備することが義務づけられていました。はじめ東海道の各宿には３６疋の馬を備えさせましたが、寛永１５年（１６３８）以降、１００疋の伝馬と１００人の伝馬人足の設置および継立が義務づけられました。」) (the national highway office's own question-and-answer page for the Tokaido. It gives what a STATION had to keep standing, which is the order of magnitude the sentence reaches for; nothing read counts the animals of one arriving train.)
  • sukego-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, the villages in the neighborhood of a highway post station that supplied men and horses supplementarily when the station's permanently kept men and horses alone were insufficient for the relay; or the levy or system itself.」 and 「In 1694 (Genroku 7) the shogunate newly laid down the sukego system ... designating the villages in the neighborhood of each post station as attached sukego, and setting the men-and-horses duty at 2 men and 2 horses per 100 koku of assessed yield.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、街道宿駅の常備人馬だけでは継ぎ送りに支障をきたす場合、補助的に人馬を提供する宿駅近傍の郷村、またはその課役・制度をいう。」 and original: 「幕府は1694年（元禄7）新たに助郷制を画定したが、それは従来の助郷が封境・国郡を限界としたのを改めて、各宿駅近傍の村々を付属助郷に指定し、高100石につき2人・2疋(ひき)の人馬役負担とした。」) and, from another work on the same page, 「At first the extent of the sukego villages was 2 to 3 ri around the station, but it was gradually widened to more than 10 ri, and when the supply of men and horses was impossible it was commuted into money, becoming a kind of tax.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「最初，助郷村の範囲は宿の周囲2～3里であったが，次第に10里以上にも拡大され，人馬提供が不可能の場合，金銭で代納し，一種の租税となった。」) (the station that could not absorb what arrived widened its catchment; it did not enlarge its yard.)
