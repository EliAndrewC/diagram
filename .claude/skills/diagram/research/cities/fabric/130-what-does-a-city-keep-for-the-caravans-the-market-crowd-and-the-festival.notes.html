• sukego-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, a corvee imposed on the villages near a post station to make up the shortfall when the men and horses kept standing at the station were insufficient.」 (translated; ) (the station's complement made up from a ring of villages, 2 men and 2 horses per 100 koku and later past ten ri; 「oxen」 appear on no page read)
  • tokaido-qa-mlit - 「For this reason each post station was placed under an obligation to keep permanently on hand porters and horses for carrying the baggage. At first each station on the Tokaido was made to keep 36 horses, but from Kan'ei 15 (1638) onward the stationing and relaying of 100 post horses and 100 post porters was made obligatory.」 (translated; ) (the national highway office's own question-and-answer page for the Tokaido. It gives what a STATION had to keep standing, which is the order of magnitude the sentence reaches for; nothing read counts the animals of one arriving train.)
  • sukego-kotobank - 「In the Edo period, the villages in the neighborhood of a highway post station that supplied men and horses supplementarily when the station's permanently kept men and horses alone were insufficient for the relay; or the levy or system itself.」 and 「In 1694 (Genroku 7) the shogunate newly laid down the sukego system, revising the former sukego, which had been bounded by domain borders and by province and district, and designating the villages in the neighborhood of each post station as attached sukego, and setting the men-and-horses duty at 2 men and 2 horses per 100 koku of assessed yield.」 (translated; ) and, from another work on the same page, 「At first the extent of the sukego villages was 2 to 3 ri around the station, but it was gradually widened to more than 10 ri, and when the supply of men and horses was impossible it was commuted into money, becoming a kind of tax.」 (translated; ) (the station that could not absorb what arrived widened its catchment; it did not enlarge its yard.)
