• crd-heiankyo-block - 「one block of Heian-kyo was 40 jo long by 40 jo wide (1 jo = 10 shaku = about 3 meters, 40 jo = about 120 meters), and this block was made one machi.」 and 「the private houses of lower officials were in principle 1/32 of a machi (5 jo long by 10 jo wide) under the shigyo-hachimon system, which, converted into the tsubo now in use, comes to about 136 tsubo allotted.」 and 「The same book also records that there were people who lived on lots narrower than this.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (a Kyoto prefectural library's reference answer, summarizing two printed books; the feet are the record's arithmetic)
  • machiwari-jawiki - 「Heian-kyo, the origin of Kyoto, was also a city built on such town planning, and machi of 40 jo (about 120 m) square, divided north and south, were laid out like a go board; but in practice there were various divisions of the ground, such as nobles' mansions extending to several machi and commoners' houses dividing the inside of one machi so as to face only the north-south streets, in what is called the shigyo-hachimon system.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; )
  • kotobank-jobosei - 「Fujiwara-kyo, as inferred from the Taiho code, had 12 rows and 4 columns of wards (bo) on both the left and right capital, each bo forming a square 90 jo (about 270 m) on a side, which was further divided equally by lanes into 4 sections (called machi), whose side is 45 jo from road center to road center.」 and 「The jobo system of Heijo-kyo was basically Fujiwara-kyo made twice as large, so the side of a bo came to 180 jo; but since this was divided into 16 machi by three lanes each east-west and north-south, the size of one machi did not change.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Fujiwara plan is the one inferred from the code, as the entry says)
  • ginza-machiwari - 「The machiwari of the townsmen's ground took as one unit a square of 60 ken in the Kyoto measure (about 120 m) on a side.」 and 「Inside the square, an open space called the kaishochi was taken in the very middle, and townsmen's lots 20 ken deep were laid along the streets.」 and 「What the kaishochi was used for is not clear.」 and 「As for what the inside of a townsmen's lot was like, shops stood in a row on the street side, and on entering the alley, the nagaya that were the craftsmen's dwellings stood in rows on both sides.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the Ginza design council's page on the quarter laid out from Nihonbashi to Kyobashi in 1603; it describes that quarter, not every part of Edo)
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「In Kyoto, as in Edo, the stratification of the townsmen was marked: they were divided into townsmen holding large machiya with a frontage of 10 ken or more, townsmen holding ordinary machiya with a frontage of 4 or 5 ken, and townsmen holding small machiya with a frontage of 1 and a half to 2 ken, or renting, and the division had been advancing since the middle of the Edo period.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; ) (the lots-per-side count is the record's arithmetic on these frontages)
