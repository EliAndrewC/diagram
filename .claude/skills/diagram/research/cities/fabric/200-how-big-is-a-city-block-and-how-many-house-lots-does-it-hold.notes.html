• crd-heiankyo-block - 「one block of Heian-kyo was 40 jo long by 40 jo wide (1 jo = 10 shaku = about 3 meters, 40 jo = about 120 meters), and this block was made one machi.」 and 「the private houses of lower officials were in principle 1/32 of a machi (5 jo long by 10 jo wide) under the shigyo-hachimon system, which, converted into the tsubo now in use, comes to about 136 tsubo allotted.」 and 「The same book also records that there were people who lived on lots narrower than this.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「平安京の一区画は縦40丈×横40丈（1丈=10尺=約3メートル、40丈=約120メートル）で、この区画を1町とした。」 and original: 「なお、下級官僚の個人住宅は、原則は四行八門制により32分の1町（縦5丈×横10丈）で、現在使われている坪数に換算するとおよそ136坪与えられていたことになる。」 and original: 「さらに、これよりも狭い敷地で暮らしていた人々もいると同書には記載されている。」) (a Kyoto prefectural library's reference answer, summarizing two printed books; the feet are the record's arithmetic)
  • machiwari-jawiki - 「Heian-kyo, the origin of Kyoto, was also a city built on such town planning, and machi of 40 jo (about 120 m) square, divided north and south, were laid out like a go board; but in practice there were various divisions of the ground, such as nobles' mansions extending to several machi and commoners' houses dividing the inside of one machi so as to face only the north-south streets, in what is called the shigyo-hachimon system.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「京都の原点となった平安京もこうした都市計画の上で成り立った都市で南北に区切られた40丈（約120m）四方からなる「町」が碁盤上に並べられていたが、実際には数町規模に及ぶ公卿の邸宅や四行八門制と称された南北の通りにのみ面するように1町の内部を区切った庶民の住宅など様々な敷地割があった。」)
  • kotobank-jobosei - 「Fujiwara-kyo, as inferred from the Taiho code, had 12 rows and 4 columns of wards (bo) on both the left and right capital, each bo forming a square 90 jo (about 270 m) on a side, which was further divided equally by lanes into 4 sections (called machi), whose side is 45 jo from road center to road center.」 and 「The jobo system of Heijo-kyo was basically Fujiwara-kyo made twice as large, so the side of a bo came to 180 jo; but since this was divided into 16 machi by three lanes each east-west and north-south, the size of one machi did not change.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「大宝令によって推定される藤原京は左京・右京ともに12条4坊，各坊は1辺90丈（約270m）の方形をなすが，それはさらに小路で4区画（〈町〉という）に等分され，その1辺は道路心々間で45丈となる。」 and original: 「平城京の条坊制は基本的には藤原京を倍大にしたもので，したがって坊の1辺は180丈をなすが，これが東西・南北3本ずつの小路で16町に区分されたので，1町の大きさは変わらない。」) (the Fujiwara plan is the one inferred from the code, as the entry says)
  • ginza-machiwari - 「The machiwari of the townsmen's ground took as one unit a square of 60 ken in the Kyoto measure (about 120 m) on a side.」 and 「Inside the square, an open space called the kaishochi was taken in the very middle, and townsmen's lots 20 ken deep were laid along the streets.」 and 「What the kaishochi was used for is not clear.」 and 「As for what the inside of a townsmen's lot was like, shops stood in a row on the street side, and on entering the alley, the nagaya that were the craftsmen's dwellings stood in rows on both sides.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町人地の町割は、京間六十間（約120m）四方の正方形が一単位。」 and original: 「正方形の中は、まんなかに会所地とよばれる空地をとって、通り沿いに奥行き20間の町屋敷が配置されていました。」 and original: 「会所地が何に使われていたのか、はっきりとはしていません。」 and original: 「では町屋敷の中はどうなっていたかといえば、通り側に店が並び、路地を入っていくと両側に職人たちの住まいである長屋が並んでいました。」) (the Ginza design council's page on the quarter laid out from Nihonbashi to Kyobashi in 1603; it describes that quarter, not every part of Edo)
  • machiya-shoka-jawiki - 「In Kyoto, as in Edo, the stratification of the townsmen was marked: they were divided into townsmen holding large machiya with a frontage of 10 ken or more, townsmen holding ordinary machiya with a frontage of 4 or 5 ken, and townsmen holding small machiya with a frontage of 1 and a half to 2 ken, or renting, and the division had been advancing since the middle of the Edo period.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「京都も江戸と同じく町人の階層分化は顕著であり、間口10間以上の大きな町屋を持つ町人、間口4、5間の一般的な町屋を持つ町人、そして間口1間半から2間の小さな町屋を持ち、または借家をする町人とに分かれており、江戸時代の中頃から分化が進んでいた。」) (the lots-per-side count is the record's arithmetic on these frontages)
