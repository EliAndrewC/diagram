  • bunka-sugimoto-omoya - 「behind the main house the Okura, the Sumigura and the Nakagura [the great, the corner and the middle storehouse] stand in a row, and a high wall is run round the estate.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「主屋の後方に大蔵、隅蔵、中蔵が並び建ち、屋敷の周囲には高塀を廻らせる。」)
  • matsusaka-hasegawa-kanko - 「Behind the earthen storehouses flows the back-to-back drain, which is also the boundary of the machi, and beyond it spreads a stroll garden centered on a pond.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「土蔵の裏手には、町境でもある背割排水が流れ、その奥には池を中心とした回遊式庭園が広がっています。」) (the garden beyond the drain is land the family bought later; that the wall stands inside the drain is this record's reading of the lot's edge)
  • hinomi-yagura-jawiki - 「It was general practice to set up a guard post (also called bansho or jishinban) in every town and to station a watchman (called hiban, bantarō or banta) there permanently, keeping watch on a twenty-four-hour footing.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町ごとに番屋（番所、自身番とも）を設置し番人（火番、番太郎・番太と呼ばれていた）を常駐させて24時間態勢で警戒にあたるのが一般的であった」) (the watch is municipal; that the tower stands on public ground and a public way, never inside a private wall or court, is this page's reasoning, not the source's)
