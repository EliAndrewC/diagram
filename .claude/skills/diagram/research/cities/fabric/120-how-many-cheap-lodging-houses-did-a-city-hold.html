How many cheap lodging houses did a city hold?

Sources: chang-morphology-walled-capitals (the suburb of inns and traffic trades that grew outside a busy gate), song-society-enwiki (when the dusk curfew over the wards ended); the GM's decision of 2026-07-24. Where a claim below carries no note, it rests on this project's general reading and has not been checked against a page a reader can open.

WHERE the cheap lodging stood is answerable: it clustered in the suburb outside a busy gate along with the rest of the traffic trades, which is the guanxiang pattern the extramural rows already draw.21 How MANY such houses a settlement held is not answerable. That the ruling class did not count or record the lodging of the poor, and that the gazetteers which survive foreground the official post-stations instead, rests on general reading; no source is cited.22 Nor is the lodging at the gate because a curfew stranded late travelers outside: the ward gate shut at dusk is a Tang institution, and it was gone well before the later period these cities are modeled on - the curfew was abolished in 1063 and the marketplaces of Kaifeng were then open every hour of the day, where a strict curfew from dusk had held over the two official markets of Tang Chang'an.23 It is at the gate because that is where the traffic is.

So the symmetric count these maps draw - one cheap lodging house inside the walls and one just outside every gate - is a GUESS, and a deliberate stylization on the generous side of a range nobody can bound. The GM decided it on 2026-07-24: a setting more attentive to its peasantry than its historical models are, drawn so that the provision reads at a glance. Two alternatives were priced and declined: concentrating the lodging on the busiest gate alone, and drawing a distinct animal-yard cart-inn compound.

The rest of the lodging program is deliberately invisible. A highway seat of about 3,000 that is not a post-station would run something like eight to fifteen lodging houses all told: the two to four proper inns and two to four cheap houses the map gives their own glyphs, plus five to eight small family hatago that were not distinct buildings at all - ordinary shophouses whose back rooms took lodgers, the household living on the meal trade, a field and porterage with lodging as the add-on, and on a procession night the whole town became the billet.24 So several of the road-market and gate-market shop rows are understood to sleep travelers, and nothing on the map counts them.

The rule the map follows: a provincial city draws one cheap lodging house inside the walls, and one within about 1,560 ft outside every gate. The one inside stands in a humble quarter - the laborer section, or the agrarian sector where a city has one - never within about 330 ft of the temple, merchant or samurai neighborhoods, and never within about 450 ft of the burakumin quarter. Its one exception is the gate caravan cluster below, where it properly stands beside an inn and a stables rather than alone.
