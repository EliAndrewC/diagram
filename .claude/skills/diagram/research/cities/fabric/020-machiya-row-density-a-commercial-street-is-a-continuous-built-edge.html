Machiya row density: a commercial street is a continuous built edge

Sources: kyototuu-unagi (frontage ~2 ken, depth 10-12 ken), kyomachiya-jawiki, hiyokechi-jawiki (breaks at block scale after 1657), udatsu-jawiki, machiya-shoka-jawiki, edo-no-kaji-jawiki. On the frontage tax, see the note in the entry above.

The question (GM, 2026-08-11), looking at Shiro Daika's north gate market: "Is that the correct amount of space between gate market buildings? No objection, they just look more spaced out than I expected."

What the research says. The Japanese urban shophouse (machiya) was built against its neighbors, its front (its frontage) on the street. Frontage was taxed, and lots are said to have been kept narrow and deep to save the tax: a Kyoto kyo-machiya typically fronted around 2 ken (about 12 ft) on a lot running some 60-70 ft back, and the fronts abutted, built continuously, each against its neighbor, down the block. The street read as a WALL of shopfronts pierced by gateways and alley mouths, not as a row of detached boxes. Post-fire policy in Edo cut firebreaks (hiyokechi) and broad avenues THROUGH the fabric and pushed tile roofing, but the breaks were a few open sites - five after the fire, thirteen by the Kyoho era - and the page on them names no rule spacing individual machiya apart. The two ken is the ordinary Kyoto shop's; how frontage grew with wealth, from a small trader's front to a great store's, and what a Chinese shop measured, is under how wide a merchant's shophouse is.

The decision. Shops in a market row stand shoulder to shoulder along the street they front, and a row may front a street that bends.

The deliberate departure from literal reality. True machiya stood adjoining, their gable walls touching - a gap of ZERO. We land at a median of 18 ft on the capital's north and south gate markets (9 ft on the crowded southwest strip, 48 ft on the loose roadside market at the east gate, which is a different thing and should stay loose). An 18 ft gap is enough that a reader can still count individual shops and see which way each one faces, which is the whole job of the drawing. The relative sizes stay honest; only the mortar joint is drawn wider than life.
