Does China house a household's servants the same way?

Sources: siheyuan-zhwiki, daozuofang, neixiang-yamen, banfang-zhwiki

Checked second, after the Japanese evidence in the servant-housing question. An elite or official quarter reads as a field of walled rectangles with a single dark notch each. Household servants occupy the daozuofang - the south row whose windowless back IS the compound's street wall, its doors facing inward - and the rear service row (houzhaofang), which the same account puts at the boundary of the innermost court and gives to the owner's daughters - so the inner rows house family rather than servants. The one attested staff-housing BUILDING is the Ming clerks' lodging (lishe / gongxiefang) - a single named courtyard INSIDE the yamen wall (Pingyao's, built 1619 behind the west three chambers; Neixiang's west line held a guest hall, dining hall and jail (寅宾馆、膳馆、监狱) instead, not a clerks' lodging), not a district. The group numerous enough to have formed a servant quarter, the runners (yayi), had only a duty station inside the yamen, the banfang; that it was an improvised shed of no fixed position and that the runners went home to houses scattered in the town is this page's reading, on no page read.
