Does a city keep its own town magistracy, and where do its constables live?

Sources: machi-bugyo-jawiki (the town magistracy and its staff), kotobank-machibugyosho (where Edo's stood), narukuchiya-bugyosho (their areas), edo-ashigaru-kitamachi (the plan of one), doshin-jawiki and chuo-kanko-hatchobori (where the constables lived), fuguo-zhwiki (the county yamen inside a prefectural city); the GM's setting notes for which office polices a city

Yes, in both traditions. In the late Qing a Chinese prefectural city usually held a county yamen as well as the prefect's - a large prefectural or provincial city two or three - each with its own institutions. In Japan the town magistrate (machi-bugyo) had charge of the administration and justice of a city's town quarters (machikata), and the domains set up the post as well as the shogunate. Edo's two magistracies stood just inside two of the castle's gates, Tokiwabashi and Kajibashi, on grounds of about 2,630 and about 2,720 tsubo - roughly 9,000 square meters, 2.2 acres, each. The magistrate lived in his office, and each office had 25 yoriki and 100 doshin; the North office, which the South resembled, was office on the east and residence on the west, the yoriki and doshin rooms in the north-east and the court with its white-sand yard and holding cell in the widest quarter, the south-east.

The staff did NOT live in it. Each yoriki was granted a residence plot of about 300 tsubo and each doshin one of about 100, in a group residence at Hatchobori, apart from the offices; that ground was granted to the corps as one block, about 40,000 tsubo, and divided into plots for fifty yoriki and two hundred doshin, and they let part or all of their plots to tenants.

The setting names no town magistrate: street policing, magistrates, courts and jails are the Ministry of Justice's, so this project reads a provincial city's town magistracy as its Ministry of Justice office. The magistrates stationed in a capital are the setting's own - one Imperial Magistrate per domain, at the domain capital, keeping a walled residence and office with ceremonial halls, a stable and a small staff of ashigaru and servants, and a few Imperial yoriki in each provincial city; Clan and Family magistrates hear their own houses' disputes.

The rule the map follows: a provincial city draws no town magistracy beside its Ministry of Justice; the constables live out, in a group residence of equal plots near it - about 100 ft square for a senior constable (300 tsubo) and about 60 ft square for a junior one (100 tsubo), the Edo sizes - never inside the ministry's walls. A domain capital's Imperial Magistrate keeps a compound of its own, built as a magistracy with the residence inside it; its size is a GUESS below Edo's 9,000 square meters, since Edo's served the shogun's capital with 125 staff to an office, and no readable source sizes a smaller one. The Clan and Family magistrates' offices are the setting's own and are not drawn as compounds of their own until the GM asks for them.
