Where does a province's government stand in its city?

Sources: the GM's setting notes for the Six Ministries; subai-ancient-city-sites (the square city's great cross, and the yamen and market in the middle of it), neixiang-yamen, xianya-zhwiki

The six ministries are the setting's own government - Rites, Revenue, Retainers, War, Works and Justice - so a provincial seat carries one office of each, and that much rests on the GM's notes rather than on any history. Where they STAND is the researched half. A provincial governor's compound was a whole city block in its own right - main hall, residence, quarters for the retinue, a jail, a granary, gardens, dozens of buildings behind one wall - which is why it is drawn here as walls with its interior implied, and why its sheer size on the sheet is correct rather than inflated. In a PLANNED seat the bureaus did not scatter. The regular Chinese form is a square city with one gate to a side and the streets between them meeting in one great cross, with a smaller cross set in each of the four quarters it makes; and the yamen and the market were laid out in the MIDDLE of such a city, which is where those main streets meet - so plainly that when a Ming prince's city was built on the central ground, the yamen, the market and part of the dwellings had to be demolished for it, and a yamen moved elsewhere in the city needed its streets and its attached offices laid out afresh. That the offices LINED the avenues around the compound is a step further than any page read goes: what the passage supports is that they belonged with it and were arranged around it, not that they fronted the great streets. The one office that sits apart is Rites, among the temples it oversees; ritual buildings did take fixed positions in a city - two Shanxi Confucian temples were both built in the southeast corner - though nothing puts a Rites office there, and the setting's arrangement is its own.

The rule the map follows: the governor's compound, the six ministry offices and a samurai neighborhood all stand in the city's INTERIOR, inside the rampart and clear of the wall's stroke and its moat. The compound is the grandest thing in the city - at least three times the size of any ministry office, and at least as large as any country estate. Five ministries cluster around it, within about 1,440 ft, threading into the samurai quarter; only Rites stands apart, in the temple neighborhood, and there is exactly one of it. Every ministry FRONTS a city street, within about 255 ft of one, which in practice means laying a short government avenue - an L wrapping the compound serves well - and seating the offices along it; a street fronted by ministries counts as a built-up street, as much as one lined with houses and shops. And a government office stands in its OWN ground: ordinary city houses may touch one another, but a ministry or the governor's compound keeps about 42 ft clear of every other building and compound.
