Where does a city keep its jail, and how big is it?

Sources: qing-yamen-tushuo (the jail inside the yamen), siyu-zhwiki (a jail office at every regional seat), iolaw-mingqing-jails (a jail's parts), noyamagoku-jawiki (a castle town's jail as a compound of its own), edo-ashigaru-kitamachi (a town magistracy's holding cell)

Two forms are attested, and they differ in where the jail stands, not only in how big it is. In China the jail was part of the yamen: every prefecture, department and county kept one, and from the prefecture up it had an office of its own to run it; a local yamen's jail stood southwest of the great hall, outside the ceremonial gate, and so was called the South Jail. Each such jail held an inner jail for the condemned and an outer one for lesser convicts, with women kept apart; in some counties the jail ran in four grades, and the women's jail stood beside the outer jail behind a wall of its own.

In a Japanese castle town the jail was a compound apart. The Chōshū domain's jail at Hagi was two: an upper jail for samurai and a lower jail for everyone else, facing each other across a road, both built later on the sites of two confiscated residences; the upper jail had twelve single cells, six facing six, with an inspection hut and an execution ground. The shogunate's largest, Edo's Tenmachō, filled a moated block of its own of about 8,850 square meters (cells are remand, not punishment). Edo's North town magistracy kept a temporary holding cell among its court rooms. In the setting, jails are the Ministry of Justice's to run, beside street policing and the courts.

The rule the map follows: the city jail's seat is a KNOB rolled per city from the seed. Either it is a walled court inside the governor's compound, at its southwest, outside the ceremonial gate, with an inner and an outer ward and a walled women's ward; or it is a compound of its own on a residence plot in the samurai quarter near the Ministry of Justice, with a holding cell kept in the governor's compound for the court's day. Its size in the first form is a GUESS - no readable source gives a prefectural jail's area - and in the second it is a samurai residence plot, the Hagi form; a provincial city's jail is never drawn at the scale of Edo's Tenmachō, the shogun's capital's jail.
