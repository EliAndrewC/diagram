What stands between the temples in a temple neighborhood?

Sources: akiba-jinja-jawiki (how small a neighborhood shrine is, how many stand in one town block, and what they were built of), hokora-jawiki

A quarter holding several temples is not only those temples. The ground between them carries small wayside shrines, put up by whoever had reason to and tended by the neighborhood around it. Small neighborhood shrines of that kind are attested and so is their density: the shrines of the fire-quelling Akiba cult, the most widely spread of them, are often small, and several of them may stand within a single town block. Two things about the drawn shrine are not: that such shrines stand specifically in the ground BETWEEN the temples of a temple quarter, and the form the map gives one - a vermilion-roofed shed with its own little gate - which for the Akiba cult runs the other way, since the account notes the small shrines are often built of stone because stone does not burn. The vermilion shed is an Inari form and, on these sheets, a map drawing convention: one glyph stands for the whole class of small shrines so that a reader can tell it from a temple at a glance. Nobody lives in one, and none of them has an approach avenue of its own. A single temple standing among houses is not a neighborhood and gathers none of them: the city's warrior-fortune temple in the middle of the samurai quarter is a building, not a precinct.

The rule the map follows: where two or more temples cluster within about 1,200 ft, at least three small wayside shrines are dotted among them, clear of the temples, the wall and the streets, and seated BEFORE the housing packs so that the rows flow around them. Religious buildings at city scale are temples, and a city may have several. Where a city street runs straight up to a temple and ends at its front, a torii stands on that street just in front of the temple; how MANY arches the approach carries is that temple's own roll rather than however much street is left over, and the arches draw in the top layer so the street passes beneath them. A temple set back from the grid, with no street running up to it, needs none.
