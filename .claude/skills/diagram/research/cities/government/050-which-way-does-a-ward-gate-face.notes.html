• kotobank-kido - 「In the Edo period, a gate set up at the boundaries between the town blocks on the main streets of Edo. The town kido, set up for security and control.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、江戸市中の表通りの町々の境にたてた門。保安、取締りのために設けられた町木戸。」) (the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten definition; the Daijisen's on the same page: 「In the Edo period, a gate for guarding set up at key points in the city and at the boundaries of the town blocks.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「江戸時代、市中の要所、町々の境界に設けられた警衛のための門。」))
  • jokamachi-jawiki - 「They also set fences and kido for each town division, closing the gates at night and posting gatekeepers, and so on, keeping suspicious persons out.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また、町割ごとに柵や木戸を設けて、夜には門を閉めて門番を立たせるなど、不審者の侵入を阻んでいた。」) (of the castle towns' defenses)
  • kotobank-kidoban - 「the large central kido, 2.5 ken wide, serving as the checkpoint for going in and out from one town block to the next」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「町から町への出入りの関門の用をなす中央2間半の大木戸」) 「The kido was left open by day, but in emergencies, such as a criminal fleeing into the block or a quarrel, it was shut to cut off traffic.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木戸は昼間はあけておくが、犯罪人が逃げ込んだり、喧嘩(けんか)など非常の際は、閉鎖して交通を遮断した。」) (the Nipponica entry, which speaks of Edo, Kyoto, Osaka and similar cities; 2.5 ken is about 4.5 m, 15 ft)
  • kidoban-jawiki - 「The kido watchman shut the kido at the hour of yotsu at night (about 10 pm).」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木戸番は夜の四ツ時（午後10時ごろ）に木戸を閉めた」)

  • kotobank-kidoban - 「These kido were set up one to a block in the large towns and one to several blocks in the small and middle-sized towns; in Edo their form was double doors hung between posts set about 2 ken apart, with a fence or a board fence at both sides running to the road's edge, and beside it the kido watchman's hut.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「この木戸は，大きな町では1町単位，中小の町では数町単位で設置され，江戸の場合，その形態は，2間ほどの間隔で建てた柱の間に，両開きの扉をつり，両脇には道路際までの柵，または板塀がとりつき，そばには木戸番の番小屋があった。」) (the Heibonsha encyclopedia's older edition, its entry for kido, quoted on the same page; 2 ken is about 3.6 m, 12 ft)
