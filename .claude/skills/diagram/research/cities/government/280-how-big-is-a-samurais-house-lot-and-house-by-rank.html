How big is a samurai's house lot, and the house on it, by rank?

Sources: fukui-bushi-jutaku (a domain's own table of lot sizes by stipend, and a senior retainer's measured plot); kotobank-bukeyashiki (lots granted by status and stipend); doshin-jawiki (the Edo constables' and their officers' lots); touken-bukeyashiki-madori (a senior retainer's plot at Kanazawa); matsue-bukeyashiki-about (a senior retainer's house); jta-ashigaru-kaga and hikone-ashigaru (the foot soldiers' lots, the floor of the ladder); semboku-kakunodate-en (a small town's lots and a junior house)

The lot is fixed by stipend. A samurai did not buy a house lot: the domain granted it, by the holder's status and stipend, and a samurai moved house on promotion to one that fit the new status. Fukui's domain wrote the rule down for its middle and lower retainers - a lot area for each stipend, and equal lots for equal stipends - and its table is the one ladder read:

The table gives no figure for the upper ranks, but their plots are measured one by one: a 1,000-koku Fukui retainer held about 1,000 tsubo (28 by 32.5 ken, about 170 by 200 ft), and a 1,200-koku Kanazawa retainer about the same. Below the ladder stand the foot soldiers, outside the samurai ward (where the foot soldiers live): a Kaga foot soldier's plot was 165 or 230 m2, about 1,800 or 2,500 sq ft, and Hikone's 5 by 10 ken, about 30 by 60 ft.

The senior staff officer's lot. A yoriki - the officer over a magistrate's constables - held 96 tsubo at Fukui, on the table above; in Edo the town magistrates' yoriki held about 300 tsubo (about 10,700 sq ft) and their constables about 100 (about 3,600 sq ft). The Fukui page lists its yoriki among the lower samurai and does not say what they did; this project reads the two as one rank at two scales, a domain's and the shogun's capital's (a guess), and puts a county seat's officer at the domain's end.

The house on the lot. A house is much smaller than its lot. Matsue's surviving retainer's house, held in turn by men of 500 to 1,000 koku, is about 67 tsubo (about 2,400 sq ft); the house of that size, and what rank it fits, is the buildings page's question on the samurai's house. Two senior Fukui retainers' suburban villas were 51 and 35 tsubo on lots of 1,200 and 1,700 tsubo. At the bottom, a Kaga foot soldier's house was 66 to 82 m2 (about 700 to 900 sq ft), and a low-ranking Kakunodate family's main building about 91 m2 (about 980 sq ft). No page read gives the house of a 100- to 300-koku retainer; a house of about 1,200 to 1,800 sq ft, between those two ends, is a guess.

A town's lots vary in generosity. Fukui was a great domain's city, of 475,280 koku until the fief was halved in 1686. In small Kakunodate the estate of the Aoyagi, a middle-ranking family of 45 to 60 koku when they arrived, is just under a hectare today, many times what Fukui gave a 100-koku retainer. How generous a town's lots are is a degree, not a choice between forms, so it is calibrated per settlement rather than rolled as a knob.

Where the lot stands against the lord's seat - the higher the rank, the nearer the castle or the governor's compound - is the siting question, Where do a city's samurai live?; how the lot is fenced or walled is What does a samurai's house look like from above?

Setting rank against the ladder. The setting's stipends are far smaller than Edo's (a stipend is the rank squared, so a rank-10 minister draws 100 koku), so a lot cannot be read off the Fukui table by koku; it is read by PLACE in the hierarchy, which is this project's decision. A city's senior samurai take the middle band of the ladder (225 to 400 tsubo), its junior samurai the lower band (55 to 96), its senior staff officer the yoriki's 96, and a domain minister or the governor the 1,000-tsubo plot.

The rule the map follows: a senior samurai's lot is about 8,000 to 14,200 sq ft, a junior samurai's about 2,000 to 3,400 sq ft, a senior staff officer's about 3,400 sq ft, and the governor's manor or a minister's compound about 35,000 sq ft; the house on a senior lot covers about 1,200 to 2,400 sq ft (the lower figure a guess) and on a junior lot about 700 to 1,000 sq ft, so a lot is mostly open ground. The lot sizes follow one domain's table; the mapping of the setting's ranks onto it is this project's decision; a town's overall generosity may be scaled up toward a small town's roomier lots per settlement, a calibration.
