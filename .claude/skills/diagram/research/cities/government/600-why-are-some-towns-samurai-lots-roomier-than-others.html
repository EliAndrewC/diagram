Why are some towns' samurai lots roomier than others?

Sources: fukui-bushi-jutaku (the great domain whose table is the ladder); harakata-sashiko-jawiki (Yonezawa's lots for its lower samurai); bunka-kakunodate and denken-kakunodate (Kakunodate's vast lots, divided by the end of the Edo period); semboku-kakunodate-en (one Kakunodate estate as it stands today)

Fukui is one domain's measure. The ladder of lots by rank (How big is a samurai's house lot?) is Fukui's, a great domain's city, of 475,280 koku until the fief was halved in 1686. Other domains gave more ground.

Yonezawa: about double, for its lower samurai. When the Uesugi house's fief was cut in 1601 and its lower samurai were settled on the moors around Yonezawa by about 1609, the domain gave each of them a lot of 150 tsubo (about 5,300 sq ft), the back half a vegetable field, with a frontage of 6 to 8 ken by status and a depth of about 25 - the same page's two measures, which multiply out to 150 to 200 tsubo. Against the 55 to 96 tsubo of Fukui's lower band, that is one and a half to nearly three times as much ground, much of it a kitchen garden.

Kakunodate: vast, but not measured. Small Kakunodate's samurai district still keeps the lot division of the end of the Edo period, on lots the national heritage database calls vast, but no page read gives the area of any of its lots in the Edo period. The one figure there is modern: the estate of the Aoyagi, a middle-ranking family of 45 to 60 koku when they arrived, is just under a hectare today, and whether that is one Edo lot or several joined is not on record, so the map does not scale its lots to it.

A degree, not a choice. How generous a town's lots are is a degree along one scale, not a choice between forms, so it is calibrated per settlement rather than rolled as a knob.

The rule the map follows: a town's samurai lots are the ladder's sizes, scaled up per settlement by a factor of at most about two, toward Yonezawa's 150 to 200 tsubo (about 5,300 to 7,100 sq ft) against Fukui's 55 to 96 for its lower samurai (a calibration against the one premodern figure found); the present-day Kakunodate hectare is not used.
