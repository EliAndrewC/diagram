• jishinban-jawiki - 「They were built abutting the kido that were set up at both ends of the main street of each cho, with the kido watchman stationed at one and the jishin-ban at the other.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「各町内の大通りの両端に設置された木戸に接して建てられており、一方に木戸番が、もう一方に自身番が詰めた」) (the box abutting the ward's gate, run and paid for by the town; which flank is this page's placement, the page putting the two posts at opposite corners of the crossroads)
  • kidoban-jawiki - 「there were two kido-ban」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木戸番が2人いた」), 「they closed the kido at the fourth hour of night (about 10 pm)」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「夜の四ツ時（午後10時ごろ）に木戸を閉めた」), 「after being examined by the kido-ban, they passed through the wickets on the left and right of the kido」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木戸番に改められた上で、木戸の左右にある潜り戸から通る」), 「the watch hut was fixed at a span of 6 shaku, a length of 9 shaku and an eaves height of 1 jo」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「番屋は梁間6尺、桁行9尺、軒高1丈」), 「the watchman lived in, and the rule was that he be a man without wife or children」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「番人は住み込みで、妻子の無い者という決まりだった」) and 「the kido-ban's pay was paid by each ward」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「木戸番の給金はそれぞれの町内から支払われた」) (a shaku is about 30 cm and a jo ten shaku; the page adds that later in the period huts were enlarged to house families)
