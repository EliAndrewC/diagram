Servant housing in the samurai ward - servants are drawn as WALLS, not as houses

Sources: jta-nagayamon, jta-ashigaru-kaga, fukui-bushi-jutaku, matsue-bukeyashiki, hikone-ashigaru, shibata-ashigaru-nagaya, bukeyashiki-wiki, buke-hokonin-wiki, neixiang-yamen, daozuofang, siheyuan-zhwiki, kotobank-degawari, nagayamon-jawiki, nando-jawiki, aizu-bukeyashiki-jawiki, jokamachi-jawiki, kotobank-bukeyashiki, banfang-zhwiki

What the research found

Japan (the cultural surface, so it decides the drawn form). Domestic servants of a samurai household - genan/gejo, chugen, komono - lived INSIDE their master's walled plot,7 never in their own street-fronting house in the buke-chi8. They were live-in staff on annual contracts (the degawari changeover, the 5th day of the 3rd month, set by a shogunal decree that the two reference works date to 1668 and to 1669)944 hired out of the merchant quarter through labor brokers, so a castle town's servant population is partly recruited from the chonin-chi10 while its servant housing sits inside samurai walls.56 Three plan positions, in descending order of household size:

  1. The perimeter nagaya - a long, narrow, single-story range running along the INSIDE of the street boundary, forming the plot wall itself.55 The Fukui archive's reading of the Suganuma (菅沼) plan31 (1,000 koku, 1839: a 28 x 32.5 ken plot, ~167 x 194 ft)11 states plainly that the street-facing buildings were the nagaya where the servants lived12.
  2. The nagayamon gate rooms - where the range is short it survives only as the gatehouse: servants' quarters and stable,13 a gatekeeper's room, a chugen room, a storeroom, sometimes a projecting guard box, with barred lookout windows onto the street (the Kanazawa page names only the servants' quarters and the stable; the gatekeeper's and chugen rooms, the storeroom, the guard box and "barred" are unsourced, a GUESS; the lookout windows themselves are attested, unbarred).29 Measured examples run ~15 ft deep by 70-80 ft long (Omura Yahei, Kishu, 12 ken x 2.5 ken = 76 x 15 ft; the Tokyo Important Cultural Property (ICP) gate 72 x 15 ft).45 It is a barracks range with a hole cut in it, not a gate with rooms attached.
  3. Rooms off the kitchen - below roughly 100-300 koku there is no room for a range at all, and the servants sleep in nando and service rooms under the main roof.46 Even at the top this persists for women: Aizu's Saigo Tanomo residence sorts its 38 rooms into reception, retainers' office, family, and maids'/servants' groups, all under one roof.47

Small uniform houses in ranks DO exist in a castle town - but never here. Ashigaru, kachi and doshin got individual plots in dedicated collective blocks (kumi-yashiki) at the town's EDGE:14 castle towns often put them on the fringe beyond the townspeople's quarter,48 and Hikone ringed the whole town with ~700 households as a defensive screen.15 Files of about ten identical plots per row,16 and the enclosure marks the rank - earthen wall for middle and up,17 board fence for lower,50 hedge for the lowest (that they were forbidden walls is on no page read).1851 Form is a terraced range by default (Shibata's ICP ashigaru-nagaya: 8 households under one thatch roof, 143 x 24 ft (梁間, a depth of 7.3 m),32 18 ft frontage each)19 and detached-with-garden only in rich domains (Kaga, Hikone34 - both flagged as unusual in their own documentation).20

China (checked second, and it agrees). An elite or official quarter reads as a field of walled rectangles with a single dark notch each.54 Household servants occupy the daozuofang - the south row whose windowless back IS the compound's street wall, its doors facing inward21 - and the rear service row (houzhaofang), which the same account puts at the boundary of the innermost court and gives to the owner's daughters40 - so the inner rows house family rather than servants. The one attested staff-housing BUILDING is the Ming clerks' lodging (lishe / gongxiefang) - a single named courtyard INSIDE the yamen wall (Pingyao's, built 1619 behind the west three chambers;22 Neixiang's west line held a guest hall, dining hall and jail (寅宾馆、膳馆、监狱) instead, not a clerks' lodging30), not a district. The group numerous enough to have formed a servant quarter, the runners (yayi), had only a duty station inside the yamen, the banfang; that it was an improvised shed of no fixed position and that the runners went home to houses scattered in the town is this page's reading, on no page read.52

The two traditions therefore give the same drawing rule from opposite directions, which is as strong as this kind of finding gets: service accommodation is part of the boundary or the interior of the compound it serves, and a rank of small uniform dwellings inside an elite quarter is the one thing neither tradition produces.

The decision it drove

A servant dwelling inside a samurai ward is not a freestanding house. It is service accommodation BOUND to the samurai household it serves - drawn on that household's street edge as a long, thin range (the nagayamon proportion, ~15 ft deep), or not drawn at all for the smallest households, whose servants sleep under the master's roof.

The count is NOT the problem: the setting's own budget notes give a provincial city 120 servant families, of which 72 are attached to samurai households (30 to wealthy samurai at property 2, 42 indentured to non-wealthy at property 1) against 60 samurai families - about one servant household per junior samurai household and two per senior. They are property-holding families in the GM's economic model, so they are real households that must be housed, counted, and taxed. What the GM saw on Minami (2026-08-02) - "more commoner houses in the samurai neighborhood" - was the ARRANGEMENT: a rank of detached servant houses unattached to any samurai household reads as exactly what the fence exists to exclude.

Disclosed departures

  • Our in-city samurai houses are UNWALLED (our city plans put the walled estates outside the rampart). Both traditions above put the servants inside a compound WALL that our city plans do not draw. The nagaya range is what resolves this without reversing the GM's settled doctrine: historically the range IS the wall,23 so drawing the range alone - a long thin building hard on the street line - carries the same read at the city map's scale without introducing walled compounds into the ward.
  • Ashigaru blocks are not drawn as a distinct texture at this tier. The edge-of-town kumi-yashiki is a real and attested pattern24 and would be the correct home for any genuinely ranked small housing: ranks of uniform small dwellings belong OUTSIDE the ward on the town fringe, not inside the fence.
