Which way does a ward gate face?

Sources: kotobank-kido (the gate at the block's boundary on the street), jokamachi-jawiki (the same gates in the castle towns), kotobank-kidoban (the gate as the way between blocks, its width and its wickets), kidoban-jawiki (the hour it shut); the GM's ruling of 2026-07-26; this project's measurement of the drawn cities

What the gate was. A ward gate (kido) stood on the street at the boundary between one town block and the next, in the castle towns as in Edo, where each town division had its fences and gates, shut at night and kept by a gatekeeper. Its large central gate, 2.5 ken (about 15 ft) wide, was the way from block to block: open by day, shut to cut off traffic when a criminal fled into the block or a quarrel broke out, and shut at night - at the hour of yotsu, about 10 pm - after which people passed by the wickets to its left and right. That width is a great city's gate: the entry speaks of Edo, Kyoto and Osaka, and no provincial town's gate was measured on any page read.

So it squares to the way it shuts. A ward gate exists to SHUT A WAY, so its bar stands square across the ROADBED running through it, and the fence meets the gate at whatever angle the fence happens to run. That it stood square to its street, rather than skewed to a fence, is on no page read: it is the GM's ruling of 2026-07-26, drawn from what the gate was for. Squaring the gate to its FENCE instead looks right, because the two readings agree wherever a lane crosses its fence squarely, which is most crossings. They diverge on an OBLIQUE crossing, and there the fence reading is plainly wrong: on a 44 degree jog of fence barring a patrol road that runs at 172 degrees, a gate squared to the fence stands 38 degrees off square to its own roadbed and reads as a glyph dropped on the road. A gate with NO way through it falls back to the local fence tangent, where the rule against stamped-on glyphs still holds - a fence running at 30 degrees gets a gate at 30 degrees and never a box squared to the page, exactly as a rampart tower is turned to its wall.

The rule the map follows: a ward gate's bar stands SQUARE across the way it bars, within about 7 degrees, and only a gate with no way through it squares to the fence instead. A lane running ALONGSIDE the fence, within 25 degrees of it, is not a way that gate bars and is ignored. A gate standing more than about 48 ft from every fence has nothing to align to and is left alone.
