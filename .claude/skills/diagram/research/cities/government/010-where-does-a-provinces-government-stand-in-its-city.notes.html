  • subai-ancient-city-sites - 「What deserves still more notice is that the layout of the streets inside Datong city is the same as the wards of Sui-Tang Chang'an and Luoyang (fig. 4), that is, a square city, with one gate opened on each side, the streets inside the four gates running into one another and combining to form one great cross-street; and at each of the four corners of the great cross-street, a small cross-street is set in turn」 and 「Not a few of these princely cities were built in the central area of the city, such as the Prince of Shu's city at Chengdu, the Prince of Tan's city at Changsha, and in the north the Prince of Qi's city at Qingzhou and the later Prince of Heng's city. Building a princely city of this kind always meant demolishing the yamen, the market and part of the dwellings that had originally been laid out in the middle of the city, and the larger and smaller streets and lanes were altered too; and the local yamen, moved elsewhere inside the city, then required a fresh laying-out of streets and of its attached offices.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; ) (Su Bai's own method for reading a Chinese city's older plan out of its modern street grid, by the founder of historical-period archaeology at Peking University. The square form is his reading of Datong, which he matches to a Sui-Tang ward, not a claim that every city took it; the princely-city passage is about the early-Ming princes' seats (Chengdu, Changsha, Qingzhou), built in the center of their cities. The passage puts the yamen there and treats its attached offices as belonging with it; it does not say they lined the avenues.)
