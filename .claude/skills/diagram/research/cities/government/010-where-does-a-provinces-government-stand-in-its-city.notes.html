• neixiang-yamen - 「The yamen covers a total site area of more than 47,000 square meters, with 18 successive courtyards and over 280 rooms.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「县衙总占地面积47000多平方米，有院落18进，房舍280余间。」); xianya-zhwiki - 「Besides the buildings where the officials worked and lodged, the county yamen also had a jail, storehouses and other buildings.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「县衙除官吏办公、住宿建筑外，还有监狱、仓库等建筑」) (the size, with the jail and storehouses on the encyclopedia beside it, both of a county yamen; the provincial and prefectural seats' own sizes are quoted under the question on how big a governor's compound is)
  • subai-ancient-city-sites - 「What deserves still more notice is that the layout of the streets inside Datong city is the same as the wards of Sui-Tang Chang'an and Luoyang (fig. 4), that is, a square city, with one gate opened on each side, the streets inside the four gates running into one another and combining to form one great cross-street; and at each of the four corners of the great cross-street, a small cross-street is set in turn」 and 「Not a few of these princely cities were built in the central area of the city, such as the Prince of Shu's city at Chengdu, the Prince of Tan's city at Changsha, and in the north the Prince of Qi's city at Qingzhou and the later Prince of Heng's city. Building a princely city of this kind always meant demolishing the yamen, the market and part of the dwellings that had originally been laid out in the middle of the city, and the larger and smaller streets and lanes were altered too; and the local yamen, moved elsewhere inside the city, then required a fresh laying-out of streets and of its attached offices.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「更值得注意的是大同城内的街道布局与隋唐长安洛阳里坊情况相同（图四），即方形城，每面各开一门，四门内街道相通，合组成一大十字街，大十字街四隅的每一隅，又都各设小十字街」 and original: 「这种王城有不少建在城市中心区域，如成都蜀王城，长沙潭王城，北方如青州的齐王城和后来的衡王城。兴建这类王城都是把原来布置在城市中部的衙署、市场和一部分民居拆了，大小街巷也改了；迁到城内别处的地方衙署，又要重新布置街道和附属机构。」) (Su Bai's own method for reading a Chinese city's older plan out of its modern street grid, by the founder of historical-period archaeology at Peking University. The square form is his reading of Datong, which he matches to a Sui-Tang ward, not a claim that every city took it; the princely-city passage is about the early-Ming princes' seats (Chengdu, Changsha, Qingzhou), built in the center of their cities. The passage puts the yamen there and treats its attached offices as belonging with it; it does not say they lined the avenues.)
