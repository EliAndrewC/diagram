• jta-aoyagi-kakunodate - 「After passing through the gate, visitors will find a roofed well (ido-yakata) and rice storehouse, as well as the main entryway」
  • semboku-bukeyashiki - 「a senior retainer of the Ashina」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「芦名氏の重臣」) and 「the roof is shingled, and there are shitomi shutters and a sweep-type well」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「屋根は木羽葺きで、しとみ戸やつるべ式の井戸があり」) (of the Iwahashi house, which the page says 「keeps the calm bearing of a middle-ranking Kakunodate samurai」 - translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「角館の中級武士としての落ち着いた構えを残しています」)
  • fujiclean-ido - 「according to a book written in Bunka 11 (1814), because digging a well cost as much as 200 ryo it was beyond the reach of samurai households and only great merchants could dig one, but a technique of digging wells with ease using a strange device called the "aori" was brought from Osaka, and wells could be dug for about 3 ryo and spread」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「文化11年（1814）に書かれた本によると、井戸掘りには200両もの費用がかかるため武家には手が出せず大商人しか掘れなかったのですが、大阪から「あをり」という奇器を使い無造作に井戸を掘る技術が伝えられ、3両ほどで井戸が掘れるようになって普及した」) (the column does not name the book; it speaks of Edo, where a shallow well on filled-in marsh gave salty water, so the 200 ryo is best read as the price of a deep bored well in Edo's low ground, not of an ordinary shallow well elsewhere)
  • fujiclean-ido - 「Not every house had a well. Most were communal wells.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「どの家にも井戸があったわけではありません。多くは共同の井戸でした」)
