How big is a provincial governor's compound?

Sources: nanyang-fuya-zhwiki and sohu-nanyang-yamen (the one preserved prefectural yamen, and its two reported sizes), bjd-qing-yamen (a governor-general's and a department's yamen), takayama-jinya-city (the one surviving Japanese provincial office), kokuga-jawiki (the ancient Japanese provincial offices); this project's measurement of the drawn provincial cities

A governor's compound was a whole city block, and a large one - but how large depends on which tradition it follows, and within each the figure varies. The Chinese yamen of a region were big: the prefectural yamen at Nanyang, the only one that survives complete, is given as 240 m by 150 m, 36,000 square meters (about 790 by 490 ft, 9 acres), and by another page as 300 m by 240 m, over 72,000 square meters (about 18 acres); the Zhili governor-general's yamen at Baoding, a province's seat, is 134.4 m by about 224 m, over 30,000 square meters (about 440 by 735 ft, 7 acres), and the Huozhou department yamen 38,500 square meters. Rank does not climb in step with size: the county yamen at Neixiang, a tier below all of them, is 47,000 square meters (on where a province's government stands). The Japanese seat of the later, shogunal state is a third or less of that. The Takayama jin'ya, from which the shogunate governed the whole province of Hida, covers 11,219 square meters with the plaza in front of it, 9,807 without (about 2.4 acres, some 3,000 tsubo), and the provincial offices of the ancient imperial state were districts commonly laid out at several tens of meters to about 100 m square - one example 300 by 200 m - whose sizes, the article stresses, differed more than they agreed from province to province. Those ancient offices were an older form of a different state, a laid-out district of halls rather than one household's compound, and they range too widely - under a hectare to 6 - to narrow anything, so the span below leaves them out.

So the attested span for a provincial seat runs from about 1 hectare (Takayama, a Japanese provincial office) to 3-7 hectares (the Chinese prefectural and provincial yamen). The drawn provincial cities sit near the bottom of it: Minami's compound is 525 by 300 ft, and Nagahara's and Tango's 436 by 366 ft, each about 1.5 hectares (3.6 acres) - half again Takayama, a fifth to a half of a Chinese prefecture's. That is inside the span and on the side of the tradition the record follows for administrative interiors (administrative culture is Japan-first), and it keeps the governor's compound well above a county magistrate's, which the record brackets between about 1,900 and 9,800 square meters (the Mode A scale).

The rule the map follows: a governor's compound is between about 1 and 3.6 hectares (2.5 to 9 acres - on the order of 330 by 330 ft to 650 by 600 ft), rolled per city from its seed within that span, never below the largest county compound; the drawn cities' 1.5 hectares is one value inside it. The lower bound is the Japanese provincial office, the upper the smaller Chinese prefectural figure; the larger Chinese figures (7 hectares) are left out because a compound that size would crowd a walled provincial city of a few thousand inhabitants - a judgment of this project's, not a measured limit (guess).
