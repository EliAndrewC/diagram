• buke-hokonin-wiki - 「They were basically of peasant or townsman origin, and most were degawari (rotating) service on one-year, half-year or day-hire terms; the yearly stipend was about 2 ryo 2 bu.」 (translated; ) 「In the Edo period fixed-term (nenki) contracts, or hiring from a placement broker only when needed, became common.」 (translated; )
  • kotobank-degawari - 「Anciently it was the second day of the second month and the second day of the eighth month, but by shogunal decree in Kanbun 9 (1669) it was changed to the fifth day of the third month and the fifth day of the ninth month.」 (translated; ) 「In Edo too, where it had been the 2nd and 8th months, it was changed to the 3rd and 9th months in 1668 (Kanbun 8) by order of the shogunate」 (translated; ) (the first passage is from one reference work on the page, the second from another; a third entry on the same page gives the changeover as the 5th day of the 3rd month after Edo's Meireki fire of 1657, without naming a decree)
  • kotobank-hitoyado - 「But in the early modern period it gradually shifted to one-year and half-year rotating (degawari) service, monthly hire and day hire, done for wages by peasants and townsmen employed through hitoyado (kuchiireya, keian) and the hiyoza.」 (translated; ) 「They appeared from early in the early modern period onward, along with the growth of the cities」 (translated; ) ("They" are the brokers, named in the sentence before the quote; the first passage is on samurai-household service; the page is written about Edo, and never says where in a town a broker kept its premises)
  • buke-hokonin-wiki - 「The number that had to be retained was fixed by kokudaka (assessed yield) and the like, and they were levied from the civilian population of the castle town and elsewhere.」 (translated; ) 「Most who took service were second and younger sons of peasants and the like, but in great cities such as Edo, where samurai residences were many, it was also common to hire professional servants such as the watari-chugen who went from residence to residence.」 (translated; )
  • buke-hokonin-wiki - 「Where there were no connections and hands were short, staff were dispatched by placement agents called hitoyado or kakaemoto.」 (translated; ) (brokers)
