• lifang-zhwiki - 「Tang Chang'an, the Silla royal capital, Japan's Heian-kyo and the Western Xia's Xingqing prefecture were all capital cities built on the ward system.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「唐長安城、新羅王城、日本平安京、西夏興慶府都是採用里坊制建造的都城。」) (capitals as the examples, and an end after the Song and Yuan; that smaller seats lacked it is on no page read)
  • changan-enwiki - 「Chang'an's walled and gated wards were much larger than conventional city blocks seen in modern cities, as the smallest ward had a surface area of 68 acres, and the largest ward had a surface area of 233 acres (0.94 km2). The walls enclosing each ward averaged 9 to 10 ft (3.0 m) in height.」 and 「Its grid of more than one hundred walled wards culminated a centuries-old tradition of Chinese capital planning.」 (English. 68 acres is 27.5 ha and 233 acres 94.3 ha, so the real spread is wider at both ends than a 30-to-80 band, which makes the entry's point more strongly rather than less.)
  • lifang-zhwiki - 「The ward system ceased to exist after the Song and the Yuan.」 (translated from the Chinese by this project; original: 「里坊制度於宋、元之後不復存在。」)
  • jokamachi-jawiki - 「Furthermore, palisades and wooden gates were set up for each town division, and at night the gates were shut and gatekeepers posted, so as to block the entry of suspicious persons.」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「また、町割ごとに柵や木戸を設けて、夜には門を閉めて門番を立たせるなど、不審者の侵入を阻んでいた。」) (palisades and wooden gates per quarter, shut at night; the earthwork, the samurai-quarter scope and the fence ends abutting the city wall are this page's)
