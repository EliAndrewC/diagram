Does China put martial training in the towns too?

Sources: wuxue-zhwiki (the order that county schools enroll military students), wuju-zhwiki (the levels of the military examination)

Yes, and more starkly than Japan (the Japanese evidence). An order of 1637 required the prefectural, departmental and county schools all to enroll military students, so the state's martial schooling sat in the seats of government. The military examination itself ran at the provincial, metropolitan and palace levels, the last before the emperor. That there was no dense network of private martial academies at a county seat, and that its soldiers drilled at a garrison ground rather than in a hall, are a guess, on no page read.

The map's rule for martial halls follows the Japanese form, whose look the maps take; the Chinese evidence agrees with it in putting the institution in the town, not the countryside.
