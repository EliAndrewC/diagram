Where do a samurai household's servants sleep when the house has no gate range?

Sources: kotobank-bukeyashiki (the smaller house with a wall instead of a range, and the provincial town's few ranges), nando-jawiki (the storeroom that was also a bedroom), aizu-bukeyashiki-jawiki (a chief retainer's house of 38 rooms under one roof), aizu-bukeyashiki-guide (that house's servants' rooms and the range beside its gate)

In the house. A long gate range was a large household's (the servant-housing question). A smaller samurai house, with few live-in retainers and servants, had a wall in place of the range, and in a provincial castle town, where every samurai held a plot of their own, there was no need to build many ranges at all. The few servants then slept under the main roof, in nando and service rooms: the nando, a storeroom, was also used as a bedroom. The stipend below which a house had no range, the service rooms off the kitchen and the servants sleeping there are a GUESS, on no page read.

Even at the top, the women slept inside. Aizu's reconstructed samurai residence has 38 rooms, from the lord's reception hall through the domain's office rooms, the inner rooms and the kitchen, all under one roof. It is the house of Saigo Tanomo, a chief retainer (karo) of the domain, and its rooms fall into four groups - the lord's reception suite, the retainers' office and guard rooms, the family's rooms, and the maids' and servants' rooms - while a one-sided range beside the front gate held retainers' dwellings.

What it means for the map: the smallest samurai households draw no servant building at all; their servants are inside the house the map already draws.
