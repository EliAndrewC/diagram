Does a samurai's house have its own well?

Sources: jta-aoyagi-kakunodate and semboku-bukeyashiki (two surviving samurai houses with their own wells); fujiclean-ido (what a well cost, and when it came within reach)

Yes, in a provincial town. Kakunodate's surviving samurai houses have their own wells: through the Aoyagi house's gate stands a roofed well, and the Iwahashi house, a middle-ranking samurai family's, has a sweep well. That is why a samurai ward shows no public wells: the communal wells are a commoner institution, and a samurai household drew its water inside its own fence.

A well was once dear. A book of 1814 is reported as saying that digging a well had cost as much as 200 ryo, beyond a samurai household's means, until a boring method brought from Osaka cut it to about 3 ryo and wells spread; most wells were shared. So the private well is the well-established house's, and nothing read gives the foot soldiers' blocks one per plot; that they drew from a shared well in their block is a guess.

The rule the map follows: every samurai lot inside the ward carries its own well, drawn inside the fence near the gate, and the ward carries no public well; the foot soldiers' blocks at the town's edge share one well among a file of plots, a guess.
