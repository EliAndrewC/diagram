  • kotobank-baba - 「these riding grounds were enclosed by earthen banks and fences (called rachi), and were often of a long, narrow shape, about 100 ken long yet about 10 ken wide; this was because contests in which a horse is run in a straight line while shooting with bow and arrow, such as yabusame and kasagake, were many」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「これらの馬場は土塁および垣（埒（らち）という）で囲われ，長さが100間ほどあるのに幅は10間ほどという細長い形が多いが，これは馬を一直線に走らせて弓矢を射る流鏑馬，笠懸のような競技が多かったためである」)
  • kotobank-baba - 「in the Ikeda family's time, a riding ground 5 ken wide on the riverside embankment running two and a half cho north from beside the castle's main-gate moat to the Zuio-ji temple. Below the embankment, south of the middle, a small board-roofed viewing stand of three ken by two and a half ken, and a three-horse outer tie-rail facing west」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「池田氏の時代、城の大手堀際より北は随鴎寺までの二町半の川端堤防にあった幅五間の馬場。中央より南の堤の下に三間・二間半の小板屋の馬見所、西向きに三匹立ての外繋ぎがあった」) (Ako)
  • kotobank-oimawashi - 「a riding ground for the lord's and the retainers' practice of horsemanship and mounted archery, laid out north-south from the south side of the Hanabata residence」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「花畑館の南側から南北に、白川近くの山崎南端までの間に設けられた藩主・家臣の馬術・騎射の練習のための馬場」) and 「on its east side an earthen bank (doi) was built」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「東側は土居（土手）が築かれ」) (Kumamoto)
  • kotobank-oimawashi - 「one of Sendai's three riding grounds ... here the samurai and troops strove to polish the arts of riding, mounted archery, dakyu and the like」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「仙台三馬場の一つ...士卒はここで乗馬法・騎射・打毬などの諸技の練磨に励み」) and 「below the cliff of the main bailey」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「本丸の崖下にあたり」)
  • kotobank-baba - 「at first there were only the residences of the two senior retainers Yamazaki and Ikoma and a riding ground to their east」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「当初は山崎・生駒両家老邸とその東に馬場があるだけであった」) (Daishoji)
  • takadanobaba-jawiki - 「in extent, 6 cho (about 650 m) east-west and 30-odd ken (about 55 m) north-south」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「広さは東西へ6町（約650m）、南北へ30余間（約55m）」) and 「in 1636 the third Tokugawa shogun Iemitsu built a riding ground for the bannermen's training in horsemanship, yabusame and the like」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「1636年、徳川三代将軍家光により旗本達の馬術の訓練や流鏑馬などのための馬場が造営された」)
  • kotobank-yaba - 「in samurai houses the length was fixed at 33 bow-lengths (about 76 meters) and the width likewise at one (about 2.3 meters); at the shooting place an azuchi was built and the target hung on it. Archery grounds were set inside the castle or inside residences, or in outskirts with few houses」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「武家では長さ弓杖(きゅうじょう)33丈（約76メートル）、幅は同じく1丈（約2.3メートル）と決められ、射場には垜(あずち)を築き、これに的をかける。矢場は城内や屋敷内、または人家の少ない郊外に設けられた」) (the page writes the unit as 丈; its meter figures fit a bow-length of about 2.3 m)
  • toshiya-jawiki - 「the domains made things called origake and dogata and practiced」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「諸藩では折掛・堂形と称する物を作り稽古した」) and 「dogata are said to have existed in the Kishu domain, the Owari domain, the Sendai domain, the Matsue domain, the Kaga domain and others」 (translated from the Japanese by this project; original: 「堂形は紀州藩、尾張藩、仙台藩、松江藩、加賀藩などにあったという」)
