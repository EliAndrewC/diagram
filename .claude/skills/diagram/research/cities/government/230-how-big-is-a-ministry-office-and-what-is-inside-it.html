How big is a ministry office, and what is inside it?

Sources: sohu-wuzhou-yamen (the six rooms of a local yamen), bjd-ming-qing-ministries (the capital's ministries as compounds of their own), visitbeijing-daqingmen (where each stood), hubu-zhwiki (what a ministry held inside); this project's measurement of the drawn provincial cities

Below the capital the six were not buildings at all. In a local yamen the six offices were rooms flanking the court before the great hall - personnel, revenue and rites on the east, war, justice and works on the west - generally two bays each, and alike. Only at the capital did they become compounds of their own, built one at a time: the Ming Ministry of Rites first, from 1430, on the plan of its Nanjing predecessor, the others after it either side of the palace approach, the Rites compound keeping a storehouse of its own. They did not stand as a matched set. East of the Daqing gate stood Personnel, Revenue and Rites, and further east War and Works; Justice, in the Qing, stood west of the gate, beside the Censorate and the Court of Judicial Review, with the other judicial offices rather than with its fellow ministries.

Inside, a ministry was its bureaus and its papers. The Ming-Qing Ministry of Revenue worked through fourteen bureaus, one for each province, while the things it managed - the mint, the three treasuries, the granaries - were offices of their own under it rather than rooms in it. So a ministry office is a hall of business, rooms for its bureaus and clerks, and a document store; the money and the grain are elsewhere.

How big a capital ministry's compound was is not on any page read, and a provincial ministry office has no historical counterpart to measure, since a province's six offices were rooms in its yamen. The drawn provincial cities give each ministry 114 by 78 ft (Minami) or 130 by 90 ft (Nagahara, Tango, whose Ministry of Rites is 140 by 95 ft), against the 224 by 148 ft the capital's record gives a ministry (ministry offices: six equal compounds).

The rule the map follows: a provincial ministry office is a small walled compound of about 110 by 80 ft to 140 by 95 ft - a GUESS, the drawn cities' range, since history puts these offices inside the governor's yamen rather than in compounds of their own - holding one office hall, its bureau and clerks' rooms and a document storehouse, and no treasury or granary. The six are equal in size, on the GM's reading that the six ministers are peers of the same rank by law and so take peer offices (a county yamen's six rooms were equal too); where they stand is the setting's own arrangement (where a province's government stands).
