City defenses: the research behind the wall, gate and tower rules

Gate structures - real footprints

Sources: xian-wall-zhwiki (98 mamian, 20 m wide, projecting 12 m;1 the wall 12 m high, base 15-18 m, top 12-14 m2)

  • What the research found (China first: Xi'an / Pingyao / Beijing walls; Edo sekisho secondary). A gate guard duty room is a small 1-3 bay building (~15-35 ft); a gate inspection hall (sekisho/lijin bansho) ~25-45 ft; the wall's MAMIAN bastion (马面 - the projecting spur that carries the enemy-tower) is ~65x40 ft (Xi'an's are ~20 m wide projecting ~12 m),3 the tower building on it ~30-40 ft; a provincial GATE tower (城楼) ~52x30 ft (reserve the 120-130 ft towers for a capital); a corner tower ~50-66 ft (the gate-tower and corner-tower figures unsourced). Well-attested: the mamian; no readable page supports the provincial gate-tower footprint (unsourced); approximate (bay-count estimates): the guard/inspection buildings.

Gate furniture at the throat - barbican and tax-barrier practice

Sources: hakone-seki-jawiki (the 面番所 and 向番所 lists;4 that they stand opposite each other across the road is a GUESS - no readable page supports it),5 wengcheng-zhwiki (the barbican).6 chaoguan-zhwiki (the Ming-Qing customs barriers, which stood on the canals rather than at a county seat's gates)

  • What the research found (China first: wengcheng 甕城 barbican + lijin 厘金 / chaoguan 鈔關 tax barriers;9 Japan's Hakone/Arai sekisho as the best-preserved architectural proof). An inspection/tax barrier only works where traffic is forced single-file, and a walled city's gate passage is the ONE such chokepoint in the whole wall - set the station back and arrivals disperse into the street grid before ever reaching it, defeating its purpose. So guard + inspection cluster AT the gate opening, ~20-100 ft inside (the distance unsourced), flanking the road as it enters. Hakone is decisive: just inside each gomon (gate) the Obansho (papers/goods inspection office) and the ashigaru guardhouse stand OPPOSITE each other across the road, and nobody reaches the town beyond without passing between them.8 In a wengcheng the whole cluster reads most authentically inside the urn-courtyard between the two misaligned gates.7 "Set back a few hundred feet along the wall" is historically wrong.

Chinese customs practice is not the anchor. The Ming-Qing 鈔關 customs barriers stood at commodity centers and on the Grand Canal and strategic routes, not at a county seat's gates;10 lijin is a separate, later Qing tax (unsourced). The gate-throat cluster rests on the Hakone sekisho arrangement (the two stations facing each other is a GUESS - no readable page supports it)11 and the chokepoint reasoning, not on Chinese customs practice.

Wall towers - the mamian system and bowshot ranges

Sources: xian-wall-zhwiki (the ~120 m mamian spacing, tied to bow and firearm range)

  • What the research found (China first: the mamian 马面 wall-tower system; Warring-States through Ming). The rule of thumb IS accurate, and it is the ORIGINAL design purpose of the projecting wall tower (unsourced). Adjacent mamian bastions are spaced so their fields of flanking fire (侧射) overlap - an attacker at the curtain base between two towers is shot at from BOTH (Shen Kuo's 11th-c. Mengxi Bitan describes it as 矢石相及, "arrows and stones reach each other") (unsourced). The spacing that achieves ">= 2 towers everywhere" therefore equals the effective arrow range: put towers a bowshot apart and every curtain point has two within a bowshot. Real spacings bracket the effective ranges: Pingyao ~50-60 m (>= 2 everywhere, an aimed-lethal bowshot) (unsourced), Xi'an ~120 m (= 2 x 60 m; the crossfire covers the curtain but the base near a tower has only that one at full reach - the sparser, peacetime-affordable ring).12 Effective bow ranges: aimed-lethal ~60 m (a war arrow kills reliably), full war-bow reach ~100-150 m (harassing fire) (unsourced).
