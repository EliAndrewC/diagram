# Design notes: Hoshigaoka Country Shrine

**Subject**: the shrine of Hoshigaoka village district - the seat of the district's country monk - Otsuki, of the Order of Bishamon, whose record is the Obsidian Portal character of that name - who lives here, keeps the district's registers, and performs the villagers' rites. Hoshigaoka is the reference village of the water-first family (`legacy-hand-authored-pool/villages/hoshigaoka/`), whose district names the GM dictated on 2026-08-29; on its village map the shrine stands apart from the houses at the south-east water-mouth entrance on the grazing back-slope, and this sheet is that place at 3 px = 1 ft. The exemplar of the country-shrine program (feature 254), drawn to the village map (feature 257).

**Program type**: Country shrine (a village district's shrine) - see `buildings/programs.md`.

**Form**: one roof

**On map**: legacy-hand-authored-pool/villages/hoshigaoka/hoshigaoka.json - religious at (392, 1074) = hall

**The sheet matches the village map** (the GM, 2026-09-20: "we should make the diagram view match what is shown on the larger map of Hoshigaoka"). The hall's center is the map's shrine at map (392, 1074); 6 sheet px make one map px. Feature 268 (the GM, 2026-09-27) redrew both together: the map was edited by hand - "Add grove to map ... a grove around the shrine, plus the sacred tree and a stone basin by the approach" - and its seven arches moved to the 12 ft pitch ("All maps, ~10-13 ft"); the sheet draws the same trees, one for one (the map's `village_groves` entry of role `shrine` records each sheet tree's center as a clump), and `matches_map` holds it in both directions. The frame is cropped to the ink like any sheet's (the GM: "whatever kind of cropping we are doing is not working very well on this map").

**What the sheet draws, and why** (research/religion-and-death.html, questions 090, 120, 122, 124, 126):

- **No enclosure.** No fence, wall or hedge rings the precinct: no dated fence round a whole village precinct is attested, and a wall is a temple's rank mark. The arch and the wood mark the shrine's ground (accurate).
- **The precinct is the grove**, 137 by 223 ft (30,522 sq ft, about 858 tsubo; measured on the drawn outline, feature 268 T08), from the map's shrine well at its back edge to the outermost arch, where the approach enters the shrine's ground, wide enough that the wood stands two or three crowns deep behind the hall and beside the forecourt, and one staggered crown deep beside the hall itself, where the kitchen garden takes the width - its edge ragged and the edge crowns straddling it, so it does not read as a hedge (the settlement-review rounds of 2026-09-27). That is inside the village-shrine registers as found - above Saitama's top (708), above Tochigi's mean (median 655, mean 817, range to 2,700) - accurate as a band, its place in the band a guess set by the map's depth (the well 108 ft behind the hall), the seven arches and the forecourt. The hall is 9% of it and the swept clearing, hall included, 23%; the 51 canopies (crowns 13-20 ft, true size; the edge crowns straddle the grove's ragged edge, so the wood's edge is not a file) cover 42% of it, the grove's floor the rest (measured on the layout). Its size rests on the map: the well 108 ft behind the hall, the seven arches before it, and a grove two or three crowns deep on either side; the one Edo-period set read (a Kyushu plain's hamlet shrines, about 60-240 tsubo) runs smaller, the Meiji registers larger (research 124).
- **The swept clearing** is a ragged patch, never a ruled rectangle (research 'Why does that swept ground have a ragged edge?'), and so is the grove's own edge; it holds the sanctuary behind it, the kitchen garden and privy at the west gable and a forecourt 37 ft deep before the step, where the villagers gather, with the three innermost arches standing in it (the size-audit of 2026-09-27: a 7 ft forecourt inverted the clearing's order; the depth is a guess from the use, no source splitting a precinct's open ground from its wood); a footpath runs from its north-west corner, on the kitchen side, through the grove to the well, never across the sanctuary's ground.
- **The seven arches** stand 12 ft apart, the innermost 12 ft off the hall's face (the GM's threshold rule of 2026-07-27), drawn in plan: the top beam 15 by 1.5 ft across the 10 ft approach, the posts 1.2 ft square under its ends, marked darker, 10.3 ft clear (true size). The outermost is declared first; it stands at the grove's edge. The approach runs 88 ft from the grove's edge to the hall's step.
- **The basin** - a plain stone basin, not a roofed pavilion (research 126: the pavilion is an elite form; the plain basin's form a guess) - stands beside the approach in the forecourt, between the step and the innermost arch; the map's well stays where the map has it, so the shrine has both.
- **The sacred tree** stands beside the approach on the east, between the fifth and sixth arches, its 22 ft crown over a 3 ft trunk ringed by its rope, the second and third arches from the grove's edge beside it (the GM's "the sacred tree ... by the approach"; accurate in kind, how common a guess).

**Knob settings** (every knob, defaults included):

1. Hall-and-dwelling form: **one roof** (the default - the GM's form, 2026-09-19). The building is 60 by 48 ft (the map's footprint), its uses end to end along the ridge, the villagers' hall in the CENTER - a deliberate deviation from Kaie-ji, where the one-room main hall is at one end and the priest's quarters (a wide earthen floor and tatami rooms) at the other: the map's approach runs to the building's center line, so the hall stands there and the kitchen and the monk's rooms flank it, the monk crossing the hall to his kitchen (the building-review of 2026-09-27): the kitchen's earthen floor at the west end (16 ft, its door on the west gable), the villagers' hall in the center, 28 ft wide, facing the approach with its raised step and its altar at the rear toward the sanctuary, and the monk's rooms at the east end (16 ft) with a 12 ft writing room for the district's registers in the front corner and the dwelling's door on the east wall opening onto its entry floor; the hall's rear 14 ft is an altar bay, so the worship floor before it is 28 by 34 ft.
2. Bell tower: **absent** (the default).
3. Dedication: **Bishamon**, the Fortune of Strength - the GM's word for this district (2026-09-20). Named on the sanctuary and nothing else of it drawn (no guardian figures at average wealth).
4. Grove and burial-ground side: **by the map** - the grove the map now draws; the village graveyard stays 430 ft west, outside the frame.
5. Wealth: **average** - thatch and plain timber; none of the donated stonework (guardian figures, lanterns, strength stones) and no sanctuary fence.
6. Farmers' stage: **absent** (the default; the map draws none).
7. Sumo ring: **absent** (the default; the map draws none).

**Particulars**: the seven arches. A village shrine at average wealth would carry one; this one carries seven because its country monk is a stickler for the potent number seven and raised the extra six himself (the GM's one-off, 2026-07-22; reaffirmed 2026-09-26). No page read shows a village shrine with a row of arches before 1868 (research 090); the seven are the setting's particular, and the wealth knob stays average.

**The named monk**: Otsuki registers the district's births and marriages, stamps its travel papers and teaches the village's sons and daughters their letters in the hall; his chief clerk Sadaji, the headman's second son, and two of the district's licensed ashigaru keep the records and the correspondence, so the writing room and its record chest are in daily use (his Obsidian Portal record).

**Deliberate choices and tolerated stretches**:

- The captions: the well and the fire-water tubs carry none (declared by id; a well at a shrine is obvious); the basin and the sacred tree carry one word each, since neither reads unaided at this size. Every caption is seated by `make seat-label` (feature 266).
- The trees are laid once, by a seeded dart-throw inside the grove outside the clearing, the approach, the path, the well and the captions, and the same list is drawn on the map and the sheet; crowns are 13-20 ft across, true size. Canopies touch and never overlap (`trees_overlap`), so the wood reads more open than a real closed canopy; whether that rule should give way inside a shrine grove is carried to the GM.
- The building is drawn as the map's one block, 60 by 48 ft - deeper than the one attested one-roof example (Kaie-ji, 8 to 13 m); whether the map's 24 px depth is a measured footprint or a glyph's rounding is the GM's question, carried to them on 2026-09-20.
