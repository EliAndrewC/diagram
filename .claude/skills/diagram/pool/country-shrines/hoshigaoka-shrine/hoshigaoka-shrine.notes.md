# Design notes: Hoshigaoka Country Shrine

**Subject**: the shrine of Hoshigaoka village district - the seat of the district's country monk - Otsuki, of the Order of Bishamon, whose record is the Obsidian Portal character of that name - who lives here, keeps the district's registers, and performs the villagers' rites. Hoshigaoka is the reference village of the water-first family (`legacy-hand-authored-pool/villages/hoshigaoka/`), whose district names the GM dictated on 2026-08-29; on its village map the shrine stands apart from the houses at the south-east water-mouth entrance on the grazing back-slope, and this sheet is that place at 3 px = 1 ft. The exemplar of the country-shrine program (feature 254), drawn to the village map (feature 257).

**Program type**: Country shrine (a village district's shrine) - see `buildings/programs.md`.

**Form**: one roof
**Grove form**: behind and sides

**On map**: legacy-hand-authored-pool/villages/hoshigaoka/hoshigaoka.json - religious at (392, 1074) = hall

**The sheet matches the village map** (the GM, 2026-09-20: "we should make the diagram view match what is shown on the larger map of Hoshigaoka"). The hall's center is the map's shrine at map (392, 1074); 6 sheet px make one map px. Feature 268 (the GM, 2026-09-27) redrew both together: the map was edited by hand - "Add grove to map ... a grove around the shrine, plus the sacred tree and a stone basin by the approach" - and its seven arches moved to the 12 ft pitch ("All maps, ~10-13 ft"); the sheet draws the same trees, one for one (the map's `village_groves` entry of role `shrine` records each sheet tree's center as a clump), and `matches_map` holds it in both directions. The frame is cropped to the ink like any sheet's (the GM: "whatever kind of cropping we are doing is not working very well on this map").

**What the sheet draws, and why** (research/religion-and-death.html, questions 090, 120, 122, 124, 126):

- **No enclosure.** No fence, wall or hedge rings the precinct: no dated fence round a whole village precinct is attested, and a wall is a temple's rank mark. The arch and the wood mark the shrine's ground (accurate).
- **The precinct holds the grove, behind the hall and at its sides** (feature 279; research 'What shape is a village shrine's wood, and on which sides of the hall does it stand?'). The precinct is 137 by 215 ft (29,429 sq ft, about 827 tsubo; measured on the drawn outline, feature 270), from the map's shrine well at its back edge to the outermost arch, where the approach enters the shrine's ground. That is inside the village-shrine registers as found - above Saitama's top (708), above Tochigi's mean (median 655, mean 817, range to 2,700) - accurate as a range, its place in it a guess set by the map's depth (the well 108 ft behind the hall), the seven arches and the forecourt; the one Edo-period set read (a Kyushu plain's hamlet shrines, about 60-240 tsubo) runs smaller, the Meiji registers larger (research 124). Its wood stands behind the hall and down both its sides (knob 8, `behind and sides`): the hall stands mid-slope, the ground rising behind it toward the village and falling before it down the approach, with no break of slope, so its candidates are both slope classes' forms - behind, behind and sides, sides (a guess, the record covering only a hall at a break) - and the roll gave behind and sides. The map's one terrain datum is an even fall to the south-east (`meta.down_deg` 45), diagonal to the north-south axis; no source read ties a flank to a cross-slope, so the fall's direction sets no side. The wood runs from the well at its back edge round both flanks to ragged tips beside the forecourt's foot, the west tip about 11 ft lower; the lower approach is open ground, holding the sacred tree, some 13 ft clear of the wood, and across the approach from it the monk's kitchen garden (feature 283). The wood runs past the precinct's line, about 21 ft west, 22 ft east and 16 ft behind its back line - a quarter of its 17,600 sq ft of canopy (4,600 sq ft) stands outside the rectangle - as a shrine's wood is kept in and around its precinct (research 'How large was a village shrine's precinct, and how much of it was built on?' gives the precinct, not the wood). The wood's 134 canopies (crowns 13-20 ft, true size, overlapping) cover 90% of the ground they may cover (all but the clearing, the approach, the path, the well and the captions) - a closed canopy, its density a guess, no source measuring one (measured on the layout). Its edge is its crowns' own, a smooth-noise outline with real bays and spurs, never ruled (a guess): no four edge crowns lie within 2 ft of one line (`STRAIGHT_RUN`), no stretch of edge 50 ft or longer stays within a crown's radius of one (`LONG_RUN`), and each outer side's drawn silhouette departs from its best-fit line by 10.6 ft in and 7.4 ft out (east) and 8.6 ft in and 16.6 ft out (west) - more than a crown's radius each way, so neither side reads as ruled - all measured on the layout (feature 279). The floor is the crowns' union closed over the gaps between them, never past the crowns.
- **The swept clearing** is a ragged patch, never a ruled rectangle (research 'Why does that swept ground have a ragged edge?'), and so are the wood's edges; it holds the sanctuary behind it, the kitchen yard at the west gable, the privy at its back corner (off the forecourt, where the villagers purify at the basin, 10 ft from the kitchen door and 29 ft from the sanctuary, near the path to the well - the building-review of 2026-09-27; the 2026-09-20 review's front corner kept it off a kitchen door that the longer building now clears) and a forecourt 39 ft deep before the step, where the villagers gather, with the three innermost arches standing in it (the size-audit of 2026-09-27: a 7 ft forecourt inverted the clearing's order; the depth is a guess from the use, no source splitting a precinct's open ground from its wood); a footpath runs from its north-west corner, on the kitchen side, through the wood behind the hall to the well, never across the sanctuary's ground.
- **The seven arches** stand 12 ft apart, the innermost 12 ft off the hall's face (the GM's threshold rule of 2026-07-27), drawn in plan: the top beam 15 by 1.5 ft across the 10 ft approach, the posts 1.2 ft square under its ends, marked darker, 10.3 ft clear (true size). The outermost is declared first; it stands at the precinct's edge, in open ground below the wood's tips. The approach runs 87 ft from the precinct's edge to the hall's step.
- **The basin** - a plain stone basin, not a roofed pavilion (research 126: the pavilion is an elite form; the plain basin's form a guess) - stands beside the approach in the forecourt, between the step and the innermost arch; the map's well stays where the map has it, so the shrine has both.
- **The sacred tree** stands beside the approach on the east, between the fifth and sixth arches, its 30 ft crown - the precinct's biggest - over a 3 ft trunk ringed by its rope, standing alone in the open ground below the wood's east tip, the second and third arches from the precinct's edge beside it (the GM's "the sacred tree ... by the approach"; accurate in kind, how common a guess).

**Knob settings** (every knob, defaults included):

1. Hall-and-dwelling form: **one roof** (the default - the GM's form, 2026-09-19). The building is **66 by 32 ft** (2,112 sq ft), sized from the record (research 120; feature 270, the GM 2026-09-27: the map's former 60 by 48 ft was its glyph's, "NOT a real measurement"): the whole inside the one-roof band of 2,100 to 3,600 sq ft and its 32 ft depth inside the one attested example's 26 to 43 ft (Kaie-ji) - accurate as bands, the values guesses; its uses end to end along the ridge, the villagers' hall in the CENTER - a deliberate deviation from Kaie-ji, where the one-room main hall is at one end and the priest's quarters (a wide earthen floor and tatami rooms) at the other: the map's approach runs to the building's center line, so the hall stands there and the kitchen and the monk's rooms flank it, the monk crossing the hall to his kitchen (the building-review of 2026-09-27): the kitchen's earthen floor at the west end (16 ft, the farmhouse's earthen floor, about a third of its 46 ft; its door on the west gable, opening onto the kitchen yard), the villagers' hall in the center, 28 by 32 ft (inside the village-hall band of about 20 to 35 ft on a side), facing the approach with its raised step and its altar at the rear toward the sanctuary, and the monk's rooms at the east end (22 ft) with an 18 by 12 ft writing room for the district's registers along the front and the dwelling's door on the east wall opening onto a 4 ft entry floor in the front corner; the hall's rear 10 ft is an altar bay, so the worship floor before it is 28 by 22 ft. The two ends together, 1,216 sq ft, are about the farmhouse's 46 by 28 ft (1,288 sq ft) the record gives a separate dwelling (the size-audit of 2026-09-27: a 22 ft kitchen end was half again a farmhouse's earthen floor).
2. Bell tower: **absent** (the default).
3. Dedication: **Bishamon**, the Fortune of Strength - the GM's word for this district (2026-09-20). Named on the sanctuary and nothing else of it drawn (no guardian figures at average wealth).
4. Grove and burial-ground side: **by the map** - the grove the map now draws; the village graveyard stays 430 ft west, outside the frame.
5. Wealth: **average** - thatch and plain timber; none of the donated stonework (guardian figures, lanterns, strength stones) and no sanctuary fence.
6. Farmers' stage: **absent** (the default; the map draws none).
7. Sumo ring: **absent** (the default; the map draws none).
8. Grove form: **behind and sides** (feature 279). The hall stands mid-slope, with no break of slope, so the candidates are both slope classes' forms in the knob's order; the roll is exactly `random.Random(zlib.crc32(b"hoshigaoka:shrine grove form")).choice(["behind", "behind and sides", "sides"])` - the map has no seed, so the seed is its name. (A first reading of the ground as a hall at the top of its slope rolled `sides` between two candidates; the building-review of 2026-09-28 found the map has no such break.)

**Particulars**: the seven arches. A village shrine at average wealth would carry one; this one carries seven because its country monk is a stickler for the potent number seven and raised the extra six himself (the GM's one-off, 2026-07-22; reaffirmed 2026-09-26). No page read shows a village shrine with a row of arches before 1868 (research 090); the seven are the setting's particular, and the wealth knob stays average.

**The named monk**: Otsuki registers the district's births and marriages, stamps its travel papers and teaches the village's sons and daughters their letters in the hall; his chief clerk Sadaji, the headman's second son, and two of the district's licensed ashigaru keep the records and the correspondence, so the writing room and its record chest are in daily use (his Obsidian Portal record).

**Deliberate choices and tolerated stretches**:

- Kitchen bed: a **sun** bed, the check's default (a sheet declares a half-shade bed with `data-bed="half-shade"`; the GM, 2026-09-28, "Move them all" - the half-shade bed was offered and not chosen; research homesteads 044).
- The captions: the well and the fire-water tubs carry none (declared by id; a well at a shrine is obvious); the basin and the sacred tree carry one word each, since neither reads unaided at this size. Every caption is seated by `make seat-label` (feature 266).
- **The kitchen garden's sun** (feature 283, the GM 2026-09-28): the bed stood in the yard at the kitchen door, where the hall shaded it until about 10 and the wood's west flank from about 1 in the afternoon - 2.5 hours of sun where a bed of daikon and the other sun crops needs 6 (research homesteads 044). It was moved to the open ground below the forecourt, west of the approach, where it gets 9.5, about 65 ft from the kitchen door, 3 ft clear of the wood's nearest crown. The bed moved, not the wood: the GM chose to move the shaded gardens ("Move them all", 2026-09-28), and a shrine's own wood is not cut. No seat inside the clearing gets its hours (the west forecourt 2.5 h, the east 4-4.5, measured in the building review). The cost, accepted: the service way from the kitchen, the privy and the well now runs down the clearing's west side past the forecourt's margin, about 10 ft from the basin; the alternatives priced were a half-shade bed at the kitchen door (offered to the GM and not chosen) and felling the wood's west flank (a shrine wood is not cut). Where a resident keeper kept his plot was searched and not found stated (research buildings 405); a plot near the dwelling, on the open ground its sun reaches, is a guess. The village map draws open scrub at the bed's place, though its `gardens` vocabulary could carry the plot: the map is a frozen legacy exhibit (dev/pool.md: never regenerated; feature 279 edited it only where the wood read visibly wrong), so the bed is owed to the map at its conversion to scripted generation, and is listed there (future-work/farming-communities.md). Its size, 14 by 23 ft, is a guess - the record's size knob is a samurai residence's, and no shrine keeper's plot size was found. It stands unfenced: the commons around the precinct were fodder ground, the grass cut and carried to the byres (research vegetation 'Did a village keep a meadow for fodder and green manure?'), not ground where stock ran loose (a guess from that finding). Checked by `garden_sun` (make pack-audit).
- The sheet draws the precinct's open ground as plain parchment, where the village map draws its scrub (grass tufts and shrub dots): a map drawing convention, as the sheet has always drawn the commons round it - the sheet matches the map in its trees, buildings and built features, not in the ground's scatter.
- The trees are laid once, by a seeded dart-throw inside the wood's outline, outside the clearing, the approach, the path, the well and the captions, and the same list is drawn on the map and the sheet; crowns are 13-20 ft across, true size. Crowns overlap one another into a closed canopy (the GM, 2026-09-27: "tree crowns may overlap"); no tree is drawn on top of another (`trees_overlap`).
- The building's size comes from the research, not the map (the GM, 2026-09-27: a dimension the GM did not give is presumed not a measurement); the village map's shrine glyph was redrawn to it, 33 by 16 px.

### Features

- **torii**: Seven arches at the 12 ft pitch, the innermost one pitch off the hall's face, the outermost at the precinct's edge where the approach enters the shrine's ground. A village shrine at average wealth would carry one; this one carries seven because its country monk is a stickler for the potent number seven and raised the extra six himself - the setting's particular, since no page read shows a village shrine with a row of arches before 1868.
- **shrine grove**: Here the wood stands behind the hall and down both its sides - from the well at its back edge round to ragged tips beside the forecourt, the lower approach open: on a slope a shrine's wood stands behind the hall, at its sides, or both, and this one's roll gave both. No fence and no wall: the arch and the wood mark the shrine's ground.
- **well**: The shrine's one well, its water point, stands 108 ft behind the hall at the wood's back edge, where the village map sites it, reached by the household's footpath through the wood from the kitchen side. Never under an arch. Its curb is drawn 5 ft square.
- **kitchen**: The kitchen is the earthen-floored west end of the one building that is both the villagers' hall and the monk's home - a farmhouse's doma with its hearth, its yard door on the west gable onto the kitchen yard.
- **genkan**: Not a formal entrance for guests of rank but the dwelling's own entry: a 4 ft earthen-floored vestibule inside the east door, beside the writing room.
- **latrine**: One privy, the monk's household's, at the west gable's back corner off the kitchen door, near the path to the well.
- **vegetable garden**: The monk's household bed, 14 by 23 ft, in the open ground below the forecourt, west of the approach - the one ground near the house its sun reaches: it gets 9.5 hours in the autumn shoulder month, where the yard by the kitchen door, between the hall and the wood, got 2.5 (research homesteads 044).
- **fire-water tubs**: One at each of the building's four corners, gutter-fed against the wall - the tubs of a wooden hall, as at the magistracy.
