# Design notes: Hoshigaoka Country Shrine

**Subject**: the shrine of Hoshigaoka village district - the seat of the district's country monk - Otsuki, of the Order of Bishamon, whose record is the Obsidian Portal character of that name - who lives here, keeps the district's registers, and performs the villagers' rites. Hoshigaoka is the reference village of the water-first family (`legacy-hand-authored-pool/villages/hoshigaoka/`), whose district names the GM dictated on 2026-08-29; on its village map the shrine stands at the water-mouth entrance inside its grove, and this sheet is that precinct at 3 px = 1 ft. The exemplar of the country-shrine program (feature 254): the generic program with no particulars beyond the dedication, which are the GM's to add.

**Program type**: Country shrine (a village district's shrine) - see `buildings/programs.md`.

**Form**: one roof

**Knob settings** (every knob, defaults included):

1. Hall-and-dwelling form: **one roof** (the default - the GM's form, 2026-09-19). The building is 76 by 36 ft (2,736 sq ft, inside the Kaie-ji band of 2,100-3,600), its two uses END TO END along the ridge as at Kaie-ji: the kitchen's earthen floor and hearth at the west end (with its own door on the north wall), the villagers' hall in the center, 40 ft wide, facing the approach with its raised step and its altar at the rear toward the sanctuary, and the monk's rooms at the east end with a 12 ft writing room for the district's registers and the dwelling's own door on the east wall. The hall room's 40 ft is a room of the one-roof building, whose band governs; the 18-38 ft band is a separate hall's.
2. Bell tower: **absent** (the default).
3. Dedication: **Bishamon**, the Fortune of Strength - the GM's word for this district (2026-09-20: Hoshigaoka's village shrine is Otsuki's shrine of Bishamon, and Inari, the rice Fortune, stays the program's default). The dedication is named on the sanctuary and nothing else of it is drawn: at average wealth the sheet carries no guardian figures, and the research pass on what a Bishamon dedication puts in a rural precinct is recorded under knob 3 in `buildings/programs.md`.
4. Grove and burial-ground side: the grove **west and north**, pressing inside the north corners of the fence around the sanctuary (the village map's water-mouth wood stands there), with a few trees flanking the burial ground on the east; the burial ground **east**, outside the fence on bare ground, 44 by 135 ft.
5. Wealth: **average** - thatch and plain timber, no lanterns, no guardian figures.

**Particulars**: the named monk. Otsuki registers the district's births and marriages, stamps its travel papers and teaches the village's sons and daughters their letters in the hall; his chief clerk Sadaji, the headman's second son, and two of the district's licensed ashigaru keep the records and the correspondence, so the writing room and its record chest are in daily use (his Obsidian Portal record). A relic, a second altar, a bell or a festival stage are still the GM's to add, and each goes in this file when it does.

**Deliberate choices and tolerated stretches**:

- The precinct is 110 by 135 ft - a GUESS bounded by what it must contain (the size-audit corroborated the order twice: the 1897 district-shrine floor of 500 tsubo and the one readable compound area, 2.5 mu; drawn 415 tsubo) (no Japanese precinct area could be read; the research page says so). It reads open because it is: swept gravel is the shrine's ground.
- The arch, the approach, the well and the fence carry no captions - each is declared by its `id` for the checks and reads for what it is on sight (the no-obvious-labels rule); the hall and the sanctuary carry the two labels the program finds them by. The captions that remain say what is instance-specific or not visible: the room uses, the dedication, the yard's name. The kitchen's earthen floor is drawn as earth, not captioned.
- The building is a uniform 76 by 36 ft bar. The one attested one-roof example steps in depth between its two ends (Kaie-ji, 8.0 m at one end and 13.0 m at the other); the bar is kept for legibility and is a simplification the GM may want to reaffirm or replace with a stepped plan (building-review, 2026-09-19).
- A plain break in the west fence beside the kitchen garden is the household's service way; firewood, the harvest of the tax-free plot and the dead on their way to the burial ground do not pass under the arch.
- The sanctuary is 6 by 6 ft, true size, on a plinth; the arch spans 15 ft outer, 10.7 ft clear over the 10 ft approach. Both are drawn at their real size, not as markers - and so is the purification basin, 5 ft square: the ~22 px well-curb marker the magistracies use (buildings.md, a GM-confirmed marker for a sub-glyph curb) would out-measure the sanctuary here, and at 3 px = 1 ft a basin needs no marker.
- The kitchen garden and the privy stand at the dwelling ends, the privy at the back corner, the garden beside the west end - the household bed and the attached privy of the program.

**Review log**:

- `size-audit` (2026-09-19, Opus): five findings, four applied - the burial ground grown from 33 x 73 ft to 44 x 135 ft (the record's own band for a district's dead is 0.12-0.38 acre; drawn 0.14); the writing room cut from 18 x 25 ft to a 12 ft corner room with the rest of the east end as storage (a small shrine had no office; the registers are a chest and a desk); the north fence brought to 10 ft behind the sanctuary and the wood brought inside the north corners, so no public gravel stands behind the deity's house; the arch's posts moved clear of the approach (10.7 ft clear over a 10 ft way). The fifth, the well glyph out-measuring the sanctuary, was a question about a standing marker exemption; resolved by drawing the basin true (above), which the exemption permits and does not require. Two judgment items it raised and left: the forecourt is unnamed (the swept keidai names itself under the no-obvious-labels rule; the audit's court-naming rule is the magistracy's) and pack_audit's coverage and hugging bands are the magistracy's and are not applied to this tier (they are declared per type and do not run here). Confirmed: the precinct's order, the sanctuary, the one-roof building against an independent sum of parts (1,750-2,405 sq ft), the hall room, the doma share, the privy, the garden, the tubs, the torii's proportions.
- `building-review` (2026-09-19, Opus): ten errors, all applied - the kitchen moved from a strip across the hall's rear (its hearth on the axis to the sanctuary) to the west end, hall and dwelling end to end along the ridge as at Kaie-ji; the dwelling's own doors (the kitchen door on the north wall, the entry on the east wall); the privy attached at the north-west corner; the scale bar moved off the title to the bottom-left; the four overrunning captions fixed or dropped (the generic "the villagers' hall, before the sanctuary" removed); the notes' grove sides and caption sentence corrected; the tubs weighted to the kitchen end (two on its north face); the rear strip's unnamed cells gone with the re-plan; the way made continuous under the arch. Questionables applied: an altar band and offering table in the hall; a service break in the west fence; "sanctuary of Inari" in the canon's name; the crop tightened (viewBox 140 60 600 620); the burial ground's markers grown with its area. Overruled with reasons recorded above: the uniform bar (legibility; a stepped plan is the attested form, for the GM to call), the hall room's 40 ft (the one-roof band governs, not a separate hall's), the forecourt unnamed (the swept keidai names itself). Nitpicks: the program table's column now says an item may be declared by id; the doma is drawn as earth; the well is reached by the east door.
