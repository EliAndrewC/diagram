# Design notes: Hoshigaoka Country Shrine

**Subject**: the shrine of Hoshigaoka village district - the seat of the district's country monk, who lives here, keeps the district's registers, and performs the villagers' rites. Hoshigaoka is the reference village of the water-first family (`legacy-hand-authored-pool/villages/hoshigaoka/`), whose district names the GM dictated on 2026-08-29; on its village map the shrine stands at the water-mouth entrance inside its grove, and this sheet is that precinct at 3 px = 1 ft. The exemplar of the country-shrine program (feature 254): the generic program with no particulars beyond the dedication, which are the GM's to add.

**Program type**: Country shrine (a village district's shrine) - see `buildings/programs.md`.

**Form**: one roof

**Knob settings** (every knob, defaults included):

1. Hall-and-dwelling form: **one roof** (the default - the GM's form, 2026-09-19). The building is 76 by 36 ft (2,736 sq ft, inside the Kaie-ji band of 2,100-3,600): the villagers' hall is the front room centered on the approach, 40 ft wide, with its raised step; the monk's rooms take the west end, the writing room with the district's registers the east end, and the kitchen with its earthen floor and hearth the rear strip.
2. Bell tower: **absent** (the default).
3. Dedication: **the rice Fortune** - the ordinary rural dedication, pending the GM's word on this district's; nothing else of the dedication is drawn.
4. Grove and burial-ground side: the grove **west and north** (the village map's water-mouth wood stands there), the burial ground **east**, outside the fence on bare ground.
5. Wealth: **average** - thatch and plain timber, no lanterns, no guardian figures.

**Particulars**: none yet. This is the generic instance; a relic, a second altar, a bell, a festival stage or a named monk are the GM's to add, and each goes in this file when it does.

**Deliberate choices and tolerated stretches**:

- The precinct is 110 by 150 ft - a GUESS bounded by what it must contain (no Japanese precinct area could be read; the research page says so). It reads open because it is: swept gravel is the shrine's ground.
- The arch, the approach, the well, the sanctuary, the hall and the fence carry no captions - each is declared by its `id` for the checks and reads for what it is on sight (the no-obvious-labels rule). The captions that remain say what is instance-specific or not visible: the room uses, the dedication, the yard's name.
- The sanctuary is 6 by 6 ft, true size, on a plinth; the arch spans 12 ft. Both are drawn at their real size, not as markers.
- The kitchen garden and the privy stand at the dwelling ends, the privy at the back corner, the garden beside the west end - the household bed and the attached privy of the program.
