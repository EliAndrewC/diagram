# The gate: what proves a map is right, now that nothing audits one

**Load this file when:** you are adding a rule about how a settlement is built, deciding where that
rule belongs, or wondering why a rule you expected to catch something did not.

**READ THIS FIRST IF YOU REMEMBER THE CHECK BATTERY.** Until feature 166 this file described a
1,371-segment post-placement validator (`check_village`) that every generated map was put through.
It is gone - 18,830 lines of it, plus 107 frozen bad manifests and five tools that existed to drive
or count it. Nothing here audits a finished map any more, and a session looking for `gate(M)`,
`make gate-manifest`, `make new-check` or `pool/regressions/` will not find them.

## Why it went, in the GM's own terms

> *"we have placement algorithms, which in theory should not be buggy, but then the automated checks
> essentially catch bugs as they slip through and then fix them on the maps ... if a check catches
> something, that just means that our placement algorithm is bugged."*

That was not true when the maps were drawn by hand: a person places a well by eye and a validator is
the only thing standing between a slip and the sheet. It became true the moment generation was
scripted. A check that fires on a generated map reports a defect in the code that placed the feature,
and the place to fix a defect in a placer is the placer - once - rather than to keep a standing audit
that re-discovers it on every map for ever.

The GM pushed the argument further than the session had, and was right to:

> *"I'd be really, really surprised if our win is actually only eleven checks ... can you describe to
> me a single category of automated check which should still exist?"*

No category survived the question. What replaced the battery is not a smaller battery.

## Where a rule goes now

Four destinations, and the choice is usually obvious once the question is asked in the right form:
**who guarantees this, and when?**

| the rule is about | it belongs in | example |
|---|---|---|
| a clearance, spacing or seat a placer decides | a UNIT TEST of that placer, on plain inputs | `tests/hamletgen/test_unreached_houses.py` |
| a property of a FINISHED map that no single placement owns | a SEED test in `tests/gate/`, on a cached roll | `tests/gate/test_paddy_fabric.py` |
| a fact about the CODE rather than about a map | a static test | the operations registry; `all_ink_is_ruled_on` |
| a feature no scripted generator produces | a RECORDED DROP, with its grounding kept | `pond_fed_from_edge`, and the urban vocabulary |

The 142 rules the battery held were migrated one at a time under feature 166, and
[`specs/166-retire-the-check-battery/migration-record.md`](../../../../specs/166-retire-the-check-battery/migration-record.md)
is the ledger: one row per rule, naming its destination and the mutation that proved the replacement
carries it. A drop names what was measured and why nothing on any live map can exercise the rule.

**MODE A IS DIFFERENT, AND DELIBERATELY SO** (GM 2026-08-30):

> *"for nonscripted diagrams such as the magistracy diagrams, there still are automated checks and
> those serve a valuable purpose because those maps are generated by hand rather than by a scripted
> process."*

A compound plan is placed by a person, so the argument above does not reach it: there is no placer to
fix. Mode A keeps its independent review - the `building-review` and `size-audit` subagents, run
before any plan ships - and its negative fixtures, the frozen bad SVGs in `tests/fixtures/`
(`ubame-privy-in-wall-red.svg` and its siblings), each a plan exhibiting one defect the review must
catch. Do not "tidy" those away on the strength of this document. When a building type gets a
generator, its rules move to that generator's tests the way the settlement rules moved here.

## The lessons that OUTLIVED the mechanism

These were learned inside the battery and are about verification itself, so they still bind - and
they bind harder now, because a placer test that judges nothing is quieter than a check that judges
nothing.

### A rule that never RUNS looks exactly like a rule that passes

The single most expensive shape in this repository's history. Three separate times in one feature
(2026-07-25, the water-flow work) the defect was not a bad map but a rule silently not running, with
everything green throughout. The form is always a rule gated on an optional declaration that almost
nothing declares: `meta(down_deg)` gated the whole drainage-slope block and the two provincial cities
declared none; the legacy `meta(downhill)` gated the flow rule and only 2 of 17 maps declared it.

It survives the migration intact, in two new forms:

- **A seed test whose subject list is empty passes.** So every test in `tests/gate/` asserts what it
  FOUND before it asserts the found thing is well formed - the fan exists, the flooded plots matched
  a ring, some vertex fell along a supply stroke's span. Feature 166 hit this twice while writing the
  replacements: `field_ditches_reach_source_and_sink` asserted against Inashiro would have passed on
  an empty list for ever, because only Kuwabata lays laterals.
- **A threshold calibrated against the generator rather than against the world goes blind together
  with it.** `dry_plot_furrows_vary` capped its adjacency radius at 50 px while the plots grew to
  81-87 ft sides, so it compared ZERO pairs on all four maps - and it went blind in the same moment
  and for the same reason as the generator feeding it, whose own radius was also a flat pixel figure.
  The two were calibrated against each other rather than against the plots.

**The ratchet.** When a rule needs a declaration to work, assert that the DECLARATION EXISTS.
`settlement_declares_a_land_fall` was the model and survives as
`test_the_map_declares_the_fall_its_drainage_is_judged_against`.

### An ABSOLUTE path in a test predicate is a guard that disables itself

`tests/hamletgen/test_surface.py`'s census skipped any file with `.clones` among its path parts -
which reads as "do not walk other sessions' clones" and tests the ABSOLUTE path. Every session works
inside `/diagram/.clones/<name>/`, so the condition was true for EVERY file, the census returned the
empty set, and its pin compared nothing. Inside a clone it saw **0 names; with the guard gone, 50** -
and it had been hiding two genuinely unpinned consumers introduced by the feature that added it.

Match on a path RELATIVE to the root being walked, or not on paths at all. The main-tree guards
(`settlement._assert_not_main_tree`, the Makefile's `guard`) are the legitimate exception: inspecting
the absolute path IS their job, and each is tested with synthetic paths rather than the one it happens
to be running under. And **a census must assert it FOUND something** - that is this whole section in
one line.

### The placer and its test must read the SAME source

A test that re-derives what the placer computed is testing its own arithmetic. Where the engine
already answers the question, IMPORT the answer rather than restating it - `floor_overhang`,
`pointed_ring`, `supply_bank_clearance`, `jog_vertices`, `cluster_aspect`, `unreached_houses`,
`surface_water_dist`, `matrix_policy`. Feature 166 found that three of the retired checks had already
reached this conclusion and said so in their own comments (*"imported from the engine and NOT
restated - the same call the carve makes"*), which is the clearest possible statement of what the
battery had become: the placer and the check asking one function the same question, twice.

Where a rule genuinely must be restated in a test, restate it EXACTLY. Feature 166's first draft of
the supply-bank test sampled ring CORNERS and fired on a bund past the centerline; the rule walks each
ring EDGE at a 3 px step and fires at the band plus the abutment. Both differences were the difference
between catching the defect and not.

### MEASURE WHAT THE RULE MEASURES

A gap verdict reads FOOTPRINTS, never centers (`edge_gap`, `within_edge_gap`, `sat_overlap`).
Classification, association-reach and prefilters may use centers, deliberately - say which family your
rule is in, in a comment, at the assertion. And never let an aggregate stand in for the distributed
thing a verdict is about: measure to the nearest member, or to the wall.

Units and sign conventions are measured, not guessed, and the measurement is recorded where it was
needed. Feature 166 got two wrong in one sitting: `theta` on a dry plot is in RADIANS (compared in
degrees, it called all 24 adjacent pairs identical and nearly reported a defect that is not there),
and a notice board's `rot` is PARALLEL to the way it faces, not perpendicular. Both corrections live
in the tests that needed them, because a sign convention guessed is a sign convention that will be
guessed again.

### A ROTATION-BLIND RULE PASSES AT ONE ORIENTATION AND FAILS AT ANOTHER

A whole family of defects here were correct measurements taken in the PAGE's frame instead of the
FEATURE's. A settlement rolls its own fall, so a rule asserted at one `down_deg` proves nothing about
the map that rolls another. Assert across several falls where the rule is about orientation
(`tests/waterfields/test_comb_flow.py`), and make rotation itself an assertion where the property is
supposed to be invariant (`cluster_aspect` under rotation).

### THE RECORD OF WHAT HAS FIRED BEATS THE DATAFLOW VERDICT

`make check-census` used to answer "does any stage after this check change an input it reads", and a
NO made the check a retirement candidate. It was never a ruling, because the census reads the manifest
and not the code, so it cannot see a placer that fails softly. That tool is retired with the battery,
but the reasoning generalizes to its successor: **before deciding a placer guarantees something,
grep the record for it catching that placer out.** `bridges_span_their_water` was kept on exactly
those grounds - `hamletgen/ways.py` records it catching the scripted placer four separate times on
oblique crossings.

### A DEFECT LEDGERED SOMEWHERE NOTHING READS IS A DEFECT LOST

`hamletgen.COHORT_BASELINE` pinned one expected cohort failure. Feature 166 rolled that seed and put
it through the battery one last time before deleting the pin: the verdict was EMPTY. The defect had
been fixed at some point and the pin went on excusing a seed that no longer needed it - which is
precisely the "STALE PIN ... Blocking" case `baseline_verdict` exists to catch, unnoticed because the
24-seed cohort runs only under FULL and the idle runs. Check a pin before you delete it, and record
what the check said; a migration that loses a defect on the way past looks exactly like one that fixed
it.

## Waivers are gone, and the doctrine they carried is not

A map could break one rule in writing: `s.meta(waivers={...})` with 60+ characters of real reason, and
two meta-checks (`waivers_are_documented`, `waivers_are_live`) that stopped the hatch rotting - a
waiver had to carry an actual reason and had to name a rule that really failed on that map. Neither
could itself be waived, *"or the hatch would swallow its own guard"*.

There is no gate to be exempt from, so there are no waivers. What survives is the rule they enforced,
which is a project rule rather than a mechanism: **an exception is written down with its reason and
its cost, or it is not an exception.** That is the root CLAUDE.md's "record a decision to ACCEPT a
limitation" clause, and the worked example it points at
([`settlements/water.md`](../settlements/water.md), "THE TAPER IS SUB-PERCEPTUAL AT TRUE SCALE") is
unaffected by any of this.

## What `make done` means now

The same thing it always meant to a session - the merge gate - with a different interior. It runs
lint, the type check, the unit tree, the `tests/gate/` tree (real rolls of representative specs,
served from the roll cache while nothing they execute has changed) and the coverage floors. What it
no longer does is put every map through a validator, and the pool sweep in `tests/full/` no longer
gates the manifests it produces: it proves every shipped generator RUNS, inside its time budget, and
emits a manifest - which was always the half the battery did not cover.
