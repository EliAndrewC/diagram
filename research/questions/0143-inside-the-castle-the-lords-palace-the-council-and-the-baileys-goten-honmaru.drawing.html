How our maps draw the inside of the castle: the lord's palace, the council and the baileys (goten, honmaru)

A capital's map draws its castle as walls, a moat and gates around an empty interior (How our maps draw the castle in a capital). What stood inside is drawn only on the castle's own plan, a building plan of the castle by itself, so that the two never disagree. Every feature of a capital therefore stands either inside the castle, and off the city's map, or outside it, and drawn in the city. The table sorts them by what a domain's castle is recorded to have held, and the first bullet names the four entries that are our guess.

  • Four things in the inside column are a GUESS: the treasury, the guard's barracks, the wells and the gardens.
    No page we read names them among a castle's contents. None of them is drawn, so the guess costs the map nothing.
  • The domain's rice has two granaries: the siege stock, inside the undrawn castle, and the tribute granary, drawn at the wharf.
    A castle kept reserve rice inside its walls against war and famine, and a domain collected its tribute rice at a granary of its own, which at Kumamoto stood on the water. So the siege stock is left inside the castle, and the tribute granary stands at the wharf as a feature of its own. That the domain pays its retainers' stipends (the rice a lord paid their samurai as salary) from the wharf granary is this project's reading.
  • A capital draws no House Chancellery compound.
    A domain's senior retainers worked in a room of the domain's office, in the castle, so the council meets in a room of the undrawn palace. A deliberative body of five to ten lineage representatives makes no street traffic and keeps no archive of its own: it is a chamber, not a compound, and a chancellery compound drawn anywhere in the city is an error.
  • The domain's six ministries stand outside the castle, on the grand avenue to its main gate (ote-suji).
    A Japanese domain kept its offices inside its castle, in the lord's palace, which at Kakegawa stood in the second bailey. Our maps put the ministries outside, as China's Six Ministries stood outside the palace, for the reasons at How our maps draw the offices of the six ministries (liubu).
  • The lineage compounds stay outside the castle, though great retainers' estates stood inside some.
    At Fukuoka they stood in the castle's third bailey through the Edo period, but a compound behind the castle's wall would not be drawn, and its labeled lineage would vanish from the map. For now this project keeps every lineage's compound in the town, where it can be drawn and labeled: How our maps house a capital's samurai
  • How large a palace would the castle's own plan hold?
    One the size of a mid-sized domain's: seven buildings of about twenty rooms, as at Kakegawa, holding the domain's ceremonies, the lord's residence and the domain's offices, in whichever bailey the plan puts it.
