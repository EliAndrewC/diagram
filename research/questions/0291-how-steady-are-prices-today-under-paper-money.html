How steady are prices today, under paper money?

Rokugan's prices are set beside what players know from their own shopping, where prices have drifted upward since 1971. The findings below give, for the United States and Japan since the 1970s, how far prices move under paper money from one day to the next decade, as a yardstick for the premodern figures.

Since 1971 no major currency has been tied to gold or silver, and prices have risen in nearly every decade since. The official price indexes of the United States and Japan show how much: a steady drift upward of a few percent a year, with small swings around it, while single staples still swing several times more than the index. The figures below are this project's arithmetic on the agencies' series, measured as the Edo and Qing series are in How steady were prices in Edo Japan and Qing China, from one day to the next century?, as a standard deviation of log changes.

The drift: a few percent a year, in nearly every decade

  • The United States: prices up 7.7 times since 1971.
    The consumer price index stood at 40.8 in August 1971 and 315.6 in December 2024, a rise of 3.9% a year or 47% a decade; it rose 2.17 times in the 1970s, 1.55 in the 1980s, 1.30 in the 1990s, 1.26 in the 2000s and 1.19 in the 2010s.
  • Japan: prices up 3.3 times since 1971, nearly flat since 1990.
    Japan's index went from 32.9 in 1971 to 108.5 in 2024, 2.3% a year or 25% a decade, rising 2.37 times in the 1970s but falling slightly in the 2000s.
  • Under commodity money prices rose about 1% a year, under paper money about 13%.
    Across 15 countries, inflation averaged 1.0% a year under commodity standards, weighted by the length of each episode, and 13% under paper money, leaving out the episode of the German hyperinflation.

The swing: small for the price level, larger for a staple

  • The price level: well under 1% a month and about 1-4% a year.
    The US index moved by 0.38% from month to month and 2.8% over twelve months in 1972-2024, and by 0.31% and 1.3% in the calm years 1984-2019; Japan's moved by 0.58% and 3.9% in 1971-2024, and 0.33% and 1.0% in 1995-2019.
  • A staple: 2-7% a month and 9-21% a year.
    The US average shop price of rice moved by 2.2% from month to month and 9.3% over twelve months in 1981-2024, flour by 3.3% and 9.1%, eggs by 6.9% and 21%; rice's largest twelve-month rise was 56%, and eggs' 150%. Japan's index of cereal prices moved by 1.1% a month and 5.4% over twelve months in 1972-2023, then rose from 110.4 in June 2023 to 152.4 in December 2025.
  • Day to day: about 2% for a traded staple; a shop price within a day, SILENT.
    Rough rice futures in Chicago moved by 1.7% from one trading day to the next and 4.1% over five trading days in 2016-2026. No series read gives a shop price within a day.

Beside the premodern figures

  • Today's drift is far larger; today's swing of a staple is not.
    The US price level drifts by 47% a decade, where Mitsui's rice in Kyoto and Osaka drifted by less than 1% a decade before 1859; a day's move in Chicago rice futures, 1.7%, is close to a day's move in the Osaka rice market, 2.0%; and US rice moves by 9% a year, where Mitsui's rice moved by 15-17%.
