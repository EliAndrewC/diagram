How our maps draw kitchens (daidokoro)

On our building plans a residence's kitchen is board-floored, with a small earth-floored doma at its edge, as a samurai house's kitchen was, and it cooks on a kamado. The family comes and goes by the residence's own inner entrance, and the kitchen has one door to the outside, a GUESS both in number and in place. The kitchen's size is at How our maps lay out samurai residences and their rooms (buke yashiki).

  • Our plans give a residence's kitchen a kamado, never a sunken hearth (irori).
    A samurai's kitchen cooked on a kamado. The sunken hearth belongs to the commoner's house, and no page we read puts one in a samurai kitchen, so drawing one there would be a GUESS, and our plans do not.
  • Where the kamado stands is a knob between two forms.
    It stands on the small doma at the kitchen's edge, the rule for houses of the Edo period, or, as a GUESS, on the kitchen's board floor as a movable kamado on legs. Each plan takes one.
  • Did samurai kitchens set the kamado on the board floor?
    A legged kamado stands on the boards in a commoner's tenement kitchen of the Edo period, as a museum reproduces it. In a samurai house it rests on a house-builder's history and a museum's reproduction, both modern, and no earlier page we could read puts it there, so a plan that sets it on the boards draws a GUESS by analogy.
  • The kitchen has one door to the outside, on its doma - a GUESS.
    The kitchen door is recorded, but no page we read places it on the doma or shows a kitchen with two, so a plan that draws a second one draws a GUESS too. On the magistrate's compound our maps generate, it opens west, onto the way to the kitchen's yard.
  • A route by which food reached the rooms is a GUESS wherever a plan draws one.
    No page we read draws one.
  • The family's everyday door is the residence's inner entrance, not the kitchen's.
    The inner entrance was made for the family and those of the house to come and go by. On the generated compound it opens on the residence's face toward the kitchen.
  • Our plans draw the kitchen door but do not say who came in by it.
    That those of lower status came in by an earth-floored doma rather than the entrance is found only on the modern plan of a fictional house, and tradesmen at the kitchen door only of houses today. No page from before 1868 we could read says who used it.
