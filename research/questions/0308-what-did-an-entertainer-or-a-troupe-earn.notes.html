• kotobank-senryo-yakusha - the World Encyclopedia, by Igusa Toshio: 「In the Edo period each actor became attached to a theater by a one-year contract from the 11th month to the 10th month of the next year, and one whose pay reached the high sum of 1000 ryo was called a thousand-ryo actor. The first are said to have been Yoshizawa Ayame I and Ichikawa Danjuro II, in the Shotoku and Kyoho eras (1711-36).」 (translated; )
  • japanknowledge-yakusha - 「An actor's pay was set as a yearly wage in the Edo period, and since the benchmark of the highest sum was a thousand ryo, the name "thousand-ryo actor" also arose. In fact there were also actors who received a yearly wage of more than a thousand ryo. In the Kansei era (1789-1801) the managers, who could not bear the excessive rise in yearly wages, petitioned the shogunate, and it even came to the point that the highest sum was held to five hundred ryo by order. Even so, pay was topped up under such names as extra-role fees and night money, several actors in fact took seven hundred or eight hundred ryo, and before long it returned to what it had been.」 (translated; )
