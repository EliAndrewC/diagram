How our maps size a farmhouse's ground in a clustered village

Our maps deal a clustered settlement's houses over a square of ground about 104 ft a side for each household, about 10,800 sq ft or some 305 tsubo, for its house, yard, outbuildings and the room round them. Seated, a household is spread over about 162 ft a side, some 735 tsubo, which also holds its stand of wood. Both are map drawing conventions: they are the room the maps need to seat their houses, not the size of a lot, and the record's typical lot is smaller.

  • A homestead's ground stands above the typical lot.
    About 305 tsubo is roughly twice the middle lot of the 1698 Musashi register, 157 tsubo, and above the 90-180 tsubo the record's typical lots run. It stands at the floor of the rare large lots: three of that register's 34 lots are larger, and the surveyed Manchu village has only a few lots of 1,000 sq m (about 300 tsubo) or more. That is this convention's cost: a map's homesteads stand on more ground than most real ones. The real size: a typical lot of about 90-180 tsubo, about 3,200-6,500 sq ft.
  • The seating ground does not compare with a lot directly.
    It holds the household's wood, at least 6,000 sq ft, which a register's house lot need not hold. Even so, at about 735 tsubo it is larger than every lot the record measures.
