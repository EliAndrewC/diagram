How a leader line ties a caption to its feature

A caption that cannot stand near its feature is still tied to it: a thin line runs from the words to the thing they name. Where a caption sits, and how far it may move before it needs one, is at Labels on maps (cartographic label placement).

  • A caption more than twice the usual gap from its feature is tied to it by a thin leader line.
    It is the device mapmakers use for a caption displaced from the thing it names. A map drawing convention: it ends at the nearest drawn point - a disc's curve, a stroke's edge, a scattered part - not a box that may hold bare ground; no source we read fixes this.
  • Where two drawn parts stand exactly as near, the leader goes to a closed outline before a stroke, and to the wider of two strokes.
    A map drawing convention: the nearest-point rule leaves that choice open, so the order is fixed; between two like parts equally near, the one drawn first is taken.
  • A leader ends on the ink as it is drawn: on a corner's point, on a line's end, and never in a dashed line's gap.
    A map drawing convention: a stroked line or outline is measured as the sheet draws it - its corners drawn square to their point, or cut off where they are very sharp, or rounded where the sheet rounds them; its ends flat, squared off or round as drawn; a curve as its curve; and a dashed line as its dashes alone, since a gap holds no ink.
