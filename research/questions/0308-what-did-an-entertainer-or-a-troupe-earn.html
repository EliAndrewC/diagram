What did an entertainer or a troupe earn?

A star of the city kabuki theaters was paid by the year, under a contract that ran from the eleventh month, and the top of the scale was a thousand ryo, first reached, it is said, in 1711-1736. In the fishing villages of Shima, a touring puppet company of eighteen or twenty was hired for the village festival in the 1850s and 1860s, and the village paid it from about two ryo to four or more a visit, its board and lodging included. In Qing China a luantan troupe of the 1700s was eight or ten players. What a troupe took in a year, how it shared it, what a street performer took in a day and what an instrument cost were not found.

Actors of the city theaters

  • What did a star earn?
    Up to a thousand ryo a year, and some more. An actor's pay was set by the year, and the top of the scale was 1,000 ryo, hence the "thousand-ryo actor"; some were paid more. Each actor was bound to one theater for a year from the eleventh month, and the first said to reach a thousand ryo were Yoshizawa Ayame I and Ichikawa Danjuro II, in 1711-1736.
  • Was the star's pay ever capped?
    Yes, in the Kansei era (1789-1801), though not for long. The theater managers, unable to bear the rising pay, asked the shogunate to cap it, and it was held to 500 ryo; with extra payments under other names several actors still took 700 or 800 ryo, and the cap soon lapsed.
  • What did a whole city company take and spend?
    By a playwright's book of the Kansei era, as a modern essay relays it, each of Edo's three theaters took about 8,000 ryo a year; the essay adds that it is said to have spent about 6,000 on its actors' pay and about 1,000 on making props, costumes and the like; a company had about sixty actors. Its essayist reckons that after a star's thousand an ordinary actor was left 25 to 50 ryo a year, his own arithmetic rather than a recorded wage.
  • Did a star keep all his pay?
    No, by the same essay: a star supplied his own costumes, and looked after the backstage hands, the dressers, wig-makers and stagehands, whom the theater did not pay.
  • Where did actors stand?
    Below the four classes of warrior, farmer, artisan and merchant: they were called "riverbed people" (kawaramono) and "riverbed beggars", while the public adored the stars.
  • How large were the city companies?
    The kabuki troupes recorded at one provincial market town averaged 43 players and a puppet theater's 26 (What did a play or a show cost to see?).

A touring company in the villages

The fishing villages of Shima held their festivals as a whole village and paid for their festival plays out of the village purse, and one village's account book records the payments.

  • What did a village pay a hired troupe?
    In 1857 Ura village paid the Kamiya Shichidayu puppet company 2 ryo 3 bu of gold and 12.16 monme of silver for three days, the 11th to the 13th, lunches, sundries and flower money included. In 1867 it paid the Yamamoto Zendayu company 1 ryo 1 bu of flower money and a further sum for two nights' costs of eighteen people; its historian puts the village's outlay for the company's hire, lodging and the rest at about 4 ryo or more.
  • What did that money buy?
    Flower money, 花代 (hanadai, "flower money", a gift of money to the performers besides their fee), and the players' keep. In 1860 the village paid the Shichidayu company 3 bu of flower money, 1 ryo 1 bu and 12.64 monme for the sundries of twenty people for four nights, and 18 monme to hire bedding. Its historian counts these as paid besides the performance fee: flower money, meals for twenty players, four nights' lodging and the bedding.
  • How often?
    Twice a year. In 1855 the village paid the Zendayu company 2 ryo and 5.73 monme for a parting gift and two nights' lodging, and the Shichidayu company 1 ryo 3 bu and 12.35 monme for the same and three days' costs; the village hired the two companies in turn, twice a year, and paid out a large sum, more than three ryo. How often troupes of every kind played, in the cities and the countryside, is at How often did players perform?.
  • How large was a touring company?
    Eighteen to twenty. A statement of 1832 in the Kawachi village papers gives the Kamiya Shichidayu company as eighteen strong, and its historian finds it hired each year for the festival of the third or fourth month; the Zendayu company of 1867 was eighteen, and Ura fed twenty players in 1860. These are puppet companies.
  • What did a company take in a year, and how was it shared?
    Neither was found: the village books record what one village paid out, not a company's year, and no troupe's sharing of its money was found. A provincial market town's takings from kabuki runs of 1690-1737, a run at a time, are in What did a play or a show cost to see?.

Qing China

  • How large was a troupe?
    Eight or ten, for the luantan plays that spread in the Qianlong era (1736-1795): eight or ten people gathered, beat gongs and drums and sang. A flower-drum act in Shanghai in 1862-1908 was three or four: a man on the gong, a woman on a two-headed drum, with fiddle, flute and clappers.
  • Could a magistrate hire a troupe?
    Yes, for a festival. An edict of 1799 says that for a local official now and then to hire an outside troupe at a festival was never forbidden, but to keep one in his office was extravagance. What a village festival troupe of Kaifeng cost is in What did a play or a show cost to see?.
  • What did a famous actor get at court?
    A famous actor summoned to play at the inner court or the Summer Palace was given 20 taels of silver each time, and the likes of Tan Xinpei a stipend of 20 shi of rice a year besides.

Costumes and instruments

  • What did a costume cost?
    Only the extremes were found. Edo's stars paid for their own costumes, and a theater spent about a thousand ryo a year on props and costumes together. In China, a new play's costumes and properties were once made specially at great cost; the staging of The Peach Blossom Fan by the merchants of the Huai salt district cost 160,000 (the passage's 金, "gold", in Qing usage money reckoned in taels of silver). In 1862 Ura and a neighboring village shared the gift of an Okina costume (for the ritual old-man dance) to the Zendayu company, but the sum is lost to water damage. No price of an ordinary costume was found; what cloth cost is in What did cloth and clothing cost?.
  • What did an instrument cost?
    No price of a shamisen, a bamboo flute or a hand drum was found, in Japan or China.
