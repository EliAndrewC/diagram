How our maps mark temple fields (jochi)

A village map may mark the country monk's tax-free plot, or leave it unmarked; a map that marks none is correct and complete. Where it marks the plot, it tints it vermilion and scatters it among the common fields as the setting's headman scatters every household's strips. No page we read says where a real village temple's fields lay or how large they were, so the scatter follows the setting's allotment, not an attested temple holding.

  • A map that marks the monk's plot draws it as two or three pieces.
    The setting gives the monk a single plot, which the map draws as two or three separate strips, the way every household's holding is split. It marks no more, because a larger holding would say something about the temple that the setting does not. The count is this project's choice, a GUESS.
  • The marked plots hold about two households' worth of land, all told.
    The setting's notes give the plot no size, and neither they nor the sources read support a figure; the bound is this project's choice, a GUESS.
  • The plots are tinted vermilion and outlined.
    Vermilion stands out against the greens of the fields, and this project chose it to read as the color of the village shrine the monk keeps.
  • A thin gold outline was tried first and declined.
    On a rendered comparison it blended into the tan bunds and was hard to pick out at map view.
