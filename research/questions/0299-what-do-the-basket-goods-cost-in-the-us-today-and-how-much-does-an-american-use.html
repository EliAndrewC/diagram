What do the standard basket's goods cost in the US today, and how much of each does an American use?

Rokugan's money rules tell players what a koku would be worth today by pricing a laborer's year of goods then and now. The findings below give, for each good of that year, the plainest product a US shopper buys today and what it costs, the price a US farmer is paid for the raw crop, and how much of each good an American uses in a year.

The basket is the one in What did a Kyoto laborer's year of goods cost?: rice, soybeans, other grains and beans, fish, oil, cloth, salt, fuel and a room. Its goods are priced here in two ways: a national retailer's plain household pack, its own brand where it has one, seen online on October 25, 2026, and the price farmers received for the raw crop in August 2026, which is a first sale off the farm, not a wholesale price. Every price per kilogram is this project's arithmetic. None of them sets a koku's value or a Rokugani price, and they stand apart from the money rules' own list of today's prices, which they do not replace. The room is in What does a square meter of housing cost a year in the US today?

Each good's modern product and price

  • Rice: $1.28 a kg (~58¢ a lb) for long grain, $1.65 (~75¢ a lb) for medium, in a 20 lb sack.
    A retailer's own-brand 20 lb sack of long-grain enriched rice was $11.57 and of medium-grain $14.97. The government's average shop price of long-grain rice was $1.008 a lb ($2.22 a kg) in January 2025, its last month in the copy of the series read here, about 1.7 times the sack's; how far that price moves is in How steady are prices today, under paper money? Farmers received $10.70 a hundredweight for long-grain rough rice and $18.90 for medium and short grain, $0.24 and $0.42 a kg; at the milling rate of 2010, 68.86%, that is $0.34 and $0.61 a kg of milled rice, if the hulls and bran are given no value.
  • Dried beans: $1.65 a kg (~75¢ a lb) in a 20 lb sack.
    The own-brand 20 lb sack of pinto beans was $14.98; farmers received $33.10 a hundredweight for dry edible beans, $0.73 a kg.
  • Soybeans: $5.51 a kg (~$2.50 a lb) in a 10 lb bag, $0.44 a kg at the farm.
    No own-brand pack of dried soybeans was listed; a 10 lb bag of US-grown soybeans was $24.98, and a 4 lb bag $14.99, or $8.26 a kg. Farmers received $12.10 a bushel for soybeans; a bushel of soybeans is 60 lb by the government's standard weights, which makes it $0.44 a kg.
  • Wheat: flour $0.95-1.36 a kg (~43-62¢ a lb), the grain $0.23 a kg at the farm.
    The own-brand 25 lb bag of all-purpose flour was $10.82 and the 5 lb bag of whole wheat flour $3.08, $0.95 and $1.36 a kg, while a 5 lb bag of all-purpose flour was on sale at $1.97, down from $2.38, or $0.87 a kg; the government's average shop price of all-purpose flour was $0.561 a lb ($1.24 a kg) in January 2025. Farmers received $6.23 a bushel for wheat, a bushel of wheat being 60 lb, or $0.23 a kg.
  • Barley and millet: pearled barley $4.03 a kg (~$1.83 a lb), hulled millet $2.42 (~$1.10 a lb), barley $0.25 a kg at the farm.
    A three-pack of 16 oz pearled barley was $5.49 and 10 lb of hulled millet $10.99, neither of them an own-brand pack. Farmers received $5.55 a bushel for barley; the government's food accounts count a bushel of barley as 48 lb, which makes it $0.25 a kg.
  • Small fish: canned sardines $13.36 a kg (~$6.06 a lb), dried anchovies $52.69 (~$23.90 a lb).
    The own-brand 3.75 oz can of sardines in water was $1.42, the listing giving no weight for the fish without its water; 1 lb of dried anchovies for soup stock was $23.90.
  • Oil: canola oil $2.56 a liter (~$9.68 a gallon), the seed $0.57 a kg at the farm.
    The own-brand gallon bottle of canola oil was $9.68; farmers received $25.70 a hundredweight for canola seed.
  • Cotton cloth: unbleached muslin $2.56 a m², upland cotton $1.47 a kg at the farm.
    A precut 2 yard length of unbleached cotton muslin 120 in wide was $14.27, which is $2.56 a m²; the same length 45 in wide was also $14.27, or $6.83 a m². Farmers received $0.667 a lb for upland cotton, its seed priced apart; no commodity price of plain cotton cloth was found.
  • Salt: $1.28 a kg (~58¢ a lb) plain, pickling salt $1.48 a kg (~67¢ a lb).
    The own-brand iodized salt was $0.94 for 26 oz; a four-pack of 4 lb packs of canning and pickling salt, 16 lb in all, was $10.72, 4.2¢ an oz, and a 48 oz bag of another brand was on sale at $3.22, down from $13.71, or $2.37 a kg on sale and $10.08 at the higher price.
  • Charcoal: $1.37 a kg (~62¢ a lb) in briquettes.
    A two-pack of 16 lb bags of charcoal briquettes, 32 lb in all, was $19.92, 62.3¢ a lb.
  • Firewood: $10-25 a million Btu, ATTESTED ONLY FOR one state's news report.
    A cord of wood holds 20 million Btu by the energy agency's round figure, and a Vermont news report in October 2026 put a cord for a wood stove at $200 to $500, with the average household burning about two to three cords; that is $10-25 a million Btu.

What an American uses of each in a year

  • Wheat flour: about 121 lb (55 kg) a person in 2022.
    That is white and whole wheat flour; with durum flour, for pasta, it is about 133 lb (60 kg).
  • Other grains: corn 36 lb (16 kg), oats 5 lb (2 kg), barley under 1 lb.
    Corn products came to 35.5 lb and oat products to 4.8 lb a person in 2022; barley products to 0.73 lb (0.33 kg) in 2020, their last year before the series was dropped.
  • Rice: about 20 lb (9.3 kg) of milled rice a person in 2010, the last year counted.
    The government stopped the series after 2010 because the rice its accounts implied was used in the US fell by a large amount it could not explain.
  • Dry beans: about 6.8 lb (3.1 kg) a person in 2022.
    Dry edible beans, weighed as they leave the farm.
  • Fish and shellfish: about 16 lb (7.3 kg) of edible meat a person in 2018, the last year counted.
    Canned sardines were 0.23 lb (0.10 kg) of it. The fisheries agency puts the seafood an American ate in 2023 at 19.1 lb (8.7 kg), a preliminary figure, without saying on its page whether that is edible meat or the whole fish.
  • Cooking oil: about 54 lb (24 kg) a person in 2010, the last year counted.
    Salad and cooking oils, weighed as sold; the series ends in 2010 because the Census Bureau ended the industry reports it rested on.
  • Salt: about 3.2 kg (~7 lb) a person a year, counted from sodium.
    Americans take in about 3,400 mg of sodium a day, over 70% of it from packaged and prepared food rather than salt added at home; as salt that is 8.6 g a day. It counts every milligram of sodium as salt, so it is at most the salt eaten, and it is not the salt a household buys.
  • Heating fuel: about 33 million Btu a household in 2020, wood not counted.
    US homes used 4,026 trillion Btu for space heating, 34.4 million Btu for each home that heats with those fuels; the table leaves out wood. Spread over all 123.53 million homes it is 32.6 million Btu.
  • Wood, apart: 31 million Btu a wood-burning home, 85 million where wood is the main heat.
    11.1 million homes burned wood in 2020, 31.2 million Btu each on average; the 2.3 million heating mainly with wood burned 85 million Btu each, about 4 cords of wood.
  • Cooking fuel: about 1-2 million Btu a household.
    A home cooking with natural gas used 2.02 million Btu for it in 2020, with propane 2.09 and with electricity 0.99.
  • Floor area: a median of 725 sq ft (67 m²) a person in 2023.
    The mean was 943 sq ft (88 m²) a person, across all occupied homes.
  • Soybeans eaten as food, and cloth: SILENT.
    No figure was found for the soybeans an American eats as food, or for the cloth an American uses in a year.
