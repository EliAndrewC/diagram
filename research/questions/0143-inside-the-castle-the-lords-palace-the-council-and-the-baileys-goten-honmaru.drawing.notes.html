  • jomai-jawiki - 「later it meant the rice stockpiled by the shogunate and the fudai domains and others as military provisions.」 (translated; ) and 「under the system of Jokyo 2, 442,000 koku of castle rice was stockpiled across the country, and it was to be stockpiled dispersed among 68 places in all, such as the castles held directly by the shogunate - Edo, Osaka, Nijo and Sunpu castles - the castles of the fudai domains, and post stations on the Tokaido」 (translated; ); bunka-kawashiri-komegura - 「The Kumamoto domain's Kawashiri rice-granary site is the site of the granary that in the Edo period was the collection point for the Kumamoto domain's tribute rice, and it also served as a naval port.」 (translated; ); kotobank-horokusei - 「A system under which retainers were paid kuramai (stipend rice) by their lord」 (translated; ) (the siege stock in the castle and the tribute granary at the port are two attested institutions; that stipends were paid from the port granary in particular is this page's reading)
  • goyobeya-kotobank - 「The room inside Edo Castle where the tairo, roju and wakadoshiyori were in attendance and conducted the business of government.」 (translated; ) and 「Note also that in the domain office (hancho) of each domain and in their Edo mansions as well, the room where the karo carried out their duties was called the goyobeya.」 (translated; )
  • kakegawajo-goten - 「at first a palace was also built on the site of the honmaru, but on the occasion of its collapse in the great earthquake of Hoei 4 (1707) it was moved to the second bailey」 (translated; ), 「the three functions of "the place of the domain's official ceremonies", "the castle lord's official residence" and "the offices that ran the domain's government"」 (translated; ), 「made up of seven buildings ... about twenty rooms」 (translated; ) and 「the castle lord and the karo from the shikidai entrance, other samurai from the east side of the entrance, the ashigaru from the earthen floor on the north side」 (translated; )
  • fukuokajyo-sannomaru - 「Though the layout of the estates changed over time, great retainers' estates and the like stood there from the early Edo period to the end of the shogunate.」 (translated; ) (the page says this of the eastern part of Fukuoka's third bailey; it says the western part first held middle and lower retainers' houses)
  • homemate-sannomaru - 「The third bailey held residences where senior retainers lived, places for keeping horses, a privy for the castle's lord, storehouses and the like.」 (translated; )
