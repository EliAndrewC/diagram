How our maps lay out a town's farmsteads

A town's farmhouses stand close together, as a nucleated hamlet's do, and use the same farmstead a village's farms do: the house, a threshing yard strictly to its south, a garden set where the sun reaches it, and a storehouse plot kept to its north. They stand outside the town's wall, ringing its paddies at about a village's density.

  • Each farmstead is kept off the fields by all the ground it covers, not by its center.
    A farmstead must not stand in a crop. Testing only the centers of the house and the field lets a 44 ft farmhouse through with a corner already in the crop, an error of some 14 ft toward the field. So the whole farmstead (house, yard, garden and storehouse plot) is tested against the whole of each field.
  • A town's shelter is one shared windbreak on its windward side.
    A windbreak planted for a whole settlement or a whole district of fields is attested well before modern times: the Ryukyu kingdom's village windbreaks, Tarama's made in 1742, and the Tsugaru domain's planting of 1682 on the side the sea wind came from. Both forms are recorded: Tarama's windbreak rings its settlement, while Tsugaru's stood on the windward side alone. Our maps draw only the windward form, the one the record finds usual for a shelter belt (Shelter belts on a village's windward side (bofurin)); that is this project's choice.
  • Our maps give a town's farms no groves of their own.
    This is a GUESS: no page we read says whether the farms inside a village sheltered by a shared windbreak also kept groves of their own, and it is the same rule our maps give a nucleated hamlet. A leafy scatter of copse trees stands among the farmsteads instead.
  • The farmsteads stand outside the wall, around the paddies.
    A farmer builds close to the ground they work, and our maps keep a town's fields outside its wall. That is a deliberate departure from the history: the Chinese walled city counted farmland among the ordinary uses of the ground inside it, and Suzhou's wall still enclosed farmland in the twentieth century.
