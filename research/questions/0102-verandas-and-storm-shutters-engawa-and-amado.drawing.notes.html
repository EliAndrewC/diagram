• engawa-kotobank - 「In the shoin-style house too that tradition remained: a wide veranda was set between the main room and the garden and used as a secondary space of the main room.」 (translated; ) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Suzuki Mitsuru)
  • irikawa-kotobank - 「Corridors are hardly seen in the farmhouses and townhouses of the Edo period, or in the houses of middle and lower samurai, but at the end of the Edo period there appear houses with a continuous engawa set along two or three sides, used as a passage to the privy and the bath, which comes to have a function like a corridor.」 (translated; ) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, old edition, from its article on the corridor)
  • shoinzukuri-kotobank - 「In the Keicho and Genna years (1596-1624) at the start of the early modern period, the residences of the warrior daimyo were built in every region. Their great groups of halls were divided broadly into the front and the interior, and the main buildings - the hiroma, the taimenjo and the goza-no-ma of the front, and the oue (the lady's hall) of the interior - were all of the developed shoin-zukuri form.」 (translated; ) and 「Their rooms took as the upper seat a zashiki fitted with the zashiki decorations of tokonoma, shelves, tsuke-shoin and nando-gamae (often built as a jodan-no-ma), with lower seats such as the ni-no-ma and san-no-ma and a nando attached, and were accompanied on all four sides by hiroen and irikawa-en.」 (translated; ) (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, Kawakami Mitsugu, of the daimyo mansions of the Keicho and Genna years)
  • irikawa-kotobank - 「A passage one ken (about 1.8 meters) wide between the zashiki and the engawa.」 (translated; ) (Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten)
  • katsura-rikyu-jawiki - 「To the east of the ichi-no-ma and ni-no-ma there is a hiroen one ken wide. The veranda continues at a right angle along the south of the ichi-no-ma too, but on the south side its width becomes half a ken.」 (translated; ) (of the Ko-shoin)
