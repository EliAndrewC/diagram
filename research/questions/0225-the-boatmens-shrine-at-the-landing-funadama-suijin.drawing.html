How our maps draw the boatmen's shrine at the landing (funadama)

At a landing, as at Hayakawa, our maps draw a small shrine at the head of the landing: the boat's guardian spirit given a shrine on land, as it sometimes was.

  • Our maps draw no boatmen's water-god shrine.
    River boatmen are also recorded keeping a shrine to a water god (suijin). Our maps draw nothing attested only in modern practice, and the boatmen's water-god shrine is known only from a twentieth-century record of custom, with nothing read placing boatmen keeping one before 1868.
  • Putting the shrine at the head of the landing, and making it small, is our own guess.
    A small shrine, about 3 by 5 ft, at the head of the landing is a GUESS: the boat's guardian's shrine on land is not recorded standing at a landing itself, nor with a size.
  • The shrine is drawn vermilion, with a dark red outline, the color our maps give every small shrine.
    That is a map drawing convention, not the shrine's real color: why our maps mark a small shrine in vermilion is at How our maps place shrines, temples and graves in each size of settlement, and the pages we read on the boat's guardian give its shrine on land no color.
