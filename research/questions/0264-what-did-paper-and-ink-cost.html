What did paper and ink cost?

Rokugan's magistrates post their notices and its samurai write letters and poems, and the setting judges a price by how many days of a laborer's work it would take. The findings below give what the historical record holds instead: an official price list of paper and ink in Qing Beijing, with the wages on the same list, and a silence in Edo-period Japan.

In 1736 the Ministry of Works and the Nine Chief Ministers in Beijing settled an official list of the values of materials, based on market prices, which priced paper by the sheet in some sixty kinds and ink by the jin, with the day wages of laborers and craftsmen on the same list. No price for paper or ink in Edo-period Japan was found in a source this record can cite. What a notice board's notices were made of is in Notice boards (kosatsuba).

Paper in Qing Beijing

  • What did a sheet cost?
    From 0.0003 tael of silver for the cheapest, sanbai paper, to 0.3 tael for the dearest, a yellow-and-white deer paper 1.2 zhang long and 5 chi wide.
  • And the everyday papers?
    Maobian paper and placard paper of every color, 0.01 tael a sheet; liansi paper 0.0055 to 0.0085 by its stock and color, and 0.01 for Kaihua liansi; lianqi paper of every color 0.0015; Jiangnan paper 0.027; and the first grade of Korean paper 0.08.
  • How big was a sheet?
    Among the stationery papers it prices by the sheet, the list gives a size for only two, the yellow-and-white deer paper and a brocade paper; for the rest it gives none. Only its oiled papers, priced apart, more often carry a size.

Ink in Qing Beijing

  • What did ink cost?
    By the jin: top-soot Hui ink 3.6 taels, Hui ink 0.6, blue ink 0.342 and green-soot ink 0.04.
  • And an ink stick?
    No price for a single stick was found; the list prices ink only by weight.

The day's wage beside the Beijing prices

  • What did a laborer and a craftsman earn?
    The same list pays a laborer 75 copper cash a day, and carpenters, masons, bricklayers, tilers and painters 154 cash.
  • Why are the prices and the wages not set in one unit?
    Paper and ink are priced in silver and the wages in copper cash, and this record does not give the rate between the two, so neither is converted.

Edo Japan

  • What did paper and an ink stick cost?
    No price for three Japanese papers, hanshi, Mino paper and hosho, or for an ink stick, by the sheet or the quire, was found in a source this record can cite. No wage is set beside it.
