What did a shrine's metal furnishings cost?

Rokugan's prices are counted in koku and its smaller coins, and the setting's money rules judge a price by how many days of a craftsman's or a laborer's work it would take. The findings below give what the historical record holds for the metal furnishings of a shrine in Edo-period Japan and, where Japan is silent, in Qing China, each price set beside the nearest day wages the record gives.

No price was found for any of the six things asked: a shrine's mirror, its hanging gong, hanging metal lanterns, a small gilt-bronze reliquary, an offering chest with its lock, or a lacquered chest for remains. The nearest are a village shrine's payments for its door fittings and ironwork in 1840, the price of raw copper in 1774, and, in China, the price of coarse copper ware by weight.

The furnishings asked

  • What did a shrine's mirror cost?
    No price was found. The three mirrors cast in 1833 for the Sekino shrine of Takaoka are about 73 cm (~29 in) across and 34 kg (~75 lb) in weight, of an alloy of copper, tin and lead; the man who vowed them wrote that one mirror's price had barely been met by a gift from officials and that he hoped to pay for the other two with help from the town's rich merchants, who gave it. No sum is given.
  • What did a hanging gong cost?
    No price was found.
  • What did hanging metal lanterns cost?
    No price was found.
  • What did a small gilt-bronze reliquary cost?
    No price was found.
  • What did an offering chest and its lock cost?
    No price was found, for the chest or the lock.
  • What did a lacquered chest for remains cost?
    No price was found.

The nearest prices in Japan

  • What did a village shrine pay for metalwork?
    At the Hachiman shrine of Shibasaki, in the Tama district of Musashi, in 1840, the door fittings ordered from a fittings maker of Yamaguchi were settled with a last payment of 3 bu, and 1 ryo 1 bu was handed to a villager to order the worship hall's ironwork at Okawa-dori. These are building hardware, not the furnishings asked, and the whole price of the door fittings is not given.
  • What did raw copper cost?
    In 1774 the shogunate's copper office in Osaka bought copper at 139.48 to 156.52 monme of silver a hundred kin, by its mine. Copper for export to China was fixed at 115 monme a hundred kin, and copper sold at home, the dictionary says of the years after 1738, held at about twice the China export price. A kin is 600 g (~1.3 lb).
  • What was the metal in a mirror worth?
    About 80 to 90 monme, by this project's arithmetic: a mirror of 34 kg holds about 57 kin of metal, which at the copper office's buying prices of 1774 comes to 79-89 monme. It is a floor, not a price: the mirror is of copper, tin and lead, cast sixty years later, copper sold at home cost more than the office paid, and the casting and polishing are left out.

The nearest price in China

  • What did copper ware cost by weight?
    0.27 to 0.28 tael a jin for even the coarse heavy kind, by a memorial of the early Qianlong years, when a jin of copper coins was worth only 0.17 tael; the memorial names no place. It is ware sold by weight, not one of the objects asked.

Day wages beside them

  • Beside the Shibasaki payments, 1840?
    No wage of the place and year was found. The nearest craftsman's wage read is a carpenter's at Kumehara in 1830, in the same province, ten years earlier: 2 monme of silver a day and 1 monme more that this project reads as his food allowance. The nearest unskilled wage read is a Kyoto casual laborer's of 1802-1804, about 0.92 monme a day. At 60 monme to the ryo, a rate this project borrows, the last 3 bu for the door fittings was 15 days of the carpenter or about 49 of the laborer, and the ironwork's 1 ryo 1 bu 25 or about 82.
  • Beside the copper of 1774?
    No Osaka wage of 1774 was found. The nearest unskilled wage read is Kyoto's of 1745-1754, about 0.91 monme a day, some twenty-five years earlier; at it a kin of copper at the office's buying price cost 1.5 to 1.7 days, and the mirror's metal floor about 87 to 98 days. The nearest craftsman's wage read is Osaka's carpenters' of 1802-1804, some thirty years later, at 4.7 times Kyoto's unskilled rate of about 0.92 monme, so about 4.3 monme a day: about a third of a day a kin, and 18 to 21 days for the mirror's metal.
  • Beside the Qing copper ware?
    The nearest Qing wage read is the public-construction wage norm of Jiangsu, set in 1769, some twenty-five to thirty-five years later: 0.040 tael a day unskilled and 0.051 skilled, averages over the province's districts of regulated wages that the regulations' introductory memorial calls near market prices at low market activity. At them a jin of coarse copper ware was 6.75 to 7 unskilled or about 5.3 to 5.5 skilled days.
