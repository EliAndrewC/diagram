How our maps draw pigs and ducks in south-China rice villages

On our maps, most farms in a plain paddy hamlet keep pigs, as the Shen family farm manual of the seventeenth century has a rice farm do. No ducks are drawn at the houses: the ducks of the rice country fed out on the reaped fields after the harvest, which a summer map does not show. A dike-pond hamlet is different: its sties stand on the fish-pond banks, by the rule at How our maps draw pigs and ducks at a dike-pond: sties on the pond dikes.

The rule the map follows: on a plain paddy hamlet, about two households in three, the share varying from hamlet to hamlet, have a small pig sty, drawn about 8 by 6 ft (a guess), against the farmhouse or one of its outbuildings, on the household's own plot; no duck pen is drawn.

  • How many households keep a sty?
    About two in three, the share varying from hamlet to hamlet around that figure. It is the share a 1920s survey of Chinese farms gives for the rice-growing east-central provinces, nearly two farms in three, used here as the nearest stand-in for the earlier village because it is the only count we found, not as a figure from before modern times: no count from before 1912, when the Qing dynasty fell, was found.
  • The sty stands against the farmhouse or an outbuilding - a GUESS.
    No page we read says where an ordinary paddy farm kept its sty; the one house type whose sties are placed, the Hakka enclosed house, kept them inside its walls, in its rear ring of rooms.
  • A sty is drawn about 8 by 6 ft, shed and pen together - a GUESS.
    The size of a farm's pig sty has not been searched for. The map draws it as it draws a sty on a dike-pond bank (How our maps draw pigs and ducks at a dike-pond: sties on the pond dikes), a small ridged shed with a railed pen beside it, and at the same size.
  • No duck pen is drawn in the yard.
    The ducks of the rice country are recorded out on the reaped fields after the harvest, gleaning the fallen grain, which a summer map does not show, and no page we read says whether a rice farm also penned ducks at the house.
