What did carrying stone cost, by cart and by porter?

Rokugan's builders haul stone to shrines along roads and up into the forest, and the setting judges a price by how many days of a laborer's work it would take. The findings below give what the historical record holds instead: no rate from Edo-period Japan, and the official rates of Qing China for stone by cart and for porters, each set beside the day wages of the same districts.

No Edo-period rate was found for carrying stone, by a hired cart near a road or by porters into the mountains. What survives are the rates two Qing provinces, Yunnan and Gansu, could claim back for hauling to their official building, under regulations whose memorials are dated 1769, each beside the craftsman's and the laborer's day wage of the same district in the same silver. The post stations' fares, and the Qing rates of Zhili and Hunan, are in What did hiring a horse or a porter cost?; the wages of hauling stone at Osaka in the 1620s are in What did a torii, stonework and earthwork cost?

Japan

  • What did a load of stone cost by cart near a road?
    No rate was found. The nearest figures, which are not hires of a cart, are the Osaka wages of 1620 for bringing stone out of the hills, 1.3 monme of silver a man, and for hauling it on the sledge, 1.5 (What did a torii, stonework and earthwork cost?), and the post stations' pack-horse fares by the stage (What did hiring a horse or a porter cost?). In Osaka, from the Bunsei era (1818-1830), a beka cart could be used only on paying the post-horse office, and at the post station's petition the fee came to be levied each spring and autumn, from about 2 shu of gold to over 1 ryo by how much the cart was used: a license, not a hire. The nearest day wages in this record are Osaka's own, from the same author's record of the 1830s to the 1850s, Kitagawa Morisada's, a carpenter's 3.1 monme of silver a day and a building helper's 280 mon (What did a skilled craftsman earn against a laborer?), up to some thirty years later; the fee is in gold and is not converted.
  • And by porters into deep forest, with no trail?
    No rate was found. The nearest are the Oya quarrymen who carried a block of as much as 150 kg (~330 lb) out on one man's back (What did a torii, stonework and earthwork cost?), and a highway porter's fare by the stage for 5 kan (about 19 kg) (What did hiring a horse or a porter cost?), not answers to the question.

Qing China: stone by cart

  • What did stone cost by oxcart in Yunnan?
    0.001 tael of silver for every 100 jin carried a li, in fourteen districts of the prefectures of Lin'an and Qujing, each row marked as the rule already in force. At Nanning in Qujing a craftsman was allowed 0.06 tael a day and a laborer 0.04; at Jianshui in Lin'an, 0.07 and 0.05. The laborer's rate is written per man; this project reads it as a man's day, as the Gansu database labels the same unit. So 100 jin (about 60 kg, ~130 lb) carried 100 li (about 58 km, ~36 miles) cost 0.1 tael, 2.5 days of a laborer's pay at Nanning and 2 at Jianshui, this project's arithmetic.
  • And by cart in Gansu?
    At Fuyi, Gaotai and Suzhou stone went 600 jin to a cart, at 0.01 tael a cart a li at the first two and 0.012 at Suzhou; a craftsman's day there was 0.06 tael and a laborer's 0.05. A cart of stone over 100 li came to 1.0 to 1.2 tael, 20 to 24 days of a laborer's pay, this project's arithmetic. At Yumen, where a craftsman got 0.07 and a laborer 0.06, the district asked 0.0138 tael a cart a li for timber, 720 jin to a cart, and for stone and lime, 600; the regulation found that more than a case approved in 1760, 0.00196 tael for 130 jin a li, and fixed that case's rate instead, leaving lime out, since the case set no rate for it.
  • And in the capital?
    The Board of Works paid, for a cart of the mats, bamboo poles, reed screens or rope it bought (a cart of rope was 1,300 jin), 200 copper cash in the city, 300 within 10 li outside it, and 0.02 tael of silver a li beyond; its list names no stone. The list is undated; the nearest wage is the capital's official list of 1736, a laborer's 75 copper cash a day and a craftsman's 154 (What did a skilled craftsman earn against a laborer?), so a cart in the city was about 2.7 days of a laborer's pay, this project's arithmetic.

Qing China: porters

  • What did porters cost in Yunnan?
    The rows for porters, most of which do not name the load, allow 0.001 to 0.0025 tael for every 80 or 100 jin carried a li. At Shunning one man carrying on a pole got 0.0017 for 100 jin a li, and two or more carrying it between them 0.0021; in Qujing's districts, porters carrying timber got 0.0013, beside the oxcart's 0.001 for stone. A laborer's day was 0.05 tael at Shunning and 0.04 at Nanning and at Yongbei, whose porter's rate, 0.0025, ties for the highest. So at Nanning, Shunning and Yongbei, 100 jin carried 100 li by porter cost 0.13 to 0.25 tael, about 3 to 6 days of a laborer's pay there, this project's arithmetic.
  • And in Gansu?
    At Qinzhou the old rule paid a porter carrying timber 0.04 tael a day, without reckoning weight or distance, and the district's register, which the regulation approved, gave 0.04 for 50 jin carried 60 li; 0.04 was also the laborer's day wage there. At Guide, where timber came from the Kanbula mountains, rugged country of steep ridges, porters were rated by the old rule at 0.009 tael for 60 jin a li, and the register's 0.003 was entered; a laborer there got 0.04 a day and a craftsman 0.05. At Guide's rate, 60 jin carried 10 li cost three-quarters of a laborer's day, this project's arithmetic.
  • Did porters carry stone?
    No porter's rate for stone was found. Gansu's regulation says, district after district, that its rule sets no separate freight for bricks, tiles, stone and lime, which are to be handled as before.
