What did cloth and clothing cost?

Rokugan's samurai and peasants dress in cloth bought by the bolt, and the setting judges a price by how many days of a laborer's work it would take. The findings below give what Edo-period Japan paid for cotton and silk by the bolt and Qing China paid by length, and the few garment prices found, with the nearest day wages found set beside them.

In Edo-period Japan the tan was the length of cloth for one kimono, about 10 m (~33 ft) of narrow cloth in the 1790s. A tan of cotton cost about 4 to 9 monme of silver in Ako in 1781-1790, and about 3 to 17.5 at Edo's wholesalers in 1791. For plain silk there is one Japanese price, from a book of stories. No Japanese price was found for pongee, a new or a used kimono, the sewing of one, or silk bedding.

How long was a tan?

  • A tan was the cloth for one kimono.
    Before today's metric bolts, narrow cloth for kimono was woven 9 sun 5 bu wide (a sun is a tenth of a shaku) in the whale-foot measure used for cloth, about 36 cm (~14 in), and 2 jo 6 shaku or 2 jo 8 shaku long, about 9.85 to 10.6 m (~32-35 ft), the cloth an adult's kimono took. The length of a tan was fixed in the Edo period as the cloth one kosode took, and revised many times over the ages. A hiki was two tan.
  • What did a buyer insist on?
    The Mitsui house, ordering cotton from Hoki province in the 1790s, refused any cloth narrower than 9 sun or shorter than 2 jo 7 shaku, about 34 cm by 10.2 m (~13 in by 34 ft).

Cotton

  • What did a tan of cotton cost in the country?
    At Ako in 1781, 7 monme for the best, 5.4 to 5.5 for the middling and 4 for the worst; in 1790, 9, 7 to 8, and 6. At Nishinomiya in 1790, 9 to 13 monme.
  • And in Edo?
    In 1791, when the shogunate inquired into prices, Edo's cotton wholesalers reported selling prices from about 3 monme a tan, for the cheapest Osaka cottons, to 17.5, for Ise cotton for padded clothes: Owari cotton 6 to 12, Mikawa about 6 to 8, Kawachi cotton 12 and Mooka cotton 13.6. Their historian warns that the guilds probably reported their prices low.
  • What was a day's pay beside them?
    No wage of Ako or Edo in those years was found; the nearest is the Kyoto day laborer's 2.55 g of silver in 1791, about 0.82 monme. At that wage a tan of Ako cotton was about 5 to 11 days' pay, and a tan in Edo about 4 to 21.
  • How does Bassino and Ma's cotton compare?
    Their price of "cotton linen" - plain cotton cloth - 5.83 g of silver a meter, an Osaka price of the 1840s that they use for Kyoto in 1745-1754 (What did a Kyoto laborer's year of goods cost?), comes to about 19 monme for a tan of 10.2 m: just above the top of the Edo range, and two to five times Ako's prices. No year-by-year series of cotton cloth prices in Osaka or elsewhere could be read.

Silk and pongee

  • What did plain silk cost in Japan?
    The one price found is 45 monme for a tan, half a hiki, of habutae in 1692, and it comes from a work of fiction, Seken munezan'yo, by way of a modern compiler; the book is Ihara Saikaku's collection of 20 short stories of townspeople on New Year's Eve, published in 1692.
  • How many times dearer than cotton was silk?
    In Japan, about six times, on weak evidence: in 1716 white cotton sold at 14 to 15 monme a hiki, 7 to 7.5 a tan, in another silver coin, the Yotsuho silver, rising later to as much as 40 a hiki; set against the stories' silk of 24 years before, that is about 6 to 1. Beijing's official price list of 1736 is firmer: silk of various colors, 1.4 chi wide, 0.6 taels a zhang and red Hangzhou silk 0.4, against blue cotton 0.188 and coarse white cotton 0.09, so silk cost about 2 to 7 times as much as cotton by length.
  • What was a day's pay in Beijing?
    The same list pays a laborer 75 copper cash a day, which its historian puts at about 0.094 taels; a zhang of silk of various colors was about 6 days' pay, of blue cotton 2, and of coarse cotton 1.
  • What did pongee cost?
    No Japanese price was found. The Qing writer Ye Mengzhu says that pongee of the Shandong kind, by his day woven in Jiaxing, Huzhou, Suzhou and Songjiang, then cost at best about 0.1 taels a chi, and some as little as 0.03 to 0.04. The nearest wage found is Suzhou's 0.09 taels a day in 1700.

Clothing and bedding

  • What did a new plain kimono cost?
    No Japanese price was found. The nearest found are Beijing's in 1736. Besides costume pieces, the list prices an unfaced sheepskin jacket at 1.2 taels, cloth shoes at 0.18 a pair and cloth boots at 0.6. Its silk shirts at 2.2 and its padded jacket of printed cloth at 0.54 are costume pieces for the yangge folk dance, which its historian notes differ somewhat from everyday dress.
  • What did a used kimono, or the sewing of one, cost?
    No price was found for either.
  • What did silk bedding cost?
    No price was found.
