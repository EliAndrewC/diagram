How our maps draw landless and tenant households (mizunomi, nago)

Most farmers in the empire our maps are set in are tenants, so on our maps a tenant's house is an ordinary farmhouse, not a poor one. The households that hold no land at all, or rent their house as well as their fields, are drawn in the two forms the record finds: a hut lived in inside a larger farm's enclosure, or a small house of their own at the field edge or the foot of the hills.

  • Where a landless household (mizunomi, nago) lives is a knob with two settings.
    It is either a hut or a hut by the farm's gate standing inside a larger farm's enclosure, drawn as a small outbuilding that is lived in, or a small house of its own of the kind a branch household was allowed, at the field edge or along the roads at the foot of the hills. Both are attested, the second for branch households rather than for landless ones by name, so neither is chosen over the other.
  • The split between the two forms, and how many landless households a map has, are our own choice.
    The pages we read give the two forms but not how many households lived in each, so the shares are a GUESS, set arbitrarily rather than taken from the record.
