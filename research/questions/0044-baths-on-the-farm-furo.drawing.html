How our maps draw farm baths (furo)

Our maps draw only what is recorded from before Japan began to modernize in 1868, so they draw no bath shed standing on its own. In that age a farm's bath, where it had one, was a small room of the main house, as Baths on the farm (furo) sets out; a bath in a building of its own is not found before the Meiji period, and the surveys of the twentieth century, which find a bath at more farms, are not used for how many homesteads have one.

  • A bath, where drawn, is a room of 6 by 6 to 6 by 12 ft joined to the main house.
    The baths in the house-plan registers of villages on the road to Nikko, of 1824 and 1842, were 1 to 2 tsubo, a tsubo being about 6 by 6 ft.
  • How many homesteads have a bath room?
    Two or three in ten, the share of houses with a bath in the registers of 1824 and 1842. The earlier registers, of 1770 and 1771, show a bath in only a very small part of each village's houses; following the later share, nearer the end of the age the maps draw, is this project's choice.
  • A bath room stands by the main door, at the far end of the stable wing, or joined to the floored rooms.
    All three places are attested: beside the house's main door, at the far end of its stable wing, and joined to its floored rooms - the raised, wood-floored living rooms, as against the earth floor - a place found in only three houses of two villages. A fourth place, joined to a back room behind the house, is found in one house and is not drawn. Which place a bath room takes is chosen at random for each house, in the shares the registers count (Baths on the farm): beyond the stable wing for most, about four in five; joined to the floored rooms for about three in a hundred, the one peasant's bath of the two registers' 36; by the main door for the rest, a place those registers do not give for them - drawing the rest there, where the earliest registers' baths stood, is this project's choice.
  • A headman's house has its bath room joined to the floored rooms.
    Of the three baths joined to the floored rooms in the registers, two were in headmen's houses and one in a peasant's house with a formal room. That every headman's bath stood there is a GUESS.
