What did a skilled craftsman earn against a laborer?

Rokugan's craftsmen and laborers are paid by the day, and the setting needs to know how much more a skilled hand earned than an unskilled one. The findings below give the few places in Edo-period Japan and Qing China where the historical record sets a craftsman's day wage beside a laborer's, and how far apart they were.

In Osaka the city's building helpers were paid 236 copper mon a day in 1785, and a carpenter was costed at 4 monme of silver a man, 420 mon by a historian's conversion, for rebuilding a temple that burned in 1801, about 1.8 times as much, far below the 4.7 times another study finds between Osaka's carpenters and Kyoto's laborers in 1802-1804. No such pair was found for Edo or a castle town. In Qing Beijing and its province, the official wage lists paid a craftsman about one and a third to two times a laborer.

Osaka

  • What was a building laborer paid?
    236 mon a day. In 1785 the city magistrate's notice gave the day wage of the tetsudai, the helpers who did the rough work of Osaka's building sites, as "up to now, 236 mon". The same year their would-be guild asked to set it at 248 mon, of which the helpers would get 4 mon more and the guild 8. Their historian judges them building workers of fairly low skill, whose wages suggest a low standard of living; a petition of 1782 to found their guild said many were poor and lived from day to day.
  • And a carpenter?
    4 monme of silver a man in the estimate for rebuilding the Shitennoji temple, which burned in 1801; the estimate's own year is not given. Their historian converts it to 420 mon at the rate of the time and sets it beside the helpers' day wage. Against the helpers' 236 mon of 1785 that is about 1.8 times, across sixteen years or more.
  • Is there a pair from one source?
    One: Morisada manko, a record of the customs of the 1830s to the 1850s finished in 1853, gives the Osaka carpenter's wage as 3 monme 1 bun of silver without his meal money, and the helper's as 280 mon. The two are in different coins and the study gives no rate between them for those years, so no ratio is computed. Either way, its historian says, the helpers' wage was well below the carpenter's.
  • Why does another study find 4.7 times?
    Saito sets Osaka's carpenters of 1802-1804 against Kyoto's day laborers, and finds the urban carpenter earning 4.7 times what the unskilled could (What did an unskilled laborer earn?). Takeya sets Osaka's carpenters against Osaka's own building helpers. The two do not agree, and this record has not found why.

Edo and the castle towns

  • What did a craftsman earn against a laborer in Edo?
    No source found gives both for Edo or for any castle town, in the same place and years.
  • What one-sided figures are there?
    Edo carpenters' day wages in silver, early in the 1800s and in the 1850s (What did a shrine roof and its finish cost?); Edo roofers and Tama carpenters of 1749, and a Musashi village carpenter of 1818 in mon (What did a small shrine sanctuary cost to build?); a farm day laborer of a Kazusa village in 1842, in mon (What did sake cost, by the shop and by the cup?); and the Kyoto day laborer in silver from 1745, with Edo's laborers at about Kyoto's level from 1818 (What did an unskilled laborer earn?). A ratio built from them would set a carpenter of one source against a laborer of another city, decades apart, so none is given here.
  • Is there any Japanese pair from one place?
    One, from a village: in 1802-1804 the carpenters of Kami-Kawarabayashi, about 40 km from Kyoto, were paid 2.6 times the village's spring rate for farm labor (What did an unskilled laborer earn?).

Qing China

  • What did Beijing pay?
    On the capital's official list of 1736, a carpenter, mason, bricklayer, tiler or painter 154 copper cash a day and a laborer 75, about twice as much.
  • And the districts around it?
    The Zhili province regulation whose memorial is dated 1769 sets each district's day wages in silver. In Tianjin a craftsman got 0.12 tael, a plasterer 0.15, a stonemason 0.13 and a sawyer 0.10, against a laborer's 0.09 and an earth-rammer's 0.07; in Tongzhou a craftsman 0.15 and a stonemason 0.18, against a laborer's 0.08; in Liangxiang a craftsman 0.12, against a laborer's 0.06. So a craftsman got about 1.3 times a laborer in Tianjin, 1.9 in Tongzhou and 2 in Liangxiang, and the best-paid trades up to 2.25.
  • What are these figures?
    The rates the province could claim back from the Ministry of Works for its public building, which the regulation's edicts say reflect market prices at middling activity, some extrapolated from earlier prices. Their analysts warn that such lists often give the same wage across many counties, and may show government policy rather than the market.
  • Across the empire?
    Averaged over the districts of the same regulations, unskilled construction labor was paid 0.077 tael a day in the Beijing districts and skilled 0.141; 0.071 and 0.112 around Tianjin and Baoding; 0.054 and 0.081 in the rest of Zhili; and 0.044 and 0.060 over all the regions the averages cover, about three-quarters of China's population, weighted by population, about 1.8, 1.6, 1.5 and 1.4 times. The arms-making regulation of 1813 set unskilled labor at about 0.04 tael in most provinces and skilled craftsmen 0.01 or 0.02 higher, 1.25 to 1.5 times.
  • And over all the wages one study gathered?
    An estimate: a regression over every wage the study collected, from court records of about 1740 to 1820, the Dutch East India Company's accounts at Canton and the government regulations, puts the skilled premium at 63% of an unskilled laborer's wage in the Yangzi delta, a craftsman at about 1.6 times a laborer. It is a statistical estimate, not a wage anyone recorded.

Tailors, smiths and fletchers

  • What did a tailor earn?
    In Japan, no wage was found. In Qing China only a grouped rate: in Jinzhou and Gaocheng, two Zhili districts, carpenters, stonemasons, painters, limners and tailors together 0.08 tael a day, against a laborer's 0.06. No tailor's piece rate was found.
  • And a smith?
    In Japan, no wage was found, and the Zhili lists name no smith. The only smiths' wages in this record are a Ming military manual's forge smiths, with no laborer beside them (What did arrows cost?).
  • And a fletcher?
    No wage was found, in Japan or China (What did arrows cost?).
