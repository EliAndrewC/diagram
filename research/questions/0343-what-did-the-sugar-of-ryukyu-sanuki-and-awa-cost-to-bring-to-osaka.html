What did the sugar of Ryukyu, Sanuki and Awa cost to bring to Osaka?

In Rokugan a tariff of typically about 15% of the declared sales value is charged at the gate of the city where goods are sold, and the setting's price ledger reckons a good sold far from where it was made at its price there plus freight, a 15% tariff for each border and a merchant's margin. The findings below give what the historical record of Edo-period Japan (1603 to 1868) holds for three sugars sold in Osaka: the black sugar of the Ryukyu kingdom and the white sugar of Sanuki and Awa on Shikoku.

For none of the three was a price found at both ends. For Ryukyu's sugar the record gives the freight in kind and the hands it passed through; for Sanuki's white sugar, two freights of about 1858, a levy and the way it was sold in Osaka; for Awa's, nothing. Satsuma's black sugar from the Amami islands, and tea, are in What did sugar and tea cost where they were made, against Osaka and Edo?, and the sugar that came from abroad through Nagasaki in What did imported sugar, spices and medicines cost at Nagasaki, against Kyoto and Osaka?

Sugar: Ryukyu, Sanuki and Awa

  • What of Ryukyu's sugar?
    No price at either end was found. The black sugar Satsuma sold in Osaka included Ryukyu's, and in 1807, on the domain's finance office's inquiry into the Ryukyu freight, the freight on sugar delivered to the storehouse office within the Ryukyu-kan at Kagoshima, this project's reading, was given as 23 kin 5 go 7 shaku of sugar in every 100 kin, nearly a quarter.
  • By what hands did Ryukyu's sugar reach Osaka?
    The royal government sent its sugar to the office it kept at Kagoshima, the Ryukyu-kan, whose officials sold it to the Satsuma merchants admitted there; from there Satsuma domain took charge, and the sugar was sold at the domain's warehouse-residence in Osaka by tender among the sugar brokers licensed to bid, as Kurima Yasuo (kurima-ryukyu-sato-ryutsu) reads the older histories; a price he does give is of 1880, after the kingdom's end. For the kingdom's years he gives no price at any of the three places.
  • What did Sanuki's white sugar cost to bring to Osaka?
    Takamatsu domain, in Sanuki on Shikoku, about 140 km (~87 mi) from Osaka across the Inland Sea in a straight line, shipped its white sugar there. A domain record of about 1858, quoted by Kodama Yoichi (kodama-takamatsu-sato-kawase), raises two freights and lists them apart. For white sugar and white raw sugar shipped from the coasts of two districts, the freight, until then 2 bu 2 rin, went up by 4 rin to 2 bu 6 rin; the record words the rate as "price in silver" and does not say of what it is a part, and it is read here as 2.2 and then 2.6 in a hundred of the sugar's price, this project's reading. For casked goods, the freight went from 2 monme to 2 monme 5 bu a cask. The record does not say which sugar went at the cask rate, nor in what money its monme were counted.
  • What did the domain take?
    A levy of two in a hundred on sugar sold. Kodama names it among the measures that followed the domain's plan of the first years of Tenpo (the era of 1830 to 1844) to mend its treasury through sugar, and gives it no year of its own.
  • How was it sold there?
    As a rule a fifth of each cargo was landed at the domain's sugar office in Osaka and sold by tender to the brokers admitted to the domain's Osaka residence, and the other four-fifths went to the wholesalers who took Sanuki's sugar on consignment, to be sold with the office's winning bid as the standard.
  • Is there a price at both ends?
    No. Kodama values the sugar at a flat 2 monme 3 bu a kin in domain notes for all of 1836 to 1871, with a kin of 230 monme (about 860 g) and a cask of 100 kin on average: his own round figure, not a price of any year or place. No share of a cask's value is drawn here from the cask freight: the record lists that freight apart from white sugar's own, and names no money for it, where Kodama's value is in the domain's notes. No sale price in Osaka and no dated price in Sanuki was found in the paper, about half of whose pages were only searched on their page images for prices and charges, and nothing was found for the white sugar of Awa.
