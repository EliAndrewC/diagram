What did hemp cloth and a shrine's amulets cost?

A Rokugani household's cloth and a shrine's amulets both need a price, and the setting judges a price by how many days of a laborer's work it would take. The findings below give the little the historical record holds on what hemp cloth cost in Edo-period Japan and in Ming and Qing Beijing, and on what households gave for the talismans an Ise shrine agent brought them, each set beside the nearest wages found.

Only one Japanese price for hemp cloth was found: twenty rolls of Etchu cloth bought for 180 monme of silver before 1614, a lot judged worse than another, in rolls of unknown length. In Beijing in 1736 the official list priced ramie cloth below coarse cotton by the zhang, beside a laborer's day of 75 copper cash and a craftsman's of 154. No price for one amulet or talisman was found; what survives is one Ise shrine agent's takings in 1813, 48 ryo 2 bu from 2,162 households.

Hemp cloth in Japan

  • What did a lot of hemp cloth cost?
    About 9 monme of silver a roll. Maeda Toshinaga, lord of Kaga, wrote to his retainer to pay 180 monme for twenty rolls of cloth bought at Nakata in Etchu, and called it worse than the cloth another man had presented to him a few days before. The cloth, the museum that holds the letter notes, was Etchu cloth, a hemp cloth woven in the province; before cotton arrived at the end of the Middle Ages, "cloth" meant hemp.
  • When, and how long was a roll?
    The letter carries no year; Toshinaga died in 1614, and another document in the same set, a receipt, is dated 1611. It counts the cloth in rolls, not in tan, and gives no length.
  • What did a tan of the famous hemp cloths cost?
    No price by the tan was found for Nara-zarashi, Echigo jofu or the hemp cloth of Omi.
  • And beside cotton and a day's pay?
    No Japanese cotton price or wage of the early 1600s was found. The record's Japanese cotton prices are of the 1780s and 1790s (What did cloth and clothing cost?), and its earliest Japanese wage is the Kyoto laborer's of 1745-1754, in silver (What did an unskilled laborer earn?), more than 130 years after the letter.

Hemp and ramie cloth in Beijing

  • What did they cost in 1736?
    On the official price list of Beijing's materials, hemp cloth cost 0.216 tael of silver a pi and ramie cloth 0.05 a zhang, and in the same table coarse white cotton cloth cost 0.09 a zhang. By the zhang, ramie was the cheaper of the two.
  • How long was a pi?
    Not fixed. The list's historian notes the old saying that a pi was 4 zhang, but the silks of the Ming list that give their size run 3 zhang 2 chi to 3 zhang 6 chi a pi, so prices by the pi are hard to compare directly.
  • And beside a day's pay?
    The same list paid a laborer 75 cash a day and a craftsman 154, which its historian puts at about 0.094 and 0.19 tael. A zhang of ramie cloth was about half a laborer's day and a quarter of a craftsman's; a zhang of coarse cotton about a laborer's day and half a craftsman's; a pi of hemp cloth about 2.3 laborer's days or 1.1 craftsman's.
  • And in 1615?
    The Ming Board of Works' list of 1615 priced hemp cloth at 0.2 tael a pi, ramie cloth at 0.1 a zhang and coarse white cotton at 0.094 a zhang, so ramie was then a little dearer than cotton. The historian gives the Board's day wages of the time only as a range, 0.03 to 0.07 tael for its craftsmen and laborers on long and short days together, without saying which was whose. At those wages a zhang of ramie cloth took about 1.4 to 3.3 days' pay, and a pi of hemp cloth about 3 to 7.

A shrine's amulets and talismans

  • What did a household give for an Ise talisman?
    No fixed price: what it could. Each year the oshi, the agents of the Ise shrines, brought the households on their rolls a talisman with gifts such as the Ise calendar, and took in return an offering in rice or money in proportion to the household.
  • What did one oshi's round bring in?
    An oshi who went round 2,162 households in Ozu domain, in Iyo, in 1813 took 48 ryo 2 bu in offerings, given in rice or money; his gifts and other costs came to 29 ryo 3 bu, leaving 18 ryo 3 bu. That is about 0.022 ryo a household before costs and 0.009 after, for the talisman, the calendar and the gifts together.
  • What did a single amulet or talisman cost?
    No price was found, in Japan or in China.
  • And beside a day's pay?
    No wage of Ozu in 1813 was found. The nearest unskilled wage is the Kyoto day laborer's of the same years, in silver (What did an unskilled laborer earn?), and the nearest craftsman's the Osaka carpenter's of the early 1800s, in silver too (What did a skilled craftsman earn against a laborer?). The takings are reckoned in ryo and bu, the units of the gold coinage, and the wages in silver, so neither wage is set against them.
