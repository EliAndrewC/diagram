What a staff rowhouse was built of

A rowhouse (nagaya) was a long, narrow building divided along its length into dwellings, where a lord's or an office's lower staff lived (Staff rowhouses and barracks (nagaya)).

  • What was a staff rowhouse built of?
    A country jin'ya's staff rows could be thatched. A table of the Sayama domain's dwellings gives its rowhouses a roof thatched with wheat straw, and a lean-to that the modern author reads as pantile, a roof of interlocking clay tiles; the table's year is not given. The Obata foot soldiers' row, as it stands today after its repairs, is timber and of one story, with a hipped roof (sloping on all four sides) of thatch now covered in copper sheet, and earth walls on its main part. The Shibata row of 1842 has a hipped thatched roof. A gate rowhouse could be tiled: the Kaibara jin'ya's has a hip-and-gable roof of pantiles. Of the four rows' pages, only Obata's gives its walls, earth walls in their present state; none gives the walls or the frame of an intendant's office's staff row, and the Chinese pages we read are silent on what a yamen's clerks' and runners' quarters were built of.
