• kotobank-umaya - 「A warrior's stable, that is, one attached to a shoin-style residence, has the tachiba where horses are tied, a zashiki called tozamurai, and an earth-floored kusa-no-ma. The Rakuchu rakugai screens and the Shomei, the manual handed down in the Heinouchi family, the shogunate's master carpenters, show a gable-entered stable 3 ken long, considered the most formal of its time. The tachiba is board-floored, not earth」 (translated; ) (the Heibonsha world encyclopedia entry)
  • jaanus-umaya - 「The stables used by the warrior class bushi 武士 were substantial freestanding structures in the shoin style」 and 「The classic 16th-17th century examples, as recorded in the master carpenter's manual *Shōmei 匠明, and excavated at the site of the Asakura 朝倉 mansion at Ichijōdani 一乗谷 in Fukui Prefecture were 3, 5 or 7 bays in length.」 and 「None survive」 and 「Illustrated handscrolls indicate that the quarters for grooms and retainers in warrior class stables go back to the Kamakura period.」 and 「In the warrior class stable, separate stalls of one bay width were provided.」 and 「the stalls had raised timber floors itabari 板張」
