How our maps draw sake breweries (sakagura)

Our maps put a sake brewery in the county town and in every provincial city, and in no village. A brewery is drawn as a shopfront with its vat hall and stores behind it, the hall sized from the brewery buildings that stood before 1868. What a city's brewery holds besides - its rice storehouse and private well - is at How our maps draw shops and trades in towns and villages.

  • A county town keeps one brewery.
    The setting's county has "the" sake brewer, one house.
  • A provincial city of about 3,000 inhabitants keeps one or two breweries.
    One or two is this project's estimate, a GUESS: no page we read counts the brewers of a town of a given size.
  • A village keeps no brewery, a deliberate departure from the history.
    Villages were allowed, and even encouraged, to brew after the early eighteenth century, but the setting names the sake brewer as the county's, so the maps keep the brewery in the county town. A village has one only where its map has grown it into a town.
  • The vat hall is drawn about 50 to 62 ft on its long side and about 23 to 49 ft across.
    That is the span of the three measured brewery buildings of the Edo period: a hall at Itami, a fermenting store in Tochigi and a sake store at Kanazawa.
  • How is the county town's brewery laid out?
    As a shopfront at the center of the town with its stores behind it on a deep plot, a GUESS. Brewers are commonly pictured with a solid shop at the center of the town, and the Yachiya brewery at Kanazawa keeps its sake store just north of a tool store in the middle of a long north-south plot.
  • How large is a brewery's store?
    About 62 by 23 ft, the size of the one brewery store measured, the Yachiya brewery's at Kanazawa.
  • A brewery hall of about 96 ft, built in Wakayama in the Meiji era, is not drawn.
    The maps draw nothing attested only in modern times, so a vat hall is sized from the Edo-period buildings alone.

The rule the map follows: a county town keeps one brewery - a shopfront at the center with its vat hall and stores behind it on a deep plot, the hall about 50 to 62 ft on its long side and 23 to 49 ft across, one store about 62 by 23 ft. A provincial city of about 3,000 inhabitants keeps one or two. A village keeps none unless its map has grown it into a town.
