How long did modern experimenters take to spin and weave by hand?

The period sources in How long did it take to make cloth? leave stages of making a robe untimed, so Rokugan's money rules have no figure for them. The findings below give what modern experimenters measured when they wove hand-spun yarn by hand - in another fiber, on another loom and in another age, so a yardstick for players and never a Rokugani figure.

The period figures, cotton from ginned fiber and single steps of silk and ramie, are in How long did it take to make cloth? No measured modern rate for hand-spinning or hand-weaving hemp, ramie, cotton or silk was found.

  • How fast did a modern weaver work a warp-weighted loom?
    About 3 centimeters an hour. In a research project reconstructing Viking Age clothing, weavers working wool spun by hand on drop spindles averaged 16 to 29 threads an hour, about 3 centimeters, depending on the threads per centimeter of the weave; each sample was 60 by 60 centimeters. The hand-spun yarn, hairy with fiber ends, made the warp sticky and every change of shed - opening a new gap between the warp threads for each pass of the weft - slow.
  • How much faster with machine-spun yarn?
    Three to five times: a larger cloth, 1 by 3 meters, a twill with 5 wefts to the centimeter, woven on a loom with loom weights from machine-spun yarn given extra twist, went at 10 to 15 centimeters an hour; the report does not say how much of the gain was the yarn.
  • What does that make one sample?
    About 20 hours of weaving for 60 centimeters of cloth at 3 centimeters an hour, before any spinning - this project's arithmetic. The cloth was wool, woven on a reconstruction of a Viking Age loom, so the figure says only how slow hand weaving could be.
