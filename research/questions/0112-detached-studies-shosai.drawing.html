How our maps draw a detached study (shosai)

A magistrate on our building plans may have a writing pavilion: a small study standing apart in the inner garden, a room of their own set away from both the office hall, where the county's business is done, and the family's house.

  • A study standing apart in a garden is a recorded form.
    A scholar's estate in Kyoto had one, built in 1828 with its garden on its west side, and a Chinese compound set its study in a quiet part of the house compound, beside the rear garden where there was one. So the pavilion's form is accurate.
  • Would a magistrate have had a detached study in the inner garden of their office?
    Our maps give them one, a GUESS: it extends the two recorded studies, a Kyoto scholar's and a Chinese compound's, to an official's compound, and no page we read describes a detached study at a magistrate's or any other official's compound.
  • The pavilion is drawn about 18 by 14 ft, a GUESS.
    No page we read gives a detached study's size.
  • At Ubame the pavilion stands at the inner garden's west edge, in front of the magistrate's quarters.
    The kitchen garden is beside it.
