How our maps draw a samurai's country manor

A samurai's country manor on our maps stands apart from any village, beside its own fields, as the setting has it. The manor drawn is about 1 acre, smaller than any moated residence the sources measure, and far smaller than the large warrior's residence one scholars' reconstruction gives.

The rule the map follows:

  • A manor's form is a knob rolled per manor: moated or garden.
    Both forms are recorded - the medieval moated residence and the Edo-period garden estate - so each manor rolls one.
  • The moated form is a near-square enclosure of about 1.2 to 3 acres, about 230 to 360 ft a side.
    Its size is rolled along that range, the range the measured residences give.
  • The moated form stands inside a moat and an earthen bank, with one gate and the manor's own paddy before it.
    Inside are the master's house at the center, a stable, a storehouse and a row of servants' houses.
  • The garden form has no moat: it is a house with a garden, vegetable plots and a bamboo grove.
    Those are the parts a dictionary gives an Edo daimyo's lower residence on the outskirts (shimoyashiki): gardens, vegetable gardens and bamboo groves.
  • The garden form is drawn at about 1 acre, a guess.
    No page we read gives the size of such an estate kept by a single samurai family, so it stays at the drawn 1 acre, a GUESS, until a source is found.
