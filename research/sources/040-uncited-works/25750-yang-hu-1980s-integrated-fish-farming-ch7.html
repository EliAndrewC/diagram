Yang Huazhu and Hu Baotong, "Introduction of Chinese Integrated Fish Farming and Some Major Models", Chapter 7 of Integrated Fish Farming in China, FAO (https://www.fao.org/4/ac264e/AC264E08.htm)

What it is: A book chapter from an FAO technical series describing Chinese integrated fish farming of the early 1980s: pond silt as fertilizer, fodder grasses grown on pond dikes and slopes, fish and grass rotated in one pond, and the mulberry dike and fish pond system of the Pearl River delta and the Taihu Lake basin. It gives yields, silt removal counts and dike dimensions, with worked examples from Wuxi and Suzhou fish farms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies dimensions and rates for a dike and pond landscape: an average dike crown of 3 m with slopes of 1:1.5 to 1:3, ponds of about 10 mu, silt removed 3 to 6 times a year in the Pearl River delta and Taihu basin, and silt from 1 mu of pond feeding 1 to 2 mu of dike. Its numbers come from state and commune fish farms of the 1970s and 1980s with mechanical aerators and commercial feeds, and it says only that a well-linked dike-pond system did not exist before the 16th century, so its yields are not those of the premodern farm.
