Evariste Regis Huc (Abbe Huc), Travels in Tartary, Thibet, and China, during the years 1844-5-6, Volume 2 of 2, translated from the French by W. Hazlitt (London: Office of the National Illustrated Library), Project Gutenberg eBook #33269 (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33269/33269-h/33269-h.htm)

What it is: The English translation of the second volume of a French Lazarist missionary's first-person travel narrative, in Project Gutenberg's transcription. It follows him and his companion Gabet from the frontier trading town of Tang-Keou-Eul through the Kounboum and Tchogortan lamaseries near the Koukou-Noor, with a large Tibetan embassy caravan to Lhasa (reached 29 January 1846), and back out along the Chinese imperial road through Ghiamda, Lha-Ri, Tsiamdo and Bathang.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness account from the 1840s, before China's 1895 cut-off, of Qing-administered borderland life: lamasery houses and courtyards, Lhasa's whitewashed houses and its lost walls, inns and pawnbrokers at the New Year debt settlement, and the Chinese road with its garrisons, provision magazines with stated yearly costs in silver, relay horses (the oulah) and wooden-trunk bridges. Its places are the Kokonor grassland, Tibet and Kham, not the lowland Chinese or Japanese village the setting models, its author is a missionary hostile to Buddhism who often reports hearsay, its counts and costs are the traveler's round figures, and the Gutenberg text breaks off at Bathang with the footnotes, short of the final stage the contents list names.
