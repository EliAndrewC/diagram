Chunichi Shimbun (中日新聞), "江戸時代の農民も獣害に悩まされた? 岐阜県関市に残る「シシ垣」を児童が学ぶ" (Did Edo-period farmers also suffer from wildlife damage? Schoolchildren learn about the "shishi-gaki" surviving in Seki, Gifu, title translated), 2 October 2025 (in Japanese; https://chunichi.co.jp/article/1141824)

What it is: A regional newspaper report on third-graders visiting the shishi-gaki (animal-damage walls) in the Terao district of Seki, Gifu, said to have been built in the Edo period. Only the first paragraphs are visible, the rest being for members, so the page gives the walls' height and the local use but not the full story.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the existence in a mountain village of Gifu of stone walls encircling a settlement to keep wild boar and deer from the crops, 1.5 to 2 m high and perhaps several kilometers in total length, with pits nearby and the trapped animals eaten. The date, the ring around the settlement and the eating of trapped animals are all told as local tradition, the total length is an estimate, the walls survive only as scattered stretches, and the members-only article is cut off after the lead and names no source, survey or number of the walls.
