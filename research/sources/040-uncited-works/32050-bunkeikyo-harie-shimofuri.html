bunkeikyo-harie-shimofuri

National Council of Cultural Landscape Districts (全国文化的景観地区連絡協議会), "高島市針江・霜降の水辺景観" ("the waterside landscape of Harie and Shimofuri, Takashima City", title translated), in its introductions to member districts' cultural landscapes (in Japanese; https://bunkeikyo.jp/landscape/landscape-310)

What it is: A short page by the council of cultural-landscape districts describing a Takashima, Shiga, village landscape of spring-fed household washing places (kabata), channels and lakeshore reed beds, with its designation date and the city office to contact.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the kabata as a three-basin stone washing place fed by a spring, and a water cycle that ran from the kabata through the river, the rice paddies and the lagoon to Lake Biwa, with lagoon mud used to dress the paddies and water weeds returned as fertilizer; reeds were kept for roofing. It says the kabata are still used and the reed beds are now kept up by a community group, and it does not date the practices beyond "formerly" or give any sizes, so what it shows is how a surviving village uses its water, not a map of a past one.
