kojodan-hikone-umaya

Kojodan (攻城団), "馬屋" ("Umaya, the stable", title translated), from the Hikone Castle guide, quoting the Hikone City and Shiga Prefecture Board of Education signboards (in Japanese; https://kojodan.jp/castle/2/memo/444.html)

What it is: A castle-fan site's note on the Hikone Castle stable, an L-shaped building beside the Sawaguchi turret with stalls for 21 horses, where ten-odd of the Ii lords' horses were kept at all times, with the text of the Shiga Board of Education's signboard and an English panel. It gives the date of the building, its roof and wall finish, its fire and rebuilding history and its 1966-68 repair.

Why it applies, and its limits: It documents the stable of a daimyo's castle: 21 stalls, a small tatami room at the east end, and a gate with a gatekeeper's room near the west end; the part beyond the gate to the south was pulled down in the Meiji era and not restored. The stable dates to about 1700 (Genroku), and it was shortened and reroofed in 1767 after a fire and restored only in part in the 1960s, so the plan is not entirely the original. The page itself calls it the only such large stable left in any castle in Japan, so it marks the upper end of a lord's stable, not an ordinary one.
