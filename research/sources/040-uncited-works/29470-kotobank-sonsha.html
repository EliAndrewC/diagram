kotobank-sonsha

"村社" ("sonsha, the village shrine rank", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/村社-555866)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for sonsha, the village shrine, which is also a rank in the Meiji shrine ranking system, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nipponica (by Kamata Junichi) and the Heibonsha encyclopedia on the rank's creation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a sonsha was a shrine venerated as a locality's tutelary deity, ranked below the gosha, and that the system dates from 1871; Nipponica gives 44,934 sonsha of about 110,000 shrines when it was abolished in 1945. Those figures and the rank belong to the Meiji state's classification, not to the premodern village shrine, which the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten entry only glosses as a village's chinju shrine, with a 1581 citation.
