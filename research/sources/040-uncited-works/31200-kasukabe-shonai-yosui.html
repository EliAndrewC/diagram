kasukabe-shonai-yosui

Kasukabe City Board of Education (Cultural Properties Division and Local History Museum), "庄内領の用水・排水概略図『新編図録春日部の歴史』ーその67" ("A sketch map of the irrigation and drainage of the Shonai district (Shonai-ryo), from the Illustrated Revised History of Kasukabe, no. 67", title translated), on the Hogo-log blog of the Kasukabe City Board of Education portal (in Japanese; https://schit.net/kasukabe/center/blogs/blog_entries/view/220/7635693b879dec83cc50c4c4c61b2d75?frame_id=392)

What it is: A museum staff blog post of 17 November 2018 summarizing a page of the museum's illustrated history of Kasukabe on how the Shonai district (Saitama, in present eastern Satte and the Showa area) was watered and drained in the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a concrete Edo-period system: in Kami-Shonai, the Nakajima canal took Edogawa water at Nakajima village and divided into three branch canals, the intake works were managed by a union of 16 northern villages, and field water drained to the Shonai Furukawa river at Mizukado village. In Shimo-Shonai there was no trunk canal and marsh water seems to have been used for irrigation in drought, so the page shows two contrasting arrangements in one region. It gives no canal lengths, areas or dates for the works, and it rests on one book page and a sketch map rather than stating its own evidence.
