chida-2003-toilet-madori

Chida Shuichi (地田修一), "間取りからみたトイレの位置の変遷" ("Changes in the toilet's position seen from house floor plans", title translated), a lecture at the 7th Sewage Culture Research Meeting, 15 November 2003, on the site of the Night Soil and Sewage Research Society (屎尿・下水研究会) (in Japanese; https://sinyoken.sakura.ne.jp/caffee/cayomo016.htm)

What it is: The text of a lecture, with floor plans, tracing where the toilet sat in Japanese houses by type: a Meiji-Taisho tenement, a detached middle-class house, an old tenement with a shared toilet, a merchant house, a samurai house and a farmhouse.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports three forms for a premodern setting: the farmhouse toilet under the eave or in a roofed outbuilding with no toilet inside the main house (it also shows a snow-country chumon-zukuri plan with a toilet just inside the entrance), the samurai house with separate upper and lower toilets (quoted from Aramata Hiroshi's popular history of the toilet), and the Edo back-row tenement with a shared toilet, well and rubbish heap on the alley (the memoir it rests on puts the toilet midway along the row and the well at the alley's end), while merchant houses had a back gate for night-soil collection. Most of its accounts are memoirs of Tokyo in the late Meiji to early Showa years and of a northern Nagano farm in the late Meiji, and the Edo tenement and samurai plans are inferred by resemblance (the text says the Edo plan is "thought to be similar"); the pre-Edo claim is quoted from another author.
