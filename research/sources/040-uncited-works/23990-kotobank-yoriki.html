kotobank-yoriki

"与力" ("yoriki", the Edo magistracy's senior police and judicial officer, title translated), dictionary entries from Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/与力-146520)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering eight dictionary and encyclopedia entries, with excerpts from the old Heibonsha encyclopedia, on yoriki, the term for a lower warrior attached to a commander in the Muromachi and Sengoku periods and for the officer assisting a magistrate in the Edo shogunate. The longest is a signed Heibonsha entry on the city magistrate's yoriki of Edo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Edo magistracy's staffing in numbers: 25 yoriki to each of Edo's two city magistrates from 1719, of whom 23 were shogunal retainers and 2 the magistrate's own men, each stipended at 200 koku (150 to 200 in the Nipponica; the Yamakawa dictionary says the form of the stipend varied), with doshin under them. It also names the divisions they ran (general administration with finance and personnel, trials, jail rounds, fire companies, Koishikawa infirmary) and says they lived in compounds at Hatchobori of 250 to 300 tsubo. Those figures are Edo's alone: of the outlying magistracies (Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, Yamada) the page says only that they usually had yoriki and doshin in sections for trials and police, and gives no numbers; the Sengoku sense is a different post and the list says nothing about a provincial town's smaller bench.
