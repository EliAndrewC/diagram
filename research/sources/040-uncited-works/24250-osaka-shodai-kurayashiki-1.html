osaka-shodai-kurayashiki-1

Osaka University of Commerce Museum of Commercial History (大阪商業大学 商業史博物館), "蔵屋敷 1" ("kurayashiki 1", domain storehouse compounds, part 1; title translated), in its "Walking the Edo-period town" (江戸時代の町を歩けば) lecture series on early modern Osaka commerce (in Japanese; http://moch.daishodai.ac.jp/tanbou/kurayashiki01_01.html)

What it is: The first part of a museum's transcribed lecture on the kurayashiki, the Osaka compounds where domains and the shogunate's retainers sold their rice and other goods, working through an 1806 map that places the Kurume, Hiroshima and Fukuoka compounds, and the Kurume domain's own pictures of its Osaka compound. It covers where they stood along the rivers, how boats landed the rice, how it was stacked and dried, and how it was stored.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how a domain's rice reached a storehouse compound in a river city: boats poled into a private boat-slip, rice stacked on the landing in piles of 28 bales and dried there about three days under watch, then carried by ladder into the storehouses, with neighboring plots rented when the compound's own storehouses ran short. It says about seven in ten of Osaka's compounds lay along the rivers, and that a late Edo picture (1867, Keio 3) shows 36 bales a pile where tradition set 28, which the speaker puts down to lack of space. It is a spoken lecture about one city, the landing, drying and storing it describes are read from the Kurume domain's pictures alone, and the drawings are not reproduced on the page.
