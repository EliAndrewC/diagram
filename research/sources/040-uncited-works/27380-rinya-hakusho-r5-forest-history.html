rinya-hakusho-r5-forest-history

Forestry Agency (林野庁), "第1部 特集 第1節 森林資源の利用と造成の歴史(1)" ("Part 1 special feature, section 1: the history of use and creation of forest resources (1)", title translated), in the Annual Report on Forest and Forestry in Japan, fiscal 2023 (令和5年度 森林・林業白書), on the Forestry Agency site (in Japanese; https://www.rinya.maff.go.jp/j/kikaku/hakusyo/r5hakusyo_h/all/tokusyu1_1.html)

What it is: A government white-paper section, issued June 2024 by Japan's Forestry Agency, summarizing the history of forest use in Japan from prehistory to the Meiji era. It covers the original mixed forest, felling of cedar and cypress from the Nara period, Edo-period forest restrictions and plantation forestry, and Meiji deforestation and the 1897 Forest Act.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says plantation forestry in cedar (sugi) and cypress began around the start of the Edo period in the Kitayama (Yamashiro), Yoshino (Yamato) and Tenryu (Totomi) districts, spreading to other river-rafting districts to supply cities, after disasters from ruined forests led the shogunate and domains to close forests (tomeyama) and replant. It gives two contrasting spacings: Yoshino planted cedar densely, about 10,000 per hectare, and thinned repeatedly to yield logs of many sizes and fine-grained cask wood, while Obi domain planted sparsely for large ship timbers. These are summary statements for named districts, not measurements. The page's footnoted books support only its account of the prehistoric forest; the passage on Edo planting cites nothing, and the Yoshino density is given without a date, in a passage on forestry districts that continue to the present, so it may describe later practice rather than the Edo period's; the page gives no date for each district's start beyond the period, and it says nothing of village woodlots.
