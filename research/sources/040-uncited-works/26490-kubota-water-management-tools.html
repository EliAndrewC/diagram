kubota-water-management-tools

Kubota Corporation, "桶や水車を使って行った「水の管理」" ("Water management with buckets and water-lifting wheels", title translated), from 稲作の歴史とそれを支えた伝統農具, on Kubota no Tanbo (in Japanese; https://www.kubota.co.jp/kubotatanbo/history/tools/water-management.html)

What it is: A short company educational page on watering a paddy through the growing season and the lifting tools used: the bucket, the sprinkling bucket, the dragon-bone-chain pump and the treadle paddle-wheel, with their sizes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the proverb that water watching is half the harvest, says water is needed most from mid-July to late August, and that water ran from channels and ponds on high ground down to lower paddies in turn, with a chain pump or treadle wheel needed to lift river water up. It dates the dragon-bone pump only to the Middle Ages and the treadle wheel not at all, while Kubota's own page on the Muromachi to Edo periods lists both among the tools invented in the Edo period. Its mid-July to late-August peak is stated in the present tense, for today's growing calendar, and its tool sizes are of the pieces pictured, credited to a book published in Akita (渡部景俊, 農を支えて).
