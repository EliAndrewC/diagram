kameyama-minzoku-nominka

Kameyama City (亀山市), "１ 母屋の間取り ① 農家" ("1 Layout of the main house, (1) farmhouses", title translated), in the Kameyama City History folklore survey, section 3 Dwelling (民俗 三「住」(二)母屋) ("Folklore 3, dwelling, 2: the main house"; title translated), on the Kameyama History Museum site (in Japanese; https://kameyamarekihaku.jp/sisi/minzokuhp/jirei/bunrui3/data3-2/index3_2_1_1.htm)

What it is: A city-history folklore chapter on the rooms of a farmhouse's main house in Kameyama, Mie, from interviews in the old villages: the four-room field-character plan, the guest rooms, bedroom, living room, earthen floor, bath, cooking stove, firewood store and the cattle stall.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the room names and uses of a farmhouse of the area: four rooms of 6 or 8 mats, the oku guest room with the Buddhist altar kept closed, a cattle stall of about 4.5 mats opening off the earthen floor with a floor dug down (about 30 cm in one household) and manure cleared out to ferment, about 100 bundles of brushwood stored a year. Its informants whose birth years it gives were born between 1925 and 1935 and the practices it describes run to the 1950s, so it records the farmhouse as its informants knew it, from about the 1930s to the 1950s, in the Kameyama city area of Mie, already lit by electric light and kerosene and with a radio in the living room, not the form in the setting's period.
