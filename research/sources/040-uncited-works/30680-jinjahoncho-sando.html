jinjahoncho-sando

Jinja Honcho (Association of Shinto Shrines), "参道について" ("About the approach path", title translated), in the section on visiting a shrine, on the association's site, page dated 1 July 2024 (in Japanese; https://jinjahoncho.or.jp/omairi/sandou)

What it is: A short explanatory page from the national association of Shinto shrines. It defines the sando as the road from the precinct's torii to the shrine building and explains the custom of walking beside the center of the path, which is the way of the kami.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the definition of the approach (from torii to hall) and says that some worshippers avoid the center line, called seichu, as the kami's path, following the priests' own practice in rites, and bow if they must cross it, adding that there is no strict rule. It states present-day etiquette only, with no date, no region and no length, width or paving, so it cannot say how approaches were laid out or when the custom began.
