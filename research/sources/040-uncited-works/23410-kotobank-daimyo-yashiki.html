kotobank-daimyo-yashiki

Matsuzaki Kinichi and Fujino Tamotsu, "大名屋敷" ("daimyo yashiki", the daimyo's residences in Edo, title translated), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典) and in Nipponica (日本大百科全書), with entries from the Daijisen and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A4%A7%E5%90%8D%E5%B1%8B%E6%95%B7-92055)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering seven dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the daimyo's residences in Edo, from a one-line dictionary gloss to a signed Heibonsha entry. It covers the upper, middle and lower residences, where each stood, who lived in them, and how much of Edo they filled.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the system of three residences after the Meireki fire of 1657 (upper near the castle, middle along the inner edge of the outer moat, lower in the suburbs, the lower with large gardens or river-side storehouses), and puts figures on it: for the Hikone Ii house in the mid-eighteenth century, an upper residence of 19,685 tsubo, a middle of 14,175 and a suburban villa of 174,795, and by the Tenmei era 265 daimyo upper residences and 466 middle and lower ones, with warrior land close to 70 percent of Edo (and one dictionary says over 50 percent of its area from the mid-period). All of it is the shogun's capital under the alternate-attendance system, for one of the richest houses; it says nothing about a domain's own castle town, and its dates run from 1635 to the end of the Edo period.
