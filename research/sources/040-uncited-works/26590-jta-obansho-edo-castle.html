jta-obansho-edo-castle

Kikuba Cultural Association (for the Japan Tourism Agency), "大番所" ("Obansho Guardhouse", title translated), in the Regional Tourism Resources Multilingual Explanation Database, 2018 (in Japanese; https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/H30-00471.html)

What it is: A short signboard text, in Japanese and English, on the Obansho, one of three guardhouses left at Edo Castle, standing behind the Naka no Mon. It says what the building was for, who garrisoned it and how it was used after 1868.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that the Obansho was the final checkpoint for people entering the honmaru of Edo Castle and was garrisoned by samurai of relatively high rank, which supports a guardhouse inside the last gate of a major castle. The text is under 250 words, gives no dimensions, number of guards or plan, and speaks only of Edo Castle, a shogunal castle, so it says nothing of a provincial lord's castle; the building was altered in the 19th century and returned to its original look in 1968.
