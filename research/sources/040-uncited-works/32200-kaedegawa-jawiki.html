ja.wikipedia 楓川 ("Kaedegawa", the Kaede River, a filled-in canal in Chuo ward, Tokyo, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/楓川)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Kaede River, a canal about 1.2 km long in Chuo ward, Tokyo, dug in 1612 and filled in during the 1960s for an expressway, with its history, its names and its bridges.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how an early Edo canal beside reclaimed land fixed a district's use: lumber wholesalers moved to its west bank in 1606, boat-office officials' residences stood on the east, and a second fish market was licensed in 1674, so it supports a waterfront planned around timber and fish. It is a single Edo canal and capital, and most of its dated detail on widths and boats is from the Meiji and Taisho periods, not the early period.
