en.wikipedia "Buffer stock scheme" (the article that the Ever-normal granary address redirects to; title as shown on the page) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ever-normal_granary)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on buffer stock schemes, reached by redirect from 'Ever-normal granary': it explains price-stabilizing storage in general and has a history section on China's granaries from 54 BC through the Qing, then the United States and the European Union.

Why it applies, and its limits: Only its 'Ever-normal granaries' subsection touches the setting: it says the first was built in 54 BC, that the system ran in the Han, Jin, Sui and Tang dynasties, and that the Qing held 2.2-3.3 million tonnes of grain. The rest is modern economics and EU storage with no bearing on a premodern map, and its Qing figures, including the claim that over 100 million lives were saved, rest on one encyclopedia of Chinese history (Dillon 2016). The Qing tonnage is an empire-wide total, and the article gives no granary's size, buildings or place in a town.
