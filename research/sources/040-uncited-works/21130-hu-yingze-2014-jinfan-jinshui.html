Hu Yingze, "晋藩与晋水：明代山西宗藩与地方水利" ("The Jin princes and the Jin River: Ming Shanxi princely houses and local irrigation", title translated), Zhongguo Lishi Dili Luncong 2014 no. 2, as posted by the Center for Chinese Social History, Shanxi University (in Chinese; http://hjs.rccsh.sxu.edu.cn/slshsyj/yjdt/135825.htm)

What it is: The full text of a journal article on how Ming princely estates in Shanxi took over local irrigation, chiefly on the Jin River at Taiyuan, with cases from Yuci, Jiangzhou and Fenzhou and the Song-era division of the Jin spring's water.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows irrigation as shared by turns in a Ming-Qing north-China setting, with named day-counts, water divided by tenths (seven to the north channel, three to the south, in the Ming), and an estate holding priority; it gives estate areas in qing (for example 7,203 qing of Jin princely land surveyed in 1582). It is about the north (millet and wheat country with some rice), about a privilege held by princes and not by ordinary villages, and rests on stele texts and county gazetteers it cites by footnote.
