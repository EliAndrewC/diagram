"水利" ("shuili", water management), section of "乃粒" ("nai li", the grains) in 《天工开物》 (Tiangong kaiwu, title translated: The Exploitation of the Works of Nature), on the 5000yan classics site, with a modern Chinese rendering (in Chinese; https://tiangong.5000yan.com/naili/34916.html)

What it is: A web page giving the classical Chinese text of the water-management section of the grain chapter of the Tiangong kaiwu, with a modern Chinese translation and figure captions, on a site that publishes annotated classics. It covers how paddy is kept watered in drought: the river-powered waterwheel (筒车), the ox-driven and treadle chain-pump (龙骨水车), the short hand-cranked 拔车, the wind-driven pumps of Yangzhou (which, it says, drain flooded fields for planting and do not serve in drought), and the swape and windlass, with how much paddy each can water (the swape and windlass least of all).

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the forms and labor rates of lifting water onto paddy: the text says a wheel on a weir-fed river runs day and night for a hundred mu, one person with a chain-pump waters five mu a day and an ox ten, and a hand-cranked 拔车 two mu. The page gives no date for the book and names no author, and its one place is Yangzhou, so it cannot say how widespread these devices were or where; the rates carry no soil or lift. The modern rendering is the site's own: where the original says only that "this cart" (此车) drains flooded fields, set after the Yangzhou wind pumps, the rendering names it the hand-cranked 拔车; and the page reports 0 corrections made to this text (本文已校订 0 处).
