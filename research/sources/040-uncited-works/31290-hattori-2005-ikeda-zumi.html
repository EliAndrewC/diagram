hattori-2005-ikeda-zumi

Hattori Tamotsu, Minamiyama Noriko and Matsumura Toshikazu, "猪名川上流域の池田炭と里山林の歴史" ("Ina-gawa jōryūiki no Ikeda-zumi to satoyama-rin no rekishi", The history of Ikeda charcoal and the fuelwood forest in the upper Ina River basin, title translated), Vegetation Science (植生学会誌) 22(1): 41-51, 2005, Society of Vegetation Science, on J-STAGE (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/vegsci/22/1/22_KJ00005989601/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: The full text of a peer-reviewed article, with English abstract, on the Ikeda charcoal made from coppiced kunugi oak (Quercus acutissima) in the upper Ina River basin of Hyogo and Osaka, north of Osaka Bay. It reads 29 Edo-period books, local-history documents and traditions, and the Enpo land-survey registers of 32 villages (1670s), then compares them with the surviving forests.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated, place-specific evidence for how a charcoal-producing coppice was run before Meiji: kunugi stands planted rather than wild, cut in rotation on a cycle of 7-10 years, with pollarded "daiba" kunugi, and 421 ha of kunugi-bearing land recorded in the 1670s registers, concentrated in a few named villages. Its region is one famous tea-ceremony charcoal district, so the cycle and the pollarding speak for that district first; the article itself notes that the 7-10 year cycle is far shorter than the 20-30 year average it cites for other regions, and that the 1950s stand distribution had spread somewhat beyond the 1670s one.
