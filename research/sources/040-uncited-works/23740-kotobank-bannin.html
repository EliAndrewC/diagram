kotobank-bannin

"番人" ("bannin", watchman, title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%95%AA%E4%BA%BA-607139)

What it is: A short dictionary page defining the bannin, a person who stands watch, with a Nihon Kokugo Daijiten sense that links it to the ban-ta, the town and village watchman of the Edo period, and a note on a Meiji-era police post of the same name.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the term for a watchman posted at roads and places where people gather, quoting a 1665 text that urges posting bannin at every entrance and crossing, and an 1837 text that names the street-gate keepers ban-ta and bannin. It gives no numbers, wages or duties for a village watchman, and its third sense (Tokyo police, 1873-75) is a modern office that does not apply.
