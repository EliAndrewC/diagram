shanghai-daily-siheyuan-2014

Shanghai Daily, "Traditional dwellings designed with care", 19 August 2014, reposted on China.org.cn (http://www.china.org.cn/travel/2014-08/19/content_33276463.htm)

What it is: A newspaper travel piece on the Beijing siheyuan courtyard house, describing its four-sided plan, the placement and use of the main, side and opposite houses, the gate and screen wall, and the drum-shaped gate stones.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says which building each family member occupied by how much sun it receives (the north main house for the head of the family, the east and west side houses for sons, the south opposite house, which gets least sun, for servants, daughters behind the main house) and that the street door is set at the southeast corner. It says the form dates back more than 3,000 years yet also that archaeology shows early courtyard houses lacked buildings on all four sides until the Qing (1644-1911), so its plan is the Qing one, and the surviving houses it names are Beijing's.
