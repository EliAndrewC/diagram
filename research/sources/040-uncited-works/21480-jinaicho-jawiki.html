ja.wikipedia "寺内町" ("jinai-cho", the temple-precinct town, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%AF%BA%E5%86%85%E7%94%BA)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the jinaicho, the self-governing towns built around a Buddhist temple (chiefly Jodo Shinshu) in late medieval and early modern Japan, with a prefecture-by-prefecture list of examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the whole town counted as temple precinct, and that most were ringed by a moat or earthen bank and held the temple, clergy quarters and believers' town lots, with the right to try its own offenses and lower dues won from the lord - a form of fortified, self-ruled town from the late medieval Sengoku era into the early 1600s, distinct from a monzenmachi. It gives no street grid, lot size or population for any one town, and its examples are concentrated in the Kinai, Hokuriku, Mikawa and Ise, where the Honganji was strong.
