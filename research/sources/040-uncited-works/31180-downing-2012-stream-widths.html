downing-2012-stream-widths

J. A. Downing, J. J. Cole, C. M. Duarte, J. J. Middelburg, J. M. Melack, Y. T. Prairie, P. Kortelainen, R. G. Striegl, W. H. McDowell and L. J. Tranvik, "Global abundance and size distribution of streams and rivers" (supplementary information), supplementary electronic material to the article in Inland Waters 2, pp. 229-236, DOI 10.5268/IW-2.4.502, International Society of Limnology, 2012 (https://mobile.reginfo.gov/public/do/eoDownloadDocument?eodoc=true&documentID=7716)

What it is: The supplementary tables to a 2012 limnology paper: Table 1 lists the mean wetted width in meters of several hundred stream and river segments around the world, each with its Strahler order, location, coordinates and the study it came from, and Tables 2 and 3 combine width, length and count by stream order for the contiguous United States and for Africa. A reference list of the underlying studies follows.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured channel widths by Strahler order, so it can anchor how wide a first- to fifth-order stream runs (its Table 2 gives mean widths of 0.8 m for first order, 3.7 m for third and 29.3 m for fifth) wherever a map needs a stream's width for its order. Its rows are almost all North American, with others from Australia, New Zealand, Europe, Brazil, Belize and Africa, and the only East Asian row is one Hong Kong forest stream (order 3, 10 m); it has no Japanese or Chinese river, and its widths are of natural channels estimated mostly from satellite images or from the long experience of the underlying studies' authors, so they leave out the banks, levees, weirs and diversions of a worked paddy valley.
