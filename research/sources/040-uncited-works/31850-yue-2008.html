Yue Yongyi (岳永逸), "传统民间文化与新农村建设——以华北梨区庙会为例" ("Traditional folk culture and the building of the new countryside: the temple fairs of North China's pear district as an example", title translated), Society (社会) 28(6), 2008, pp. 176-193, on html.rhhz.net (in Chinese; https://html.rhhz.net/society/html/20080610.htm)

What it is: The full text of a sociology journal article on the village temple fairs of a pear-growing district of Hebei east of Shijiazhuang, based on the author's field research from 1999. It describes the old village temples of the district (laomu, Guandi, Sanguan, Zhenwu, Yuhuang and Wudao temples), how three fairs are explained and run, and how officials treated them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that until the 1920s-30s nearly every village in the district had a simple temple of one of those gods, that such temples marked a village's boundary and landscape and each had its own fair, and it names the posts and committees that ran one fair. Its field data are from 1999-2008 and its argument is about modern policy toward folk religion, so the temple list for the early twentieth century rests on the author's summary and two cited works, and nothing here is a premodern measurement or layout.
