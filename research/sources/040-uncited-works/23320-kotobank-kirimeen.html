kotobank-kirimeen

"切目縁" ("kirimeen", a veranda of boards laid across the building, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%88%87%E7%9B%AE%E7%B8%81)

What it is: A Kotobank dictionary page defining the kirimeen, a veranda whose boards run at right angles to the building so that their end grain shows, against the kurēn, whose boards run parallel to it. It includes encyclopedia passages on shrine and temple construction and on the sunoko.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells which way the boards of a veranda run and that the end-grain form was the finer of the two; the shrine and temple passage says the end-grain form was the commoner there. The earliest dated use is a text of about 1727, and the page gives no dimensions or regions, so it fixes a drawing convention for a veranda's boards and nothing about its width.
