kotobank-deshoku

"出職" ("deshoku", a trade worked away from home, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%87%BA%E8%81%B7)

What it is: A short Kotobank dictionary page defining deshoku, a trade practiced at the customer's site (plasterers, steeplejacks, gardeners), as the opposite of ijoku, a trade worked at home. It adds an encyclopedia passage on itinerant village blacksmiths.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a division between craftsmen who work at a shop and those who go out to the job, and says that blacksmiths who made hoes and sickles often traveled seasonal rounds through farm villages, each with a territory of their own. The only dated example quotes a 1928 book, and the encyclopedia passage gives no region or period, so it cannot say how many such smiths a given village had.
