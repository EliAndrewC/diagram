ja.wikipedia 浅草寺 ("Sensoji", the Asakusa Kannon temple in Tokyo, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/浅草寺)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Sensoji, Tokyo's oldest temple, in Asakusa, Taito ward: its founding legend of 628, its history through the Kamakura, Edo and modern periods, its gates, halls, pagoda and subsidiary halls, its festivals, its designated cultural properties and its archaeology.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the layout of one great Edo-period temple precinct: a gate at the head of an approach road about 250 m long to the inner gate, stalls along that road from about 1685 (shed-shops set at the eaves of the sub-temples; the present shop rows are reinforced concrete of 1925), a pagoda, a main hall, a shrine to its east and a ferry-landing hall on the Sumida River. The main hall, pagoda and inner gate are reinforced concrete rebuilt after the 1945 air raid (the outer gate, lost in 1865, was rebuilt in 1960, and the east gate of 1618 survives), the page dates the oldest extant building to the Muromachi period, the pagoda was rebuilt in 1973 west of the main hall where the earlier one stood to its east, and the ferry-landing hall, rebuilt in 2003, now faces the street rather than the river, and its founding account is a legend traced to the temple's own chronicle; a metropolitan temple of the shogun's capital is not a village or provincial temple.
