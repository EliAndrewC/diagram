en.wikipedia "Landed gentry in China" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landed_gentry_in_China)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the scholar-official gentry of imperial China, covering how passing the imperial examinations made them eligible for office and how they held their wealth in land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says gentry status was not inherited but in practice came to families able to educate their sons, and that gentry retired to estates living on tenants' rents, which suits a landed local elite in a Chinese-model district. It is a short general account across the Tang to Qing examination era, with no figures, and cites Chang Chung-li's 1955 study of the nineteenth-century gentry only for its sentence that land preserved their wealth; its account of gentry retiring to estates on tenants' rents carries no citation.
