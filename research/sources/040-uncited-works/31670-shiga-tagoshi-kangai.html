shiga-tagoshi-kangai

Shiga Prefecture (滋賀県), "(3) 田越しかんがいと一筆内みずすましほ場" ("(3) tagoshi kangai to ippitsunai mizusumashi hojo", Field-to-field irrigation and the in-plot water-strider paddy, title translated), p. 19 of a prefectural document (in Japanese; https://pref.shiga.lg.jp/file/attachment/1009952.pdf)

What it is: One page of a Shiga Prefecture document describing two water-saving methods for paddies: tagoshi irrigation, in which water passes from one field to the next lower one, and a strip left unplanted at a field's drain.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains that in tagoshi irrigation water used in a field falls into the next lower field instead of a drain and is used there for puddling, which saves water and keeps muddy water out of the drains, that work must go from the top field down by agreement of the whole hamlet, and that a drop of at least 10 cm between fields is needed. The page is a modern environmental-farming guide, mentions tractors, and says nothing of how old or how common the method was, so it supports how the method works and not its history.
