ja.wikipedia コナラ ("konara", the Japanese konara oak, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B3%E3%83%8A%E3%83%A9)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the konara oak (Quercus serrata), covering its form, ecology, range, timber and uses, and its place in the Musashino coppice woods.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says konara grows 15 to 20 m tall, lives in village-edge woods and below 1,000 m on Honshu, sprouts strongly from the stump so it can be cut again about every 20 years for fuel, and is used for firewood, charcoal and mushroom logs; it says the acorns need leaching, and that Tohoku mountain villages ate them into the 20th century. Its uses are described chiefly by present-day practice and its examples of charcoal regions are northern Japan, so it supports the tree's habit and coppicing more than any Edo-period use.
