taito-imadoyaki

Taito Ward, "今戸焼作り" ("Making Imado Ware", title translated), in its list of living cultural properties, on the Taito ward website (in Japanese; https://www.city.taito.lg.jp/gakushu/shogaigakushu/shakaikyoiku/bunkazai/seikatubunkazai/imadoyaki.html)

What it is: A ward page on Imado ware, the pottery made at Imado in Edo (Tokyo) and nearby from the Edo to the Meiji period, and on its last maker in the ward, the Shirai family, a living cultural property designated in 2012. It lists the goods (roof tiles, household ware, clay dolls, crafts) and the steps of making clay dolls.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that Edo's Imado potters made tiles, everyday utensils, dolls and crafts for the city's inhabitants, that there were over fifty such houses at the end of the Edo period (citing a pottery treatise), and that a pair of stone guardian dogs at the local shrine were donated by potters. The figure is quoted from another work with no date, the page covers one trade in one district of Edo, and after the decline it describes only a single present-day workshop, with no information on kilns, lots or workshop layout.
