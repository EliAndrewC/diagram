zh.wikipedia 灵隐寺 ("Lingyin si", Lingyin Temple, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/灵隐寺)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Lingyin Temple beside the West Lake in Hangzhou, founded in 326, with its history through the Five Dynasties, Qing and Taiping destruction, and a tour of its present halls and the Feilai Peak caves.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the temple reached nine towers, eighteen pavilions, seventy-seven halls and three thousand monks under the Wuyue king of the Five Dynasties, and that it was burned in 1860 and the main hall rebuilt only by 1910, now 33.6 meters high with a 1956 statue. The page carries a banner asking for more sources and gives no source for the Wuyue figures, which differ from the English article's (72 halls, over 1300 dormitory rooms), and its present layout is the rebuilt one, so it speaks for a famed Jiangnan temple's scale and not a typical one.
