aizukanko-nisshinkan

Aizu-Wakamatsu Tourism Bureau (会津若松観光ビューロー), "會津藩校 日新館" ("Aizu Domain School Nisshinkan", title translated), in its official tourism guide Aizu-Wakamatsu Kanko Navi (in Japanese; https://www.aizukanko.com/spot/55)

What it is: The tourism guide's entry for Nisshinkan, the Aizu domain school founded in 1803 and now rebuilt as a museum in Kawahigashi, Aizu-Wakamatsu: its purpose, its pupils, its grounds and facilities, and visitor details.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the school's founding year (1803), the entry age for a retainer's son (10), a site of about 8,000 tsubo (about 26,400 square meters) holding martial-arts halls, an observatory and a swimming pond, and says the present buildings are a faithful restoration. The size is that of one large domain's flagship school. The page does not say that the original stood beside the castle and burned in 1868, or that the present school was rebuilt in 1987 at Kawahigashi from surviving plans, so it shows the old plan but not the old site or its fabric. It is an upper bound for a domain school and not a typical one.
