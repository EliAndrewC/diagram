jsidre-heiya-shinden

Japanese Society of Irrigation, Drainage and Rural Engineering, "平野の拡張，新田開発" ("Heiya no kakucho, shinden kaihatsu", Expanding the plains: new-field development, title translated), in its series on the history of regional development (地域づくりの歴史) (in Japanese; https://www.jsidre.or.jp/tabata3-a/)

What it is: A page of a professional society's reading series on rural history, summarizing Edo-period shinden (new-field) development: its three peaks, the settlement forms it produced, and the technology behind it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the peaks as the Meireki-Kanbun years (about 1660), the Kyoho years (about 1720) and the Tenpo-Ansei years (about 1840), says new-field reclamation was mostly upland clearing and lake and marsh drainage in eastern Japan but tideflat reclamation in western Japan, and ties it to row villages and dispersed farmsteads laid out with their fields by a planning will. It gives no figures for any one village or plot, and it sketches Japan as a whole in a few paragraphs.
