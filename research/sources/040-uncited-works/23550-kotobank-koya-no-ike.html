kotobank-koya-no-ike

Futaki Toshiatsu, "昆陽池" ("Koya no ike", Koya Pond, title translated), in the Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei (日本歴史地名大系), in Nipponica (日本大百科全書) and in the older Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, with entries from the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Daijisen Plus, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%98%86%E9%99%BD%E6%B1%A0-1320620)

What it is: A Kotobank page of seven entries on Koya Pond, an irrigation reservoir in Itami, Hyogo, said to have been dug by the monk Gyoki in the Nara period, with its place on the Itami plateau and later history.

Why it applies, and its limits: The place-name entry says that around 731 Gyoki built here, by the Gyoki chronicle, an upper and lower pond with channels and a lodging, one of five ponds on a depression of the plateau; it stored water for the south-west and carried off plateau runoff so that it would not overflow south, and that the ponds were a complete scheme for opening the Ina plain. Nipponica says the plateau holds many ponds and paddies, but that the tie of the present pond to Gyoki's is unclear, and the Daijisen Plus says the lower pond was filled in during the Keicho era. The attribution to Gyoki is by tradition, the pond's circumference of about 4 km is the modern figure, and the page gives no depth or the area it watered.
