ja.wikipedia 豆腐 ("tofu", bean curd, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%B1%86%E8%85%90%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on tofu, covering its names, its disputed origin in China, its spread to Japan, Okinawa and Korea, the Japanese kinds and coagulants, the steps of making it, and its dishes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says tofu reached Japan by the Kamakura period, spread across the country in the Muromachi period and was an ordinary food by the Edo period, when, it says, the only kind was the firm momen type, though it also says Edo's Sasa no Yuki first sold silken tofu there in the Genroku years (the page contradicts itself here), and Edo's magistrate's office capped its price. It also says itinerant tofu sellers worked the streets and that a Tochigi domain's meal records show tofu dishes on nearly every day of a month; but its kinds, coagulant and factory sections describe the twentieth-century industry, and its hawker's horn dates only to the early Meiji period.
