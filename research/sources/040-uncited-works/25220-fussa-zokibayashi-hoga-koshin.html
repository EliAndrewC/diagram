fussa-zokibayashi-hoga-koshin

Fussa City, "雑木林の萌芽更新" ("Sprout regeneration of the mixed woodland", title translated), on the Fussa City (Tokyo) website (in Japanese; https://www.city.fussa.tokyo.jp/life/city/park/1005574/1002248.html)

What it is: A municipal page, updated in 2016, explaining coppice regeneration of the mixed woodland (zokibayashi) of Fussa in the Tama area, why the city now cuts its woods again, and what the old woods were for.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that cutting a broadleaf trunk makes many shoots grow from the stump, which was how woods were repeatedly renewed for thin wood for firewood and charcoal, that cutting is done in the winter dormant season at the ground at a slant, that fallen leaves fed the fields, and that mulberry was pollarded and, in part of Tama, zelkova branches grew for seaweed-farm stakes. It dates the end of the use to the 1960s and gives no cutting cycle, stump age or yield; it is a conservation page of one Tokyo suburb, so the custom is told in outline and not as the rotation a village practiced.
