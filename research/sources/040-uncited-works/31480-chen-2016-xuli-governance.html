chen-2016-xuli-governance

Chen Zhonghai, "古代的“胥吏治国”" ("Governing the state through clerks in ancient China", title translated), part 1, China Development Observation (中国发展观察), on the Communist Party News site of People's Daily Online, 28 July 2016 (in Chinese; https://theory.people.com.cn/big5/n1/2016/0728/c83865-28591848.html)

What it is: A magazine article, reproduced on a party news portal, on the xuli, the unranked clerks and runners of Chinese government from the Han to the Qing. It sets their numbers against the ranked officials, describes their clerical and legal work, and says why officials, who changed posts often, depended on them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It can back the general picture that a Chinese county or ministry was run day to day by a large body of unranked clerks, far outnumbering the officials, who held posts briefly. It names the period work behind some figures (the Tongdian for about 18,000 ranked officials against 350,000 clerks in the Tang, the Da Qing huidian shili for the clerks of each Qing ministry) but credits an average magistrate's tenure of 0.9 years by the 19th century only to "a scholar", with no page references, and it moves among dynasties from the Han to the late Qing; its one county-scale figure is a general one for the Song, four or five officials in a large county and two or three in a small one, against clerks from dozens to hundreds; it is the first half of a two-part article and says nothing of settlements or buildings.
