zh.wikipedia 灵谷寺 ("Linggu si", Linggu Temple, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/灵谷寺)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on Linggu Temple, a Buddhist temple on the southeast slope of Purple Mountain in Nanjing, with its founding in 514, its move in 1381 beside the Ming tomb, its layout by named part and the 1930s cemetery built on its old site.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the temple's history from a Liang foundation to the Ming relocation and says the Ming temple was large, over 500 mu, with most of it burned in the nineteenth-century wars; the one surviving Ming building, the beamless hall, is measured at 50 meters by 34 meters under a double-eave roof. Most of the named parts it lists (the tombs, the pagoda, the main gate) are of the 1930s and later, and no figure is given for the number of buildings or monks, so it describes a great imperial-patronage temple and not a village one.
