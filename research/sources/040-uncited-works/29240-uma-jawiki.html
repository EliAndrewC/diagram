ja.wikipedia ウマ ("uma", the horse, title translated; reached by redirect from 農耕馬, "nokoba", the farm horse) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/農耕馬)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the domestic horse, covering its biology, breeds, history of use, and Japan's horses from the Kofun period to the postwar decline, with a short section on the farm horse. The farm-horse section itself is a few lines on plow and harrow work beside mostly European and modern pictures.

Why it applies, and its limits: For Japan it says horses reached Japan from the Korean peninsula in the Kofun period, that a Tokugawa-period farm horse was kept for plowing, hauling timber and, secondarily, dung for the fields, that Japanese horses wore straw shoes (umagutsu) instead of iron shoes, and that a pack horse load was fixed at 40 kan (150 kg) or 36 kan (135 kg) to the da. It also says Edo-period horses ran 10-15 cm smaller than Heian ones, though the article marks the cause as uncertain. It gives no village-level numbers for how many horses a farm kept, and most of its farm-work material is Western or twentieth-century.
