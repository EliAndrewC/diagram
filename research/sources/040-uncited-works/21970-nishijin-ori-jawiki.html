ja.wikipedia 西陣織 ("Nishijin-ori", Nishijin brocade, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%99%A3%E7%B9%94)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Nishijin-ori, the dyed-thread silk weaving of Kyoto: its origin with the Hata clan, its rise after the Onin War, the weaving types, the Jacquard loom brought in 1872, and its use today.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places Kyoto's silk weaving in the Nishijin quarter, a weavers' guild (the Oatoneri za) that held the twill-weaving monopoly from 1513, and a peak in the Genroku and Kyoho years ended by the 1730 fire; this supports a Kyoto weaving quarter and its guild as a premodern form. The page itself flags missing citations, much of the text is on the present loom technology (the floppy-disk and USB loom control, the Expo pavilion), and it describes one famous quarter, not weaving in a provincial town.
