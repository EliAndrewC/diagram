kotobank-nureen

"濡縁" ("nureen", the weathered veranda, title translated), entries from Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha Mypedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%BF%A1%E7%B8%81)

What it is: A Kotobank page of two short dictionary entries on the nureen, a veranda on the outer side of the rain shutters, left exposed to the rain. The Mypedia entry names two ways of laying its boards, along the room's edge (kure-en) and at right angles to it (koguchi-en), and adds that the first was common in the houses of the high-born.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells how to draw a veranda beyond the rain shutters, and that the board direction differed with rank. Its earliest quotation is of the Muromachi period (1522-27), and it gives no width or house type, so it does not say how common the nureen was in a village house.
