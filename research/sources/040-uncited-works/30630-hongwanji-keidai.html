hongwanji-keidai

Hongwanji (Nishi Hongwanji, Kyoto), "境内と建造物" ("The precinct and its buildings", title translated), in the temple's website (in Japanese; https://hongwanji.kyoto/see/keidai.html)

What it is: The temple's own page on the buildings of its Kyoto precinct: the Founder's Hall and Amida Hall (rebuilt 1636 and 1760, with their dimensions), the gates, the drum tower, bell tower, wall, hand-washing pavilion and a ginkgo tree. Each is listed with its date and its designation as a national treasure or important cultural property.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the footprints and heights of two large Edo-period halls (the Founder's Hall 48 by 62 meters and 29 meters high, the Amida Hall 42 by 45 meters and 25 meters high) and says the drum tower's drum gave the time to the surrounding neighborhood in the Edo period. It is the head temple of a major sect in Kyoto, so its sizes are an upper bound rather than a village temple's, and many entries record later repairs and relocations (the main gate was moved three times) rather than the original state.
