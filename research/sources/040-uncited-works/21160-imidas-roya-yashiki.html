imidas-roya-yashiki

Yamamoto Hirofumi, "牢屋敷（ろうやしき）" ("Roya-yashiki, the jail compound", title translated), in the Jidaigeki yogo shinan (period-drama term guide), on imidas, 8 May 2008 (in Japanese; https://imidas.jp/jidaigeki/detail/L-57-018-08-04-G252.html)

What it is: A signed dictionary entry by a historian on the Edo jail compound at Kodenmacho, which held people awaiting judgment, with its cell divisions, its hereditary warden and its self-governing inmate officers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells how Edo's city jail was divided (a common cell, a cell for people without registration, one for samurai and priests, one for farmers), run by a hereditary warden under the city magistrate, with the inmates' own twelve officers led by the cell head. It describes the Edo city jail only, as the Edo magistrate's compound, so it does not say how a district or county jail was built or run, and it gives no sizes beyond a common cell of about 30 mats.
