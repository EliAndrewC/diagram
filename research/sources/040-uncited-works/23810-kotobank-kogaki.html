kotobank-kogaki

"紺搔" ("kogaki", the indigo dyer, title translated), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%B4%BA%E6%90%94)

What it is: A signed encyclopedia entry on the kogaki, the medieval name for an indigo dyer, with its origin in stirring the indigo vat, Muromachi dye prices and the trade's place in estates and towns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports that indigo dyeing was a trade of towns that also held land grants within estates in the medieval period, that it was often women's work through the early 1700s, and that it was the first dyeing trade to separate out. The dye prices (120 mon for asagi, a pale indigo blue, 150 for a pattern, which the entry says was cheaper than other dyes) are from one Muromachi text and no equivalent for a later period or place is given.
