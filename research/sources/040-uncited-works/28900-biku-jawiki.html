biku-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 比丘 ("biku", the bhikkhu or Buddhist monk, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/僧侶)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the bhikkhu, the fully ordained male Buddhist renunciant, reached from the redirect for sōryo (monk). It gives the term's origin as 'one who begs for food', the Pali and Sanskrit forms, the Zen name unsui for a wandering or training monk, and the note that Japan's traditional sects marry and pass temples down.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in Japan's traditional Buddhist sects, as in Newar and some Tibetan Buddhism, monks' marriage and hereditary succession are the norm and the precepts are not kept, which bears on temple households and succession. It states this as present practice, with no counts, dates or Japanese period, and the English Wikipedia article on Buddhist monasticism says clerical marriage was never widespread outside Jodo Shinshu until the Meiji government's Nikujiki Saitai law, so for a premodern temple the page supports a married, hereditary household only in a Jodo Shinshu temple; it says nothing of how many monks a temple housed.
