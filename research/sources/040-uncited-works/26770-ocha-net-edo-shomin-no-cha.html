ocha-net-edo-shomin-no-cha

World Green Tea Association, "江戸庶民の茶" ("Tea of the common people of Edo", title translated), in O-CHA NET, "Teach me TeaCha!" (in Japanese; https://www.o-cha.net/teacha/bunka/edo.html)

What it is: A short page of the tea association's website on how tea was drunk in Edo from the 17th to the 19th century, from whisked tea among the warrior, priestly and wealthy townspeople to the pan-fired and sun-dried teas of ordinary people, steamed sencha in the mid-18th century, gyokuro in the mid-19th, and the teahouses at gates and scenic spots.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the steamed-leaf sencha process to the mid-18th century (Nagatani Soen, 1681-1778, and tea farmers near Uji) and gyokuro to about the mid-19th century, and says teahouses stood at shrine and temple gates and along roads. It is a one-paragraph overview of Edo, listing three books as references, and does not say how common tea was in a country village or what a cup cost.
