mlit-nagayamon-kanazawa

"長屋門" ("nagayamon", the longhouse gate, title translated), interpretive-sign text for the Takada family's gate in Kanazawa, in the multilingual tourism-text database on the MLIT site (in Japanese; https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/common/001552178.pdf)

What it is: A one-page explanatory text on the nagayamon, the gatehouse of a samurai residence in Edo-period Kaga domain (Kanazawa), using the Takada family's gate as its example.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in Kaga a nagayamon, a gate with living rooms built into it, was permitted to middle-rank and higher samurai, that servants lived in it and watched who came and went, and that the Takada gate, thought to date from 1861 to 1864, has servants' quarters and a stable on either side of the gate. It also says the flanking stone walls were meant to be unworked natural fieldstone only but the rule was not kept, and that the restored gate's wall is faced with thin tortoise-shell stones to imitate cut stone; it is one Kanazawa gate, restored from old posts, and gives no dimensions.
