ntj-nagaya-rakugo

Japan Arts Council, "登場人物と庶民の暮らし：長屋の様子" ("Characters and the common people's lives: how a nagaya looked", title translated), in Bunka Digital Library, Popular Entertainment, Yose, "Rakugo: hayawakari" (in Japanese; https://www2.ntj.jac.go.jp/dglib/contents/learn/edc20/geino/rakugo/kurashi2.html)

What it is: A teaching page of a national performing-arts library describing the nagaya, the row tenement of the common townspeople, as it appears in rakugo stories: gate, alley, drain boards, shared toilet and well, landlord.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists the parts of a townsman's tenement: a wooden gate at the entrance opened in the morning and shut at night, two rows facing across an alley of about one meter with a drain board down the middle, a shared toilet with a door covering only the lower half, a shared well where the wives washed clothes, and the landlord living in a unit near the entrance. It does not name a city or date (the context is the Edo townsman of rakugo) and describes the tenement as the stories present it, not from a survey, and nothing about a village or a samurai's house.
