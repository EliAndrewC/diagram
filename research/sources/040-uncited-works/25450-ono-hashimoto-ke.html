ono-hashimoto-ke

Ono City, "重要文化財 旧橋本家住宅" ("Important Cultural Property: the former Hashimoto house", title translated), Ono City official website (in Japanese; https://www.city.ono.fukui.jp/kosodate/bunka-rekishi/shokai/hashimotoke.html)

What it is: A city page on the former Hashimoto house, a mid-Edo farmhouse in the mountains of Ono, Fukui, moved from the Hokyoji hamlet and restored in 1973, with its size, form, room names and structural timbers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a dimensioned 18th-century farmhouse of a headman's family: a thatched hip-and-gable roof, 6 ken across the front and 4 ken deep, a horse stall (umaya) about 2 shaku deep with a stone-walled pit just inside the door, a niwa with a hearth about 4 ken square taking two thirds of the floor area beside two board-floored rooms of about eight mats each, and a water room with a cut-stone sink and a water tank; it is of the type the page calls Echizen type 2. It is one snowy mountain house, restored in 1973 to what the city takes to be its original form; before that its floor was boarded everywhere except the entry (hakumonba), and both rooms had a veranda. The page gives no fields, grounds or outbuildings.
