kotobank-senzai

"前栽" ("senzai", the planted garden in front of a house, title translated), dictionary entries from Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia and Nippon Daihyakka Zensho, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/前栽)

What it is: A Kotobank page on senzai, the plantings in front of a house, which began as the herbs and flowers in a Heian aristocrat's garden and later covered all garden plantings. It also records the later sense of vegetables.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the front planting of a house as an old word with several senses: the garden before the hall of a noble house, the bedding between buildings (tsubo senzai), and in Nippon Daihyakka Zensho ordinary people growing vegetables at the house front, so that senzai came to mean greens. The Nihon Kokugo Daijiten adds that the vegetable sense was used in Edo and the garden sense in the Kamigata region. The page's evidence is largely a Heian court garden, so it says little on what a farm household grew in its yard.
