kotobank-shoryo

Britannica International Encyclopedia and Heibonsha Maypedia, "精霊" ("shoryo" and "seirei", the ancestral spirit and the spirit, title translated), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/精霊-79974)

What it is: A set of dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the word read shoryo (the ancestors' spirits welcomed at Bon) and seirei (a spirit that dwells in things), reproduced on Kotobank. The Britannica entry describes Japanese Bon customs; the seirei entries are about spirits in general, citing Tylor and the Nuer.

Why it applies, and its limits: The Britannica entry describes how households welcome the ancestors at Bon: welcome fires, a Bon shelf with offerings, a cleared path from the grave, and a spirit boat or offerings sent out to river or sea on the 15th or 16th, which supports the form of the rite. It gives no century or place for it beyond "many regions", and its seirei entries are comparative religion that says nothing of Japanese village practice.
