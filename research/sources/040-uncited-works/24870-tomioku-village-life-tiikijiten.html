tomioku-village-life-tiikijiten

"第十七章 村人の生活 (第一節 家々のくらし)" ("Chapter 17, Village life, section 1: life in the households", title translated), in a digital local history of Tomioku village (富奥, 郷土), on tiikijiten.jp (in Japanese; http://tiikijiten.jp/~digibook/tomioku_kyoudo/keitai.php?no=0017)

What it is: A chapter of a local history of Tomioku village in the Ishikawa district near Kanazawa, written from the memory of elders and from district records, on the daily round, clothing, food, household goods, house-raising, fuel and light, water, baths, privies and medicine of the village's farm households. It runs from the domain's time through the Meiji and Taisho eras to the postwar years.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a first-hand account of a Kaga plain village's households under the domain and just after: a daughter-in-law rising first to revive the banked fire, the pit privy set in the manure shed, water from the stream beside each kitchen and from a few shared wells (the page says the water table is too deep for a well per household, though its depth figure lost its unit), and the house-raising done by the neighbors and kin. Most of its detail is of the late Meiji to Showa years and it says so where it changes (lamps to electric light in 1921, simple waterworks about 1951-52), so each item has to be read for its date; it describes one flat, wood-poor district (fuel was mostly rice straw) and cannot speak for upland or coastal villages.
