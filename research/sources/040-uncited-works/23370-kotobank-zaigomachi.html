kotobank-zaigomachi

"在郷町" ("zaigomachi", a market town in the countryside, title translated), in the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Wakita Osamu), the Mypedia, the Yamakawa Japanese History Dictionary and the Britannica, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%9C%A8%E9%83%B7%E7%94%BA-67877)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering encyclopedia entries on the zaigomachi, a place that was legally a village in the Edo period but worked as a town. It gives the kinds (post station, port, specialty market, daimyo's jinya), their usual size and their rise from the late seventeenth century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the size of a country town in figures: most had 200 to 300 up to 1,000 households and 1,000 to 5,000 or 6,000 inhabitants, with a few near 10,000, and farmers lived in them as merchants held land. The figures are the entries' own summary for all of Japan and are not dated closer than the Genroku and Kyoho years, so a given region may differ; the page draws no layouts.
