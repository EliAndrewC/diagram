"农业经营思想" ("Nongye jingying sixiang", Ideas of farm management, title translated), in the agricultural history section of the encyclopedia on the 智汇三农 (Zhihui Sannong) agricultural knowledge service, China Agriculture Press, 2016-04-13 (in Chinese; https://pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/560481.shtml)

What it is: An unsigned encyclopedia entry on the farm-management thought in China's old agricultural treatises, from the Warring States to the Qing. It covers intensive farming, land-use planning, mixed farming and multiple cropping, planning, labor efficiency, costing and thrift, with passages quoted from Chen Fu's Nongshu (Song), Qimin yaoshu and the Shen family's Nongshu (Ming-Qing).

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a plain statement that small Chinese farms were worked intensively and diversified, and gives concrete rules from the treatises: Chen Fu's advice to dig a pond on about two or three mu in every ten of high paddy land and plant mulberry on its bank, and Zhang Lüxiang's plan for ten mu of poor land (3 mu mulberry, 3 mu beans, 2 mu bamboo, 2 mu fruit, a fish pond, five or six sheep). It is a summary of the writers' ideals, mostly of the Yangzi delta in the Song and Ming, written by and for landlords and prosperous farmers, so it is not a record of how ordinary farms were laid out, and the page names no author and cites no edition.
