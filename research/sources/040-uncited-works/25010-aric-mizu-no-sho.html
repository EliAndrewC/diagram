aric-mizu-no-sho

Agriculture and Rural Area Information Center (ARIC), "水の章" ("mizu no sho", the water chapter, title translated), in "水土里の軌跡" ("the trail of water, soil and village", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.aric.or.jp/kiseki/jp/mizu/index.html)

What it is: A web chapter by the ARIC, an agricultural-engineering information center, giving a history of Japanese water use for farming from the Jomon and Yayoi periods to the Heisei era, section by section. It covers paddy irrigation, weirs, reservoirs, flood works, the Edo-period new-field boom and modern dams.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries dated, named examples of premodern water works that a paddy settlement could draw on: the Toro site's paddy zone of about 10 ha with a combined supply-and-drain ditch, Edo-period works such as the Tamagawa aqueduct, which also fed new fields on the Musashino plateau, and the statement that cultivated land roughly doubled from 1.5 million ha to 3 million ha in the first 100 years of the Edo period. Its Edo coverage is a short summary of national and regional projects, and more than half the text concerns Meiji and later dams and engineering, which do not describe the setting; it gives no village-scale layouts.
