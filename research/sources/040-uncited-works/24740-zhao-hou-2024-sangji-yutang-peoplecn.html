Zhao Yu (赵玙) and Hou Nannan (侯楠楠), "二千多岁的桑基鱼塘仍未老……" ("The mulberry-dike fish ponds, over two thousand years old, are not yet old", title translated), Guangming Daily, 19 March 2024, reposted on People's Daily Online (in Chinese; http://society.people.com.cn/n1/2024/0319/c1008-40198302.html)

What it is: A newspaper feature on the mulberry-dike fish-pond system around Digang village in Nanxun District, Huzhou, Zhejiang: its claimed origin, its decline and revival, and a villager's yearly work calendar.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the cycle in one line (mulberry on the dikes, leaves to silkworms, silkworm droppings to the fish, fish waste to the pond, pond mud back to the mulberry), describes ponds cut into blocks by belts of mulberry, and gives a present-day villager's calendar from stocking fry in the first and second lunar months to catching fish from autumn. Its origin claims (2,500 years, mulberry on the dikes from the Five Dynasties, rapid growth in the Ming and Qing) are asserted with no source, and the landscape it reports is the 2020s heritage zone, with shorter bred mulberry and newer oil, fruit and vegetable dike variants.
