gunma-kinsei-jisha-jinja-60-110

Gunma Prefecture early-modern shrine and temple survey, "1. 本調査: 神社建築" entries 60-110 ("main survey: shrine architecture", entries 60-110, title translated), in 群馬県近世寺社総合調査報告書 - 歴史的建造物を中心に - 神社編 (Gunma Prefecture Comprehensive Survey of Early-Modern Shrines and Temples, focusing on historic buildings: shrine volume, title translated), on the Nara National Research Institute for Cultural Properties archive of excavation reports (in Japanese; https://sitereports.nabunken.go.jp/files/attach/45/45604/121895_3_群馬県近世寺社総合調査報告書－歴史的建造物を中心に－神社編.pdf)

What it is: One section of a Gunma Prefecture survey report on early-modern shrine buildings: the main survey's shrine entries 60 to 110 (about pages 151-254 of the printed report), one signed entry per shrine, covering shrines from Takasaki, Fujioka, Tomioka and Annaka to the Kanna and Ueno valleys and the Numata, Tone and Katashina uplands. Each entry gives a table of name, former rank, festivals and founding, a text and site plan of the approach and layout (torii, steps, haiden, honden, cover building), the shrine's history, and each building's plan with a table of date and its evidence (ridge tablet, ink inscription or style), builder, size in meters, foundation, roof and timber.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is detailed, building-by-building evidence for how Edo-period shrines in one Kanto mountain-edge province were laid out and built: honden only 0.60 m to about 5.7 m wide, dated mostly from the 1600s to the 1860s, with approaches of 10 to 83 steps, cover buildings, cedar groves and a few ties to a post-town or castle plan; precinct area is given only for Nukisaki (about 26,000 tsubo). It covers one prefecture and only the buildings that survive, seen in their present restored state (copper over shingle or thatch, cover buildings added in 1987 and 2019-20, Meiji mergers of 1906-1912); some entries are large patronized shrines (Nukisaki, Myogi) that are not village norms, founding dates are shrine tradition, the site plans are drawings the text extraction cannot show, and the plans of entries 84, 85 and 88 are garbled by OCR so some dimensions can be recovered only from the tables.
