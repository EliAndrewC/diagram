dudie-ordination-certificate-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 戒牒 ("jie die", the ordination certificate, title translated; the address 度牒 redirects to it) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/度牒)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the monk's certificate (度牒 under the officials, 戒牒 under the monasteries), with its history from the Southern and Northern Dynasties to the Qing, the materials it was made of in each dynasty, and the modern certificate issued by the Chinese Buddhist Association.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a Chinese monk or Daoist priest needing a state certificate to be ordained, which exempted the holder from tax and labor service, so that the court sold certificates in hard times; the first Ming emperor abolished the sale in 1373, the Jingtai reign restored it, and the Qianlong reign ended issue. It is about the paper and the policy, not a temple's ground or buildings, and much of its summary rests on one 2019 article by Deng Jiazhou in the Hong Kong Buddhist magazine Wennuan renjian, though its notes quote the Ming veritable records and the Ming and Qing statutes.
