shirai-edo-shobo-9

Shirai Kazuo (白井和雄), "江戸時代の消防事情⑨" ("Fire fighting in the Edo period, part 9", title translated), last installment of a lecture series in Fire Science and Information (消防科学と情報) no. 114, pp. 53-55 (in Japanese; https://isad.or.jp/pdf/information_provision/information_provision/no114/53p.pdf)

What it is: A journal article by a former director of the Tokyo Fire Department's fire museum on the three kinds of watch post in Edo: the tsujiban in samurai districts, and the kidoban (ward gates) and jishinban (neighborhood watch houses) in townsman districts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the gate and watch rules of Edo's townsman wards with dates: gates 2.5 ken (about 4.5 m) wide, closed at 10 pm, a side door for those on urgent business, with doctors, midwives and fire brigades let through at night, a 1661 order to keep about 60 water buckets at each pair of gates and 3 to 10 buckets per house by its frontage. It describes the capital city only, in the seventeenth century and after, and says nothing of a provincial town or a village, so it is a model for a large city's wards, not for a small settlement.
