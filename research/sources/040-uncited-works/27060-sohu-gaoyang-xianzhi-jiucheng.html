sohu-gaoyang-xianzhi-jiucheng

Gaoyang County Archives, "图说高阳之第一章千年古县（三）县治古今" ("Gaoyang in pictures, chapter 1, the thousand-year county, part 3: the county seat past and present", title translated), posted on Sohu (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/360020475_120209830)

What it is: A news-portal repost of a county archive's illustrated history of the county seat of Gaoyang in Hebei, covering the old earthen city, its move after a flood in 1370, the rebuilt layout, the county yamen and school, and the wall's building and repairs. It follows the wall to its demolition in 1938 and the town to 1990.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a dated sequence for one county seat: a wall of earth in 1461 with four gates and a moat 10 meters deep and 20 wide, raised in 1550, brick-topped in 1592, and rebuilt in brick in 1631. It also gives the yamen's order of halls and gates and the school's buildings, and says the new seat after 1370 copied the old street layout. Its early parts rest on legend (the town is said to have been built in the shape of a turtle), and the archive's sources are not named, so only the dated building record is usable evidence. Its Ming figures are given in modern meters with no original measures, so a figure such as the moat's 10 m depth cannot be checked against the record it came from.
