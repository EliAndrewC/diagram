John Lagerwey (劳格文) and David Faure (科大卫), eds., "中国乡村与墟镇神圣空间的建构" ("The construction of sacred space in Chinese villages and market towns", title translated), the preface and conclusion as excerpted on the WeChat account "社會學會社" and posted on Tencent News, 2024-09-27, from the volume of that title (Social Sciences Academic Press, 2014) (in Chinese; https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20240927a0aav600)

What it is: The preface and conclusion of an edited volume of eight village studies from Anhui, Shandong, Shanxi, Jiangxi, Hunan, Guangdong and Guangxi, reposted with an editor's introduction on feng shui; the editors' summary of each village covers its ancestral halls, temples and shrines, their founding dates and the festivals held at them. The repost states that the volume's notes and references were removed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in most of these villages the temples to the local protector gods stand at the village entrance or at the water mouth (shuikou, where a stream leaves the village), that one village's temples all stand outside it to guard it with their power, that ancestral halls came late (in the north often only after the lineage's first jinshi), and that a village's shrines and temples can be older than its lineage. The evidence is Ming and Qing steles, genealogies and fieldwork of about 2011 across seven provinces from north to south, often of a single village, and with the notes removed no claim on the page can be checked against its source.
