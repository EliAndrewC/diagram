Sugimoto Atsunori, "江戸時代大坂の茶屋・料理屋の分布" ("The distribution of teahouses and restaurants in Edo-period Osaka", title translated), Osaka Museum of History Research Bulletin (大阪歴史博物館 研究紀要) no. 22, March 2024, pp. 55-74, on J-STAGE (in Japanese; https://jstage.jst.go.jp/article/omhbull/22/0/22_55/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A museum-bulletin paper that maps the teahouses and restaurants of Osaka from nine published shopping guidebooks of 1679 to 1846, counting teahouse licenses by district, and sorting restaurants into those inside the city, on its edge and in the suburbs. It then reads the diaries of domain storehouse officials of 1840 to 1862 and a 1827 account for how banquets were arranged.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows where the licensed pleasure-teahouses stood against a large Edo-period merchant city (ringing the built-up area, on newly reclaimed river land and on the Uemachi plateau) and gives license counts, such as 471 teahouse shares in 1777, and the three-way siting of restaurants, with suburban ones valued for large grounds and borrowed scenery. It is a city of Osaka's size and trade, not a provincial town or village; its maps are schematic, it says the addresses of famous restaurants are only roughly given, and the guidebooks show only what they chose to list.
