kanazawa-kaga-renkon

Kanazawa City, "27.加賀れんこん" ("27. Kaga lotus root", title translated), leaflet on Kaga vegetables (in Japanese; https://www4.city.kanazawa.lg.jp/material/files/group/33/27kagarenkon.pdf)

What it is: A city leaflet on Kaga lotus root, one of Kanazawa's traditional vegetables: the stories of how it came to Kaga, the old and the present cultivar, its qualities and local dishes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says lotus was grown in the Kaga region in the Edo period, that the old cultivar, called jibasu, had to be dug up from 1 m below ground and was short and grown in small quantity, that it came to be grown at Kozaka in Kanazawa and was widely eaten only in Meiji 20-29 (1887-1896), and that today's cultivar was completed about 1970 (the middle of Showa 40-49). The Edo origin is given as several competing stories (a lord's wife's favorite, seed brought from Owari, cut flowers for the Bon festival) with no document, and the page gives no field size, yield or planting method.
