kotobank-une

Hoshikawa Kiyochika (星川清親), "うね" ("une", a ridge or planting row, title translated), in Nipponica (日本大百科全書, Nihon Daihyakka Zensho), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E3%81%86%E3%81%AD-1508458)

What it is: A signed Nipponica entry defining the ridge (une) of a dry field: the soil raised, sunken or level in rows for planting. It describes the kinds (raised, sunken, flat, wide), the effects on drainage and soil temperature, and how rows are run on a slope.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the farming logic for how a field's rows are set: raised rows drain better, sunken rows hold moisture, and on a slope rows running up and down drain fast but lose soil, while rows along the contour hold water for dry land. It is a general agronomy description with no date, region or crop, and it names a plow or modern ridging machine as the tool, so it does not say how Edo-period rows were spaced or sized.
