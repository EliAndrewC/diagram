ja.wikipedia 鍛冶屋 ("kajiya", blacksmith, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%8D%9B%E5%86%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the blacksmith's shop and the smith, covering its equipment and fuels, the Sengoku-period smiths gathered in the castle town, the village smith, and famous smiths and mythical ones.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the Sengoku-period sword and gun smiths were gathered in one quarter of the castle town to fill the orders of lords and samurai, which is the origin of the many smith-quarter place names, and that the village smith (nokaji) made and mended knives, tools and farm tools. It gives no size of shop, number of smiths or place of the village smith in the settlement, its account of decline is dated to after 1955, and the page is a stub that cites one source, the Bible.
