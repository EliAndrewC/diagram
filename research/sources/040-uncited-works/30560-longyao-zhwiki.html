zh.wikipedia 龙窑 ("long yao", the dragon kiln, also called the snake kiln, title translated; reached from the redirect 蛇窯) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/蛇窯)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the dragon kiln, a long narrow pottery kiln built up a hillside, with its parts (firebox, chamber, tail and chimney), its length and slope, a section on ancient kilns from the Shang onward, and a list of surviving dragon kilns in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan and Singapore.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives figures a hillside pottery-kiln drawing can use: a slope of about 15 degrees and lengths that can pass 100 m, stated generally and cited to a Miaoli County (Taiwan) cultural-heritage handbook, and for old ones about 10 m (at most 15 m) in the Shang and Zhou, about 50 m in the Tang and Song, with a 78 m Tang kiln found at Jingdezhen. The early-kiln lengths are cited from a thesis, the Tang lengths from a news report, and the slope and 100 m from a Taiwanese county handbook, it names no Ming or Qing size, and it records that scholars disagree over whether early dragon kilns had a chimney, so it cannot say what a given kiln of the setting looked like.
