shigematsu-2022

Shigematsu Toshihiko, "江戸時代日本人の食糧事情" ("Edo jidai Nihonjin no shokuryo jijo", The food situation of the Japanese in the Edo period, title translated), a monthly lecture printed in Transactions of the Research Institute of Oceanochemistry (海洋化学研究) vol. 35 no. 1, April 2022, pp. 50-56 (in Japanese; https://oceanochemistry.org/publications/trioc/pdf/trioc_2022_35_50.pdf)

What it is: The printed text of a 2021 lecture to the Kyoto Chemists' Club by a professor emeritus of Konan University, estimating how much grain a Japanese person ate in a year in the late Edo and early Meiji periods. It works from farm labor energy, from domain and household grain figures (Morioka, Sendai, Hirosaki, Saga and others), and from 1870s-1890s grain output and population.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a yearly grain intake of about 1.8 koku per person, or 2.0 koku counting grain used for sake and koji, and sets it beside the figures it gathers: Morioka 1653 (over 2.0 koku), Sendai 1754 and Hirosaki 1816 (1.8-1.9 koku), Saga 1844 (1.2 koku rice and 0.6 koku barley), and a 1772 farm manual's household (1.5 koku of rice and barley, over 2.0 with millets and soybeans). Its method leans on metabolic surveys of farmers made in 1900-1950 to stand for Edo labor, and the author himself calls the four domain figures few, differing in date and region.
