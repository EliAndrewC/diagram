kotobank-tateba

Shogakukan Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (Selected Edition) and Britannica International Encyclopedia, "立場" ("tateba", the rest stop on a highway, title translated), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/立場)

What it is: Three dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the word tateba/tachiba, reproduced on Kotobank. The relevant sense is the Edo-period rest place on a highway, where porters and palanquin bearers rested, which after Meiji became a terminus for rickshaws and carriages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the tateba as a roadside rest place with tea stalls, where travelers and bearers rested but could not stay overnight, and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten cites a c.1560 use of tachiba under a tateba sense the page does not print, so it does not show the rest place was that old. The entries disagree on where it stood: Digital Daijisen says between post stations, Britannica says at a post station's entrance. None gives a size, a spacing or a layout, and the page's other senses (standpoint, position, a scrap-dealer's wholesaler) are unrelated.
