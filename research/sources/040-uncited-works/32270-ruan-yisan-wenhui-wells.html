Zhao Zhengnan, "井字为何这样写？我国发现的第一口井是方的！听阮仪三谈井文化" ("Why is the character for well written this way? The first well found in China is square! Ruan Yisan on well culture", title translated), an interview with Ruan Yisan in Wenhui Bao (文汇报), 2018-02-28 (in Chinese; https://wenhui.whb.cn/zhuzhan/jjl/20180228/190600.html)

What it is: A newspaper interview with Ruan Yisan, director of the National Research Center of Historic Cities at Tongji University, on the meaning and conservation of old wells in Chinese towns: wells as the root of the market (shijing), multi-eyed well rims, and separate clean and waste-water ditches at the well platform.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the well as a settlement's gathering and trading place, a shared-use rim with two, three or four openings so buckets do not collide, shrines to a dragon palace beside wells, and clean and foul ditches kept apart; these are stated as general features of old wells in Suzhou, Yangzhou, Chaozhou and other towns, with no date or place attached to each. The one dated claim is that rim stone changed by dynasty (igneous in Song and Yuan, sedimentary in Ming, granite in Qing), and no source is given for it.
