kotobank-tsuji

Takahashi Yasuo and Amino Yoshihiko (the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia entry), "辻" ("tsuji", the crossroads, title translated), with the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Nipponica and older Heibonsha entries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/辻)

What it is: A Kotobank page on tsuji, the crossroads, whose main entry is a signed Heibonsha encyclopedia article on what stood and happened at a Japanese crossroads, with a second article on the medieval crossroads as a place of asylum.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists what a crossroads held: roadside shrines and halls, watch huts in Kamakura Kyoto, neighborhood gates shut at night in Edo, shared wells and privies, and the notice-board site (kosatsuba) at important crossroads in the early modern period. It is mostly about Kyoto and Edo towns, gives no sizes or counts, and the Nipponica entry on this page is about Tsuji, the Okinawan Naha licensed quarter, which is unrelated to a road junction.
