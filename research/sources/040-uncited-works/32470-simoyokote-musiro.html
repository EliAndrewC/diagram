simoyokote-musiro

Folk-life tool section, "ムシロ" ("mushiro", straw mat, title translated), entry on simoyokote.sakura.ne.jp (in Japanese; https://simoyokote.sakura.ne.jp/mingu/musiro/musiro.html)

What it is: A short entry on the straw mat (mushiro) as a village household article: what it was used for, how it was woven, and how much straw it took.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says most houses laid new mats on the main room's floor each New Year's Eve, a village loom was shared in turn, one mat took about 16 bundles of straw and three could be woven in a day, and mats also served as grain-drying sheets and soil retainers. It is a local remembrance with no place, date or source named on the page. It mentions the farm cooperative (農協) buying mats ten to a bundle, so the figures are one village's memory of the modern years when mats were still woven by hand.
