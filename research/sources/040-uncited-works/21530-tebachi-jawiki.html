ja.wikipedia 手水鉢 ("chozubachi", the hand-washing basin, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%89%8B%E6%B0%B4%E9%89%A2)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the hand-washing basin, a vessel kept filled with clean water for rinsing the mouth and hands before a shrine or Buddhist altar, later also set in tea gardens and before privies. It sorts basins into natural-stone, repurposed-object and designed types.

Why it applies, and its limits: It confirms that a basin of running, overflowing water served the purification of a shrine or temple visitor, and that the form was taken into the tea garden. Most of its text is a catalog of named stone shapes tied to Kyoto temples and gardens, with no sizes and no dates beyond saying that designed basins became standard in tea gardens in the Edo period, so it says little about a plain village shrine's basin; the page itself carries a notice that it cites no sources.
