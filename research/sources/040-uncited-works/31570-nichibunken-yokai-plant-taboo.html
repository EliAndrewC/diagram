nichibunken-yokai-plant-taboo

International Research Center for Japanese Studies (Nichibunken), "植物に関する禁忌" ("shokubutsu ni kansuru kinki", taboos concerning plants, title translated), card C0410160-000 of its Yokai database, summarizing Miyagi-kenshi (宮城縣史), Minzoku 2, vol. 20, Miyagi Prefectural History Publication Society, 1956, pp. 227-228 (in Japanese; https://nichibun.ac.jp/cgi-bin/youkaidb3/youkai_card.cgi?id=c0410160-000)

What it is: A database card from a research center's folklore index, giving a short summary of one passage on plant taboos in the 1956 folklore volume of the Miyagi prefectural history. It names the source volume and pages and the prefecture, Miyagi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records three folk taboos about trees on a house plot: that one who falls from a persimmon tree will not live three years, that planting ground-cherry (hozuki) inside the house grounds brings illness, and that a loquat tree likes the groans of the sick, so one planted there keeps the household ill. It is a few lines of summary, not the passage, from one prefecture in the northeast, recorded in the mid-twentieth century with no date for the beliefs themselves, and it says nothing about whether such taboos shaped where anyone planted.
