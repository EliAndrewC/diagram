"周禮註疏/卷十六" ("Commentary and subcommentary on the Rites of Zhou, scroll 16", title translated), on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%91%A8%E7%A6%AE%E8%A8%BB%E7%96%8F/%E5%8D%B7%E5%8D%81%E5%85%AD)

What it is: The Wikisource text of scroll 16 of the Rites of Zhou with Zheng Xuan's notes and the subcommentary on them (the page's notes cite Han practice throughout). It covers the Ministry of Earth's officers over the countryside: grain collectors, wood and mountain wardens, the paddy officer, the soil officer, the granary and rice officers, from the granary stores down to the provisioner of meals for officials on duty at court, who also fattens the sacrificial dogs.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a Zhou-era schema, written down and glossed in Han and later times, of how a royal domain managed its land and stores. It has the paddy officer's works (a pond to store water, a dike, a ditch, a small channel from the field head, a drain at the field foot), the soil officer's "manuring" of seed by soil type (both notes read it as soaking the seed in a broth boiled from the animal, or for one soil the hemp, assigned to that soil, not spreading manure on the field), a mountain warden's cutting seasons (yang trees in midwinter, yin trees in midsummer), and a monthly grain ration of 4, 3 or 2 fu by year (a fu of 6 dou 4 sheng). It is an ideal plan of offices and not a record of any village, its commentary adds Han practice to Zhou text, and the page carries private-use characters that could not be shown.
