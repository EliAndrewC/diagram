ibaraki-rekishikan-joka-machi

Ibaraki Prefectural Museum of History, "城下町の形成" ("The formation of castle towns", title translated), in its history dictionary (in Japanese; https://www.rekishikan.museum.ibk.ed.jp/06_jiten/rekisi/jyoukamati.htm)

What it is: A prefectural history museum's dictionary entry on how Edo-period castle towns in Ibaraki formed, with estimated town populations for Mito, Tsuchiura, Yuki and Shimodate.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says an Edo castle town was laid out around the lord's residence and castle, with samurai quarters for retainers and townsman quarters for merchants and craftsmen, and estimates the town populations (Mito about 20,000 to 30,000, Tsuchiura over 10,000, Yuki over 6,000, Shimodate over 3,000), with townsmen about a tenth of each domain's total population. The figures are called estimates, carry no date within the Edo period and no stated method, and cover four towns of one prefecture, from a large domain seat to small ones.
