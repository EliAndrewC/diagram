washi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 和紙 ("washi", Japanese paper, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%92%8C%E7%B4%99)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on washi, covering its raw materials, the history of papermaking in Japan from its introduction to the Meiji decline, its uses, and a list of producing regions by prefecture.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the founding of the Kamiya-in state paper workshop to the Daido era (805-809) and describes the Edo-period domain monopolies and wholesaler guilds, the use of paper for shoji and fusuma, and a 1489 price showing a bundle of paper cost two and a half days of a craftsman's wage. The page itself opens with a notice that it lacks verifiable sources, so its dates and prices are only as good as the books it names in the notes. It covers the craft and the trade, not how a village's paper-mulberry plots or drying sheds were laid out.
