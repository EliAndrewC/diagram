ja.wikipedia "慶安御触書" ("Keian no ofuregaki", the Keian Edict, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%85%B6%E5%AE%89%E5%BE%A1%E8%A7%A6%E6%9B%B8)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Keian Edict, a 32-article document dated 1649 that tells farmers to avoid luxury, tea, sake and tobacco, eat millet rather than much rice, and work from dawn into the night, with the dispute over whether the shogunate ever issued it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows what a ruling class wished farm households to be - thrifty, early rising, men farming and wives weaving - and gives a sample of the articles (the copies differ in which they hold). But the page says no original survives, that scholars now hold it was not a shogunate law, and that its text spread after a 1830 print by the Iwamura domain from older village-instruction texts (1665 onward), so it is evidence of an ideal held by rulers from the later 17th century and spread by domains from the 1830s, not of what farmers did in 1649.
