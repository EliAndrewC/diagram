Tako Town history, "神域内の小祠・奉献物" ("small shrines and offerings within the sacred precinct", title translated), region volume, former Tako town, Iizasa, religion, shrines and temples, on the Adeac digital archive (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/tako-town/texthtml/d100010/mp000010-100010/ht205060)

What it is: A section of the Tako town history, in Chiba, on the precinct of the Uga shrine at Iizasa: the approach, the steps, torii, plaza, stone lanterns and fox statues, the many small shrines inside, and the festival procession.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a village shrine's layout in sequence: seven stone steps built in 1801, then a stone torii and a wooden one, then about 200 m east through an old pine and cedar wood to a plaza in front of the worship hall that was used for rites, festivals and the sending-off of soldiers, with a precinct of 4,043.89 tsubo given in a 1951 inscription on the land's transfer in 1950. Its dated lanterns and small shrines run from 1669 to 1971 and the inscription says the wood was planted in 1609, so what it shows is a precinct as it stood in the twentieth century with older pieces; it does not say how the approach was laid out when the grove was planted.
