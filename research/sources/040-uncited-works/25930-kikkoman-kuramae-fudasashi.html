kikkoman-kuramae-fudasashi

Kitahara Susumu, "旗本八万騎の台所事情 ～浅草蔵前の札差（ふださし）～" ("The kitchen finances of the 80,000 bannermen: the Asakusa Kuramae fudasashi", title translated), Kikkoman Food Culture Lecture, Kikkoman Institute for International Food Culture, 23 July 2016 (in Japanese; https://www.kikkoman.co.jp/kiifc/info/rp20160723.html)

What it is: The written summary of a 2016 lecture by the historian Kitahara Susumu on the fudasashi, the rice-stipend brokers of Asakusa Kuramae in Edo who lent at high interest against the shogunate's rice stipends to its bannermen and housemen. It covers their guild of 1724, their wealth, the debt-cancellation order of 1789 and their survival to the Meiji Restoration.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports that the shogunate's retainers lived on granted fiefs or on rice from the Asakusa granaries, and that those paid in that rice borrowed at high interest from the brokers there against it (the page does not describe the brokers' handling of the rice itself), and gives concrete figures: 96 to 98 brokers after the guild's recognition in 1724, and losses of 1,188,000 ryo in 1789. It is about retainers of the shogunate in Edo in the middle and late Edo period, not a domain's samurai, and it is a short summary of a talk, not a study, so the figures carry no cited source.
