ja.wikipedia "寄棟造" ("yosemune-zukuri", the hip-roof style, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%AF%84%E6%A3%9F%E9%80%A0)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article, marked as a stub, on the hip roof (four sloping faces), its structure and a list of examples in Japan and abroad.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the hip roof is the second most common form in Japan after the gable, that it was found mostly in eastern Japan and called azumaya in ancient times, and that it sheds rain well but ventilates the roof space worse than a gable or a hip-and-gable roof. It gives no pitch, span or roofing material, and its Japanese examples are temples, a storehouse and a castle gate, so it does not say how common the form was on a farmhouse or a samurai house.
