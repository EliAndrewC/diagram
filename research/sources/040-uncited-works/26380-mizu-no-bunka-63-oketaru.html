Koizumi Kazuko, interviewed in "江戸時代の日本を支えた桶と樽" ("Tubs and barrels that supported Edo-period Japan", title translated), no. 63, "Stories of tubs and barrels", of the magazine Mizu no Bunka, Mizkan Water Culture Center (in Japanese; https://www.mizu.gr.jp/kikanshi/no63/02.html)

What it is: A 2019 magazine interview with a life-history researcher who wrote a book on Japan's staved tubs and barrels, giving their origins, how they spread, their uses and their decline.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the spread of staved tubs (yui-oke) in five phases, from small imported ones at Hakata in the late 11th century to nationwide use from the 17th century, when brewing tubs of about 100 koku appeared; says sake moved from jars to cedar barrels as Edo grew; and says every village had several coopers, since tubs could be mended. It is a summary of her research given aloud, citing on the page only her own book 桶と樽—脇役の日本史 (2000) and period illustrations, and with no sizes for ordinary household or farm tubs.
