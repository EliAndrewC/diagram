aozorapota-2024-tama-sumi

aozorapota8823kun, "〈資料で探る〉江戸の町で使われた多摩炭" ("Looking into the sources: Tama charcoal used in Edo", title translated), on the blog "多摩川の筏道" (The rafters' road of the Tama River), Hatena Blog, 6 April 2024 (in Japanese; https://tamaikada.hateblo.jp/entry/tamasumi)

What it is: A hobbyist's blog article that gathers quotations from local-history publications on charcoal in Edo: how much the city burned, which provinces supplied it, and how charcoal from the Tama hills reached Edo by pack horse and by raft down the Tama River.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives figures with named sources: about 809,710 hyo of charcoal arriving by boat in 1726 and an estimated 2.5 million hyo a year by the end of the Edo period for a city of about a million, a hyo of Okutama charcoal weighing 4 kan (about 15 kg), 25 to 30 hyo on one raft, and 20,000 to 30,000 hyo a year carried by Tama raft. It contrasts this with a dozen or so hyo on a horse cart. The numbers are secondhand, the raft total comes from Meiji-era counts, the cart photograph is from the 1930s-40s, and the distances were measured on modern roads.
