Akimoto Etsuko, "近世の街道並木と御林の関係 ― 下総・上総国の土気・東金両街道の並木を例に ―" ("The relationship between roadside trees and the shogunate's forests in the early modern period: the Toke and Togane highways of Shimousa and Kazusa as an example", title translated), 歴史地理学 (Rekishi Chirigaku, journal of historical geography) 44-4 (no. 210), September 2002, pp. 22-44 (in Japanese; http://hist-geo.jp/img/archive/210_2.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a journal article, with an English abstract, on the pine rows along the Toke and Togane highways between Chiba in Shimousa and Togane in Kazusa. It reads the Edo-period village records and a map of 1768 to show how the shogunate managed those rows beside its own forests (ohayashi).

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows, from named villages and dated documents, that a secondary highway's trees were kept in registers, inspected by shogunate officials and sold as timber and firewood when they fell, and it lists how many ken of row each village maintained in 1768. Its documents run from 1666 to the 1860s and concern two roads in one part of the Kanto, so it speaks to who owned and used roadside trees rather than to how they were planted or spaced.
