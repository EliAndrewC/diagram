Fukui Prefectural Archives, "第三章 近世の村と浦 第二節 平野の村々 一 検地と石高 内検地" ("Chapter 3, villages and coves of the early modern period; section 2, villages of the plain; 1, the survey and the rice yield; the internal survey", title translated), in 『福井県史』通史編3 近世一 (History of Fukui Prefecture, general history vol. 3, early modern 1) (in Japanese; https://www.library-archives.pref.fukui.lg.jp/fukui/07/kenshi/T3/T3-3-01-02-01-04.htm)

What it is: A section of the prefectural history on how Echizen villages adjusted to the Taiko survey: internal surveys that reapportioned land when fields washed away, villages that kept their assessed yield despite shrinking area, and a village's table of expected yields.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that a village's assessed yield could stay fixed when its fields fell, for instance Hojioka's area falling to 67 percent of its 1598 extent by 1607 with no change in village yield, met by raising each field grade's assessed yield, while Kami-Kanaya shared its waste land's assessment among holders by their holdings; and that one Kuzuryu-side village's standing-crop estimates for 1705 to 1714 ran well below the survey's grades in every year but 1709, though the text warns they are not the actual harvest. It covers a few villages of the Echizen plain in Sakai, Yoshida and Imadate districts, some losing fields to the rivers, and only Shihisakai is placed on the Kuzuryu; its yield table is not reproduced on the page.
