tokyo-mokuiku-forest-history

Tokyo Metropolitan Government Bureau of Industrial and Labor Affairs, "3-5 森・木・人の歴史" ("3-5 the history of forests, trees and people", title translated), in "Tokyo's forests" (東京の森林), Tokyo Wood Education Promotion Project (in Japanese; https://mokuiku.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/forest/3-5.html)

What it is: A page of the Tokyo government's wood-education site for schoolchildren, giving a short history of how people in the Tama area of Tokyo used forests, from ancient use of timber, firewood and leaf litter through Edo-period timber, charcoal and paper to the planting of cedar and cypress plantations about sixty years ago.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in the Edo period Tama cedar and cypress timber was rafted down rivers to build houses, shrines, temples and daimyo compounds in an Edo of a million people, that Hinohara village made firewood and charcoal for kitchens and heating, that rebuilding after Edo's frequent fires drew heavily on Tama's woods, and that Akiruno hillsides made paper from paper-mulberry bark. It also says fallen leaves were used as fertilizer once fields were tilled. It is a short account for children about one area of Tokyo, gives no figures beyond Edo's million people, and cites no source.
