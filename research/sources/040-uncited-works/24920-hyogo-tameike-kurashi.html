hyogo-tameike-kurashi

Hyogo Prefectural Government, "ため池と地域の暮らし" ("Irrigation ponds and local life", title translated), in its Manabu (learn) pages, updated 2025-11-19 (in Japanese; https://web.pref.hyogo.lg.jp/recommend/learn/learn01.html)

What it is: A prefectural feature on Hyogo's irrigation ponds (tameike): over 21,000 farm ponds, the most of any prefecture and about 14 percent of Japan's, why a dry Seto Inland Sea country needed them, their oldest examples, the legends of human sacrifice told of their dikes, and their present conservation and flood-control use.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a count and a reason for the ponds' density (low rainfall in the Seto Inland Sea country), says some date to the Yayoi era and the oldest, the Oka-oike, is traditionally dated 675, and tells two dike-building legends. The counts are present-day, the early dates are tradition rather than finds (it adds that many are of unknown date or said to be Edo), it is a Seto Inland Sea case that does not carry to wetter regions, and it gives no pond sizes except two ponds holding over 1,500 thousand cubic meters, Showa-ike and Kamida-ike, both twentieth-century works (finished 1934 and 1932, Kamida-ike as a concrete dam), so that figure says nothing of a premodern pond; Kako-oike, which it calls the largest, gets no size.
