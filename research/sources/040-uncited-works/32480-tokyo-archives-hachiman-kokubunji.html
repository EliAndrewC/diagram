Tokyo Metropolitan Archives, "資料解説―神社明細帳を読み解く～八幡神社（国分寺市）" ("Reading the shrine register: Hachiman Shrine, Kokubunji City", title translated), in the series "Getting to know Edo-Tokyo", on soumu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp (in Japanese; https://soumu.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/01soumu-archives/07edo_tokyo/0703kaidoku/0703kaidoku_03/0703kaidoku_03_2)

What it is: An archive article reading the 1879 shrine register entry of Hachiman Shrine, the village tutelary shrine of Kokubunji village (then in Kanagawa Prefecture), with the shrine's size and parishioner count.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the 1830 Shinpen Musashi fudoki that the village tutelary shrine stood in a temple's wooded grounds with a small roofed hall of 2 by 3 ken, and gives the 1879 register: main hall (honden) 2 ken 3 shaku (about 4.5 m) by 4 ken (about 7.2 m), precinct 513 tsubo (about 1,700 square m) and 94 parishioner households, so it shows a village shrine's size and its ties to a nearby temple. The register is from 1879, after the Meiji separation of shrines and temples, so its precinct reflects the Meiji era, though its attached plan shows the main hall and worship hall built in 1720; the register was annotated as late as 1941 and, the archive notes, omits some later changes such as a torii of 1892.
