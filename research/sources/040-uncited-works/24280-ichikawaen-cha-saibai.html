ichikawaen-cha-saibai

Ocha Museum (お茶ミュージアム), "栽培方法によるお茶の区分" ("Classification of tea by cultivation method", title translated), on the Ocha Museum tea website (in Japanese; https://museum.ichikawaen.co.jp/knowledge/cultivation.php)

What it is: A page of the Ocha Museum, the educational web museum of the tea company Ichikawaen, on how Japanese tea is grown: shading of gyokuro and tencha bushes under frames and cloth, the shapes and heights of tea hedgerows by hand or machine plucking, and organic certification rules.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says shading began to protect new shoots from frost, gives the shading periods (for gyokuro under a frame, about 20 days in two stages or 15 days at 90 percent shade; for kabusecha, under a frame or a black cloth laid straight on the rows, about 7 to 12 days), and that hand-plucked gardens are mostly trained high, over 1 m, or medium, about 75 cm, while machine-plucked hedges run from that medium height down to low hedges of 30 to 40 cm and follow contour lines on slopes. It describes present-day Japanese tea growing, with machine plucking and the modern organic standard, and gives no date for when any of the forms arose.
