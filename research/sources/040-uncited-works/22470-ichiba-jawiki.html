ichiba-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 市場 ("ichiba", market, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/定期市)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on markets, defining the market as a place where people gather regularly to trade, then surveying markets from Mesopotamia, Greece and Rome, Tang China, Islamic cities and medieval Europe to Japan, with a closing section on modern Japanese wholesale and retail market law.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Japan section gives the premodern market calendar: government east and west markets in the capitals under the Taiho code, opening at noon and closing at sunset, then periodic markets at transport points, held on days ending in 8 (the 8th, 18th and 28th) and in the three-a-month form, with the six-a-month form from the 15th and 16th centuries, and it names towns whose markets go back to the Edo period. The article marks that Japan section itself as lacking sources, and most of its length is on world history and present-day law, so only the Japan paragraphs bear on the setting, with the short, unsourced Tang China paragraph (licensed market quarters in the capitals, later periodic markets at busy places, garrison-town and temple-front markets).
