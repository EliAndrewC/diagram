kotobank-fudasashi

"札差" ("fudasashi", the rice-stipend brokers of Edo, title translated), dictionary entries from Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, Nippon Daihyakka Zensho and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/札差-124940)

What it is: A Kotobank page on the fudasashi, Edo merchants near the shogunate's rice stores at Asakusa who received the rice stipends of bannermen and housemen for them, sold it and lent against it. The longest entries are a signed Heibonsha one and a Nippon Daihyakka Zensho one, both by Kitahara Susumu.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the trade in numbers: 109 members of a licensed guild from 1724, a commission of 3 bu of gold per 100 bales, loan interest near 18 percent cut to 12 percent in 1789 when the shogunate wrote off 1.18 million ryo of debt, the peak in the An'ei and Tenmei years, and the end with the stipend system at the Meiji Restoration. It also says Kofu had about ten such brokers serving its garrison, smaller than Edo's. The figures are Edo's, and the entries differ on the commission (3 bu in most, 2 bu in Britannica), so the page cannot carry a rate for any other town.
