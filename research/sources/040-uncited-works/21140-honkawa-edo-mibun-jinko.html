honkawa-edo-mibun-jinko

Honkawa Yutaka (本川裕), "図録▽江戸時代の身分別人口" ("Chart: population by status in the Edo period", title translated), on the data site Honkawa Zuroku (in Japanese; https://honkawa2.sakura.ne.jp/7860.html)

What it is: A statistics page, compiled chiefly from Sekiyama Naotaro's 1958 study 近世日本の人口構造 (the population structure of early modern Japan), giving the share of Japan's population by status in the early Meiji domain surveys (samurai class 6.4 percent, clergy 1.2 percent, commoners 90.6 percent, outcaste groups 1.7 percent), the occupations of working people aged 15 and over in 1873, samurai shares in several domains, and a 1622 breakdown of 14,313 people in Buzen's rural districts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies status proportions the record can compare with a setting's caste mix, such as 79.2 percent of working people aged 15 and over in farming in 1873 and samurai shares of 6.9 to 9.8 percent in the Nanbu, Kubota and Tsuwano domains. The national figures come from the domain surveys ordered from 1869 and the 1873 occupation count, the domain figures range from 1681 to 1849, the samurai share runs as high as 26 percent where retainers farmed, and the Buzen table mixes status, trade and disability classes.
