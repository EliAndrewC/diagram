chuo-oiwa-inari-torii

Chuo City, "於岩稲荷田宮神社の鳥居" ("The torii of Oiwa Inari Tamiya Shrine", title translated), in the Chuo City cultural properties pages (in Japanese; https://www.city.chuo.lg.jp/a0052/bunkakankou/rekishi/bunkazai/kuminbunkazai/oiwainaritamiyajinjyanotorii.html)

What it is: A municipal page, from a 2016 newsletter column by a named adviser, on a stone torii of 1897 at the Oiwa Inari Tamiya Shrine in Shinkawa, Chuo ward, which first describes torii forms and then the shrine, its history and the torii's dimensions.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the torii as about 2.5 m high, 3.15 m wide, with pillars about 20 cm across, calls it rather small, and names the two basic forms (the straight-topped Shinmei and the curved Myojin) tied to the shrine hall's roof style. It also reports the shrine's 1900 guide entry, a precinct of about 500 tsubo with a stone wall, two bronze lanterns and a plain-wood torii. It is one Meiji-era stone torii at a shrine that began as a retainer family's house shrine and was moved after a fire in 1879, so it is not a village shrine's gate, and wooden torii are not covered beyond that one line.
