Okuni Jinja, "境内の施設等" ("keidai no shisetsu to", The facilities in the shrine grounds, title translated), on the shrine's own site 遠江国一宮 小國神社 (Totomi no kuni ichinomiya Okuni Jinja) (in Japanese; https://okunijinja.or.jp/facilities/equipment)

What it is: The shrine's own page listing what stands in the grounds of Okuni Jinja, the chief shrine of Totomi Province in Mori town, Shizuoka: the two torii, the approach for the imperial envoy, the purification basin, offices, a hall for lodging and weddings, stones, a sutra mound and a shopping lane.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the first torii (a myojin type, set up in 1969) stands at the shrine's front, that a two-story gate stood where the second torii now is until a fire in 1882, and that a sutra mound from the Heian period (an inscription of 1168) survives in the grounds, half of it cut away in 1885. Nearly all the listed buildings are twentieth-century (1953, 1969, later), so it shows what a shrine ground has held, not how a premodern one was laid out, and the early dates it gives (the envoy of 701) are the shrine's own tradition with no source named.
