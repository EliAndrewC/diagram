kotobank-tsukuda

"佃" ("tsukuda", a lord's directly farmed field, title translated), entries in the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the popular edition of Heibonsha Jitsu (字通), the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Seki Yukihiko), Nipponica (by Abe Takeshi), Yamakawa's Japanese History Dictionary and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%BD%83%28%E6%BC%A2%E5%AD%97%29-2789339)

What it is: A set of entries on the tsukuda, the field an estate lord, steward or land steward farmed directly, with seed and tools supplied and the whole harvest taken, worked by dependents or by the estate's farmers' labor service. They give its four forms, its share of an estate's fields (from 2 to 6 tenths early, under 1 tenth on some estates later), and its fading by the Muromachi period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the manor's home farm as a good, well-watered field kept by the lord, and it shows how the form changed from labor-service land to farmers renting it at a high rate, until it was like any other holding. Its evidence runs from the 9th to the 15th century, before the Edo period, and it concerns estate lands, so it describes a lord's demesne in earlier times and not Edo village fields or a domain's fields as later set out.
