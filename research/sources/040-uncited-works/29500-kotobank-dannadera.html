kotobank-dannadera

"檀那寺" ("dannadera, the family temple", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/檀那寺-95362)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for dannadera, the temple to which a household belongs as parishioners, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha Maypedia and Britannica small-item entries and the older Heibonsha encyclopedia's mentions of it under temples and the temple-parish system.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in the Edo period the shogunate required every household to hold a dannadera, whose priest issued the temple certificate (terauke) that the registers of religion (shumon aratame) needed, and that after the Meiji Restoration the certificates were abolished while the tie of household to temple continued as the temples' basic way of keeping track of believers. It also notes households whose members belonged to different temples (handanka), so a household was not always tied to one temple. It gives no number of households per temple or any distance, and describes the system as shogunal policy, not as it worked in one village.
