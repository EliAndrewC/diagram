suido-ishizue-kokudo-rekishi

Agriculture and Rural Development Information Center (ARIC), "国土づくりの歴史" ("The history of land-making", title translated), in the site Suido no Ishizue (水土の礎, "Foundations of Water and Soil") (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part1/01/07.html)

What it is: A short overview page on how Japan's farmed land was made over time, arguing that paddy farming led people to pile labor into the ground through water, so that village, fields and the woodland beyond form one linked structure.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the stages of planned development in Japan - the jori field grid, the manor (shoen) clearances of the Middle Ages and the new-field (shinden) reclamation - and says woodland came to serve as the paddy's fertilizer source, which supports the village-fields-woodland arrangement as the Japanese norm. It is an essay without a single place, date or figure, and says nothing about how large any of these parts were.
