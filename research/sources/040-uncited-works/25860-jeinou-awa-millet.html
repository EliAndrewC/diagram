jeinou-awa-millet

Kuranai Nobuyuki, "アワ" ("awa", foxtail millet, title translated), in the Farm Work Handbook (農作業便利帖), Minna no Nougyou Hiroba, supported by the National Agricultural Improvement and Extension Support Association and Kubota (in Japanese; https://www.jeinou.com/benri/others/cereals/2008/08/041030.html)

What it is: A cultivation guide for foxtail millet (awa) on a farmers' information site, revised February 2024 and signed by a Nihon University professor. It gives the crop's origin and spread, Japanese spring and summer types, sticky and non-sticky uses, and sowing, thinning, weeding, harvest and drying advice.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says foxtail millet reached Japan by way of Korea, was already grown in the Jomon period and was, with barnyard millet, the country's oldest crop and probably the staple before rice. It also says harvested sheaves are tied and dried in the sun for about a week, and that non-sticky millet is mixed with rice as millet rice (awa-meshi) while sticky millet goes into dumplings, rice cakes and sweets. Its sowing dates (given for Kanto), spacing and manure rates are written for present-day farms, recommending plowing, binders, combines and rice threshers, with chemical fertilizer where manure is lacking and a hoe and hand cutting named only as alternatives, and the Jomon claim is stated without evidence, so it backs the crop's old standing but not premodern field practice.
