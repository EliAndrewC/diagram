kotobank-iseki

"井堰" ("iseki", a river weir for irrigation, title translated), dictionary entries from Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/井堰)

What it is: A short Kotobank page defining iseki as a place where river water is dammed with earth or wood to divert it, and also used of an irrigation channel. It adds one passage from the old Heibonsha encyclopedia's article on water disputes.

Why it applies, and its limits: The encyclopedia passage names the main causes of medieval water quarrels: turn-taking order and time, payments for irrigation water, and the building or repair of a weir that harmed weirs downstream, the commonest cause. This supports the intake weir as an old, shared structure and a source of conflict between villages. The page is about 1,400 characters, gives no weir's size, form or date beyond the Heian-period word, and says nothing on how a weir was built.
