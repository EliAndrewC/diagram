daimyo-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 大名 ("daimyo", the feudal lord, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大名)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the daimyo, tracing the word from holder of a large named field in the Heian period through the warrior lords of the Kamakura, Muromachi and Sengoku periods to the Edo lord of 10,000 koku or more, with the kinds of Edo estate a daimyo kept and the Ryukyu use of the title.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Edo rank line (10,000 koku, first written into the 1635 laws, though not clear from the start of Edo) and the three kinds of daimyo estate in Edo - the upper residence near the castle, the middle residence for heir and retired lord, and the lower residence away from the center. The article itself carries a notice that it lacks verifiable sources, and says little about how a domain was laid out.
