ankyo-sango-akusui

Yaura Wakami (大名古屋暗渠録), "三郷悪水" ("The Sango drainage channel", title translated), a guide on the covered waterways of Nagoya, on ankyo.nagoya (in Japanese; https://www.ankyo.nagoya/guide/sango-akusui/)

What it is: A hobbyist's guide page to the Sango akusui, a drainage channel dug in 1792 in what is now Kita and Nishi wards, Nagoya, to carry off the water of the three diked villages between the Yada and Shonai rivers, with its history through the 1877 Kurokawa cut and the 1930 river diversion to its covering over in 1994.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains how a drainage channel (akusui, "bad water") was added to an irrigation network: dug to drain the three villages of Sango, a polder (wajū) hemmed in by two raised-bed rivers, and linked to the Shonai irrigation canal, with two widths: the Oyosui, the canal feeding Nagoya castle's moat, widened to 5 ken (about 9.1 m) to carry the Shonai water as well, and a 2-ken (about 3.6 m) inverted siphon taking the new Shonai channel under the Yada. It speaks for one Owari district in the 1790s to 1930s, the author says part of the text is garbled and under revision, and its sources (old maps and a canal history) are named only in a list at the foot.
