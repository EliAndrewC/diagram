Guo Pu (attributed), "葬書" ("Book of Burial", title translated), on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%91%AC%E6%9B%B8)

What it is: The Wikisource front page of the Book of Burial, a geomancy text attributed to Guo Pu of the Eastern Jin. It carries the Siku quanshu summary of the book's history, a later editor's preface and the contents list of its inner, outer and miscellaneous chapters, not the chapters themselves.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the burial-site doctrine for the setting: the summary says the Zhou officials buried by clan and did not choose sites, that the art began in the Han, and that the book first appears in the Song history (not the Tang). It names the two later schools, the Fujian one by star and trigram and the Jiangxi one by form, and quotes "seize the living qi" and "wind scatters it, water stops it". The page itself doubts that Guo Pu wrote it and gives only chapter lengths, so the doctrine's text is not here.
