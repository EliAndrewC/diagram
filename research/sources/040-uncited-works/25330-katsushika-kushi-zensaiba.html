Katsushika Ward, "葛飾区史 第5章 暮らしの移り変わり 第3節 都市近郊の農村" ("History of Katsushika Ward, chapter 5, section 3: farm villages near the city", title translated), on the ward website (in Japanese; https://www.city.katsushika.lg.jp/history/history/5-3-1-316-1.html)

What it is: One page of the ward history of Katsushika, Tokyo, on the zensaiba, the market-vegetable villages around Tokyo, set against the tanbasho, the rice-growing villages beyond them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the two zones that supplied the city with vegetables, Kasai to the east (Katsushika, Edogawa) and Nishiyama to the west (Nerima, Itabashi), and says Katsushika grew vegetables from the Edo period on, on intensively worked land with work all year, while rice villages grew vegetables mostly for their own use. It also says the difference in farm rhythm lasted into the Showa 20s (1945-1954). The names zensaiba, Kasai, Nishiyama and tanbasho are given as used by Tokyo-area farmers and market people, and the zones are described as they stood around Tokyo up to the early Showa years; only the start of Katsushika's vegetable growing is dated to the Edo period. The page gives no acreages, yields or crop lists.
