kusatsu-shukuba-honjin

Kusatsu City, "草津宿と本陣について" ("About Kusatsu post town and its honjin", title translated), Kusatsu-juku Gakumonjo pages on the Kusatsu City website (in Japanese; https://www.city.kusatsu.shiga.jp/kusatsujuku/gakumonjo/shukuba_kaisetsu.html)

What it is: A city explainer on Kusatsu, the Shiga post town where the Tokaido and Nakasendo meet, covering the two roles of a post town, the 1843 count of its inns, the surviving honjin (daimyo lodging) of the Tanaka family, and where the word honjin comes from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It cites the 1843 Tokaido post-town register for 2 honjin, 2 secondary honjin and 72 inns at Kusatsu, and lists the other services of the street (teahouses, a freight office and a weighing station), so it gives a dated count for a large junction post town. It says the surviving honjin, held by the Tanaka family since 1635, is the largest of those surviving, so it is a top-end example, not a typical honjin, and the page gives no sizes or plan.
