aric-medieval-village

ARIC (一般社団法人農業農村整備情報総合センター), "中世のむら" ("The medieval village", title translated), in "むらとその生活" (Villages and their life), "大地への刻印", in Suido no Ishizue (水土の礎) (in Japanese; https://suido-ishizue.jp/daichi/part1/03/04.html)

What it is: A page of an agricultural-engineering association's online history of Japanese land-making, on the medieval village: the shift from scattered farmsteads to clustered villages, moated villages, double cropping and the plow, and water shrines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates and places the clustering: from Nara to mid-Heian a settlement was usually 1 to under 10 farmsteads, while on the Kinai plains houses gathered in one place between late Heian and Muromachi, some within moats. It adds that Owari and Echizen clustered later and looser on natural levees, and the large river fans (Oi, Sho, Tenryu) stayed dispersed. It also says the village shrine of an estate became the sign of the villagers' union, and that grass cut from the hills, not night soil, was the chief manure. These are regional summaries with few figures, centered on the Kinai and central Japan.
