tsuijimatsu-kyogikai-izumo

Tsuijimatsu Landscape Conservation Promotion Council (築地松景観保全対策推進協議会), "築地松とは" ("tsuijimatsu to wa", What is the tsuijimatsu, title translated), on the council's website (in Japanese; https://www.tsuijimatsu.com/55)

What it is: A page on the website of the council that promotes conservation of the tsuijimatsu, the black-pine windbreak hedges clipped to a set height on the west and north sides of farmhouse grounds on the Izumo plain (Shimane), with the Izumo city housing office named as contact. It tells how the hedge is said to have begun and lists six theories of what it was for.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a regional form of the farmstead tree belt: black pines on the west and north of the house ground, said to descend from the tree and bamboo planting that held the earthen bank (tsuiji) that local magnates built round house grounds they had raised several meters against river floods, on the very wet Izumo plain; the page says the first trees were not pines and black pine was planted later. Its six uses (blocking winter wind, flood defense, fuel from lopped branches, firebreak between houses, food from the matebashii nuts and bamboo shoots, and the dignity it gave a house) are offered as competing theories, and the page calls the origin uncertain and dates it only to the formation of village society, so it cannot say how old the pine hedge as drawn is.
