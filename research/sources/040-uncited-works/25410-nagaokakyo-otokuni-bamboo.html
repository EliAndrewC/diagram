nagaokakyo-otokuni-bamboo

Nagaokakyo City, "ピカイチ特産物！竹とタケノコ" ("A top local product: bamboo and bamboo shoots", title translated), Nagaokakyo City Hall website (in Japanese; https://www.city.nagaokakyo.lg.jp/0000011470.html)

What it is: A city history page on bamboo and bamboo shoots in the old Otokuni district west of Kyoto, with a timeline from 927 to 2012, notes on shoot cultivation, the digging tool (hori), bamboo paid as tax in kind, and a bamboo grove designated as a prefectural natural monument.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its timeline shows bamboo from Otokuni listed for chopsticks in the 927 Engishiki, bamboo traders in 1416, a 1717 rule on bamboo delivered to Nijo Castle, and the rapid spread of moso-bamboo shoot cultivation in the Tenpo years (1830-1843), and it says bamboo was a tax item (agetake) in the Edo period. That dates moso shoot farming to the late Edo period and makes the bamboo tax a regional practice; it also reports a tradition that moso came from China in the early Edo period, so it does not support moso groves in an earlier setting, and it gives no grove areas or yields. The Kyoto-style softening cultivation (京都式軟化栽培) it describes as the local method is placed in its timeline among the late-Meiji entries (1897-1912), before 1918, so it is not evidence for how shoots were grown in the Edo period.
