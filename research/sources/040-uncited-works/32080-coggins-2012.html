Chris Coggins, Joelle Chevrier, Maeve Dwyer, Lindsey Longway, Michael Xu, Peter Tiso and Zhen Li, "Village Fengshui Forests of Southern China - Culture History and Conservation Status", ASIANetwork Exchange 19(2), 2012, pp. 52-67, DOI 10.16995/ane.43 (https://doi.org/10.16995/ane.43)

What it is: The journal's landing page for a peer-reviewed article on village fengshui forests (fengshuilin) in southern China, giving the abstract, the authors, the citation and the PDF link; the article's full text is not on the page.

Why it applies, and its limits: The abstract says the study assessed fengshui practice, forest management, harvesting bans and tree-species selection in seventeen villages with over 40 forest patches, found little sign of use-based species choice, and notes that fengshui was banned by the state from 1949 to 1979 while many lineage villages kept protecting the forests. Only the abstract was read: it gives no forest sizes, positions or dates of planting, and the fieldwork was in the summer of 2011 in a post-reform revival, so it supports that such forests exist and are protected, not any layout.
