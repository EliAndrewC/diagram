xuansuoqiao-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 悬索桥 ("xuansuoqiao", the suspension bridge, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%90%8A%E6%A1%A5)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on suspension bridges, mostly their modern structure and construction, with a history section listing early bamboo and iron-chain bridges in China.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its history section says the earliest suspension bridges are Chinese, of woven bamboo cable, gives the Jihong bridge of Yunnan (first built 58-68 AD of bamboo cable, rebuilt in wood under the Yuan and as an iron-chain bridge under the Ming, repaired through the Qing and used until 1986) as 115 meters long, 3.8 wide with a 56.2-meter clear span of 18 chains in 9 strands, the size of the chain bridge rather than the first one, the Luding bridge of 1706 at about 110 meters, and a level-deck bridge of 1420 in the southwest. Those dates and sizes are the whole premodern content and rest on Needham's history, cited only for the bamboo origin, and an unnamed archived web page cited at the paragraph's end; the rest of the page is steel-cable engineering, and it is flagged for missing sources.
