nikkenkyo-roots-furo-kitchen

"ルーツを探れ！" ("Seek the roots!", title translated), a page on baths, toilets and kitchens, on the nikkenkyo.jp site (in Japanese; http://nikkenkyo.jp/before/4joho/roots/roots.htm)

What it is: A web page tracing the history of the Japanese bath, toilet and kitchen from the Jomon period to the postwar housing corporation, drawn from three books of the series 物語 ものの建築史 (on the bath, the toilet and the kitchen), published by Kajima.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the lowest samurai houses had only a living room and a kitchen while higher ranks added parlors, inner rooms and a cooking room, and that an upper samurai kitchen had seven stoves and was run by hired cooks, that the farmhouse hiroma plan centered on a hearth with the kitchen beside it, that snowy regions kept the kitchen within the house while southern islands often put it in a separate building, that pit toilets, kept to collect night soil as fertilizer, appeared from the Kamakura period on, and that Edo tenement toilets were shared. It is a general outline with no sizes, no region for most claims, and no citation for any single statement.
