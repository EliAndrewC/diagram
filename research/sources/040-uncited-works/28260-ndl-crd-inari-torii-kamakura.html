Kamakura City Central Library (鎌倉市中央図書館), "鎌倉の佐助稲荷にはどうしてたくさん鳥居があるのか" ("Why Sasuke Inari in Kamakura has so many torii", title translated), reference case 202205, in the Reference Collaborative Database (レファレンス協同データベース) of the National Diet Library (in Japanese; https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/index.php?id=1000313088&page=ref_view)

What it is: A library reference answer on why Inari shrines, Sasuke Inari in Kamakura among them, have many torii. It quotes the Fushimi Inari Taisha FAQ and four books to say that torii are given as a wish or in thanks, and that the rows form tunnel-like approaches.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports that torii in great numbers are a votive custom of Inari shrines in particular, and quotes the Fushimi Inari FAQ that the custom spread from the Edo period on, with the explanation that a wish "passes" (toru). It names no count or date for Sasuke Inari itself and quotes the books, so it does not say what a village Inari shrine had before the Edo period.
