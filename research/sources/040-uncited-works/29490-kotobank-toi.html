kotobank-toi

"樋" ("hi / toi, the water channel, flume and gutter", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/樋)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for the character hi or toi, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, Nipponica, Britannica and others on two uses: a conduit or sluice door that carries water, and a roof gutter. The Heibonsha entry includes a section by Kitamura Toshio on pond sluices (the uchi-hi outlet, and the buried and shakuhachi types) with examples such as Mannoike and Sayama-ike.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the pond sluice in detail: buried and shakuhachi types, a pipe laid slanting up the dam's inner face with several holes about 10 cm across (five on Mannoike's old sluice), plugged and opened from the top down as the water falls, and the premodern advice (Jikata no Kikigaki) that ponds most often failed at the sluice, so it needs firm ground (the entry puts it at eight or nine failures in ten); it also dates the old shakuhachi sluices of Mannoike (1631 to 1854) and Sayama-ike (from 1608). Its examples are large irrigation reservoirs, not a village's small pond, and the roof-gutter material (copper, zinc-plated iron, vinyl) is modern, with a note that wood and bamboo were used before and that Edo-period farmhouses of the kudo-zukuri and bunto types had a receiving gutter where their roof valleys met.
