kyoshi-soshi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 墟市 ("kyoshi", periodic village markets of China, also called "soshi" 草市, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/草市)

What it is: A Japanese Wikipedia article on the small periodic markets that arose in China from the late Tang in crowded places and at crossroads, called kyoshi mainly in Jiangnan and soshi elsewhere. The page was opened from the address 草市, which redirects to 墟市.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says these markets met every few days and stood nearly empty between, that landlords and merchants set them up to earn rent, that the state placed a garrison town (chin) beside them, that temple markets traded over longer distances than the daily-goods markets, and that from the Northern Song the state taxed them and raised some to towns. It covers China, not Japan, gives no spacing, size or counts, and the page itself carries a notice that it has no references, so each claim is unsupported there.
