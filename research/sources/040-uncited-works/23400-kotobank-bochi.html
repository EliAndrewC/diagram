kotobank-bochi

"墓地" ("bochi", a burial ground, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Kirishiki Shinjiro), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A2%93%E5%9C%B0-599821)

What it is: A Kotobank page defining the burial ground and giving the encyclopedia's account of cemeteries from ancient Egypt through the Greeks, Rome and medieval Europe to the public cemeteries opened in Tokyo from 1874. It also has a short note that Yayoi villages had a communal burial ground next to the settlement.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Japanese content that bears on a setting is the Yayoi note and the Tokyo cemeteries, whose figures (Aoyama at 273,000 square meters, Tama at 1,330,000) are for large modern parks founded in 1874 and 1923 and do not carry back. Most of the entry is about Egypt, Greece, Rome and Europe, and it says nothing about a village's or a temple's burial ground in the Edo period.
