kotobank-chuma

"中馬" ("chuma", the Shinano pack-horse carriers, title translated), entries in the Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (by Furushima Toshio), Nipponica (by Fujimura Junichiro), Yamakawa's Japanese History Dictionary and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%AD%E9%A6%AC-97470)

What it is: A set of encyclopedia entries on the chuma, the horse-borne freight business that farmers of Shinano ran on the Ina Road and other routes as a sideline, selling to Edo and Nagoya and bringing salt, fish and cotton back. The Heibonsha entry gives the 1764 shogunate ruling that fixed 679 villages and 18,768 horses, their share by county, and the loads and routes of a carrier.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies numbers for the packhorse trade in a mountain province with no river boats: a carrier drove three or four horses carrying about 100 kan at a time, loaded through without changing horses at post stations, which set carriers against the post-station freight agents. It speaks for Shinano, mostly its south, not for flat rice country; its totals disagree between entries (18,768 horses against 18,614), and the carriers' growth from the 1670s and decline after the Meiji railways bound its evidence to roughly 1670 to 1870.
