kotobank-nagasaki-bugyosho-tateyama

"長崎奉行所立山役所跡" ("nagasaki bugyosho tateyama yakusho ato", site of the Tateyama office of the Nagasaki magistrate), in the Heibonsha Historical Place Names of Japan (日本歴史地名大系), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%95%B7%E5%B4%8E%E5%A5%89%E8%A1%8C%E6%89%80%E7%AB%8B%E5%B1%B1%E5%BD%B9%E6%89%80%E8%B7%A1-3099813)

What it is: A partial reproduction on Kotobank of a Heibonsha place-name entry on the site of the Nagasaki magistrate's Tateyama office, giving its moves and dates from 1592 to its completion in 1673 and the magistrate's duties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the Nagasaki magistrate's offices were split in two in 1633, with the east office moved to Tateyama in 1634 and finished in 1673, and that the Tateyama office did most of the work. The magistracy answered to the senior councilors and handled trade, the ban on Christianity and foreign affairs, so it describes a unique port-city post, not a typical magistrate's seat, and the page gives no plan or size.
