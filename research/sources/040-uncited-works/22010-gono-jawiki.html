gono-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 豪農 ("gono", wealthy farmer, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%B1%AA%E8%BE%B2)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the gono, the wealthy landholding farmers of Edo-period Japan, tracing their rise in the mid-eighteenth century, their roles as landlords, merchants and moneylenders, and their dissolution in the Meiji period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a gono often served as a village official, was sometimes allowed a surname and a sword, and was the largest landholder of the village, and it pictures the great Niigata gono houses such as the Ichijima residence. It sketches a class scheme and a scholarly debate in the terms of postwar Marxist historians, it is a stub with an unwritten section on the wider sense of the word, and its account of tenant dependence after the 1830s is an interpretation, not a count.
