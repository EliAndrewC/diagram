kotobank-kagan-dankyu

"河岸段丘" ("kagan dankyu", river terrace, title translated), entries from Shogakukan's Nipponica (Ikeda Hiroshi), the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (Yonekura Nobuyuki) and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B2%B3%E5%B2%B8%E6%AE%B5%E4%B8%98-43437)

What it is: A Kotobank page of dictionary entries defining the river terrace, a stair-step landform along a river with a flat terrace surface and a steep terrace scarp, standing above the floodplain. It explains how terraces form from changes in climate, sea level, uplift and sediment supply, and notes that they are widespread in Japan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells the map-maker what a terrace is and that its surface is an old riverbed or floodplain left above flood level, which supports siting fields and houses on a terrace above a river. It dates terrace formation to geological time (the last glacial period and after) and says nothing about farming, settlement or irrigation on terraces, so it carries no figure for a village's land.
