ja.wikipedia 検地帳 ("kenchicho", the land-survey register, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%A4%9C%E5%9C%B0%E5%B8%B3)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the kenchicho, the village-by-village register that came out of a land survey, setting out what a register recorded for each plot and how it was made.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists what each plot entry carried - place name, land type, a grade of upper, middle, lower or lowest, area, assessed rice yield and registered holder, with house plots and the occupant's status noted - and that two copies were sealed and kept one by the lord and one by the village. It describes the system as a whole across the Sengoku, Toyotomi and Tokugawa regimes, with no figures from any one village, and it says pre-unification registers varied in format and may not reflect the actual survey.
