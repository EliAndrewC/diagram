北京印迹inBeijing (a Beijing mapping-society group), "“护城河”的古今演变与北京内城的变迁" ("The moat's ancient and modern evolution and the changes of Beijing's inner city", title translated), on the Beijing Daily's Beijing Hao platform (in Chinese; https://peking.bjd.com.cn/content/s6122ff5ae4b0637be8aa2fc0.html)

What it is: A 2021 article on the city moat (huchenghe) in China and the moats of Beijing, with maps of the moat's position at dates from 1151 to 1553, and an account of how they were buried after 1965.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the moat as the water body that wraps a wall, notes earth dug from it was used to build the wall, and gives a chronology for Beijing: the Jin Zhongdu moat of 1151-1153 as the earliest documented, the Yuan Dadu moat of 1281, the Ming moves of the north wall (2.5 km, 1371) and the south wall (1 km, 1420), the palace moat dug under Yongle, and the 1553 outer city. Its general account of Chinese moats is brief, it names a prehistoric site's moat width (7.5-8 m) but no source for it, and from 1965 the account is of modern burying, not of premodern use.
