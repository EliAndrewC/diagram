mitaka-nihon-no-suisha

Kosaka Katsunobu (Society for Industrial Archaeology, waterwheel and mortar subcommittee), "日本の水車" ("Waterwheels of Japan", title translated), in the resource collection of Mitaka City's "Shinguruma" waterwheel pages (in Japanese; https://www.city.mitaka.lg.jp/suisya/shiryo/nihon.html)

What it is: A short signed overview on a city website of waterwheels in Japan: when they arrived, the split between lifting wheels for irrigation and power wheels, what the Musashino wheels did, and a list of working wheels in other prefectures and the trades they serve.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the waterwheel reached Japan in 610 by way of China and Korea and that wheels in the Musashino region increased from about 1774, peaking in the late Meiji period; and that most Musashino wheels were power wheels for hulling and milling grain, with lifting wheels (such as the Asakura triple wheel) a separate type. It gives no wheel counts or head and one size only (the lifting wheels at Kurashiki, just under 2 m across), its date for Musashino growth is approximate, and its list of trades is of wheels still working or recently working, several in modern industries (band-saw mills, gara spinning, a starch factory), so it supports what Musashino wheels were used for, not how many a village had, and not every listed use is a premodern one.
