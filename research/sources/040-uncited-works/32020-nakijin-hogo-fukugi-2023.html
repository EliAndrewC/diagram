nakijin-hogo-fukugi-2023

Nakijin Village Board of Education and Imadomari ward, "ほうご フクギによる抱護の手引き -まもるために5つの大事なこと-" ("hogo: a guide to fukugi windbreak groves, five important things for protecting them", title translated), March 2023 edition, Okinawa Prefecture, on the Nakijin castle-site site (in Japanese; https://www.nakijinjoseki-osi.jp/pdf/landscapes02.pdf)

What it is: A 19-page illustrated management guide for residents of Imadomari, Nakijin, Okinawa, explaining the hogo (the beach, village and house windbreak groves, mainly of fukugi trees), how they work, and how to prune, replant and look after them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names three kinds of hogo (beach, village, and the house grove that surrounds each yard), says the house grove closes the four sides except the frontage, which faces south, and shows two or more houses sometimes sharing one surround; it states that hogo were formed from the late 1730s under the Shuri royal government, that fukugi seedlings are set 0.5 to 1 m apart, and that a new grove is said to take 20 years to work. It is a present-day conservation guide for one Ryukyuan village, whose aerial photographs from 1944, 1977 and 2020 show the groves thinning, and it is not a history of how they were planned or planted.
