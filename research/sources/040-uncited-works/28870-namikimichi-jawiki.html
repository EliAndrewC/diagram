ja.wikipedia 並木道 ("namikimichi", the tree-lined road, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/並木道)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on tree-lined roads (namikimichi, allee). It covers what a row of trees along a road is and its advantages and drawbacks, the history of roadside trees in Japan from the Nara period, and famous examples at home and abroad, including the Edo-period pine and cedar lines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the first Japanese roadside planting to the Nara period, citing a council of state order of 759 to plant fruit trees along the post roads, notes the Engishiki rule on trees beside post roads, and says Nobunaga and the Edo shogunate planted trees along main roads, which were largely felled in the Meiji era. Its history rests on two cited modern books and is mostly about the great highways (the Nikko and Tokaido), so it does not say how often a village road had trees, and its European and Napoleon passages do not bear on Japan.
