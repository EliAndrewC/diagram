ja.wikipedia 紙 ("kami", paper, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%B4%99%E6%BC%89%E3%81%8D)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on paper: its raw fibers (mulberry-family bast such as kozo, mitsumata and gampi, bamboo, straw, rags), its classes (washi, Western paper, board), a history from China to Japan, the Islamic world and Europe, how it is made by hand and by machine, and the modern industry.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the plant fibers of Japanese hand-made paper and says paper came from China by the seventh century, stayed expensive in Japan and was made as a regional specialty, and that straw paper is weak and bamboo paper was the main Chinese stock from the Song. Most of the article is about machine papermaking, wood pulp and modern standards from the 1800s on, so only its early-history and hand-making sections speak to the setting; it names Edo-period recycled papers (Asakusa, Nishinotoin, Minato) without describing their making.
