fushimi-inari-taisha-about

Fushimi Inari Taisha, "伏見稲荷大社とは: 宮司のご挨拶" ("Fushimi Inari Taisha to wa: guji no go-aisatsu", What is Fushimi Inari Taisha: greeting from the head priest), on the shrine's website (in Japanese; https://inari.jp/about)

What it is: The head priest's greeting on the shrine's own site, a page of a few paragraphs on Fushimi Inari Taisha in Kyoto as the head shrine of the Inari shrines and on Mount Inari as the origin of Inari belief.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states, in the shrine's own words, that Inari shrines are said to number 30,000 across Japan and that the Inari deity was enshrined on the mountain in 711 (the first horse day of the second month, 1,300 years before 2011). It gives the figure only as a saying, with no period, and the 711 date is the shrine's own tradition, so the page can show the shrine's reach and claimed age but not a count or the form of any village Inari shrine.
