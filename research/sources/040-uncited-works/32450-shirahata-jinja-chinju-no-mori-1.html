Watanabe Hiroyuki, "鎮守の森の過去・現在・未来～そこが知りたい社叢学～①" ("chinju no mori, past, present and future", title translated), column in Jinja Shimpo, 24 January 2022, reposted on the Shirahata Shrine blog (in Japanese; https://shirahata-jinja.jp/blog/【神社新報社コラム】鎮守の森の過去・現在・未-2)

What it is: A reposted trade-paper column on sacred and giant trees of shrine grounds: why they are large, what trees are planted there, how many shrines have a sacred tree, and the costs of keeping them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the shrine ground is treated as a no-entry wood where no tree is felled, yet notices at shrine gates forbid cutting bamboo and trees, and that of about 80,000 shrines surveyed 1,028 report a sacred tree, most often cedar, then camphor, zelkova and cypress. Its counts come from a 2012 survey and 1991 giant-tree list, its upkeep problems are today's, in cities and in depopulated villages, and the columnist's sacred-tree frequencies are of today's shrines, not of an Edo village's.
