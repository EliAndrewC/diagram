en.wikipedia "Sacred grove" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_grove)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on sacred groves worldwide, arranged by culture and country, from ancient Greece, Rome, the Celts and the Baltic peoples to present-day Africa, India, Japan and Southeast Asia. Its Japan section covers shrine forests tied to Shinto shrines, naming the Atsuta, Kashima and Tadasu no Mori woods with their areas.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Japan section says sacred groves there are typically associated with Shinto shrines, have existed since ancient times, and that shrines are often built in the midst of preexisting groves, with three named woods of about 20 ha (Atsuta), 1,500 ha (Kashima) and 12.4 ha (Tadasu no Mori). Those are three famous major-shrine woods stated as present-day areas, so they are upper bounds for a village shrine's grove, and the Japan section and the one-line mention of China's fengshui woodland are brief and give no dates or village-scale figures.
