Nagai Yoshio, "剣術道場は｢江戸のベンチャービジネス｣だった…江戸時代に剣術ブームが起きた\"最大のきっかけ\"" ("Sword-fighting schools were \"Edo's venture businesses\": the biggest trigger of the Edo swordsmanship boom", title translated), page 4 of an excerpt from his 『剣術修行の廻国旅日記』 (Asahi Bunko), PRESIDENT Online, 2023-10-25 (in Japanese; https://president.jp/articles/-/75048?page=4)

What it is: The fourth page of the same magazine excerpt, on the swordsmanship school as a business in Edo, taking students of any rank, and warriors' scorn for commoners who trained.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the bannerman Moriyama Takamori's Shizu no odamaki, written in 1802, complaining that newly licensed young men set up as teachers, and says Edo schools admitted townsfolk and farmers of any status and could pay as a business. It is about who attended and why schools multiplied; it gives no dojo size, layout or fees, and the 1802 passage is one aging bannerman's complaint.
