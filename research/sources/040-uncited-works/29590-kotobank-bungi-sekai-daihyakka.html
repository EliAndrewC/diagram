kotobank-bungi-sekai-daihyakka

Kitamura Toshio, "分木" ("bungi", the graduated post that divides irrigation water), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典), with the Shogakukan Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (精選版 日本国語大辞典) entry, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/分木-1203322)

What it is: A signed encyclopedia entry and a dictionary entry on the bungi, a graduated post set at the point where one canal divides its water between two areas or villages, with the worked example of the Oi canal off the Ane river in Omi, where officials of both sides witnessed the post being driven in.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that dividing irrigation water by a height-marked post at the division point was a recorded practice, with a dispute in the Tenbun years (1532-55) settled by it, and the dictionary defines the word in this sense, though its dated uses (1530 and 1717) are for its other senses, a compass post and a boundary stake. It is one case from Omi and says nothing of the post's size, its look or how common it was elsewhere.
