Tanigawa Akio (Waseda University), "墓には時代、家族、家意識、社会が表れる" (Graves show the age, the family, household consciousness and society, title translated), Waseda Weekly, 29 June 2018 (in Japanese; https://www.waseda.jp/inst/weekly/academics/2018/06/29/40014/)

What it is: A university magazine article by an early-modern archaeologist summarizing how Edo-period graves, gravestones and posthumous names reflect household and rank, drawing on a survey of gravestones in the Takataki and Yoro districts of Ichihara, Chiba.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says Edo-period burial shifted from cremation to burial in seated-posture coffins, that gravestones appeared in the seventeenth century and spread in the eighteenth, that temples and the parishioner system grew from the late sixteenth century, and that village gravestones came to show rank from about the 1700s. It is about stone forms and posthumous names, not where graves lay relative to a village, and its detailed evidence is one Chiba area.
