jingan-yuanli-pawnshop

Jing'an District Media Center (via Shanghai Observer), "元利当铺旧址博物馆修缮后重开，典当原来是这样一个老行业" ("The Yuanli Pawnshop site museum reopens after restoration; pawnbroking turns out to be such an old trade", title translated), Jing'an District government site, 2019-11-11 (in Chinese; https://www.jingan.gov.cn/rmtzx/003001/20191111/0dbcb0f4-714d-4e39-b136-e8979c264be4.html)

What it is: A district news item on the reopening of the Yuanli Pawnshop site museum on Wuding Road, Shanghai, a three-story brick-and-timber pawnshop built in 1932. It describes the building, its high walls, counter, well and lookout, and the pawn-ticket script.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a surviving pawnshop's layout: a walled block over ten meters high with 50 cm thick walls, a screen inside the door, a counter 1.8 m high with a railing and a raised clerk, a courtyard well kept for fire, and a guarded third-floor lookout. It adds that pawnbroking goes back to the Northern and Southern Dynasties and that pawnshops wrote tickets in their own cursive script. The building is a treaty-port Shanghai structure of 1932, of over 1,000 square meters, with Huizhou and European features, called in its day the first pawnshop of Shanghai and founded by a man who had first run pawnshops in Suzhou. Its hall and counter are a 2019 museum reconstruction described by a museum guide, so it shows the type, not a premodern county pawnshop's size or plan.
