ja.wikipedia 河岸段丘 ("kagan dankyu", the river terrace, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/河岸段丘)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the river terrace, a stepped landform along a river's middle and lower course made of flat terrace surfaces and steep scarps. It explains how terraces form and names the best-known Japanese examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the terrace surface stands above the water table while springs often rise at the foot of the scarp, which bears on where a village on a terrace would find water. Its Japanese examples are the Numata, Tenryu (Ina valley) and Shinano (Tsunan, nine steps) terraces, Sendai's city center and the Musashino scarp line called hake. It is a description of a landform, not a settlement, and says nothing of how terraces were farmed or built on; the page carries a notice of missing sources.
