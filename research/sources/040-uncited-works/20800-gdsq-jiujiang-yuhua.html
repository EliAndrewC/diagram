Chen Xiongchang, "【粤故事】九江鱼花：从装捞到养殖" ("Guangdong stories: Jiujiang fish fry, from netting to rearing", title translated), Guangdong Provincial Situation Network (广东省情网), Guangdong Provincial Local Chronicles Office (in Chinese; http://dfz.gd.gov.cn/index/dqyj/content/post_4616824.html)

What it is: A 2024 article on a provincial local-gazetteer office's site about Jiujiang in Nanhai, on the West River, where wild carp fry were netted and sorted and then raised, from a Ming grant of the fry landings in 1501 to hatchery breeding after 1958.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a trade in wild carp fry netted from the West River and raised in ponds, with the Ming grant of 1501 giving Jiujiang villagers the landing rights, as many as 900 landings by the Guangxu years, and quotes Qu Dajun's Guangdong Xinyu on sorting fry by how they float. It also covers the bamboo fry sieve and the grid-form fish ponds, but the grid ponds and the 2023 figure of 34,400 mu are of 2002 and later, so they are not Ming or Qing evidence. It is a retelling with a short reference list and gives no pond sizes.
