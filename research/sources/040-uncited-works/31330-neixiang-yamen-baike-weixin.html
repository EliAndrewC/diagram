"内乡县衙" ("The Neixiang county yamen", title translated), an encyclopedia entry on Weixin Baike (in Chinese; https://baike.weixin.qq.com/v195939.htm?baike_inner=movie)

What it is: An unsigned encyclopedia entry on the county government compound at Neixiang, Nanyang, Henan, now a museum, with its founding date (1304), its axial plan of gate, great hall, second hall and third hall, the residence and garden behind, and visitor information, honors and news.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the layout of a county office in the order a visitor meets it (screen wall, memorial gate, gate with petition drum and stelae, great hall with its raised platform, offices of the six sections on either side, the magistrate's residence and rear garden) and says the plan follows the Ming History and the Qing statutes, which makes it a model of a late-imperial Chinese county seat. The entry gives two different sizes for the same compound - 8,500 square meters with 6 courtyards and 85 rooms in one place, and over 20,000 (and elsewhere 27,000) square meters with 18 courtyards and over 260 rooms in another - and says most of the standing buildings were built under the Guangxu emperor (1894), so the figures are not interchangeable and the layout is a Qing one, not that of 1304.
