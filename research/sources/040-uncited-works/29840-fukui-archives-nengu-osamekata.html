Fukui Prefectural Archives, "8 領主と農民 (2): 年貢の納めかた" (Lords and farmers 2: how the tribute was paid, title translated), an illustrated history of Fukui (in Japanese; https://library-archives.pref.fukui.lg.jp/fukui/07/zusetsu/c08/c082.htm)

What it is: A short prefectural archive page on how Echizen villages paid the land tribute (nengu) in the early 1600s, with documents from Fukui domain villages and charts of tribute rates to 1812.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives worked early Edo figures: Omi-ura village in 1615, 51.998 koku of village yield at a 66 percent rate, high because the village also had fishing income, plus mountain and sea levies, and a Kawai village notice of 1604 asking 22.651 koku of 47.09. It charts three other villages from 1686 to 1812: a shogunal village mostly under 15 percent, a Sabae domain village in the 20s, and Torihama, in the Obama domain's Mikata district outside Echizen, fixed near 50 percent in the 1700s. It speaks for Echizen and the Obama domain only, and the 66 percent village is a coastal fishing one.
