inubashiri-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 犬走り ("inubashiri", the narrow dog-run path, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/犬走り)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the inubashiri, a narrow strip of level ground between a fence or wall and a ditch or on a slope, with its kinds: beside an earthen wall, at a castle between its stone wall or rampart and the moat, on an embankment, on a hillside, beside a railway and under a house's eaves.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the strip as a width only a dog could pass, gives the earthen-wall and castle forms (a narrow open space between a wall or rampart and the moat, seen at Hikone and Kishiwada), and says that in Edo's city area a house facing a public road was required to keep a 3 shaku (about 91 cm) inubashiri. The page carries a notice that it has no or too few verifiable sources, its castle explanation rests on a single popular citation, and most of its kinds are modern engineering uses (dams, railways, hillside works).
