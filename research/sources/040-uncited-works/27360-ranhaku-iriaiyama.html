ranhaku-iriaiyama

Ranzan Town (嵐山町), "入会山と村の性格" ("The common mountain and the character of the village", title translated), from the History of Ranzan Town (嵐山町誌, 1968), in the Ranzan Town Web Museum, vol. 6 (嵐山町web博物誌), on the Ranzan town site (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/10_083.html)

What it is: A section of the 1968 history of Ranzan town, Saitama, on common land (iriai). It contrasts the town's village-owned forests of the 1950s with the Edo-period village commons, quoting at length a legal scholar's analysis of the Edo village's rights over its common land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says how a common worked: any resident of a village could use it, with no further title; the village as a body set the season, method and share of use through its assembly (yoriai); the common could not be divided or sold except by consent of all villagers; and an individual could not claim a share of it. It contrasts this with the village of the Meiji municipal system, which owned forest as a legal person (the page's example is the Sugaya village forests of 2.7 cho and 3.5 cho, pine sold in 1950 and 1954 to build a school, with leaf-litter and brush rights contracted to two hamlets). The legal analysis is an unnamed legal scholar's general account of the Edo village, quoted without author, title or date (the page shows only that it predates the 1968 history), not a reading of this village's own records, and the local case it describes is the 1950s, not the Edo period.
