Yutong Wang, Jingyuan Guo and Kent Deng, "Inputs, Outputs and Living Standards in Rural China during the 1920s and 30s: A quantitative analysis", Economic History Working Papers No. 359, Economic History Department, London School of Economics and Political Science, September 2023 (https://lse.ac.uk/asset-library/information/wp359.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a 2023 economic-history working paper that runs regressions on the tables of John Lossing Buck's 1929-33 "Land Utilization in China" survey (which covered 168 counties in 22 provinces; the regressions use 115 localities) to relate farm inputs, output and food intake. It reproduces Buck's tables of farm size, draught animals per hectare, hired labor, grain yield per hectare and calories by farming zone, and splits the results into a northern and a southern China.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives surveyed farm sizes (national average 1.69 ha, from 0.38 to 1.12 ha for small farms by zone), draught animals per hectare and hired-labor shares for each of eight farming zones (grouped into a wheat and a rice mega-zone), and finds that smaller farms yielded more per hectare and that families supplied most of the labor, so it can bound how large a household's holding and team ran in a hand-tilled landscape. Its evidence is the 1929-33 survey of student-collected household data, after Qing-era conditions and with some years of republican upheaval, not premodern registers; the authors only argue that the Qing would have done at least as well, and say that the charge that its student surveyors over-sampled richer households has not been substantiated, citing recent checks that found no such bias, though a note on their own dataset names it as the survey's limitation: University of Nanking students, mostly from richer families, each surveyed 100 families in their home towns. It reports yields in kilograms of grain-equivalent and averages whole zones, so it says nothing of an individual village's layout, and its regressions concern economic correlations, not how plots were laid out.
