kotobank-heinushi

Mukai Ken, Yoshida Takashi, Noda Reishi and others, "戸主" ("heinushi" and "koshu", the household head and the Heian-kyo lot unit, title translated), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典), in Nipponica (日本大百科全書) and in the Britannica International Encyclopedia, with entries from the Daijisen and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%88%B8%E4%B8%BB-64756)

What it is: A Kotobank page of about nine entries on 戸主, mostly the head of a household in the ritsuryo registers and in the old civil code (abolished in 1947), with the second sense, heinushi, the smallest house-lot unit of the capitals Heiankyo and Heijokyo.

Why it applies, and its limits: On the lot it says that a block (cho) of 400 shaku square was divided into 32 lots, each 100 shaku east-west by 50 north-south, about 139 tsubo (about 460 square meters), and it quotes a document of 912 for the use of the term; Britannica adds that it is also found at Kamakura. That is the planned lot of an ancient capital, not a provincial town or a village, and the page does not say how a house sat in it. Most of the page is about the household head as a legal office, which has no bearing on a place.
