kotobank-sae-no-kami

"塞の神" ("sae no kami", the barrier deity at a village's edge, title translated), dictionary entries from Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Britannica International Encyclopedia and the Heibonsha encyclopedias, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/塞の神-68397)

What it is: A Kotobank page on the sae no kami, a god at a village's boundary who keeps out evil spirits and plague and is also a god of travelers and of fertility. Its main text is a Britannica entry on its sites and its stones, and it quotes the Heian and Kamakura texts that use the word.

Why it applies, and its limits: The Britannica entry says the god stands at a village's edge, where gods and also enemies and epidemics were thought to enter, with a tree or rock nearby. Its stone is carved with the name or a figure, in Yamanashi a round stone, and rites there include fire festivals at the small New Year, pest-sending and a rope strung across the road in an epidemic. It is a general account and gives no region, period or count for these stones, and the Heibonsha entries on the page only point to the dosojin article.
