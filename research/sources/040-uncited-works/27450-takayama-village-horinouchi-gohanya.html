Takayama Village, Nagano (高山村), "堀之内御判屋（高札場及び高札）" ("Horinouchi gohan'ya (kosatsuba oyobi kosatsu)", The Horinouchi gohan'ya, a notice-board site and its notice boards, title translated), village cultural-properties page, 10 June 2017 (in Japanese; https://www.vill.takayama.nagano.jp/docs/296.html)

What it is: A village-office page on a cultural property of Takayama, Nagano: the Horinouchi gohan'ya, a kosatsuba (the structure where the shogunate's public notice boards were posted). It says when such sites were built, what became of this one, and that the present building is a faithful 1984 reconstruction.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that kosatsuba were built in busy places on the routes across the whole country in the early Edo period to make known the shogunate's authority and the basic law for commoners, and that this one is thought to date from then. The building as seen is a 1984 reconstruction with a metal-sheet roof (the original was auctioned in 1935 and demolished within a few years), described as very faithful in building and stone wall; the page gives no dimensions, plan or materials of the original, so it supports the existence and siting of a notice-board site, not its size.
