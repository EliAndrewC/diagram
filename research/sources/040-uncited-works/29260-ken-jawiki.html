ken-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 間 ("ken", the span, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/間)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the ken, the traditional Japanese length of six shaku, covering its origin as the spacing between a building's posts, its use in land surveying, and its fixing at six shaku by the 1891 law, the shaku being 10/33 m, so about 1.818 m.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports reading ken as a house-bay unit that became a survey unit, and gives the lengths by ruler: 6.5 shaku under Nobunaga, 6.3 under Hideyoshi's survey, 6.01 in the Edo period, with the page noting actual values varied by period and region. It also gives the Edo-ma (6 shaku, tatami 5.8 by 2.9 shaku), used mainly in eastern Japan, and the Kyo-ma (6.5 shaku, tatami 6.3 by 3.15 shaku), used mainly in the west, as the house modules of today's design, with no date for their origin. The exact metric figure comes from the 1891 definition; for the earlier ken the article, flagged since 2016 as lacking sources, cites only the Koji ruien for the 6.01-shaku story and says actual values varied by period and region, so a premodern distance in ken has no single metric value from it.
