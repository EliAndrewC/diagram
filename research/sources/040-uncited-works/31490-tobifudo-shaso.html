tobifudo-shaso

Tobifudoson Shoboin, "社僧" ("Shaso", the monks attached to a shrine, title translated), in "やさしい仏教入門" ("A gentle introduction to Buddhism", title translated), on the Tobifudo temple's website (in Japanese; https://tobifudo.jp/newmon/jinbutu/shasou.html)

What it is: A short page from a Buddhist temple's website defining the shaso, monks who lived in a miyadera (a temple built beside a shrine) and performed Buddhist rites there. It lists their ranks and duties, how far they ruled the shrines they served, and the loss of the shrine temples at the Meiji separation of Shinto and Buddhism.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the setting's pairing of shrine and temple: from the early Nara period a shrine could have a temple within its grounds or near it, staffed by monks who read sutras before the god and held prayers, and the senior ones could outrank the shrine's own priests. It names no shrine, region or century between the Nara period and 1868 and gives no sizes or numbers for a temple's monks; its one figure, about 230,000 temples in the Edo period falling to about 80,000, is stated without a source and concerns all temples, not shrine temples alone.
