kotobank-daiku

Nishi Kazuo, Kirishiki Shinjiro and Endo Motoo, "大工" ("daiku", carpenter, title translated), in the revised edition of the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia (改訂新版 世界大百科事典) and in Nipponica (日本大百科全書), with entries from the Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%A4%A7%E5%B7%A5)

What it is: A Kotobank page of about ten dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the word daiku, led by a long signed Heibonsha entry on the carpenter in Japan from the Nara-period office of that name to the Edo-period carpenters' guild, with a section on carpenters in the West. It also covers the shrine-and-temple carpenter (miya-daiku), the shipwright and the house carpenter, and notes that the word means something else in the salt-works of Hofu.

Why it applies, and its limits: It traces who a daiku was in each age: an official of the Wood Office in the ritsuryo state, the head of a craft group under a temple in the Heian to Muromachi period, any house builder from the sixteenth century, and in the Edo period a member of a district carpenters' guild (daiku-gumi) with written rules, a tally of members kept small, and a monthly meeting (taishi-ko) held before a portrait of Prince Shotoku. It also says Edo shogunal building was run under the commissioner of works (sakuji bugyo) by four hereditary houses of chief carpenters (daiku-gashira) over four of master builders (daitoryo), and that castle towns set aside carpenters' streets. It describes institutions and the trade, not buildings or sizes, the guild detail is the Edo-period trade (the entry says each carpenter belonged to the guild of the district he lived in, without separating town from village), the Nipponica and Britannica entries carry modern matter (nineteenth-century contractors, power tools, a 1950 licensing law) that does not apply, and the section on Western carpentry is of little use to an East Asian setting.
