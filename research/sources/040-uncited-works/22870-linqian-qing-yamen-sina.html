Lin Qian, "衙署(1)", from Qing yamen illustrated (清代衙门图说), Zhonghua Book Company, reposted on Sina News (in Chinese; https://k.sina.cn/article_5953466483_162dab0730660147dz.html)

What it is: An excerpt from a book on Qing government offices, reposted from a blog on Sina, describing the standard layout of a yamen along its central axis and the reasons given for placing the pieces where they stood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists the axis from screen wall through gate, second gate and hall, puts the six clerks' offices on either side of the court between the second gate and the main hall, and the jail outside the second gate to the southwest, and says the yamen sat at the city's center facing south. It draws on Qing regulations and one county's gazetteer, the Youxian one, and gives no measurements or ground area, only the Ming rule the Qing kept that a sixth-to-ninth-rank official's hall be three bays wide and his gate one bay, and it reaches us as a repost.
