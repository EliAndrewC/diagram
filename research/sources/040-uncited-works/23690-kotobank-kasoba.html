kotobank-kasoba

"火葬場" ("kasoba", the cremation ground, title translated), entries from Shogakukan's Nipponica (Asaka, 浅香勝輔) and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%81%AB%E8%91%AC%E5%A0%B4-463108)

What it is: A Kotobank page of dictionary entries on the cremation ground. The Nipponica entry traces its names (sanmai, hiya, yakiba) from the Heian period, names the five cremation grounds of Edo and the seven graves of Osaka, and follows modern crematoria from the first modern furnace of 1878.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the premodern words for a cremation ground and where the cities put them: Edo's five sanmai at Kozukappara, Sendagaya, Kirigaya, Shibuya and Horokushinden, and Osaka's seven graves including Nagara, Umeda and Sennichi. It says that even the first modern crematoria, from 1878, burned wood and gathered the bones on the next day, until oil-fired furnaces came in 1927; the page does not describe practice before 1878, so reading this as the older way too is the record's inference. It names sites for two cities only and says nothing about a village's own burning place, and its latter half is about modern crematoria.
