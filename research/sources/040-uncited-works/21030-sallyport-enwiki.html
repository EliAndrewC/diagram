What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the sallyport, a secure side entrance to a fortification, with its etymology, its military use in sorties during a siege, and its modern use at jails and courthouses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes a sallyport as a door in a castle or city wall for sorties, and quotes an 1802 military dictionary on passages about six feet wide and eight and a half feet high with steps, which gives a European size for a small wall gate. It says nothing of Japanese or Chinese wall gates, and the dimensions are for the underground passages of European bastioned fortifications of about 1800.
