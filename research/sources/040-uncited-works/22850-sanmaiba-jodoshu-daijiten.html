sanmaiba-jodoshu-daijiten

Kumai Yasuo, "三昧場" ("sanmaiba", a cremation and burial ground, title translated), in the New Compendium Dictionary of Jodo Shu (新纂浄土宗大辞典), web edition (in Japanese; https://jodoshuzensho.jp/daijiten/index.php/%E4%B8%89%E6%98%A7%E5%A0%B4)

What it is: A signed entry in the Jodo sect's dictionary, defining the sanmaiba as the place where bodies were cremated or buried, and describing the tradition tying such grounds to Gyoki and the lay monks who handled funerals there.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that of old, bodies in Japan were laid out or buried away from the settlement, and that once cremation began after Buddhism arrived, cremation and burial were done at fixed places called sanmaiba, with halls (sanmaido) built around them for funerals and memorial rites. It speaks for Japan in general terms, rests its Gyoki account on a legend text, and gives no date for the grounds, no size and no distance from a village.
