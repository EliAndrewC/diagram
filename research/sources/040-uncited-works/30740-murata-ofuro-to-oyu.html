murata-ofuro-to-oyu

Murata Yukitaka (村田幸隆, Tokyo Gas Co.), "お風呂とお湯をめぐる話" ("A talk about the bath and hot water", title translated), pp. 60-70 of Chapter 2, "キッチン、バスをめぐるはなし" ("Talks about the kitchen and bath"), posted as a PDF on kitchen-bath.jp (in Japanese; https://kitchen-bath.jp/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/19ohurotooyuwomeguruhanasi.pdf)

What it is: A signed essay by a Tokyo Gas engineer on the history of the Japanese bath and of hot-water equipment, from the arrival of Buddhism through the Edo bathhouses and household baths to postwar gas bath boilers, water heaters and bathroom heating. Only about its first two pages are on the period before Meiji; the rest is on twentieth-century appliances.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its pre-Meiji passage gives a short account of how bathing changed in Japan: steam baths at temples, with bathing offered to ordinary people as an act of merit, the town bathhouse beginning in the Heian period, bathrooms in rich Muromachi houses, the Edo bathhouse and the wealthy townsman's own bath, and the remark that in the harsh-working farm villages of that time bathing at the well was usual. It cites its books only in a list at the end, with no footnote for any statement, and it gives no figures for the old forms, so it carries only what a bath was and who had one; its bathing remarks about villages are one sentence and its later sections on gas appliances are modern.
