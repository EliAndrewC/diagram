kinsei-izen-shishigaki

"猪垣" ("Boar walls", title translated), a prefecture-by-prefecture table of boar and deer walls rated B or higher, on Kinsei-izen (kinsei-izen.com), ranking page 48 (in Japanese; https://kinsei-izen.com/ranking/48_shishigaki.html)

What it is: A web table of several dozen surviving or recorded boar and deer fences (猪垣, 鹿垣) across Japan from Miyagi to Okinawa, giving for each the place, the form (earthwork, stone wall, ditch, pit line), the length and height, the date of construction, the condition and notes, with the source it was taken from (usually a city or town board of education). Dates run from a Muromachi or early Edo origin to the Meiji era, with one group of walls dated from the middle Edo period into the Showa era, with Ryukyu-period walls on Okinawa and the Yaeyama Islands.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that boar and deer walls were a widespread Edo form in hilly and mountain districts. The table treats the usual wall as a line between hill and field, and some ring a whole settlement. It gives numbers to calibrate a drawn wall: a ring wall of 235 m around one field area (which the table calls a rare form), runs of 1.5 km to 12 km around a settlement, heights from about 0.5 m to 3 m, most between 1 m and 2 m, and stone, earth or ditch construction. It is a ranked list of the better-preserved walls, not a survey of all, many sizes are the length of what is left against the original (shown with an arrow), several dates are queried or given only as a period, and it names no author of its own.
