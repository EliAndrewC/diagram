ehime-kenshi-minzoku-harvest-rites

Ehime Prefecture, "収穫儀礼" ("shukaku girei", harvest rites), in 愛媛県史 民俗 上 (Ehime kenshi: minzoku, jo, History of Ehime Prefecture: Folk Customs, vol. 1, 1983), ch. 2, "稲作儀礼" (rice-farming rites), on the Ehime no Kioku database of the Ehime Prefectural Lifelong Learning Center (in Japanese; https://i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/49/view/6487)

What it is: The full text of one section of the prefectural history of Ehime on folk customs, on the rites that mark the rice harvest. It describes the shanichi ear-offering, the Hassaku and Inoko festivals, the end-of-threshing celebration (niwaage, senba-age) and the hulling celebration, with village-by-village examples across Ehime.

Why it applies, and its limits: It records, by district, how farm households marked the end of each harvest stage, and names the threshing yard (niwa) and the threshing tools as the places and objects honored: the senba comb was offered sake and special food and the scattered grain was gathered for dumplings. It is a late-Showa survey of customs surviving in the twentieth century, mostly of Iyo (Ehime), its own text admits the examples are uneven and clustered by region, and it gives no sizes or layouts.
