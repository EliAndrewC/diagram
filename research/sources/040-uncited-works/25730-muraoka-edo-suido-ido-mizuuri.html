muraoka-edo-suido-ido-mizuuri

Muraoka Shoji, "江戸の水道と井戸｜飲用水売り・冷や水売り" ("Edo's waterworks and wells: drinking-water sellers and cold-water sellers", title translated), in "Nihon shokubunka no shoyu o shiru" (Learning about soy sauce in Japanese food culture), part of its pages on eating out in the Edo period (in Japanese; http://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-8b.html)

What it is: A personal-site essay on Edo's piped water: the Kanda and Tamagawa aqueducts, the wooden and stone mains, the shared water wells, the water tax, the yearly well cleaning on the seventh of the seventh month, and the water and cold-water peddlers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives concrete Edo figures, such as water wells about one to a 20-30 m square by the mid-period, mains about 1 m underground, the well built of stacked bottomless tubs with a bamboo feeder pipe, a water carrier's load of 3-4 to (about 54-72 liters) sold for about 4 to 6 mon, and it quotes Morisada manko and the Ehon Edo fuzoku ojorai on wells. It is about the shogunal city's piped supply, not a village, the page's figures are given without sources and it says some documents give very different prices per load, and it quotes secondary books and a magazine piece without full citation.
