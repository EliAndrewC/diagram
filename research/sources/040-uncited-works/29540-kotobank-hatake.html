kotobank-hatake

"畑" ("hatake, the dry field", title translated), a headword compiled on Kotobank from several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/畑-114626)

What it is: A Kotobank headword for hatake, the unflooded field as against the paddy, gathering Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, Nipponica, Britannica, Jitsu and the old Heibonsha encyclopedia's mentions. The Heibonsha entry carries a history of the dry field in ancient, medieval and Edo Japan (Kuroda Hideo, Furushima Toshio).

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Edo field grades and names, the Edo-period view of the dry field as the household's own-food ground against the tax-paying paddy, a Shimotsuke farming book's two-tan plot with its crops by area, and Tsushima's slash-and-burn cycle (a wood of about 20 years, two to three years of crops). Its figures come from the Shimotsuke book and a few documents, not a survey, and the Nipponica areas (2000) and Britannica's (1991) are modern national totals.
