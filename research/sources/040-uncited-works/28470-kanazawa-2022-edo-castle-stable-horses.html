Kanazawa Masatsugu (金澤真嗣), "江戸城御厩の繋養馬について - 将軍はどのような馬に乗っていたのか" ("Edo-jo omaya no keiyoba ni tsuite", on the horses kept in the Edo Castle stables: what kind of horses did the shogun ride?, title translated), offprint of 『馬の博物館研究紀要』 no. 23 (Research Bulletin of the Museum of Horses), 14 December 2022, Equine Culture Foundation (in Japanese; https://bajibunka.or.jp/uma/pdf/20230113_03.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a journal paper by a curator of the Museum of Horses, analyzing the ledger of the four Edo Castle stables of 1864 (185 horses in 216 stalls) for the horses' origin, age, height and coat color, and the shogun Tokugawa Iemochi's mounts. It then uses the diary of the Morioka domain to trace what the shogunate asked for in horses under the shoguns Tsunayoshi, Ietsugu and Yoshimune (late 17th to mid 18th century), and compares the heights with excavated medieval warrior horses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured sizes for the best horses a premodern Japanese ruler kept: median height 136.4 cm, 31 of 185 horses over 140 cm, and the Tohoku (Oshu) breeds as the benchmark, with a coat-color and temperament preference that it traces to warrior custom. Its numbers describe the shogun's own stable, not a warrior's or a farm's horses: the paper itself sets them 18.2 cm above the working mares of a Boso farming village and above medieval excavated horses (about 126 to 131 cm, with one site at 140 to 143 cm), and its 1864 ledger is short by at least seven horse slips by the author's count.
