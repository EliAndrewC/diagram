kotobank-kinban

"勤番" ("kinban", rotating duty, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Britannica and the old Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8B%A4%E7%95%AA-54450)

What it is: A Kotobank dictionary page defining kinban, service by turns, and in the Edo period the retainers of a domain who took turns serving at the domain's Edo residence or guarding a distant post. An encyclopedia passage gives the Hikone Ii house's Edo residences and its retainer counts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the staffing of a daimyo's Edo compounds: the passage on daimyo residences says the Hikone house held four sites, two of about 19,685 and 14,175 tsubo, and that a 1695 source counts 1,458 resident retainers and 1,764 on rotation. That is one of the largest houses at one date, so it is an upper case; the page does not say what a typical domain kept.
