bousaihaku-edo-bouka

Institute of Scientific Approaches for Fire and Disaster, "(2)江戸時代の防火対策" ("Edo jidai no boka taisaku", fire-prevention measures of the Edo period, title translated), in "江戸時代の消防" (Firefighting in the Edo period, title translated), Fire and Disaster Management Museum (in Japanese; https://www.bousaihaku.com/ffhistory/11279/)

What it is: A short page of the virtual Fire and Disaster Management Museum on how Edo-period towns defended against fire: the weak wooden hand pumps, firefighting by pulling down houses, the firebreaks laid out after the Meireki fire, and the shogunate's building and fire rules.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the ryudosui hand pump was used by the town fire brigades from 1764 and threw water only 15 or 16 meters, so firefighting was demolition of the houses around a fire, and that firebreak roads and open lots (hirokoji, hiyokechi) and embankments were set up after the Meireki fire to protect the castles of Edo and Osaka, with substitute land provided for seized town plots. It also lists the rules the shogunate issued: tiled roofs and earthen-walled storehouses encouraged, town gate guards, water buckets kept by every town and house, and no fire on a second floor. It illustrates from Osaka's fire bureau history and speaks of Edo and Osaka only, giving no widths or distances for the firebreaks and no regional or village practice.
