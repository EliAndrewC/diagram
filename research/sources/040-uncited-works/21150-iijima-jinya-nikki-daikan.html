Iijima Jinya Museum, "陣屋と代官" ("The jin'ya and the intendant", title translated), on the blog Iijima Jinya Nikki, 30 April 2024 (in Japanese; https://iijimajinya.hatenablog.com/entry/2024/04/30/142115)

What it is: A short blog post from the restored Iijima Jin'ya in Nagano on who ran shogunal land: the intendant (daikan), who was usually on duty in Edo, and the resident clerks (tezuke, tedai) who did the daily work, plus the jin'ya's main entrance and reception hall.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that resident clerks, not the intendant, handled the office, tax and court duties of a shogunal district, which tells how a jin'ya was staffed in practice. It is three short paragraphs for museum visitors, names no numbers of staff or villages, and describes a jin'ya rebuilt in 1994 from excavation and records, so its entrance and hall are the reconstruction's.
