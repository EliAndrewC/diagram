ja.wikipedia 大内裏 ("daidairi", the great palace enclosure of Heian-kyo, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/大内裏)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Heian palace enclosure at the north of the capital Heian-kyo, giving its size, its fourteen outer gates (the twelve formal gates and two later roofless openings in the wall) and their names, its main halls, and its history of fires, neglect and ruin from the tenth century on.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the size of the enclosure (about 1.2 km east-west and 1.4 km north-south, within an earthen wall) and the gate plan of an imperial palace, and shows an official compound already mostly empty inside its walls in the late Heian period, given up after the 1227 fire, and used as open waste (uchino) for horse-riding in the Kamakura period, for battles from 1333 and for fields under the Kitano shrine in the Muromachi period. It is the emperor's palace of the capital, far larger than any provincial or domain office, and its later centuries describe decay rather than a working compound.
