shijonawate-ido-exhibit

Shijonawate City Museum of History and Folklore (四條畷市立歴史民俗資料館), "井戸をのぞいてみれば ―考古学からみた井戸と人のかかわりとまつり―" ("ido o nozoite mireba", If you peer into the well: wells and people seen through archaeology, title translated), booklet of its 39th special exhibition, October 2024, hosted on sitereports.nabunken.go.jp (in Japanese; https://sitereports.nabunken.go.jp/files/attach/65/65567/140808_1_井戸をのぞいてみればー考古学からみた井戸と人のかかわりとまつ.pdf)

What it is: A 31-page museum booklet for a 2024 special exhibition in Shijonawate, Osaka, walking through wells found in excavations there and in neighboring Neyagawa, Hirakata and Takatsuki, from the Yayoi period to the Muromachi period. It ends with wells that are still venerated in the city today.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured excavated wells (a Yayoi pit well about 2.0 m by 1.6 m and 1.35 m deep; a Heian plank frame of boards about 1.2 m long; a Muromachi stone-lined well 7.2 m deep, dug to the level of the nearby river, at a terrace residence the booklet takes for the lords of Tawara castle, so a lord's well and not a village's), the rope-and-pot buckets, and the rites closing or protecting a well, which shows how a premodern well was built and treated. Its finds are of the Kawachi plain near Osaka and mostly earlier than the Edo-period villages the maps draw, and its readings of why an object lies in a well are stated as possibilities ("may have"), citing almost no sources (court chronicles for horse offerings, a 1982 dictionary of sayings).
