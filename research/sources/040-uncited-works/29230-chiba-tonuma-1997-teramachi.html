chiba-tonuma-1997-teramachi

Chiba Kazuteru (千葉一輝) and Tonuma Koichi (戸沼幸市), "近代以降における寺院集積の変容について - 東京の寺院集積地区(寺町)に関する研究 その1" ("On changes in the accumulation of Buddhist temples after modern ages: a study on Buddhist temple districts (teramachi) in Tokyo, part 1", title translated), Journal of Architecture, Planning and Environmental Engineering (日本建築学会計画系論文集), Architectural Institute of Japan, no. 491, January 1997, pp. 149-156, on J-STAGE (in Japanese, with an English abstract; https://jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aija/62/491/62_kj00004221890/_pdf)

What it is: The full text of a peer-reviewed planning paper from the Architectural Institute of Japan on how Tokyo's clusters of Buddhist temples (teramachi) formed and changed from the Meiji era to 1995. It counts 97 present-day temple districts (the criterion is four or more temples to a city block, chome), sorts them by size, and traces which were made by temples moved out under the Meiji city reform, the 1923 earthquake reconstruction and the post-war reconstruction.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that in Edo and Tokyo a temple and its cemetery were not separable (the paper puts about nine temples in ten with a cemetery in 1891), and that temples stood in rings near the edge of the old city, so it can back a temple placed beside its graveyard and a temple quarter at a town's margin. Its evidence is the 1870s to 1990s: it tells how Edo-period districts were broken up by modern road-widening, land readjustment and cemetery law, not how Edo's own districts were laid out, and its counts and the four-temple threshold are for a modern metropolis, not a provincial town. It leaves the study of individual districts to later parts.
