chi-2024-dike-pond

Wenxiu Chi, Tobias Plieninger, Guangsi Lin and Koushik Chowdhury, "Governing Landscape Simplification in Rapid Commercialization Contexts - the Case of the Dike-Pond System in the Pearl River Delta, China", International Journal of the Commons 18(1), 260-275, 2024 (https://thecommonsjournal.org/articles/10.5334/ijc.1300)

What it is: The full text of a peer-reviewed open-access article on the dike-pond system of Ruxi village in the Pearl River Delta, studied through 17 interviews in eight village cooperatives and read against Ostrom's design principles for commons. It describes how ponds, dikes and rivers are governed and how the landscape has simplified since the reform of the 1980s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries a few figures for the dike-pond form: the system is said to have a history of more than 600 years, the average pond is 0.3 ha, and arable dikes once about 20 m wide have eroded to under 4 m, with sugar cane and mulberry trees named on the dikes. All of its evidence is from the 1980s to 2020, taken from interviews, one 1980s photograph and a 1987 study, so the pond size is a commercial fish-farming figure and the 20 m dike and the older farming are reported rather than measured; it covers one village, and its subject is governance, not layout.
