shimada-kawagoshi-seido

Shimada City Museum, "川越制度の歴史" ("The History of the River-Crossing System", title translated), on the Oi River Kawagoshi site pages, on the Shimada city website (in Japanese; https://www.city.shimada.shizuoka.jp/shimahaku/docs/kawagoshi-seido.html)

What it is: A museum page on the system of carrying travelers across the Oi River between Shimada and Kanaya on the Tokaido, where bridges and boats were forbidden. It explains the river offices, the daily fee by water depth, the ways of crossing (on the shoulders, on a litter, on a log, on horseback) and the ranks and numbers of the porters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies figures for a controlled ford on a main road: fees set each morning by depth and posted at a notice board, a normal depth of 2.5 shaku (about 76 cm) and a closing depth of 4.5 shaku (about 136 cm), crossings only from about 6 a.m. to 6 p.m., 350 porters at each bank by the shogunate's rule and over 650 by the end of the shogunate, and free crossing for pilgrims and outcasts. It is one river on one road, a border river that the shogunate kept unbridged for defense, so it describes a special case of crossing; the yen equivalent given for the 48 mon fee is the museum's modern conversion.
