kotobank-kaisho

"会所" ("kaisho", a meeting place or hall, title translated), dictionary entries from Digital Daijisen, Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the revised Heibonsha World Encyclopedia and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/会所)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering dictionary and encyclopedia entries on kaisho, a place where people meet. It runs from the Muromachi gathering hall of a noble or warrior house to the Edo-period offices of merchant guilds, rice exchanges, town officials and the post-station's clerks.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a town-meeting hall as an Edo form: town halls (machi kaisho) in Kyoto and Nara used for town self-government, and an Edo post-station office also called a kaisho, a clerk's office part of the post-station master's house or built apart. A village headman's office is named in the Daijisen entry only in passing, and the page gives no plan or size for any hall. Most of its text is about the Muromachi room for poetry and display, and the Edo town sense is a smaller part.
