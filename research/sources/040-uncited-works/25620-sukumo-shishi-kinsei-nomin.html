sukumo-shishi-kinsei-nomin

Sukumo City History, "近世編 農村の組織と生活: 農民の生活" ("Early Modern Volume, Village Organization and Life: The Life of the Farmers", title translated), on the Sukumo city website (in Japanese; https://www.city.sukumo.kochi.jp/sisi/057801.html)

What it is: A chapter of the Sukumo city history, published on the city's site, on the lives of farmers in the Tosa domain in the Edo period. It quotes shogunate and domain statements on taxing farmers, a 1629 account, the Seiryoki, of a farmer's annual labor in person-days, an estimate of a farm's rice and barley yield and its costs, and the yearly rice balance of one Aki-district village.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies Edo-period figures: the Seiryoki of 1629 putting 293 male person-days, and over 200 female, on a 1-cho holding, farms mostly under 1 cho, a yield of 7 koku of rice and 7 of barley on 5 tan of good land with half the rice paid as tax, and a village of 2,124 people (1,865 counted as rice-eaters, children under seven left out) short of rice by 546 koku, made up, the page says, with other crops - the village's barley, buckwheat, taro and sweet potato. The labor account is for an ichiryo gusoku samurai-farmer's holding, which the page says applies to farmers too, the page itself notes it has found nothing that measures farm life of the time, and the sources are from different centuries and places, mostly Tosa and Okayama, and are named only by title, with no page or edition.
