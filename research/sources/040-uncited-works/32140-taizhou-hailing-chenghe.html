taizhou-hailing-chenghe

Hailing District Place-Names Committee Office (海陵区地名委员会办公室), "城河" ("the city moat", title translated), in 地名文化 > 海陵区的河 (place-name culture, rivers of Hailing district), 28 April 2017 (in Chinese; https://hldm.05236.com/index.php?m=content&c=index&a=show&catid=35&id=89)

What it is: A four-paragraph entry from a district place-names office on the moat (chenghe) that surrounds the old walled city of Taizhou (Hailing), Jiangsu, giving its purpose, who dug it and its widths at the four corners.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the walled cities of old China were ringed by moats to add to their defense and to supply them with water, and that this moat was dug at the start of the Southern Tang, was repaired by successive dynasties, and was last overhauled in the late Qianlong period, when it measured 270 m wide at the northeast corner, 281 m at the southeast, 260 m at the northwest and a printed "27 m" at the southwest. The four figures differ by a factor of ten, so either that one or the other three are misprinted, and the page does not say which. They are a local office's figures, with no source given. They describe the moat of old Taizhou, the Hailing county seat, after a Qing overhaul, so under either reading they are not a measure for the moat of a small walled place.
