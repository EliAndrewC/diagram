What it is: The Cambridge Core page of a 1966 journal article on the landlord bursaries (rent-collecting and tax-farming offices) of the Suzhou area, built on their surviving rent books, land books and tenancy contracts. The page shows the article's opening paragraph as an extract, the reference list and the author's footnotes, but the article's body is behind a purchase.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its footnotes say what the study rests on: rent and tax books of bursaries such as the Fei clan's Kung-shou-chan, a land book recording acquisitions of 1866-1870 with disposals to 1910, twelve reprinted tenancy contracts, and the Yu-lin t'u-ts'e fish-scale books. They also name the tenancy features it covers, among them contracts mediated by guarantors and embankment headmen and runners from the county yamen sent to collect rent. The text of the article cannot be read from this page, so any figure or claim from it would need the full text; its documents date from the 1860s to the early twentieth century in the Suzhou area, with some tax and rent practice later than the setting's period.
