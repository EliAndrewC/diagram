tokorozawa-musashino-shinden

Tokorozawa City Cultural Properties Protection Division, "武蔵野新田" ("The Musashino New Fields", title translated), in "The Tokorozawa History Story", on the Tokorozawa city website (in Japanese; https://www.city.tokorozawa.saitama.jp/iitokoro/enjoy/rekishi/rekishimonogatari/rekishimonogatarishortcut/dasc3_3.html)

What it is: A city history page, summarizing pages 52 to 53 of the Tokorozawa History Story, on the opening of the Musashino plateau's wasteland to new fields in Tokorozawa, Saitama, in the eighteenth century. It says the northern wasteland had been used by the medieval villages to the south as fodder and fuel ground, that the shogunate ordered its development in 1722, that the villages resisted, and that a peasant-born magistrate, Kawasaki Heiemon, lent out bran and stored crops to set the new fields on their feet.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the use of upland grass and woodland as common fodder, fertilizer and fuel ground by the older villages, the reason for opening it as new fields (shogunate finance), and the naming of each new field for the village assigned to develop it or the farmer who took it over. It is a short narrative with no field sizes, household counts or yields, and the map it refers to is not in the text.
