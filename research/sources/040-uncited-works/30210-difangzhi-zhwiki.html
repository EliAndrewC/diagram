difangzhi-zhwiki

zh.wikipedia 地方志 ("difangzhi", local gazetteers, title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/地方志)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the local gazetteer, the Chinese genre of books recording a place's geography, history, people and trades, with its history from the Zhou onward, its classification, the offices that compile it today, and examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what a gazetteer held and how it was used under the Ming and Qing: a newly posted prefect or county magistrate looked up the local tax and corvee registers and the gazetteer to learn the area, its household count, property registration, taxes and customs, and the prefect or magistrate led the compiling, paid for mostly by local donations and done by noted local literati; that passage cites a 2015 Jiefang Ribao newspaper feature. It names Song gazetteers, and says the early-Qing scholars Gu Zuyu and Gu Yanwu drew mostly on gazetteers, Gu Yanwu on over a thousand Ming and early-Qing ones. It lists titles rather than quoting their contents, and its sections on administration describe the Republic and the People's Republic, which is no evidence for the setting.
