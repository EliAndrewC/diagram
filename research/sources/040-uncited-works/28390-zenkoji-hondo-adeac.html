zenkoji-hondo-adeac

Nagano City history, "本堂の建築様式" ("the architectural style of the main hall", title translated), in 第一二章 "Architecture and stonework", section 1 "The buildings of Zenkoji", part 2 "The main hall", on the Adeac digital archive (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/nagano-city/texthtml/d100040/ct00000004/ht001930)

What it is: A section of the Nagano city history describing the main hall of Zenkoji, completed in 1707, from its recorded dimensions and foundations to its columns, eaves, roof, ceilings and ornaments.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured figures for a very large temple hall: about 53.7 m by 23.7 m and 27 m high, 108 round and square columns, a platform 91 cm high, a deep eave of about 4.5 m carried on cantilevered beams, and a board roof later replaced with cypress bark, so it is a ceiling for hall size, not a norm. The hall is the main hall of one of Japan's great temples rebuilt in a hurry after the timber stock burned in 1700, and the page itself notes the green timber and the twisting it caused; it describes timber and joinery, not the temple's wider grounds.
