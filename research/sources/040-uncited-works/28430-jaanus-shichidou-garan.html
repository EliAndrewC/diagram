jaanus-shichidou-garan

Japanese Architecture and Art Net Users System (JAANUS), "shichidou garan 七堂伽藍" ("the seven-hall temple compound", title translated), entry in the online dictionary of Japanese architecture and art (https://aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/s/shichidougaran.htm)

What it is: A dictionary entry naming the seven principal buildings a Buddhist temple was held to need, in the 7th to 12th century and again for Zen temples from the 12th century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists the earlier seven (main image hall, pagoda, lecture hall, belfry, sutra storage, priests' quarters, refectory) and the Zen seven (main hall, lecture hall, main gate, kitchen building, meditation hall, bathhouse, toilets), which gives a checklist of what a full temple compound held in each period. It names the buildings only, with no sizes, positions or counts, and it says the earlier term may have meant a finished building more than a fixed set of seven, so it does not say which temples actually had them.
