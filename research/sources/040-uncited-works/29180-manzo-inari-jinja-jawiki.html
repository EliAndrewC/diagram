ja.wikipedia 萬蔵稲荷神社 ("Manzo Inari Jinja", the Manzo Inari shrine, title translated), Japanese Wikipedia (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/萬蔵稲荷神社)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on a small Inari shrine at Ohara in Shiroishi, Miyagi, giving its founding about 1785 by a mountain-ascetic (shugen) priest of the Kumagai family, then going by the name of the packhorse driver Manzo, its later history, a founding legend, and photographs of its approach.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates a single shrine to the late Edo period, records that it took the old rank of village shrine in 1909 when sixteen neighboring shrines were merged into it, and lists what stands there (torii along the approach, a purification basin, a worship hall, a main hall) by photograph captions only. It gives no precinct size or layout, and it describes one shrine in Tohoku founded by a shugen priest of a hereditary line (still its priestly family), with a legend, so it does not show how a village's own tutelary shrine was set out. Its one reference is the shrine's own short history (神社略記), and its photographs show the precinct as it stands today.
