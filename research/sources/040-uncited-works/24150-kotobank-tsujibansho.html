kotobank-tsujibansho

"辻番所" ("tsujibansho", the street-corner guard post), entries in the Digital Daijisen and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, with excerpts from the older Heibonsha encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/辻番所-571659)

What it is: A short Kotobank page defining the tsujibansho, the guard post set at street corners in Edo's samurai quarters from 1629 against street killings, with the town-quarter counterpart, the jishinban.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the posts stood at corners of the warrior districts with a guard posted, were set up by daimyo and bannermen on the shogunate's order, and that towns had the jishinban instead and key bridges had bridge posts. It gives no count, spacing or size of the posts, and its date comes from one dictionary's quotation of a 1633 law.
