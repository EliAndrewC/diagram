gakken-nada-sakagura

Gakken, "酒処を拝見!" ("a look at the sake country", title translated), part 4 of "お江戸の科学" ("the science of Edo", title translated), on the Gakken Kagaku Souken site (in Japanese; https://www.gakken.jp/kagakusouken/spread/oedo/04/haiken2.html)

What it is: An educational web page on Nada, the Edo-period sake-brewing coast between Kobe and Nishinomiya, built from visits to two brewery museums. It describes the brewery tools, the water-wheel rice polishing on the Rokko streams, the cold-season brewing, the barrel ships to Edo and the discovery of the Miyamizu well water in 1840.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the sake shipment from the Kamigata to Edo to the Keicho years (1596-1615), says the large water-wheel mills carried more than 100 mortars, and says a large barrel ship carried 1,000 koku and took 10 to 14 days to Edo. These are brewery-museum statements. The 100-mortar figure is in the caption of a photograph of early-Showa water-wheel polishing, so it may describe the mills at their largest rather than in the Edo period. One of the museums is a brewery built in the early Taisho years and used until 1969, so the page is not a source on the Edo-period brewery's buildings or ground plan.
