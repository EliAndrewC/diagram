gakken-edo-kasai-zaisan

Gakken, "火災から財産を護る" ("protecting property from fire", title translated), part 6 of "お江戸の科学" ("the science of Edo", title translated), on the Gakken Kagaku Souken site (in Japanese; https://www.gakken.jp/kagakusouken/spread/oedo/06/kaisetsu2.html)

What it is: A short educational commentary on how Edo's town and its merchants guarded property against fire: the town ordinances, the fire lookout at each watch post, plastered and tile-roofed buildings, the plastered-over storehouse, and the pit store dug under the floor.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the town rules (a lookout at each neighborhood watch hut, rotating night watches, rain barrels kept ready, penalties for not joining the fire fight) and says tile roofing was first banned to townsmen as a luxury, then encouraged. It gives no dates or figures and does not say how many merchants had a storehouse or a pit store, so it shows the measures but not how widespread they were.
