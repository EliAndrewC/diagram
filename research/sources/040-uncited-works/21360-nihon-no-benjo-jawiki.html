ja.wikipedia 日本の便所 ("nihon no benjo", Japanese toilets, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%92%8C%E5%BC%8F%E4%BE%BF%E6%89%80)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on toilets in Japan, with a history from the Yayoi period to the present, the names for the toilet, fixture types, and public and school toilets.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its history section gives premodern detail: pit toilets in cities from the Kamakura to Sengoku periods, shared toilets in Edo-period nagaya because night soil sold for fertilizer, and Kaempfer's 1691 account of clean floors, husks under the bucket and a washing basin by the door. It also says Heian commoners relieved themselves outdoors and that farmhouses kept the toilet apart from the main house until after the war. The page is mostly about twentieth-century fixtures, standards and manufacturers, and the premodern part is a short run of anecdotes with few dates and no place-by-place counts.
