Jorg Henning Husemann, "Soil Treatment in Two Late Ming Farming Manuals: Soil as Body", chapter 4, Brill open-access volume (doi:10.1163/9789004748484_006), via the Max Planck repository (https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3691154_1/component/file_3691219/content)

What it is: The full text of a scholarly chapter on how Ma Yilong's Nongshuo (Jiangsu) and Yuan Huang's Baodi quannong shu (Baodi, Tianjin), two sixteenth-century rice-farming manuals, explain soil and its treatment in terms of yin-yang and medicine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It translates Yuan Huang's soil pairs (strong or weak, light or heavy, dry or wet, fat or lean) with the tillage and manure each calls for, and says manuring before sowing transforms the soil while late manuring only feeds the seedling, so it is a Ming source for how a farmer was taught to treat fields. It covers two late-Ming authors in Jiangsu and Tianjin, reads their theory, not field yields or plot sizes, and says nothing of Japan.
