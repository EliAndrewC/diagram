chinaknowledge-gentry

Ulrich Theobald, "The Chinese Gentry", in ChinaKnowledge.de, an encyclopaedia on Chinese history, literature and art (http://www.chinaknowledge.de/History/Terms/gentry.html)

What it is: A signed encyclopedia article (dated 25 February 2021) on the gentry of late imperial China: who counted as gentry, their income, their duties in local affairs, their legal and tax privileges, and their role in the 19th-century militias, drawing mainly on Chang Chung-li's studies.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the gentry were landowners with examination degrees or purchased titles who acted as intermediaries between the thin county bureaucracy and the people, running public works, relief, local defense and dispute settlement, and that landholding gave about a third of their income (after Chang 1962). It also lists their exemptions from labor service and parts of the land tax and their special legal treatment, and notes that peasants sometimes registered land under a gentry member to escape tax. Most of its evidence is from the nineteenth-century Qing (Chang's 1955 and 1962 studies). Its militia and tax-resistance passages describe the mid-century rebellions and the likin tax, when the gentry took over duties of a weakened provincial administration, so they show a crisis rather than ordinary county life. It names the Ming only for a few legal privileges.
