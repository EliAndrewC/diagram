Tokyo Metropolitan Library, "「寺子屋」ってなに?" ("What is a terakoya?", title translated), in the Edo-Tokyo Digital Museum, Edo Learning (in Japanese; https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/portals/0/edo/tokyo_library/gakumon/page1-1.html)

What it is: A short museum page defining the terakoya, a commoners' school teaching reading, writing and sometimes the abacus, with its numbers in the late Edo period and who the teachers were.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says terakoya stood in towns and villages across Japan, grew markedly from the Tenpo era (1830-1844) on, and numbered at least 15,000 at the end of the shogunate, with recent studies estimating tens of thousands. Its teacher data come from a Tokyo survey of the early Meiji years, so it speaks mainly of Edo, and it gives nothing for the earlier Edo period.
