toki-motoyashiki-kamayato

Toki City Cultural Promotion Division, "国指定史跡「元屋敷陶器窯跡」" ("The Nationally Designated Historic Site, Motoyashiki Pottery Kiln Remains", title translated), in its guides to designated cultural properties, on the Toki city website (in Japanese; https://www.city.toki.lg.jp/kanko/bunkazai/1004852/1004853/1006503/1003297.html)

What it is: A municipal page on the Motoyashiki kiln site in Toki, Gifu, a national historic site, where Mino ware of the Momoyama period was fired. It describes four kilns with their sizes and wares: three earlier ground-level kilns (ogama) and the oldest chambered climbing kiln in Mino (renbo-shiki noborigama), and the park that preserves them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives measured kilns: East kiln no. 1, of unknown length, with a firing chamber 3.9 m at its widest, narrowed to 3.4 and then 3.0 m in rebuildings; East kiln no. 2, 7.5 m long and 3.9 m wide; East kiln no. 3, 5.8 m remaining and 2.9 m wide; and the climbing kiln, about 24.7 m long and 2.2 m wide with 14 chambers and a stoking chamber, thought to be introduced from Karatsu in Kyushu. The dates are the late sixteenth century onward, before the Edo period proper, the page says Mino was then Japan's largest pottery-producing region, and it covers one site in one pottery district, so it speaks for a large kiln industry and not for a village potter.
