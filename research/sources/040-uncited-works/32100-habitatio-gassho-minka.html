habitatio-gassho-minka

Bessenyei Krisztina, "Gassho type Minka", in Habitatio, the teaching collection of the Department of Residential Building Design, Budapest University of Technology and Economics, no. V 32 (https://habitatio.epitesz.bme.hu/en/portfolio/gassho-type-minka)

What it is: A short English entry in a Hungarian university's teaching collection of dwellings, describing the Gassho-style minka of Shirakawa-go and Gokayama, with its roof pitch, orientation, frame, floor sizes and room use.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the roofs slope at almost 60 degrees, the ridge runs along the valley, posts are set one ken (1.82 m) apart and beams span 5 to 7 m, and floor plans range from 3.5 by 7 ken to 7 by 12 ken, with an earth-floored barn and workplace and five rooms on the raised floor, the loft used for silkworms. It dates the type to the 17th century. It is a short teaching summary with no sources, covers one snowy mountain region whose houses are large multi-generation farmhouses, and its "population 600" is not explained.
