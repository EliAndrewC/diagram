Hakone Sekisho (the Hakone barrier station), "キッズページ 箱根関所で江戸時代体験" ("Kids page: experience the Edo period at the Hakone barrier", title translated), on the Hakone Sekisho site (in Japanese; https://www.hakonesekisyo.jp/kids/index.html)

What it is: A children's page on the site of the reconstructed Hakone barrier station on the Tokaido, walking through its gates, guard houses, watch tower, stable, privy, archery range and notice board, with notes on how travelers and officials used them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the parts of one Edo-period barrier and says what each was for: two gates open only from sunrise to sunset, two 'thousand-person' waiting yards, a watch tower reached by 69 steps and the only two-story building, a stable for two horses, and officials sent from Odawara domain a month at a time, with a woman inspector for women's papers. It gives no dimensions or plan, writes for children in simplified terms, and describes a single barrier on the Edo-Kyoto road, not barriers in general.
