Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries (MAFF), "農村の伝統文化をはぐくむ散居村砺波平野" ("The Tonami plain, the scattered-settlement villages that nurture the traditional culture of the countryside", title translated), in its Mido-ri electronic museum, "Four seasons of Mido-ri" (in Japanese; https://www.maff.go.jp/j/nousin/sekkei/museum/m_siki/39_tonamisa/)

What it is: A short ministry page introducing the Tonami plain in Toyama as a Japanese scattered-settlement (sankyoson) landscape of farmhouses set among house groves called kainyo, and the conservation work done on it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states in one line that the plain is a compound alluvial fan built by the Sho and Oyabe rivers, a granary since long ago, with farmhouses dotted among house groves, and that its channels run like a net and also serve for household water, fire-fighting and snow melting. It describes the present landscape and its preservation program, gives no dates, sizes or counts, and does not say when or how the dispersed pattern arose.
