ranzan-tokuonji

Ranzan Town Office, "徳音寺" ("Tokuon-ji", a temple, title translated), in Ranzan-machi web hakubutsushi, vol. 5, "Ranzan-machi no chusei" ("The Middle Ages of Ranzan Town: fighting, prayer and people's lives", title translated) (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web05/c1/1_03tokuonji.html)

What it is: A short town-office web page on Tokuon-ji, a temple in Ranzan town standing at the foot of the hills behind the Yoshinaka hall, with its founding tradition and the burial ground in its precinct.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a temple's founding story: Kiso Yoshinaka buried his mother there and made it the family temple, and the temple's chronicle (engi) is said to record that an older temple at the foot of Mount Yamabuki was moved to the present site and renamed in 1184. It is a founding account passed on by the temple's own tradition, a medieval date, and says nothing of the precinct's size, layout or trees.
