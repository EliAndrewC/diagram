kumano-sakakiyama-tamagaki

Kumano Town (Hiroshima), "榊山神社玉垣" ("the sacred fence of Sakakiyama Shrine", title translated), a tourist-spot page of the town office (in Japanese; https://town.kumano.hiroshima.jp/www/contents/1520225343618/index.html)

What it is: A town-office page on the stone sacred fence (tamagaki) of Sakakiyama Shrine in Kumano, Hiroshima, built in 1859, and the stone steps and lanterns that wealthy brush merchants gave to the shrine.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the shrine's stone fence, a flight of 99 stone steps and the lanterns along its approach were paid for by merchants, with donor names from Nara, Osaka and Arima cut into them, and that a fence with a dated inscription is rare. It is one brush-making town in the last decade before Meiji with unusually rich donors, so it shows what merchant gifts could build and not what an ordinary village shrine had.
