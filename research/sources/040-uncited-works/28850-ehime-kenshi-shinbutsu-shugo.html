ehime-kenshi-shinbutsu-shugo

Ehime Prefecture, "神仏習合" ("shinbutsu shugo", the mixing of kami and buddhas), in 愛媛県史 学問・宗教 (Ehime kenshi: gakumon, shukyo, History of Ehime Prefecture: Learning and Religion, 1985), part 2, ch. 2, sec. 2, "中世の仏教と寺院" (medieval Buddhism and temples), on the Ehime no Kioku database of the Ehime Prefectural Lifelong Learning Center (in Japanese; https://i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/54/view/7295)

What it is: The full text of a section of the prefectural history of Ehime on how shrines and temples merged in the medieval province of Iyo. It covers sutra-lecture fields given to shrines, shrine-temples (jingu-ji) and their administrators, temple-guardian shrines, and the honji suijaku doctrine, with named Iyo examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that most old Iyo shrines had an attached temple run by shrine monks, with a recorded endowment (the Mishima shrine's temple held 4.2 cho of tax-free fields, split between its officers) and that temples in turn kept a guardian shrine in their precinct. The founding dates it gives for individual temples are legend or temple tradition, the fields come from one Kamakura-period record of Iyo, and the picture is of Iyo, not of the whole country, and ends with the Meiji separation of kami and buddhas.
