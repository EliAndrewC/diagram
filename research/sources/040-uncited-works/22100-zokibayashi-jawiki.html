zokibayashi-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 雑木林 ("zokibayashi", mixed broadleaf woodland, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9B%91%E6%9C%A8%E6%9E%97)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the zokibayashi, the managed woodland of oak, konara and similar broadleaf trees around villages, with its use for firewood, charcoal and compost leaves and a second forestry meaning of the same characters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the woods were planted and kept for fuel, that the trees sprout again from the stump and are fit for reuse in a few years, and that fallen leaves were gathered for compost, so that regular harvesting kept the wood from turning into evergreen forest. It gives no rotation length, area or place on the village map, says the system ended with the fuel shift to coal and oil, and notes a different 1955-1965 forestry sense; the article cites only one preservation site and is marked as lacking sources.
