nihombashi-uogashi-edo

Monthly Nihombashi secretariat (運営事務局 月刊日本橋), "江戸の繁栄を象徴する―魚河岸" ("The fish market, a symbol of Edo's prosperity", title translated), in "東京 日本橋", the Nihombashi history pages (in Japanese; https://nihombashi-tokyo.com/jp/history/245.html)

What it is: A short history page on the Nihombashi fish market (uogashi), which stood on the north bank of the Nihombashi River between Nihombashi and Edobashi from the early 17th century until its move to Tsukiji (completed 1935), with how it was founded, what it received and who traded in it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies the market's place, its founders (the Mori family, who came from Osaka with Tokugawa Ieyasu) and numbers for the Edo fish trade: Edo's population at 150,000 in the early 17th century and over one million in the 18th, and 500 wholesalers and dealers in four groups in the early 18th century. It also gives the price of a plot at one ken (about 1.8 m) frontage by 20 ken (about 36 m) deep as 1,000 ryo, and the fish racks (itabune) on the street. It is about one city's wholesale market, not a provincial one, and for the later centuries it says nothing of where the trade sat in a smaller castle town.
