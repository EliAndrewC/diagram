ome-narugi-sekkai

Ome City, "知っていますか。青梅と石灰石のおはなし。" ("Did you know? The story of Ome and limestone", title translated), Ome City City Promotion Division website (in Japanese; https://www.city.ome.tokyo.jp/soshiki/76/71363.html)

What it is: A city promotion page on Narugi lime from Ome, Tokyo: how it was ordered for the Edo Castle rebuilding from 1607, how it was burned and slaked in kilns, why it declined, and the Ome Kaido road opened to carry it, followed by a section on a limestone-based plastic.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the process: stone cut from deposits, crushed to 5-20 cm and piled over a fuel of brushwood, fired only once a year from late autumn to early spring (lit around Setsubun), heated above 900 degrees C, taking about ten days, then slaked with water, sieved and packed in straw bags; it says about 40 tsubo of stone went into one burn, and that fuel and water are why Narugi suited lime. It cites only a local history book, gives no kiln dimensions, and its quantities are rough, so it supports the sequence and season of lime burning, not a kiln's size or output.
