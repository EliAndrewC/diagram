kotobank-dobashi-daijisen

"土橋" ("dobashi" or "tsuchibashi", the earth bridge), in the Shogakukan Digital Daijisen (デジタル大辞泉), the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (精選版 日本国語大辞典), the Heibonsha Nihon Rekishi Chimei Taikei (日本歴史地名大系) and the Heibonsha Jitsu, popular edition (普及版 字通), on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/土橋)

What it is: Dictionary entries on the word, defining it as a bridge covered with earth, and in a castle as a strip of ground left uncut when a moat was dug so that it forms the path before a gate. Two place-name entries on places called Tsuchibashi (in Wakayama and in Chiba) and a note on a Fukagawa pleasure district of that name sit beside them.

Why it applies, and its limits: The Nihon Kokugo entry carries two senses a map could use: a timber bridge covered with earth, and a causeway of undug ground before a castle gate, with a 1565 castle-building text advising an earthen bridge at the main gate because a plank bridge can be set afire. Both are definitions with no length, width or construction detail, and the two place-name entries (part of Awa village in Wakayama, said to take its name from the Tsuchibashi family, and a place in the Chida estate in Chiba with the temple Tozenji) are about the places, not the structure.
