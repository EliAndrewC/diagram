kotobank-kohai-shicchi

Takayama Shigemi, "後背湿地" ("koohai shicchi", backswamp, title translated), in the Saishin Chigaku Jiten (最新 地学事典) and Nipponica (日本大百科全書), with entries from the Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Britannica International Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%BE%8C%E8%83%8C%E6%B9%BF%E5%9C%B0-63035)

What it is: A Kotobank page of definitions of the back marsh, the low wet ground behind a river's natural levee, from a geology dictionary, an encyclopedia and general dictionaries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains how the ground forms (flood water spills over the levee, cannot return to the channel, and stands as marsh) and that on plains the back marsh is usually made into paddy, while settlements and dry fields sit on the slightly higher levee; Britannica says that in Japan much of it has long been paddy. It states a physical process with no date and no place, so it supports the pattern of paddy behind and houses on the levee but not any width, depth or yield.
