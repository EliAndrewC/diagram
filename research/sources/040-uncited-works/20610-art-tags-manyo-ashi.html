"たのしい万葉集: 葦(あし/よし)を詠んだ歌" ("Tanoshii Manyoshu: ashi/yoshi o yonda uta", enjoyable Manyoshu: poems that sing of reed, title translated), on the Tanoshii Manyoshu site, updated 2023 (in Japanese and English; https://art-tags.net/manyo/flower/asi.html)

What it is: A hobbyist poetry site's page on reed (ashi, later called yoshi) in the Manyoshu, giving a short plant description and listing the 51 poems that mention it, the short poems in full and the long poems by their opening lines only. Many of them pair reed with cranes, geese, ducks and harbors.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows reed as a familiar plant of Nara-era wetlands and harbors, and several listed lines name practical use: cutting and loading reed as cargo, burning reed for fuel, and reed fences around houses. The page gives the poems in the original with no translation or commentary, so each use must be read from the poem itself, and a poem's image is no measure of how common the thing was; the poems date from before the ninth century and lean to Naniwa and the coast, with a few eastern folk and frontier-guard songs among them.
