kotobanomado-edo-no-uma

Shogakukan Dictionary Editorial Office, "第94回 江戸の馬いろいろ" ("Edo's horses of many kinds", title translated), column in "そのことば、江戸っ子だってね!?" on Kotobanomado, the official site of Shogakukan's dictionaries (in Japanese; https://kotobanomado.jp/column/1150/)

What it is: A short column from a dictionary publisher's site about horse words, which also says that Edo-period farms kept horses in eastern Japan and cattle in the west, and that Edo had a great many horses because daimyo and bannermen had to keep a number by rank.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says a warrior house kept its horses in a stable, the umanokuchi, beside the entrance, and that horse manure fermented about 6 degrees hotter than cattle manure and so suited the cold east; it names no source for these. Most of the column is wordplay and a joke print from 1791, and it gives no number of horses per house or stable size.
