hininban-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 非人番 ("hininban", outcaste watchman, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%9D%9E%E4%BA%BA%E7%95%AA)

What it is: A short Japanese Wikipedia article on the hininban, the Edo-period village watchmen, chiefly in the Kansai, who kept order in farm villages and were paid in rice and barley.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says their charge was thieves and the roaming destitute, that they were paid a grain stipend known as hininbankyu, and that they were a different group from the beggars they policed. It is two short paragraphs with no date within the period, no village count or figures and no named place, and a notice on the page says it cites no sources.
