Fukushima Prefecture, "炭の焼き方（黒炭）について" ("How to burn charcoal (black charcoal)", title translated), prefectural website (in Japanese; https://www.pref.fukushima.lg.jp/sec/36240a/aidusumi01.html)

What it is: A long prefectural technical page on building and firing the Otake black-charcoal kiln, devised by a Fukushima prefectural charcoal instructor: the firing, kiln and smoke-vent parts, the kiln's history from the Meiji 40s to the 1930s, a plan with dimensions, and the steps of loading, drying, lighting and burning.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a measured plan of a type of black-charcoal kiln (chamber 165 cm deep, 132 cm wide at the middle, walls 25 to 30 cm thick) and the loading and firing steps, with a damp-proofed floor and a rough cost table. The kiln is a modern improved one, spread from the mid-Taisho to about 1938-39 and tested in 1920, so it is a later design than a premodern kiln. Its firing steps are timed by smoke-temperature readings, and its materials include an iron firing door, a damper plate on a clay pipe, vinyl damp-proofing and optional tin roofing, none of which a premodern burner had. The text is excerpted from the prefecture's 1979 booklet on the history of its charcoal, which says it was written as a record.
