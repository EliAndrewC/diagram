asai-2009-terakoya-kaifu

Asai Atsushi, "寺子屋師匠の教育力 - 愛知県海部地区の筆子塚等の調査を通して" ("The teaching power of terakoya masters: through a survey of the pupils' memorial stones of Kaifu district, Aichi", title translated), 愛知県総合教育センター研究紀要 第99集 (Aichi Prefectural Education Center research bulletin, no. 99, 2009), full text (in Japanese; https://apec.aichi-c.ed.jp/kenkyu/chousa/kiyo/99syuu/terakoya/terakoya.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a research bulletin paper on terakoya (commoner writing schools) in the Kaifu district of southwest Owari, in Aichi, from the memorial stones their pupils raised, old documents and tables of masters by occupation, followed by the early Meiji schools that took them over.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tabulates terakoya opened and closed by period and by the master's occupation for three Aichi towns (priests dominate in temple-rich Tsushima and Miwa, farmers in rural Yatomi), says masters were mostly priests before the Kyoho years and commoners after, and records the day's lessons, textbooks, fees paid in cash or produce and punishments. It says most terakoya date from the last years of the shogunate to Meiji, and it covers one lowland district whose tables come from a prefectural list and local histories, so it describes a late, regional pattern more than the early Edo one.
