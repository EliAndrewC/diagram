Missouri Botanical Garden, "Diospyros kaki" (kaki, the Japanese persimmon), Plant Finder (https://missouribotanicalgarden.org/plantfinder/plantfinderdetails.aspx?taxonid=279920)

What it is: A Plant Finder profile from the Missouri Botanical Garden on Diospyros kaki, the kaki or Japanese persimmon, giving its hardiness zones, size, soil and sun needs, and a description of the tree, its flowers and its fruit.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the tree's habit for a persimmon planted in a village or farmhouse yard: 20-30 feet tall and as wide, a rounded spreading crown, full sun, hardy in USDA zones 7-10, and fruit 3-4 inches across that ripens bright orange in late fall and can hang on the bare tree into winter. It describes the tree as grown in a modern American garden and as a landscape tree, and it says only that kaki is native to India, Burma, China and Korea and widely cultivated in Japan, so it carries nothing on how Japanese farmers planted, pruned or used it.
