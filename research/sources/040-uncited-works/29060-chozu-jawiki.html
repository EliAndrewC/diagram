chozu-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 手水 ("chozu", the water for purifying hands and mouth, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/手水)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on chozu, the water used to cleanse hands and mouth before worship at a shrine or temple, and on the chozuya, the pavilion that holds the basin. It covers the washing procedure, the practice's origin, the pavilion's usual form, and the modern flower-filled basins.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the practice began in Shinto as washing in the river or spring water around a sacred place, still visible at the Ise Shrine's mitarashi bank on the Isuzu River, and that pavilions were added when river water became too fouled to use. It also says a chozuya is usually open on four sides under splayed posts and stands beside the approach or the hall, which supports a roofed basin at a shrine's approach. It gives no date for when the pavilion replaced the river, cites no source for that history, and its washing procedure and photographs are modern.
