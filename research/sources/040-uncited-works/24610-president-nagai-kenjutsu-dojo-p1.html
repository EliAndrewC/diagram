Nagai Yoshio, "剣術道場は｢江戸のベンチャービジネス｣だった…江戸時代に剣術ブームが起きた\"最大のきっかけ\"" ("Sword-fighting schools were \"Edo's venture businesses\": the biggest trigger of the Edo swordsmanship boom", title translated), page 1 of an excerpt from his 『剣術修行の廻国旅日記』 (Asahi Bunko), PRESIDENT Online, 2023-10-25 (in Japanese; https://president.jp/articles/-/75048?page=1)

What it is: The first page of a magazine excerpt from a novelist's book on Edo swordsmanship, on how kenjutsu schools multiplied and why practice with wooden swords was dangerous, so that form drills (kata) dominated.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that by the end of the Edo period more than 700 sword styles (ryū, lineages rather than training halls) existed, branching from perhaps three or four early ones, and that the first schools were founded from the mid-15th century. It covers the practice method and the reasons students quit, not a dojo's building, size or siting; the author is a novelist, and the page cites no document.
