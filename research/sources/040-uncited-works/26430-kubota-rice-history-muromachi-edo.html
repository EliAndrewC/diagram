Kubota Corporation, "室町・戦国・安土桃山・江戸時代" ("The Muromachi, Sengoku, Azuchi-Momoyama and Edo periods", title translated), from 稲作から見た日本の成り立ち ("The making of Japan seen through rice farming"), on Kubota no Tanbo (in Japanese; https://www.kubota.co.jp/kubotatanbo/history/formation/generation_03.html)

What it is: A company educational page summarizing rice farming and its governance from 1336 to 1868: the village self-governing leagues, flood-works by warlords, the Taiko land survey, Edo-period reclamation, the village officers, farm tools, fertilizer and famines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states that an Edo village was about 50 to 60 households, ruled through a headman, an assistant and a peasant representative, and that a koku is about 180 liters of rice, a person's yearly ration. Its 50 to 60 household average and famine tolls are not tied to any region or record.
