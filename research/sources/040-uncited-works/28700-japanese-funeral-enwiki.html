en.wikipedia "Japanese funeral" (the English Wikipedia article on funerals in Japan) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_funeral)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on funeral practice in Japan: the wake, funeral, cremation, bone-picking, grave and memorial services, with a history from the Kofun tombs to the medieval Soto Zen funeral and a section on today's funeral industry.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its history section says the burial mound gave way to family tombs, that Zen funerals for laypeople spread through the medieval Soto school after Keizan sent monks into the countryside, and that until the early 20th century most bodies were buried with cremation limited to the wealthy (marked as needing a citation). The ritual descriptions, the prices and the 99.8 percent cremation rate are of the 2000s, and the page does not date when the wake, the bone-picking or the posthumous Buddhist name became usual (beyond two illustrations of 1867 showing a cremation and the picking of bones, and its statement that the posthumous ordination of laypeople was standardized by the early Tokugawa period), so the modern account cannot be read back onto a premodern village.
