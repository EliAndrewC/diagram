kotobank-yudono

"湯殿" ("yudono", the bathing room, title translated), entries from Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, Shogakukan's Nipponica (Inagaki Shisei) and other dictionaries, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B9%AF%E6%AE%BF-652010)

What it is: A Kotobank page of dictionary entries defining the yudono as a room for bathing, with a plank floor and a drain, where hot water heated elsewhere is brought to a tub, and as the palace room where the emperor bathed. The Nipponica entry sets the yudono against the steam bath.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what a yudono was built like (plank floor with a drain gutter, a tub, water carried in from another kettle) and that the word was used for the room, for the act of bathing, and for the attendant. Its earliest quotations are of the Heian period and its detail on the imperial yudono is about the court, so it says nothing of how a farmhouse or a village bathed.
