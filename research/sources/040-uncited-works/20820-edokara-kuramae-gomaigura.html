edokara-kuramae-gomaigura

"蔵前の地名の由来-御米蔵と札差" ("The origin of the name Kuramae: the shogunate rice storehouses and the fudasashi", title translated), edo→tokyo (edokara.tokyo), a blog on Edo place names (in Japanese; https://edokara.tokyo/conts/2016/07/19/521)

What it is: A blog article, dated 2016, on the Edo shogunate's rice storehouses at Kuramae (Taito Ward), giving their area and layout, how retainers were paid in rice there, and the fudasashi brokers who took over collecting and cashing the rice stipends.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the Kuramae storehouse was moved from the Wada-kura area in 1620 and held 54 storehouses along eight canals, with 400,000 to 500,000 koku stored at a time, and that stipends were paid three times a year, changed in 1723. It gives the area as 36,650 tsubo, which it converts to about 99 hectares, but 36,650 tsubo is about 12 hectares, so the hectare figure is wrong and only the tsubo figure can be used. Its numbers cite one book on moneylending, with no dated survey.
