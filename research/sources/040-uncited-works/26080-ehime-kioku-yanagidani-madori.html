ehime-kioku-yanagidani-madori

Ehime Prefectural Lifelong Learning Center, "柳谷村誌 第六編 第一章 第三節 四 家の間取り" ("Yanagidani Village history, part 6, chapter 1, section 3: house plans", title translated), in the database Memories of Ehime (in Japanese; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:3/60/view/14448)

What it is: A section of a village history on farmhouse plans in Yanagidani, a mountain village in Ehime, describing the common plan of Meiji-era houses, the earth-floored work room, the hearth and its seats, and how retirees' quarters were placed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says most Meiji houses of the village had rooms in a single row along the earth floor, with the tatami laid only for festivals and events, a hearth of three shaku square with fixed seats by rank, a smoke vent in the roof, a retirement house built apart or in the same range (more in the west valley, said to be for the heavy snow), and a yard in front for drying. The earth-floor room held a large kettle for paper-mulberry and mitsumata processing, so it speaks for a papermaking mountain village, and it dates its plans to the Meiji period, not before.
