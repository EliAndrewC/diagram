Uno Aiko, "村明細帳とは 村高、家数、御用、建造物等早わかり" ("Murameisaicho towa", what a village detail register is, at a glance, title translated), in Komonjyo Net (古文書ネット), on reading cursive Edo-period documents (in Japanese; https://komonjyo.net/murameisaichyou.html)

What it is: A short explanatory page on the village detail register (murameisaicho), the book a village submitted to officials when a lord or intendant changed. It lists what such a register held (village yield, households, population, mountains and forests, rivers, oxen and horses) and introduces one example from Onumata Shinden in Tama County, Musashi (now Kodaira, Tokyo).

Why it applies, and its limits: It tells a reader what to look for in an Edo village register and which items it recorded, such as irrigation, hawking duty, water mills and castle ruins, and notes that a village map was often attached to it. It is an introduction only: it gives no figures from any register, and its one example, Onumata Shinden, is a Kanto reclaimed village whose register (dated Bunka 13, 1816, in the library listing) does not state livestock numbers.
