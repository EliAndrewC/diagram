isana, "播磨国駅路を仮想探索！⑦確認第一号の駅家・布勢（ふせ）駅" ("Virtually exploring Harima Province's post road, part 7: Fuse Station, the first post-station confirmed", title translated), on the blog Nihon Koro Kiko (日本古道紀行) (in Japanese; https://kodokiko.com/harima-vr-7/)

What it is: A blog post by an ancient-road enthusiast on Fuse Station (the Koinumaru site, Tatsuno, Hyogo), the first post-station building confirmed by excavation in Japan, with its walled courtyard of about 80 m on a side and the Sanyodo's side ditches found beside it. It also tries to trace the road's line near the station from a 1961 aerial photograph.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a concrete excavated plan of an ancient official post-station: a tile-roofed, stone-footed compound with white walls and red paint inside an earthen-walled square, with inscribed pottery naming it as a station. Its evidence is from the ancient (Nara and Heian) ritsuryo road system, long before the Edo-period setting, and the author's reconstruction of the road line is admitted guesswork ("let's boldly draw a presumed line"); the site is in Harima, and the page says the road's links on either side are unknown.
