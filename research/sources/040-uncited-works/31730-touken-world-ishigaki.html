Touken World (刀剣ワールド), "城の石垣" ("The stone walls of castles", title translated), in the guide for history-loving women (歴女向け歴史ガイド), on Touken World, the sword site of the Nagoya Token Museum (in Japanese; https://touken-world.jp/tips/114708)

What it is: A popular article on the stone walls of early-modern Japanese castles, from Azuchi Castle in 1579 to the Genna era after 1615. It covers the three ways of working stone and two ways of laying it, a wall's inner layers, corner stacking, and the highest walls at Osaka, Iga Ueno and Kumamoto.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated points for how castle stonework changed: walls of about 4 m before Azuchi against over 10 m there; rough fieldstone from the mid 16th century, dressed joints from the late 16th, cut stone after the Genna era; and about 32 m at Osaka. It concerns the walls of daimyo castles built by corps of stonemasons, and it says the method used also depended on quarries and a lord's means, so it does not describe a village, a house wall or a lesser lord's seat.
