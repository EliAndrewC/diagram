ja.wikipedia 薩摩藩 ("Satsuma-han", the Satsuma domain, title translated), Japanese Wikipedia (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/薩摩藩)

What it is: The long Japanese Wikipedia article on the Satsuma domain of the Edo period, covering its history, rice-yield figures, lords, samurai ranks and education, its tojo (outer-castle) and kadowari land systems, its checkpoints, a table of its 113 districts, and its trade.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest account at hand of a domain that kept its samurai out in the countryside: 113 districts (92 under the lord's direct rule, 21 held by senior retainers), each with a government office and samurai living around it, then a merchant quarter, then the farmers' villages, and farmland divided among farm groups (kado). It says samurai of the whole domain were nearly 40 percent of the population, and that the soil was poor volcanic ash, so a nominal 770,000 koku yielded about 350,000. Satsuma is the page's own exception to the usual castle-town model, its figures are the domain's alone, and the page flags one section as short of reliable sources.
