Ando Yuichiro, "江戸に単身赴任した「勤番武士」の生活って？" ("What was the life of the kinban samurai posted alone to Edo like?", title translated), part 3 of the series 下級武士の生活事情, on BEST TiMES (in Japanese; https://www.kk-bestsellers.com/articles/-/3486/)

What it is: A short popular-history article by a historian on the lodgings and daily life of the retainers who served a domain lord's year in Edo under the alternate-attendance system, living in long-houses built into the walls of the domain's Edo compound. It covers their outing limits, the 6 p.m. curfew, meals, baths and the rental-book peddlers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where such retainers lived (long-houses forming the compound's outer wall, with lattice windows on the road), that they cooked for themselves or ate out, and that a shared bath existed inside though many used public baths, which bears on a domain's Edo residence. It describes Edo only, gives no dates beyond the 2016 posting, and names no particular domain, so it does not say how a domain's home castle town housed its samurai.
