ja.wikipedia 桶 ("oke", the wooden tub or bucket, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%A1%B6%E5%B1%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the oke, the open-mouthed cylindrical wooden container of staves and hoops, with its forms, its difference from the closed-lid barrel, its history and its uses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the cooper's wares were for - water and hot water for the household, rice, miso and sushi, and large vats for sake, miso and soy sauce - and that rain-water tubs kept against fire became common only after the Kansei era (1789-1801). It gives no sizes and no workshop layout, and its history is mostly about the craft's modern survival.
