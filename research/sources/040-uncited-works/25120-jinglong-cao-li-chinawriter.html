Jing Long, "“槽”与“枥”的不同" ("The difference between cao and li", title translated), in Beijing Evening News (北京晚报), reposted on China Writers Network (中国作家网) (in Chinese; http://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2022/0913/c419926-32525271.html)

What it is: A newspaper column, dated 2022, arguing that the classical word li (枥) is not the stable or the trough (cao) but the wooden rail inside a stable above the trough that restrained the horses, from the dictionaries and from ancient usages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports a stable with a trough low at the horse's head and a rail above it to tie horses and keep them from kicking and biting each other, and it reports that the over two hundred small horse pits at Shangjiaocun beside the First Emperor's tomb yielded pottery troughs but no rails, which it takes to mean the rails were wood and rotted. It is a short argument about one word from classical quotations, against the readings of the Cihai (a stable), the Xinhua Dictionary and Wang Li (a trough), and no rail has been excavated; it gives no dimensions, and describes the fittings of Qin and later Chinese stables, not Japanese ones.
