historist-jochi

"除地(じょち)" ("jochi, tax-exempt land", title translated), in 山川 日本史小辞典 (改訂新版) (Yamakawa Shuppansha's small dictionary of Japanese history, revised edition), 2016, as reproduced on Yogo Nihonshi (用語日本史), HISTORIST (in Japanese; https://historist.jp/word_j_shi/entry/033644)

What it is: A one-paragraph dictionary entry, reproduced on the Historist site, defining jochi (also read yokechi): the shrine and temple precincts, fields and house plots that an Edo-period lord exempted from land tax and labor duty.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says jochi ranked below the shuinchi grants, were recorded either by a tax-free certificate or as jochi in a note outside the survey register, and could be entered with a yield (jochi-daka) and then exempted as a deduction; land newly exempted without a certificate was recorded as "abandoned" instead. It is a definition of an Edo-period tenure, not a description of any place, and it gives no sizes or counts, so it shows how a precinct's land was treated for tax and not how big a precinct was.
