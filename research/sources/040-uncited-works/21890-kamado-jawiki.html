kamado-jawiki

ja.wikipedia "かまど" ("kamado", the cooking hearth or stove, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%AB%88)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the kamado, the enclosed cooking hearth, covering its origins in China and Japan, its building and regional use in Japan, its heating relatives (Korean ondol, Chinese kang) and Western ovens.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says built-in kamado reached Japanese pit-houses in the fifth century, were in 72.4 percent of sites by the sixth, and were used for cooking in the west but largely replaced by the irori hearth in the east and north, with a Mito domain remark criticizing eastern farmers for it. It describes a smoke hole in the roof with no chimney until after Meiji. Several of these claims are marked in the page as needing citation, and the rest of the page (modern stoves, aid work in Kenya) is not about the premodern house.
