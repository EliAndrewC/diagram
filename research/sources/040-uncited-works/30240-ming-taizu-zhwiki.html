zh.wikipedia 明太祖 ("Taizu of Ming, the Hongwu Emperor", title translated; the article is titled 朱元璋, Zhu Yuanzhang, and the URL redirects to it) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/明太祖)

What it is: The long Chinese Wikipedia biography of Zhu Yuanzhang, founder of the Ming dynasty (reigned 1368-1398): his rise from a poor Anhui family through the Red Turban rebellion, his government and law, his policies on land, population, schools and religion, his family, and later assessments.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its sections on Hongwu-era policy carry facts about the setting's Ming model: the 1393 count of 65 million people, the fixed hereditary registration of households as military, artisan, civilian and salt households, the 100 li travel pass, the military-farm colonies said to hold nearly a tenth of the cultivated land, the one-large-temple-per-prefecture limit on monasteries, and local gazetteer lines that villagers never saw a city. It is a court-centered biography of the first reign only, not a study of any village, and the page itself carries notices that it needs editing and may contain original research, flags several claims as unsourced, and notes that the stories of literary-inquisition killings rest on later anecdotal books that modern scholars doubt.
