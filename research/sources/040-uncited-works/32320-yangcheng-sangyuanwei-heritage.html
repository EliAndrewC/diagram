Li Cungen and Ren Haihong (黎存根、任海虹), "“世界灌溉工程遗产”佛山桑园围：千年水脉顺势而围 文兴商盛涵育一方" ("Foshan's Sangyuanwei, a World Heritage Irrigation Structure: a thousand-year waterway enclosed by following the current, culture and trade nurtured", title translated), Yangcheng Evening News (羊城晚报), 2023-07-21 (in Chinese; https://ysln.ycwb.com/content/2023-07/21/content_52089069.html)

What it is: A newspaper feature, with an interview of a water-heritage expert, on the Sangyuanwei dike-and-sluice polder in Foshan, Guangdong: its founding in the Northern Song, its Ming closing of the dike ring, its sluices and mulberry-dike fish ponds, and its 2020 listing as a World Heritage Irrigation Structure.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the first banks of 1102-1110 were two earthen flood dikes 1-2 m high along the West and North Rivers, the dike system later reached over 60 km with sluices opened and closed to let water in or drain it out, and that in the Ming the fruit-dike fish pond became the mulberry-dike fish pond, said to earn ten times the grain. It is about one Pearl River delta flood polder, a large engineered works rather than a village's own dikes, and its figures (over 200,000 mu once irrigated, now about 62,000) and its tale of floating chaff to site the dike are told without sources.
