ja.wikipedia 半鐘 ("hansho", the small hanging bell used as a fire alarm, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8D%8A%E9%90%98)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the hansho, a small temple-style bell hung at the top of a fire lookout tower to call the volunteer fire brigade and warn a town of fire or flood, with the strike signals used in Tokyo.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that in the Edo period each area set its own strike pattern so the bell told people roughly where a fire was or what kind of disaster it was, and that the bell was first a temple signal. The pattern tables it prints are from Tokyo in 1903 and 1923, so they show the later system and not Edo practice. It gives no bell sizes or tower heights, and the sections on cycling races and metal theft in the 2000s are unrelated.
