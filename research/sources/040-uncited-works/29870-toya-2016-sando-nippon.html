Toya Manabu, "'Sandō': The Worshipper's Path," in "Insider's Guide to Shintō Shrines," Nippon.com, 4 Jul 2016 (https://nippon.com/en/views/b05203)

What it is: A short magazine guide by a Shinto priest to the sando, the path from the outermost torii to a shrine, and to the chinju no mori, the sacred wood along it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says older shrines' paths are usually lined with large trees, that the wood is held to be the kami's dwelling so that not a fallen leaf may be removed, and that most paths are gravel or flagstone, which supports a tree-lined approach and a protected shrine grove. It describes shrines as they are now, gives no sizes, dates or regions beyond captioned photos, and does not say how old the leaf prohibition is.
