touken-world-tenma-seido

Touken World (刀剣ワールド), "伝馬制度" ("The tenma relay system", title translated), in the Web Japanese-history dictionary, important terms of the Edo period (Web日本史辞典 江戸時代の重要用語), on Touken World, the sword site of the Nagoya Token Museum (in Japanese; https://touken-world.jp/history/history-important-word/tenmaseido)

What it is: A short article on the tenma system, the relay by which porters and horses carried official letters and goods from post station to post station, from its Nara-period origin to its abolition after the Meiji Restoration. It names the five main roads Tokugawa Ieyasu set up in 1601 and the burden the system laid on stations and nearby villages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says each station had to keep porters and horses, that these carried only to the next station, that the station was compensated with tax exemption and trade in lodging and carrying, and that the sukego levy on nearby villages grew until it disrupted farming and sometimes caused uprisings. It gives no counts of horses, fees or distances between stations, so it explains the arrangement but carries no numbers.
