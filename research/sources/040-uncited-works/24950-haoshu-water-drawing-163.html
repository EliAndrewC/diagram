好书天天伴 (a NetEase Hao account), "从抱瓮取水到辘轳汲水，说说中国古代汲水工具的变迁" ("From carrying water in a jar to the windlass: how ancient China's water-drawing tools changed", title translated), posted 2020-02-10 on NetEase Subscription (网易订阅) (in Chinese; https://www.163.com/dy/article/F50J7ELI0541B0TH.html)

What it is: A self-media essay surveying ancient Chinese water-drawing: carrying a jar from a pit or well, the shaduf (counterweighted lever, jiegao), the windlass (lulu), their literary imagery, and quotations from the Zhuangzi, the Qimin yaoshu, Wang Zhen's Nongshu and the Tiangong kaiwu.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the passage from the Qimin yaoshu that a deep well takes a windlass and a shallow one a shaduf, and Wang Zhen's contrast of the shaduf's up-and-down action with the windlass's turning, and says the crank windlass appears from the Han and that many villages still used the shaduf in the 1950s. It is a general survey that jumps across two millennia without dating its own claims, quotes the classics through its own paraphrase, and gives no well depths, spacing or sizes.
