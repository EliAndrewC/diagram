Zhuang Ruojiang, "运河水弄堂：《水漾无锡 风月无边》第二编" ("The canal's water alleys: part 2 of Water-Washed Wuxi, Boundless Wind and Moon", title translated), posted by Jiangnan Times on Sohu (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/830154589_120596020)

What it is: A scholar's essay, reposted on Sohu, on the Grand Canal at Wuxi in Jiangsu and its place in the old city. It covers the canal's three phases relative to the city, the bow-and-string pattern of the canal and inner river, the Qingming bridge district and the brick kilns, and the ancestral halls at Huishan.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that Wuxi's canal passed beside the city, then through it, and after a pirate raid in 1554 in the Ming Jiajing years, around it, when the water gates were shut and large boats were sent along the outer moat. It gives the old streets running along the waterways, with shops on one bank and kilns on the other, 300-odd brick kilns at the canal's meeting with a river (all that appeared there after the Tang and Song, at their peak in the Ming), and 150-odd ancestral halls on 0.3 square kilometers at Huishan (all that stood there at one time or another from the Tang to the Republic, of which 108 survive), so neither count is of buildings standing at once. It is one canal town of the lower Yangtze and a popular essay with legends mixed in, so it supports the general form of a water-town and not the figures, which it gives without sources.
