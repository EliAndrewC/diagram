kotobank-oometsuke

"大目付" ("oometsuke", the Edo shogunate's chief inspectors, title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (selected edition), Heibonsha's Mypedia and World Encyclopedia (revised edition), Nipponica, the Yamakawa Japanese History Dictionary and others, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/竈-39622)

What it is: A Kotobank page gathering several Japanese dictionary and encyclopedia entries on the oometsuke, the Edo shogunate's office of chief inspector, set among job advertisements. It gives the office's founding in 1632, its four or five holders chosen from the bannermen, its duties and its pay.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the office watched (the daimyo, the shogunate's officials and the conduct of the shogun's government) and that after the early period it came to carry the shogunate's laws to the domains and to order the daimyo's seats and manners at Edo Castle, with side duties such as the road magistracy and the Christian and gun inspections. It is about a national office at Edo, with nothing on a county or a local magistrate's work, and its figures (a 1723 stipend of 3,000 koku, as many as ten holders at the close of the shogunate) are the shogunate's own.
