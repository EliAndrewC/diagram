kotobank-akusui

"悪水" ("akusui", bad water, title translated), from the Daijisen and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%82%AA%E6%B0%B4-423399)

What it is: A short Kotobank page of dictionary glosses on 悪水, water unfit to drink or to irrigate with; in the Edo period it also meant standing water in a poorly drained paddy that harms the crop.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the Edo-period farming vocabulary set akusui against yosui, the water supplied to fields, and cites the Jikata Hanreiroku of 1794 for that sense, which supports treating the drain as the other half of a field's water. It gives no measure of a drain and no region, and its earlier senses (a Zen poem of the fifteenth century, a Chinese history) have nothing to do with fields.
