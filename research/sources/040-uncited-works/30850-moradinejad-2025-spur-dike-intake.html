Amir Moradinejad, Amir Hamzeh Haghiabi, Mojtaba Saneie and Hojjatallah Younesi, "Experimental Study of Sediment Control in a Lateral Intake Channel Using a Spur Dike Combined with a Skimming Wall", Advances in Civil Engineering and Environmental Science 2(2), 2025, pp. 85-96 (https://aceesjr.com/article_219298_15278c1b355b4a49c94430ae7fc7aa68.pdf)

What it is: The full text of a 2025 open-access journal paper by Iranian authors reporting flume experiments on how a spur dike on the far bank and a skimming wall at the mouth of a side intake keep bed sediment out of the diverted water.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows, for a lateral intake at 60 degrees to the main channel, that a spur dike set upstream of the intake and angled at 60 degrees diverted the least sediment and the most water, and that sediment entering rose as the share of flow diverted rose. The tests ran in a 14 m plexiglass-walled concrete flume with 1 mm silica sand, carried bed load only (the authors list the absence of suspended load among their limits), and give no field or historical case, so the numbers describe a laboratory model, not a premodern brook intake.
