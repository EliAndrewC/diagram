kotobank-tsujido

"辻堂" ("tsujido", the crossroads hall, title translated), in the Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, the Nipponica and the Britannica concise encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/辻堂-99226)

What it is: A Kotobank page defining tsujido, a Buddhist hall standing at a roadside or crossroads, and describing Tsujido, the district of Fujisawa, Kanagawa, which takes its name from it.

Why it applies, and its limits: The dictionary definitions confirm only that a small Buddhist hall at a road junction was a recognized kind of building, with a Taiheiki (14th century) usage as its earliest quoted example. They give no size, plan or count, and the long encyclopedia entries are about the Fujisawa district as it is today (coastal dunes, a residential area, a beach park), not about the hall.
