minato-ku-shugen-machinaka

Minato City, "修験 町なかの宗教者" ("shugen: machinaka no shukyosha", mountain ascetics: the religious figures among the townspeople, title translated), 第三章 寺社地 第四節 第四項, in "港区のあゆみ:港区史 通史編 近世（上）" (The path of Minato Ward: history of Minato Ward, general volume, early modern, part 1), pp. 420-427, on the ward's ADEAC digital archive (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/minato-city/texthtml/d110021/mp100010-110021/ht002350)

What it is: A section of a ward history on the many kinds of itinerant and licensed religious figures who lived among the townspeople of Edo - mountain ascetics, shrine men, diviners, mendicant monks and others - and how the shogunate counted and regulated them. It draws on the Edo-period compiled reports on the town wards and on temples and shrines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the religious population of Edo's town wards: 94 mountain ascetics living in the town in 1778, most registered both with the temple and shrine magistrate and with the town officials, and the Tenpo reform order of 1842 that barred priests, ascetics and shrine men from living in the wards. Its evidence is Edo, mainly its Minato district in the 1820s and 1840s, and the page itself says the order threatened to leave many of them homeless and that the old practice probably returned once the reform faded late in the shogunate; the page does not cover villages or other cities.
