ja.wikipedia "姨捨棚田" ("Obasute tanada", the Obasute terraced paddies, title translated) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A7%A8%E6%8D%A8%E6%A3%9A%E7%94%B0)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Obasute terraced rice fields in Chikuma, Nagano, famed for the moon reflected in each flooded plot, covering the slope they sit on, their history, their water supply and their cultural-property status.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates terraces here with a 1564 letter naming the moon in the paddies, an excavated Yayoi-period terrace, and the statement that the many upper terraces came after the Edo period began, and it names the water sources: springs, then an artificial reservoir (the Oike, begun in the Meireki years of the 1650s) with pumped river water added for dry summers. It describes one landscape protected for its beauty (64.3 ha as a cultural landscape), and gives no plot sizes, rises or crew counts.
