en.wikipedia "Edo-period village" (the English Wikipedia article on the Edo-period mura, title as written) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo-period_village)

What it is: A short English Wikipedia article on the self-governing village (mura) of Japan's Edo period, led by a headman (shoya), covering its tax and punishment as a unit, its by-work industries, and its role in ancestral and funerary practice.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the premodern facts that a village was taxed and punished as one unit under its headman, that taxes ran 'often 40 to 50% of the harvest', and that samurai were made to choose between the land and the castle town in the sword hunt. The page itself carries a 'confusing or unclear' banner, rests on three footnoted books, and gives no region, date within the period, or village size, so its tax figure is a single unqualified range (and Edo land tax was levied on a village's assessed yield, its kokudaka, which often lagged the real harvest, so 40 to 50% of the assessment was often a smaller share of what was actually grown), and that sentence, like the one on the sword hunt, carries none of the article's three footnotes.
