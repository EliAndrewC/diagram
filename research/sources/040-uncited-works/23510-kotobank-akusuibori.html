kotobank-akusuibori

"悪水堀" ("akusuibori", the drain ditch, title translated), from the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%82%AA%E6%B0%B4%E5%A0%80)

What it is: A short Kotobank page with one dictionary entry defining 悪水堀 as a channel that carries away unwanted water, a drain, with one Edo-period example.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the drainage ditch as a thing of its own and quotes one example from the Mimibukuro (written 1784-1814) of a rice-bran rat floated off into an akusui ditch to charm field mice away, so the word was in use by then. It says nothing of a ditch's width, depth, course or who dug it.
