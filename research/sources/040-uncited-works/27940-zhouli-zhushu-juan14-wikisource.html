"周禮註疏/卷十四" ("Commentary and subcommentary on the Rites of Zhou, scroll 14", title translated), on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%91%A8%E7%A6%AE%E8%A8%BB%E7%96%8F/%E5%8D%B7%E5%8D%81%E5%9B%9B)

What it is: The Wikisource text of scroll 14 of the Rites of Zhou with Zheng Xuan's notes and the subcommentary. It covers the Ministry of Earth's officers for teaching and for the people's household order: the equalizer of levies, the tutors, the mediator of feuds, the matchmaker and the market director.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lays out the market rules a royal capital's officials were to follow: a three-part market day (the great market from afternoon, the morning market of merchants, the evening market of peddlers), a market post for the officials, listings by row, tallies and contracts to settle suits, and a graded set of penalties. It also sets annual corvee at 3, 2 or 1 days by harvest and none in a famine year, and a marriage rule of men at 30 and women at 20 with a spring matching month. It is the Zhou ideal as glossed later, not a count of any known market or village, and the subcommentator himself doubts that three market sessions fit a market plot of 100 paces on a side.
