ozu-city-chozubachi

Ozu City, "手水鉢" ("chozubachi", the hand-washing basin, title translated), in "わがまちの文化財" ("Our town's cultural properties", title translated), Ozu City website (in Japanese; https://www.city.ozu.ehime.jp/site/bunkazai/59019.html)

What it is: A city page on a stone hand-washing basin at the Sumiyoshi Shrine in Nagahama, Ozu (Ehime), designated a city cultural property in 1973. It gives the basin's size and stone and says it was first an Oei-era torii pillar.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that a shrine's hand-washing basin could be a reused stone column, 140 cm high and 37 cm across, of biotite andesite, and that a stone torii pillar dated 1402 survives at the Mishima Shrine in Nakayama, Iyo City, the shrine the column came from. The page itself says the carved date is weathered and only one reading gives the year, and the tale of the second Ozu lord carrying the pillar toward Edo is reported as what is "said" to have happened; it covers one basin in Shikoku, not shrines in general.
