yamaguchi-minzoku-kurumaido

山口民俗資料 (Yamaguchi folk materials), "車井戸" ("kuruma-ido", the pulley well, title translated), exhibit no. 26, on the Yamaguchi folk museum site (in Japanese; https://yamagutiminzoku.sakura.ne.jp/3zyuuseikatu/bunnruizyuuseikatu/zyuuseikatu26.html)

What it is: An exhibit entry of the Yamaguchi Folk Museum, run by a preservation society for materials owned by Tokorozawa City, Saitama, for the pulley well, in the museum's housing-and-living category under well tools, describing how water was drawn and noting the shallow groundwater of the Yamaguchi district.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the pulley well: a small bucket (tsurube) on a thin rope run over a pulley on a frame above the well, and as other forms the sweep well with a weight on one end of a bamboo pole and a long bamboo pole with the bucket fixed directly on it. It adds that the Yamaguchi district's groundwater is generally shallow, so wells were not dug deep. The entry gives no date for the objects, and the museum's home page says its collection shows Yamaguchi life from the Meiji period on, so the objects may be modern. Its Yamaguchi is a district of Tokorozawa in Saitama, not Yamaguchi Prefecture, and the entry says nothing of how many households shared a well or where wells stood.
