yoriki-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, "与力" (yoriki, the Edo shogunate's senior police and clerical officers, title translated), Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/与力)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the yoriki, a post of the Edo shogunate (and, earlier, a word for a warrior placed under a commander), covering its medieval meaning, the town magistrates' yoriki of Edo and Osaka, their pay and their daily life.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says each of Edo's south and north magistrate offices had 25 yoriki and Osaka's east and west offices 30 each, that a yoriki was a house-man (gokenin) rank allowed to ride, was paid 150 to 200 koku and was given a lot of about 300 tsubo at Hatchobori, and that on an arrest a yoriki usually led three doshin, who made the capture. It speaks only of the Tokugawa towns of Edo and Osaka, it dates none of its counts, and the page carries a notice that its sources are absent or insufficient: most of it is uncited, though the 300-tsubo lot is footnoted to a 1981 National Diet Library paper on a Kaei 6 map of the Hatchobori compounds and the 150 to 200 koku pay to a 2024 Toyo Keizai Online article, and the three doshin to a yoriki on an arrest to a 2004 illustrated popular book on period drama (Sasama Yoshihiko, 『絵で見て納得！時代劇のウソ・ホント』).
