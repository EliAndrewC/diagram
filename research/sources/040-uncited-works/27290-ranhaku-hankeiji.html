ranhaku-hankeiji

Ranzan Town (嵐山町), "曹洞宗 班渓寺 菅谷村大字鎌形" ("Soto sect, Hankei-ji, Kamagata in Sugaya Village", title translated), from the Hiki County temple register (比企郡寺院明細帳), in the Ranzan Town Web Museum, vol. 6 (嵐山町web博物誌), on the Ranzan town site (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/09ter15.html)

What it is: A town-hosted transcription, from a 1963 file of the Saitama prefecture school affairs section, of the Hiki County temple register entry for Hankei-ji, a large Soto Zen temple in Kamagata village, Musashi, with the town's footnotes on its head temple and lineage. It lists the temple's buildings, precinct, outlying land and parishioner count.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest building list among the Ranzan temple entries: a main hall of 8 ken by 7 ken, a priest's residence of 7 ken by 6, a monks' dormitory, a bell tower, a storehouse, and front and rear gates with their sizes, on a precinct of 1,254 tsubo, with 555 parishioners (counted in persons, not households) and many paddy, field and forest plots. The register dates its founding to 1190 and, in its own hedged wording ("it is said"), to the mother of a son of Kiso Yoshinaka; that is a tradition, not evidence of a twelfth-century temple. The sizes are the register's, which the page does not date beyond notes of 1942 and 1948 (one a figure of 1,355.87 tsubo dated 1948, set beside the residence's line), and the page covers one temple.
