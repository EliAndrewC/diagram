kotobank-kosatsuba

"高札場" ("kosatsuba", notice-board site), in the Digital Daijisen, the Concise Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%AB%98%E6%9C%AD%E5%A0%B4-495282)

What it is: A Kotobank page defining the kosatsuba, the place where the edict boards were posted, with excerpts from the encyclopedia's entries on kosatsu, the Koraibashi bridge in Osaka and tsuji (crossroads).

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the boards stood at central crossroads, gates and bridge ends, that Edo had six main sites and thirty-five others, fenced with a stockade or raised on a stone wall or turf bank, and that Osaka's Koraibashi site displayed eight kinds of notice. The counts are Edo's and Osaka's, the great cities, and the page does not say how a village or small town placed or built its board.
