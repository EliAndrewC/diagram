marshman-1862-dharwar

J. C. Marshman, "Notes on the Cultivation of Cotton in the District of Dharwar; Past, Present, and Future", Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society of Great Britain and Ireland 19 (January 1862): 351-360 (https://zenodo.org/record/1742888/files/article.pdf)

What it is: A paper read to the Royal Asiatic Society in March 1862 and compiled in England from others' reports: a Collector's and an American planter's of the 1840s and a Manchester Cotton Company agent's letter of November 1861. It covers the East India Company's failed and then successful trials of American cotton in one district of the Bombay Presidency, the seed's later spread under private trade, and the case for roads and a port to carry the crop to England.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated acreages for the one district in India found where American seed took hold. Those acreages are for Dharwar alone, though it reports the seed spreading into neighboring territories. Its largest American figure, more than 178,000 acres in "the last year", is given as "stated", not as measured; the 379,000 acres of 1861-62 is all cotton, American and native. The figures reach the author secondhand, and the paper argues for English investment in Indian cotton, so its account of the success is a promoter's. The spread depended on English export demand and on saw gins from a Dharwar factory, because the native foot-roller could not clean this cotton. It is a colonial-era pattern, and neither its pace nor its acreage says how fast such a crop would spread in the setting.
