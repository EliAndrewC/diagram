kasama-iwama-jinya

Kasama City, 「Exploring Kasama's history」 (translated; original: 「笠間の歴史探訪」) (in Japanese; https://www.city.kasama.lg.jp/sp/page/page006384.html)

What it is: A page of Kasama City's (Ibaraki) local-history series, collecting ten short articles by local historians; the tenth, by Matsushima Shigeru (February 2013), is on the Iwama jin'ya, the district office the Tsuchiya house (later lords of Tsuchiura domain) is said to have placed there in 1658, where a magistrate and their officials, six in all, lived. It places the compound by an 1827 picture map of Atago-yama and its roads, and describes its grounds of about one chō (a hectare), its gate, walls and fence, and what stood inside, without saying where each detail comes from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one official compound read that keeps a vegetable garden inside its enclosure - about two tan of it, worked, it is said, by the jin'ya's own officials. Its limits are that it reports a tradition ("it is said"), not a plan; that it does not say where in the grounds the garden lay; that the grounds were closed by a bamboo fence, with earthen wall only beside the gatehouse and the white wall round the office yard alone, so the garden lay inside the compound's fence, not inside a wall; that the layout's other details, the well right of the formal entrance among them, are given without their source, the 1827 map being cited only for where the jin'ya stood; and that it is one jin'ya, said to be larger than its domain's other district offices, with more than twice their buildings and rooms.

Used for: whether an official compound kept a vegetable garden inside its walls; the well on the right of the formal entrance inside the gate (Estates and other compounds).
