Sir George Staunton, An Authentic Account of an Embassy from the King of Great Britain to the Emperor of China, volume 2 (London, 1797), full text on the Internet Archive (https://archive.org/stream/authenticaccount02stau/authenticaccount02stau_djvu.txt)

What it is: The official account of the British embassy to the Emperor of China, taken chiefly from the ambassador's papers and printed in London in 1797; the second volume follows the embassy's journey south through the interior to Canton and describes Chinese farming along the way.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness account printed before modern times, so what it saw of farm practice is a premodern attestation - here, earthen jars buried to the rim on farms and near paths and roads to collect night soil. Its limits: the travelers were foreigners passing through; the manure passage is written of Chinese practice in general and names no province, so it cannot place the jars in any one region; the online text is an uncorrected machine reading of the scan, with broken words and run-together letters.

Used for: manure jars sunk in the ground on farms and by paths and roads in eighteenth-century China (Field archetypes)
