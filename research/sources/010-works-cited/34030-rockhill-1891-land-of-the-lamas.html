William Woodville Rockhill, The Land of the Lamas: Notes of a Journey through China, Mongolia and Tibet, New York: The Century Co., 1891 (Cornell University Library scan; https://archive.org/stream/cu31924098819877/cu31924098819877_djvu.txt)

What it is: An American diplomat's account of his journey of 1888-1889 from Beijing through Gansu, the Kokonor and eastern Tibet to Ta-chien-lu (Kangding) in Sichuan and down to Ya-chou (Ya'an), with his notes on the brick-tea trade between them.

Why it applies, and its limits: He saw the tea porters on the road and a Ya-chou packing house himself, and gives their loads, their pay a package, the days on the road and prices at both ends in silver. His grade-by-grade price list from Ta-chien-lu to Lhasa is not his own: he quotes it from Father Desgodins' pamphlet of 1883, in Indian rupees. His Ya-chou price for the best tea, Tls. 2 a pao, is the packing-house manager's word, and Rockhill thinks it exaggerated (p. 311 n.): at Ta-chien-lu, the far end of the porters' road, he paid Re. 1 (about Tl. 0.32 by his own rate) for one brick of the same first grade, a quarter of a pao (p. 279 n.). His other Ta-chien-lu price, Tl. 0.24 a brick, is for the third grade (p. 280 n.).

Used for: the tea porters between Ya-chou and Ta-chien-lu, and tea prices from Ya-chou to Lhasa (Money and prices)
