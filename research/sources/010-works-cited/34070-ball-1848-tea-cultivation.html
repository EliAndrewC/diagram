ball-1848-tea-cultivation

Samuel Ball, An Account of the Cultivation and Manufacture of Tea in China, Derived from Personal Observation during an Official Residence in that Country from 1804 to 1826, London, 1848 (archive.org; https://archive.org/stream/accountofcultiva00ballrich/accountofcultiva00ballrich_djvu.txt)

What it is: A book by the East India Company's former inspector of teas at Canton on how tea was grown, made and sold in China, with the cost of black tea from the hills of the Wuyi (Bohea) country to Canton and London.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the cost of a picul of Congou at its source, its packing, its carriage to Canton and the charges there, and day and month wages in south China, from a witness of the trade. The carriage passage is his paper first printed at Macao in 1816, the cost table is for the Company's years at Canton, which ended in 1834, and the wages are given in English money at his reckoning of the Spanish dollar and the tael. The cost at the source is his estimate, set beside Pigou's 11 taels of fifty years before, from accounts he admits he could not wholly reconcile. The London figure is the price at the Company's sales, before the English duty and the grocer's margin. He wrote to aid the growing of tea in British India, setting China's costs and wages against India's.

Used for: Wuyi Congou tea's cost at origin, its carriage and its price at Canton and London, with the wages beside it (Money and prices)
