Philip C. C. Huang, "Further Thoughts on Eighteenth-Century Britain and China: Rejoinder to Pomeranz's Response to My Critique", with Kenneth Pomeranz's reply, Communications to the Editor, Journal of Asian Studies vol. 62, no. 1, 2003, on Cambridge Core (https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/86E4245B44EE04011FC293FCEE4816EE/S0021911800000917a.pdf/communications-to-the-editor.pdf)

What it is: An exchange between two historians over the Yangzi delta's economy in the 1700s, in which they settle how the days to make a bolt of cotton cloth divided between spinning and weaving and count the days of labor for a mu of rice.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the 7-day bolt with its split, agreed by both sides, and Jiang Gao's 1834 rate for husking rice. It is a debate, so each figure is one side's reading of earlier sources (Jiang Gao, Fei, the Mantetsu survey of Wuxi in 1941); the bolt is cotton for sale, counted from ginned cotton (fluffing, spinning, weaving and sizing, not growing or ginning); Jiang's husking rate is a most ("at most one shi in a day"), not a usual day's work; and Huang notes that husking was generally done at a mill.

Used for: the 7-day bolt of cotton and its split; one person's day of husking rice (Money and prices)
