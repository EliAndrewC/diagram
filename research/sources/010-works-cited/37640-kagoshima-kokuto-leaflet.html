kagoshima-kokuto-leaflet

Kagoshima Prefecture, "漫画で読む 黒糖物語" ("The story of black sugar, told in comics", title translated), a two-page spread, pp. 12-13, of an illustrated booklet on black sugar in the islands south of Kyushu, published after the islands' way of making black sugar was listed as a cultural property in 2024 (in Japanese; https://www.pref.kagoshima.jp/ba08/kokuto/documents/128908_20260617134239-1.pdf)

What it is: A prefectural booklet for the general reader. Its history column, by Takehara Yuki, head of the Tokunoshima town history office, names the studies it draws on. It reports, from two documents of 1830 as a 1983 study by Matsushita Shiro reads them, how much dearer goods were for the Amami islanders than at Osaka's market, and, from a 2008 study by Kito Hiroshi, what share of the sugar gathered at Osaka in 1830-1832 was Satsuma's (about 51 percent, most of it from Amami and Ryukyu).

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the one ratio found between the islands' end and Osaka's for Satsuma's sugar: how much more sugar an islander gave for a koku of rice than the Osaka market would ask. It is a summary at second hand, with no price at either end, and it does not say which grades, islands or months the range covers. The island rate is not a market's: under the domain's monopoly the islanders had to sell their sugar to Satsuma and could not trade even their leftover sugar with anyone else, so they took rice and goods on the domain's terms, which the column calls an unequal exchange. The ratio measures the domain's take, not a merchant's margin or the cost of freight. The study it rests on was not read.

Used for: how much more sugar an Amami islander gave for rice than Osaka's market asked, in 1830 (Money and prices)
