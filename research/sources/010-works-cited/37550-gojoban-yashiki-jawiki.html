ja.wikipedia 御城番屋敷 ("castle guards' residences", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/御城番屋敷)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the two rowhouses built at Matsusaka in 1863 for the Kishu domain's castle guards and their families, which still stand and are still lived in; it gives each row's length along the ridge, its count of dwellings and the standard size of a dwelling.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the length of two surviving warriors' dwelling rows, 90.9 m and 83.6 m, the longest rows the record holds a quoted figure for. The rows housed retainers of 40 koku with their families, at a standard of 5 ken of frontage and 5 ken of depth a dwelling, so they are larger dwellings than a foot soldier's or a junior official's. The article counts the dwellings that remain (10 and 9, where its history names 20 guards) and does not say how many were built, and 5 ken is the standard, not every dwelling's width: the west row's 83.6 m is about 46 ken for its 9 dwellings, so a dwelling's width is not the row's length divided by its count; they date from the last years of the Edo period, and the article is a stub that cites the national register for the designation only, not for the lengths.

Used for: the length of the Matsusaka castle guards' rows, their dwellings and their date (Estates and other compounds)
