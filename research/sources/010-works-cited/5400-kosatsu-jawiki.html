kosatsu-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 高札 ("public notice board", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高札)

What it is: Japanese Wikipedia's article on 高札 (kosatsu), the boards on which laws, carriage-fee schedules and prohibitions were written up and posted publicly, and on the kosatsuba where those boards stood.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the edict-board as an institution, the shogunate's order siting the boards at conspicuous places - points of heavy traffic, barrier stations, ports, the ends of large bridges, and the entrances and centers of towns and villages - and Edo's six great boards, Nihonbashi first among them, which is what puts a notice board on every map and tells us where. Its limits are that it lacks sufficient citations, so the siting rule that governs a feature on every map rests on thin references; that it is taken for the institution and the rule and not for a number; and that its counts - the six great boards and 35 others - are Edo's, the six hedged even there.

Used for: the edict board institution - standing law, rate tables, ban edicts; Nihonbashi as the first of Edo's six great boards; where the boards stood; notices at the gates of courts and prefectural offices after the boards ended; a board written in ink on wood, re-inked only with permission, and the lords' own boards (Estates and other compounds; Trades and services)
