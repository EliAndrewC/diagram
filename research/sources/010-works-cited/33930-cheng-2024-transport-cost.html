Ruoran Cheng, "Transport cost in the Great Divergence: Yangtze China vs England", London School of Economics, Economic History Working Papers 371 (2024) (https://www.lse.ac.uk/asset-library/information/wp371.pdf)

What it is: A working paper, not yet refereed, by an LSE doctoral student in economic history, estimating freight costs in the middle and lower Yangzi in the late 1600s and 1700s, set against England's, built from a merchants' route book of 1694 and the Qing statutes' official freight rates of 1738-1776, with appendix tables of every rate he used in its original units.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one work read here that states a ratio of water to land carriage for Qing China and prints the official rates behind it, province by province. Its summary tables are his conversions to taels per 100 kg per 100 km, which rest on his conversions of a shi of grain to 84 kg (after Peng Xinwei) and of a li to 500 m; the land rates are averaged across provinces that vary, and the laborer's wage he divides by is another historian's estimate for the end of the 1600s. His one-to-ten ratio is water against a pack mule on flat land in the Yangzi country: by his own table a cart cost about a third of the mule's rate, a porter about seven times it and a hill road about twice it, and his figures for Zhili in 1812 give about one to seven. The statute rates are what the Qing state paid carriers to move its own grain, not what merchants paid; he sets aside the halved river rates of 1776 as a political act rather than a market price.

Used for: the ratio of water to land carriage in Qing China, and the official freight rates by province (Money and prices)
