Ruoran Cheng, "Transport cost in the Great Divergence: Yangtze China vs England", London School of Economics, Economic History Working Papers 371 (2024) (https://www.lse.ac.uk/asset-library/information/wp371.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's estimate of freight costs in the lower Yangzi in the late 1600s and 1700s, set against England's, built from a merchants' route book of 1694 and the Qing statutes' official freight rates of 1738-1776, with appendix tables of every rate he used in its original units.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one work read here that states a ratio of water to land carriage for Qing China and prints the official rates behind it, province by province. Its summary tables are his conversions to taels per 100 kg per 100 km, which rest on his own weights for a shi and lengths for a li; the land rates are averaged across provinces that vary, and the laborer's wage he divides by is another historian's estimate for the end of the 1600s.

Used for: the ratio of water to land carriage in Qing China, and the official freight rates by province (Money and prices)
