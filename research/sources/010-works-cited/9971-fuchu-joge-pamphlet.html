fuchu-joge-pamphlet

Fuchū City (Hiroshima), 上下陣屋（代官所）のようす / 上下陣屋（代官所）の痕跡 (The Jōge jin'ya (intendant's office) as it was, and its traces), a two-page pamphlet (in Japanese; https://www.city.fuchu.hiroshima.jp/material/files/group/23/daikanpanfu2.pdf)

What it is: An illustrated pamphlet by the city of Fuchū on the shogunate's post at Jōge, which it calls an office set up in a governed territory away from the intendant's office. The city's designation record for the site (fuchu-joge-daikansho-ato) says whose: an outpost of the Iwami Ōmori intendant's office from 1717 to the end of the Edo period. Its first page reproduces a drawing of the post made in 1869 (Meiji 2), taken from the Jōge town history of 2003, with the rooms, gates, shrines and court labeled; its second reports the excavation of 2008 to 2010.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the plan of a branch office (出張陣屋) of an intendant's office (Ōmori's, by the city's designation record), which the pamphlet likens to a sub-office, staffed in 1867 by three officials who "had come" there. That is the scale of a small county post on our sheets, but it was an outpost staffed from elsewhere, not a seat with its own magistrate, and a seat would have been larger. Its limits: it is a pamphlet written for a general and young audience, several of its statements - the two shrines and the three officials among them - sit in a cartoon character's speech bubbles, and the plan is a drawing of 1869, after the shogunate fell, not an excavation plan; the dig confirmed walls, steps, ditches and foundation stones, not the shrines, and the pamphlet hedges the date of the well it found. The drawing gives no gate width. Its measures are the site's 35 by 14 ken (the dig estimates 70 by 27 m), each room's size in tatami, the central building's "about 80 tsubo", and the stone walls' heights of 5 to 9 shaku. The two shrines are labeled on the drawing itself as well as named in a speech bubble.

Used for: the branch office's two shrines, its central building, its white-gravel court and its three officials; its main gate with a small gate and gatekeepers' room; its entrance facing the ground inside the gate; the room behind the step and the court to the south of the entrance's row; the excavated site (Estates and other compounds)
