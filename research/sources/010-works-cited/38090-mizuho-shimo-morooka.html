mizuho-shimo-morooka

Mizuho Town Library, 瑞穂の地名 (The place names of Mizuho), "三、下師岡新田 (一)" ("3. Shimo-Moro'oka Shinden (1)", title translated), pp. 29-30, on the ADEAC regional archive (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/mizuho-lib/texthtml/d100070/mp000070-100070/ht000830)

What it is: A town library's book of local place names. Its entry on one Edo-period new-field settlement in western Tokyo opens with who cleared it and how its house lots, dry fields and woods were allotted between two highways, then explains the settlement's place names.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names a departure from the rectangular strip: a strip slightly wider at its woodland end than at its house lot (14 ken against 12, which the page puts down to this site's ground, calling the strips nearly rectangular), and, where the two highways drew together, shallower strips made up with irregular land elsewhere. It is one upland settlement of dry fields and woods, watered by a well, the page gives no date for the allotment, and no canal enters it.

Used for: a strip widening toward its back (Fields)
