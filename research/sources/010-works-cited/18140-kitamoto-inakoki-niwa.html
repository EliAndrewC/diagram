kitamoto-inakoki-niwa

北本市史 民俗編 ("Kitamoto City History, Folklore Volume", title translated), threshing and cleaning the rice (https://kdas.jp/detail_display.php?t_cd=1&acc_cd=3&aclc_cd=0&chap=3&hp_page=48&bc_cd=1)

What it is: A page of the folklore volume of the published city history of Kitamoto in Saitama prefecture, read in the city's own digital archive: the rice harvest's threshing, drying and husking as the district's oldest farmers remembered them, from the hand-drawn threshing comb to the first powered threshers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where each step of the harvest was done - threshing in the yard, the grain dried on mats filling it, husking indoors - which is what the yard's drawing has to show. Its limits are era and place: practice remembered by farmers born about 1900, told without separating the threshing-comb years from the treadle and engine threshers that followed into the 1940s, so the yard full of mats and the 100 to 150 mats of a large farm are figures of that later time - the form is older than the count; and one Kanto district, a region where rack and ground drying were both in use.

Used for: threshing in the yard; mats spread over the whole yard to dry the grain; 100 to 150 mats on a large farm; husking in the earthen-floored room or the barn
