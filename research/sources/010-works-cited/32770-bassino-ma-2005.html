bassino-ma-2005

Jean-Pascal Bassino and Debin Ma, "Japanese Unskilled Wages in International Perspective, 1741-1913", Research in Economic History 23 (2005), pp. 229-248, on Debin Ma's site (https://www.econhistdbm.com/uploads/8/1/9/7/81977286/reh_japanese_wages_bassino_and_ma.pdf)

What it is: A study by two economic historians of the day wages of unskilled laborers in Kyoto from 1741 and in Tokyo to 1913, the Tokugawa wages taken from the Mitsui house's account books and the Meiji ones from Japan's Long-Term Economic Statistics, set against consumption baskets they reconstructed for Japan and priced in grams of silver, so that the wages can be compared with Europe's.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source of the Kyoto day wage of the mid-1700s and of the year's basket of goods priced against it. Its baskets are the authors' reconstruction, built to supply set amounts of calories and protein from consumption data of the early 1700s, the late 1800s and the 1920s, not a budget anyone recorded; only the rice and lamp-oil prices of 1745-1754 were recorded in Kyoto, and the rest are derived from later price ratios or stand-ins; and the 250 working days a year it mentions belong to the earlier comparison it describes, not to its own measure, which sets a day's wage against a year's basket. That basket is one adult's at an average diet, not a household's: it has nothing for fuel, rent or dependants, and the subsistence basket it also builds costs 236 grams of silver for 1745-1754 against the average basket's 326 (its note 8 labels the 236 "basket A", plainly a slip for B, since A is 326 in its Table 3), about 28 percent less (the text's "roughly 40 percent cheaper" is how much more the average basket costs). Its Tokugawa wage is Kyoto's alone; the Edo series in the same account books (1818-1871) is said only to be broadly equal in level, with a different trend, and is not given separately.

Used for: the Kyoto laborer's year of goods, its prices and the form of each good, and the Kyoto and Edo day wage (Money and prices)
