bassino-ma-2005

Jean-Pascal Bassino and Debin Ma, "Japanese Unskilled Wages in International Perspective, 1741-1913", Research in Economic History 23 (2005), pp. 229-248, on Debin Ma's site (https://www.econhistdbm.com/uploads/8/1/9/7/81977286/reh_japanese_wages_bassino_and_ma.pdf)

What it is: A study by two economic historians of the day wages of unskilled laborers in Kyoto from 1741 and in Tokyo to 1913, taken from the Mitsui house's account books, set against consumption baskets they reconstructed for Japan and priced in grams of silver, so that the wages can be compared with Europe's.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the source of the Kyoto day wage of the mid-1700s and of the year's basket of goods priced against it. Its baskets are the authors' reconstruction, built to supply set amounts of calories and protein from consumption data of the early 1700s, the late 1800s and the 1920s, not a budget anyone recorded; only the rice and lamp-oil prices of 1745-1754 were recorded in Kyoto, and the rest are derived from later price ratios or stand-ins; and its 250 working days a year is a convention of the comparison it follows.

Used for: the Kyoto laborer's year of goods and its prices, and the Kyoto and Edo day wage (Money and prices)
