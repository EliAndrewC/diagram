shomonya-saiko-10-2-salt

『諸問屋再興調』 (Records of the restoration of the wholesale guilds), 24 volumes, 十之二 (volume 10, part 2), a manuscript copy in the National Diet Library (in Japanese; https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/2548694/1/38)

What it is: The papers gathered for the restoration of Edo's wholesale guilds; this part holds the salt trade's, among them a copy of the answer the brokers of salt shipped from the west gave to an official inquiry in the eighth month of 1791, on the price at which they were then selling Ako salt.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the brokers' own statement, from the city, of a price in gold by the bale. It is the price at which the brokers sold on to their buyers, not a household's price, at a season when few ships were in. The bale is entered as holding 3 to 5 sho, but a slip pasted beside the entry (as NDL's OCR reads it) says it held about 3 to 2 sho in spring and winter and from 3 to 2 sho down to 3 to in summer and autumn, so a price by the sho taken from it carries that spread. It survives as a later copy, in a cursive hand of which only the price and bale lines are read here.

Used for: what Ako salt cost at the Edo brokers in 1791 (Money and prices)
