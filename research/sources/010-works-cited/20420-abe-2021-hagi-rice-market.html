Abe Shinya, "19 seiki no chiho beishijo ni okeru torihiki seido no doko: Hagi-han no okitte to sobajo o jirei to shite" ("Trading institutions in a regional rice market of the 19th century: the rice bills and rice exchange of the Hagi domain", title translated), Shakai keizai shigaku 87-1 (May 2021), pp. 25 ff. (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sehs/87/1/87_25/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A peer-reviewed article in the journal of the Socio-Economic History Society on how the Hagi (Choshu) domain paid its retainers' stipend rice in rice bills and how those bills were traded at the castle town's rice exchange in the 19th century. Its introduction sums up earlier estimates of how much of a domain's tax rice went to Osaka and how much was spent elsewhere.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a specialist's study from domain records, and its introduction gives the shares of a domain's tax rice shipped to Osaka and paid out as stipends, the very split a castle town's granary sat in. Its limits: the Osaka share (30 to 45 percent of each domain's tax revenue, Genroku to Bunsei eras) is an estimate it cites from Miyamoto (1988), and its own note 1 says Miyamoto measured Osaka shipments against each domain's assessed yield (kokudaka) and did not give them exactly as a share of tax revenue. The stipend share (32 percent, the largest item in Hagi's budget of 1754, when rice shipped to Osaka was 14 percent plus 8 percent) is one domain in one year, cited from Ogawa (1996), and is a share of a budget, not of tax rice measured the way the Osaka figure is. The same introduction gives Hagi's stipends as about 55 percent of the rice from the lord's own lands in 1821 and 59 percent in 1840 (from Tanaka 2013a), and about a third of its tax rice going to Osaka in the 1840s, so the split moved within this one domain; other domains differed again.

Used for: how much of a domain's tax rice was paid out as stipends and how much was shipped to Osaka to be sold (Domain capitals)
