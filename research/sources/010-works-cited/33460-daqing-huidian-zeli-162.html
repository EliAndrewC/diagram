欽定大清會典則例 (Precedents of the Collected Statutes of the Great Qing, title translated), volume 162, the Imperial Household Department, the Office of Ceremonial (内務府掌儀司), part 2, the Siku Quanshu edition (in Chinese; Wikisource transcription; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定大清㑹典則例_(四庫全書本)/卷162)

What it is: A volume of the Qing government's own compilation of the rulings behind its statutes, on the court's ceremonies and on the imperial orchards near Beijing and at Shengjing (Mukden) and Guangning in Manchuria, whose gardeners delivered fruit to be set against their tax.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its rulings of 1706 and 1729 give the silver value at which a gardener's pears, crabapples and peaches were credited against the tax he owed, values they take from an earlier rule (a ruling of 1683 had ordered the delivered fruit counted and its value fixed). These are valuations fixed for that credit, not prices anyone paid, and they are of the Manchurian and capital orchards alone.

Used for: the value set on fruit delivered in place of tax at the imperial orchards (Money and prices)
