Ai Lisi (艾栗斯), 《食物有故事：番茄的征服与妥协》 (Food has a story: the tomato's conquest and compromise), first printed in 《北京日报》 as 《番茄，从“毒果”到美食》 (print date not given), posted on The Paper's platform 11 May 2020 (in Chinese; https://m.thepaper.cn/baijiahao_7335825)

What it is: A newspaper feature on the tomato's history around the world, with a section on China and a closing note on the dish of tomato and egg.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the Qunfang pu at more length than the other pages read, but at second hand and naming no edition, and its transcription of the parallel Guizhou gazetteer passage is visibly corrupt (火伞赦卯, 第条似篙), so the wording should not be taken from it. That the tomato stayed ornamental until the end of the nineteenth century is the author's statement, not something the passage shows. On the Chinese dish of tomato and egg it says only that by the 1930s the dish was already widespread and that who first made it is unknown, with no evidence given; and its own account of the plant sits uneasily with that date, having tomatoes grown only around four big cities in the late Qing and early Republic, spreading by degrees after that, and a countrywide vegetable only after 1949. Its stories of Europe and America are told as anecdote.
