en.wikipedia, Roving bridge (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roving_bridge)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the roving bridge, the bridge that carries a canal towpath from one bank to the other, with the note that bridges were also needed at junctions and wherever a side arm interrupted the towpath.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests that a working towpath is carried over the channels that interrupt it, which is how our towpath is drawn where a drain meets the river. Its limits are large: it speaks of canal junctions and side arms rather than field drains meeting a river, and its bridges are those of the English canal age (1804-1854), several of them cast iron from an ironworks, crossed by a towing horse rather than by human trackers.

Used for: a towpath bridged where a side channel interrupts it
