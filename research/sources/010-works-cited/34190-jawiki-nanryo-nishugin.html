jawiki-nanryo-nishugin

"南鐐二朱銀" ("Nanryo nishugin"), Japanese Wikipedia (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/南鐐二朱銀)

What it is: An encyclopedia article on the nanryo two-shu piece, a silver coin counted in gold units (eight to the ryo), issued from 1772, with its weight, an assay by the modern Japan Mint (cited to Koga, 1930) and the shogunate's own justification of its value.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives how much pure silver a ryo of these coins held against a ryo of chogin, and reports, without quoting it, the order of 1773 that justified the coin by reckoning a ryo at 24 monme of pure silver. That was the shogunate's defense against the money-changers, not a market rate: by the article's account the changers counted a ryo at 60 monme of current silver (27.6 monme pure), demanded a 25 percent premium to exchange the coin, and silver had risen to 50 monme a ryo by 1786. Its reckonings in monme of pure silver are the article's, cited to Mikami's 1996 and Hisamitsu's 1976 histories of Japanese money, which were not read, and they count the coin at its prescribed fine silver, not its assay.

Used for: the silver in the nanryo and the order of 1773 (Money and prices)
