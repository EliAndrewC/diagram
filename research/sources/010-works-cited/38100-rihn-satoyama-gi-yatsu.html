Satoyama Green Infrastructure Study Group (里山グリーンインフラ勉強会), 自然とかかわり豊かに暮らす【谷津編】: 北総地域における里山グリーンインフラの手引き ("Living richly with nature, the yatsu volume: a guide to satoyama green infrastructure in the Hokuso region", title translated), 2nd edition, March 2022, issued by a project of the Research Institute for Humanity and Nature and published on its site (in Japanese; https://www.chikyu.ac.jp/publicity/publications/others/img/yatsu.pdf)

What it is: A booklet on the small valleys (yatsu) cut into the terraces of northern Chiba: where their water comes from, how their floors were farmed and later abandoned, and how their wetlands might be used again.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where in such a valley the terrace's groundwater comes out - at the edge of the valley floor, where its slope meets it - which is the one wet side the record finds. It describes the small valleys cut into the terraces of one region of Japan, northern Chiba, in the present day. Their springs are fed by rain soaking into the flat terrace above, so it attests a wet edge only where a valley's slope, with upland behind it, meets the valley floor. It says nothing of the sides of an alluvial fan or of a mountain valley. It gives this as a general explanation with a diagram, not a survey of springs, and what it says of past farming reaches back only to the 1950s.

Used for: springs where a valley's slope meets its floor (Water)
