chiu-2015-yuehu

Chiu Chung-lin (邱仲麟), 〈明清的樂戶：基於一種特殊官方體制的考察〉 ("The musician households of the Ming and Qing: a study of a peculiar official institution", title translated), 《明代研究》 24 (June 2015), pp. 105-208 (https://ming.cnhis.ncnu.edu.tw/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/ming24_04Chiu_full.pdf)

What it is: A journal article on the yuehu, the hereditary musician households of Ming and Qing China, a debased status registered apart from commoners: their duties to court, princes and local offices, their numbers, where they lived, and their emancipation in the 1720s. It works from Ming and Qing gazetteers and collected writings, quoted at length.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the households' lanes against the walls of named cities - inside at Zhending and Boye, outside at Ganzhou, Puzhou (in the suburb outside the east gate) and Canton - which is the Chinese evidence on which side of a wall a debased group's quarter stood. Not every such household lived in a quarter: at Hangzhou a few dozen households on the capitals' music registers, having fled there, lived among ordinary residents without the right to, and in Shanxi many lived in villages. Neither case says whether Hangzhou or any other town had a quarter of its own. Its households were musicians and entertainers, not handlers of hides or the dead; that Zhending's "debased households" were musician households, and that the Canton women a Portuguese priest saw were theirs, are the author's inferences; and it gives each siting in a sentence, with no plan and no reason for the side chosen.

Used for: the quarters of China's hereditary debased households inside and outside city walls
