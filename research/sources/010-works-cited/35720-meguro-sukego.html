meguro-sukego

Meguro City, 「歴史を訪ねて 街道筋の助郷制度」 ("Visiting history: the sukego system along the highways", title translated), reworked from the ward's monthly 月刊めぐろ of 1979-1985 (in Japanese; https://www.city.meguro.tokyo.jp/shougaigakushuu/bunkasports/rekishibunkazai/sukego.html)

What it is: A Tokyo ward's local-history page on the villages drafted to supply men and horses to the Shinagawa post station on the Tokaido, from 1694 to the end of the system in 1872, and the burden it laid on them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that the shogunate's official travel came mostly in the busy farming season, and that the drafted men's travel time was unpaid, and gives a count of men a day for three villages in the Kyoho era; it speaks of the drafted villagers, not the station's own porters, and gives no count of anyone's working days. Its season is one clause, naming no month and not saying whether planting or harvest; and its count is from Shinagawa, the first stage out of Edo on the busiest highway, whose villages also served the shogun's hawking grounds, so it runs higher than a lesser station's would, and its "over 100 a day" is the three villages together, without saying whether every day or at the peak.

Used for: the seasons of a post station's drafted porters (Money and prices)
