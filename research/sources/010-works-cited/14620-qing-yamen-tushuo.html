Lin Qian 林乾, 《清代衙门图说》 (Zhonghua Book Company, 2006), sample chapter on Douban Books: 《清代衙门图说》试读：“居中而治”的衙署 ("Illustrated Qing Yamen, sample chapter: the yamen that governs from the middle", title translated) (https://book.douban.com/reading/10187786/)

What it is: A free sample chapter, posted on the Douban book site, of Lin Qian's illustrated general-audience history of Qing government offices, 《清代衙门图说》 (Zhonghua Book Company, Beijing, 2006): the Qing rule for what a yamen holds, its axial order, its place in the city, where its jail stood, and the Ming rule of 1393, which the Qing kept, on the size and colors of officials' houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the Qing regulation on yamen - the halls, the gates, the residence, the clerks' rooms, and that a senior official has the full set and a junior one a reduced set - which is the tier rule a governor's compound follows. It quotes the regulation without naming where it is recorded, so the rule is second-hand. Its worked layouts are county offices: Youxian from the Tongzhi-era county gazetteer, and Mayang from the recollection of Chen Tianxi, once a private secretary in a county yamen, as told in an interview published long after the dynasty fell (Zhang Weiren, in Shihuo yuekan) - a memory of the late Qing, not a record of the time. A governor's compound follows either only by carrying the tier rule upward. It gives the yamen's place at the city's middle as the usual rule and a fengshui ideal, with one county town as its example, not as a count of cities; the same chapter says many county offices were run-down or worked out of rented houses. Its explanation of the jail's direction by divination is the book's own gloss and is not used here. The Ming rule of 1393 is the book's paraphrase, and the book names no record for it. The rule covers officials of the sixth to ninth ranks and governs the houses they built, a ceiling on what they were allowed rather than a description of what was built; that the Qing kept it, and that it made county yamen gates black, are the book's own statements, unsourced.

Used for: the program and order of a Qing yamen, its place at the city's middle, and the jail outside the ceremonial gate (The government quarter); the Ming rule of 1393, kept by the Qing, giving lesser officials a one-bay gate; the great hall on the gate's axis (Estates and other compounds)
