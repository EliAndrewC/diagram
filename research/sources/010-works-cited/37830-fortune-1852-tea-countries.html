fortune-1852-tea-countries

Robert Fortune, A Journey to the Tea Countries of China; Including Sung-lo and the Bohea Hills (London: John Murray, 1852), archive.org copy (https://archive.org/download/ajourneytoteaco00fortgoog/ajourneytoteaco00fortgoog_djvu.txt; the page read for the table of distances, https://archive.org/download/ajourneytoteaco00fortgoog/page/n295_w1400.jpg)

What it is: A plant collector's account of his journeys of 1848 to 1851 into the green-tea country of Anhui and the black-tea hills of Wuyi in Fujian, with a chapter reckoning what the farmer sold black tea for, what it cost to carry to Shanghai, and what it sold for there in 1846 to 1849.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one account found that sets a farmer's selling price for tea beside a port price of the same years, with every charge between them. Fortune traveled the road and vouches for its distances and days, but says he cannot speak for the costs with the same confidence: he had them with the help of a Shanghai merchant, the route is one he supposes the tea to take, the farm price has no year, and the fine-tea price is hearsay he thinks too high. Shanghai's prices are those paid by English exporters with the export duty in them, in years when much of the trade was barter, so the hill dealer probably got less than they show. It is the treaty-port export trade of the 1840s, not tea sold to a Chinese city.

Used for: Wuyi black tea's farm price, its carriage and charges to Shanghai and its price there, 1846 to 1849 (Money and prices)
