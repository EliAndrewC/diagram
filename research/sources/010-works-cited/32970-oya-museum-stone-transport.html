大谷資料館 (Oya History Museum, Utsunomiya), 「大谷石搬出、輸送の移り変わり」 ("How Oya stone was carried out and transported", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.oya909.co.jp/contents/%E5%A4%A7%E8%B0%B7%E7%9F%B3%E6%90%AC%E5%87%BA%E3%80%81%E8%BC%B8%E9%80%81%E3%81%AE%E7%A7%BB%E3%82%8A%E5%A4%89%E3%82%8F%E3%82%8A/)

What it is: The quarry museum's page on how Oya stone, a soft volcanic tuff of Shimotsuke, was carried out of the quarries and to market, from hand quarrying (until mechanization) and the pack horses that carried it from before the Edo period into Meiji, to horse carts, hand-pushed rail cars (1897-1952) and trucks.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the size of a pack-horse load of cut stone: one 駄, two blocks of 5 sun by 1 shaku by 3 shaku, which the page says was the same load as two bales of rice. It gives a size, not a weight. It also gives the weight one quarryman carried: a block of as much as 150 kg, on a back-frame, carried up out of the pit by the quarry's specialist carriers in the hand-quarrying era. The page does not date that era, and it lasted until quarrying was mechanized, long after Meiji. That figure is a short lift out of the pit, not a load carried along a road. The page does not say what size of block weighed 150 kg; it is a larger block than the horse's, two of which made one load. Its account of the times before Meiji names no record; Oya stone is light for a stone, so a load of a denser stone would be smaller; and it gives no price for carriage.

Used for: how much cut stone a horse or a man carried (Money and prices)
