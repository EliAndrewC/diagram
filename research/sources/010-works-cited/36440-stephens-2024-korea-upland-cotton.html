Holly Stephens, "Cotton, capital, and colonialism in southern Korea, 1910-1945: Semi-governmental organizations in the construction of imperial agriculture", Modern Asian Studies 58, no. 3 (May 2024): 717-738 (https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/45EB595BD89540934AD849E2DA467945/core-reader)

What it is: A historian's open-access article on the colonial campaigns to grow American upland cotton in Korea.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the shares of upland cotton in the Korean crop, the total area under cotton, and India's undated share, over half, of Japan's raw cotton imports, with no tonnage. Its figures start with colonial rule in 1910 and come from the colonial government's yearbooks and a cotton association's statistics. Upland cotton was a modern introduction, and its shares record a state campaign: associations issued seed and arranged credit and fertilizer, and the article reports beatings and destroyed crops where peasants refused. They do not show how farmers took up a crop unforced, and the article does not discuss Korean cotton farming in the Joseon period. It mentions the investigations of the consul at Mokpo before annexation and gives no date or place for upland cotton's first planting.

Used for: American cotton in Korea, and Japan's imports of raw cotton (Money and prices)
