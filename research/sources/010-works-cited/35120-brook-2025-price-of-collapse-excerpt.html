Timothy Brook 卜正民, "Hewei yi fen, yi qian, yi liang" 何为1分、1钱、1两 (What were 1 fen, 1 qian, 1 liang), an excerpt from Bengpan: xiao bingqi yu Da Ming wangchao de shuailuo 《崩盘：小冰期与大明王朝的衰落》 (Jiuzhou chubanshe, 2025), published by Yicai 第一财经, 16 December 2025 (https://www.yicai.com/news/102960940.html)

What it is: A newspaper's excerpt, published with the publisher's permission, from the Chinese translation of a historian's book on prices and climate under the Ming, summing up what one fen, one qian and one tael of silver bought in the Wanli reign (1573-1620) from the price records of Hai Rui, Shen Bang and others.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable report found of a fish price in Beijing in the Wanli reign, a Jesuit's letter of 1602 as the historian gives it. It is a translation of the book, not its English text, which was not read, and an excerpt that drops the book's tables and notes; it blends prices from Hai Rui's Zhejiang county of about 1560, before the Wanli reign began, with Shen Bang's Beijing of the 1590s, without always saying which a figure is, so its fish against meat need not be Beijing's or Wanli's, and the Jesuit's letter itself was not read; the excerpt gives that letter's trout at 1 fen a jin as an exception, half its usual price for fish, which it puts at about meat's 2 fen a jin. For Ming book prices it is the historian's own summary of 78 prices he collected, but it gives no date, place or source for any of them, setting them among Wanli's prices without saying they are all Wanli's; its 6 fen is for one fascicle of a set, not a whole work; its figure of about 2 qian a work for Qi Chenghan's library is the collector's own valuation for his heirs, not a sale price, and a tenth of the 78 prices' mean of 2 taels, so its book prices vary tenfold with whose books are counted; and it gives Hu Yinglin's ranking of Fujian, Zhejiang, Suzhou and Beijing books as his summary of book prices, though the ranking it quotes names no price.

Used for: a fish price in Beijing in 1602, fish against meat under Wanli, and Ming book prices (Money and prices)
