Timothy Brook 卜正民, "Hewei yi fen, yi qian, yi liang" 何为1分、1钱、1两 (What were 1 fen, 1 qian, 1 liang), an excerpt from Bengpan: xiao bingqi yu Da Ming wangchao de shuailuo 《崩盘：小冰期与大明王朝的衰落》 (Jiuzhou chubanshe, 2025), published by Yicai 第一财经, 16 December 2025 (https://www.yicai.com/news/102960940.html)

What it is: A newspaper's excerpt, published with the publisher's permission, from the Chinese translation of a historian's book on prices and climate under the Ming, summing up what one fen, one qian and one tael of silver bought in the Wanli reign (1573-1620) from the price records of Hai Rui, Shen Bang and others.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one readable report found of a fish price in Beijing in the Wanli reign, a Jesuit's letter of 1602 as the historian gives it. It is a translation of the book, not its English text, which was not read, and an excerpt that drops the book's tables and notes; it blends prices from Hai Rui's Zhejiang county of about 1560, before the Wanli reign began, with Shen Bang's Beijing of the 1590s, without always saying which a figure is, so its fish against meat need not be Beijing's or Wanli's, and the Jesuit's letter itself was not read.

Used for: a fish price in Beijing in 1602, and fish against meat under Wanli (Money and prices)
