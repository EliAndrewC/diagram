Wikipedia, "Groundling" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundling)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the groundlings, the poorest playgoers of London's theaters, who stood in the yard before the stage; it quotes, in English, Thomas Platter's account of 1599 of what London's theaters charged at each door.

Why it applies, and its limits: The record takes from it only its quotation of Platter, which the article neither cites nor credits to a translator, so the English is an uncredited translation (most likely Clare Williams's of 1937, unconfirmed) and cannot be checked against a named edition; and Platter describes London's standing theaters, not a touring company.

Used for: Platter's penny at each door of a London theater, and the food and drink sold in the pauses (Comparative money: other societies)
