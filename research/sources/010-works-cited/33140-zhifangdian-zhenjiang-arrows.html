zhifangdian-zhenjiang-arrows

Gujin Tushu Jicheng (欽定古今圖書集成), 方輿彙編, 職方典, volume 731, "Zhenjiang prefecture: land tax, part 3" (in Chinese; Wikisource transcription; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/方輿彙編/職方典/第0731卷)

What it is: A volume of the imperial encyclopedia compiled in the early eighteenth century, copying from the gazetteers of Zhenjiang prefecture, on the lower Yangzi, the land-tax accounts of Jintan county: the land on the registers and the goods and silver the county owed the throne, as the old Ming fiscal book set them and as the Qing commuted them into silver from 1646.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the price per arrow at which Jintan county's levy of arrows was booked in the Ming fiscal book, a primary figure for what an ordinary war arrow was reckoned to cost. The book's word for them is 攢箭 and it never calls them war arrows; that reading rests on the levy's going to the Board of Works' arms office beside helmets, armor and sabres. The price is the arrow's alone: the book charges a further 69.9 taels for packing and delivery on the 9,000 arrows' 122 taels, more than half as much again. It is an accounting price, set by officials to value a levy, not a price paid in a shop; the fiscal book is dated only as older than items added at the end of the Wanli reign (1573-1620); and the silver rate per arrow at which the Qing commuted the levy in 1646 is a tax rate, not a cost. The Wikisource punctuation was added by a machine.
