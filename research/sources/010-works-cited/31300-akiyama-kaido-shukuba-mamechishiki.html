akiyama-kaido-shukuba-mamechishiki

Akiyama Yutaka (秋山 豊), "改訂 街道と宿場の豆知識" ("Revised: Small facts about the highways and the post towns", title translated), a ten-page handout, on the author's personal site (in Japanese; https://akiyama.my.coocan.jp/kaiteikaidoumame.pdf)

What it is: A signed ten-page handout surveying Japan's highways and their post towns from antiquity to the Edo period, centered on the Tokaido: the facilities of the road (barriers, mile mounds, roadside trees, signposts) and of the post town (the headquarters, the honjin and waki-honjin inns, inns, teahouses, gates and the bent entrance, the notice board), with sizes for several of them. It also covers the relay of horses and porters with their loads, the river crossings and bridges, and the travelers who used the road (among them the shogun's tea-jar procession), and ends with roadside stone monuments, a short reading list and an appendix of drawings of townscape features.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives drawn-from-the-page dimensions for road furniture and post-town buildings: a mile mound of five ken (about 9 m) square and one jo (about 3 m) high, road classes from 6 ken down to 3 shaku wide, honjin floors mostly 220-300 tsubo (Narumi 600, Kumagaya 700) and inns of 10-50 tsubo. It states the road widths as set out in the "Ieyasu one hundred articles", a text it calls only "said to be" Ieyasu's testament, and gives no source at the point for the other figures beyond a closing reading list, and it describes the highways under the Tokugawa shogunate, so a local lord's road or a village lane is outside it. For loads it gives the shogunate's rules for the post stations' relay horses and porters: a pack horse (honma) one load of 30 kan, later 40 kan, with no date given for the change; a riding horse with baggage (norikake) a rider and 20 kan; a light horse (karajiri) a rider and 5 kan of hand baggage; and a porter 5 kan, with a surcharge above that. These are the regulated loads of official relay on the five highways, the loads fares were charged on, not loads weighed on the road. The porter's 5 kan is his base load, not his limit, and a private pack horse or a village's horse off the highway was not bound by them. The handout names no source for these figures beyond its closing reading list. For the shogun's tea-jar procession (ochatsubo dochu) it says the jars went out to Uji each year by the Tokaido and came back by the Nakasendo until 1737, while they were stored at Katsuyama castle in Yamura, Kai, and by the Tokaido from 1738. It says that because the jars disliked damp the procession sometimes avoided the Shichiri and Imagiri ferries by going round by the Minoji and the Honzaka pass, and that the jars lodged at honjin. That is an occasional detour, not a fixed route, and the handout gives the reason without a source.

Used for: the loads of a pack horse, a light horse and a porter, and the ferries the shogun's tea jars avoided (Money and prices)
