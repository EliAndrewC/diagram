jawiki-kaomise

Japanese Wikipedia, 顔見世 ("Kaomise", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/顔見世)

What it is: The encyclopedia's article on kaomise, the first program of the kabuki year, given in the 11th month by the newly contracted company: its origins, which it calls unclear, with the custom apparently nearly settled by the Manji and Kanbun eras (1658-1673), the ceremonies leading to its opening day, and the form of its plays.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the opening day's drum at four in the morning and the kaomise's usual length of about ten days. Of its two footnotes, one, a dictionary of yearly observances of 1958, is on the sentence about the actors' one-year contracts, and the other is a news story on the ink of today's Minamiza signboards. Its account of the ceremonies, the opening drum and the program's length cites nothing, and it is given for Edo's three theatres; the Kamigata calendar is not described. The figures are for the form as it was settled by the 1660s and kept for about two centuries, not for any one year: one season in Edo, in 1849, moved its kaomise to the spring, and Osaka had lost the form before the 1850s. Its "four in the morning" is its own rendering of the seventh hour, a seasonal hour that fell near 4 a.m. in the eleventh month.

Used for: the kaomise program's length and the opening day's first drum (Money and prices)
