"欽定古今圖書集成/方輿彙編/職方典/第0451卷" ("Imperially Authorized Complete Collection of Illustrations and Writings, Past and Present: Geography Section, Gazetteer Statutes, volume 451", title translated), on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定古今圖書集成/方輿彙編/職方典/第0451卷)

What it is: The transcribed text of one volume of the Qing imperial encyclopedia's gazetteer section (the Gujin tushu jicheng, compiled under Chen Menglei and then Jiang Tingxi, printed in 1726), on Chinese Wikisource, covering Nanyang Prefecture (Henan) as its prefectural gazetteer (府志) and county gazetteers described it: its list of enfeoffed lords from the earliest dynasties to the Ming, and its register of government offices, with each county and department (zhou) seat's halls, gates, granaries, shrines and other buildings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It lists, county by county, what an official seat of the Qing prefecture held and in what order the gates and halls stood (great hall, front gate, jail, granaries, pavilions), with dates of founding and repair from the Jin and Yuan to Kangxi 33 (1694), so it carries a layout of a Chinese county or prefecture office compound. It describes one prefecture in Henan, describes offices of mixed date - some entries give the Ming compound as it stood before the rebels burned it in the 1630s and 1640s, others the Qing rebuilding, sometimes partial or in houses taken over - so each plan must be dated from its own entry; gives no measurements, only the bay count of some halls and rooms (a great hall of five bays); and the page's punctuation is a later machine-and-volunteer addition to an unpunctuated original, and it sometimes breaks a sentence where a building's position depends on it (Wuyang's 房後為獄儀，門前為譙樓, which should read 房後為獄。儀門前為譙樓).

Used for: where four of Nanyang's county and department offices kept their arms, money and regalia stores, on either side of the hall or the ceremonial gate (Towns)
