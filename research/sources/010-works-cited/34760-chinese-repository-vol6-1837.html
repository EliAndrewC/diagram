A letter dated "Canton 1st May, 1837", with its postscript "Weights and Prices", The Chinese Repository, vol. 6, no. 1 (Canton, May 1837), p. 44, archive.org copy (https://archive.org/details/chineserepositor6118unse)

What it is: An issue of the missionaries' Canton monthly; the passage used is a letter-writer's reckoning of what an opium smoker's daily dose cost, in dollars and in cash.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only figure read that sets cash beside silver at Canton itself in the 1830s. It is a conversion made in passing by an unnamed foreign writer, a round figure per dollar, not a quoted market rate, and not per tael.

Used for: cash to the dollar at Canton, 1837 (Money and prices)
