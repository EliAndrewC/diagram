Niiza City, 野火止用水・平林寺の文化的景観 (the cultural landscape of the Nobidome canal and Heirinji), chapter 2, sections 2-3, c. 2012 (in Japanese; https://www.city.niiza.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/7104.pdf)

What it is: The chapters of the same city report on the Nobidome plateau's ground and its settlements: how each village of 1661 lay to the canal and to its road, how the households drew water from the canal, and the house lots and strips of the Shinbori settlement.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says how each village lay against the canal and its road - Nobidome village strung along the Kawagoe highway, which crossed the canal at right angles, so that strips squared to the highway ran along the canal (the report gives the settlement's direction there, not the strips'); at Shinbori, strips squared to a road running beside the canal, with fields between the road and the house lots - which is the nearest case the record holds of strips beside a canal. It speaks of one canal and its four villages, new-field settlements of dry fields on the Musashino upland, where the canal was dug for drinking and household water, not to water the strips; it reads their layout from a picture map of 1871 and from the strips that survive today; its strips are whole household holdings, and much of what it says of water use comes from twentieth-century recollection.

Used for: strips squared to a road beside the canal at Shinbori, and running along the canal at Nobidome village (Fields)
