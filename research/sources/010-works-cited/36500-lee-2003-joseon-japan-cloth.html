Ja-Yeon Lee (Lee Ja-yeon), "Cloths Culture Study Based on the Trade Goods between Chosun Envoy and a Japanese Envoy in the Early Chosun Dynasty", The Research Journal of the Costume Culture 11, no. 6 (December 2003): 826-834 (https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO200311922064774.pdf)

What it is: A journal article on the cloth Joseon Korea gave Japanese envoys in the 15th and 16th centuries, working from the Korean annals.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated volumes of cotton cloth and a date for cotton as the general dress of Korea. Its volumes are what the court gave Japanese envoys or held in store, not what Korea grew or wove. It places cotton as the dress of all ranks in the later 15th century, but the entry it cites is Yang Seong-ji's memorial of 1456, which says that high and low, men and women, all wear cotton; that is a courtier's assertion in a proposal to honor Mun Ik-jeom, not a count, and it dates the claim to 1456. Its figures of 1485-86 (724,500 bolts of cotton in the Saseomsi's store, over 500,000 a year to the Japanese) cite an annals entry of 1488 that, as read here, states neither: that entry gives over 100,000 bolts of cloth returned to the Japanese in three summer months and over 800,000 left in the Saseomsi's store, so the two earlier figures rest on this article alone.

Used for: Cotton in India and Korea (Money and prices)
