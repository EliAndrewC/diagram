forestry-jp-tarama-hogo

The Japanese Forest Society, Forestry Heritage No. 34, 「The hogo of Tarama island in the Ryukyu kingdom period and the Rinsei hassho」 (translated; original: 「琉球王朝時代の多良間島の「抱護」と『林政八書』」), selected 2018 (in Japanese; https://www.forestry.jp/forestryheritage/2018-3231/)

What it is: The Japanese Forest Society's page for one of the sites it lists as forestry heritage: the hogo tree belt of Tarama island in the Ryukyus, which it dates to about 1742, and the Meiji-era compilation of the Ryukyu kingdom's forestry orders.

Why it applies, and its limits: A learned society's dating of a planted belt that shelters a whole settlement and its farmland, to the eighteenth-century Ryukyu kingdom under the chief minister Sai On, so it places a communal windbreak before the modern period. It is a short heritage citation, not a study: it names the belt's functions for Sai On's coastal and inland belts together, gives the belt's length as it stands today rather than as planted, and describes one small, flat, typhoon-swept island of the Ryukyus, a kingdom with its own forestry policy, rather than the Japanese main islands or China.

Used for: that the Ryukyu kingdom encouraged village tree belts called hogo in the eighteenth century, and that Tarama's, made in 1742, still shelters its settlement and farmland (Towns)
