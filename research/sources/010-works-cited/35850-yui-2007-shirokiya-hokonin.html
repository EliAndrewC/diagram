Aburai Hiroko, 『江戸奉公人の心得帖―呉服商白木屋の日常―』 ("Rules for Edo's shop servants: daily life at the Shirokiya dry-goods house", title translated), Shinchosha, 2007, its page at the publisher (in Japanese; https://www.shinchosha.co.jp/book/610242/)

What it is: A book for general readers, by a history graduate who lectures on reading old documents (NHK Gakuen), on the shop servants of Shirokiya's Nihonbashi store, one of Edo's five largest dry-goods houses, drawn from about four hundred old documents; read here only in the publisher's description and the book's chapter titles.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its chapter titles state the house rules on when a servant first went home, when he could marry, private errands, visits to kin and pastimes. They are the rules of a great house's live-in shop staff - all men, taken in at eleven or twelve, unpaid until coming of age, unmarried until they left, first sent home in their ninth year, a journey up to the house's Kyoto head store where they had been hired - not of a servant in an ordinary city household or of a maid, so the nine years measure the distance to a Kamigata home as much as the leave a servant was given. A chapter title is a summary of one house's rules, so it gives no count of days off, no date for the rules, and does not say how far they were kept.

Used for: a great Edo shop's rules on its servants' home leave, marriage and errands (Money and prices)
