bunka-mikazuki-omotemon

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Online, 三日月藩乃井野陣屋表門 ("The front gate of the Mikazuki domain's jin'ya at Noino", title translated) (in Japanese; https://online.bunka.go.jp/heritages/detail/434033)

What it is: The national database entry for the front gate of the jin'ya of the Mikazuki domain (15,000 koku) at Noino in Sayo, Hyogo prefecture, a town-designated cultural property: a timber yakuimon thought, from the inscription on its end tile (onigawara) and the domain's diary, to be of 1774, with its roof, side walls and dimensions, and a note that it served as the domain's main gate.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a surviving main gate of a small domain's jin'ya, the kind of seat our magistracies are drawn from, described with its form and size. Its limits: it gives the gate's width and depth as plain figures without saying whether the width takes in the side walls, and it does not count the gate's bays; it is one gate, which the entry, comparing two old plans, thinks was probably rebuilt and moved in 1774, so it shows what one domain built late in the period rather than an original layout. And it is not the gate as it stood: sold off with the jin'ya in Meiji 9 (1876; the entry prints 1875), it is thought to have served as the main gate of a temple, Saihōji, until the town, given it in 2016, moved it back near its first site and restored it in 2017, so its side walls and its figures describe that restoration, and the entry does not say how much of them is original.

Used for: a jin'ya's main gate built as a yakuimon with a short wall each side (Estates and other compounds)
