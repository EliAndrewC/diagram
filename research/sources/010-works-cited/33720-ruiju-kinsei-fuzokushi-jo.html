喜田川季荘 (Kitagawa Morisada), 『類聚近世風俗志 : 原名守貞漫稿』 (Customs of recent times, by topic: the Morisada manko, title translated), 上 (volume 1), 國學院大學出版部, 1908, in the National Diet Library (in Japanese; https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/88; the polishers' entry at https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/94; rents at https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/57; soba shops at https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/74, https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/75 and https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/76; inns at https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/79 and https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/80; sake then and now at https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/137; standing shop signboards at https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991466/1/82)

What it is: The 1908 printed edition, arranged by topic, of the notebook an Osaka-born townsman kept on the customs of Edo, Kyoto and Osaka from 1837 to 1853, with additions until about 1867. This first volume holds his chapters on the trades and street sellers, with the day's hire of the carpenter and the building helper in Osaka and in Edo, the inns of the three cities and the highways, the soba shops' price boards, how Edo townsmen paid their rent, and what Edo's carved standing shop signboards cost.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one observer's account of the wages and trades he saw in the three cities, set down from 1837 and added to until about 1867 without saying which year a figure is of, so a wage may be from the 1860s, when prices and wages rose steeply; he lived in Osaka until 1840 and in Edo after, so his figures for the two cities are likely of different years; its wages are what he gives as usual, not a ledger's payments; and they are the wages, rents and prices of the realm's three largest cities, Edo then a city of about a million, which ran above a castle town's, a post town's or a village's. His signboard figure, 70 or 80 ryō at the least and 140 or 150 at the most, is the whole outlay: the board, its post, and the fees for the official permit and inspection that a board standing on the street required. So it is not a carver's price. It is also the display of Edo's great shops, most of all on Honchō 4-chōme; he says Kyoto and Osaka made no such boards because their streets were too narrow. A printed transcription of a manuscript, it may differ from his hand in places.

Used for: what a carpenter and a laborer were paid in Edo and Osaka, the street sharpener's trade, a night at an inn, a bowl of soba and a cup of sake, an Edo tenant's payments to the landlord, and a carved signboard's price (Money and prices)
