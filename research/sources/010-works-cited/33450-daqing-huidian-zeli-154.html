欽定大清會典則例 (Precedents of the Collected Statutes of the Great Qing, title translated), volume 154, the Court of Imperial Entertainments (光祿寺), part 1, the Siku Quanshu edition (in Chinese; Wikisource transcription; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定大清㑹典則例_(四庫全書本)/卷154)

What it is: A volume of the Qing government's own compilation of the rulings behind its statutes, on the court office that supplied the banquets and offerings of the capital: what each banquet used, the licensed suppliers it bought from, and the price it paid for each food, from pork and fowl to fresh and dried fruit.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, in silver, the price the court paid its fruit supplier for persimmons, apples, crabapples, pears and dried persimmons, with the pork, flour and other foods that the volume prices in lists of their own. These are the court's purchase prices in Beijing, paid from a tax on its orchard land, not prices in a market; the fruit list does not say whether its prices are by the piece, and it carries no year of its own: it comes just after a ruling of 1748, in a volume whose rulings run from the 1660s to 1754, and a ruling of 1729 had the fresh fruit bought at audited, reduced prices.

Used for: the Qing court's purchase prices for fresh and dried fruit in Beijing (Money and prices)
