muraoka-edo-nagaya-life

Muraoka Shoji (pen name), "江戸庶民の暮らし(1)" ("the lives of Edo commoners, part 1", title translated), in the Edo dining-culture section of the site "日本食文化の醤油を知る" ("learning about soy sauce in Japanese food culture", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-8.html)

What it is: A long personal-site article on how Edo's commoners lived in tenements: the front row of shops against the back-alley rows, the landlord's duties and income, the alley gates, the shared well and privy, rents and daily earnings, and the rental and secondhand-clothing trades. It ends with a list of the books and museum pages it draws on.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the layout of an Edo townsman block in concrete terms: a block of 60 ken (about 118 m) a side, 20-ken house lots, back tenements of 9 shaku by 2 ken reached by a lane of 3 to 6 shaku, gates shut at dusk, and rents of 300 to 500 mon a month in the Bunka-Bunsei years (1804-1830). It is about the capital's townsman districts only, mostly the late Edo period, and none of the figures the record takes from it is tied to a named source, only to a general reference list at the foot (it names a period work for only a few other figures, such as the Morisada mankō for its count of landlords). Its laborer's day rate of about 200 mon, and its 320 to 540 mon a day for carpenters, plasterers and navvies together, are given for no year, and its carpenter's wage, in silver, it converts to copper at the rate it gives for about 1800 (1 monme to 105 mon), applied unchanged to the Bunsei years and to 1855, and that rate does not agree with its own equivalence of 1 ryo to 63 monme and 6,400 mon (about 102 mon a monme). It also contradicts itself once on the standard tenement, giving 9 shaku by 2 ken as a 3.6 m frontage and 2.7 m depth where elsewhere the 9 shaku (about 2.7 m) is the frontage.

Used for: an Edo laborer's day rate of about 200 mon, undated, and a carpenter's wage in silver in the Bunsei era (Money and prices)
