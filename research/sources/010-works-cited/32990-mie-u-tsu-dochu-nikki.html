mie-u-tsu-dochu-nikki

Mie University Library, 「道中日記に見る津の記述」 ("Tsu as described in travel diaries", title translated), a one-table exhibit of its online gallery (in Japanese; https://www.lib.mie-u.ac.jp/r_and_d/gallery/gallery3/img/itiranhyou.pdf)

What it is: A table compiled by the university library of the passages that travel diaries, most of them of pilgrims to the Ise shrines, give about the castle town of Tsu from 1767 to 1880, each with its year and the published transcription or archive it was taken from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives prices travelers paid for a night at Tsu in their own words: 130 mon in 1790, a firewood fee of 50 mon in 1823, and 6 monme of silver (or 1.6, as the entry may be read) in 1867. Each is one night at one inn, quoted as an excerpt without the rest of the diary. The 1823 figure is a kisen (木銭), the firewood fee charged for a room and fuel where travelers cooked or bought their own rice, so it probably does not include meals and does not compare directly with the 130 mon of 1790. The 1790 figure is 130 mon apiece (宛), a price per person, paid at an inn the party was sent to because the one it wanted was very crowded, and the entry does not say what the 130 mon took in. The 1867 figure falls in the inflation of the shogunate's last years, when prices had risen several-fold over the 1820s, so it does not compare directly with the other two. The same diaries of 1790 and 1834 give the fee to see the hidden image kept at the Kannonji temple, which the 1790 diarist calls the original Buddha of the Ise Grand Shrine and the 1834 diarist names as the Amida of Kofu (国府), a draw for Ise pilgrims, each in a line of a traveler's notes, without saying whether the showing was a special one.

Used for: a night's lodging at Tsu in 1790 and 1823, and the fee to see the hidden image of Tsu's Kannonji in 1790 and 1834 (Money and prices)
