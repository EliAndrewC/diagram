daxue-yanyi-bu-33

Qiu Jun (丘濬), Daxue yanyi bu (大學衍義補, Supplement to the Extended Meaning of the Great Learning), chapter 33, "What befits grain transport, part 1" (漕輓之宜上), the Siku quanshu text on Chinese Wikisource (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大學衍義補_(四庫全書本)/卷033)

What it is: A chapter of the statecraft treatise a Ming minister presented to the throne in 1487, quoting the classics and the histories on how grain was carried, from the Tribute of Yu and the Spring and Autumn period through the Qin, Han, Sui and Tang to the Later Zhou of the 950s, with his own comment after most passages, some of them comparing the Ming canal transport of his own day.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gathers the old histories' figures for what carriage cost against the grain delivered: the Qin and Han armies' supply, and the Tang canal under Liu Yan in the 760s. The figures are the histories' as Qiu Jun quotes them, abridged, not read here in the histories themselves, and the Qin and Han ratios are hauls to the frontier (the Qin army's against the Xiongnu, the Han laborers' opening the southwest), not a capital's tax grain. They come from a memorial and a history arguing against those campaigns' cost, as Jia Yi's "tens of coins to deliver a coin of tax" is a memorial's, so they are round and may be overstated; Qiu Jun himself reads the Qin ratio as the whole road's cost, not the sea leg alone. The Tang costs are in cash per dou in 764, just after the An Lushan rebellion, when grain was dear, so they compare only with a grain price of those same years.

Used for: the Qin, Han and Tang costs of carrying grain, as Qiu Jun quotes them (Money and prices)
