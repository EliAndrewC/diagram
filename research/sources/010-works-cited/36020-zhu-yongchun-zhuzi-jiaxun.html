zhu-yongchun-zhuzi-jiaxun

Zhu Yongchun (born 1617), 『朱子家訓』 ("Master Zhu's family maxims", also called 『朱柏廬治家格言』, title translated), late Ming or early Qing, on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/朱子家訓)

What it is: A short set of household maxims by a scholar of Kunshan, in a Wikisource transcription in simplified characters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It opens with rising at first light and tells the household to live frugally and not to seek fine houses or good fields, so it shows early rising taught as a household virtue. The rising line itself promises no wealth, but a later line warns that a household content with sloth will not prosper (颓惰自甘，家道难成): the text ties diligence to the family's fortunes without naming early rising as a road to riches. It is a gentry family's rule, not a farmers' saying, and it is undated beyond its author's life.

Used for: early rising as a gentry household rule (Money and prices)
