Hong Kong Herbarium (AFCD), "The Fung Shui Story of Lai Chi Wo" (https://www.herbarium.gov.hk/en/special-topics/fung-shui-woods/the-fung-shui-story-of-lai-chi-wo/index.html)

What it is: A short page in the Hong Kong Herbarium's series on fung shui woods, telling how the village of Lai Chi Wo came to protect its wood: marked boundaries, a ban on destroying it, a fine and public denunciation for damage, and firewood gathering allowed on one or two days a year.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one account read of a southern Chinese village's own rules for its fengshui wood, from the region whose groves the record measures. It tells the village's story in a paragraph, dates the custom only to after a fengshui wall built "more than a hundred years ago" on an undated page, which may put it at or after 1898, when Lai Chi Wo, a Hakka village in Hong Kong's New Territories, came under British lease rather than Qing rule, tells the firewood days in the present tense, names no stele, covenant or document, and says neither what the fine was nor who levied it.

Used for: Lai Chi Wo's fine and public denunciation for harming its wood, and its firewood days (The countryside)
