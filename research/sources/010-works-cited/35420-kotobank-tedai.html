kotobank-tedai

"手代" (tedai, "clerk"), Kotobank, the entries of several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias, among them the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia's by Murakami Tadashi and Miyamoto Matao (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%89%8B%E4%BB%A3-100991)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering reference entries on the tedai, both the merchant house's clerk between apprentice and head clerk and the commoner clerk of a shogunal intendant's office, with how each was hired, promoted and paid.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its encyclopedia entries, signed by historians, give the intendant's clerk's pay from 1725 and describe the merchant clerk's career, what came in kind at promotion, and the Konoike money-changers' deferred bonus funds. They describe the usual practice in general terms, with figures for only those two cases; they add that an intendant's clerk's treatment varied from man to man, and that some merchant houses asked a new clerk for a guarantee bond.

Used for: the intendant's clerk's pay from 1725, and a merchant clerk's promotion, keep and deferred bonuses (Money and prices)
