kotobank-tedai

"手代" (tedai, "clerk"), Kotobank, the entries of several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias, among them the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia's by Murakami Tadashi and Miyamoto Matao (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%89%8B%E4%BB%A3-100991)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering reference entries on the tedai, both the merchant house's clerk between apprentice and head clerk and the commoner clerk of a shogunal intendant's office, with how each was hired, promoted and paid.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its encyclopedia entries, signed by historians, give the intendant's clerk's pay from 1725 and describe the merchant clerk's career, what came in kind at promotion, and the Konoike money-changers' deferred bonus funds. They describe the usual practice in general terms, with pay figures only for those two cases and a range of 20 to 30 bales for clerks of other shogunal offices. They give no amount for a merchant clerk's own wage, and the Konoike silver sums carry no year, though silver's value changed with the recoinages. Their account of the merchant clerk comes from the country's great houses (Mitsui, the Omi merchants' Tonomura, Osaka's Konoike), so it does not show a small town's shop or what such a shop paid; they add that an intendant's clerk's treatment varied from man to man, and that some merchant houses asked a new clerk for a guarantee bond.

Used for: the intendant's clerk's pay from 1725, and a merchant clerk's promotion, keep and deferred bonuses (Money and prices)
