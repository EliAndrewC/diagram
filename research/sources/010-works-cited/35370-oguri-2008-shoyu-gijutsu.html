小栗朋之 (Oguri Tomoyuki), "醤油製造技術の系統化調査" ("Development of Soy Sauce Manufacturing Technologies", its English title), 国立科学博物館 技術の系統化調査報告 (National Museum of Nature and Science, Survey Reports on the Systematization of Technologies) vol. 10, 2008 (in Japanese; https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/https://sts.kahaku.go.jp/diversity/document/system/pdf/040.pdf)

What it is: A history of soy sauce making in Japan from its Chinese roots to the present, written for the national science museum, with a table that sets the ingredients of the Edo-period recipes and of a present-day whole-soybean soy sauce side by side by volume, and an account of Noda's brewing about 1900 from a tax bureau survey.

Why it applies, and its limits: It puts the Edo recipes and a modern one on one footing, and gives the share of the fermenting mash pressed out as raw soy sauce at Noda about 1900. Its Edo proportions are taken from another historian's table, and it does not state how many liters of soy sauce a kilogram of soybeans made in any period.

Used for: the proportions of Edo and present-day soy sauce, and Noda's press yield about 1900 (Money and prices)
