kotobank-shukuson-taigaicho

コトバンク 宿村大概帳 ("general register of post-stations and villages", title translated) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%AE%BF%E6%9D%91%E5%A4%A7%E6%A6%82%E5%B8%B3-841874)

What it is: A page on kotobank, a Japanese reference site that reprints several dictionaries' entries for one word, here the Shukuson Taigaicho, the survey the shogunate's highway commissioners compiled late in the Edo period of every post-station on the five highways and some side roads, and of the villages along them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the survey recorded for each station - its households, its number of inns, its headquarters inn, its shrines and temples - which shows that inns with meals were counted and what else was. It is three dictionaries' short summaries of the survey (one of them cut short on this site), not the survey itself, so it gives no station's figures, and that it never mentions the cheap firewood-fee inns shows only that these summaries do not list them, not that the survey kept no count of them; its household figures date mostly from 1843, a few from the 1850s; and it covers post-stations on the shogunate's highways, not the provincial seat that is not a station.

Used for: that the survey recorded each station's households and number of hatago, and that its dictionary summaries name no count of the cheap firewood-fee inns (Urban fabric)
