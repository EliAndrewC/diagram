irri-drying-floor

IRRI Rice Knowledge Bank, sun drying and drying-floor area (https://www.knowledgebank.irri.org/grainQuality/module_4/popups/pu_drying.htm)

What it is: A page from the Rice Knowledge Bank of the International Rice Research Institute, an agricultural research organization, on drying parboiled paddy - rice soaked and steamed before milling - by machine and in the sun on large floors beside the mill: how thin the grain is spread and how much floor a given tonnage of it needs.

Why it applies, and its limits: It carries the only plain statement of area we could find on a public page - grain spread a few centimeters deep, five hundred square meters of drying floor handling six tons of paddy - a depth-to-area ratio from which the size of the drying yard in front of a house is derived. Its limits: this is modern advice for parboiling mills, where the grain comes to the floor far wetter than a farmer's (36% moisture) and a large crew turns it, and we flag it as such wherever it is used; and its ratio is for a mill's floor, while our households dry on racks first and spread in batches, so the yard on the map is a fraction of the ratio's answer, worked out in the record rather than taken from the page. Sun-drying grain on a floor is nonetheless an old technique whose arithmetic does not change with the century, so we take the ratio from it and nothing else.

Used for: the spread depth ("paddy should be spread about 2.5 cm thick over the floor") and the only direct area statement ("500 square meters of drying floor can handle 6 tons of paddy") - MODERN practice, for parboiled paddy at a rice mill, flagged as such, used only to convert a crop volume into a floor area
