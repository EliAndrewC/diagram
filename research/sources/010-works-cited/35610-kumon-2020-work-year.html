kumon-2020-work-year

Yuzuru Kumon, "The Labor Intensive Path: Wages, Incomes and the Work Year in Japan, 1610-1932", CIRJE discussion paper F-1154, University of Tokyo, August 2020 (https://www.cirje.e.u-tokyo.ac.jp/research/dp/2020/2020cf1154.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's series of Japanese male farm servants' yearly wages from servants' contracts, 1610-1932, with an estimate of the working year: from the contracts that state the days of rest each month, and by dividing the yearly wage by the day wage, for villages in Harima and Kai and for the Edo region, 1780-1840.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one count of a Japanese worker's days in a year read (the paper checks it against village surveys of the work year compiled by Abe, 1998, and Saito, 1998, which were not read), and it is a count only for farm servants hired by the year: 83 contracts carry the rest days, and the author cannot tell whether servants also had the New Year, Bon and seasonal holidays, which he leaves out (about 11 days, if they did). He finds eastern Japan's working year about 10 days shorter than western Japan's, so one figure for the whole country hides that spread, and he cannot see when the long year began: it is attested from about 1700, after the seventeenth century's growth in population, and may not hold for earlier Japan. It is the working-paper draft of an article published, revised, in the Journal of Economic History (vol. 82, no. 2, 2022, retitled to end at 1890); that version was not read.

Used for: farm servants' rest days and working year (Money and prices)
