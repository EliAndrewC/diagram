Jacqueline M. Newman, "America's Foods Came Early To China", Flavor and Fortune 19(1), Spring 2012, pp. 12 and 14 (https://www.flavorandfortune.com/ffdataaccess/article.php?ID=843)

What it is: A food magazine's summary of the historian Ho Ping-ti's article of 1955 on American food plants in China.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only page read that gives Ho's dates for the sweet potato, and it should be read as a summary: it misspells his name once, misnames his journal ("American Anthropology" for American Anthropologist) and does not quote him. Its route contradicts itself: the sweet potato comes "from the Philippines", then "from India or Manila, of that he is not sure", yet Ho is "certain" it came first into Yunnan, so the route cannot be taken as Ho's without his article. It names no text or place for the record of 1563, and the dates are Ho's of 1955; the page itself reports, under maize, a caution of 1977 that such firsts might be superseded. Ho's own article was not read, so what his "red tuber" of 1563 really is cannot be checked.
