tsunaba-nikkoku

kotobank, 綱場 ("tsunaba", title translated), entries from the Digital Daijisen and the Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E7%B6%B1%E5%A0%B4-101428)

What it is: Two Japanese dictionary definitions of the tsunaba, the place on a timber-floating river where a strong rope was stretched across the water to stop the floated logs, with Nishikori on the Kiso and Shimo-Aso on the Hida as the examples.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says what the catch rope was and where it stood: at a key point of the river's middle course, a river harbor rather than a city waterfront. It is a dictionary definition, so it gives no date, no size and no drawing of the rope; that the two places named worked in the Edo period comes from other entries, not from this one.

Used for: the tsunaba as a rope stretched across a timber river's middle course (Trades and services)
