liu-ge-song-cotton

Liu Pingsheng (柳平生) and Ge Jinfang (葛金芳), "'农商社会'视野下南宋商品性农业述论" (Commercial farming in the Southern Song seen as an agrarian-commercial society), in Chinese, as posted on Aisixiang (https://www.aisixiang.com/data/121418.html)

What it is: An article by two Chinese scholars on Southern Song cash crops, published in Social Sciences in Yunnan (云南社会科学), 2017 no. 6, one section of which traces cotton's spread and its marketing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets out, against what it calls the view of most scholars that cotton flourished only from the Yuan, the case for an earlier spread into Zhejiang and the lower Yangzi. That case rests on a few poems, a gloss by Hu Sanxing and one cotton blanket from a Southern Song tomb at Lanxi, read by the authors in support of a thesis about the Song economy, and it counts no cotton land anywhere. The blanket shows cloth in a Zhejiang grave, not where its cotton grew or where it was woven. The gloss was written after the Song fell and the Yangzhou poem is by a man of the Song-Yuan transition, so the lower Yangzi evidence falls late. That poem also speaks of Yangzhou "of old" (旧), so it may not describe the Song prefecture of its author's day. The poems and the Guangzhou case say only 木棉, which the article says was a native tree before asserting that the word means herbaceous cotton. The Guangzhou case shows weaving, not growing, and the article dates it 1078 while its own note cites an entry of 1084. The Siming cargoes are a customs class, coarse goods, that the authors read as cheap; the article gives no quantity or price for them, and it says they came by sea from Fujian, Guangdong and Hainan, so they show southern cloth reaching a Zhejiang port and nothing about growing cotton on the Yangzi. The page gives its sources as endnotes that this project did not follow.

Used for: cotton in the Yangzi region under the Song, the Guangzhou weaving case they date to 1078, and the cotton goods the Siming gazetteer lists on ships (Money and prices)
