不二考匠 (Fuji Kosho; the page gives no reading of the pen name, and this one is a guess),「【歴史小話】大工の家計」 ("A short history: a carpenter's household budget", title translated), note.com, 9 March 2023 (in Japanese; https://note.com/takamasa_jindoh/n/nef9a0e8fdb30)

What it is: A blog post by a former systems engineer, now on a university's staff, that restates the Edo carpenter's household budget given in the popular book 『江戸のお勘定』 (edited by Oishi Manabu): his day wage, his working days (294 of a 354-day lunar year), and his spending on rent for two rooms of four and a half mats, rice, food and fuel, tools, clothing and social costs, for a household of three (himself, his wife and one child), in silver, valued at the Bunka-Bunsei (1804-1830) prices the book takes as its base.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only figure read for an Edo carpenter's day wage early in the 1800s. It is third-hand: the blog reports the book, which it believes drew on the 『文政年間漫録』, and neither the book nor that record was read; it gives one rate for a whole generation and does not say whether it describes a master carpenter or a journeyman; and the rate includes the carpenter's meal money (食事代込み), so it is not the bare wage for his labor.

Used for: an Edo carpenter's day wage early in the 1800s (Money and prices)
