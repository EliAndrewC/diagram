tonami-sanson-kaiteiban

Tonami City Tonami Dispersed Settlement Research Institute (砺波市立砺波散村地域研究所), "砺波平野の散村 改訂版" ("The Dispersed Settlement on the Tonami Plain, revised edition", title translated), 2010, on the Tonami settlement databank (in Japanese; https://databank.tonamino.jp/sansonkaiteiban)

What it is: The full text of a booklet by a municipal research institute in Toyama, in its revised 2010 edition, on the dispersed farmsteads of the Tonami plain: how the scattered settlement arose, the irrigation network, the farmstead plan with its surrounding fields and tree belt (kainyo), village life, house form, towns and later change.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supports the dispersed form for a premodern Hokuriku fan: the Kaga domain's allotment system let farmers work the fields round their houses, each farm worked about 1 ha round its house, so neighbors stood about 100 m apart, a 1955-era plan shows a plot of about 1,000 square meters with cedar on the west and south, and it lists the tree-belt species and the 1717 domain order protecting seven kinds of tree. Its house plans, tree counts and village details describe the twentieth century (the plan is of about 1955, the tree survey from 1983); the booklet says that under the domain's allotment fields were drawn by lot and swapped to lie near the house, and that most farms came to hold their paddy round the house only through the post-war land reform and exchange of plots, so the 1 ha holding and 100 m spacing are present-day figures; the booklet reports the old dispute between an ancient and a post-1600 origin and itself follows the ancient one (scattered farmsteads in the eighth-century Todaiji estate maps, in medieval records and in excavation, kept by Kaga policy). For the use the record makes of it, that clearing went out first from the fan's sides and toe, the booklet says only that this "is thought" to be so, and of the Nara-to-Heian centuries; that settlers reached the fan's center about 500 years ago fits the same order. It speaks of one plain in Toyama only, and of an unusual fan: the Sho river brings so much water that even the fan's center is paddy, where most Japanese fans are dry fields and orchards there.

Used for: a great fan's sides and toe as the ground clearing went out from (Water)
