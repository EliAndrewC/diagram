herodotus-macaulay

Herodotus, The History of Herodotus, volume 1, translated by G. C. Macaulay (Macmillan, 1890), as Project Gutenberg eBook 2707 (https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2707/pg2707.txt)

What it is: The Greek historian's account of the known world, written in the 5th century BCE, here in an English translation of 1890.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is cited for cloth in ancient India: it says (3.106) that the Indians wore cloth from trees that grow wool. The text never says cotton; that the tree-wool is cotton is the modern reader's identification. In that statement it speaks of India's inhabitants in general, naming no region or class; eight chapters earlier (3.98) the same book says there are many tribes of Indians and that those living in the swamps of the river wear clothing made of rushes. It gives no price or quantity, and dates the practice only to the author's own time. It names no informant for the statement, which stands in a list of marvels at the far edge of the inhabited world, directly after a story of gold carried off from ants that he credits to the Persians; read it as report about a distant land, not as an eyewitness's description.
