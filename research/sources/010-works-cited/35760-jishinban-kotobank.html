jishinban-kotobank

Kotobank, 自身番 ("jishinban, the ward's watch house", title translated) - Nipponica (entry by 稲垣史生, Inagaki Shisei), the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and other reference works (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E8%87%AA%E8%BA%AB%E7%95%AA-73425)

What it is: The Kotobank page carrying several published reference entries on the watch houses of Edo's town wards: who kept them, their duties, and the ward clerk (kakiyaku) created in 1721 and paid by the ward.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the evidence that a ward kept a clerk on its own pay rather than hiring one by the day: the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten calls him the clerk the ward hired (町でやとった書役), and Nipponica says he drew pay from the ward (町からの給料). No entry states his term of hire, by the day, the month or the year, so "kept on" is our reading of those words. Nipponica's pay sentence covers the clerk and the hired watchmen together and names a second payment besides the ward's (店番銭), whose payer and amount it does not give. The 1721 creation of the post rests on Nipponica alone. The page gives no amount of his pay, and speaks of Edo chiefly.

Used for: the Edo ward clerk's terms (Money and prices)
