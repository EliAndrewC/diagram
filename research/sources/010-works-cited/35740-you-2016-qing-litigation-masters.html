尤陈俊 (You Chenjun), 「清代讼师贪利形象的多重建构」 ("The many constructions of the Qing litigation master's image of greed", title translated), reposted on 爱思想 (Aisixiang), 2016-04-01 (in Chinese; https://www.aisixiang.com/data/98382.html)

What it is: A legal historian's article on how Qing officials portrayed litigation masters as greedy, which sets against that portrait what official scribes and others actually charged to write a plaint, from county archives, memorials, court papers and account books of 1741 to 1909. It first appeared in 《法学研究》 (Chinese Journal of Law), 2015 no. 5; the Aisixiang repost drops its footnotes, so the archive behind a figure cannot be checked from this copy.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated fees per sheet or per plaint for writing a legal document in several counties, and says, without a figure, that a scribe living on the set rates lived like a hired laborer; its fees are for lawsuits only, many of them the rates the offices set, which scribes often charged well above, and it gives no rate for a letter or a copy outside the courts. Its litigation masters' fees (3,000 to 12,000 cash a plaint in Guangxi, 1778-1781; 1,000 to 5,000 in Tang County, Zhili, 1903-1908) come from men prosecuted for it, above what the many occasional writers took, whom it seldom finds recorded (one took 200 cash). Its fees are in copper cash over 170 years, the set rates rising from 10-30 cash a plaint (1741, 1794) to 100-300 after 1850, so each figure belongs to its decade and place; those after 1895 come from the modern era.

Used for: what a scribe charged to write a plaint in Qing China (Money and prices)
