尤陈俊 (You Chenjun), 「清代讼师贪利形象的多重建构」 ("The many constructions of the Qing litigation master's image of greed", title translated), reposted on 爱思想 (Aisixiang), 2016-04-01 (in Chinese; https://www.aisixiang.com/data/98382.html)

What it is: A legal historian's article on how Qing officials portrayed litigation masters as greedy, which sets against that portrait what official scribes and others actually charged to write a plaint, from county archives, court papers and account books of 1794 to 1909.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated fees per sheet or per plaint for writing a legal document in several counties, and says what a scribe could live on; its fees are for lawsuits only, many of them the rates the offices set, which scribes often charged well above, and it gives no rate for a letter or a copy outside the courts.

Used for: what a scribe charged to write a plaint in Qing China (Money and prices)
