lou-yue-gongkui-ji

Lou Yue (1137-1213), 『攻媿集』 ("Collected works of Gongkui", title translated), chapter 9, Sibu congkan edition, on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/攻媿集_(四部叢刊本)/卷第九; the author page, https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/作者:樓鑰)

What it is: The collected poems and prose of a Southern Song scholar-official, in a Wikisource transcription; chapter 9 holds his short poems, among them "Written in jest on a midday nap".

Why it applies, and its limits: The poem's first line, that rising early three mornings makes one day's work, is the earliest dated form read of that half of the saying, written by 1213. It is one learned man's joking poem, so it dates the line, not who first said it or how widely.

Used for: the old saying that rising early adds a day's work (Money and prices)
