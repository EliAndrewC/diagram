「赤穂塩田」 ("The Ako salt fields", title translated), Hyakka Jiten Mypedia and Hiroyama Takamichi (広山尭道) in the Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, on Kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/赤穂塩田-814801)

What it is: Two encyclopedia articles on the salt fields of Ako, on the Harima coast, one of Edo Japan's chief salt-making districts, which shipped mainly to Edo and Osaka.

Why it applies, and its limits: They date the Ako domain's salt monopoly, from 1680 under the Asano lords and from 1809 under the Mori, and its end in 1821 when the producers resisted. They give one value of salt from inside the monopoly - an account of about 1820 puts a salt works' 4,000 bales a year at 12 kanme - and none from outside it, so they date a monopoly without saying what it did to the price. That one value is a works' gross receipts in silver, half of it spent on fuel, in bales of unstated weight, so it gives no price of salt by weight.

Used for: Japan's domain salt monopolies (Money and prices)
