kikori-bofurin

きこりの森林・林業の教科書 ("The woodcutter's textbook of forests and forestry", title translated), 「Farmland and windbreaks」 (translated; original: 「農地と防風林」) (in Japanese; https://textbook.kikori.org/11-function/wind-forest.htm)

What it is: A chapter of a forestry textbook site written by a practitioner, on how farmland windbreaks work: how the wind passes through and over a belt, what its porosity, width and height do, and how belts are laid out against the wind.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the textbook layout, that windbreak belts are laid in parallel at right angles to a wind from one fixed direction, which is the bearing the towns page tests the drawn belts against; the same page says that where winds come from several directions the belts are laid in a checkerboard instead, so the right-angle rule holds only against a single prevailing wind. Its example is the American-type belts planted in Hokkaido in the modern period rather than the homestead groves of older Japan; the layout is the physics of a wind wall, not a record of how pre-modern farmers planted.

Used for: planted windbreak belts laid at right angles to the wind (Towns)
