kotobank-douza

kotobank 銅座 ("Douza, the copper office", title translated), the entry by 佐々木潤之介 (Sasaki Junnosuke) in 『改訂新版世界大百科事典』 (Heibonsha) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%8A%85%E5%BA%A7-103544)

What it is: An encyclopedia entry on the shogunate's copper office in Osaka, first set up in 1701 and run by the silver mint, closed in 1712, revived from 1738 to 1746 and again from 1766 to 1868, which bought up the country's copper and divided it between export at Nagasaki and sale at home.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the office's five buying prices for copper (Akita copper at two prices, Nanbu, Besshi-Tatsukawa and Yoshioka) and the fixed export prices, from records of 1774 in the office's third period (from 1766), the one price of raw copper found. It does not name the record; its export prices were set by policy and held almost fixed; and it gives the home-market price only as about twice the China export price, in its account of the years after 1738, not as a figure for 1774. The buying prices are what the monopoly paid the mines for rough copper, above the export price, so that the office sold export copper at a loss; they are not a price a caster paid, and the entry gives no level for the home price after 1766, when the shogunate fixed it (to 1797), so what a caster paid in 1774 is not known from it.

Used for: the price of raw copper as a floor under a cast object's cost (Money and prices)
