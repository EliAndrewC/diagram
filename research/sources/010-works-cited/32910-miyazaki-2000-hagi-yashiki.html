miyazaki-2000-hagi-yashiki

宮崎勝美 (Miyazaki Katsumi), 「大名屋敷の作事・普請と江戸遺跡」 ("Building and earthworks at daimyo mansions, and Edo's archaeological sites", title translated), the revised summary of a paper to the 13th meeting of the Edo Iseki Kenkyukai, 2000 (in Japanese; https://edo.jpn.org/cyber/2000/miyazaki2000.htm)

What it is: A historian of the University of Tokyo's Historiographical Institute summarizes the Hagi (Choshu) domain's records of rebuilding its three Edo mansions after the great fire of 1772: what the work cost, and the timber, tiles, nails and earth it shipped from the domain or bought in Osaka and Edo, with quantities and prices.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one price found for copper roof tiles, and shows cypress-bark nails shipped for the same work (83,000 of them from Hagi), though it names no quantity, area or price of the bark itself, nor which buildings it roofed: the nails are the only evidence that cypress bark was laid. Its buildings are a great domain's mansions bought for in bulk, not a village shrine. It prices the copper tiles only as one lot: 11,000 tiles of 1 shaku by 1.2 shaku, bought in Osaka in 1772-73, in the shortage after the great fire, for "a little under 30 kan" of silver. Its own figures do not quite agree: it gives the Osaka purchase as 107 kan 481 monme in all, and its timber (45 kan 436 monme) and clay tiles (35 kan 93 monme) leave at most about 27 kan for the copper tiles and everything else bought there. So a price per tile (about 2.4 to 2.7 monme) is our own division of a rounded and uncertain total. The summary does not say whether that total also covers the 500 larger copper sashi-gawara (1.4 by 3 shaku) and the 98,300 copper-tile nails listed beside it, and it is the cost of buying alone, without freight to Edo or the roofer's labor. It gives the tiles' size but not how many covered a tsubo. And it is a meeting summary: it quotes several passages of the domain's records, but gives the copper figure only in the author's own words (the full record was printed in a 1999 research-grant report).

Used for: what copper roofing cost, and whether cypress-bark roofs were laid (Money and prices)
