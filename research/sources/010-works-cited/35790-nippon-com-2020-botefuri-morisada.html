nippon.com, "庶民の手軽な商い棒手振 : 『守貞漫稿』（その5）" ("The street peddler, the commoner's easy trade: from the Morisada Manko, part 5", title translated), 20 December 2020 (in Japanese; https://www.nippon.com/ja/japan-topics/g01009/)

What it is: A magazine article on Edo's street peddlers, drawn from the Morisada Manko, the late-Edo survey of city customs, with the stock money, interest and takings of a vegetable peddler that it credits to the Bunsei nenkan manroku, an account of Edo around the 1820s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives how a peddler started out on borrowed stock and what he kept of a day's takings, and the 1659 count of licensed peddlers. Its figures are the writer's report of the period accounts, not quoted from them, its yen conversions are the writer's own, and its line that married peddlers worked every day while single men idled cites no source.

Used for: an Edo vegetable peddler's stock money and takings, and the article's own contrast of single and married peddlers (Money and prices)
