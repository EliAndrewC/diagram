hansho-kotobank

kotobank 半鐘 ("fire alarm bell", title translated) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8D%8A%E9%90%98-118280)

What it is: Kotobank's entry for 半鐘 (hansho), aggregating standard Japanese reference dictionaries: the small bronze hanging bell hung on a fire-watch tower, and the code by which the number of strokes told a hearer what kind of incident had happened - for a fire, a continuous peal for a near one, fewer strokes the further off it was. The entries also date the usage: one of the dictionaries puts the hanging of the bell on a fire-watch tower from the Edo period onward, and the historical dictionary among them carries an attestation of the word from 1686.

Why it applies, and its limits: It supplies the one thing our fire-tower explanation needs beyond the tower itself: that the bell carried information by its cadence rather than simply making a noise, which is why a watchman on a tower is worth drawing, and it puts that bell on a tower within the period our settlements are pitched in rather than leaving it undated. Its limits are that its 1686 attestation is of the WORD in another of its senses, the bell of a striking clock, the earliest example it gives for the fire-alarm sense being dated 1706. Nor does it date the stroke code or say what size of place hung the bell: the encyclopedia entry among them describes the code in the same paragraph as the iron towers and pump sheds of the modern period, and ties the bell's spread to fire brigades it glosses with the Meiji-era term 消防組 (the national fire-brigade system of 1894), so it supports a bell on a tower in an Edo-period town but not in an Edo-period village, and its code cannot be dated to the Edo period from this page.

Used for: the alarm bell hung on a fire-watch tower and the code its strokes carried - a continuous peal for a near fire, fewer strokes the further off it was - and the bell's dating to the Edo period; the wooden signal board before it, the bell spreading with the fire brigades, and the ladder before the tower (Trades and services)
