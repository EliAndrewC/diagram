chinese-repository-1835-diet

"Diet of the Chinese", The Chinese Repository, vol. 3, no. 10 (Canton, February 1835), pp. 457-471, archive.org copy (https://archive.org/stream/chineserepositor3101unse/chineserepositor3101unse_djvu.txt; the page read, https://archive.org/download/chineserepositor3101unse/page/n46_w1400.jpg)

What it is: An unsigned article in the English-language monthly published by the foreign missionaries at Canton, describing what the Chinese ate, with a list of the prices of food and fuel in Canton's market, the wages of laborers and the rents of houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one of the few lists of a Chinese city's retail prices, wages and rents together before the Opium War. It is a foreigner's account, compiled, it says itself, "According to the best information, which we can obtain", in a city where foreigners could not shop freely and paid their compradors more than the market; its prices are round ranges for one year, with no grade stated; and Canton, the empire's foreign-trade port, was not a typical city.

Used for: Canton's food and fuel prices, a laborer's day wage and a small house's rent in 1835 (Money and prices)
