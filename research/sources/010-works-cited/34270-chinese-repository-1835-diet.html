"Diet of the Chinese", The Chinese Repository, vol. 3, no. 10 (Canton, February 1835), pp. 457-471, archive.org copy (https://archive.org/stream/chineserepositor3101unse/chineserepositor3101unse_djvu.txt; the pages read, https://archive.org/download/chineserepositor3101unse/page/n44_w1400.jpg, https://archive.org/download/chineserepositor3101unse/page/n46_w1400.jpg)

What it is: An unsigned article in the English-language monthly published by the foreign missionaries at Canton, describing what the Chinese ate, with a list of the prices of food and fuel in Canton's market, the wages of laborers and the rents of houses.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one of the few lists of a Chinese city's retail prices, wages and rents together before the Opium War. It is a foreigner's account, compiled, it says itself, "According to the best information, which we can obtain", by foreigners who met, it says, "many obstacles in the way of ascertaining the actual market rate" and whose compradors charged them 10 to 100 percent above it, though the list is meant as the natives' price, not theirs; its prices are undated single figures with no grade stated, a few of them wide ranges (rice 1½ to 3 taels a picul), and it says itself that floods and the extortions of local officers moved them; and Canton, the empire's foreign-trade port, was not a typical city.

Used for: Canton's food and fuel prices, a laborer's day wage and a small house's rent in 1835 (Money and prices)
