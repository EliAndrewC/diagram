English Wikipedia, "Kyakhta trade" (in English; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyakhta_trade)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the trade between Russia and Qing China through Kyakhta, on the Mongolian border, from the Treaty of Kyakhta of 1727, including the founding of the Russian post and the Qing fort of Maimaicheng facing it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests the other historical form of regular dealing across a land border: a post on each side of the line, a few hundred feet apart, with officials of both handling diplomacy and disputes. Wikipedia tags it as lacking inline citations and needing factual verification, it says it is a summary of Clifford M. Foust's monograph Muscovite and Mandarin (1969), and its passage on the two posts a few hundred feet apart, with officials on both sides, carries no citation, so that distance is the article's and not a checked figure (Foust is the closer source); the parties are the Russian and Qing empires in the eighteenth century, not domains or clans, and it describes no building where the two met. Its route from Kyakhta to Beijing, in three stages southbound (170 miles to Urga, 625 miles across the Gobi to Kalgan in about 30 days by camel, 100 miles to Beijing in about four days by mule), covers the 1700s and 1800s without a year and, like the posts, carries no citation (its sources list only Foust and Alexander Michie, who traveled the road in 1863), so the days are typical freight times, not one caravan's and not the northbound tea run's specifically. It gives no days for the leg to Urga, and its own earlier section on the route puts the Gobi crossing at no less than 30 to 40 days, so the 30 days is the low end of its range.

Used for: Kyakhta and Maimaicheng as a post on each side of the border (Estates and other compounds); the stages and days of the road from Kyakhta to Beijing (Money and prices)
