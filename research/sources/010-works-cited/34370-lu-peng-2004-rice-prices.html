lu-peng-2004-rice-prices

卢锋 (Lu Feng) and 彭凯翔 (Peng Kaixiang), 《我国长期米价研究（1644-2000）》 ("A study of China's long-run rice prices, 1644-2000", title translated), China Center for Economic Research, Peking University, Working Paper C2004009, July 2004 (in Chinese; https://nsd.pku.edu.cn/attachments/e0f218104a1f41bba495f50835b5d4af.pdf)

What it is: Two economists' survey of Chinese rice prices from 1644 to 2000, assembled from the economic historians' regional series, with trends and correlations of five-year average prices for Suzhou, Guangdong, Hubei and Hunan in the 18th century, beside Haizhou's December price taken every fifth year from 1740, and the copper cash to silver rate for 1721-1900 taken from Lin Man-houng (1994).

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the 18th-century trend of rice prices in silver and the long movement of the cash-to-silver rate. Its regional figures are five-year averages, so they hide year-to-year swings, and Haizhou's is one month's price every fifth year; the authors note that the Hubei and Hunan series are too sparse for averaging to smooth them, so correlations with those places and Haizhou tend to run low. The Guangdong, Hubei and Hunan averages come through Wu Chengming's (2001) table, so it builds on others' series at second hand rather than new archival work.

Used for: the long trend of Qing rice prices in silver, and the cash-to-silver rate (Money and prices)
