ja.wikipedia 道場 ("training hall", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/道場)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the dojo, the martial-arts practice hall, with its history from private and outdoor practice to the built hall; an encyclopedia article.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests practice at a private residence, outdoors or on an earthen floor before the mid-Edo boarded hall, and the great enrolled dojos of Edo, which is the ladder this page's county and city tiers rest on; it frames the change by period, not by town and country (its one urban note, uncited, is that populous places such as Edo grew large halls from small private ones), and gives no floor areas or counts. Its Edo account rests on a single reference, the Japanese Academy of Budo kendo section's 剣道を知る事典 (pp. 122-123), cited on one sentence; it hedges the teachers' settling into fixed halls as only "thought to" have happened, and its evidence is chiefly swordsmanship and jujutsu. It also says the Edo-period hall was usually called 稽古場 (keikoba, "practice place"), 道場 becoming the common word for it only from Meiji.

Used for: where samurai trained, the master's seat and its scrolls at the head of the hall, and the shrine shelf's late arrival (Estates and other compounds); that many domains attached a practice hall to their school (The government quarter)
