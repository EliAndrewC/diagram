竹越与三郎 (Takekoshi Yosaburō), 『日本経済史』 第9巻 (Economic history of Japan, vol. 9), 平凡社, 1935, the chapter on craftsmen's guilds of the Tokugawa period (in Japanese; https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1448699)

What it is: A historian's survey of Japan's economic history, whose ninth volume treats the Tokugawa period's craft guilds and quotes the rules the Sakai tobacco-knife smiths' guild set in the seventh month of 1761, signed by thirty members, with his reading of them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source found for how many knives a Sakai smith might make in a day: the rules cap night work by size of knife, and the author says the daytime rule is not known. The knives are tobacco-cutting knives, not kitchen knives; it quotes the rules in a modern print, and its levy per knife disagrees with the city history's (sakai-shishi-1930-3).

Used for: the Sakai tobacco-knife guild's rules of 1761: its levy and its cap on night work (Money and prices)
