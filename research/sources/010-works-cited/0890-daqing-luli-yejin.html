daqing-luli-yejin

《大清律例》兵律 ("Military Law", title translated), 卷十九 (volume 19), the articles 夜禁 (the night prohibition) and 毀棄軍器 (destroying or discarding military arms), Chinese Wikisource transcription (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/%E5%A4%A7%E6%B8%85%E5%BE%8B%E4%BE%8B/%E5%8D%B7%E5%8D%81%E4%B9%9D)

What it is: The military-law chapter of the Great Qing Code, the penal code in force across Qing China, in a Wikisource transcription of the primary text; its night-prohibition article sets the hours of the curfew by the watch bells and the punishment for breaking it, in the capital and in the provinces, and its article on arms sets what an officer or soldier owed for arms he kept, discarded or lost, with a sub-statute on the name each arrow had to bear.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the law itself, the best kind of source for what a curfew was and what it cost to break, and it applies directly to the Chinese half of the two-traditions argument our block gates rest on; its arms article says how the state accounted for arms lost in battle. Its limits are that a statute records the rule and not how often it was enforced, and that the curfew's hours are given in watches, which this page converts to clock time only roughly; the arms article says what the state would not charge for, not whether arrows were gathered up after a fight.

Used for: the curfew's hours by the watch bells and its penalties, graded between the capital and the provinces; arms lost in battle written off, and the owner's name on each arrow (Money and prices)
