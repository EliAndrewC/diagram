tarumizu-onagaya-kagoshima

Kagoshima prefecture, お長屋 ("the rowhouse", title translated), a one-page sheet on a prefecturally designated building at Tarumizu (https://www.pref.kagoshima.jp/ba08/documents/101223_20221118114551-1.pdf)

What it is: Kagoshima prefecture's one-page description of the rowhouse at Tarumizu, designated a prefectural cultural property in 2014: a building put up in 1611 when the castle there was built, 15 ken across its front and 3 ken at its side, which the prefecture thinks was a samurai guardroom.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the front and side of a surviving rowhouse of 1611, one of a castle's buildings. The building was a guardroom, not dwellings, by the prefecture's own reading ("it is thought"), and the sheet calls it a freestanding rowhouse in the manner of a tamon turret (多門櫓風), the long defensive building on a castle's walls, so its long, narrow shape may be a castle building's shape rather than a residential row's; and the sheet says its size has no parallel in the prefecture, so it is no typical row; it gives no rooms and no word on its roof or walls.

Used for: the size and use of the Tarumizu rowhouse (Estates and other compounds)
