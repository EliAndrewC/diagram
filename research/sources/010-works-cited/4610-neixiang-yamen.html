Neixiang county yamen (内乡县衙), Henan - site documentation and plan descriptions (https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202103/12/WS604b0287a3101e7ce9743b4c.html)

What it is: A feature article by the Henan Daily reporter Zhang Dongyun, republished on China Daily's Chinese-language site in March 2021, on the Neixiang county yamen in Henan - a county government compound first built in 1304 and rebuilt by a late Qing magistrate, now a museum. It describes the plan in detail: more than 47,000 square meters, eighteen courtyards in succession and over 280 rooms, laid out by Ming and Qing regulation with the offices in front and the residence behind, and it names what stands on each side of the first two courtyards.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the plan of a real county yamen in the words of a reporter walking it - the central axis, the two front courtyards, the Six Chambers, the total area and room count, what stood east and west of the first court, and the great hall's width and depth - which is what our magistracy plan is laid out from. Its limits are that the buildings are a late-Qing rebuild of 1892-1895, which the article says was repaired and enlarged several times afterward and restored as a museum in the 1980s, so the plan, the area and the room count are those of the preserved museum compound rather than a documented original; that the great hall's 27.22 by 12.37 meters is a present-day figure for that one standing hall, with no word on who measured it, in what the article calls the most completely preserved county yamen, its three halls modeled on the Forbidden City's and rebuilt by a former Board of Works official on a large subscription, so it is one well-funded county's hall rather than a typical one; and that its west line holds the guest hall, the dining hall and the jail, not a clerks' lodging, and it names no sheds against the wall - the record uses Neixiang as the counter-example on both, and the runners' duty courtyard it does name stands on the east.

Used for: the three-axis yamen plan, the two front courtyards, the Six Chambers, the area and room count; the runners' duty courtyard on the EAST; the west line as guest hall, dining hall and jail - the counter-example to a clerks' lodging on the west (吏舍 and banfang sheds are not on the page); the great hall's measured width and depth, a modern figure for the standing hall
