鷲崎俊太郎 (Washizaki Shuntaro), 「江戸の町屋敷経営と不動産収益率の長期分析：1775〜1872」 ("A long-run analysis of the management of Edo town lots and their real-estate yields, 1775-1872", title translated), 經濟學研究 (Keizaigaku Kenkyu, Kyushu University) 79(4), 2012, from p. 95 (in Japanese; https://api.lib.kyushu-u.ac.jp/opac_download_md/25880/p095.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's study of the forty Edo town lots the Mitsui house held as security for its government remittance business, from the half-yearly rent accounts the house kept for nearly a century: the ground and house rents taken in, the ward charges and the managers' pay paid out, and what the owner kept, set against the lots' assessed value.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures how much an Edo town lot, let with its houses, earned its owner each year against what it was worth, the ratio of rent to value the record otherwise lacks. Its yields are for one great merchant house's lots in the commercial heart of Edo, held more for their worth as security than for income, and they are measured against the lots' assessed value with the houses on them, not against the buildings alone - a single total of the lots' deed values (56,565 ryō), the figure at which they were valued when the family divided its property in 1774, held unchanged to 1872 rather than a price that moved with the market. The owner's share it counts is before repairs and the rebuilding after fires, which the paper says came out of it, so its yields are gross: counting those costs, they fell to about one per cent or less in the years of great fires. It gives no rent for any single tenement, shop or storehouse; its table of one street's lots in the Kansei era and 1840 gives monthly ground rents on the front of a lot and house rents on the back, by heads that imply a tsubo without saying so, for a commercial street of central Edo.

Used for: the yield of an Edo town lot against its value, when rents were collected, and one street's ground and house rents by the tsubo, Kansei era and 1840 (Money and prices)
