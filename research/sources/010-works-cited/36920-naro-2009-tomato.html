naro-2009-tomato

National Agriculture and Food Research Organization (農研機構), "今が旬の"トマト"のお話" (On the tomato, now in season), 23 May 2009 (in Japanese; https://www.naro.go.jp/publicity_report/season/006534.html)

What it is: A seasonal explainer on the tomato from Japan's national agricultural research agency, covering its origin, history in Japan and varieties.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the agency's account of why early varieties failed in Japan and which variety changed that. The early varieties it means are Meiji and Taisho ones and the Ponderosa came in the Showa period, all after the period the setting is modeled on. For the years before that it has only three sentences: the tomato reached Nagasaki from Europe by way of China in the 17th century, Kaibara Ekiken's Yamato honzo (大和本草) called it 唐柿, and it was then thought of as a medicinal and ornamental plant. It gives no closer date for that arrival, no year for the Ponderosa's arrival beyond the Showa period, and no source for this history: the references it lists concern only NARO's own cooking tomato and a hydroponic method.
