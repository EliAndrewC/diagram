izumo-kokuso-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 出雲国造 ("Izumo no kuni no miyatsuko", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/出雲国造)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the hereditary office of the Izumo kokuso, the chief priesthood of the Izumo shrine, held in the Senge and Kitajima lines, with the list of its holders by generation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It numbers the holders of one hereditary shrine priesthood down to the present holder (84 in the Senge line, 79 in the Kitajima line, both counted through the single line before the two houses split in the 1340s), which is the longest such an office could run, not what a common one did. Its list follows the Senge house's own genealogy, the Izumo kokuso dento ryaku, which the page names as the source of the list. This is the priesthood of a shrine of the first rank, one of the very few houses allowed to keep the ancient title of provincial governor, so it does not show that a village or town shrine's priestly family reached such a count. The count passed eighty only after the Edo period: the page gives the 81st Senge holder's succession as 1882 (the 80th was born in 1845), and the Kitajima line stands at 79. The numbers count holders, not father-to-son generations: brothers succeeded brothers (the 55th after the 54th, the 81st after the 80th), the page's genealogy marks adoptions, and the page judges six of the early generations to have really been three. The early generations are traditional rather than historical, the first holders being gods, and the page numbers holders inconsistently: it calls the present holder's father both the 83rd and the 84th, Takatoki both the 53rd and the 54th, and Hatayasu both the 23rd and the 24th. For the 1549 enshrinement list of Kizuki shrine (杵築大社遷宮入目注文), the page quotes the document from the 2023 printed edition of the Kitajima house papers, so the list is a document of the time read through this article. It lists the vestments, brocades, cloth, treasures and horses for the enshrinement at one first-rank shrine, not a village one. It is not the whole cost: it leaves the ridge-raising decorations to the carpenters' own statement, and it names silver (しろかね) among the carpenters' gifts, so it does not show that the enshrinement used no money.

Used for: a shrine priesthood counted past eighty generations, handed down within one house, the present holder succeeding his father (Religion and the dead); the list of an enshrinement's needs at Kizuki in 1549, in goods and no money (Money and prices)
