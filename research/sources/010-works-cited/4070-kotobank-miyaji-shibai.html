kotobank-miyaji-shibai

Digital Daijisen, Seisenban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and Kaitei Shinban Sekai Daihyakka Jiten (Heibonsha; article by Abe Yūzō), s.v. 宮地芝居 ("shrine-precinct theater", title translated), on kotobank (https://kotobank.jp/word/宮地芝居-1157374)

What it is: Kotobank's reproduction of two dictionary entries and Heibonsha's encyclopedia article (Abe Yūzō) on miyaji-shibai, the Edo-period plays licensed by occasion in shrine and temple precincts; the license's length (100 fair-weather days, hence 百日芝居) is in the Heibonsha article, not the dictionaries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the precinct play, which is the stage this page gives a town, and what that stage was forbidden (a drum tower, a hanamichi, a revolving stage, a draw curtain); nothing on the stage's orientation. Its 100 days were counted in fair-weather days, so a run took longer, and the article says licenses were renewed until runs were almost permanent, so it is no fixed season. It is the Edo shogunate's rule under the temple and shrine magistrate, banned in the Tenpō reforms and revived until the end of the shogunate, and its examples are Edo, Kyoto and Osaka shrines, with provincial shrines given one clause.

Used for: the precinct stage (Towns); how long a shrine-ground license ran (Money and prices)
