冷鹏 (Leng Peng), "生存需求、政策引导、经济转型与技术革新：明清苏南生态农业的发展动因探析" ("Subsistence, policy, economic change and technical innovation: the drivers of ecological agriculture in southern Jiangsu in the Ming and Qing", title translated), Journal of Nanjing Agricultural University (Social Sciences Edition) 20(6), 2020, 105-114 (in Chinese; https://html.rhhz.net/njnydxxbskb/20200611.htm)

What it is: A journal paper by a doctoral student of Nanjing Agricultural University's Institute of Chinese Agricultural Civilization, on why intensive, recycling farming (the mulberry-dike fishpond, multiple cropping, water vegetables) grew up south of the Yangzi in the Ming and Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a modern historian's reading of Ming-Qing southern Jiangsu (Suzhou, Changzhou, Zhenjiang and Jiangning prefectures and Taicang), which takes in the Lake Tai plain the record's polders come from as well as the Yangzi-bank high fields and the Nanjing-Zhenjiang hills; its water-vegetable paragraph rests on a 2012 degree thesis (Cao Ying) rather than a period text quoted on the page, gives no dates within the Ming and Qing, and names districts, not areas or shares. Its cotton share is one sentence relaying Li Bozhong's reckoning (about 50% of the fields in the late Ming, about 70% by the mid-Qing) without his method; it holds only for the cotton zone, mainly on the Yangzi-bank high fields and not the polders, so it cannot stand for the region's fields as a whole. The page does not say which districts that cotton zone takes in: the figure comes from Li's book on Jiangnan as a whole, and the paper's southern Jiangsu leaves out Songjiang prefecture, a core of the Jiangnan cotton belt, so it may not be southern Jiangsu's figure alone.

Used for: Wu county the main lotus-root district among Suzhou prefecture's water-vegetable districts in the Ming and Qing (Field archetypes); Li Bozhong's estimate of cotton's share of the fields in the cotton zone, as this paper on southern Jiangsu relays it, here at second hand (Money and prices)
