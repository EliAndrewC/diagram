Li Xinsheng (李昕升), 《美洲作物与人口增长》 (American crops and population growth), 《中国经济史研究》 (Researches in Chinese Economic History) 2020, no. 3, reposted without its notes or tables by the Chinese Academy of History on Tencent News, 25 January 2021 (in Chinese; https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20210125A054N800?id=20210125A054N800&path=a&app=news&suid=&redirect_pc=1)

What it is: A research article by an associate professor at Nanjing Agricultural University, arguing against crediting maize and the sweet potato with the growth of Qing China's population, and setting out why those crops stayed limited in Qing China, mattering only from the mid-19th century and then mainly in the southern hills.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest account read of what held the American food crops back in China, by an author who says he went through the produce sections of over 8,000 local gazetteers. By his own account those sections show where and when the crops spread but mostly give only a crop's name, so they cannot show its acreage, output or share; the reasons he gives for the crops' slow spread rest on rent records, grain-price reports, Republican surveys, crop rotations and his own inference. It argues one side of a dispute, covers China and the food crops only, and the repost drops the notes, so the evidence behind each statement cannot be followed. Its maize and sweet potato yields are Republican-era ones that the author carries back to the Qing.
