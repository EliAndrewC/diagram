washizaki-2007-edo-land-market

鷲崎俊太郎 (Washizaki Shuntarō), 「江戸の土地市場と不動産投資―収益還元法による地代・地価分析―」 ("The land market of Edo and investment in real estate: an analysis of rents and land prices by the income-capitalization method", title translated), 社会経済史学 (Socio-Economic History) 73, no. 2, July 2007 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sehs/73/2/73_KJ00008198243/_pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's study of what town lots in Edo sold for against the ground rents they earned, from the 1710s to the 1840s, treating a lot's price as its net rent capitalized at the rate of interest.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a price in years of net income attested from the time, the Edo town office's valuation of a town lot, and it names the studies that found farmland priced the same way. Its own figures are for town lots in the shogun's capital, in its Nihonbashi and Kyōbashi commercial core, not for farmland. The twenty years is one valuation example, for a central lot let wholly as ground leases, which it takes from Yoshida Nobuyuki's study. It is an assessed market rate, not a sale price, and the paper's own finding is that lot prices moved away from capitalized rent: they rose while rents fell in the 1700s and collapsed from the 1790s while interest fell. Its passage on farmland reports others' findings, from farm records of the Kinai and Sanuki, without a rate; and its text layer is machine-read, with some characters misread.

Used for: a town lot's price as twenty years of its owner's net income, and farmland priced as its capitalized net yield (Money and prices)
