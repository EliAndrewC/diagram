Baba Norie, "アイロンに漂白、オーガニック石鹸。江戸の洗濯事情を洗いざらいご紹介" ("Irons, bleach and organic soap: all about washing clothes in Edo", title translated), Waraku web, Shogakukan, 2 February 2026 (in Japanese; https://intojapanwaraku.com/culture/294401/)

What it is: A magazine article on how clothes were washed in the Edo period, the lye and other cleaners used, and the first washing shops, drawn from Ochiai Shigeru's history of washing (1984) and Kaibara Ekiken's moral writings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that shops washing clothes as a trade already existed by the first years of the Genroku era (about 1688), a date they are attested by rather than one they began in, and it names no city for them. Its line that Edo's shops were worked mostly by women and Kyoto's mostly by men is undated and passed on as hearsay, with no source of its own. It gives no price, and does not say whose clothes the shops washed.

Used for: washing shops by about 1688, and who worked them in Edo and Kyoto (Money and prices)
