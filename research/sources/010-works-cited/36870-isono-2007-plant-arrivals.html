isono-2007-plant-arrivals

Isono Naohide (磯野直秀), "明治前園芸植物渡来年表" (Exotic garden flowers and trees brought in Japan: a chronology to 1868), Hiyoshi Review of Natural Science (Keio University), no. 42 (2007), pp. 27-58 (in Japanese; https://koara.lib.keio.ac.jp/xoonips/modules/xoonips/download.php?file_id=13125)

What it is: A chronological table by a professor emeritus of Keio University, listing year by year the plants recorded as reaching Japan up to 1868, each row naming the old text or later reference it rests on, with an overview by period and an index of plants.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one dated run of arrivals read for the Edo period. It says it centers on ornamental garden plants and adds only some vegetables and fruit, and many of its dates are the first time a plant is sketched or named rather than the date it landed, so it cannot give a rate of arrival, and a first record after the seclusion edicts does not show that the plant landed after them (the first dated tomato, in 1668, is a sketch by Kano Tan'yu). It warns that a stated year of arrival may be the year a plant reached Edo, Kyoto or Owari rather than Japan. It omits the place of origin in most cases and many rows name no route, so routes are known only for some plants. Its rows rest on chronicles and reference works not read here, and it repeats some dates from modern references that give no source for them.

Used for: Dates and routes of plants reaching Japan before and after the seclusion edicts, and the first dated tomato (Money and prices)
