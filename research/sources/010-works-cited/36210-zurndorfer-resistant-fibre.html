zurndorfer-resistant-fibre

Harriet T. Zurndorfer, "The Resistant Fibre: The Pre-modern History of Cotton in China", a paper filed on the London School of Economics' Global Economic History Network pages under Helsinki, undated on its face (https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/Helsinki/HELSINKIZurndorfer.pdf)

What it is: A Leiden historian's short paper surveying cotton in China from before the common era to the eighteenth century, drawn from the secondary literature. It is filed with the papers of the Global Economic History Network's Helsinki cotton session of August 2006.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one survey read that takes cotton through every stage, the state's part included. Its first page says "Not for citation without written permission of the author", so it is cited here with that said. That notice reads as a draft's, which is this project's inference since the paper does not call itself one, so the peer review its label describes should not be assumed. Its facts are at second hand from the works it names, and some of them conflict with other accounts read: it calls the 1273 manual a Song work where Pelliot has it made for Qubilai. The only quantity of cotton or cloth it gives is the "annual quota of 5000 bolts" of early Song Fujian, taken from So: a tribute quota, not an output, in a bolt it does not define. It gives no price, no wage and no cloth per head, so it carries the order of events and not a money figure. That hemp was "worn by the majority of inhabitants" is one clause, taken from Cartier, about the centuries before the Song, and the same paper names ramie and bark cloth among the materials cotton displaced.

Used for: the hemp-wearing baseline, Song Fujian's cotton quota, and the Yangzi and Suning stages (Money and prices)
