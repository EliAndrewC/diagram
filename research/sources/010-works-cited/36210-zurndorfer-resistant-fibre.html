Harriet T. Zurndorfer, "The Resistant Fibre: The Pre-modern History of Cotton in China", a paper for a Global Economic History Network conference at Helsinki, undated on its face (https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/Helsinki/HELSINKIZurndorfer.pdf)

What it is: A Leiden historian's short survey of cotton in China from before the common era to the end of the Ming, drawn from the secondary literature.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one survey read that takes cotton through every stage, the state's part included. Its first page says "Not for citation without written permission of the author", so it is cited here with that said. Its facts are at second hand from the works it names, and some of them conflict with the primary texts: it calls the 1273 manual a Song work where Pelliot has it made for Qubilai.

Used for: the hemp-wearing baseline, Song Fujian's cotton quota, and the Yangzi and Suning stages (Money and prices)
