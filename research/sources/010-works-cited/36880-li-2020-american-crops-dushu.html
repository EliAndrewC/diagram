Li Xinsheng (李昕升), 《美洲作物的中国故事》 (The story of American crops in China), 《读书》 2020, no. 1, reposted by the magazine on The Paper, 8 January 2020 (in Chinese; https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_5453847)

What it is: An essay for general readers by Li Xinsheng, who cites his own research on the output of American crops, surveying the American crops in China and arguing that they spread slowly because of fixed habits of eating and farming.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the tomato's history two sentences, with a reign and no figure, names it with the chili as one of the two American crops that did most for Sichuan cooking, which it says formed in the late Qing and early Republic, though those two sentences keep the tomato an ornamental until the Republic, little grown even then and mainly near the big cities, with its heyday only after 1949; it never says what the tomato added to Sichuan dishes or when. Only the first clause, a record under the Wanli reign and an ornamental plant after it, bears on the setting's era. The essay has no footnotes: it names Wang Xiangjin's 《群芳谱》 for that first record and otherwise rests on the author's own wider work. It also says in passing that American crops "soon" spread over China, in tension with its own argument for slowness.
