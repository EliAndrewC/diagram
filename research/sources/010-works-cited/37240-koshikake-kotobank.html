koshikake-kotobank

Kotobank, 腰掛 ("koshikake", title translated) - Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen and its Seisen-ban Nihon kokugo daijiten (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/腰掛-501050)

What it is: Two Japanese dictionaries' entries on the word koshikake, one sense of which is the waiting place of parties at the shogunate's courts, with the Nihon kokugo daijiten's earliest dated example, a story of 1694.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the waiting place to at least 1694 and names courts that kept one (its list ends "and others") - the Hyojosho, the temple-and-shrine magistracy and the town magistracy; the dictionaries say nothing of where it stood or its size. The page also carries a cut-short excerpt from a third work, Heibonsha's old-edition Sekai daihyakka jiten (from its entry on the waiting-place tea-house), which sets the waiting place before the gate and gives the South Town Magistracy's as 94 tsubo (about 310 square meters), a figure for one court of the shogun's capital; nothing on the page speaks of any office outside the shogunate's own courts.

Used for: the hearing court's waiting shed (Estates and other compounds)
