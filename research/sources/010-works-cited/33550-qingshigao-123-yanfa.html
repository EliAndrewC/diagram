清史稿 (Draft History of the Qing, title translated), volume 123, 食貨四, 鹽法 ("Food and goods, part 4: the salt laws"), Wikisource text (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%B8%85%E5%8F%B2%E7%A8%BF/%E5%8D%B7123)

What it is: The treatise on the Qing salt monopoly in the draft dynastic history compiled after the dynasty fell, summarizing its districts, taxes and reforms from the 1600s to 1911 and quoting the memorials and edicts behind them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the officials' own figures for what salt cost at the saltworks, what the state fixed it at for the Changlu district, what a censor said it sold for in copper cash, and what a person spent on it a day. Its figures are those officials gave when arguing for a change, and the history was compiled in the 1910s and 1920s from the court's records, not from markets.

Used for: the price of salt at the saltworks and at the official price in Qing China (Money and prices)
