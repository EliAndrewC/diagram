kotobank-kanjin-no

Kaitei Shinpan Sekai Dai-hyakka Jiten (Heibonsha), Nihon Daihyakka Zensho (Nipponica), Seisen-ban Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and others, s.v. 勧進能 ("fund-raising noh", title translated), aggregated at kotobank (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/勧進能)

What it is: The Kotobank page for the fund-raising noh performance, a large noh show open to the public for an entrance fee, carrying several encyclopedias' entries on its history from the medieval period to the last of the once-in-a-career shows in 1848.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is where the noh's few public prices are found: an admission of 30 mon in a jokebook of the 1610s-1620s, and how Edo's once-in-a-career shows, a privilege the shogunate granted a school's head once in his life, sold their tickets by forcing them on the townsmen of each ward. The 30 mon comes from a collection of comic tales, not an account; the ward allotment was a forced sale at a rare licensed event, not a price the market set; and the page gives no price list by seat class for any show.

Used for: what a fund-raising noh charged and how its tickets were sold (Money and prices)
