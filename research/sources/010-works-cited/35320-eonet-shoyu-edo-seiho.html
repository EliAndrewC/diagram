eonet-shoyu-edo-seiho

Muraoka Shoji (pen name), "江戸時代の醤油の製法" ("How soy sauce was made in the Edo period", title translated), in the Edo dining-culture section of the site "日本食文化の醤油を知る" ("learning about soy sauce in Japanese food culture", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.eonet.ne.jp/~shoyu/mametisiki/reference-4.html)

What it is: A long personal-site article, by the author of muraoka-edo-nagaya-life, that quotes the Edo-period soy sauce recipes one after another, with modern Japanese renderings: among them the Honchō shokkan (1697), the Wakan sansai zue (1712), and the soy sauce chapter of Ōkura Nagatsune's farm manual Kōeki kokusan kō (1844).

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that quotes the 1844 manual's household brewing at length, with its measures of beans, barley, salt and water for a household of five. Its rendering of the Honchō shokkan is a modern translation, whose translator it does not name, not the 1697 text. Other than the Iwanami Bunko edition of the Kōeki kokusan kō in its reference list, it rarely names an edition. Its own modern paraphrase of the 1844 measures gets the water wrong, as 5 to 4 shō for every 3 shō of koji. The quoted original gives 3 shō of water to each shō of beans (5 to 4 shō for 1 to 8 shō of beans), so the record takes the proportions from the quoted text, never the paraphrase. The 1844 manual is an agronomist's advice on brewing at home, not a record of what households did. The passage quoted gives one summer's batch for a household of five and does not itself call it a year's supply.

Used for: the beans a household of five set aside for a year's soy sauce in 1844, and the Edo soy sauce recipes' proportions (Money and prices)
