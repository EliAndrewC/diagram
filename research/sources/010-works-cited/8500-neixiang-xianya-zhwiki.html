内乡县衙 (the Neixiang County yamen), Chinese Wikipedia (https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%86%85%E4%B9%A1%E5%8E%BF%E8%A1%99)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the county yamen at Neixiang in Henan, the best-preserved Qing-dynasty county government office in China, of which only the central axis still stands (the side courts are mostly gone), describing its layout and the regulation and ritual conception that layout follows.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the fallback orientation of our magistrate's manor as a rule rather than a preference - the seat sits to the north and faces south, in a layout that follows the regulation for a local government office and expresses a ritual-propriety conception - and the terms behind it mean proper rather than lucky, so the orientation is the formal one and not an auspicious one. Its limits are that it describes one surviving building of one dynasty, with halls rebuilt in 1894 and only the central axis standing, so the flanks the paired rules speak of can no longer be seen. It asserts the regulation without quoting it: its orientation sentence carries no footnote, the section's one reference is a 2010 article in a science-education magazine, and Wikipedia flags this article for citations that may be misapplied or misrepresented. It gives the five bays of each hall as this building's measure, not as the regulation.
