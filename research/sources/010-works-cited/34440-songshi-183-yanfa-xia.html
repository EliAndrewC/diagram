宋史 (Songshi, History of the Song), 卷183, 食貨志下五 (Treatise on Food and Money, second part, 5), the last section on salt (鹽下), compiled under the Yuan and presented in 1345 (Wikisource, in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/宋史/卷183)

What it is: The official history's account of the Song salt monopoly in Fujian, Guangnan, Hedong and Sichuan, where salt came from wells, from the coast and, in Hedong, from saline earth, with the memorials on official and private salt.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets the price of the state's salt beside that of merchants' and private salt in Sichuan in 1086, says that in Fujian by 1181 the official price was high and the private cheap, and gives the state's buying and selling prices in Hedong, its selling prices in Sichuan, and the rate (20 to 30 cash a jin) at which Sichuan's salt dues were reckoned for payment in silver and silk. Its private prices are those an official cited against a colleague, not a market series, and each figure is of one district at one date. Sichuan's prices are in its iron cash, not the copper cash of the other salt chapters, so they compare with one another but not with a copper price or wage; the Hedong figures are undated and do not say which coin.

Used for: official against private salt under the Song (Money and prices)
