Kito Hiroshi (鬼頭宏), "日本における甘味社会の成立―前近代の砂糖供給―" ("The making of a sweet-eating society in Japan: the supply of sugar before modern times", title translated), 上智経済論集 (Sophia Economic Review) 53, 2008 (in Japanese; https://fe.sophia.ac.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/53-03.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's survey of how much sugar Japan had before the 1870s: the imports through Nagasaki, the sugar districts of the Edo period and what each sent to Osaka, and the first national production returns of 1874.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives how much of Satsuma's sugar reached Osaka a year in the 1830s and what ten years' sales came to, the two totals from which an average Osaka price can be reckoned. It gives no price itself, and its yearly quantity is a round figure for the whole Tenpō era, not a count of each year. It gives two such figures and does not reconcile them: 12 million kin a year in the passage, and 13.2 million for 1830-32 in its Table 1 (from Uemura 1998 and Oka 1983), both called 51% of the national total. It does not say which of the two, or which market, the 2.35 million ryō of sales was earned on, and it cites no source of its own for that sum. Most of Satsuma's sugar was black sugar, so a price reckoned from these totals is a price for brown sugar.

Used for: Satsuma's sugar reaching Osaka in the 1830s and its sales (Money and prices)
