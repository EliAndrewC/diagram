What it is: A season-by-season digest of the English East India Company's China records for 1742 to 1774, quoting the diaries and letters of the Company's agents at Canton and, in 1755 to 1757, at Ningbo, with the prices at which they contracted for tea and raw silk.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated prices for raw silk and for tea at two Chinese ports, from the buyer's own books. The ports overlap in one season only: Ningbo prices survive for 1757 alone, against Canton prices for 1755 and 1757. They are the prices one foreign company agreed with the merchants of each port, and it does not name the district the silk came from; no price in the silk or tea country is given. At Ningbo the Company paid the export duty itself, separately from the price (the 1756 contracts were for delivery on board with "the export duties Excepted"), so a Ningbo price is before duty. Morse summarizes and excerpts: the Ningbo diaries of 1755 and 1756 are lost, and the Ningbo figures of 1757 are his summary of the season, with no month or grade; the Canton figures of 1757 come from a quoted letter, which gives the contract's month and the silk's grade.

Used for: raw silk and Bohea tea prices at Canton and Ningbo, 1755 to 1757 (Money and prices)
