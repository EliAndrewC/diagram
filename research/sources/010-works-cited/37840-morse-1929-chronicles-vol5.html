Hosea Ballou Morse, The Chronicles of the East India Company Trading to China, 1635-1834, vol. 5, Supplementary, 1742-74 (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1929), archive.org copy (https://archive.org/download/morse-1929-the-chronicles-of-the-east-india-company-volume-5/Morse%201929%20The_Chronicles_of_the_East_India_Company%20Volume%205_djvu.txt)

What it is: A season-by-season digest of the English East India Company's China records for 1742 to 1774, by a former commissioner of the Chinese customs, quoting the diaries and letters of the Company's agents at Canton and, in 1755 to 1757, at Ningbo, with the prices at which they contracted for tea and raw silk.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives dated prices for Jiangnan raw silk and for tea at two Chinese ports in the same years, from the buyer's own books. They are the prices one foreign company agreed with the licensed merchants of each port, for export grades, and include whatever those merchants paid to bring the goods down; no price in the silk or tea country is given. Morse summarizes and excerpts: the Ningbo diaries of 1755 and 1756 are lost, and the 1757 figures are his summary of the season, with no month or grade.

Used for: raw silk and Bohea tea prices at Canton and Ningbo, 1755 to 1757 (Money and prices)
