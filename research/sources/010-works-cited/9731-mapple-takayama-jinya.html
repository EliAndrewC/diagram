mapple-takayama-jinya

Mapple Web, 高山陣屋の見どころナビ (A guide to the sights of the Takayama Jin'ya), Mapple Magazine editorial staff, updated 2024-04-19 (in Japanese; https://www.mapple.net/article/48327/)

What it is: A travel guide's article on the Takayama Jin'ya by the editors of the Mapple guidebooks, walking through the office, the residence where the district intendant lived, its living room, kitchen and doma, and the storehouse.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says in so many words that the district intendant lived with the family in the residence inside the compound, which is the arrangement our postings follow. Its limits are that it walks through the buildings as they stand today, which it says are basically Edo-period buildings with parts repaired, rebuilt and restored, without saying which, so a room it shows may be a restoration; who lived there is a matter of what it says of the Edo office, not of the buildings. Takayama was also not always an ordinary daikan's office: the page says the officials sent there from Edo were daikan or gundai, and calls it the one surviving gundai's or daikan's office. The sentence that has the family living in the residence names the gundai (郡代), the higher rank, and calls the house the 郡代役宅; for the daikan it says only that he lived there, so that an ordinary daikan's family lived in the compound is our reading, not its words.

Used for: the district intendant living with the family in the residence inside the compound; the goyoba beside the goyakusho and the writing room, as rooms of the office (Estates and other compounds)
