tiangong-kaiwu-xiafu

Song Yingxing, 《天工開物》 (Tiangong kaiwu, 1637), the section 「夏服」 ("Summer clothing", title translated) of the chapter "乃服", on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E5%B7%A5%E9%96%8B%E7%89%A9/%E5%A4%8F%E6%9C%8D)

What it is: The section on ramie and other bast fibers in the late Ming encyclopedia of crafts: growing ramie, splitting and bleaching the fiber, spinning it, and the looms used.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one rate read for preparing ramie, the fiber split in a full day, and says a skilled spinner on a treadle wheel did the work of three. The rate is one sentence, a loose "three or five zhu" by weight of fiber, not by bolt, for the splitting alone; it names no fineness of thread and is not a count, and the work of three is an unmeasured comparison. The section's uses of ramie (summer clothes, curtains, sewing thread, shoe cord, and the coarsest cloth for lacquerers' work and palace torches) are a list as of 1637, with cotton already woven: it gives no quantities and no share against cotton, and says nothing of how those uses changed. The page is in Wikisource's 25% text-quality category and names no edition; it prints 一女土, as Project Gutenberg's text does, and we read 一女工, "one woman worker", by the sense, without a printed edition to check it or the figure against.

Used for: the ramie fiber split in a day and treadle spinning; ramie's lasting uses after cotton (Money and prices)
