iwata-2003-shohin-ryutsu-chakune

Iwata Kotaro (岩田浩太郎), "商品流通と「着値」" ("The circulation of goods and the landed cost", title translated), 国立歴史民俗博物館研究報告 (Bulletin of the National Museum of Japanese History) 103, March 2003, in Yamagata University's repository (in Japanese; https://yamagata.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2984/files/iwt_11.pdf)

What it is: A study of how the safflower shippers of the Yamagata basin reckoned profit and loss on goods sent to Kyoto: they entered for each load a landed cost, and set the sale against it. Its sixth table lists the Horigome house's lots of 1822, each with its landed cost a load, the price on the wholesaler's invoice and the profit or loss.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the landed cost in the shippers' own terms and shows a whole year's lots of one house against their sales, losses included. The invoice prices carry the wholesaler's 1% commission, which the table's notes take off; the table's figures are the author's reckoning from two account books, in decimal ryo. One house, one year, a dyestuff. That year's lots went to nine wholesalers, seven in Kyoto and two in Osaka, and made about 7% in all by the author's reckoning; the same house lost about 24% the next year, in the slump after the ban on showy silks, so 1822's margin is one good year's, not a typical one.

Used for: what the landed cost of safflower included, and the Horigome house's lots of 1822 against their sale in Kyoto and Osaka (Money and prices)
