Wikipedia, "Arshin (length)" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arshin_(length))

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the arshin, the old Russian unit of length, and its Turkish counterpart.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the length a Russian visitor's arshin stood for in the 1820s, 28 inches (71.12 cm), as Peter the Great set it, so that cloth he measured in arshins can be put in meters. It dates the standard only to "the 18th century", says the arshin "had different length at different times" (27 inches in the 16th century), and never says the 28 inches still held in the 1820s; that is the record's inference. The 28-inch sentence carries no footnote of its own; the article's general sources are the Brockhaus and Efron dictionary (1906) and the Great Soviet Encyclopedia.

Used for: the length of the Russian arshin (Money and prices)
