kumon-2022-gpih-japanese-wages

Yuzuru Kumon, "Japanese wages, prices, and living standards 1600-1938", a spreadsheet for the Global Price and Income History Group, the data of his "The Labor-Intensive Path: Wages, Incomes, and the Work Year in Japan, 1610-1890", Journal of Economic History 82.2 (2022) (https://gpih.ucdavis.edu/files/Japanese%20Wages.xlsx)

What it is: An economic historian's yearly series, 1600-1938, of a servant's cash wage and the market prices of the goods of a living-standard basket in Japan, in silver monme to 1867 and yen after, each good's sources named and every estimated figure marked with a fill.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Osaka's rice in nearly every year and recorded prices of soybeans, barley and cloth from the 1720s, lamp oil from the 1740s and edible oil from the 1800s, taken mostly from the Mitsui Bunko's printed price tables, which were not read here; it names Osaka for its rice and edible oil only, though its paper says all its prices are Osaka's, the rice trade's central market, with missing years of rice filled from Hiroshima's, so they are a great city's prices, not a village's or a castle town's. Many of its cells are estimates from the price of rice, at ratios to rice that its paper takes from the Meiji period (after 1868), so they carry Meiji relative prices back into Edo years; every one for fish, other beans and buckwheat before 1879 is such an estimate; its cloth is headed "linen", its tan defined as enough cloth for one garment, without saying what fiber; and its wage is a servant's year, not a town laborer's day.

Used for: the recorded prices of rice, soybeans, barley, oils and cloth by era (Money and prices)
