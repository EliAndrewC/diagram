Huang Guoxin, "Market orientation and administrative rationality: the principles and mechanism of the Qing salt administration" (in Chinese), Shenzhen daxue xuebao 2021 no. 1, reprinted on the site of the Center for Historical Anthropology, Sun Yat-sen University (https://ha.sysu.edu.cn/article/90)

What it is: A historian of the Qing salt monopoly's study of how the state allotted salt licenses among its salt districts, setting the official allotments against the official population counts of 1776-1850 and against an estimate of how much salt a person ate a year.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one figure found for a person's yearly salt in premodern East Asia. That figure is the author's estimate from three official sources (a Jiaqing-era salt gazetteer of Guangdong and Guangxi and the Yuan and Ming dynastic histories), the yardstick he sets the state's allotments against, not a measured consumption, and it is China's, not Japan's.

Used for: how much salt a person used in a year (Money and prices)
