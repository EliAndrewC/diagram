broadberry-gupta-2006-great-divergence

Stephen Broadberry and Bishnupriya Gupta, "The early modern great divergence: wages, prices and economic development in Europe and Asia, 1500-1800", Economic History Review 59, 1 (2006), pp. 2-31, a copy on Stanford's Center on Poverty and Inequality site (https://cpi.stanford.edu/_media/pdf/Reference%20Media/Broadbery%20and%20Gupta_2006_History%20of%20Inequality.pdf)

What it is: Two economic historians' comparison of the daily wages of unskilled and skilled building workers in Europe, India and China from 1500 to 1800, counted both in silver and in the grain they would buy.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how wages counted in grain alone can mislead and what a full basket corrects, and reports how Allen averaged his European wages. Its account of Allen's work is a summary of another study. The basket's correction it shows (its Table 3, from Allen's prices) is for European cities only, where wage earners bought bread. For China it gives only silver and grain wages, for the Yangzi delta alone in two half-centuries (1550-1649 and 1750-1849), with rice counted as wheat by a flat caloric factor of 1.5. So it never shows what a basket corrects in a rice economy.

Used for: grain as a yardstick for wages and its limits (Money and prices)
