Ann Waltner, review of Francesca Bray, Technology and Gender: Fabrics of Power in Late Imperial China (University of California Press, 1997), H-Asia, April 2000, on the USC US-China Institute's site (https://china.usc.edu/node/21628)

What it is: A historian of China's review of Bray's book on houses, women's work and reproduction in China from the Song to the early nineteenth century, summarizing its argument with page references.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports Bray's argument that women's cloth was tied to the state through taxes in kind until the late Ming, and that their economic activity was masked once it no longer was. It is Bray at second hand, in a reviewer's summary; the reviewer notes the book paints with broad strokes and could attend more closely to chronology and to state policy.

Used for: how women's cloth work in China came to be masked in the record (Money and prices)
