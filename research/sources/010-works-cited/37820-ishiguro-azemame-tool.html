ishiguro-azemame-tool

石黒の昔の暮らし ("Old life in Ishiguro", title translated), 畔(あぜ)豆つけ用具 ("The tool for setting bund beans", title translated) (https://simoyokote.sakura.ne.jp/mingu/azemametukeyougu/azemametukeyougu.html)

What it is: A page of a local website recording the old tools and farm work of Ishiguro, a village of terraced paddies in the snowy hills of Takayanagi, Kashiwazaki, Niigata, written by someone who grew up there: the dibber used to open holes in the paddy bund for azemame (畦豆, soybeans sown on the bund), how the beans were sown and weeded until the 1950s, and that some households took several bales from the bunds alone.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that gives how bund beans stood: one row down the middle of the bund, holes about 30 cm apart, two or three beans to a hole, grown by every household. Its limits: one author's memory of one terrace village in the mid-20th century, where small terraces make the bunds a large share of the land; it gives no length of row and no share of bunds sown, and the hole-making tool it shows is not dated.

Used for: azemame sown in one row down the middle of the bund, about 30 cm apart, two or three beans to a hole; every household growing them on its paddy bunds
