錢泳 (Qian Yong), 履園叢話 ("Collected talk from the Lü garden", title translated), chapter 1, 田價, 米價 and 銀價 ("Land prices", "Rice prices", "The price of silver"), Wikisource text (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/履園叢話/01)

What it is: A Jiangnan scholar's miscellany of the early 1800s, whose short entry on land prices runs from the middle of the Ming to the author's own day, fifty years after his childhood, in taels a mu.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a single observer's series of what land fetched over about three centuries; the entry names no place, and the region is the author's own, the Lower Yangzi, which the next entry, on rice prices, names. It gives prices only, with no rent or yield beside them, in round ranges, and for its earlier reigns it reports what was said rather than what the author saw. Its prices are taels of silver as they stood, not adjusted for the rise in prices over the 1700s that the next two entries, on rice and on silver, record, so part of the climb from seven or eight taels to fifty is a rise in prices generally, not in the worth of land; and its high and low figures, the mid-Ming fifty to a hundred taels and the one or two taels at the fall of the Ming, are a boom and a crisis rather than ordinary years. His rice prices, for the four prefectures of Suzhou, Songjiang, Changzhou and Zhenjiang, and his rates of cash to the tael are recollections in round ranges, dated by reign or by "my youth", not by year, and give no other good.

Used for: how far a Jiangnan land price moved between the late Ming and the early 1800s; the Lower Yangzi's rice in cash a sheng and its rates of cash to the tael in the 1700s (Money and prices)
