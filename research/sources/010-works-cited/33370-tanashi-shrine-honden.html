tanashi-shrine-honden

田無神社, 「本殿について」 ("About the sanctuary", title translated), the Tanashi shrine's page on its cultural properties (in Japanese; https://www.tanashijinja.or.jp/bunkazai/honden)

What it is: The Tanashi shrine's own page on its sanctuary of 1858, in today's Nishitokyo, in western Tokyo: its measurements from a survey, its form and timber, its carving by the Edo carver Shimamura Shunpyo, and how its building was paid for.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one contract price found for the carving of a small sanctuary, 135 ryo for 147 pieces, and the sanctuary's size. It names the contract and the carving estimate book of 1858 for the count but quotes neither for the price; it calls the price exceptionally low and guesses why, yet the carver headed one of Edo's three leading carving houses, official carvers to the shogunate, and carved nearly every member of the building, so the figure is a famous master's discounted fee for unusually rich work, not what an ordinary village shrine's carving cost, and the page does not say whether it covered the timber or only the work; it gives no cost for the building itself, which the village headman paid alone; and its figure of about 2,000 ryo for the Kawagoe Hikawa sanctuary names no source.

Used for: what a small sanctuary's carving cost (Money and prices)
