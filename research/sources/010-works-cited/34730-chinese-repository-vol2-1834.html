"Journal of Messrs. Lindsay and Gutzlaff", a review of the voyage of the Lord Amherst in 1832, The Chinese Repository, vol. 2 (Canton, May 1833-April 1834), April 1834, p. 534, archive.org copy (https://archive.org/details/chinesereposito01willgoog)

What it is: The second volume of the missionaries' Canton monthly; the passage used is its word-for-word quotation of the East India Company agent Hugh Hamilton Lindsay's commercial remarks on the voyage's leg along the Guangdong coast (the paragraph opens "while in Canton province"), February-March 1832, which ended at Nan'ao.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one rate read for cash to the dollar in Guangdong outside Canton before 1837. It is a remark in passing about the whole Guangdong coast, naming no port: the poorer classes among the ship's visitors, bartering provisions, preferred four yards of calico to 1000 cash, and "equivalent to a dollar" is Lindsay's own round conversion, not a rate quoted in a market. The party sold no calico on that coast, and Lindsay himself warns that, "as we sold none", the retail price of ten dollars a piece he works out from the barter is no fair conclusion.
