morse-1908-trade-administration

Hosea Ballou Morse, The Trade and Administration of the Chinese Empire (London: Longmans, Green, 1908), Internet Archive text (https://archive.org/stream/tradeadministra00mors/tradeadministra00mors_djvu.txt)

What it is: A survey of the late Qing state's revenue, currency and trade by a commissioner of the Chinese Maritime Customs, written from his service and from the reports of others such as the British consul George Jamieson.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes from inside the system how taxes, treasury taels, banks and cash were reckoned about 1905, and outlines, fifty years after the fact and citing no source, the paper money of 1853-1861 (it wrongly says no government notes were issued between about 1403 and 1853, when the Qing issued them in 1651-1661). Its practice is that of the 1900s, after the treaty ports and decades of foreign trade, so it is not evidence for earlier centuries; the machine-read text garbles some decimals, which are checked against the page images.

Used for: the treasury tael, the banks' two weights, short strings, county tax conversions and the 1850s notes (Money and prices)
