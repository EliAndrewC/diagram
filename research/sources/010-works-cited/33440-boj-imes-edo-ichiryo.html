Bank of Japan Institute for Monetary and Economic Studies, Currency Museum, 「江戸時代の１両は今のいくら？」 ("What is one ryo of the Edo period worth today?", title translated), a four-page teaching sheet, September 2017 (in Japanese; https://www.imes.boj.or.jp/cm/history/historyfaq/mod/1ryou.pdf)

What it is: A handout of the Bank of Japan's currency museum explaining why a ryo of the Edo period has no single modern value, with a table of everyday prices in copper mon and a few wages, given as examples of the first half of the 1800s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the price of a persimmon, with other village goods, for the villages of the Kanto in the first half of the 1800s. It says the items come from several different documents, because it is hard to find the count or quantity and the price together in one document, and it names none of those documents (its last page gives a reading list of works on Edo prices, such as the Mitsui Bunko price series and Ono Takeo's 江戸物価事典, but ties no item to any of them), so no single item can be traced to its year, place or record. Its table gives only 柿 1個 6文: it does not say whether the persimmon was fresh or dried, or in what season, and the sheet itself warns that farm prices rose and fell with the harvest and the season.

Used for: the price of a fresh persimmon in the Kanto countryside (Money and prices)
