makihara-2020-kinseishi-kogi

Makihara Shigeyuki, introduction to Takano Toshihiko (ed.), 『近世史講義：女性の力を問いなおす』 ("Lectures on early modern history: rethinking the power of women", title translated), Chikuma Shinsho, 2020, on the University of Tokyo's UTokyo BiblioPlaza (in Japanese; https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/biblioplaza/ja/F_00081.html)

What it is: A University of Tokyo historian of early modern Japan, one of the book's authors, introducing a 2020 collection of lectures on Edo-period history written mostly by women historians, with the book's table of contents; read here only in that introduction, not in the book.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states directly, for Edo-period Japan, why women appear so little in the surviving documents: writing served rule, and the samurai and village officials who wrote were men. It is a short essay by one author introducing a book, so it gives the argument without its evidence, and its chapter titles say only what the book covers.

Used for: why Edo-period records show few women, and the records historians read for them (Money and prices)
