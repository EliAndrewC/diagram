nakabayashi-2020-kyoho-land-law

中林真幸 (Nakabayashi Masaki), 「享保元文農地法の成立―市場拡大と社会的安定の平衡―」 ("The making of the farmland law of the Kyōhō and Genbun eras: balancing market growth and social stability", title translated), 経済研究 (The Economic Review) 71, no. 2, April 2020 (in Japanese; https://hit-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2048820/files/keizaikenkyu07102144.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's study of the shogunate's law on pledged farmland as it was settled between the 1720s and the 1740s, read from the edicts and the court precedents, and of how it set who paid the land tax on a pledged field.

Why it applies, and its limits: It defines the two arrangements that decide what a lender on land took home: whether the holder of a pledge paid the field's tax and levies, or made the farmer pay them. It is about the law the shogunate's courts enforced on its own and its bannermen's lands and in the fudai domains that followed it. It says what those courts would enforce, not how most pledges ran: its own cases show the arrangement that made the farmer pay continuing under other names (助合, 越石), checked only when a suit reached a court. Its one rate of return, about 5 to 10 percent a year, is the author's estimate for a single 1816 settlement in the fudai Oyumi domain, not shogunal land, assuming rice at 1 to 2 ryō a koku. The sums lent against fields in its cases (50 ryō on about 8.6 tan of dry field in Musashi, 1840) are single loans, not a price of land.

Used for: who paid the tax on a pledged field, and the pledge leased back to its debtor as tenant (Money and prices)
