kitakyushu-yasaka-torii

Kitakyushu City, 【県指定】八坂神社石鳥居 1基 ("Prefecture-designated: the stone torii of Yasaka Shrine, one", title translated), the city's cultural-property page (in Japanese; https://www.city.kitakyushu.lg.jp/contents/02100290.html)

What it is: Kitakyushu City's page on a granite torii close to the Myojin type at Yasaka Shrine in Kokura, designated by Fukuoka prefecture in 1963, giving its height, the length of its top beam, the span between its posts and their thickness.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one of the few pages that measures both a stone torii's top beam and its post span, so the two can be set against each other. The page gives no date of building (Kokura Kita ward's page says only that the torii is said to be of the Muromachi period, a tradition rather than a dated record), and the shrine's address is Jonai, "within the castle", in Kokura, not a village; the arch was moved within the grounds, from north of the worship hall, in 1992. It is one torii, of an unusual form whose top beam, second beam and roof-like cap are cut from one stone, and its posts lean outward, so the 3.61 m between them holds at a height the page does not state.

Used for: a stone arch's beam length and post span (Religion and the dead)
