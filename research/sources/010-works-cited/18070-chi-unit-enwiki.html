en.wikipedia, Chi (unit) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chi_(unit))

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the chi, the Chinese foot, with a section on its historical values as estimated from surviving ancient rulers and other objects.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the length of the Chinese foot from the Qin to the Three Kingdoms, and says the Warring States foot was essentially the same, so a width given in chi in a classical text can be put in inches. It does not say which foot any given text used: before the Qin standardized measures in 221 BC each state kept its own, and the Kaogongji has been argued to use a Qi foot (Wen Renjun, Kaogu 1983), so its widths in inches assume that foot matched this one. Its Qin-to-Three-Kingdoms range rests on one companion to a Han mathematical classic (Shen, Crossley and Lun, 1999) and its Warring States sentence on a translation of Sima Qian, neither read here; it calls the figure what researchers surmise; and the range is how the foot varied over five centuries, not a single value.

Used for: the foot of the Warring States and the Han at about 23 to 24 cm, to put the classical field-channel widths in inches (Fields)
