handa-museum-otsukawa-1842-prices

Handa City Museum (柴田邦彦, Shibata Kunihiko), 「古文書に見る江戸時代の食材と食生活」 ("Edo-period foods and meals seen in old documents", title translated), the slides of a talk, January 25, 2020 (in Japanese; https://www.city.handa.lg.jp/_res/projects/default_project/_page_/001/002/711/r1komonnjo.pdf)

What it is: A city museum curator's slides on the food in the old documents of Handa, in the Chita district, its document slides setting a photograph of the handwritten page beside the museum's printed transcription; the first document is a list of the prices of goods that the village of Otsukawa submitted in 1842.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the price at which a village dealer bought salt by the ryo and sold it by the sho, in one document of a known village and year. Otsukawa was a village on the Chita shore, whose fish dealers supplied the Owari lord, so its price is a shore village's and not that of an inland place. The list answered an order to lower prices, which the museum takes to be part of the Tenpo reforms, so its prices are those a village reported to the authorities; the museum shows only its food prices, with no wage among them. The document gives no rate between gold and copper: the 6,000 mon to the ryo with which the museum's slides convert prices is the official rate, which the slides say the market rate departed from, and not a rate the village gave.

Used for: the price of salt in a village of the Chita district in 1842 (Money and prices)
