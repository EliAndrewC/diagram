anada-edo-uji-chashi

Anada Sayoko (穴田小夜子), "江戸時代の宇治茶師" ("The tea masters of Uji in the Edo period", title translated), an article in Gakushuin Shigaku (学習院史学) no. 8, pp. 47-70, in the Gakushuin University repository (in Japanese; https://glim-re.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1510/files/shigaku_8_47_70.pdf)

What it is: A historian's article on the tea masters of Uji, the houses that grew and prepared tea for the shogun, the court and the lords. It covers their standing, their yearly duty of filling the shogun's tea jars, and their finances, and in a note gives the prices the shogunate paid them for each grade of its tea, before and after a raise of 1642.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a price at Uji, by grade, that stood from 1642 to the end of the shogunate. That price is what the shogunate allowed its own tea masters for the powdered-tea leaf it took for itself: an administered payment for the finest tea, not a market price. In another note it counts 200 monme of the powdered-tea leaf to the kin, and one of its earlier prices is printed at a tenth of what the raise implies; it gives no price in Edo or Osaka.

Used for: what the shogunate paid Uji's tea masters for its tea (Money and prices)
