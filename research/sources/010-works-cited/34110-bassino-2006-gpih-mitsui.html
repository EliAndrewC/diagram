Jean-Pascal Bassino, "Japan 1710-1871 (Mitsui Bunko)", a spreadsheet for the Global Price and Income History Group, last revised 1 August 2006, an appendix to Bassino and Ma's "Japanese Unskilled Wages in International Perspective, 1741-1913" (2005), transcribing Mitsui Bunko, Kinsei kōki ni okeru shuyō bukka no dōtai (1989), Tables 6-8 (https://gpih.ucdavis.edu/files/Japan_1710-1871.xls)

What it is: A transcription, sheet by sheet, of the retail prices and the unskilled day wages that the branches of the Mitsui merchant house entered in their account books in Kyoto (1713, 1741-1762, 1791-1871), Edo (1710-1711, 1713, 1716, 1818-1871) and Osaka (prices only, 1713, 1818-1871), each good in its own unit and every price in silver monme, a spring and an autumn figure a year, with the transcriber's notes on units and conversions on a sheet of its own.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one yearly series found that prices salt, firewood, charcoal and a day's labor in the same city, the same coin and the same books. It is a transcription of a printed table that was not itself read here, so a unit or a figure is as the transcriber gives it; Kyoto has no charcoal column and Osaka no wage, there is no Kyoto row from 1763 to 1790, and Edo's prices and wages are Mitsui's account-book monme in a city that paid in gold and copper. They are prices headed retail and laborers' wages in the three great cities, from one great merchant house's account books, which may not be what a laborer paid in a shop, so they speak for a metropolis and not for a village or a provincial town. The monme is a unit of account whose silver content fell as the coinage was reissued: the file's own conversions sheet assumes 3.75 g of pure silver and lists lower figures for the debased coins. So a wage set decade beside decade is a nominal figure, never a weight of silver or a real wage.

Used for: Kyoto's salt, firewood, miso, soy sauce and day wage of 1745-1754, Edo's and Osaka's charcoal and firewood, and the Kyoto and Edo day wages by decade; each good's price from period to period, and how far the three cities stood apart (Money and prices)
