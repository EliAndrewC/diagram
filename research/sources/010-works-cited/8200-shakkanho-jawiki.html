shakkanho-jawiki

ja.wikipedia 尺貫法 ("the traditional shaku-kan system of weights and measures", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/尺貫法)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the shakkanho, the traditional Japanese system of weights and measures, a tertiary entry that tabulates each unit against the metric system - among them the tsubo at 400/121 square meters, the se at thirty tsubo, and the tan at ten se, and the kin at 160 monme, or 600 grams.

Why it applies, and its limits: Every area a Japanese source states in tsubo, se or tan has to be turned into square meters before it can be drawn, and this is the conversion we use; the figures are exact definitions rather than measurements, so there is little for a source to be wrong about. Its limits are that the article's own values are the legally fixed modern definitions of units whose local sizes varied before they were standardized, so a premodern document's tsubo may not be exactly this tsubo; and - the one that can put a figure badly wrong rather than slightly - that the tan was redefined in the course of Hideyoshi's cadastral survey, from 360 bu to the 300 bu this page's tan assumes, so an area a pre-1590s document states in tan and we convert with this table comes out a sixth or more too small (more where the old bu was laid out with a longer rod). Nor is the tsubo itself fixed: a tsubo or bu is one ken square, and the page itself says the ken was set at six shaku only by the Meiji weights-and-measures law, having been "about six shaku" before; Hideyoshi's survey rod was six shaku three sun, which makes its bu about a tenth larger than this table's. So an area in tsubo or bu converts closely for a late-Edo or later document and only approximately for an earlier one. The kin's 600 grams is likewise the modern legal value (the 1891 law fixed the kan at 3.75 kilograms); the page says the Edo-period kan was slightly lighter, and its own table shows the Chinese jin far from constant - about 227 grams under the Han, 668 under the Sui, 597 under the Tang - so the figure serves a Japanese weight closely and a Chinese one only once its dynasty is known.

Used for: the shakkanho land measures the record converts from: the tsubo, the se and the tan in square meters; the kin in grams (Money and prices)
