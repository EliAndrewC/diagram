What it is: Japanese Wikipedia's article on 石高 (kokudaka), the rice-volume measure used to rate land and to pay retainers, including what one koku was taken to represent and what a retainer was actually issued.

Why it applies, and its limits: We use its rule of thumb - one koku as an adult's rice for a year - to turn a settlement's assessed yield into a number of mouths it could feed, and the article supplies its own hedge, that a standard stipend ran nearer 1.8 koku of brown rice, which cuts against the round figure: an adult man's ration was nearly twice a koku, so one koku a head can hold only as an average over men, women and children, and adult men alone come to about 0.55 per koku. Neither sentence carries a footnote in the article. The yield being converted is the assessed figure (表高), which the article shows falling behind the land's real yield (内高) through the Edo period, so a count of mouths from it is a count from the assessment, not from the harvest. The koku equivalence is in any case a convention of assessment rather than a measured diet.

Used for: one koku as an adult's annual rice (「One koku corresponds to the quantity of rice that one adult eats in one year.」 (translated; original: 「一石は大人一人が一年に食べる米の量に相当する」)), with the page's own hedge that real stipends ran ~1.8 koku of brown rice; the 5 gō of brown rice a day, about 1.8 koku a year, issued to an adult man (Money and prices)
