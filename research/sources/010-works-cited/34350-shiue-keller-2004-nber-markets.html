Carol H. Shiue and Wolfgang Keller, "Markets in China and Europe on the Eve of the Industrial Revolution", National Bureau of Economic Research Working Paper 10778, September 2004 (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w10778/w10778.pdf)

What it is: Two economic historians' comparison of grain markets in 18th-century China and Europe, on the rice prices of 121 Chinese prefectures from the Qing grain price reports and wheat prices of 15 European markets, mostly for 1742-1795.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures how much Chinese rice prices swung from one half year to the next and how far they spread around their mean, beside Europe's. It is a working paper. Its Chinese sample is the ten main rice provinces of central and south China, not the wheat and millet north, and each price is a prefecture's, the midpoint of its highest and lowest county price in the second or the eighth lunar month, so it says nothing of movement between those two months or within one market town. Its half-year figure is the swing left after the usual difference between the two months is taken out, and its spread around the mean is of prices not deflated over 1742-1785, so it includes the century's slow rise.

Used for: how far Qing rice prices moved over a half year, and how closely prices moved together at each distance (Money and prices)
