喜田川季荘 (Kitagawa Morisada), 『類聚近世風俗志 : 原名守貞漫稿』 (Customs of recent times, by topic: the Morisada manko, title translated), 下 (volume 2), 國學院大學出版部, 1908, in the National Diet Library (in Japanese; https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991467/1/229; the sake handbill at https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991467/1/228; the Osaka cart fee at https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/991467/1/251)

What it is: The second volume of the 1908 printed edition, arranged by topic, of the notebook an Osaka-born townsman, born in 1810, kept on the customs of Edo, Kyoto and Osaka from 1837 to 1853, with additions until about 1867. This volume holds his chapters on food and drink, among them what the best sake cost a sho in Osaka and Edo from his childhood on and an undated sake shop's handbill, and his chapter on carts (book 29), with Osaka's beka cart and the fee its owners paid.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its sake prices are one observer's memory and report, not a ledger's: his childhood figures are dated only by his own birth year, his later ones only as "from about Tenpo", and the handbill he reproduces he could not date. A printed transcription of a manuscript, it may differ from his hand in places, and the handbill is reproduced in cursive. Its chapter on carts describes them and gives the fee Osaka came to charge a beka cart's owner each spring and autumn, not what hiring one cost. That fee is a range, from about 2 shu to over 1 ryo a cart each season, graded by how much the cart was used, and he dates it only as coming after the Bunsei-era (1818-1830) licensing he describes, not to a year.

Used for: what sake cost a sho in Osaka and Edo from about 1815 to the 1840s, and the fees Osaka's post-horse office came to charge its beka carts, from the Bunsei era on (Money and prices)
