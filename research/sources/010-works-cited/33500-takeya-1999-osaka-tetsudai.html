武谷嘉之 (Takeya Yoshiyuki), 「近世大坂における家作「手伝」職の仲間形成」 ("The formation of the guild of building 'helpers' in early modern Osaka", title translated), Shakai keizai shigaku 65(1), 1999 (in Japanese; J-STAGE, https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/sehs/65/1/65_KJ00004591901/_pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's study of the tetsudai, the helpers who did the unskilled and semi-skilled work on Osaka's building sites, and of how they came to form a licensed guild, from their failed petitions of 1782 and 1785 to its recognition under the temple Shitennoji in 1796 and as a chartered guild from the 1830s, with the few wage figures he found for them and for the city's carpenters.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets a helper's day wage of 1785, from the city magistrate's notice, beside an Osaka carpenter's of the early 1800s and the pair Morisada manko gives, so a craftsman's and a laborer's pay of one city can be compared. The author says no run of helpers' wages survives, so these are fragments: the carpenter's figures are of other years and in silver: the one of 1801 is a builder's estimate for rebuilding Shitennoji, converted by the author to 420 mon, and only Morisada's says it excludes meal money. They are the wages of the realm's great merchant city, so it is the ratio of carpenter to helper, more than the coin amounts, that carries to a castle town or a village. And the helpers were a guild of building workers rather than the city's casual laborers in general.

Used for: the Osaka building helper's day wage against the carpenter's, 1785 to the 1850s (Money and prices)
