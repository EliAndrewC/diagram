"Koku", English Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koku)

What it is: The English Wikipedia article on the koku: its volume in liters and in gallons, the weight of a koku of brown rice and of milled rice, a merchant family's figure for a man's yearly rice, and the Chinese dan it descends from.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the koku in imperial and US gallons side by side, how much milled rice a koku of brown rice yields, and the Tang dan's volume. Its 180-liter koku is the kyō-masu standard of the 1620s, made nationwide in 1669 and fixed in metric in 1891; earlier Japanese measures were smaller and differed from place to place, so the gallons hold for the Edo standard only. Its 59.44-liter dan is the Tang dynasty's, and the unit changed after the Tang, so it does not stand for a later imperial shi. Its brown-rice weight and its 0.91 milled yield rest on English economic histories (Francks 2006; Rose's statistical appendix of 1985), the 0.91 being a factor for converting past figures to modern milled-rice statistics rather than a measurement of Edo milling; its white-rice weight rests on a 2017 Niigata University paper, and its merchant family's 1.8 koku on Ramseyer's 1979 article on Tokugawa merchant house codes.

Used for: the koku in gallons, the milled yield of a koku of brown rice and the Tang dan (Money and prices)
