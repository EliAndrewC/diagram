kawashima-2012-kaji-review

河島一仁 (Kawashima Kazuhito), 「日本における鋳物師・鍛冶に関する研究の進展 ―歴史地理学的「職人集団研究」の可能性―」 ("Progress in research on founders and smiths in Japan", title translated), 『立命館文學』 627 (in Japanese; https://www.ritsumei.ac.jp/acd/cg/lt/rb/627/627PDF/kawashima.pdf)

What it is: A historical geographer's review of the research on Japan's founders and smiths, which summarizes Yamaguchi Morito's industrial-geography studies, reported from the late 1960s to 1970, on how the cutlery districts of Seki, Sakai, Miki and others took shape.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source found that names the trades a Sakai blade passed through in the 1750s, but it is a summary of another scholar's studies, its sentence hedged as hearsay, and it gives no figures. Its dates (Sakai's in the Hōreki era, 1751-1763; Seki's in the Kyōhō era, 1716-1735) are for when each town's cutlery trade took its form as a cluster of smiths and wholesalers, not for when smithing began there, and it names none of the period documents Yamaguchi took them from.

Used for: the trades of Sakai's cutlery about 1757, and when Seki's took shape (Money and prices)
