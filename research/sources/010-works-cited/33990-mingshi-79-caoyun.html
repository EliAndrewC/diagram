mingshi-79-caoyun

Zhang Tingyu (張廷玉) and others, Ming shi (明史, History of the Ming), chapter 79, "Food and money 3: grain transport" (食貨三 漕運), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明史/卷79)

What it is: The chapter of the official Ming history, finished in 1739, on how the Ming carried the tribute grain of the south to Beijing by the Grand Canal, and how the work and its cost were shared between the farmers and the army; its second half is on the state's granaries and treasuries.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the surcharge of 1431 that farmers paid the army, in grain, for carrying each shi of their tax grain to the capital, graded by province by distance, and the lower rates it fell to later. It is the surcharge on tribute grain, set by the state and paid partly in other goods, not a merchant's freight, and the lower rates are undated after 1431. Nor is it the whole cost of the haul: the surcharge (加耗) also covered grain lost on the way, and the soldiers were paid besides in silver (輕齎銀) for the locks and transshipments and were allowed to carry goods of their own, so carrying a shi cost more than the surcharge alone.

Used for: the Ming surcharge on carrying tax grain to the capital, by province (Money and prices)
