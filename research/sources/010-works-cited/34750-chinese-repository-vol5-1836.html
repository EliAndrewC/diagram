Translations of Xu Naiji's memorial on opium (July 1836, p. 140) and of Xu Qiu's memorial against admitting opium (January 1837, p. 398), The Chinese Repository, vol. 5 (Canton, May 1836-April 1837), archive.org copy (https://archive.org/details/chinesereposito09unkngoog)

What it is: The fifth volume of the missionaries' Canton monthly, with its own English translations of two memorials to the Daoguang emperor, one urging that opium be legalized and one opposing it, each describing the rise of silver against copper cash.

Why it applies, and its limits: They give two officials' figures for cash to the tael "of late years" and "now", against 1,000 in earlier times. Both speak of the empire at large, name no market and no year for the figure, and argue a case in which the dearness of silver was the point; the English is the editors' translation, and the Chinese originals were not read.

Used for: cash to the tael across the empire in 1836 (Money and prices)
