明會典 (Collected Statutes of the Ming, title translated), volume 136, Board of Punishments, part 11, the Siku Quanshu edition (in Chinese; Wikisource transcription; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明㑹典_(四庫全書本)/卷136)

What it is: A volume of the Ming government's statutes on the stolen goods and fines its courts recovered, ending with a table of the value at which each kind of stolen thing was reckoned, from gold and silk to rice, livestock, fruit and clothing, in the state's paper money.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its table values persimmons, pears, small apples and dried persimmons in the same paper money as rice and silver, so their worth can be set against each other. The table is a court's standard for judging a theft, though its own rule says goods are valued at the price current where the crime was done; it carries no year, standing after a ruling of 1497, and it gives no wage in the same money.

Used for: the Ming valuation of fruit against rice and silver (Money and prices)
