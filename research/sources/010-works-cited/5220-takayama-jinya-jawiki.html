ja.wikipedia 高山陣屋 ("Takayama Jin'ya, the town's magistrate's office", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/高山陣屋)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 高山陣屋 (Takayama Jinya), on the shogunal intendancy office of Hida province at Takayama, held under direct shogunal rule from 1692 until the Meiji Restoration - the only such office to survive - with one reference, the 1992 survey report of the prefecture's cultural-property center, and a link to the national cultural-property register. It records the 1816 rebuild of the office's parts - 玄関・吟味所・御役所・大広間, the numbered rows of storehouses, the historic-site designation of 1929, and the 1996 restoration from an 1830 drawing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the parts of a provincial office compound as it survived - the office range and its rooms, the numbered storehouses, the gate and gatekeeper's lodge - and dates them, which is what our magistracy plans are checked against. Its text gives no layout or dimensions (the plan is only a captioned photograph of a model), and its list of the office's parts ends in など, so it is not complete. Its limits are that this is one atypically preserved compound in a mountain province under direct shogunal rule, and from 1777 a gundai's office (郡代役所) with branch offices in Mino, Echizen and Kaga, so it ranks above, and was likely larger than, an ordinary intendant's office of the kind our magistracies stand for; and that the buildings it lists describe the post-1996 state, in which the storehouse-keepers' rowhouse, the intendant's residence and the inner rooms are a reconstruction from a drawing rather than standing Edo fabric.

Used for: the 1816 rebuild of the office's parts - 玄関・吟味所・御役所・大広間; the 25 intendants over 177 years are on the city's page, not this one
