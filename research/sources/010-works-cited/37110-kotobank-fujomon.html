kotobank-fujomon

kotobank 不浄門 ("fujomon, the unclean gate", title translated) (https://kotobank.jp/word/%E4%B8%8D%E6%B5%84%E9%96%80-618268)

What it is: The Kotobank page reprinting three dictionary entries on the fujomon, or imimon: Shogakukan's Digital Daijisen, the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten and the encyclopedia Nipponica (by Inagaki Shisei), the first two defining it as a gate or wicket at the rear of a samurai residence, castle or the like for carrying out night soil, the dead and criminals, and Nipponica as a castle gate for sending out criminals and the dead that was also the way out for night soil.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only page the record holds that names a way out for night soil at a samurai house. The entries are modern definitions of an Edo-period thing: they name no particular residence and give no size; the first two say only that it stood at the rear, and Nipponica alone places it more exactly (most often a simple wooden wicket in an inconspicuous spot beside the rear gate), and it says so of castles and a lord's residence, and of the gate for criminals and the dead, which it says was also the way out for night soil, not of a lesser samurai house; and the Nihon Kokugo Daijiten's earliest quoted use is a kabuki play of 1875; the one concrete case, in Nipponica, is a gate of Edo Castle.

Used for: the rear wicket for night soil at a samurai residence (Estates and other compounds)
