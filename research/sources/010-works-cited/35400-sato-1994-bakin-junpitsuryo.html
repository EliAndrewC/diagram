佐藤悟 (Sato Satoru), 「馬琴の潤筆料と板元 ―合巻と読本―」 ("Bakin's writing fees and his publishers: gokan and yomihon", title translated), 『近世文藝』 59 (1994), pp. 21-33 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kinseibungei/59/0/59_21/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: A literary historian's study of what the novelist Kyokutei Bakin was paid by his publishers in the 1820s to 1840s, read from Bakin's diary and his letters and those of his circle, with the wholesale and trade prices of his storybooks and novels and the costs of making their blocks.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source found that gives Edo book prices from period letters (the author's own paper on the format and price of storybooks, 『近世文藝』 56, 1992, is not yet read), and the one cost of a block-ready copy made by hand: Kimura Mokuro's letter of 1846 puts the making of a forty-leaf block copy of a storybook, with Utagawa Kunisada's drawings, at ten-odd ryo for copyist, illustrator and writing fee together, which the author reads as under 15 ryo with Bakin's own fee of 8 ryo inside it, so the copyist's share is not stated and can only be bounded from above. Its prices are wholesale or trade prices between publishers and booksellers, not what a reader paid, but for an 1841 letter's 5 to 6 monme for a set of his older novels, which is what they could be had for in Edo; they are of Bakin's books, the best-selling of the age; and its novel prices run wide - back prints at 2 shu (7.5 monme) a set, new printings at 1 bu (15 monme) and more, and 15 monme for the 1828 Sekikonroku, which Bakin called the dearest of his books and which sold 200 copies at first - so no one figure is the price of a novel; and the scan's text layer interleaves its two columns, so a passage is read across the column breaks.

Used for: the wholesale price of a gokan in the 1830s, the trade prices of a yomihon, and the cost of a fair copy in 1846 (Money and prices)
