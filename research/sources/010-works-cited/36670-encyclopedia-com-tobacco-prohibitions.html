encyclopedia-com-tobacco-prohibitions

"Prohibitions", Tobacco in History and Culture: An Encyclopedia, on Encyclopedia.com (https://encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/prohibitions)

What it is: An article in a reference work on tobacco (ed. Jordan Goodman, Thomson Gale, 2005) on attempts to ban tobacco from the seventeenth century to the present, mostly in Europe and North America, with a sentence or two each on the bans in Japan and China.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the end of Japan's bans and the Chinese edicts in one place. It cites no source for those dates at the point it gives them, its bibliography lists no work on Japan, China or Asia, and it calls European travelers' accounts the only sources for that period, so its dates for Japan and China rest at best on general Western histories of tobacco. It says the Japanese bans on growing and using tobacco were all lifted in 1625, a flat date that a closer source contradicts for growing: Lublin's 2024 article in Studies on Asia (vol. 9, no. 1, pp. 3-4) has the shogunate's 1643 regulations for villagers still objecting to fields put under tobacco, and ordinances late in the seventeenth century allowing it only on specially reclaimed land and smoking only inside homes. So 1625 cannot stand as the end of Japan's restrictions. On the Chinese edicts it differs from Laufer's study: it puts the threat of decapitation on the edict of 1641 where Laufer puts it on that of 1638, and it lists edicts of 1612 and 1776 that Laufer does not give.
