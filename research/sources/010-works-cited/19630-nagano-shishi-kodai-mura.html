nagano-shishi-kodai-mura

Nagano City, Nagano Shishi (the Nagano city history), vol. 2, history, prehistory to the middle ages, part 1 ch. 3 section 4, 「The ancient village as archaeology sees it」 (translated; original: 「考古学からみた古代のムラ」), pp. 427-430, on the ADEAC digital archive (in Japanese; https://adeac.jp/nagano-city/texthtml/d100020/ct00000002/ht001860)

What it is: The Nagano city history's account, from excavation, of how the villages of the Nagano basin moved and changed from the late Kofun period to the 12th century.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the siting of villages on the dry natural levees, with the wet ground behind them farmed as paddy, to the mid-Heian period, and calls that siting a tradition seen from the Yayoi period on. It is one basin's archaeology; the villagers' paddy use of the wet ground is given as what they are thought to have done, though behind one of these villages (the Shinonoi site group) excavation has found the flood-buried, grid-laid paddies of the Ishikawa jori site, from the Yayoi period to the middle ages; in the same period, it says, small villages also spread onto low wet ground and the middle of alluvial fans, land poorly suited to paddy, so it does not show wet ground kept unbuilt everywhere; and it says nothing about wells, graves or shrines.

Used for: Heian villages on the natural levees, the wet ground behind them farmed, not built on (Water)
