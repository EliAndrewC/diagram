chuo-ichikokubashi-maigo-sekihyo

Chūō City, 一石橋迷子しらせ石標 ("the Ichikokubashi lost-child notice stone", title translated), by Masuyama Kazunari, curator of the ward's board of education, posted 24 July 2024 (in Japanese; https://www.city.chuo.lg.jp/a0052/bunkakankou/rekishi/tokyobunkazai/sekihyo.html)

What it is: The Tokyo ward's page on a designated cultural property, the granite pillar set up in 1857 at the southwest end of Ichikokubashi bridge in Edo on which notices of lost children were pasted, with its measurements and the history of its petition from a ward headman's records.

Why it applies, and its limits: A curator's account of one surviving object, drawing on the ward headman's records (Sen'yō eikyū roku) and the petition itself. It shows paper pasted on one structure a magistrate had permitted, in the shogun's capital in 1857; it does not say whether paper could be pasted on an official board, or anywhere unpermitted, or what was done outside Edo.

Used for: pasted paper notices on a structure the magistrate permitted (Trades and services)
