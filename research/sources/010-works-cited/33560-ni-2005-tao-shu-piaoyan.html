倪玉平 (Ni Yuping), 「政府、商人与民众——以陶澍淮北票盐改革为中心」 ("Government, merchants and the people: Tao Shu's ticket-salt reform in Huaibei", title translated), posted October 29, 2005, Institute of Modern History, Chinese Academy of Social Sciences (in Chinese; http://jds.cssn.cn/xscg/ybwz/201605/t20160506_5253985.shtml)

What it is: A historian's study of the reform by which Tao Shu, governing the Huai salt district in the early 1830s, let small traders buy salt at the saltworks on tickets in Huaibei, the district's smaller northern half (about 300,000 of its 1.69 million yin a year), and never in Huainan, drawn from the reform's own compilation of documents and the memorials in the palace archives.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the price of a yin of salt at the saltworks under the new rules and what a yin had cost the merchants by the time it reached their selling districts before them. These are Huaibei's figures; Huainan, the larger part of the district, kept the old system and its costlier river trade to Huguang and Jiangxi. They are also the merchants' costs per yin, not what a household paid for a jin, and the paper does not say that the yin of the old system weighed the same as the new one's 400 jin.

Used for: the cost of a yin of Huai salt at the saltworks and at the selling districts, early 1830s (Money and prices)
