eia-btu-recs-2020

US Energy Information Administration, "British thermal units (Btu)", Units and calculators explained (https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/units-and-calculators/british-thermal-units.php); and the 2020 Residential Energy Consumption Survey (RECS), Tables HC10.1 (https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/hc/xls/HC%2010.1.xlsx), CE3.1 (https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/c&e/xls/ce3.1.xlsx), CE5.3b (https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/c&e/xls/ce5.3b.xlsx), CE5.4 (https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/c&e/xls/ce5.4.xlsx) and CE7.2 (https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/c&e/xls/ce7.2.xlsx), XLSX

What it is: The federal energy agency's table of the heat in a unit of each fuel, a cord of wood among them; and its household survey of 2020, which gives the floor area of US homes by tenure and the energy a household uses for heating and cooking, with wood counted in a table of its own.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a comparative source: how much fuel and floor space a US household has, as a yardstick for a premodern laborer's year, never a figure of the setting. The cord's heat is one round figure for all wood, whatever the species or dryness; the survey's tables are averages per household, not per person, and its heating table leaves wood out.

Used for: the heat in a cord of wood and the fuel a US household uses; floor area per rented home (Comparative money: other societies)
