Justus Doolittle, Social Life of the Chinese, vol. 1, New York: Harper & Brothers, 1865, at the Internet Archive (https://archive.org/download/sociallifeofchin01dool/sociallifeofchin01dool_djvu.txt)

What it is: An American missionary's account of the customs of Fuzhou, written after fourteen years in its mission, with chapters on death, mourning and burial and on the trades and their wages.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a day wage of carpenters and masons and a month's wage of hired workers in Fuzhou, and what one poor widow set aside for her coffin and burial, from his own observation. The figures are one foreign observer's round ranges, in the dollars of the treaty-port trade, with no source named; the widow's case is one anecdote, and her money was for the coffin and other burial costs together.

Used for: what a poor woman set aside for her coffin and burial, and the day wages beside it (Money and prices)
