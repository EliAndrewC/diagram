chouroudaigaku-sumidawara-tsukurikata

"炭俵の作り方" ("How to make a charcoal bale", title translated), a page of the notebook site 長老大学宝の山 (Elders' University treasure trove), unsigned and undated (in Japanese; https://scrapbox.io/chouroudaigaku/%E7%82%AD%E4%BF%B5%E3%81%AE%E4%BD%9C%E3%82%8A%E6%96%B9)

What it is: A nine-line note, in the first person, on weaving charcoal bales at Tosayama in Kochi prefecture: the bale woven of kaya grass on rope twisted from straw, the weights that hold the rope, who taught the speaker, who bought the bales and how long one took.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only text found in which charcoal bales are woven to be sold to a charcoal dealer, and the only one that times a single bale. It names no speaker, no year and no price; the speaker learned from their mother, so the memory is of living practice, the twentieth century at the earliest reading, and it says nothing of the Edo period. Its "less than an hour" is a recollection, not a measured rate, and covers the weaving as described, not cutting the grass or twisting the rope.

Used for: charcoal bales woven for sale to a dealer at Tosayama, in living memory (Money and prices)
