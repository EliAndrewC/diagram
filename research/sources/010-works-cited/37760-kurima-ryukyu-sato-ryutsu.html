Kurima Yasuo (来間泰男), "17～19世紀琉球の砂糖生産とその流通" ("Sugar Production and Circulation in Ryukyu Islands, 17-19 cent.", the article's own English title), an article in Okinawa International University's 沖縄国際大学経済論集 (Journal of Economics and Environmental Policy) vol. 12 no. 1, February 2021, pp. 1-25 (in Japanese; https://okiu1972.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/3096/files/18801706_12_1_YasuoKurima.pdf)

What it is: A review of what earlier historians wrote on Ryukyu's sugar from the 1600s to the 1800s: sugar taken by the royal government in place of tax grain, sent to the kingdom's office at Kagoshima and sold there, and from there to Osaka.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says by what hands Ryukyu's sugar reached Osaka, the route this record's other sources leave out. It is a critical reading of older histories, quoting them at length, and most of what it says of the kingdom's system rests on accounts written after the kingdom ended (reports of 1894-1896, Ajichi in 1915, Nakahara in 1963). The Osaka step is the author's inference: the bidding sale at Satsuma's Osaka warehouse is documented for Amami's sugar from the 1720s, and he judges that Ryukyu's went the same way. It gives quantities of sugar (deliveries to the Kagoshima office, 1722-1834, about 470 to 1,500 tons a year). Its only prices and money sums are of the Meiji years after 1879: the prefecture's purchase price of 3 yen 20 sen, set in 1880 and raised to 4 yen in 1883, which an 1883 document gives for 100 kin but Ichiki gives for one barrel; an Osaka market price of 6 or 4 yen with no unit; and district and village sums. It gives no price for the kingdom's route through Kagoshima.

Used for: the route of Ryukyu's sugar to Kagoshima and Osaka and how it was sold (Money and prices)
