boso-jinya-chiba-kiyo

Chiba Prefectural Foundation of Education, Center for Cultural Properties, 房総における近世陣屋 (Early-modern jin'ya in Boso), 研究紀要 28, March 2013, part 2 (in Japanese; https://www.echiba.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/kiyo_028_p2.pdf)

What it is: A survey by the prefecture's archaeological center of the jin'ya of Kazusa and Awa (today's Chiba prefecture) - the seats of small lords, their detached offices and the bannermen's - each entry giving its lord, its site and what the plans and local histories record of its buildings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one of the few readable sources that names where a small office kept its jail. Its limits are that each entry is a short summary drawn from older local histories and plans the survey does not reproduce in full, that the offices it names are domains' detached jin'ya rather than shogunate intendancies, and that it does not say how the jail was built. The Iwaito jin'ya is itself an inference: the survey says only that 「there seems to have been」 one at Iwaito (original: 「岩糸陣屋があったらしい」), reasoning from a plan of it kept by a master carpenter's family, and takes its jail and rowhouses from the Maruyama town history. By the survey's own inference at both ends, it stood only from the domain's reassignment of 1853 to about 1861: it 「was presumably built as the jin'ya for governing these Awa lands」, and 「as it became shogunate land in Bunkyu 1 [1861], the jin'ya too, it seems, was abolished」 (translated; original: 「この安房領支配のための陣屋として建設されたのであろう」; 「文久元年に幕領となるに従い、陣屋も廃されたと思われる」).

Used for: the jail on the west side of the Naruto jin'ya, and the jail and rowhouses the Iwaito jin'ya had besides its office (Estates and other compounds)
