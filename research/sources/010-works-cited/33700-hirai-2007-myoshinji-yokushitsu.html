hirai-2007-myoshinji-yokushitsu

平井俊行 (Hirai Toshiyuki), 「妙心寺の浴室について」 ("On the bathhouse of Myoshinji", title translated), 建築史学 49 (2007), pp. 21-39 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsahj/49/0/49_21/_pdf)

What it is: An architectural historian's study of the bathhouse of the Zen temple Myoshinji in Kyoto, from the temple's own building accounts: the 1656 account of the bath's building, set out by trade and month in silver with the man-days of each trade, and a note on the carpenter's pay in the Joo era (1652-1655), the years the temple built its new kitchen (1653).

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a carpenter's and a day laborer's pay of the 1650s from one employer's books, the nearest found in date to the provinces' craft prices of the 1660s. They are a great Kyoto temple's payments, not a provincial town's, and its totals by trade are a whole crew's, not one man's. No rate is cash alone: the Joo-era rate, one round figure in a note (note 9) that does not name the document it comes from (probably the kitchen's account of 1653), is the wage and a rice allowance together (飯米作料とも, wage with food-rice), and the 1656 carpenters' totals add meal money and a smaller share to the wage, and the 1656 book has some arithmetic errors and never closes its accounts, so the author doubts it survives complete, and his recomputed totals are the ones to use.

Used for: what a carpenter and a day laborer were paid in the 1650s (Money and prices)
