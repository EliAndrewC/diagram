nagashima-2017-farm-women

Nagashima Junko, 「江戸時代における農村女性の労働・生活とジェンダー」 ("Labor, life and gender of rural women in the Edo period", title translated), 『農業および園芸』 (Agriculture and Horticulture) vol. 92, no. 7 (the page header prints no. 6), 2017, pp. 572-577, on the AgriKnowledge repository (in Japanese; https://agriknowledge.affrc.go.jp/RN/2010912071.pdf)

What it is: A short survey by a historian of women in Tokugawa villages, drawing on farm books and account books: the Seiryoki's passage on the peasant wife, the Imai household's accounts of 1821 in Shinano, the Muramatsu house rules of Noto (late 1700s to early 1800s), and a farm picture book of 1717 from Kaga (178 colored pictures).

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source read that names which farm tasks fell to women and how their work was valued against men's, with a household's women-only tasks and its wages by sex. Its wages, its women-only tasks and its rest-day rules come from two wealthy households with servants (the Imai of Shinano, 1821; the Muramatsu of Noto), and the article itself says the strict division of tasks by sex was the large household's pattern, small farms mixing men's and women's work; only the Seiryoki's passage, a 17th-century farm book of Iyo, speaks for a small farm's wife. It counts no days or hours. Its figure that ramie took ten times the labor of cotton is reported as "said to" (といわれている) and taken from Nagahara Keiji's history of ramie, silk and cotton (2004), not from the author's own reading of period records.

Used for: a farm wife's round, the division of farm tasks by sex, women's wages and work on rest days in a wealthy household, and ramie's labor against cotton's (Money and prices)
