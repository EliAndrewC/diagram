saruhashi-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, s.v. 猿橋 (Saruhashi) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%8C%BF%E6%A9%8B)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Saruhashi, the cantilever bridge over the Katsura River at Otsuki (Yamanashi prefecture), with a section on how a hanebashi is built and a history of the bridge's rebuildings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes the hanebashi as an Edo-period form whose timbers are set into holes in the bank rock, and dates the Saruhashi's rebuilding records from 1676 and its present form from at least 1756. Its limits: it is an encyclopedia article whose general description of the form is not tied to one dated source; the bridge standing today is the 1984 restoration of the 1851 bridge, its members changed to H-section steel and its bank footings set in concrete, so its landings as seen now are modern; the size the article gives (30.9 m long, 3.3 m wide, 31 m above the water) is that restored bridge's, cited to the Otsuki tourist association's page and a 2005 popular trivia paperback rather than to a survey of the Edo bridge, and its dating of the rebuildings (1676, 1756) carries no footnote; and it concerns a gorge crossing, not the low river or ditch crossings our maps mostly draw, so it attests a crossing carried back into its banks and gives no landing length.

Used for: the hanebashi, a premodern bridge set into its banks, and the Saruhashi's size and date (Ways)
