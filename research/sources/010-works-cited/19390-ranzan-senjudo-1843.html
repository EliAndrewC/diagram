ranzan-senjudo-1843

Ranzan Town web museum (嵐山町web博物誌), 村差出明細帳下書 天保14年：千手堂 関根関太郎家文書 (in Japanese; http://www.ranhaku.com/web06/01chishi/01_10.html)

What it is: The Ranzan town office's transcription, from the town history of 1968, of a draft of the account of itself that Senjudō village (Hiki district, Musashi) submitted in 1843: its yield, its fields, its 33 households and two monks, its two temples, the tax-exempt land held by the Kannon hall and the shrine of the thirty guardian deities that its two temples looked after, the Kannon hall's size, and its three tutelary shrines, which held no exempt land.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an Edo record of the same village whose two temples, Senjuin and Kōshōji, appear in the district's post-1868 temple register, so it sets Edo figures beside the register's. Its limits: it gives the exempt land held for a hall, fields included, not the precinct's area, and no main hall's size, and the one size it does give, the Kannon hall's 三間四面, is read in the Edo way as three ken square, where the older bay-and-aisle notation would make it a larger building; it is a draft kept by one house; and Senjudō is a small hill village, below the scale of a town.

Used for: the Edo exempt holdings of the two holy places in the care of two of the register's temples and a hall's size in 1843 (Religion and the dead).
