Arthur H. Smith, Village Life in China: A Study in Sociology (New York: Fleming H. Revell, 1899; Project Gutenberg; https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33485/33485-h/33485-h.htm)

What it is: A book by an American missionary who lived for decades in the villages of North China, describing the village, the house and yard, and village customs as he saw them in the late nineteenth century, under the Qing.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness account of the Qing countryside before 1912, so what it says a village household kept is a premodern observation, not a survey read back. Its limits: it is one foreign observer's general description of villages on the plain of North China, not of the rice south, and the passages used here name no province; it counts nothing, so it shows that chickens were an ordinary part of a village yard but gives no share of households; and its tone is often condescending, so its judgments of Chinese life are not relied on here, only what it reports seeing.

Used for: that chickens were kept in the yards of North China village households before 1912 (Homesteads)
