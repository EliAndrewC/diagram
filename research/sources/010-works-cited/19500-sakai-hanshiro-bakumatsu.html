sakai-hanshiro-bakumatsu

Bakumatsu Guide (幕末ガイド), 幕末のピリピリした江戸に単身赴任！なのに緊張感ゼロな武士の日常系日記が面白い【酒井伴四郎日記】 ("posted alone to tense late-Edo Edo, yet a warrior's everyday diary with zero tension is fun: the Sakai Hanshiro diary", title translated), 2019 (in Japanese; https://bakumatsu.org/blog/2019/12/sakai_hanshiro)

What it is: A popular history blog's retelling of the diary kept in 1860-1861 by Sakai Hanshiro, a junior retainer of the Wakayama domain on duty in Edo, covering his lodging, meals and outings.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says who shared his duty rowhouse unit and how they split the cooking, the one account read of how many retainers posted to Edo without their families lived in one unit (Hanshiro had left a wife and child in Wakayama). It paraphrases the diary rather than quoting it (a scholarly study of the diary exists and was not read); it gives no plan or size of the unit; and it describes one unit at one great domain's Edo residence, not a country office - three men grouped by office and kinship (his uncle and superior, himself, a colleague), one case and not a rule for how many shared a unit. What it says he ate, sardines and salmon among his fish, is the blogger's hedged summary, not a dated diary entry (the entries name mackerel he bought and horse mackerel and bonito given to him). It describes one samurai with a stipend of 25-30 koku who, while posted to Edo, also drew a special allowance and rice in kind, selling the rice he did not eat. It describes 1860, a year when, the blog says, inflation had driven prices up. It is not a laborer's diet, nor Kyoto's or Osaka's.

Used for: three retainers sharing one duty rowhouse unit in 1860 (Estates and other compounds); the fish one low-paid samurai ate in Edo in 1860 (Money and prices)
