What it is: The English Wikipedia biography of Alfred Weber, carrying his least-cost theory of industrial location and its material index.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives Weber's material index, the ratio of the weight of raw materials to the weight of the product, as a rule of where making is cheapest: weight-losing work near its materials, weight-gaining work near its market, and work using a material found everywhere free to sit near either. It does not say that heavy, cheap goods are made near their market; that reading, and its application to storage jars, are this page's own, and since firing a jar loses the weight of its water and fuel, the article's own rule would put a kiln by its clay and fuel unless the clay is found everywhere. The theory is Weber's of 1909, a model of industrial location under freight costs; the article's least-cost section is unsourced and its material-index section rests on one website.
