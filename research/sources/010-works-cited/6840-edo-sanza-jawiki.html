ja.wikipedia 江戸三座 ("the Three Licensed Theaters of Edo", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B1%9F%E6%88%B8%E4%B8%89%E5%BA%A7)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article 江戸三座 (the three theaters of Edo), on the playhouses licensed to stage kabuki by the Edo town magistrate's office from the middle of the period onward. It records that the city once had a great many playhouses, which were consolidated to four and finally to three.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives us the institution we need for a town map - that a theater is a licensed establishment, that the magistracy decided how many there were, and that the number ended up very small, capped because a big timber hall in a townhouse block was a fire the city could not fight - which is why a settlement gets one licensed playhouse or none rather than a row of them. That is our inference, not the article's: it describes the capital only and says nothing of how many houses a provincial town had; and the cap was on licensed houses flying the yagura tower - it records that unlicensed shrine-ground theaters (miyaji shibai) played alongside them in simple huts, roofed only over the stage and barred from the draw curtain, revolving stage and hanamichi, and that the provinces had their own local theater (jishibai). Its limits are that this article carries the encyclopedia's own warning that its sources are unclear; that the licensing was an Edo city policy under one government, so we take the arrangement rather than the number itself; and that the count is dated - four houses by the 1670s, three only from 1714 - so "three" is not true of the whole period. It also shows the licensed houses standing together - two on one street, and all three sent in 1842-43 to one site of some ten thousand tsubo far outside the outer moat, a move the Tenpō Reforms ordered to purge the pleasure trades from the castle town rather than a fire rule - which is the capital's theater street. Of a house's own measures it gives only a footnote's stage opening of 6 ken (about 11 m) for the late-Edo Nakamura-za, and the teahouses each house kept in the 1760s (Nakamura-za 16 large and 15 small, Ichimura-za 10 and 15, Morita-za 7 large); those footnotes are unsourced like the rest of the article.

Used for: the three playhouses licensed by the town magistracy after consolidation from many, to four, to three; the houses side by side on one street and their move to one site outside the moat (Trades and services)
