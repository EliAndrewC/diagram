kotobank-tanmono

コトバンク 反物 ("Tanmono, cloth by the tan", title translated), the entries of Shogakukan's Nipponica (by Tsunoyama Yukihiro), Heibonsha's World Encyclopedia (by Yamashita Etsuko) and other dictionaries (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/反物)

What it is: Encyclopedia entries on the tan, the bolt in which kimono cloth was woven and sold, and on the other units of cloth, the hiki above all.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the tan's width and its two lengths in the old cloth measure, and says that the length was fixed as one kimono's cloth in the Edo period and revised many times. It does not date the lengths it gives: Tsunoyama sets them only against today's metric tan, so they may be the standard of any time before the metric change, Meiji and Showa included, rather than an Edo one, and a tan of one decade may be shorter or longer than another's. The Edo fixing of the length is Yamashita's statement, with no figure of its own. Its hiki was strictly a unit of silk, the tan arising as half of one, so the hiki as two tan holds for silk and not for every cloth.

Used for: the length and width of a tan, and the hiki as two tan (Money and prices)
