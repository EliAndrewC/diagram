measuringworth-explaining-measures

Lawrence H. Officer and Samuel H. Williamson, "Explaining the Measures of Worth", MeasuringWorth (https://www.measuringworth.com/explaining_measures_of_worth.php)

What it is: The method essay of MeasuringWorth, a non-profit site run by two economic historians that turns past amounts of money, chiefly US and British, into later ones. The essay, undated, explains the site's ten measures of relative worth with worked examples; the measures first appeared in the authors' article "Better Measurements of Worth" (Challenge 49:4, 2006).

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the practice of answering with several measures at once, each against a different yardstick, and shows one past price coming out at a range of values. Its examples are American, from 1931, and their modern values are for 2011, one column for 2010, so the range it shows is that of one year's price carried across eighty years. Each of its yardsticks is a modern national statistic (a consumer price index, a GDP deflator, a household-spending survey, a wage series, GDP) that no premodern economy kept, so for the setting the practice of answering with several named measures carries over, but each yardstick has to be rebuilt from scholarship. The page's table and its prose disagree in places (the Empire State Building's GDP-deflator value is $5.7 million in the table and $507 million in the text), so a figure is taken where the two agree, or from the table with that said.

Used for: several modern values for one past price, each named for its yardstick (Money and prices)
