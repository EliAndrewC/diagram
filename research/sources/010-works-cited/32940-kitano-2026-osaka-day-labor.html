北野博司 (Kitano Hiroshi), 「徳川期大坂城石垣の普請労働力 - 役人労働と日用労働 -」 ("The labor force of the stone walls of Tokugawa Osaka Castle: retainers' labor and day labor", title translated), Tohoku University of Art and Design, Institute for Conservation of Cultural Property, bulletin (2026) (in Japanese; http://archives.tuad.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/tuad-iccp-R7bulletin-2.pdf)

What it is: An archaeologist's study of who built the stone walls of Osaka Castle when the shogunate rebuilt it in the 1620s, the daimyo houses' own men or hired day laborers, from the account books of the work, chiefly a 1628 book that settles the day-labor contracts job by job with quantities, costs and rates.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives earthwork priced by the tsubo of earth and day laborers' wages in the same ledger, and the men a tsubo of rubble took. It is castle work by the shogun's order, on a scale far beyond a village, and its day wages are those of Osaka, Japan's great commercial city, in the building boom of the early 1600s, not a village's or a provincial town's; its rates are contract rates for jobs, which may carry the contractor's margin; it does not define its tsubo of earth; and its figures are of the 1620s, a century and more before most other wages here.

Used for: what earthwork and carrying stone cost, beside the day wages of the same work (Money and prices)
