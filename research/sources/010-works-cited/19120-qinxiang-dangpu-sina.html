qinxiang-dangpu-sina

Zhui Qing (blog), "无锡洛社秦巷当铺" ("The Qinxiang pawnshop at Luoshe, Wuxi", title translated), 2023, quoting the Wuxi media article "秦巷当铺：两百多年的岁月故事" (in Chinese; https://blog.sina.com.cn/s/blog_4eebd24b0102z7hq.html)

What it is: A personal travel blog's account of a visit to a restored pawnshop on the old street of Qinxiang, a rural market town north of Wuxi in Jiangsu, followed by a passage the blog says it excerpts from a local news article on the building's history.

Why it applies, and its limits: The excerpted article dates the pawnshop to about the mid-Qing and gives its original size and form - 18 m wide and 20 m deep, five bays, three courts, two stories, a street gable 8 m high and fire walls 5 m high on the other sides - so it attests a pawnshop on a market-town street in the Qing, and a large, storied, high-walled pawnshop there before its closure in 1937. Its limits: it is a blog repeating a newspaper, which cites unnamed "records" for the figures, and the date is approximate ("about the mid-Qing"); the page does not say which part of the building held the pledges, and the older wall footings found under the floor during the recent renovation show, in the article's own words, that the pawnshop was rebuilt or extended at some point - so the measured 18 x 20 m, two-story form cannot be dated to the mid-Qing founding itself, only to before the shop closed after a bandit raid in the winter of 1937 (the building was later a school from the 1950s and a small workshop).

Used for: a mid-Qing market-town pawnshop's size, stories and walls (Trades and services)
