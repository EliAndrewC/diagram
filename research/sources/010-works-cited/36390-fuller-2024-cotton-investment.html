Dorian Q Fuller, Louis Champion, Cristina Cobo Castillo and Anna den Hollander, "Cotton and post-Neolithic investment agriculture in tropical Asia and Africa, with two routes to West Africa", Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports 57 (2024), article 104649 (https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10206456/1/Cotton%20investment%20agriculture%20%26%202%20routes%20thru%20Africa.pdf)

What it is: A review by archaeobotanists of published cotton finds across Asia and Africa, from the earliest fibers up to AD 1492.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one work read that sets out the Old World species, their origins and the Indian chronology together. Its authors say seeds of the two species have proven difficult to tell apart, and they presume the early South Asian seeds to be G. arboreum because that species is thought native to South Asia, so those finds are not identified as it. On East Asia it has only written evidence from China, and it says nothing of Japan or Korea.

Used for: Old World cotton species and India's earliest dates (Money and prices)
