Arthur J. Rolnick and Warren E. Weber, "Inflation, Money, and Output Under Alternative Monetary Standards", Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis, Research Department Staff Report 175, revised October 1995 (https://www.minneapolisfed.org/research/sr/sr175.pdf)

What it is: Two Federal Reserve economists' comparison of inflation, money growth and output in 15 countries under commodity money and under paper money, over 33 episodes, some reaching back to the 1600s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a comparative source: it measures how much faster prices rose under paper money than under commodity money, the contrast the question asks about, never a figure of the setting. It does not tell one commodity standard from another, its data cover at least 80 years for each country and more than 100 for most, and its later journal version, whose figures may differ, was not read. Its commodity standards are gold and silver standards with paper notes and bank money convertible at a fixed rate, not coin economies, so it does not measure inflation from a ruler debasing and recoining his money, which is how prices moved in the Edo series.

Used for: inflation under metal money against paper money (Comparative money: other societies)
