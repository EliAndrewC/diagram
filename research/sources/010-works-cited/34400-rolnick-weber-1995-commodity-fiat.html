Arthur J. Rolnick and Warren E. Weber, "Inflation, Money, and Output Under Alternative Monetary Standards", Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis, Research Department Staff Report 175, revised October 1995 (https://www.minneapolisfed.org/research/sr/sr175.pdf)

What it is: Two Federal Reserve economists' comparison of inflation, money growth and output in 15 countries under commodity money (gold, silver) and under paper money, over 33 episodes, some reaching back to the 1600s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a comparative source: it measures how much faster prices rose under paper money than under metal money, the contrast the question asks about, never a figure of the setting. Its countries are European and American ones, its commodity-standard data for most begin in the 18th or 19th century, and its later journal version, whose figures may differ, was not read.

Used for: inflation under metal money against paper money (Comparative money: other societies)
