lingwai-daida-6

Zhou Qufei (周去非), Lingwai daida (嶺外代答, Answers in Place of Letters from beyond the Passes), chapter 6, the Siku quanshu text on Chinese Wikisource (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/嶺外代答_(四庫全書本)/卷06)

What it is: The chapter on implements, clothing and food in a Song writer's account of the far south, which Pelliot dates to 1178.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the earliest account read, with a date, of cotton being ginned, spun and woven on the south coast. For Hainan's Li cloth it is not the earliest: Fan Chengda's Guihai yuheng zhi of 1175, which Pelliot cites for Li curtains south of the sea, also names the 黎單 as cotton cloth that the people of Guilin bought as bedding. Zhou had served under Fan and drew widely on that book, and he does not say he saw the Hainan cloth made, so on Hainan he is not an independent eyewitness. It speaks for this far frontier only: on Hainan the cloth is that of the Li, a non-Han people, who Pelliot says did most of the growing there. Zhou describes a low tree and does not know whether a herb of the same name is another plant, so it cannot be carried over to the annual cotton of later centuries. Two words in its key sentence can be read as "plentiful" or as "some of the rich": Pelliot translates "some of the rich", but the same chapter's 廣西觸處富有苧麻 (of ramie in Guangxi) fits only "plentiful". It gives no quantity or price for cotton, though the chapter prices other goods. The text read is a transcription of the eighteenth-century Siku copy of a book whose original is lost, with blanks elsewhere in the chapter; no critical edition was read, so the two words above rest on that copy.

Used for: cotton on the south coast and Hainan in 1178 (Money and prices)
