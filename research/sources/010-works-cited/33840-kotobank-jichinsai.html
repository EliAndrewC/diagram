kotobank-jichinsai

Kotobank, "地鎮祭" ("jichinsai", the ground-pacifying rite), the entries of 精選版 日本国語大辞典 (Nihon Kokugo Daijiten, shorter edition) and 改訂新版 世界大百科事典 (Sekai Daihyakka Jiten, the latter by 大河直躬, Okawa Naomi), among others (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/地鎮祭)

What it is: A dictionary aggregator's page for the rite held before building on a site: the historical dictionary's earliest attestation of the word, and the encyclopedia's account of the rite's history and present form.

Why it applies, and its limits: The dictionary quotes one line of the Kōyasan Okunoin building ledger of 1544, some sixty years before the Edo period, giving what the monastery spent on the rite for an offering hall. That is a great temple's Buddhist rite for its own building, not what a household or a village paid, and this record has read it only as the dictionary quotes it. The encyclopedia says such rites were then uncommon in building, and the page gives no cost for the lay Shinto rite whose form it dates to the late Edo period; the encyclopedia's account of when the rite became important and what its present form is rests on Edo carpenters' manuals it does not quote.

Used for: what a rite to purify the ground cost, and when its present form arose (Money and prices)
