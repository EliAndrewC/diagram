mannering-2021-viking-age

Ulla Mannering, "Fashioning the Viking Age: status after the first three years", Archaeological Textiles Review no. 63, 2021, pp. 139-144 (https://tidsskrift.dk/atr/article/download/166728/207807/371498)

What it is: A project report on reconstructing Viking Age textiles and clothing, with tools copied mostly from finds at Haithabu: wool sorted, combed and spun on drop spindles, and samples woven on a warp-weighted loom, with the weaving speeds measured.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a measured speed of hand weaving from hand-spun yarn, the only modern rate read: 16 to 29 weft threads, about 3 cm, an hour on 60 cm samples. The paper puts that slowness down to the hairy hand-spun wool warp sticking at every change of shed, and the same team's larger sample on machine-spun yarn, a twill of 5 wefts a cm, went 10 to 15 cm (about 50 to 75 wefts) an hour, so the figure is for one yarn on one loom, a low bound rather than a typical rate. The report does not say how many weavers were timed or how practised they were, leaving the full account to a later publication. It is wool, a European warp-weighted loom and modern craftspeople using reconstructed tools, so it is a yardstick for how slow such work was, never a figure for hemp, ramie, cotton or silk or for Rokugan; it gives no spinning speed.

Used for: hand-weaving speeds on a warp-weighted loom, as a comparison (Comparative money: other societies)
