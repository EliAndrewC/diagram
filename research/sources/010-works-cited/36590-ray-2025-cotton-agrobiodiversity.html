Avik Ray, "The erosion of agrobiodiversity of cotton in India: interplay of politics, science, and technology", Science, Technologie, Développement 5, no. 1 (2025): 84-98, DOI 10.21494/ISTE.OP.2025.1339 (https://openscience.fr/IMG/pdf/iste_std25v5n1_6.pdf)

What it is: A journal article reconstructing how India's cotton species changed over the last century and what drove the change.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates American cotton's share of India's cotton acreage to 1947-48: 2-3% in its prose and 3% in its table, which gives no other year before 1989. That is a share of land, not of the crop; the article calls American cotton high-yielding, so its share of the harvest may have run higher. The table is taken from a 2014 work not read here, and its 1947-48 column sums to more than 100%, while the article's text gives 3, 65 and 32% for 1946-47, so the native species' shares are not clean. Those seasons straddle the Partition of India, and the article does not say what territory they cover, so they may not stand for the undivided country.

Used for: The share of American cotton in India (Money and prices)
