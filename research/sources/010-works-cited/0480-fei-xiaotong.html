Fei Xiaotong, Kaixiangong / the Xichang polder layout (https://archive.org/details/PeasantLifeInChina, full text https://archive.org/stream/PeasantLifeInChina/PeasantLifeInChina_djvu.txt)

What it is: Fei Xiaotong's Peasant Life in China (1939), his study of the village of Kaixiangong in the Lake Tai delta of Jiangsu from fieldwork in July and August 1936, read here for its plan of the farm layout in the Xichang polder - the map and passages showing houses standing on the raised polder margin along the interior streams, with mulberry on the same high strip - and for the official survey of 1932 it reports, which gives the village's land in each of its eleven polders (from about eight mow to over nine hundred), and for the drainage units (cien) that the larger polders were divided into.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is our worked example of how a settlement sits inside deep-water, diked ground: not on a hill but on the only raised line there is, the dike itself. The limits are the same as for the book at large - one delta village observed in the 1930s, in China - and it is read here only for the polder layout and the polders' sizes, not for the wider ethnography. Its polder figures are the village's own land in each polder, not always the whole polder: two of the eleven are shared with other villages, and for those Fei gives only his rough estimate of Kaixiangong's part, so a shared polder may be larger than its figure. The scan's OCR garbles some words and figures (yii for yu, a hyphen for a decimal point), so its numbers are read against the survey's own total.

Used for: houses inside the polder along its interior streams (the deep-water settlement case); the sizes of the polders, the largest tract found, and their drainage units (Fields)
