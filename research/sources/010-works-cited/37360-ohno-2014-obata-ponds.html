大野暁彦 (Ohno Akihiko), Chisen isho kara miru toshi kankyo to teien isho no sogo kankei ni kansuru kenkyu (A study of the relation between the urban environment and garden design, seen from the design of garden ponds), doctoral thesis, Chiba University, 2014 (in Japanese; https://opac.ll.chiba-u.jp/da/curator/900118699/HMA_0062.pdf)

What it is: A doctoral thesis in landscape architecture on how a city's setting, water channels and waterside life shaped its garden ponds, in three cases: Edo's garden ponds read from a Tokyo 1:5000 map, the channels of Obata, and the canal banks of Suzhou. Its second chapter is a field survey of the surviving gardens on the samurai-house lots of Obata (Kanra, Gunma), the small town of the Obata domain, whose lots and channels it reads against the Edo-period plan of the Obata jin'ya; its third chapter measures the banks of eight Suzhou private gardens.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source read that describes the banks of the ponds on samurai-house lots: viewing ponds ringed by large stones or planting, and working ponds walled off from the channel by dry-laid stone, and it gives the viewing ponds' depth, about 40-60 cm. The gardens are modern survivals that the thesis itself says are of uncertain date, observed in the 2010s, in one small domain town whose channels are its own; it does not say what a pond's bank or depth was when the gardens were made.

Used for: the banks and depth of surviving samurai-house garden ponds at Obata (Estates and other compounds)
