shimomukai-mitsui-cotton

Shimomukai Norihiko (下向井紀彦), 「寛政年間における三井越後屋の木綿仕入状況とその特質」 ("The Mitsui Echigoya's purchases of cotton in the Kansei era and their character", title translated), Mitsui Bunko Ronso 47 (in Japanese; Mitsui Bunko, https://mitsui-bunko.or.jp/archives/pdf_data/mbr-47_shimomukai.pdf)

What it is: A study, from the Mitsui house's own records, of how its Kyoto, Osaka and Edo stores bought cotton cloth in the 1790s, above all from Hoki province, with the Edo wholesalers' cotton prices reported at the shogunate's price inquiry of 1791.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the prices for a tan of cotton by place of origin that the officers of Edo's two cotton wholesalers' guilds reported to the town magistrate in 1791. These are the wholesalers' selling prices, not what a townsman paid at a shop. It also gives the size of a tan that a great buyer would accept, from an order of 1798 to its Hoki buying agent. Its author warns that the wholesalers probably reported their prices low to the shogunate, so the prices serve to compare cottons more than as what a buyer paid. Its profit rates on cotton are the Mitsui house's own Edo cotton store's, reported from an earlier study and a clerk's note of 1798; the paper does not call the store a retailer, but it says the house then meant to push cotton as cloth for common people, sold over the counter for cash at thin margins against Daimaru's cash trade. So the rates bear on a shop's margin, though the paper does not say how much of the store's cotton went over the counter.

Used for: cotton prices a tan in Edo in 1791, Mitsui's minimum size of a tan, and the Mitsui Edo cotton store's profit rates on cotton (Money and prices)
