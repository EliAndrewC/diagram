宋史 (Songshi, History of the Song), 卷182, 食貨志下四 (Treatise on Food and Money, second part, 4), the second section on salt (鹽中), compiled under the Yuan and presented in 1345 (Wikisource, in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/宋史/卷182)

What it is: The official history's account of the Song salt monopoly in the sea-salt districts and Hebei, with the memorials that argued over it: what the state paid the makers, how it priced salt by distance, and how the price of salt stood against rice.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the state's buying price at the works (4 to 6 cash a jin, an early-Song figure the chapter later shows raised), its rule of pricing by distance from them, a censor's report that Hebei's price doubled under the new monopoly law, and a proposal of 1122 by the Monopoly Bureau, arguing for a higher salt price, setting the price of salt beside the price of rice before 1068 and in its own day. Its prices are officials' figures in their own arguments, rounded and given for one district or the empire at large; the proposal gives no wage, and says nothing of why rice rose.

Used for: the Song state's buying and selling prices of salt, and salt against rice (Money and prices)
