ishiyama-hirosaki-cotton

Ishiyama Akiko, "弘前藩領における木綿・古手類の移入および流通について" ("The import and circulation of cotton and old clothes in the Hirosaki domain", title translated), 弘前大学國史研究 (Hirosaki University Journal of National History) no. 144, pp. 37-52, in Japanese; the year is not given on the pages read (https://web.archive.org/web/20250918060503/https://hirosaki.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/6322/files/HirodaiKokushi_144_37.pdf)

What it is: A regional historian's article on how cotton and secondhand clothing reached the Hirosaki domain at the northern tip of Honshu, built on the domain's diary, its clothing orders, its port records and its counts of cloth merchants, nearly all from the 1660s to the 1860s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one study read of how a domain too cold to grow cotton clothed itself, and the only one that follows one domain's clothing orders class by class. Its summary of the orders leans on Kurotaki Jujiro's earlier studies, the orders say what was allowed rather than what anyone owned, and its Morioka material is at second hand from a study by Nishimura. Its port figure is one port's cotton for one year, 1850-51, after the shortages the article describes up to the late 1700s and when cotton was also being shipped on to other domains, so it speaks neither for earlier times nor for what the domain wore. Its author's remark on the Furukawa travel diary concerns one traveler's inquiry, and the article's conclusion that all but the poorest peasants could get old cotton clothes by the late 1700s rests on that diary and a few men's clothing recorded in the domain's diary from 1757 to 1760.

Used for: cotton's arrival in the cold north, the clothing orders by rank, and old clothes among the peasants of Tsugaru (Money and prices)
