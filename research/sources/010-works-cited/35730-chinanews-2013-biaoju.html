中国新闻网 (China News Service), 「古代镖局靠强盗“赏”饭吃 行业守则是远离妇人」 ("The old escort agencies lived on the robbers' 'favor'; their rule of the trade was to keep away from women", title translated), 2013-09-12, by way of 人民网 (People's Daily Online), first printed in the popular-history magazine 《国家人文历史》 2013 no. 18 (in Chinese; https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cul/2013/09-12/5277624.shtml)

What it is: A popular-history article on the Qing escort agencies (biaoju): what they carried and guarded, how clients contracted with them, what an escort-master was paid, the agencies' other business of guarding houses, and their decline after remittance houses (early banks) and railways came, the Tongxinggong agency of Shanxi closing in 1914.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that gives an escort-master's wage and terms and how clients engaged an agency, but it gives no year or place for the wage. Its one client rate (50 taels for each 10,000 taels guarded, plus a charge by distance) is that of the Tongxinggong agency of Pingyao in Shanxi, which the article says ran for more than half a century until 1914, so the rate is one Shanxi agency's, from the late Qing, and may be from after 1895. It names its references (among them a study of Tongxinggong and one of Ming and Qing escort agencies) only as a list at its end, so no figure can be traced to its source. And it was written as a tie-in to a television comedy, mixing wuxia novels, a comic crosstalk and the lore of the trade (the passwords exchanged with robbers) with the history, so the record takes only its terms of trade from it.

Used for: an escort agency's terms with its escort-masters and its clients (Money and prices)
