日向進 (Hinata Susumu), 「丹波柏原の彫物師中井氏とその営業形態 - 近世丹波・丹後における建築界の動向 -」 ("The Nakai, carvers of Kaibara in Tanba, and how they worked: the building trades of Tanba and Tango in the Edo period", title translated), 日本建築学会計画系論文報告集 (Journal of Architecture, Planning and Environmental Engineering, AIJ) 396 (February 1989), pp. 55-66 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/396/0/396_KJ00004074727/_pdf/-char/ja)

What it is: An architectural historian's study of a family of carvers of shrine and temple ornament in the provinces of Tanba and Tango, northwest of Kyoto, from the 1750s to the 1930s, from their own work books and the carved inscriptions on their buildings, with what they and the region's carpenters and sawyers were paid.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the carvers' fee by the man-day in the 1810s, a few of their jobs with day counts and totals, and carpenters' and sawyers' rates of the region from a ruling recorded at Miyazu in 1777, single buildings of 1808-1822 and an undated Fukuchiyama source, each quoted or summarized from a document the paper names. The rates are a handful of cases from one family of specialists in one region, not a series; the carvers' work books give their own fee, not what a shrine paid in all; and the paper names no laborer's wage. A Kyoto carver's prices by the piece are quoted from an order whose year is not given.

Used for: what a carver, a carpenter and a sawyer were paid by the day in Tanba and Tango, and a worship hall's carpentry (Money and prices)
