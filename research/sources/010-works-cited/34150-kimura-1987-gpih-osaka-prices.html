kimura-1987-gpih-osaka-prices

David Jacks (extended by Ernest Boffy-Ramirez), "Osaka 1600-1650", a spreadsheet for the Global Price and Income History Group, 2006, transcribing Masahiro Kimura, La Revolución de los precios en la cuenca del pacífico, 1600-1650 (Mexico City: UNAM, 1987), the title the file gives; the work is Masahiro Kimura Inoue, La revolución de los precios y el sakoku (aislamiento japonés del exterior) (licenciatura thesis, Facultad de Economía, UNAM, Mexico City, 1987) (https://gpih.ucdavis.edu/files/Osaka_1600-1650.xls)

What it is: A spreadsheet transcription, labeled Osaka, of the price appendix of Masahiro Kimura Inoue's undergraduate thesis in economics (UNAM, 1987) on the rise of Japanese prices in 1600-1650 and the sakoku: rice, soybeans, salt, oil, sake, cloth, paper and other goods by year, each in its own measure and in silver monme. The thesis takes its prices chiefly from Kobata Atsushi's study of price changes in the fifteenth to seventeenth centuries (Kyoto, 1962), and says its rice and soybean figures are mainly Kyoto's; Miyamoto Mataji's Osaka series (1963) and Takekoshi's economic history supply only the years it marks.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one series read that prices goods other than rice in a Japanese city before 1650. Its figures are sparse, many goods priced in only a handful of years; it does not say whether its oil was for lamps or food; its fuel and light are priced by units a year's basket cannot use, pine by the 9 by 9 cm, candles apiece and coal by the sack, the coal marked as not convertible; the figures are mostly Kyoto's, not Osaka's, and three removes from the period's records (Kobata's and Miyamoto's compilations, read through Kimura's thesis, read through the GPIH transcription), each one Kimura's conversion to silver monme and an average where a year had several prices; and they span the price rise that is the thesis's subject, rice going from 9.41 monme a koku in 1600 to 59.4 in 1637 and nineteen of its twenty-one foods peaking in the 1630s, so a 1630s figure is a peak, not a typical year.

Used for: City prices of rice, soybeans, salt, oil and cloth in 1620-1650, chiefly Kyoto's, Osaka's in the years the thesis marks (Money and prices)
