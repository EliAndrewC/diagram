Takaya Mariko, 若狭野浅野家資料にみる堺奉行所の家中長屋 (The magistrate's retainers' rowhouses at the Sakai magistracy, as seen in the Wakasano Asano family papers), 東京造形大学研究報 19, 2018 (in Japanese; https://zokei.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/192/files/08_19takaya%20%E8%8B%A5%E7%8B%AD%E9%87%8E%E6%B5%85%E9%87%8E%E5%B1%8B%E8%B3%87%E6%96%99%E3%81%AB%E3%81%BF%E3%82%8B%E5%A0%BA%E5%A5%89%E8%A1%8C%E6%89%80%E3%81%AE%E5%AE%B6%E4%B8%AD%E9%95%B7%E5%B1%8B.pdf)

What it is: An architectural historian's paper in a university bulletin reading the plans of the Sakai town magistracy kept among the papers of Asano Nagatsune, magistrate there from 1711 to 1729: the magistrate's residence and offices, the lots of the yoriki, the magistrate's senior officers, the doshin rowhouse and the rowhouses of the magistrate's own retainers, redrawn from plans of about 1728.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is one of the few readable plans of a provincial magistracy that shows where each kind of staff lodged - the magistrate's retainers inside the grounds, the doshin on the next lot - and gives a doshin's dwelling a size. Its limits are that Sakai was a town magistracy of the shogunate, not a rural intendant's office, that its plans are of one magistrate's tenure, and that the doshin dwelling's size is given as the plans' common measure, not a measured building.

Used for: the magistrate's retainers' rowhouses inside the Sakai magistracy's grounds, and the doshin's dwelling of 2 by 3 ken a man (Estates and other compounds)
