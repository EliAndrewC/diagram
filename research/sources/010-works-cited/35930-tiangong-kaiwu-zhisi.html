Song Yingxing, 《天工開物》 (Tiangong kaiwu, 1637), the chapter 「治絲」 ("Reeling silk", title translated), on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A9%E5%B7%A5%E9%96%8B%E7%89%A9/%E6%B2%BB%E7%B5%B2)

What it is: The section on reeling silk from the cocoons in the late Ming encyclopedia of crafts by Song Yingxing, describing the reeling frame and the silk reeled in a day.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one rate read for reeling silk, by weight in a full day: thirty liang of the ordinary silk for damask and gauze, but only twenty of the head-wrap silk, which is reeled from half as many cocoons. It is a scholar's account of the best practice he knew - he ranks the Huzhou reeling frame above Sichuan's - not a count of what households did; it times reeling only, not weaving.

Used for: the silk one person could reel in a day (Money and prices)
