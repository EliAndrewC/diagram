G. J. Gordon, "Memorandum of an Excursion to the Tea Hills" (June 1835, p. 78) and a review of Joseph Thompson's Considerations respecting the Trade with China (April 1836, p. 543), The Chinese Repository, vol. 4 (Canton, May 1835-April 1836), archive.org copy (https://archive.org/details/thechineserepository18351836v4.112)

What it is: The fourth volume of the missionaries' Canton monthly; the passages used are a land tax quoted in cash in a Fujian tea district, with the cash to the dollar beside it, from G. J. Gordon's memorandum of a trip to the Ankoy tea hills with Gutzlaff in November 1834, which the Repository reprinted from the Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal (February 1835), and a correspondent's review of a London book on the China trade.

Why it applies, and its limits: The review, printed at Canton by an unnamed correspondent, scoffs at the book's table of "1,000 cash=1 tale or six shillings" ("he might as well say as many pence"), because a tael of sycee "must vary with the market price of that article"; that the 1,000 was only nominal is this record's reading of this, not the review's word. It gives no market rate of cash in its place, and its appeal to the Canton Price Currents (p. 544) is against Thompson on the dollar as money of account, not on the cash. The tea-district rate is a traveler's parenthesis for one inland district of Fujian, not Canton's. And it is no independent market rate: at the Canton reckoning of 0.72 tael to the dollar, 720 cash to the dollar coincides with 1,000 cash to the tael, the very ratio the review scoffs at, and the record has no Fujian market quotation of that date to set beside it.

Used for: cash to the dollar in a Fujian tea district, 1834, and that a Canton review rejected 1,000 cash to the tael (Money and prices)
