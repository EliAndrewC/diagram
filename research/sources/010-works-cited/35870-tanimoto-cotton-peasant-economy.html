tanimoto-cotton-peasant-economy

Masayuki Tanimoto, "Cotton and the Peasant Economy: Response to the 'Foreign Superior Fiber' in the Early Modern Japan", a paper for the Global Economic History Network meeting at Helsinki, London School of Economics (https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/Helsinki/HELSINKITanimoto.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's draft paper on how cotton reached Japanese farm households under the Tokugawa and after, with a table of the Akita region's cloth supply in 1808-1810 and one of a Niikawa (Etchu) weaving household's output and working days in the mid-1800s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one count read of the days to spin and weave a tan of cotton and of a household's cloth for its own use and for sale. Its author marked it "Not to be quoted or cited without permission of the author", so it is a draft whose figures may have changed in print; the Niikawa table names no source for its 4 days a tan, which starts from ginned cotton and leaves out growing, ginning, dyeing and sewing; and its ramie figure is Nagahara Keiji's estimate at second hand.

Used for: the days to spin and weave a tan of cotton, a household's yearly cloth, the Akita cloth supply, and ramie's labor against cotton's (Money and prices)
