Japanese Wikipedia contributors, "原方刺し子" [Harakata quilting] (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8E%9F%E6%96%B9%E5%88%BA%E3%81%97%E5%AD%90)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on harakata sashiko, the embroidered foot-wiping cloths (hana-zōkin) made by the women of the harakata-shu, the lower samurai of the Yonezawa domain settled on the moors around the town after the Uesugi house's fief was cut in 1601; its opening section gives their history, and a later section the lots the domain granted them, cited to a 1991 popular regional compendium (Nōbunkyō's Hitozukuri fudoki, Yamagata), a 1989 study of sashiko, and a library reference note on a 1769 (Meiwa 6) map of the harakata residences.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a domain's own lot for its lowest samurai, settled on the moors by about 1609, larger than the lower lots of Fukui's table, with half of it a vegetable field. It clashes with itself on two figures (some 6,000 retainers moved, yet some 8,000 became harakata-shu; a lot of 150 tsubo, yet a frontage of 6 to 8 ken by a depth of about 25 ken, which makes 150 to 200), the article does not date the lot rule, and the depth is cited to a note on a 1769 map of the harakata residences, so the figures may record the lots as they stood a century and a half on rather than as first granted; any moor a household cleared beyond its lot became its own, so a holding could be larger than the lot; and these were half-farming samurai on the town's edge, not a castle town's ordinary ward.

Used for: how generous a domain's lots for its lower samurai could be (The government quarter)
