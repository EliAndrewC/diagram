ma-2004-gpih-china-wages

Debin Ma, "China wages 1807-1925", a spreadsheet for the Global Price and Income History Group (https://gpih.ucdavis.edu/files/China_wages_1807-1925.xls)

What it is: An economic historian's transcription of the day wages of unskilled workers in Beijing, its suburbs, Sichuan and Canton, from published studies, with his conversions to silver taels and grams.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Beijing building laborer's wage of 1900-1925 from Meng and Gamble's study, which was not read here, and the rural Beijing fuel store's wage of 1807-1902 with a cash-to-silver rate beside it. Its city wages include the food the workers were given, and they are the capital's: Allen and his co-authors find the wages of Zhili, Beijing's province, well above the rest of China's, so they run high for a laborer in a lesser town or a village. Its rural wage is the cash alone: Gamble reported that the fuel store's workers also had food allowances the books did not record. Its rural sheet's silver wage is twice the one the published study of the same series prints: a note on the sheet says that all its wages are doubled to allow for the food the workers were also given.

Used for: Beijing's laborer's wage of 1900-1919 and the two readings of the rural Beijing wage (Money and prices)
