boe-2017-millennium-macroeconomic-uk

Bank of England, "A millennium of macroeconomic data for the UK", version 3.1 (updated to 2016), a spreadsheet, sheet "A47. Wages and prices" (https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/statistics/research-datasets/a-millennium-of-macroeconomic-data-for-the-uk.xlsx), listed on the Bank's research datasets page (https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/research-datasets)

What it is: The Bank of England's collection of long British economic series, one sheet a topic; its wages and prices sheet carries a consumer price index for every year from 1209 to 2016, spliced from the series of several economic historians and, from 1914, official statistics, each source named in the column's note.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a comparative source: one country's price level over eight centuries, read for how far English and British prices drifted and swung under metal money, never a figure of the setting. Its years before 1661 are Clark's cost of living index for England rescaled, so they add nothing Clark's own series lack; from 1661 the column covers Great Britain and then the United Kingdom, and each join between its sources is a place where one index's basket gives way to another's. It is an index of a whole basket, so it moves less from year to year than any one good in it.

Used for: how far English prices drifted and swung, 1209-1913, and Britain's since 1972 (Comparative money: other societies)
