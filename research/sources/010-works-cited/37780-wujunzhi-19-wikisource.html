范成大 (Fan Chengda), 吳郡志 ("Gazetteer of Wu Commandery"), 卷十九 ("Chapter 19", title translated), completed 1192, Chinese Wikisource transcription (https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/吳郡志/卷19)

What it is: The water-works chapter of the Southern Song gazetteer of Suzhou. Most of it is the memorials of Jia Dan (郟亶), a man of Kunshan, the first sent up in the third year of Xining (1070) and the others undated in the chapter, on the fields and waterways of the Suzhou country by Lake Tai: the lengthwise and crosswise channels he says the ancients cut, their widths and depths, the dikes raised from their spoil, and how households later cut the banks to make small creeks for their boats.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a Northern Song official's own account of polder country: how the channels round a polder were laid out, and boats kept on household creeks. It is rice-polder country by Lake Tai, not a block of dike-ponds, so it speaks to waterways that carried boats among diked fields and says nothing of a channel between ponds. Jia Dan argues a case: his chessboard of broad channels is what he says the ancients built, read from old traces he saw about 1070, and his plan to restore it was tried and stopped, so the grid's spacing and widths are a reformer's reconstruction (he gives the ancient widths differently in two memorials) and not a survey of his own day; what he says he saw himself is of his own day - family-named household creeks every half li to two li between the great channels, and the Xiaoyu and Zhihe channels narrowed in his lifetime from twenty or thirty zhang to ten-odd and six or seven - though that the creeks were cut through an ancient grid's dikes remains his argument. He gives the great bounding channels' widths in zhang and none for a household creek. The Wikisource transcription is not checked here against a printed edition and carries copying slips (it prints 取上 where 取土, "take earth", is meant).

Used for: households cutting polder banks to make small creeks for their boats, and the bounding channels cut lengthwise and crosswise with the water outside and the fields inside (Field archetypes)
