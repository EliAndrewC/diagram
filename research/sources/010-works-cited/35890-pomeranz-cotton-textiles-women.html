pomeranz-cotton-textiles-women

Kenneth Pomeranz, "Cotton Textiles, Division of Labour and the Economic and Social Conditions of Women: A Preliminary Survey", a paper for the fifth Global Economic History Network conference, London School of Economics (https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/GEHNConferences/conf5/PomeranzGEHN5.pdf)

What it is: A historian's draft survey of Chinese women's cotton spinning and weaving from about 1350 to the 1930s, closest on about 1750 and the 1930s, with estimates of the days a bolt took and of women's working days, drawn from Li Bozhong, Huang and twentieth-century surveys.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets the days for a bolt of cotton beside women's and men's working years in Jiangnan. Its cover reads "Incomplete draft: please do not quote or cite"; its days a bolt are its author's estimates and change with era and tool: 7 with hand-spun yarn, for about 1750 and in its 1905/9 and 1932/6 counts alike (the setting's figure), 6 in one note costing Dingxian work in 1931 (three days of spinning, where the text says an improved wheel was cutting spinning time), and 3 with machine-spun yarn; its bolt is ginned cotton made into cloth of about 3.6 square yards (some 3 square meters), smaller than a Japanese tan; fuel-gathering as women's and children's work is a passing remark about one North China case; and its count of textile days for about 1750 is of all the cotton cloth made, home use included, while its Jiangnan women's days are days worked for income.

Used for: the days to make a bolt of cotton and the hours of spinning to weaving; women's and men's working days in Jiangnan; fuel-gathering as women's and children's work (Money and prices)
