pomeranz-cotton-textiles-women

Kenneth Pomeranz, "Cotton Textiles, Division of Labour and the Economic and Social Conditions of Women: A Preliminary Survey", a paper for the fifth Global Economic History Network conference, London School of Economics (https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/GEHNConferences/conf5/PomeranzGEHN5.pdf)

What it is: A historian's draft survey of Chinese women's cotton spinning and weaving from about 1350 to the 1930s, closest on about 1750 and the 1930s, with estimates of the days a bolt took and of women's working days, drawn from Li Bozhong, Huang and twentieth-century surveys.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets the days for a bolt of cotton beside women's and men's working years in Jiangnan. Its cover reads "Incomplete draft: please do not quote or cite"; its days a bolt are its author's estimates and change with era and tool: 7 for about 1750 with hand-spinning on the old wheel (the setting's figure), 6 in 1931 with an improved wheel, and 3 with machine-spun yarn; its bolt is ginned cotton made into cloth of about 3.6 square yards (some 3 square meters), smaller than a Japanese tan; fuel-gathering as women's and children's work is a passing remark about one North China case; and every count of women's textile days in it is of cloth for sale.

Used for: the days to make a bolt of cotton and the hours of spinning to weaving; women's and men's working days in Jiangnan; fuel-gathering as women's and children's work (Money and prices)
