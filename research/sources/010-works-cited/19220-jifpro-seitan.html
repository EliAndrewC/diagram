Japan International Forestry Promotion and Cooperation Center (JIFPRO), ChiePro, 日本の製炭技術 - 高品質は築窯と精煉の技 - ("Japanese charcoal-making technique: high quality from kiln building and refining", title translated) (https://jifpro.or.jp/chiepro/charcoal/)

What it is: A knowledge page by Yatagai Mitsuyoshi (谷田貝光克) of the Sumiyaki no Kai, a charcoal-burners' society, written for ChiePro, a collection of Japanese forestry know-how that a forestry-cooperation body made under a Forestry Agency subsidy for transfer to developing countries (the site dates from 2021; the page itself is undated). It explains how charcoal is made in Japan today, black and white, from building the kiln to putting out the fire, with a short history drawn from secondary works such as the 1958 Nihon mokutanshi.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says where the charcoal is cooled: black charcoal inside the kiln, sealed so that no air enters, and white charcoal drawn out red-hot and smothered beside the kiln under a damp powder of ash and river sand; it says the white-charcoal method is held to have taken its settled form in the Heian period. It describes the craft as practiced today and dates each method only as tradition: the black-charcoal method as largely settled in the Muromachi period, the white-charcoal method in the Heian period, both improved since (binchotan is an Edo invention). Before them, charcoal was burned in an earth-covered pile, and it does not say how that was put out. It says nothing of where charcoal was kept once it left the kiln.

Used for: that charcoal is cooled at the kiln, in it or beside it (Trades and services)
