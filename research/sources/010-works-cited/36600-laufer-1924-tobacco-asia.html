laufer-1924-tobacco-asia

Berthold Laufer, Tobacco and Its Use in Asia, Field Museum of Natural History, Anthropology Leaflet 18, Chicago, 1924, full text on the Internet Archive (https://archive.org/stream/tobaccoitsuseina18lauf/tobaccoitsuseina18lauf_djvu.txt)

What it is: A short non-technical museum leaflet by a sinologist, by his own account a sketch of a longer unpublished manuscript, tracing how tobacco and pipes spread across Asia. It quotes Chinese, Persian and European witnesses in English and reports unnamed Japanese records; for Korea it has only the Dutch captive Hamel and a Korean folk tale.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one work read that covers all four countries, and it holds the words of Cocks, Asad Beg, Terry and Jahangir, and Hamel in Laufer's summary. It gives no notes, so none of its readings of Chinese or Japanese texts can be traced. Its Chinese authors are in Laufer's own English and were not read in the original; several are unnamed, among them the writer it reports on the 1638 edict and on boys smoking by the 1640s. No Japanese or Korean writer is named: the "Japanese records" are only reported, and Korea rests on Hamel, a Dutchman. Its firsts are a 1924 synthesis: "about 1605" for Japan is inferred from Cocks's "not ten years", and its first Chinese author on tobacco is Chang Kiai-pin.

Used for: Tobacco in China, Japan, Korea and India (Money and prices)
