kim-dg-late-joseon-markets

Kim Dae-gil, "The Development of Local Markets in Late Joseon and Changes in Social and Cultural Life" (Korean), Korean Studies Quarterly 35, no. 4 (2012): 87-113 (https://accesson.kr/ksq/assets/pdf/40861/journal-35-4-87.pdf)

What it is: A journal article by a historian of the National Institute of Korean History on the five-day rural markets of later Joseon and what they changed.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places raw cotton and cotton cloth among the goods those markets sold, in a list it cites to the Imwon gyeongjeji (林園經濟志) for the first half of the nineteenth century, and names the regions famous for producing the cloth, Honam and Yeongnam in the south, which says where it was made and not where it was sold. Cotton is a few passages in an article about markets: that list, a 1754 court record of village women weaving cloth for market days, and an 1858 diary's price for raw cotton at one Gyeongsang market. It does not date the start of cotton's market trade, and its regional line is a single undated sentence quoted from a work it cites only by title and section (近民要覽, 勸農桑, a section on encouraging farming and sericulture), which was not read here.

Used for: Cotton in India and Korea (Money and prices)
