parkes-1854-kyakhta-caravan

Harry Parkes, "Report on the Russian Caravan Trade with China", Journal of the Royal Geographical Society of London 24 (1854) (archive.org copy of the JSTOR early-journal scan; https://archive.org/stream/jstor-3698123/3698123_djvu.txt)

What it is: A British consular officer's report, written at Canton, on the overland tea trade from China to Russia through Kalgan (Zhangjiakou) and Kiakhta (Kyakhta) in the early 1850s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the camel load, the days and distance across the Gobi, and the freight for 100 catties in silver, with the weight and cost of the chests and bricks of tea. He did not travel the road: these are his informants' figures, which he gives as ranges that rose and fell with the supply of animals and goods. The freight is what Mongol camel and ox caravans charged across the desert to a regulated frontier trade. It shows how much a very long overland haul could add to a good's price, not what porters, packhorses or boats charged on ordinary roads. Since money was banned at Kiakhta, where commissioners set prices in barter, he gives no money price there, only a value in China and a retail price in Russia.

Used for: the camel freight between Kalgan and Kiakhta (Money and prices)
