michie-siberian-overland

Alexander Michie, The Siberian Overland Route from Peking to Petersburg, London: John Murray, 1864 (Project Gutenberg; https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45167/45167-h/45167-h.htm)

What it is: A British merchant's account of his journey in 1863 from Beijing across Mongolia and Siberia to St Petersburg, beginning with the voyage from Shanghai to Tianjin, the road on to Beijing, where he lodged at a city inn, and on through the Nankou pass, where he stayed at the village inn.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is an eyewitness of north China's inns in the late Qing, in 1863. One is a city inn in Beijing whose courtyard held travelers' horses and mules. The other is the village inn at Nankou, on the road north, whose courtyard was crowded with travelers' gear and animals and where he slept in what he calls our dormitory. He never says that room was shared with strangers, so a common dormitory is our reading of his word, not his statement. It is one foreigner's passing impressions, written with a traveler's disdain, of a few inns on one road in the north; it gives no plan and no size.

Used for: the inn yard holding travelers' animals and the common dormitory, in 1863 (Estates and other compounds)
