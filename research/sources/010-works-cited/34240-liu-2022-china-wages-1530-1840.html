liu-2022-china-wages-1530-1840

Ziang Liu, "Wages, Labour Market, and Living Standards in China, 1530-1840", London School of Economics, Economic History working paper 339, 2022 (https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/WorkingPapers/Economic-History/2022/WP-339.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's estimates of the day wages of unskilled and skilled workers in China from 1530 to 1840, built from archival wage records, chiefly building and craft work in the Lower Yangzi and Beijing, with a discussion of the silver and copper money the wages were paid in.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one unskilled wage series found that reaches back into the Ming, half-century by half-century, and states the weight of the tael and how the capital's cash was counted. Its series is the Lower Yangzi's building laborer, not Beijing's; its earliest half-century averages only 1530-1549 and its last only 1800-1840; and its levels run well below the Allen et al. estimates it sets beside them, a disagreement it discusses and does not settle. Its tael weights and exchange rates are taken from other works it cites, not measured.

Used for: the Lower Yangzi laborer's day wage from the 1500s to the 1800s, the weight of the tael, and the capital's double-counted cash (Money and prices)
