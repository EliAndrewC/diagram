pwsannong-gudai-feiliao

"Gudai feiliao jizhi" (古代肥料积制, making and storing manure in premodern China), an entry in the soil-and-fertilizer history section of the encyclopedia on Zhihui Sannong (智汇三农), a Chinese agricultural knowledge database published by China Agriculture Press (in Chinese; https://www.pwsannong.com/c/2016-04-13/560262.shtml)

What it is: A Chinese encyclopedia entry on how manure was made, rotted and stored in China from the Han to the Qing, quoting the old farming books on trampled dung, composting, the treatment of night soil and the pits and sheds it was kept in.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the premodern farming books in their own words - Wang Zhen's on the brick manure pit at the head of a southern field, the Shen family manual on deep, large pits - so it carries premodern attestations of where and how manure was stored. It is a modern secondary summary posted in 2016 that gives no page references; it dates the Shen manual only to the Ming and gives no date for Wang Zhen's book; some of what it reports of the Shen manual is its own paraphrase rather than a quotation; it speaks of jars only as sealed vessels buried in the ground to ripen night soil (its "golden juice") and the Ming concentrated manures, never as an open jar at the field or the path; and its remark that brick field-head pits usually have covers describes such pits today, not Wang Zhen's.

Used for: manure pits at the head of the field in the old farming books (Field archetypes)
