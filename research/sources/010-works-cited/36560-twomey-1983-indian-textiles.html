Michael J. Twomey, "Employment in Nineteenth Century Indian Textiles", Explorations in Economic History 20 (1983): 37-57 (https://backend.production.deepblue-documents.lib.umich.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/23d0dc93-9aaf-4d7c-a03d-7310fd85725f/content)

What it is: An economic historian's estimate of India's cotton cloth and textile employment from 1800 to 1930, from trade and production figures.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates when India began to import more cloth than it exported and gives shares of consumption, separately from Roy's estimates, which cite it. Its consumption figures are estimates, from 1880 onward as well as before (Table 5 heads them "Approximate totals"), and its disagreement with W. W. Hunter's figure of 1886 concerns the early 1880s. Hunter's work is known here only through its quotation in this article. Its figures are subcontinent-wide totals for a colonial market being taken over by British machine-made cloth and, after 1850, Indian mills (by 1929 mills and imports held 70% of consumption, and cloth from hand-spun yarn 4%), so the date and the shares describe an industrial displacement the setting does not have and say nothing of one district.

Used for: Cotton in India and Korea (Money and prices)
