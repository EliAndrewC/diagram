hou-hanshu-86

後漢書 (History of the Later Han, title translated), volume 86, 南蠻西南夷列傳 ("Account of the southern and southwestern peoples"), Wikisource text (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/後漢書/卷86)

What it is: The chapter on the peoples of the south and southwest in the dynastic history of the Later Han (25-220 CE), compiled by Fan Ye and completed about 445, partly from older records.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the text a modern survey reads as an early mention of cotton cloth, so its own words are given. Its own words do not settle the fiber. Most of the cloths it names have no stated fiber, including the broad cloth of Hainan, set at the end of Emperor Wu's reign (he died in 87 BCE). Among the Ailao of western Yunnan it lists 帛疊 (bo die, an early form of 白疊, the word that came to mean cotton cloth) and a cloth spun from 梧桐木華 (wutong mu hua, "wutong tree flower"). The cotton reading is Pelliot's, not the chapter's: he suspects wutong is a misnomer, and says the tree may be the cotton tree (Gossypium arboreum) and the bo die cloth woven from its floss. He also says the passage seems indebted to the Hua-yang kuo-chih, written shortly after 347, so it speaks for the southwest of the third and fourth centuries more surely than for the Later Han. This Wikisource text lacks the Tang commentary.

Used for: whether its cloths of the southwest and Hainan were cotton (Money and prices)
