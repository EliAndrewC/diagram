Huang Yang 黄洋 and Du Yao 杜瑶, 古代"一尺"有多长？有哪些常见的长度计量单位？ ("How long was an ancient chi? What were the common units of length?", title translated), 2021, republished by the Chinese Academy of History (http://hrczh.cass.cn/lszg/lszg_sgwx/202502/t20250218_5846894.shtml)

What it is: A short popular article, first published on 15 April 2021 by the WeChat account Museums of China (博物馆中国) and republished on the Chinese Academy of History's site, on the ancient units of length, built around two rulers excavated near Sanmenxia: an Eastern Han bone foot of 23.2 cm and a Tang bronze ruler of 15.2 cm that the authors read as a half-foot.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the Tang foot as 30.7 cm, the figure the authors check a Tang ruler from Shanzhou against; that ruler is 15.2 cm, unevenly graduated, and a half-foot only by the authors' own reading, so it gives a foot of about 30.4 cm and does not itself establish the 30.7, which the article asserts without a source, and the article does not say that the Tang kept a large and a small foot, 30.7 cm being the large. It gives the pace as eight feet under the Zhou, six under the Qin, and five builder's feet (yingzao chi, the Ming and Qing builder's foot of about 31 to 32 cm) in the old reckoning, which turns paces into meters; the five-foot pace is therefore stated for the late imperial reckoning, not for the Tang or the Song, and holds for them only on other evidence. A foot's length varied from dynasty to dynasty.

Used for: the Tang foot, 30.7 cm, and the five-foot pace (City defenses)
