kotobank-senryo-yakusha

"千両役者" (senryo yakusha, "thousand-ryo actor"), Kotobank, the entries of several Japanese dictionaries and encyclopedias, among them the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia's by Igusa Toshio (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E5%8D%83%E4%B8%A1%E5%BD%B9%E8%80%85)

What it is: The Kotobank page gathering reference entries on the "thousand-ryo actor", the kabuki star whose yearly contract reached a thousand ryo, with the contract year and the first actors said to reach it.

Why it applies, and its limits: Two signed encyclopedia entries date the first thousand-ryo contracts to 1711-1736 and say the contract ran a year from the eleventh month. They say the first are "said to be" those actors, so the date is a tradition rather than a contract seen; a thousand ryo was the mark of the top rank, not the most an actor was paid: the entries say later stars earned more and the term came to mean the highest rank whatever the sum, and none says what most actors earned.

Used for: the date of the first thousand-ryo actors and the length of an actor's contract (Money and prices)
