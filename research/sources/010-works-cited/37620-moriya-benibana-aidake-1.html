moriya-benibana-aidake-1

Moriya Madoka (森谷圓人), "近世中期における紅花荷主の経営構造と上方取引（一）" ("The business of a safflower shipper and its trade with Kyoto and Osaka in the mid Edo period, part 1", title translated), a journal article in the Yamagata University library's collection of writings on safflower (in Japanese; https://www.lib.yamagata-u.ac.jp/database/benibana/img/bunken/75_moriya.pdf)

What it is: A study of the Aida house, safflower shippers outside the castle town of Yamagata in Dewa province, from the house's own papers. Its first table lists, year by year from 1727 to 1765, how much safflower the house sent to Kyoto, what the lot cost or sold for, and the house's profit, in the documents' own words.

Why it applies, and its limits: For seven years it gives both what a lot cost the shipper and what it sold for to the Kyoto safflower wholesalers - in six of them the year's whole lot, in 1763 only one mark's six loads of the year's thirty-three. The cost is named differently from year to year - a landed cost in one, a purchase cost in another, plain "price" in most - though the author describes the column as the price paid in the producing district together with the charges of shipping it; a year named a purchase cost may be the purchase alone. It is one house's lots, of a dyestuff, sold while fourteen licensed Kyoto wholesalers held the buying of safflower to themselves (1735-1765), so the sale is what a buyers' monopoly paid. The years around the recoinage of 1736 are counted in the old gold or the new Genbun gold, which were not of equal worth, so those years' sums cannot be set against each other as they stand (within a year the cost, the sale and the profit agree); the 1727 lot is counted in silver.

Used for: what a Yamagata shipper's safflower cost against its sale in Kyoto (Money and prices)
