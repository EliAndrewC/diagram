Zhang Jian (张箭), 《中国人餐桌上的玉米、土豆、甘薯》 (Maize, potatoes and sweet potatoes on the Chinese table), excerpted from the magazine 《文明》, August 2025, on the Beijing News 新京号 platform, 21 August 2026 (in Chinese; https://www.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1787267359019245.html)

What it is: A popular article on three American crops by Zhang Jian, a professor of world history at Sichuan University who has written a book on the spread of New World crops, though the page does not say so. He mentions his own research once, for a count of American crops, and the article has no notes. It is reposted on a newspaper's contributor platform.

Why it applies, and its limits: It alone gives 1601 for Japan and reads the older word tu-dou as the potato. It names no source for 1601 beyond "Western historical records", and the year, port and carrier are hard to fit: the first Dutch ship reached Japan in 1600, driven ashore in Bungo, the Dutch post at Hirado is usually dated 1609, and they moved to Nagasaki only in 1641. Its earliest text for tu-dou, the Bencao gangmu's tu-yu entry, credits its descriptions to the Tang herbalists Su Gong and Chen Cangqi, centuries before the potato left America, and gives tu-dou only in a list of names beside huang-du, now the air yam Dioscorea bulbifera, so that entry describes an Old World plant; its northern route into Hebei rests on the same word. The other texts it quotes for tu-dou were not read; its reading of them conflicts with Laufer's.
