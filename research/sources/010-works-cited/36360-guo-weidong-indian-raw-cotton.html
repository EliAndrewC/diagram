Guo Weidong (郭卫东), "印度棉花: 鸦片战争之前外域原料的规模化入华" (Indian cotton: the large-scale import of a foreign raw material into China before the Opium War), a journal article in Chinese filed on a Peking University site (https://fh.pku.edu.cn/docs/2018-11/20181110172100744658.pdf)

What it is: A Peking University historian's 2014 article on raw cotton imported into China from India in the 18th and 19th centuries, up to the Opium War.

Why it applies, and its limits: One sentence of its opening states when cotton replaced hemp and silk, which is the summary the wider literature commonly gives. The sentence is a passing generality in a paper about a later period, whose trade history begins in 1704. It says only that in the Yuan and Ming cotton cloth took the place of hemp and silk as the Chinese people's most important material for clothing and bedding: a span of nearly four centuries for all China, with no narrower date, no region and no class, and it ranks the fibers rather than saying the older ones went out of use. It carries no citation of its own; the note closing its paragraph only lists general histories of Chinese cotton.

Used for: a modern summary of when cotton displaced the older fibers (Money and prices)
