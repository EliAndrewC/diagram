kazusa-bugyosho

To KAZUSA (a local-history blog), 南町奉行所と大岡越前 ("The South Town Magistracy and Ooka Echizen", title translated) (https://kazusa.jpn.org/b/archives/2883)

What it is: A local-history blog post (2014, by the site's pseudonymous author "kazusa", whose site is chiefly on the Kazusa domains) on the Edo South Town Magistracy and its best-known magistrate, Ooka Tadasuke, with the site and building sizes of the two Edo magistracies and a former South magistracy officer's recollection of its gate and forecourt; it ends with a list of works it drew on (the Tokyo shishi-ko, Okuma Yoshikuni's Edo kenchiku sowa, Matsudaira Taro's study of Edo institutions, among others) but ties no figure to any of them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one readable figure found for the size of an Edo magistracy's ground, which is the anchor our Imperial Magistrate's compound is calibrated to. It is an enthusiast's post that hedges the figure with "it seems" and does not say which of its listed works the figure comes from (a search summary attributes a 2,617-tsubo figure to a plan of Ooka's official residence, not read). The post does not date the figure. It gives it after the South magistracy's move to Sukiyabashi in 1707, where that office stayed to the end of the shogunate, but the North magistracy, as the post itself notes, later moved again to Gofukubashi, so the figure need not describe one site for the North. Both were the town magistracies of the shogun's capital, not a provincial office; they were not certainly the largest of their kind (a search summary, not read, gives Osaka's East magistracy about 3,000 tsubo). The paving and black gravel come from the recollection of Hara Taneaki (born 1853), a yoriki of the South magistracy in its last years, which the post paraphrases without saying where or when it was recorded; it describes that one office's 1860s buildings, which the post says kept Ooka's design through rebuildings after fires.

Used for: the two Edo magistracies each on about 2,500 to 2,600 tsubo with a mansion of over 1,000 tsubo (Domain capitals); a former officer's recollection of the paving and black gravel from the South Town Magistracy's gate to its entrance (Estates and other compounds)
