杨煜达 (Yang Yuda), 《滇铜、汉铜与清代中期的汉口铜市场》 ("Yunnan copper, Hankou copper and the copper market of Hankou in the mid-Qing", title translated), first published in 《清史研究》 (Qing History Journal) 2013 no. 2, reposted on the site of the Institute of Qing History, Renmin University (in Chinese; http://iqh.ruc.edu.cn/old/qdjjsyj/jjs_ztyj/syjt/41c78389d9b24041a36cfcee20e0321b.htm)

What it is: A historian's study of the copper trade at Hankou in the eighteenth century, largely from the memorials of officials to the throne.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes a memorial of the early Qianlong years giving what coarse copper ware sold for by weight, the one price of copper goods found for Qing China. The memorial is dated only to the early Qianlong years and names no place; it argues that coins were being melted for profit, so its prices serve that case; the figure is what even the coarsest heavy ware fetched, so finer pieces sold for more; the article places the memorial in years it calls a copper shortage, when copper was dear, so the figure may run high against later decades, when the same article puts raw copper at Hankou at 17.5 to 19.5 taels per 100 jin; and ware sold by weight is not a cast object of a temple or shrine.

Used for: the price of copper ware by weight in Qing China (Money and prices)
