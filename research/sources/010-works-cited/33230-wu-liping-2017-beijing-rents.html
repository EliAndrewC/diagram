吴丽平 (Wu Liping), 「清朝人在北京怎样租房?房租有多高?」 ("How did people of the Qing rent a house in Beijing, and how high were the rents?", title translated), 文史知识 (Wenshi Zhishi), reposted on Sohu, 26 June 2017 (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/152159933_672913)

What it is: A historian's article for a general magazine of history on renting in Qing Beijing, from the government's rent schedules, rental contracts and banner archives, and the diary of the official Li Ciming, with the rents of government houses, shops and empty sites from the 1720s to the 1900s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the government's rent schedule for houses by the bay to 1727, and gives dated rents for shops and for one official's house, each with the archive or diary it comes from. It quotes rents in the units its documents use, silver taels for the schedule and strings of copper cash for the contracts and the diary, without saying which count of cash the strings were reckoned in; the shops it names stood in Beijing's busiest streets.

Used for: Beijing's government rent schedule of 1727, its shop rents and an official's house rent (Money and prices)
