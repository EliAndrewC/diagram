吴丽平 (Wu Liping), 「清代北京的房屋租赁」 ("Renting houses in Qing Beijing", title translated), 文史知识 (Wenshi Zhishi) 2014 no. 9, reposted on Sohu on 26 June 2017 as 「清朝人在北京怎样租房?房租有多高?」 ("How did people of the Qing rent a house in Beijing, and how high were the rents?", title translated) (in Chinese; https://www.sohu.com/a/152159933_672913)

What it is: A historian's article for a general magazine of history on renting in Qing Beijing, from the government's rent schedules, rental contracts and banner archives, and the diary of the official Li Ciming, with the rents of government houses, shops and empty sites from the 1720s to the 1900s.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the government's rent schedule for houses by the bay to 1727, and gives rents for shops and for one official's house, each with the archive or diary it comes from. It quotes rents in the units its documents use - silver taels, Beijing cash, or strings of copper cash - without saying which count of cash the shops' strings were reckoned in; the government shops it names stood in the city's more commercial districts, where it judges their rents relatively high; some of its figures, such as a district's rent per room, are its own inferences from a few contracts; the three shop rents it names in the commercial districts are dated only by a banner archive running from 1871 to 1911, and one of their tenants, the Commercial Bank of China, opened only in 1897, so they are late-Qing figures, some from after China's premodern cut-off; and Li Ciming's four taels a month were the rent of a whole house of three courtyards of four bays each, taken jointly with a fellow provincial, Li living in the rearmost, not the rent of the rooms he used.

Used for: Beijing's government rent schedule of 1727, its shop rents and an official's house rent (Money and prices)
