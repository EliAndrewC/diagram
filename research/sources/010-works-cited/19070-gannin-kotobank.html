gannin-kotobank

Kotobank, "gannin bozu" (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E9%A1%98%E4%BA%BA%E5%9D%8A%E4%B8%BB-49427)

What it is: Kotobank's page for gannin bozu, the Edo mendicants who begged and performed in the streets, gathering the entries of several Japanese reference works (among them the Heibonsha world encyclopedia and the Nipponica encyclopedia).

Why it applies, and its limits: Its entries quote a report to Edo's town magistrate's office of 1842 naming the blocks where the gannin lived, Shitaya Yamazaki-cho among them, and the Nipponica entry places them in Bakuro-cho and Hashimoto-cho in the Genroku era (1688-1704) and in Shitaya Yamazaki-cho by the Tenpo era (1830-44), so the block is attested as theirs from the Tenpo years, not before. It is a reference summary, not the report itself, and the report is of the Tenpo reform year, when the town was moving such people out of townsmen's quarters; it says nothing of lodging houses.

Used for: that the block of Edo's first kichin-yado was a mendicants' quarter by 1842 (Trades and services)
