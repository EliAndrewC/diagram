gujin-xie-yamen

Qinding gujin tushu jicheng (欽定古今圖書集成), Fangyu huibian, Zhifang dian, juan 1472 (on Wikisource; in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%AC%BD%E5%AE%9A%E5%8F%A4%E4%BB%8A%E5%9C%96%E6%9B%B8%E9%9B%86%E6%88%90/%E6%96%B9%E8%BC%BF%E5%BD%99%E7%B7%A8/%E8%81%B7%E6%96%B9%E5%85%B8/%E7%AC%AC1472%E5%8D%B7)

What it is: A juan of the Qing imperial encyclopedia (completed 1725) that copies county gazetteers' descriptions of their seats; the entry for the county office of Xie'e (嶍峨縣, Yunnan) lists its buildings in order from the front gate to the rear.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows a Chinese county yamen keeping a vegetable garden inside its compound, listed behind the residence gate after the kitchen, the stable and the archery ground - the rear service ground - with only the granaries named after it - the county yamen's form, which this setting mixes with the Japanese. Its limits are that it is an inventory, not a plan, so it gives the garden's place only by the order of the list and not its side; that it is one prefecture of a southwestern frontier province, described as the yamens stood under Kangxi (Xie'e's was enlarged in 1686); and that the county entries in the same juan differ - Xinping's also ends its residence with a vegetable garden (菜園一圃), while Mengzi's lists none, Hexi's a fish pond and Tonghai's old yamen a garden and pond (園池). The Wikisource punctuation was made by a program; each item in the list carries its own measure word, so the reading does not rest on it.

Used for: a county yamen's vegetable garden in its rear service ground (Estates and other compounds).
