ja.wikipedia 弾左衛門 ("Danzaemon, the hereditary head of the outcaste hierarchy", title translated) (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%BC%BE%E5%B7%A6%E8%A1%9B%E9%96%80)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on Danzaemon, the hereditary head of the eta of eastern Japan under the shogunate, and on his Asakusa Shinmachi compound: its enclosure, its buildings, its court and jail, and the officials housed there.

Why it applies, and its limits: It describes an outcaste head's precinct in Edo as an enclosed block with residences, storehouses, a shrine, a court and a jail. It does not say where the residents worked, nor whether the block's ground, on the Sanya canal, counted as inside the city, nor how large the block was or in which decade it stood as described; its one paragraph on the compound, including the 300 to 400 people of officials' families housed there, cites no source, and of the article's sources (a bibliography of two books, Uramoto 2004 and Shiomi 2014, and footnotes to more works) none is cited for the compound. And the compound was the headquarters of the man who administered the eta and hinin of the Kanto provinces (less Mito and Kitsuregawa domains, the Nikko lands and others), Izu and parts of Kai, Suruga, Mutsu and Mikawa, in the largest city in the country, with a court and a jail because it held delegated jurisdiction; an ordinary provincial seat's outcaste block is a far smaller and plainer thing.
