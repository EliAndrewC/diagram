kyomasu-jawiki

Japanese Wikipedia, "京枡" (the Kyoto measure) (in Japanese; https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BA%AC%E6%9E%A1)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the Kyōmasu, the official grain measure of Japan from the late sixteenth century to the 1950s: the older and the newer measure's dimensions, the shogunate's order of 1669 making the newer one the only official measure, the domains that kept their own, and its metric value under the act of 1891.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page that dates the change of the shō, and so the koku, from the older measure to the newer, and says that some domains refused the order. It is footnoted to histories of measurement, but it gives no capacity for any domain's own measure, so how far a domain's koku differed is not on it. The older measure's 62,500 cubic bu is only the commonly given size: the page notes other views, and one of its notes gives other dimensions for the same measure. Its date for the newer measure, 1622-23, is the Edo scholar Nakamura Tekisai's inference from period texts. Its metric value, 1.8039 liters, is the 1891 legal definition; a study it cites finds the period's measures were made to a foot about 0.2 percent longer.

Used for: the older and newer Kyoto measures, the order of 1669 and the domains' own measures (Money and prices)
