makishima-2016-village-courier

巻島隆 (Makishima Takashi), 「江戸後期、村名主の通信環境 ―上野国那波郡福島村、渡辺三右衛門を事例に―」 ("A village headman's communications in the late Edo period: the case of Watanabe San'emon of Fukushima village, Kozuke", title translated), 郵政博物館研究紀要 (Bulletin of the Postal Museum) 7, March 2016 (in Japanese; https://www.postalmuseum.jp/publication/research/research_07_03.pdf)

What it is: A postal historian's study of how a village headman in Kozuke sent and received letters, money and goods, from his diary of 1842 to 1869 and his family's papers (the village's expense books, a courier house's passbook of 1831 and its receipts): the village's own courier, couriers hired by the run, the courier houses of the highways and the robberies of their convoys.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the terms on which one village kept its courier and hired others by the run, with the amounts paid, and shows the courier houses' convoys carrying cash under their own convoy leaders, with no guard named; it is one headman's records from one village of the Kanto, in a silk-raising district on the highways to Edo that had more courier houses and more cash on the road than ordinary farming country (the convoys robbed were carrying silk-market money to Kiryu); its amounts are copper cash of particular years, most from 1845 to 1853, and the later ones carry the inflation of the last Tokugawa years (the village courier's pay rose from 12 kan 800 mon in 1866 to 26 kan 400 by the end of 1867, and a letter to Tokyo cost 400 mon in 1868); and the robberies it records are those that reached him.

Used for: couriers hired by the run and by the year, and how cash went on the road (Money and prices)
