kawashima-1986-santome

川嶋雅章 (Kawashima Masaaki), 三富新田集落の現況と土地利用秩序保全上の問題 ("The present state of the Santome Shinden settlements and the problems of conserving their land-use order", title translated), 農村計画学会誌 (Journal of Rural Planning) 6(1), June 1987, pp. 44-47, a paper from the 1986 rural planning seminar (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/arp1982/6/1/6_1_44/_pdf)

What it is: A rural-planning paper by a Meiji University engineer on Santome Shinden as it stood in the 1980s: its founding figures (the strip of 40 by 375 ken, the households at the opening), the directions its strips run, the one-sided row of Shimotome, its woods, and how a homestead is used and planted.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one read source that says how the grove sits on a row-village homestead - the homestead covered by its grove, with zelkova and white oak (shirakashi) planted along its road side to form a line of trees - and that names a row with houses on one side of the road only (Shimotome, which it calls a one-sided road village). Its limits: it is a survey of the 1980s, not of the Edo period, so the trees and the homestead it describes (the south-facing house with its front yard, by then holding a garage and workshop) are as they stood then, not dated to the founding, and it says itself that the groves had much thinned and that houses for second and third sons had risen across the road at Shimotome; it is one planned dry-field colony of the Kawagoe domain, laid out in identical strips, more regular than a village that grew on its own; its 172 households at the opening are the registered holders of house lots in three villages together (83, 40 and 49), the headman and the colony's leaders each holding two lots (it gives no count of lots; the extra lots would account for the 180 that other sources give for the 1696 survey) - so one village's row held 40 to 83 households on 40 to 91 lots; and its founding figures are summarized without the page of the register they come from.

Used for: the grove covering a row-village homestead, its road-side line of zelkova and white oak, and the south-facing house, the one-sided row at Shimotome, and the households at the opening (Homesteads)
