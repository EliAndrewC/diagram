Samuel and Sarah Adams, The Complete Servant (London: Knight and Lacey, 1825), Project Gutenberg eBook #69487, plain text (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/69487.txt.utf-8)

What it is: A London household manual of 1825 by two former servants, Samuel and Sarah Adams, "Fifty years Servants in different Families": a guide to the duties of every post from the housekeeper to the servant of all work, with the wages each post commanded, a table for dividing a large income among the household's heads of expense, a list of the servants kept at each income, and reckoning tables. The Gutenberg text keeps none of the printed edition's page numbers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a comparative source: it says what an English household of the 1820s paid and spent, read only as a yardstick for players who know shillings from Austen and Dickens, and never for a koku's value or any figure of the setting. Its wages are the ranges two servants give for the posts of a gentleman's establishment, in town or country, not a survey, and they are cash paid over board, lodging and (for men in livery) clothing, with perquisites besides; its income table is a rule of thumb "given in round numbers" for large incomes; and it quotes almost no prices of provisions.

Used for: servants' wages by post, the division of a household's income, and what little it prices (Comparative money: other societies)
