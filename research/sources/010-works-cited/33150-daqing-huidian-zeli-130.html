daqing-huidian-zeli-130

欽定大清會典則例 (Precedents of the Collected Statutes of the Great Qing, title translated), volume 130, Board of Works, the Yuheng office, "Military equipment, part 1", the Siku Quanshu edition (in Chinese; Wikisource transcription; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/欽定大清㑹典則例_(四庫全書本)/卷130)

What it is: A volume of the Qing government's own compilation of the rulings behind its statutes, setting out year by year the rulings on making the arms of the banner and provincial troops, among them the cost in silver allowed for making each piece of a provincial soldier's equipment, and closing with the Board's schedule of the fixed prices it paid for miscellaneous materials. It was compiled under the Qianlong emperor; the latest ruling in this volume is dated 1753.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its ruling of 1650 sets the silver allowed for a provincial soldier's arrow beside the bow, armor, sabre and musket, a primary figure for an ordinary arrow early in the Qing; its undated list of the prices of miscellaneous materials prices a kitchen cleaver. The arrow's figure is an allowance for making the arms, fixed for all the provinces at once, so it says what the state would pay, not what an arrow cost in any one market; a ruling of 1751 in the same volume found that what the provinces paid for the same arms had come to differ widely, and had each governor fix prices from their own province's. The cleaver's price is of another kind: the list is the Board of Works' fixed price for goods it bought from the shops of the capital, so it is an official purchase price in Beijing, not a market price in the provinces, and it can be dated only by the compilation, under the Qianlong emperor in the mid-eighteenth century, about a century after the arrow.

Used for: the Qing allowance for a provincial soldier's arrow in 1650, and the price of a kitchen cleaver (Money and prices)
