wigmore-1892-servitudes

John Henry Wigmore (ed.), Materials for the Study of Private Law in Old Japan, Part V, "Property: Civil Customs", chapter II, section 3, "Sundry Servitudes" (pp. 39-43), Asiatic Society of Japan, Tokyo, 1892 (https://archive.org/details/materialsforstu00japagoog)

What it is: John Henry Wigmore's English edition, published in Tokyo in 1892, of two Japanese compilations of local customary law published in 1877 and 1880, reported district by district under each province; its section on sundry servitudes records who might cross whose land to reach a highway, a well or a drain, and on what terms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says what a household did when its land had no way of its own to the road: passage over a neighbor's land, owed by custom, sometimes for a rent. Its limits: the compilations it translates were published in 1877 and 1880, a decade after the Restoration, and report the customs in the present tense, only a few of them given as old custom ("from ancient times", "for generations"), so an entry may describe early Meiji practice rather than Edo practice; each entry is one district's custom, several marked as holding in towns; most of its reports of passage speak of plots of land, and only one names a house; and the scan's text is machine-read, so it is quoted only where its words stand clean.

Used for: passage over a neighbor's land for land with no way of its own to the road (Ways)
