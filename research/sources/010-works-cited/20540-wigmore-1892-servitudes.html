wigmore-1892-servitudes

John Henry Wigmore (ed.), Materials for the Study of Private Law in Old Japan, Part V, "Property: Civil Customs", chapter I, "Ownership and Title" (pp. 1-19), and chapter II, section 2, "Vicinage" (pp. 25-39), and section 3, "Sundry Servitudes" (pp. 39-43), Asiatic Society of Japan, Tokyo, 1892 (https://archive.org/details/materialsforstu00japagoog)

What it is: John Henry Wigmore's English edition, published in Tokyo in 1892, of two Japanese compilations of local customary law published in 1877 and 1880, reported district by district under each province; its section on sundry servitudes records who might cross whose land to reach a highway, a well or a drain, and on what terms, and its section on vicinage how far a house stood from its neighbors, a road and the fields.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that says what a household did when its land had no way of its own to the road: passage over a neighbor's land, owed by custom, sometimes for a rent; and the one that gives how far a village house stood back from a road, in one district. Its limits: the compilations it translates were published in 1877 and 1880, a decade after the Restoration, and report the customs in the present tense, only a few of them given as old custom ("from ancient times", "for generations"), so an entry may describe early Meiji practice rather than Edo practice, though the two the record leans on speak of the domain era: the Ono district's setback stands in an entry whose town houses need a petition to "the feudal official", an office gone with the domains in 1871, and the Otori district's passage has been required "for generations"; each entry is one district's custom, several marked as holding in towns; most of its reports of passage speak of plots of land, and only one names a house; and the scan's text is machine-read, so it is quoted only where its words stand clean.

Used for: passage over a neighbor's land for land with no way of its own to the road, and how far a village house stood back from a road (Ways); land mortgaged where its sale was forbidden (Money and prices)
