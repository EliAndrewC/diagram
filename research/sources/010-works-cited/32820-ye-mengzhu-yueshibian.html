ye-mengzhu-yueshibian

葉夢珠 (Ye Mengzhu), 閱世編 ("Notes on the world as I have seen it", title translated), chapter 1, 田產一 ("Landed property, part 1"), and chapter 7, 食貨一 and 食貨六 ("Food and goods", parts 1 and 6), Wikisource text (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/閱世編)

What it is: A Shanghai county writer's notes on his own prefecture, Songjiang, now within Shanghai, from the last years of the Ming into the Kangxi reign, recording what land sold for a mu, what rent it was let at, the price of rice in many of those years, and the prices of goods sold there, cloth among them.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives land prices and rents for the same land in the same passage, from someone who watched them move, so a price can be set against its rent. Its figures are one observer's for one prefecture, recalled in round ranges; only his Chongzhen prices are from ordinary years, and the rest are those of crises: the dear rice after the Ming fell, the tax burden of the early 1660s and the dear rice of 1680. The best land of Huating and Qingpu (Huating and Lou in 1680) sold for two to five times as much as Shanghai's, but it was not the same land: it was let at about twice Shanghai's rent, so its price against its rent differs far less than its price, and no one figure speaks for the prefecture. Songjiang was taxed, as he says himself, at the empire's highest rate, so its land sold low against its rent; and he does not say whether a rent was counted in husked rice or in unhusked grain, or what tax the owner paid out of it. His pongee is Shandong pongee (山東繭綢), a silk named for Shandong, whose own was the best; but he says that Shandong kind had died out, and the cloth he prices is a thinner one, looser than floss silk, woven everywhere in Jiaxing, Huzhou, Suzhou and Songjiang, so it is a Jiangnan cloth's price, not the Shandong silk's. He prices it by the chi, not the bolt. He gives its price undated, only as what it sells for "now" against what it fetched under the Ming, so "the late 1600s" is inferred from when he wrote. And it is the price of a cloth he says was getting cheaper every year as more of it was woven and worn, not a steady price.

Used for: land prices against rents in Songjiang from the 1630s to 1680; the price of pongee in Songjiang in the late 1600s (Money and prices)
