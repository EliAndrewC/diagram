ye-mengzhu-yueshibian

葉夢珠 (Ye Mengzhu), 閱世編 ("Notes on the world as I have seen it", title translated), chapter 1, 田產一 ("Landed property, part 1"), and chapter 7, 食貨一 ("Food and goods, part 1"), Wikisource text (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/閱世編)

What it is: A Songjiang writer's notes on his own prefecture, near today's Shanghai, from the last years of the Ming into the Kangxi reign, recording what land sold for a mu, what rent it was let at, and the year-by-year price of rice.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives land prices and rents for the same land in the same passage, from someone who watched them move, so a price can be set against its rent. Its figures are one observer's for one prefecture, recalled in round ranges; the prices he records are those of crises, the dear rice after the Ming fell, the tax burden of the early 1660s and the dear rice of 1680; and he does not say whether a rent was counted in husked rice or in unhusked grain, or what tax the owner paid out of it.

Used for: land prices against rents in Songjiang from the 1630s to 1680 (Money and prices)
