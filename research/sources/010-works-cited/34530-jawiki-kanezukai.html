jawiki-kanezukai

"金遣い" ("kanezukai", gold use), Japanese Wikipedia (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/金遣い)

What it is: A short encyclopedia article on the custom of reckoning in gold in eastern Japan, against silver in the west, in the Edo period, citing entries in three historical dictionaries (the Kokushi daijiten, the Nihonshi daijiten and the Nihon rekishi daijiten), and quoting the Kinsei fuzokushi, a record of Edo-period manners.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the border between the gold-using east and silver-using west, which it says cannot be drawn strictly, the two ways of quoting rice, and, as part of one theory of why the shogunate kept to gold, the tax of the Kanto's dry-field districts reckoned in ei, a unit of old copper coin at 1,000 mon to the ryo, and paid in money (its word, kinno, means payment in money rather than in kind, not gold coin as such). It says nothing of the Kanto's paddy tax, which the Heibonsha entry on nengu has paid in rice. It gives no dates for when the border held.

Used for: the regional split between gold and silver, and the Kanto's tax paid in gold (Money and prices)
