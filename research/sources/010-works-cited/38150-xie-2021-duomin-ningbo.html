Xie Yibiao (谢一彪), 《浙江堕民研究》 ("A study of the duomin of Zhejiang", title translated), Beijing: 中国社会科学出版社 (2021), chapter 2, section 3, 〈宁波堕民〉 ("The duomin of Ningbo", title translated) (https://m.yunqi.qq.com/read/40667624/18)

What it is: The section of a scholarly monograph on the duomin, the hereditary "beggar households" of Zhejiang, that lists where they lived in Ningbo and the counties under it - the city's West Gate, Fenghua, Cixi, Yuyao and others - from Ming and Qing writings, local gazetteers and twentieth-century recollections.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places several counties' quarters against their walls: outside the city at Fenghua in a Ming writer's words, outside the East Gate at Cixi, and at Ningbo's West Gate, where its own statement puts the quarter outside the gate and a report of 1896 it quotes has a fire begin inside the gate and burn the households' houses. The households were musicians, barbers, sedan-bearers and attendants at weddings and funerals, not tanners or handlers of the dead, though the modern Xiangshan gazetteer it quotes also names butchery among the men's trades; much of its siting rests on recollections of the twentieth century, and only the passages it quotes from Ming and Qing texts are evidence of that date; at Fenghua it says that by the Republican era the households had no fixed quarter but lodged rent-free in the temples and ancestral halls they kept, and at Cixi the East Gate site is the author's paraphrase beside a note on the Guangxu-reign gazetteer, its distance (one or two li from the town) taken from a present-day writer's fieldwork; it gives no reason for any quarter's place.

Used for: the beggar households' quarters outside the walls of Fenghua and Cixi, and Ningbo's quarter at its West Gate
