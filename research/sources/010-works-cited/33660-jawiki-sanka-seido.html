jawiki-sanka-seido

"江戸時代の三貨制度" ("The three-currency system of the Edo period"), Japanese Wikipedia (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三貨制度)

What it is: An encyclopedia article on the Edo period's gold, silver and copper currencies, their official rates and their market rates over time, with single-day price lists drawn from published price histories.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the official rate of 1700, Osaka's rates on one day in 1766, citing the Mitsui Bunko's price study, and Kyoto's in the first month of 1867, citing a history of the money-changers. Its rates are single dates, and its account of the long fall of copper is a summary without figures by year. The 1700 rate was decreed for payments to the shogunate, and the article itself says the market did not follow it: silver stayed near 50 monme to the ryō until the Hōei recoinage. The 1867 rate falls in the inflation that followed the opening of the ports in 1859. For the end of the system, it dates the abolition of silver reckoning to the fifth month of 1868, three years before the New Currency Act of 1871 made the yen equal to the ryō, so a price in silver monme already crosses a break in 1868. It says domain notes were redeemed from 1872, each at its own domain's market rate, and gives none of those rates.

Used for: the official rate of 1700 and the Osaka rate of 1766; the New Currency Act of 1871 and domain notes, as breaks a borrowed price is not converted across (Money and prices)
