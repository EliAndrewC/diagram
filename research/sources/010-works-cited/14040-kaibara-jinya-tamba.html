kaibara-jinya-tamba

Tamba City, 柏原藩陣屋跡 ("The site of the Kaibara domain's jin'ya", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.city.tamba.lg.jp/soshiki/shakaikyoikubunkazaika/gyomuannai/7/1/2441.html)

What it is: The Tamba city education board's page on the jin'ya of the Kaibara domain, a 20,000-koku domain without a castle, quoting the text of its 1971 designation as a national historic site (the gate is also, separately, a prefectural cultural property) and describing the surviving buildings, among them the rowhouse gate of the 1710s with its rooms.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures a rowhouse gate of a small lord's seat and lists its rooms, which is the narrow rowhouse form a staff range is drawn in. Its limits: it gives two figures for the gate's length (thirteen and a half ken in the designation text, thirteen ken, one shaku and two sun in the city's own description), and it describes the rooms twice, the designation text by their mats and the city by their use, the two agreeing on which side holds which. The city also judges the gate altered: its north end is not original, and the range once ran further north (a drawing of the jin'ya shows the rear gate in the same building), so both lengths are of a shortened gate. Both lengths and the depth are given in ken, shaku and sun only, with no metric figure, and the page does not say how long its ken is, so any length in metres is the record's own conversion, not the page's. Its horse-viewing room, now plank-floored, was probably once matted. The front hall is not of the gate's date: it burned in 1818 and was rebuilt in 1820, and the designation text says only part of it survives (the entrance and its step, the two shoin rooms, the envoys' room and its anteroom, and the corridor round them), the naka-no-kuchi and the large halls having been rebuilt after 1914 and other rooms pulled down in its years as a school. The city's longer list of the rooms round the entrance names rooms the designation's list does not, and the page does not say which of them are of 1820. Its cypress-bark gable roof is the one re-laid in 2005-2006, and the page does not date that material earlier than the roof it replaced. And a domain lord's seat is not a county magistracy.

Used for: the depth and length of a jin'ya's rowhouse gate and the rooms in it; the front hall's form and the rooms round its formal entrance, among them the envoys' rooms; the cypress-bark roof of the front entrance's cusped gable (Estates and other compounds)
