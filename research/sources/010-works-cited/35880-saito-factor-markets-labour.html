saito-factor-markets-labour

Osamu Saito, "Factor markets and their institutions in traditional Japan (I): Labour", a paper for the sixth Global Economic History Network conference, London School of Economics (https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/GEHNConferences/conf6/Conf6-Saito.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's draft survey of Japan's labor markets before industry, with tables from the 1879 pilot census of Yamanashi (the Kai no kuni genzai ninbetsu shirabe) on how many farm people also had a side occupation.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives shares, by sex, of farm people with side trades, the nearest the record holds to a split of a farm household's work between farming and other work. It is marked "Draft. Please do not cite"; its census is of 1879, eleven years after the Meiji Restoration and in a silk district; and it counts people with a side trade, not the days or hours they spent at it. Its census also leaves raising silkworms out of the side trades, so its quarter of farm people with one is a floor; Saito says the share would be "much higher" with sericulture counted.

Used for: the share of farm men and women with a side trade in Yamanashi in 1879 (Money and prices)
