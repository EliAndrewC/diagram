sano-jinya-fukushige

Fukushige Keiko, 北関東における譜代藩飛地領の支配と在地役人 - 彦根藩下野佐野領を事例に (The government of a hereditary domain's detached territory in the northern Kanto and its local officials: the Hikone domain's Sano territory in Shimotsuke), お茶の水史学 66 (in Japanese; https://teapot.lib.ocha.ac.jp/record/2003870/files/shigaku_66_01_fukushige.pdf)

What it is: A historian's paper on how the Hikone domain governed its detached territory at Sano, far from its castle, from the domain's records and its local officials' family papers, from the territory's founding in 1633 into the nineteenth century (its staff tables are of 1695 and 1805, its list of the jin'ya's buildings of 1739): the jin'ya at Sano, its buildings, and the local officials (zaichi yakunin), from its intendants to its foot soldiers, who staffed it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source read that says, for one office, both what stood on its grounds and where its staff actually lived. Its limits are that Sano was a domain's detached territory staffed by its local officials, not a shogunate intendancy staffed by the intendant's clerks, and that the paper itself calls Sano unusual even among domains' detached territories, most of which were run by domain samurai posted in rotation. Its staff lived in the town because they were local men with homes there, so it does not show where clerks posted in from elsewhere lodged.

Used for: the rowhouses on the Sano jin'ya's grounds in 1739, and its staff living in the town around it (Estates and other compounds)
