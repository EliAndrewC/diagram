新版歌舞伎事典 (Heibonsha; served on JapanKnowledge), 地方の歌舞伎 ("Regional Kabuki", title translated) (https://japanknowledge.com/contents/kabuki/sousetsu/08chiho.html)

What it is: The article on provincial and village kabuki in the kabuki encyclopedia JapanKnowledge serves openly : the local plays performed by village youth at the tutelary shrine's festival and other rites, on stages that in many villages stood permanently in the shrine precinct.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests permanent stages on shrine ground, the origin of the shrine-ground theater our provincial towns carry. Its limit is that it describes the village, and a stage rather than a roofed playhouse. The article, signed by the kabuki scholar Hattori Yukio, puts village kabuki's peak in the late Edo period and into Meiji, later than most of what these maps draw from. For the money question it says only that a village could buy in (買芝居, 請芝居) a troupe of local actors from nearby villages, and that a village play ran for one day or three; it gives no fee and does not say how a troupe was paid.

Used for: permanent stages in village shrine precincts for the local kabuki; how long a village play ran and who played it, and the bought-in troupes (Money and prices)
