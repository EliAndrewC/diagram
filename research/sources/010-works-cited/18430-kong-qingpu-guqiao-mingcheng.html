Kong Qingpu, "Ancient Chinese stone arch bridges: the renaming of the parts of an old bridge", Mao Yisheng Science and Education Foundation, Ancient Bridges Committee, October 2009 (in Chinese; https://www.mysf.org.cn/Detail/index.html?id=691&aid=291)

What it is: A short piece by Kong Qingpu, a Beijing bridge engineer and historian of old bridges, published by a bridge-engineering foundation, copying out the list of names for the parts of a traditional stone arch bridge that Beijing's construction bureau drafted and the national highway bureau promulgated on 1951-12-01, with the older name of each part beside the new one.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows that the abutment and its walls were distinct, named parts of a premodern Chinese bridge, the names it marks as old ("anciently", "old books use") being the pre-1951 ones. Its limits: it is about stone arch bridges, not timber ones; it gives no dates or sources for the old names beyond calling them old; by its own account the old names were too many and too mixed to be useful, which was why they were replaced, and this list was drafted in Beijing, so the old names it gives are one set among several (probably northern, late-imperial usage), not the only premodern names; the same old names were used for parts of a pier as well; and it gives no dimensions, so it says nothing about how far an abutment sits from the water.

Used for: the old Chinese names for an abutment's front wall and wing walls (Ways)
