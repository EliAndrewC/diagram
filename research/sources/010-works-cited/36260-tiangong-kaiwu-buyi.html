tiangong-kaiwu-buyi

Song Yingxing, 《天工開物》 (Tiangong kaiwu, 1637), the section 「布衣」 ("Cloth garments", title translated) of the chapter "乃服", on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/天工開物/布衣)

What it is: The short section on cotton in the late Ming encyclopedia of crafts: its kinds, the spinning and weaving, and where weaving and finishing were best done.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states, in 1637, that cotton cloth for keeping out the cold was the same for high and low and grown across the realm. These are one writer's general statements with no count behind them, and his line that a loom is sure to be found in any ten houses is a manner of speaking. "The same for high and low" means that both wore cotton, not that their winter clothes matched: the same chapter's section on furs ("裘") grades them by price and rank, from sable and fox down to sheepskin. It speaks for late Ming China only, and cannot be carried back to earlier dynasties or across to Japan.

Used for: cotton as winter cloth for all ranks by 1637 (Money and prices)
