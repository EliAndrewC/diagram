nerima-kushi-meisaicho

Nerima Ward, 「Nerima Ward History, History volume: part 7, historical documents」 (translated; original: 「練馬区史 歴史編 第七部 歴史資料編」) (in Japanese; https://www2.i-repository.net/contents/myc/text_2rekishihen/rekishihen_honcho7.xhtml)

What it is: The documents part of Nerima ward's official history, published online as text: published by the ward in 1982: transcriptions of the detailed returns (murameisaichō) of Kami-Nerima, Doshida, Seki and other villages now in the ward, from 1720 to 1876 (most of them Edo-period, a few early Meiji), from originals held by local families, with extracts from the shogunate's gazetteer of Musashi, from Edo-period topographies and travel diaries, and from Meiji and later gazetteers.

Why it applies, and its limits: The returns are primary Edo records, filed by each village's headman with its intendant, listing the shrines and temples with their exempt land in tan, se and bu, and some halls' sizes; the smallest shrines have no exempt land and appear only as plots of about ten or twenty tsubo outside the survey. They are two villages of one district near Edo; an exempt-land figure can include fields held for a shrine, so it is the shrine's precinct or more, not the precinct itself; and the history prints the returns' columns as running text, so a figure is not always plainly tied to one shrine. It is a modern transcription, and where an original is damaged (the Doshida return of 1804 most of all) characters are lost or restored by the editors in brackets. For the reservoir, the returns of 1804, 1831 and 1850 repeat the same clause, differing only in the spelling of the place and the district: "about 7 cho of paddy" watered from a reservoir of about 3 tan on the Kogure boundary. The registered paddy changes from return to return (11.9, 7.4, then 2.4 cho), and the 1850 return itself registers only 2 cho 4 tan. So the 7 cho is a carried-forward phrase, not an 1850 measurement. The same clause says the reservoir runs dry in drought and that the paddy is on the whole rain-fed, so the reservoir added to rain rather than watering the paddy alone. It is also a village of upland dry fields (170 to 370 cho of dry fields against 2 to 12 cho of paddy), not a wet-rice lowland.

Used for: Edo village shrines' exempt lands, 1804 to 1831, as a check on the precinct band (Religion and the dead); Doshida's 7 cho of paddy watered from one small reservoir, 1850 (Fields).
