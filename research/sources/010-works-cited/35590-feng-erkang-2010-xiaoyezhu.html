冯尔康 (Feng Erkang), 「乾嘉之际小业主的经济状况和社会生活 ——兼述嘉庆朝刑科题本档案史料的价值」 ("The economic condition and social life of small proprietors around the Qianlong-Jiaqing transition", title translated), posted on sinoss.net, 2010 (in Chinese; https://www.sinoss.net/uploadfile/2010/1130/4960.pdf)

What it is: A social historian's survey of small masters, shopkeepers and farmers who hired labor, around 1800, drawn from the Board of Punishments' case reports of the Jiaqing reign (1796-1820), trade by trade.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source found on how many hands a Qing smithy hired. Its cases are homicides that grew out of land and debt disputes, and it gives no smith's takings. On smiths it has only two cases: a Shanghai smithy working to order, with no hired hand named, and a Henan smith who in 1785 hired one helper when work piled up. Its "one or two, at most two or three" hired hands is the author's impression from some four hundred cases of every trade, not a count, and he leaves out by definition any master who hired more, so it cannot rule out larger smithies.

Used for: the hired hands of Qing smithies (Money and prices)
