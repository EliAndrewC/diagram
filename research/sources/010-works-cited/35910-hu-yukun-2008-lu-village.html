hu-yukun-2008-lu-village

Hu Yukun (胡玉坤), a review of Laurel Bossen's Chinese Women and Rural Development: Sixty Years of Change in Lu Village, Yunnan (2002), on Aisixiang (in Chinese; https://www.aisixiang.com/data/11673.html)

What it is: A 2006 review (in 《中国图书评论》, reposted on Aisixiang in 2008) by the book's Chinese translator, a Peking University population scholar, of a McGill University anthropologist's restudy of Lu Village, the Yunnan village Fei Xiaotong surveyed in 1938, summarizing both studies' findings on women's farm work.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports, from Fei's 1938 survey, that women did more of the staple-crop work than men there and usually earned half a man's day pay. It is third-hand - the reviewer summarizing Bossen summarizing Fei - and one poor village of southwest China in the 1930s, where many men had left farming. The review itself presents that as a modern change: women pushed into the fields after factory goods displaced their home spinning and weaving from the late Qing. So it does not show the work of a household that still spun and wove. It also sets Fei's village against Buck's survey of 1929-1933, which found women doing little field work in the rice south and almost none in the wheat north. And the half pay is what Fei's informants told him, a rate the review says was not fixed.

Used for: women's farm work and pay in Lu Village, 1938 (Money and prices)
