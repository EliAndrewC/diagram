shigematsu-2022

Shigematsu Toshihiko, "江戸時代日本人の食糧事情" ("Edo jidai Nihonjin no shokuryo jijo", The food situation of the Japanese in the Edo period, title translated), a monthly lecture printed in Transactions of the Research Institute of Oceanochemistry (海洋化学研究) vol. 35 no. 1, April 2022, pp. 50-56 (in Japanese; https://www.oceanochemistry.org/publications/TRIOC/PDF/trioc_2022_35_50.pdf)

What it is: The printed text of a 2021 lecture to the Kyoto Chemists' Club by a professor emeritus of Konan University, estimating how much grain a Japanese person ate in a year in the late Edo and early Meiji periods. It works from farm labor energy, from domain and household grain figures (Morioka, Sendai, Hirosaki, Saga and others), and from 1870s-1890s grain output and population.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a yearly grain intake of about 1.8 koku per person, or 2.0 koku counting grain used for sake and koji, and sets it beside the figures it gathers: Morioka 1653 (over 2.0 koku), Sendai 1754 and Hirosaki 1816 (1.8-1.9 koku), Saga 1844 (1.2 koku rice and 0.6 koku barley), and a 1772 farm manual's household (1.5 koku of rice and barley, over 2.0 with millets and soybeans). Its method leans on metabolic surveys of farmers made in 1900-1950 to stand for Edo labor, and the author himself calls the four domain figures few, differing in date and region. Its two miso figures are of different scale. The first is 0.13 koku of soybeans a person a year in the 1760 household precepts (Kakun zensho) of Yoda Sozo of Katakura village, Saku district, Shinano: one household's allowance, whose grain (1.8 koku of brown rice and 1 koku of barley a head, nearly 3 koku) runs well above the lecture's own 1.8 koku. The second is 14,000 koku for the whole population of Hirosaki domain, 200,000 people, in 1816 (about 0.07 koku a person, by division): a domain-wide average that the lecture quotes at second hand from Kikuchi Toshio (1986). Neither original was read for the record.

Used for: the soybeans set aside for miso a person a year in a Shinano village in 1760 and in Hirosaki domain in 1816 (Money and prices)
