kotobank-koshibai

Kotobank, 小芝居 ("Small theater", title translated), the entries of several dictionaries, among them the Nipponica encyclopedia's, by Moriya Takeshi (https://kotobank.jp/word/小芝居-64729)

What it is: A dictionary page on the small theaters of the Edo period, the theaters outside the licensed great ones: shrine-ground plays, booth plays and the like, and how they differed from the great theaters.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its Nipponica entry, by Moriya Takeshi, says the small theaters played a limited number of days under a license sought each time, but gives no number; the 100 days on the page (the "hundred-day theater") is from the Heibonsha entry by Abe Yūzō, a limit that came in gradually, and the rule changed over the period (at first booths only, later permanent houses; banned outright in 1714 and 1841-43). Moriya's counts, 14 places in Kyoto in the Genroku era (1688-1704) and twenty-odd in Edo in the early 1800s, are tallies of known sites over a span of years, not theaters open in one year, and he gives no source for them. It counts no theaters in a provincial city.

Used for: the small theaters' licensed days, and how many there were in Kyoto and Edo (Money and prices)
