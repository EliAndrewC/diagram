yao-2011-edo-sato-shokubunka

Yao Keisuke (八百啓介), "江戸時代の砂糖食文化" ("The culture of eating sugar in the Edo period", title translated), a short article dated January 2011 on the sugar pages of the Agriculture and Livestock Industries Corporation (ALIC), a Japanese government agency (in Japanese; https://www.alic.go.jp/joho-s/joho07_000217.html)

What it is: A professor of the University of Kitakyushu, writing for the general reader, on the sugar that Chinese and Dutch ships brought to Nagasaki: how much came, what became of it, and what it cost. It sets the price of imported white sugar at the Dutch post on Dejima beside its retail price in Kyoto in 1692, the Kyoto price on the word of the post's physician Engelbert Kaempfer, and gives prices for the years after 1697 and for 1808.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one statement found of an imported good's price where it was landed beside its price in a city of central Japan in the same year, with the carriage between them. It carries no footnotes: the Kyoto price is credited to Kaempfer, without a title or a page; a few import figures (the peak of 1759, the gifts from 1715) are credited to the Dutch post's account books; and the other prices are credited to nothing. The article names its sources only in general: the post's account books, the records of Nagasaki and of the Saga and Fukuoka domains, and the records of residents of Edo. None of them was read. Its two prices of 1692 are at different stages, an import price and a retail price, and its later figures set an auction or a post's price beside retail prices whose place or year it does not always give.

Used for: imported white sugar at Dejima and at retail in Kyoto in 1692, and the later prices beside them (Money and prices)
