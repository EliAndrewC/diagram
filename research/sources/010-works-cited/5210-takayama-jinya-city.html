takayama-jinya-city

Takayama City, 高山陣屋跡 ("site of Takayama Jin'ya, the town's magistrate's office", title translated) (https://www.city.takayama.lg.jp/kurashi/1000021/1000119/1000847/1000954/1000956.html)

What it is: The city of Takayama's official page for 高山陣屋跡 (the site of the Takayama Jin'ya), the one surviving office and compound in Japan of a Tokugawa intendant (daikan, later raised to gundai), who governed the whole of Hida for the shogunate. It is published by the city's cultural-properties section, but the site is owned by Gifu Prefecture (the front plaza alone by the city) and managed by the prefecture's Board of Education. It lists the buildings and how they are built, notes the one structure moved in from the castle, the rice storehouse (1695), and gives the extent of the protected ground.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the strongest source in the record for what a provincial office contained: a whole compound described building by building, with dates, by the city it stands in - the examining hall and its court, laid with cobble and roofed, the rice storehouse moved from the castle, the office range of 1816 - and its designated area. Its limits are that this is the only survivor of more than fifty such offices, in a mountain province under direct shogunal rule, and one raised to the higher rank of gundai, its entrance hall dressed to a 100,000-koku standard, so it is grander than an ordinary intendant's office; that its main buildings served as government offices from Meiji until 1969, and what stands is the 1970-1983 restoration, with the intendant's residence rebuilt in fiscal 1992-1995 from the results of an excavation, so a feature is Edo work only where the page dates it (the gate, the office range, the genkan room, the storehouses); and that the area figure, 11,219 square meters, is an exact sum of ten surveyed parcels under a modern legal boundary accumulated across four designations between 1929 and 1989, not a measured Edo footprint - so the compound area our maps derive from it, the total less the front plaza, carries the hedge rather than the source.

Used for: the 吟味所 standing with the 白州 (「The ginmisho (examination hall) and the shirasu are characteristically laid with guri stone (coarse cobble ballast) and roofed.」 (translated; original: 「吟味所、白州はグリ石敷で屋根のあることが特徴的である」)); the 御蔵 moved from the castle's third bailey in 1695; the designated site figure 「Number/quantity: 11,219.05 square meters」 (translated; original: 「員数 11,219.05平方メートル」) (員数 is the listing's field for a designated site's extent; the ten parcels the page lists sum to it exactly); the genkan room surviving as rebuilt in 1816 and the low step kept for visitors of high rank (Estates and other compounds)
