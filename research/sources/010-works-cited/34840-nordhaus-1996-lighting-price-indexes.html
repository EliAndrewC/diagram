William D. Nordhaus, "Do Real-Output and Real-Wage Measures Capture Reality? The History of Lighting Suggests Not", in Timothy F. Bresnahan and Robert J. Gordon (eds.), The Economics of New Goods, University of Chicago Press for the NBER, 1996, chapter c6064 (https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c6064/c6064.pdf)

What it is: An economist's measure of the true price of light from early lamps to the electric bulb, set against the price indexes of the last two centuries, with a rough sorting of all consumption by how much it has changed since about 1800.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows which goods an ordinary price index carries well over two centuries and which it does not, the question behind carrying a basket that far. Its sorting of goods is, in its own words, extremely rough, and its evidence is chiefly American and British.

Used for: which goods a price index carries over two centuries and which it misses (Money and prices)
