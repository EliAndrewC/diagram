mingshi-78-fuyi

明史 (History of the Ming, title translated), volume 78, 食貨二, 賦役 ("Food and goods, part 2: taxes and labor service"), Wikisource text (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明史/卷78)

What it is: The treatise on the Ming land tax and labor service in the official dynastic history finished in 1739, from the first registers to the surcharges of 1618 to 1639.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the 1436 commutation into silver of some four million shi of the grain tax of seven southern provinces, later extended empire-wide except for the grain forwarded by army transport, and the Single Whip, tried on and off in the Jiajing reign and applied throughout from 1581, which set the tax in silver; the same chapter still lists the Wanli-era taxes as mainly rice and wheat. It also gives the founder's order that holdings of five to ten mu each plant half a mu of mulberry, hemp and cotton, and double above ten mu, with cloth owed by those who did not plant, and it names cotton and cotton cloth among the tax items of the Hongzhi reign. It states the rules more than how far each county carried them out: it offers no count of counties, and it says itself that after a little over ten years the Single Whip's rules had fallen into disorder and could not be fully followed. It gives per-mu rates and tax categories for cotton but no total grown or paid. It was finished nearly a century after the dynasty fell, from drafts begun within decades of its end.

Used for: the Ming land tax set in silver; the founding order to plant cotton and its place among the taxes (Money and prices)
