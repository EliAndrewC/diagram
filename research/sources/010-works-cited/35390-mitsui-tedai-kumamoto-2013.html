mitsui-tedai-kumamoto-2013

Kumamoto Prefecture, statistics section, 「熊本の統計情報」 ("Kumamoto statistics", title translated), the monthly page of 25 January 2013 on the pay of a great merchant house's clerks in the Edo period (in Japanese; the Internet Archive's copy, https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://www.pref.kumamoto.jp/uploaded/attachment/13947.pdf)

What it is: A three-page popular piece by a prefecture's statistics office, setting the yearly silver pay of the clerks of a great Kyoto merchant house, from a first-year clerk to a sixth-year manager, beside a present-day starting wage. Its table is taken, it says, from a labor-ministry newsletter, which took it from Nishizaka Yasushi's study of the clerks of the Mitsui house's Echigoya shop in Kyoto.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only readable copy of the clerks' pay ladder found, and it reaches it at third hand: the page labels the table its source's estimate of pay in the first half of the 1800s, gives no single year, and says it is excerpted and reformatted. It is one great house, Japan's largest draper, whose clerks were better placed than a small shop's; its notes on how the pay was handed out are the page's own summary, not the study's words.

Used for: a great merchant house's clerks' yearly pay by rank and year of service, early 1800s (Money and prices)
