陶小军 (Tao Xiaojun), 《近代书画润例的商业广告与文化认同：以《申报》刊载为中心》 ("The commercial advertising and cultural identity of modern fee schedules for painting and calligraphy, centered on those printed in the Shenbao", title translated), Academic Monthly 57:8 (August 2025), pp. 160-167 (in Chinese; https://www.xsyk021.com/fileXSYK/journal/article/xsyk/2025/8/PDF/xsyk-2025-8-160.pdf)

What it is: A study of the price lists Chinese painters and calligraphers posted for their work, mostly those printed in the Shenbao newspaper, which opens with their forerunners, among them Zheng Banqiao's list of 1759.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes in full Zheng Banqiao's list of 1759, with its prices in silver by format: the only calligrapher's price list of the period the record has read with its prices. The article names an earlier one, Li Rihua's of 1629, priced by format and number of characters and printed in his notes (《六研斋笔记》), which the record has not read. It quotes Zheng's list at second hand from a collected edition of 1979, though the stone it was carved on survives in the Yangzhou Museum. The list is one celebrated artist's, in Yangzhou, a rich salt merchants' city at the top of the art market. It prices calligraphy and painting together by format, with no separate price for writing. Its line "paper six feet high, three thousand" is a verse in copper cash, not part of the list. And it has no line for a plaque.

Used for: what a noted calligrapher charged (Money and prices)
