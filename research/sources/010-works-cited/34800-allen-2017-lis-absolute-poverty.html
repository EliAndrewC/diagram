Robert C. Allen, "Absolute Poverty: When Necessity Displaces Desire", LIS Working Paper Series No. 706, Luxembourg Income Study, July 2017 (https://www.lisdatacenter.org/wps/liswps/706.pdf)

What it is: The working-paper version, of July 2017, of the economic historian's basic-needs poverty line published that year in the American Economic Review, costing nutrition, housing, clothing, fuel and lighting in each country's own prices.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sets the floor space of a basic-needs line, 3 m² a person, and the rents per square meter it found, and says how it chose them. Its 3 m² is the author's own choice, argued from the crowding of city slums (Bombay mill workers in 1921, Ahmedabad in the 1920s, Shanghai in 1952, Nairobi today) and the United Nations' definitions of overcrowding, so it is a floor for crowded city lodging, not a measure of a premodern house. Its rents are those of 2011 in twenty countries: for six poor countries the World Bank's price survey of 'traditional' structures, and for the other fourteen, poor and rich alike, market rents in the poorest districts of capitals and other large cities, gathered from various sources and some for illegal flats, for dwellings that generally have electricity and indoor water; they show how a basic-needs line weighs housing against food, never what a room rents for in the setting. Its table of rents is an image this project could not read, so only the figures in its text are used; it is not the journal's final text, which was not read.

Used for: the housing allowance of a basic-needs poverty line and the rents of 2011 behind it (Money and prices)
