zeitlin-qing-theater-scandals

Judith Zeitlin (蔡九迪), 清代两起戏曲丑闻事件：1689和1874 ("Two theatrical scandals of the Qing: 1689 and 1874", title translated), Theatre Arts Communication vol. 1, Center for Theater Arts Collaboration, Binghamton University, Chinese text (https://www.binghamton.edu/centers/ctac/thea-comm/issues/vol1/zeitlininchinese.html)

What it is: A study by Judith Zeitlin, a scholar of Chinese theater, read here in a Chinese translation of her English chapter (in Lecercle and Ogawa, eds., Sophia University Press, 2021, pp. 163-182), of two disastrous performances in Qing Beijing, one of them the private staging of Hong Sheng's play in 1689 during a national mourning.

Why it applies, and its limits: It attests that staging a play during a national mourning was an offense in 1689, relaying the Cambridge History of Chinese Literature for the facts. It is one case: a private performance in a residence during the mourning for an empress (Empress Tong), and Zeitlin reports the consensus that the charge was a pretext in a factional attack, with only a few of the many guests punished. So it shows the rule existed and could be invoked, not how strictly or how generally plays stopped during a mourning. It gives no length for the mourning's ban on plays.

Used for: imperial mourning stopping plays in Qing Beijing (Money and prices)
