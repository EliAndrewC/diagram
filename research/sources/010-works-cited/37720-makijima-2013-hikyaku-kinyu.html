Makijima Takashi (巻島隆), "飛脚問屋京屋・嶋屋の金融機能―店卸勘定と手形の分析―" ("The financial work of the courier houses Kyoya and Shimaya: an analysis of a stock-taking and of bills", title translated), 逓信総合博物館研究紀要 (Bulletin of the Communications Museum) 4, March 2013 (in Japanese; https://www.postalmuseum.jp/publication/research/docs/research_04_03.pdf)

What it is: A study of two Edo courier houses' branches in Kozuke province (Joshu) from 1804 to 1866, from a branch's stock-taking of 1839 and from the bills of exchange and deposit notes its customers kept. It shows the couriers carrying thread and cloth to Kyoto and paying the country shipper at once against a bill on the Kyoto wholesaler or, in ten of one shipper's twelve bills, on an Edo house that the author supposes settled with the Kyoto wholesaler.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows how the silk districts' thread was sent and paid for: by courier, against bills, with the freight settled twice a year. Its sums of freight are one branch's totals at a single stock-taking, which the author takes for a midsummer reckoning, with no rate for a bale or a load, and it gives no road and no days; its cases are Joshu's, with one deposit note from the Fukushima district, which shows only a 1,000-ryo deposit standing behind silk-thread shipments and names no destination or wholesaler. Its bills are one family's, the Hatori of Shibukawa village, and nearly all for silk cloth, with thread in only a few orders; that the courier advanced purchase money to a buying agent and was paid its Kyoto and Osaka freight at midsummer and year-end rests on one note of 1819 from Omama.

Used for: how thread went from Joshu and Fukushima to Kyoto's wholesalers and how it was paid for (Money and prices)
