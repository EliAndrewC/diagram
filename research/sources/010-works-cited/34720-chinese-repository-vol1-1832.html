"Gutzlaff's Journal", The Chinese Repository, vol. 1 (Canton, May 1832-April 1833), no. 5 (September 1832), p. 186, archive.org copy (https://archive.org/details/chinesereposito10unkngoog)

What it is: The first volume of the English-language monthly published by the foreign missionaries at Canton; the passage used is from the missionary Karl Gützlaff's journal of his voyage of 1831 up the coast to Tianjin aboard a Chinese junk, printed a year later.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a traveler's figure for cash to the tael at Tianjin, the only rate read for a northern port in these years. It is one foreign visitor's statement of a range (1,300 to 1,400 cash), seen during his stay at Tianjin in the autumn of 1831 (he left on 17 October), not dated to a day, and with no word on which cash or which tael.

Used for: cash to the tael at Tianjin, autumn 1831 (Money and prices)
