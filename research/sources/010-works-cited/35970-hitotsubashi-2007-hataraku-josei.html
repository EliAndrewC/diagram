Hitotsubashi University Library, TZ〈ほんの窓〉 no. 11, 「働く女性の江戸時代」 ("Working women in the Edo period", title translated), February 2007 (in Japanese; https://www.lib.hit-u.ac.jp/images/2019/12/tz_011.pdf)

What it is: A two-page newsletter from a university library's book-introduction group, by a librarian, surveying Japanese books on Edo-period women's labor and women's history from the 1980s to 2006, with each book's call number.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says why historians of Edo-period women have had to pick women out of administrative records written around male household heads, and names the records they read: village headmen's diaries, temple population registers and prescriptive texts. It reports the historians' findings in a sentence each, so it is a guide to their arguments, not their evidence, and it ends in 2006; its line on Duby and Perrot's history of women is about Europe.

Used for: why Edo-period records show few women, and the records historians read for them (Money and prices)
