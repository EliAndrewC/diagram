Henry Yule and A. C. Burnell, Hobson-Jobson, the letter S, the Wikisource transcription of the enlarged edition with William Crooke's additions (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Hobson-Jobson/S)

What it is: The standard glossary of Anglo-Indian words (Yule and Burnell, 1886; new edition by William Crooke, 1903), here its entry on the sweet potato.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the plant was grown in most parts of India when the glossary was written in the 1880s, what it was called, and how its names overlapped with the potato and the yam. That is one clause without a count or a region, written of British India with its railways, so it gives only a latest date for the crop being common. Its names are regional: shakar-kand is given for the Bengal Presidency, and rat-alu is the yam in Bengal and the sweet potato in the Deccan. It gives no date for its arrival in India or for its becoming common there.

Used for: The sweet potato in India (Money and prices)
