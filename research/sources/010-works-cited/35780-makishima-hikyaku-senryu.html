makishima-hikyaku-senryu

巻島隆 (Makishima Takashi), 「飛脚への眼差し ―近世文芸・芸能・伝説から探る―」 ("How couriers were seen: from early modern literature, performance and legend", title translated), 郵政博物館研究紀要 (Bulletin of the Postal Museum) 9 (in Japanese; https://www.postalmuseum.jp/publication/research/research_09_02.pdf)

What it is: A postal historian's reading of how Edo-period comic fiction, haikai, comic verse (senryu), kyoka, kabuki, rakugo and the fox-courier legends portrayed the courier houses and their couriers.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one statement found of how a Japanese cash courier protected what he carried, but as the author's gloss on a comic verse, not a record of a courier house's rules; the verse is undated in the passage read.

Used for: how a cash courier went armed (Money and prices)
