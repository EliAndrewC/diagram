Alfred W. Crosby, The Columbian Exchange: Biological and Cultural Consequences of 1492, chapter 5, "New World Foods and Old World Demography" (ABC-CLIO edition, copyright 2003), PDF on the University of Warwick's food history pages (https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/arts/history/research/centres/foodhistory/the_columbian_exchange_biological_and_cultural_con..._----_5_new_world_foods_and_old_world_demography_.pdf)

What it is: A chapter by an environmental historian surveying what American food plants did to the populations of Europe, Africa and Asia, with notes naming the works each passage draws on.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its few pages on China say that most of their remarks come from Ping-ti Ho's population study, and its paragraphs on Japan rest on older works in Western languages, so it reports East Asia at second hand. Its latest statistics are from the early 1960s, and it gives one Japanese story as "so one story goes". It predates the later scholarship that disputes how much these crops mattered.

Used for: What set the pace of new crops (Money and prices)
