oishi-1966-tenpo13-bukka-kakiage

大石慎三郎 (Oishi Shinzaburo), 「〈資料紹介〉天保13年8月"銭相場公定に伴う物価引下げ令"による「物価書上」について」 ("Source introduction: the price returns made under the price-reduction order that came with the fixing of the copper rate, Tenpo 13 [1842], 8th month", title translated), 学習院大学経済論集 2-2 (1966), pp. 69-128 (in Japanese; https://glim-re.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/202/files/keizai_2_2_69_128.pdf)

What it is: A historian's edition of the Edo price returns of 1842, opening with a table of the market rate of copper coin in mon to the gold ryo, two figures a year from 1751 to the fifth month of 1842, nearly all with their months from 1781, which he takes from Mitsui Takasumi's 1933 table of the gold, silver and copper rates of the three cities "centered on Edo", and going on to print the returns themselves, trade by trade, each item at its old and its reduced price.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one public printed series of the copper rate in Edo's own form of quotation found for these years. It is a copy of Mitsui's table, not of the money-changers' books, with ranges averaged and fractions dropped by its editor; that its rates are Edo's is this record's reading of the table's title and form, since the paper does not say so in those words; and it stops in 1842, when the official rate replaced the market. Its ryo is each year's gold coin, not a fixed weight of gold: the span crosses the Bunsei recoinage (koban from 1819) and the Tenpo koban (1837), each holding less gold than the coin before it, so a change in the mon to the ryo is partly a change in the ryo. Its mon is likewise a count of coin at face value, not a weight of copper: iron one-mon pieces (cast from 1739), brass four-mon pieces (from 1768) and the hundred-mon Tenpo coin (from 1835) all passed in it, so a change in the rate can be a change in the mon as well. Its returns are what the ward headmen reported the trades had cut their prices to under an order, not prices seen paid; its charcoal return gives grades by the bale but no bale weight, and does not say whether the prices are a shop's or a wholesaler's.

Used for: what copper coin fetched in gold in Edo, 1781-1842, and Edo's charcoal prices by the bale in 1842 (Money and prices)
