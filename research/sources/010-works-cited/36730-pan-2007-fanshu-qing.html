Pan Honggang (潘洪钢), "番薯、满汉全席与清代社会" (The sweet potato, the Manchu-Han banquet and Qing society), first published in Lishi yuekan (歷史月刊) 235, August 2007, reposted at Aisixiang (in Chinese; https://www.aisixiang.com/data/22698.html)

What it is: A short article on two pieces of Qing food history, with a section on the spread of the sweet potato that quotes reports to the throne.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the memorials of 1725 and 1728 with their places, gives the prices of the 1728 one (the 1725 one gives none, saying only that rice was dear) and draws the conclusion about the poor of Fujian and Guangdong. The memorials were read only as he quotes them, and they describe coastal districts in those years, not a whole province; the 1728 prices are those of a full harvest. His list of the plant's names is his own and gives no date or place for any name, so it does not show which word a given time or region used, and he says the same words were confused with the native yam. He also reports that in Beijing after the Qianlong reign rich and poor alike ate it boiled as a delicacy, so "food of the poor" does not hold everywhere.

Used for: The sweet potato as a food of the poor in Qing China, and the words used for it (Money and prices)
