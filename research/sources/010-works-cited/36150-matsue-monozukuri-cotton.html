matsue-monozukuri-cotton

Koyama Shoko, "木綿" ("Cotton", title translated), pp. 21-22 of a City of Matsue local-history booklet on the crafts of the Matsue domain, in Japanese (https://www.city.matsue.lg.jp/material/files/group/32/history_mono_6.pdf)

What it is: Two pages signed by a member of the city's historical staff, from the city's booklet on Matsue's crafts, 『ものづくりのヒストリー』 (March 2024), on the cotton cloth of Izumo province, with a short national background. It ends on a list of three works: two city publications and a 2013 book on Izumo cotton.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one source read that places cotton as commoners' main clothing in the later Edo period, and the one that reports household weaving in Izumo by the late 1400s. The later-Edo statement is a passing clause in an account of the domain's cotton markets, with no year, no evidence and no region; the page's own account of where cotton was made runs from the Kanto to Kyushu, so the north is not covered. The Izumo date is the author's inference from the one work named for it, Kishizaki Sakuji's 田法記, written about two centuries later, in 1682. The arrival date is a bare "14th to 15th century" from the short national background, and it refers to imported luxury cloth; the page puts production in Japan only at the end of the 1400s.

Used for: when cotton arrived, when it became commoners' main clothing, and household weaving in Izumo (Money and prices)
