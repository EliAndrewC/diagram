Japan Tourism Agency, Oka House, multilingual interpretive text, prepared 2020 for the Iwami Ginzan council (in English, with the Japanese and other versions on the page; https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R2-00289.html)

What it is: The national tourism agency's interpretive text for the Oka house at Omori, a samurai residence designated a historic site in 1974, where officials serving under the Omori intendant, who governed the Iwami silver mine, lived in the Edo period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places an intendancy official's house in the town, not far from the office, rather than on its grounds. Its limits are that it names no date for the house's building, and speaks of two families who in turn held the post of overseer of the silver's storage, a well-paid post, not of the office's clerks.

Used for: a mid-ranking official of the Omori intendancy living in a house of his own in the town (Estates and other compounds)
