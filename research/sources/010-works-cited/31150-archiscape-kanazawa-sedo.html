archiscape-kanazawa-sedo

Kurasawa Satoru (Kurasawa Satoru Architectural Design Office), "武家屋敷の中の果樹園 背戸という空間" ("buke yashiki no naka no kajuen: seto to iu kūkan", an orchard inside the samurai residence: the space called the seto, title translated), blog Archiscape, posted 2020-04-29 (in Japanese; https://kurarc.exblog.jp/30027032)

What it is: A short post on an architect's blog summarizing a passage from Shimamura Noboru's book on Kanazawa's townscape in modern literature, on how the gardens of Kanazawa's samurai residences were used.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports, from that book, that a Kanazawa samurai residence had four kinds of garden (the front court, the kitchen yard, the viewing garden by the reception room and the rear seto for fruit and vegetables), that the lower the rank the more the seto served self-sufficiency, and that the orchards stayed after the Restoration and their fruit and vegetables were sold (apricot, plum, loquat, honey peach and pear named). It is a secondhand summary of one book with no sizes, quotation or page numbers, and speaks of Kanazawa, a large castle town, not of villages. That book reads the town through modern novels set in Kanazawa (the post names Izumi Kyōka, Murō Saisei and Tokuda Shūsei), and the post gives the fruit as planted in orchards it says stayed after the Restoration, dating none of them to the Edo period, so the fruit list may be of after the Restoration; the garden in flower is Shimamura's conjecture, which the post says he imagines. The four-garden layout it gives for the samurai residences "of that time" (当時), without saying which time; the post says only that the novels show a Kanazawa with what was left of Edo, so the layout may be the samurai residence as it survived into the novels' years, not one the post dates to the Edo period.

Used for: the kitchen yard apart from the rear garden of a samurai house (Estates and other compounds).
