archiscape-kanazawa-sedo

Kurasawa Satoru (Kurasawa Satoru Architectural Design Office), "武家屋敷の中の果樹園 背戸という空間" ("buke yashiki no naka no kajuen: seto to iu kūkan", an orchard inside the samurai residence: the space called the seto, title translated), blog Archiscape, posted 2020-04-29 (in Japanese; https://kurarc.exblog.jp/30027032)

What it is: A short post on an architect's blog summarizing a passage from Shimamura Noboru's book on Kanazawa's townscape in modern literature, on how the gardens of Kanazawa's samurai residences were used.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reports, from that book, that a Kanazawa samurai residence had four kinds of garden (the front court, the kitchen yard, the viewing garden by the reception room and the rear seto for fruit and vegetables), that the lower the rank the more the seto served self-sufficiency, and that the orchards stayed after the Restoration and their fruit and vegetables were sold (apricot, plum, loquat, honey peach and pear named). It is a secondhand summary of one book with no sizes, quotation or page numbers, and speaks of Kanazawa, a large castle town, not of villages. That book reads the town through Meiji and Taishō novels, so its orchard is partly post-Restoration and partly conjecture: the honey peach (suimitsutō) came to Japan only in 1875, and the garden in flower is its author's guess. The four-garden layout is the Edo form; the fruit list is not an Edo list.

Used for: the kitchen yard apart from the rear garden of a samurai house (Estates and other compounds).
