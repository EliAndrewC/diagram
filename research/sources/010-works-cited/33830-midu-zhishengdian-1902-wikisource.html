黃玉書 (Huang Yushu), 《至聖殿重修碑記》 (Stele on the rebuilding of the Hall of the Supreme Sage, title translated), 1902, transcribed on Chinese Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/至聖殿重修碑記)

What it is: The text of a stele set up in 1902 at Midu in Yunnan, now in the county library, recording the rebuilding of the town's hall of Confucius after its burning in the 1850s, with the whole account of money received and spent, line by line, in copper cash.

Why it applies, and its limits: It prices the furnishing of a small Qing temple hall item by item: tablets, niche and lattices, painting, gilding, plinths, an incense burner and the carving of the stele, and gives its own rate of silver to cash. It names no wage rate and no count of days, only lump sums of labor money, several lines lump materials and labor or several items together, and it is a market town's hall to Confucius, built by local elders because the town lay far from the official temple and rebuilt with the sub-prefect's and the academy's funds, not a county seat's state temple, in the last decade of the Qing (Yunnan had no railway until 1910). Its rate of 1,300 cash to the tael (both conversions on the stone give it) is one frontier province's rate in one year, when rates varied by province, and is neither the empire's rate nor the par of 1,000. It gives no size of the hall, and contracts out its main hall, front hall and two wings as lump sums, so its sums cannot be scaled to a hall of another size. The transcription marks a few characters of the stone as unreadable.

Used for: what a Qing hall's tablets, niche, lattices, painting and gilding cost, as a comparison (Money and prices)
