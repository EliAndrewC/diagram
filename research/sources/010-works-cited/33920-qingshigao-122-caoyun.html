Zhao Erxun (趙爾巽) and others, Qing shi gao (清史稿, Draft History of the Qing), chapter 122, "Food and money 3: grain transport" (食貨三 漕運), the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/清史稿/卷122)

What it is: The chapter of the draft dynastic history, compiled in 1914-1927, on how the Qing moved the tribute grain of the south to the capital: by the Grand Canal, by river lighters near Tianjin, and from 1826 by sea in sand junks from Shanghai, with the rates and allowances set in each reign.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the official freight paid per boat or per shi of grain on named routes and dates: the Tianjin lighters of 1695, the sea route's length in 1826, the sand-junk rate restated in 1868, and the cost ceilings argued over in the 1870s. They are the state's rates for tribute grain, condensed by the compilers from memorials, not merchants' fares, and a clause sometimes leaves its unit to be read from the clauses beside it.

Used for: the official freight on the Tianjin lighters and the sea route to Tianjin (Money and prices)
