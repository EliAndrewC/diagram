Yeh-chien Wang and Ying-chueh Huang, "The Distribution of Grain Crops, Grain Supply, and an Analysis of Grain Prices on the Southeastern Coast of China in the Middle of the Qing Period", Bulletin of the Institute of History and Philology, Academia Sinica 70.2 (1999), pp. 363-397, its abstract (https://www1.ihp.sinica.edu.tw/en/Publications/Bulletin/99/Article/359)

What it is: The abstract of two historians' study of grain crops, supply and prices on China's southeast coast in the 1700s, with a statistical analysis of rice prices in four prefectures from 1741 to 1760.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states how closely the rice prices of Suzhou and Guangzhou moved together. Only the abstract was read, so its finding is taken as stated, without the paper's measures.

Used for: how far apart the Lower Yangzi and Canton rice markets stood (Money and prices)
