ehime-kenshi-yashikidori

愛媛県史 民俗 上 ("History of Ehime Prefecture, Folklore, vol. 1", title translated), 1983, chapter 1, section 3 ("住居"), part 1, "屋敷取り" ("Laying out the house lot", title translated), in the prefecture's database えひめの記憶 (in Japanese; https://www.i-manabi.jp/system/regionals/regionals/ecode:2/49/view/6470)

What it is: The folklore volume's account of how a house lot was chosen, cut out of a slope and laid out in Ehime's farming, mountain and fishing villages, with the lots' usual areas and a 1924 survey of how fifty farm lots in one district of Imabari were used.

Why it applies, and its limits: It sizes whole house lots and says how much of one went to buildings and the drying yard, which bounds what was left for a vegetable bed, and it records a hillside settlement whose houses had vegetable ground all round them and no boundary. Its limits: its figures are Meiji-era and later (a lot map of 1872, the 1924 survey, undated recollections), from one prefecture of Shikoku; the survey's table of land uses is not in the online text, so its per-use areas cannot be read; and it never sizes the vegetable bed itself. The 1924 surveyor wrote that vegetable and poultry ground was small, while ornamental garden and trees took considerable room and hedges and paths nearly a tenth, so the share left after buildings and the drying yard is an upper bound on the bed, not its size.

Used for: the area of a house lot and its share left after buildings and the drying yard; a vegetable field round the houses of a hillside settlement, with no special boundary (Homesteads)
