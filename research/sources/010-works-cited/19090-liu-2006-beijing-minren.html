liu-2006-beijing-minren

Liu Xiaomeng, "Commoners in the banner society of Qing Beijing" (in Chinese), in the proceedings of the Palace Museum's 80th-anniversary conference (Forbidden City Press, 2006), posted by the Institute of Modern History, Chinese Academy of Social Sciences (http://jds.cssn.cn/xscg/xslw/201605/t20160506_5252169.shtml)

What it is: A historian's paper on the non-banner commoners who served Qing Beijing's banner population, trade by trade - water carriers, night-soil men, jewelers, horseshoers and others - from Qing records and later gazetteers.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its paragraph on the horseshoe trade says the shoers made and nailed iron shoes, worked first as itinerants and from the mid-Qing kept unnamed shops, with a frame at the door to tie mules and horses, near the horse inns and horse doctors. It is about the capital only, and it takes that paragraph from the draft gazetteer of Beijing, a compilation of the Republican years looking back on the Qing, so the date "mid-Qing" is that gazetteer's; it gives no size of shop or frame.

Used for: that nailed iron shoeing was a Qing trade and where its shops stood (Trades and services)
