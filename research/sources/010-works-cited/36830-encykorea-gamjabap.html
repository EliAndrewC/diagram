Kang In-hee (강인희), "감자밥" (Potato rice), Encyclopedia of Korean Culture (한국민족문화대백과사전), the Academy of Korean Studies (in Korean; https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0000820)

What it is: The encyclopedia's signed article on a dish of rice cooked with potatoes, which opens with the potato's arrival and spread.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the Oju yeonmun on bukjeo directly and alone gives the four provinces of 1847, but it names no text for that year and counts nothing. Written in 1995, it lists as references only the Oju yeonmun and the author's own 1978 history of Korean food, so the year rests on this one author's word. The encyclopedia's own article on the potato gives no such year and puts the potato's arrival at Seoul at 1879, on a 1912 farm journal. The 1824-25 is the author's reading of two texts ("seems to be") for the first entry into the far northeast, not a date of planting at large. It tells one spread from that northern entry, though the potato article reports its second text, Kim Chang-han's Wonjeobo, as also describing seed handed out from a British ship on the west coast in 1832, so the southern provinces need not have been reached from the north. It is an article on food, by a scholar of cookery.

Used for: The white potato in Korea: the 1824-25 date and the spread by 1847 (Money and prices)
