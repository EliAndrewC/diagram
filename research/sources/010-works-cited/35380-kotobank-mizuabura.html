kotobank-mizuabura

「水油」 ("liquid oil", title translated), on Kotobank: the entries of 『デジタル大辞泉』 and 『精選版日本国語大辞典』 (Shogakukan), and the passages on 水油 from the articles 「油」 ("oil", title translated), 「髪油」 ("hair oil", title translated) and 「ナタネ」 ("rapeseed", title translated) in 『世界大百科事典（旧版）』 (Heibonsha) (in Japanese; https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%B0%B4%E6%B2%B9)

What it is: Two dictionary definitions of the word 水油, a liquid oil for the hair or for lamps, and an encyclopedia's account of how pressed rapeseed oil (水油) and cottonseed oil (白油) became the chief oils of the Edo period, with 水油 the one good shipped out of Osaka in 1714 worth over 10,000 kan of silver.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says which oil the Edo period meant by 水油 and by 白油, the names a trade order of 1860 and a table of Osaka prices use. Both dictionaries give 水油 as any liquid oil, hair oil as well as lamp oil, and the passage from the hair-oil article uses the word for a hair oil. That the 水油 of the trade was rapeseed oil rests on two sentences: the oil article's, that 水油 was pressed from rapeseed and 白油 from cottonseed, and the gloss 水油(ナタネ油), "liquid oil (rapeseed oil)", in the rapeseed article's sentence on Osaka's shipments of 1714. The dictionaries name rapeseed oil only as one example of a lamp oil. Its encyclopedia passages are sentences in longer articles, not a study of the oil trade, and they say nothing of the 1860s.

Used for: which goods' prices the opening of the ports changed in Edo (Money and prices)
