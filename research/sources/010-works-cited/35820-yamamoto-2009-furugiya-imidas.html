Yamamoto Hirofumi, "古着屋（ふるぎや）" ("Used-clothes dealer", title translated), in the 時代劇用語指南 (a guide to the terms of period drama) on imidas, Shueisha, 19 January 2009 (in Japanese; https://imidas.jp/jidaigeki/detail/L-57-117-08-04-G252.html)

What it is: A short dictionary entry on Edo's used-clothes trade: what commoners wore, where the dealers stood, and the licensed buyers who walked the streets.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says much of commoners' cotton clothing was used and gives the number of used-clothes buyers licensed in 1659. It gives no market price for a used garment; its one figure comes from a tragic tale by the storyteller Sanyutei Encho, in which a doctor's daughter pays 42 ryo at a used-clothes shop for wedding clothes and other things that prove to be shoddy fakes - a story's price for a swindle, not a market price.

Used for: used clothing as commoners' ordinary dress in Edo, and its buyers in 1659 (Money and prices)
