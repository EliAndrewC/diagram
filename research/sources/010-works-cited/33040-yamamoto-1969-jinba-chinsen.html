Yamamoto Mitsumasa (山本光正), 「東海道における人馬賃銭について」 ("On the fares for men and horses on the Tokaido", title translated), Hosei Shigaku 21 (1969), from p. 89 (in Japanese; Hosei University repository, https://hosei.ecats-library.jp/da/repository/00010841/shigaku_21_yamamoto.pdf)

What it is: A historian's study of how the official fares for post horses and porters on the Tokaido were made up, from the base fare of 1711 and the later surcharges to how each station shared out the base fare and the surcharges and kept a cut of both. It rests chiefly on the returns the 57 stations made to the road commissioner in 1825 and 1826, as later compiled, with station records from 1696 to 1853 read mostly in printed town histories and source collections.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the base fare and the surcharged fare for one stage, Maisaka to Hamamatsu, in the 1820s, how the base fare of 1711 was reached, and the negotiated fare against the official one. It prints the base and the surcharged fares of a handful of stages in a table of 1825, not of the whole road, and this record takes only the one stage it sets out in full; it hedges the negotiated fare's "twice" itself, and that "twice" is a passing remark in its introduction, resting on Kodama Kōta's study and tied to no year or stage. The official fare was owed only to travelers on official warrants, and everyone else hired at the negotiated fare, so for a private traveler the official fare is a floor, not his price. The unpaid way back is one station's case: Shinagawa's petition of 1853 for a surcharge, which argued that the men and horses it sent into Edo found no paid load back. It is a station pleading for a raise, and the paper does not say the same held at other stations.

Used for: the base and surcharged stage fares, the negotiated fare, and the unpaid way back (Money and prices)
