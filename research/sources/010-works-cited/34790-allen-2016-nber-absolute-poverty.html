allen-2016-nber-absolute-poverty

Robert C. Allen, "Absolute Poverty: When Necessity Displaces Desire", a paper for an NBER conference, 2016 (https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f88142/f88142.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's draft of a new world poverty line, which finds the cheapest diet meeting set nutritional needs in each of twenty countries from their own prices in the International Comparison Program's survey of 2011, adds set amounts of other necessities, and sets that cost beside the national poverty budgets and the World Bank's line.

Why it applies, and its limits: It shows what a basic-needs basket is computed for and why it is priced in local prices: its own figures show India's three official poverty budgets costing about half as much in dollars when priced in rupees and converted at the PPP rate as when priced at American retail prices, and ranking differently. It is a conference draft: the version published in 2017 revised it, and its housing in particular is a flat 5% of the food and other budgeted spending here, where the later version prices floor space. Its other necessities (three metres of light cloth, soap, fuel for cooking and light but none for heat, no footwear) were set for the tropics, and Allen says they would not do in cold climates, so they understate a basket for a country with winters like Japan's or northern China's.

Used for: pricing a basket in local prices against converting it at an exchange rate, and its first housing allowance (Money and prices)
