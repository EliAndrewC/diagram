lostplays-norwich-affray-1583

David McInnis, "Play marred by an affray at Norwich", Lost Plays Database (University of Melbourne and the Folger Shakespeare Library), quoting the depositions of June 1583 as transcribed in REED Norwich, 70-76 (https://lostplays.folger.edu/Play_marred_by_an_affray_at_Norwich)

What it is: A scholarly database's page on an untitled play the Queen's Men, a touring company, gave in the yard of the Red Lion inn at Norwich on 15 June 1583, quoting at length the witnesses' depositions about the fight that broke out at the gate during the performance and the editor's own account of it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a comparative source: what English players did about money in the 1580s, read only as a yardstick for players, never for any figure of the setting. The depositions are the period's own words, but they were taken about a killing, not about how admission was taken, so what they say of the gate money is incidental; and they describe one company at one inn on one afternoon, transcribed by a later editor whose insertion marks the page keeps.

Used for: money taken at the gate of an inn yard while a touring company played (Comparative money: other societies)
