Wulan Qiqige (烏蘭其其格), "前近代日中における蚕糸絹織物業の社会経済的分析" ("A social and economic analysis of the raw-silk and silk-weaving industries of premodern Japan and China", title translated), doctoral thesis, University of Hyogo, 2014 (in Japanese; https://u-hyogo.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/141/files/%E5%89%8D%E8%BF%91%E4%BB%A3%E6%97%A5%E4%B8%AD%E3%81%AB%E3%81%8A%E3%81%91%E3%82%8B%E8%9A%95%E7%B3%B8%E7%B5%B9%E7%B9%94%E7%89%A9%E6%A5%AD%E3%81%AE%E7%A4%BE%E4%BC%9A%E7%B5%8C%E6%B8%88%E7%9A%84%E5%88%86%E6%9E%90.pdf)

What it is: A doctoral thesis comparing silk reeling and silk weaving in Japan and China before the modern era. Its section on prices reprints four tables from Yamazaki Ryuzo's 1983 study of Edo-period prices: raw-silk prices in the market towns of the Shindatsu district around Fukushima from 1757 to 1859, the same against rice, raw-silk prices in Kyoto and Maebashi from 1818 to 1867, and Kyoto's price as an index of Shindatsu's.

Why it applies, and its limits: It prints Kyoto's raw-silk price beside Maebashi's and Shindatsu's year by year, the two ends of the trade that fed Kyoto's weavers. The tables are reprinted, not built from documents the author read, and the thesis does not say what records or grade of thread each price comes from; the Shindatsu table states no unit, and its index was not built from one column of that table throughout. Kyoto's column has gaps after 1839, and the thesis itself calls Maebashi a district's market, whose thread, it notes after another historian, is said to have been finer and dearer than other thread. It gives no route, carriage cost or breakdown of the gap.

Used for: the price of raw silk in Kyoto against Maebashi and the Fukushima district (Money and prices)
