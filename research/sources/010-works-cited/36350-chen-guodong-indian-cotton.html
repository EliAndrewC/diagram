Chen Guodong (陳國棟), "印度棉布與中國: 以十六、七世紀為中心" (Indian cotton cloth and China, centered on the 16th and 17th centuries), 故宮學術季刊 (National Palace Museum Research Quarterly) 34:1, in Chinese (https://www.npm.edu.tw/NewChineseArtDownload.ashx?bid=3896)

What it is: A 2016 journal article by a historian at Academia Sinica on Indian cloth reaching China, with a survey of the earlier record.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the fullest account read of imported cotton before the Ming and of the Southern Song customs lists. Its subject is the 1500s and 1600s, so the earlier centuries are background, and his early finds are relayed from others: the Dunhuang pieces from a general history of indigo, and the Minfeng cloth, which he calls the earliest known within China, from an exhibition catalog. He states no date for that cloth himself, though a study in his footnote titles it Eastern Han. His reading of the Southern Song lists is inference: only the jibei goods are cotton by name, the rest he calls probably cotton, those marked Hainan he takes as made there, and India is not named. The lists as he quotes them give names and no quantities, so his import "at scale" is his judgment and John Guy's, not a count.

Used for: early finds, and the import of cotton cloth under the Southern Song (Money and prices)
