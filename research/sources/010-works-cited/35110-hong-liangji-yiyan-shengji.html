hong-liangji-yiyan-shengji

Hong Liangji 洪亮吉, "Shengji pian" 生計篇 (On livelihoods), in Yiyan 意言, in Hong Beijiang shiwen ji 洪北江詩文集, Sibu congkan edition, Juanshi ge wen jia ji, juan 1, Wikisource transcription (https://zh.wikisource.org/zh/洪北江詩文集_(四部叢刊本)/甲集卷第一)

What it is: A short essay by a Qing scholar of Yanghu county, Changzhou, on how a growing population had pressed on livelihoods, setting what he had heard rice and cloth cost in his grandfather's and father's time against what they cost as he wrote, in copper cash.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one cash price of cloth by the length found from a Lower Yangzi writer in the 1700s that can be read. Its figures are an essayist's round numbers, the older ones by hearsay ("I have heard") and the later ones unattributed to any market - Hong had been away from Changzhou since 1790, in the Hanlin at Beijing and from autumn 1792 as education commissioner of Guizhou, so if the essay is of about 1793 its present prices need not be the Lower Yangzi's, and only the remembered ones are tied to his family's county, and they serve an argument that prices had risen; it names neither a place nor a grade of cloth, nor its fiber - it says only bu 布, read here as cotton because his essay on form and substance (形質篇) in the same chapter, on how appetites have grown since antiquity, sets the warmth of jibei 吉貝 (taken here as cotton) against the hemp a middling household once wore - nor which foot his zhang is measured by or which sheng his rice, and the cloth foot differed from the builder's and from place to place, as the market grain measure did; and the transcription carries no date for the essay.

Used for: cloth and rice by the length and the measure in cash, about the 1740s and in the 1790s (Money and prices)
