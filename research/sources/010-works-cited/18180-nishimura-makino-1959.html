nishimura-makino-1959

西村嘉助・牧野洋一, 稲架の分布とその意義 (Nishimura Kasuke and Makino Yōichi, "The Distribution of the Rice-Drying Rack and Its Significance", title translated), 人文地理 (Human Geography) 11(4), 1959, pp. 293-306 (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjhg1948/11/4/11_4_293/_pdf)

What it is: A geography paper by two Hiroshima University geographers mapping, across Japan, how cut rice was dried - on racks or on the ground - and which forms of rack stood where, with their local names; it draws on field surveys, folklore records and the answers farmers gave to a national agricultural conference in 1881, which it maps.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the study of where the rack stood and why, read from distribution maps: a technique born in the Kinki (a record of 841) that spread outward, taken up readily where harvests were rainy or paddies wet and slowly where autumns were sunny, its tall form following deep snow. Its limits: it maps the practice of the 1880s to the 1950s, after rack drying had spread for two centuries, and it reads the map at the scale of regions, noting that the forms mix locally. Its thesis is diffusion, so whether a region had racks also turned on its distance from the Kinki and on local resistance (Kyushu still dried on the ground in 1881), not on weather alone. The racks gathered by the house rest on one sentence about the changeable San'in coast, with no count, whose stated reason is threshing in the yard; the paper does not say that racks stood away from the house where the weather was settled - its one such example is a Sanyo town building a rack in each field - so that half of our rule is our inference.

Used for: racks gathered by the house where the weather is changeable; the tall rack as a climate landscape of the snow country; the national divide and its local mixing; the 1881 map of rack and ground drying; the rack used only for wet paddies and wet harvests in a mixed district; climate and tradition both slowing its spread into sunny regions
