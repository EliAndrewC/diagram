keihan-oharame

Keihan Electric Railway, "第四十九回 大原女" ("No. 49: the Ohara women", title translated), in its Kyoto guide series 京都ツウのススメ ("Kyoto for connoisseurs", title translated), produced April 2012 for the May 2012 issue (in Japanese; https://www.keihan.co.jp/navi/kyoto_tsu/tsu201205.html)

What it is: A railway company's guide to the village of Ohara north of Kyoto, with a paragraph on the Ohara women who carried brushwood, firewood and produce on their heads to sell in the city, a practice it dates from the Kamakura period to the early Showa period.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the distance the women walked and the load they carried, the one figure read here for a head-load of fuel into a city. The load is given as what is said, not measured, and it describes the practice as remembered: it says the custom ran to the early Showa period and, elsewhere on the page, that the women disappeared in the Showa 30s (1955-1964).

Used for: the distance and load of the Ohara women who carried firewood to Kyoto (Money and prices)
