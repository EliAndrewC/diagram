hamamashike-jinya-site-bunka

Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Database, 庄内藩ハママシケ陣屋跡 ("The site of the Shonai domain's Hamamashike jin'ya", title translated) (https://online.bunka.go.jp/db/heritages/detail/189992)

What it is: The designation text of the national historic site of the Hamamashike jin'ya in Hokkaido: the post's founding in 1859-1860, the 193 posted to it, and its layout as the drawings handed down show it, with its buildings, its office and its powder store.

Why it applies, and its limits: It places the post's powder store on the hill behind the jin'ya, apart from its storehouses. The post was the Shonai domain's seat on the Ezo frontier, built under the shogunate's 1859 order to guard and clear the land: the 193 posted there included a county magistrate and a daikan beside forty ashigaru, twenty samurai and forty land-clearers, craftsmen and laborers, and it had a magistrate's office, but it was fortified with an earthwork, a squared gate, palisades and a moat, the defenses of a frontier post rather than of a magistracy alone, and it was abandoned in 1868, within ten years; the layout comes from old drawings, not excavation, and the text names no weapons store and gives no sizes.

Used for: where a seat kept its arms and powder (Estates and other compounds)
