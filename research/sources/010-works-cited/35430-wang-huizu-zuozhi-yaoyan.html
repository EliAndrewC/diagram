wang-huizu-zuozhi-yaoyan

汪輝祖 (Wang Huizu), 『佐治藥言』 (Advice on assisting in government, title translated), about 1785, the Wikisource text (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E4%BD%90%E6%B2%BB%E8%97%A5%E8%A8%80)

What it is: A handbook of advice for the private secretaries whom Qing magistrates hired at their own cost to handle law and revenue, written by a man who spent nearly thirty-four years as one.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states, from the inside, what such a secretary was paid a year against a village schoolteacher, and what a hundred-tael fee left a family after costs. Its sums are rounded and given as a range of posts, not one man's accounts: the hundred-tael fee is that of the lesser posts (correspondence, registry, tax-pressing), while the law and revenue secretaries' pay is given only as several to tens of times a teacher's few tens of taels. It speaks for county offices in southern Jiangsu and Zhejiang, the empire's richest region, from 1752 to 1785, and a secretary was a classically schooled scholar, often one who had failed the examinations, not a shop's clerk. The Wikisource text is a transcription whose edition is not named, with copying errors, and a later commentator's notes run into Wang's text unmarked (one cites a case of 1823).

Used for: a Qing private secretary's yearly fee against a schoolteacher's, about 1785 (Money and prices)
