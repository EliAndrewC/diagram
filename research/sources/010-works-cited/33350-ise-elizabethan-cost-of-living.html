Internet Shakespeare Editions (University of Victoria), "The Elizabethan cost of living", Life and Times, last modified 31 May 2012 (https://internetshakespeare.uvic.ca/Library/SLT/society/city%20life/cost.html)

What it is: A university project's teaching page on money in Shakespeare's England: wages by kind of worker, the prices of a soldier's daily food, what each place at a theater cost, and a baker's weekly accounts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a comparative source, read only as a yardstick for players. It names no source for its wages and dates none of them beyond "Shakespeare's money", so its wages are round ranges for the period as a whole; only its journeymen are said to be in the city, and their shilling a day, more usually half that, came "with food and drink included", its thatchers, hedgers, threshers and reapers being farm workers; and its theater prices are the public playhouses', set against the dearer private theaters.

Used for: an English day wage of Shakespeare's time beside the penny to stand at a play (Comparative money: other societies)
