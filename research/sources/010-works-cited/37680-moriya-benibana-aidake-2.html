moriya-benibana-aidake-2

Moriya Madoka (森谷圓人), "近世中期における紅花荷主の経営構造と上方取引（二）" ("The business of a safflower shipper and its trade with Kyoto and Osaka in the mid Edo period, part 2", title translated), a journal article (part 2, printed pp. 38-58, of a series whose part 1 appeared in 山形史学研究 (Yamagata shigaku kenkyū) no. 50, August 2022) in the Yamagata University library's collection of writings on safflower (in Japanese; https://www.lib.yamagata-u.ac.jp/database/benibana/img/bunken/76_moriya.pdf)

What it is: The second part of a study of the Aida house, safflower shippers outside the castle town of Yamagata, from the house's own papers. It follows the lots of 1736 to 1738 mark by mark: how they were shipped, what each load cost landed at Otsu, what each sold for, the freight sent with each waybill, and what the Kyoto wholesalers took off the sale. It goes on to the house's losses on the lots of 1739 and 1740, the petition of the Yachi and Sagae shippers to the Kyoto magistrate in 1740-1741, and the wholesalers' change of their sale slips from silver to gold in 1742.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its table of the 1737 lot sets a load's landed cost beside its sale and the freight, and its reckoning of the 1738 lot shows the wholesalers' two deductions. Those deductions fall on the credit sales only: by the article's reading a credit sale's silver price still holds the wholesalers' take, while a cash sale's gold is what the house received after it, so the table's sales are not all net of the same charges. The landed costs are in the old gold coin, the credit sales in silver (but for one 1737 credit lot of four loads priced at 50 ryo a load in the new gold) and the cash sales in the new gold coin of the 1736 recoinage, which was worth less than the old (the article names the two golds and does not compare them; the comparison is this record's), so no sale is in the landed costs' money (only the house's own final reckoning of profit and loss puts both in old gold); the freight column is what was sent with the waybills, which the article does not say was the whole cost of carriage. One house, and three of the years the article covers. It was a large shipper which, unlike the small Yachi and Sagae shippers and unlike later practice (the article's words), sold through a Kyoto intermediary from its agent's storehouse at Otsu. So its landed cost is reckoned at Otsu where the usual reckoning was on arrival at Kyoto, and the intermediary's fee (a share of the sale before 1738, which the article estimates at about 5.8 percent for 1735, and a fixed fee from the Genbun years) is a cost beside the wholesalers' deductions. The wholesalers' deductions of 15 and 3 percent are those of the fourteen-house Kyoto guild licensed in 1735; the shippers' petition of 1740 says the 3 percent replaced an earlier charge of 30 monme of old silver a load, so they are not an Edo rate for every year.

Used for: the route of Yamagata's safflower, what a landed cost included, the freight and the wholesalers' deductions (Money and prices)
