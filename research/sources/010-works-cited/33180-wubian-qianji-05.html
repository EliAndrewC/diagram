wubian-qianji-05

唐順之 (Tang Shunzhi), 武編 (Wubian, "a compilation on war", title translated), first part, volume 5, the Siku Quanshu edition (in Chinese; Wikisource transcription; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/武編_(四庫全書本)/前集卷5)

What it is: A volume of a Ming military compendium by the scholar-official Tang Shunzhi, who died in 1560, on weapons and how they were made, with two itemized costings in silver of a matchlock musket, one submitted by a master gunsmith and one an estimate the text attributes to 王直 (Wang Zhi): its iron and charcoal, and the days of each kind of worker at the forge and the bench with the pay of each.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives mid-Ming day wages of craftsmen, the forge's tongs-man, its ordinary smiths and a carpenter, the nearest Ming wages to the arrow prices in the record. They are the pay of skilled hands at a weapons workshop, so they are not an unskilled laborer's wage; and the costings name no place or year, only that they were written before 1560. The two costings disagree: the gunsmith's charcoal is 170 jin for 0.51 tael, about 3 li a jin, and the second estimate's is 500 jin for 0.85 tael, about 1.7 li a jin; and the volume's note on refining iron pays its smiths 5 fen a day, against 3 and 4 fen in the musket costings. They are estimates submitted for official manufacture, not prices seen paid, and the volume itself says official estimates for weapons run above cost.

Used for: mid-Ming craftsmen's day wages beside the arrow prices, and its charcoal by the jin (Money and prices)
