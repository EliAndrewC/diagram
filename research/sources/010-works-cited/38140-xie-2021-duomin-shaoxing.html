xie-2021-duomin-shaoxing

Xie Yibiao (谢一彪), 《浙江堕民研究》 ("A study of the duomin of Zhejiang", title translated), Beijing: 中国社会科学出版社 (2021), chapter 2, section 1, on Sandaijie, the largest duomin quarter (https://m.yunqi.qq.com/read/40667624/16)

What it is: The section of a scholarly monograph on the duomin, the hereditary "beggar households" of Zhejiang, that describes their quarter in the city of Shaoxing: the ward of the city that an older text it quotes assigns them, the three streets called Sandaijie, the canals around it and its bridges, and a field survey made of it in 1947.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the record's one case of a debased group's quarter in the middle of a walled Chinese city, with an older text's words for the ward and a Ming gazetteer's account of beggars killing a tiger in Qianqiu Lane in 1568. The section names the households as drummers, singers, musicians and actors, and its 1947 survey has them keeping over a hundred shops on their streets, but it says nothing of hides or the dead, though among the lanes they spread into it lists one named 硝皮弄, for hide-curing, a name that is no evidence of their trade; the section's description of the quarter's ground - the city center, ringed by canals, two li around - is the modern author's, drawn largely from twentieth-century memoirs and a survey of 1947, so the dated Ming evidence is the gazetteer's sentence on the tiger of 1568 and Xu Wei's poem on the same tiger (which dates it 1571); and the text that assigns them the ward is undated, its source given in a footnote that is not on the page read. The author's carrying of the quarter back to the Southern Song is his own reading of three ward names, one of them as a garrison to guard the beggars, and beyond that guess it gives no reason for the quarter's place; he says himself that when the households came to the three streets cannot be established, quotes a text saying they first lived at the foot of Jishan near Chang'an and moved there later, and dates the streets' present names to the late Qing.

Used for: Shaoxing's beggar households living in a ward inside the city
