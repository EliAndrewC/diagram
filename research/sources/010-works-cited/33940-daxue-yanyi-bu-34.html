daxue-yanyi-bu-34

Qiu Jun (丘濬), Daxue yanyi bu (大學衍義補, Supplement to the Extended Meaning of the Great Learning), chapter 34, "What befits grain transport, part 2" (漕輓之宜下), the Siku quanshu text on Chinese Wikisource (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大學衍義補_(四庫全書本)/卷034)

What it is: A chapter of a Ming official's statecraft treatise, written as a memorial to the throne, arguing that the grain the canal carried to Beijing could also go by sea.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states, as a Ming official's reckoning, how much river and sea carriage saved against land carriage, and that once the sea's risk of loss is weighed the two come out about even. The saving is given in tenths with no costing behind it; the only figures the chapter carries are, from the Yuan shi, the sea grain lost each year from 1283 to 1329. The sea's saving is reasoned from that Yuan route, which the Ming had given up in 1415, not from a Ming operation. It is a reckoning for the state's tribute grain, carried in bulk by soldiers some 3,700 li from the lower Yangzi to Beijing with official surcharges counted in its cost, not for a merchant's freight, and it is written to argue for sea transport.

Used for: a Ming official's reckoning of what river and sea carriage saved against land (Money and prices)
