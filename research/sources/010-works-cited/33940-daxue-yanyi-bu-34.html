daxue-yanyi-bu-34

Qiu Jun (丘濬), Daxue yanyi bu (大學衍義補, Supplement to the Extended Meaning of the Great Learning), chapter 34, "What befits grain transport, part 2" (漕輓之宜下), the Siku quanshu text on Chinese Wikisource (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大學衍義補_(四庫全書本)/卷034)

What it is: A chapter of a Ming official's statecraft treatise, written as a memorial to the throne, arguing that the grain the canal carried to Beijing could also go by sea.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states, as a Ming official's reckoning, how much river and sea carriage saved against land carriage, and that once the sea's risk of loss is weighed the two come out about even. The saving is given in tenths, with no figures behind it, and is written to argue for sea transport.

Used for: a Ming official's reckoning of what river and sea carriage saved against land (Money and prices)
