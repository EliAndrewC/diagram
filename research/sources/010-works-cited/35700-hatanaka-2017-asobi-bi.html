畑中章宏 (Hatanaka Akihiro), 「過労の現代人よ、「休日増」を勝ちとった江戸の若者たちをご存知か」 ("Overworked moderns, do you know the young people of Edo who won more days off?", title translated), 現代ビジネス (Gendai Business), Kodansha, 2017-12-04 (in Japanese; https://gendai.media/articles/-/53636)

What it is: A magazine essay by a folklorist and writer, set against present-day overwork, that retells the historian Furukawa Sadao's study of the rest days Edo-period villages kept, how many there were around 1700 and after 1800, and who demanded more.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the dated counts of a village's rest days that no other page read gives, but as a retelling of Furukawa's book with no page references, written to make a point about the present, and its figures are ranges across Japan's villages rather than any one village's.

Used for: the number of a village's rest days, and who asked for more (Money and prices)
