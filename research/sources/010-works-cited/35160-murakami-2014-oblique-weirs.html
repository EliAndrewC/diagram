murakami-2014-oblique-weirs

Murakami Shuichi, 河道および隣接地形との関係にもとづく斜め堰の取水点における景観の可能性 ("Research on possibilities of landscape at intake points of oblique weirs based on spatial relationships with both river channels and adjacent landforms", the paper's own English title), ランドスケープ研究 ("Landscape Research Japan", the journal's own English title) 77(5), 2014 (https://jila-zouen.org/wpdata/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/461.pdf)

What it is: A landscape-architecture paper on Japan's oblique weirs, the weirs that cross a river slantwise rather than square, the old form that rebuilding has been replacing. It measured from maps and air photographs the 153 of them confirmed still standing in field surveys of 2008-2012, among them each weir's slant off the square to its channel, and found an average of 30 degrees.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only measured count of the old weirs' slant the record holds. Its weirs are river works on channels 20 to 800 m wide, not a hamlet's brook, and, on this project's reading, some of the survivors may have been rebuilt in their long lives; the slant is the line from the intake to the far bank against a 200 m arc fitted to the river, not the weir's own bent outline, as the paper itself warns.

Used for: how far an oblique weir slants off the square to its stream (Water)
