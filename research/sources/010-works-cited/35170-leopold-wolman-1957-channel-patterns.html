leopold-wolman-1957-channel-patterns

Luna B. Leopold and M. Gordon Wolman, "River Channel Patterns: Braided, Meandering and Straight", U.S. Geological Survey Professional Paper 282-B, 1957 (reprinted 1963) (https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0282b/report.pdf)

What it is: A study by two geomorphologists of why natural rivers braid, meander or run straight, from field measurement of American streams and a laboratory flume. It says that truly straight natural channels are almost nonexistent and that reaches straight for more than ten channel widths are rare.

Why it applies, and its limits: How free water shapes its own course is the same in any country and era, so its rule bears on a brook on our maps. Its own field measurements are of rivers and creeks of the United States (it adds Indian rivers from the literature for other questions), the ten-width figure is a generalization from the authors' experience rather than a count, and it says nothing of a ditch dug and kept to a line.

Used for: how far a natural stream runs straight, against its own width (Water)
