Li Xinsheng (李昕升), "救荒活民：清代民国美洲作物与西南民族地区的民族交融" (American crops and famine relief among the peoples of the southwest in the Qing and Republic), at Aisixiang (in Chinese; https://www.aisixiang.com/data/177018.html)

What it is: A historian's article (Journal of Minzu University of China, Philosophy and Social Sciences edition, 2026 no. 1, reprinted at Aisixiang) on the American crops as famine food in southwest China, which reports his own earlier national estimates of their share of the fields.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says the sweet potato stayed under 1% of China's total crop area for most of the Qing (maize under 3%), a rounded ceiling and not a yearly series or a share of the land under cultivation, and it dates the American crops' value to after the mid-1800s, chiefly in easing population pressure in the mountains. The study behind those figures was not read, the page does not show how they were calculated, and the article's own subject is the southwest, where it finds the crops much more important locally.

Used for: The share of the sweet potato in Qing cultivated area (Money and prices)
