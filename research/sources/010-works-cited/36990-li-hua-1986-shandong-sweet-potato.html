Li Hua (李华), 《清代乾隆年间番薯在山东传播的原因及其意义》 (Why the sweet potato spread in Shandong in the Qianlong reign of the Qing, and what it meant), in 《清史研究集》 (Collected Studies of Qing History) 5, pp. 139-161 (Guangming Ribao Press, 1986), reposted with its notes on an agricultural-history site (in Chinese; http://agri-history.ihns.ac.cn/scholars/lihua1.htm)

What it is: A study of one crop in one province, built on Shandong's local gazetteers, with a count of which gazetteers mention the sweet potato and the 18th-century manuals that promoted it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one account read of the reasons for a crop's spread in a single northern province, with its sources noted. A gazetteer's mention shows the crop was known in a county, not how much of it was grown; the author takes the yields claimed by the crop's own promoter and the registered population figures as they stand. That promoter's own book also supplies most of the account of the crop's advantages against drought, locusts and heavy labor, and of his family's part in spreading it. The reasons are the author's argument: he gives no total area for the sweet potato or for the cash crops he says took grain land, and does not compare the counties that took it up at different times. The repost is an OCR text with garbled characters, and its figures include errors, whether the author's or the OCR's (Shunzhi 4 printed as 1667), so a number from it must be checked.
