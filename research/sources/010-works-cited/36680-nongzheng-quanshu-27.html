nongzheng-quanshu-27

Xu Guangqi (徐光啟), Nongzheng quanshu (農政全書, Complete Treatise on Agriculture), chapter 27, the Chinese Wikisource transcription (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/農政全書/卷27)

What it is: The chapter of the great late-Ming agricultural treatise on melons, gourds, taro, yams and other such plants, with Xu Guangqi's own notes on the sweet potato (番薯) and how to plant it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one contemporary text read that separates the sweet potato from the native tubers by its growth and shape, and that says Fujian and Guangdong relied on it against famine. Xu gives no year for the arrival or the planting: he says only "recent years" (近年), retelling what local people say, and the treatise was printed in 1639, after his death and revised by others, so the phrase cannot be dated from the book. What he says of Fujian and Guangdong comes from reports, not from any count, though his planting directions draw partly on his own growing of seed he brought north from Fujian. In this section 甘薯 names both plants: by his account the older works quoted under that heading mean the 山薯, and his own notes use the word for the 番薯.

Used for: Which plant the Chinese words mean, and its early use in Fujian and Guangdong, in the sweet potato question (Money and prices)
