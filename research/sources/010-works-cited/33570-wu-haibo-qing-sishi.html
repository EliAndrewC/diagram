吴海波 (Wu Haibo), 「清代私盐盛行的经济视角思考」 ("An economic view of the spread of private salt in the Qing", title translated), 盐文化研究论丛 (Studies in salt culture), volume 4, pp. 41-46 (in Chinese; https://tsjyb.jsbc.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/75/69/7346c62b453cbd4126bdc45fd5a0/bca8ad31-2170-4285-b227-c25c94f43143.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's essay on why smuggled salt flourished under the Qing monopoly, arguing from how far the official price stood above the cost of making salt.

Why it applies, and its limits: It states the spread between the price of official salt at the saltworks and what consumers paid, and quotes the historian Xu Hong's figure from his study of the Huai saltworks. Its range on p. 42 (a few wen a catty at the works, thirty to fifty or even sixty or seventy wen to the consumer) is given for the dynasty as a whole, with no year or place, and its footnote is Xu Hong's sentence, itself a generalization for the Huai works with no year, so the two figures are one witness, not two. On p. 44 it places the official price at sixty to seventy-odd wen a catty in the counties of Jiangxi and Huguang, against thirty-odd for private salt, citing a period source whose note the scan leaves illegible. The volume's year is not printed on the pages read, but its notes cite work of 2008, so it is no earlier.

Used for: how far the official price of salt rose from the saltworks to the consumer in Qing China (Money and prices)
