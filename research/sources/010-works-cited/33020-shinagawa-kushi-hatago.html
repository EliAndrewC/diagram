shinagawa-kushi-hatago

Shinagawa Ward History (品川区史), part 4, the early modern period, "(四) 宿駅制度 / 4 休泊の施設 / (2) 旅籠屋と茶屋" ("The post-station system: places to rest and lodge: inns and teahouses", title translated), pp. 751-754 (in Japanese; Shinagawa Digital Archive, https://adeac.jp/shinagawa-city/texthtml/d000010/mp000010/ht002820)

What it is: The section of the ward's history on the hatago and the teahouses of the Shinagawa post station at the Edo end of the Tokaido, which first traces how the hatago grew out of the self-catering firewood-fee inn, quoting the shogunate's and its officials' regulated charges of 1611, 1663 and 1686, then counts Shinagawa's inns.

Why it applies, and its limits: It quotes the regulated firewood fee for a person and for a horse, and argues that the 1663 "room money" was still a firewood fee, not a hatago's charge. It takes the 1663 order from the 駅遞志稿考証, a compilation of the post-station records, whose editor's term it says the "room money" is; the 1663 rate was fixed for the shogun Ietsuna's procession to Nikko, an official rate for one progress rather than a standing price. Its 1686 charge was what an intendant's clerks paid the villages they stayed in on their rounds, not what an inn charged a traveler: 12 mon for a master and 6 for a servant (not a person and a horse), paid on top of a ration of five go of rice a day, and the history reads it as paying the host to cook, not as a self-catering fee.

Used for: the hatago and the kichin-yado; the regulated firewood fee of 1663 (for a person and a horse) and 1686 (for a master and a servant) (Money and prices)
