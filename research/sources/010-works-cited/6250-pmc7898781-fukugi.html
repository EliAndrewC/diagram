pmc7898781-fukugi

"Distribution and utilization of homestead windbreak Fukugi trees", PMC 7898781 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7898781/)

What it is: A peer-reviewed ethnobotanical study by Bixia Chen and Hikaru Akamine of the University of the Ryukyus, "Distribution and utilization of homestead windbreak Fukugi (Garcinia subelliptica Merr.) trees: an ethnobotanical approach", Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine 17:11 (2021), read through PubMed Central: field measurements of large trees and interviews with village elders on the fukugi trees planted in strips around houses in the villages of Okinawa, which the authors, citing earlier work, trace to the Ryukyu Kingdom of roughly three hundred years ago and to the feng shui planning of its rural landscapes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a survey of a surviving, dated example of the thing we plant on our maps - a maintained belt of trees set around a homestead to break the wind - so the form is attested rather than assumed. The limits are of place and of time: Okinawa is subtropical and the fukugi a tropical evergreen, so we take the form and not the species or the spacing - though the siting the paper credits, the Ryukyu Kingdom's feng shui, descends from Chinese practice; and the trees measured today are the survivors of a much older planting, which makes the modern distribution an incomplete record of the old one.

Used for: Okinawan homestead windbreaks planted around 300 years ago
