What it is: The English edition of the account by George Timkowski of the Russian mission's journey to Peking and stay there in 1820-1821, with a list of the prices of food, fuel, cloth, servants and labor at Peking in 1821, in copper cash and silver taels, and an account of the city that names a house's rent.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one list found of Beijing's shop prices in the 1820s, the years for which the record otherwise has only a modern reconstruction. It is a foreign visitor's list, which does not say how or from whom its prices were gathered, naming no shop or season (its journal opens at the end of December 1820 and it sells sturgeon frozen, so its prices are at least partly winter's) and grading only coarsely (wheat flour of the first quality, coals ordinary or best, fish frozen or alive, the Nankin cloth the best), in single figures or ranges as wide as 60 to 400 cash a kin for frozen sturgeon; it reckons the tael at 1,100 cash and says the rate varied, and it does not say whether its cash are the great string's count of 1,000 or the little string of 500 it also describes; its cloth prices print no unit, and its cloth is measured in Russian arshins; and it gives its kin two ways, as about a Russian pound and a quarter (about 512 grams) at the head of the list and, in its own table of weights, as 16 liang at 11 to 11.6 liang to the Russian pound (about 565 to 596 grams), so a price per kin converts to grams only to within a sixth or so. The one rent it names is a guard's, who also had to guard the church, and the house is one of several small government houses by the church in a quarter the journal calls very poor, so it may sit below the market.

Used for: Beijing's market prices of fish, salt, fuel, grain flour and cotton cloth and a small house's rent in 1821 (Money and prices)
