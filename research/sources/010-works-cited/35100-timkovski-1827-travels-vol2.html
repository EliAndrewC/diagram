George Timkowski, Travels of the Russian Mission through Mongolia to China, and Residence in Peking, in the Years 1820-1821, with corrections and notes by Julius von Klaproth, vol. 2 (London: Longman, Rees, Orme, Brown, and Green, 1827), archive.org copy (https://archive.org/download/travelsrussianm04klapgoog/travelsrussianm04klapgoog_djvu.txt; the pages read, https://archive.org/download/travelsrussianm04klapgoog/page/n211_w1400.jpg, https://archive.org/download/travelsrussianm04klapgoog/page/n212_w1400.jpg, https://archive.org/download/travelsrussianm04klapgoog/page/n213_w1400.jpg, https://archive.org/download/travelsrussianm04klapgoog/page/n214_w1400.jpg, https://archive.org/download/travelsrussianm04klapgoog/page/n216_w1400.jpg, https://archive.org/download/travelsrussianm04klapgoog/page/n218_w1400.jpg, https://archive.org/download/travelsrussianm04klapgoog/page/n57_w1400.jpg)

What it is: The English edition of the account by George Timkowski of the Russian mission's journey to Peking and stay there in 1820-1821, with a list of the prices of food, fuel, cloth, servants and labor at Peking in 1821, in copper cash and silver taels, and an account of the city that names a house's rent.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one list found of Beijing's shop prices in the 1820s, the years for which the record otherwise has only a modern reconstruction. It is a foreign visitor's list, compiled through interpreters, with no grade, shop or season given and single figures or short ranges; it reckons the tael at 1,100 cash and says the rate varied, and it does not say whether its cash are the great string's count of 1,000 or the little string of 500 it also describes; its cloth prices print no unit, and its cloth is measured in Russian arshins. The one rent it names is a guard's, who also had to guard the church, so it may sit below the market.

Used for: Beijing's market prices of fish, salt, fuel, grain flour and cotton cloth and a small house's rent in 1821 (Money and prices)
