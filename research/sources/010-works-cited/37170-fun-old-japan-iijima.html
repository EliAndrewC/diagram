fun-old-japan-iijima

Fun of Good Old Japanese, "Shinshu Iijima Jinya、信州飯島陣屋", 2025-08-20, a personal blog in English and Japanese (https://fun-old-japan.blogspot.com/2025/08/shinshu-iijima-jinya.html)

What it is: A visitor's walk round the rebuilt Iijima Jin'ya, room by room, with photographs: the writing room, the court, the goyoba and the intendant's study behind it.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says who worked in the rebuilt office's writing room, locals the intendant hired, where the museum's own blog speaks of apprentices on night duty, likely the same junior clerks seen from another side. It is a visitor's account of the rebuild (dated 1993 by another page; the post gives no year), written by a folklore blogger and volunteer guide. It names no source for the writing room (it names the town's museum page at its foot and quotes a guide on other points) and gives no sizes. Iijima was, as the post itself says, a branch office (出張陣屋) whose intendant did not live there: he came from Edo at tax time and lodged at the honjin. Its office room (goyoba), the post says, was staffed by samurai officers and by tedai promoted from the clerks, and the retainers had residences built there. So its writing room speaks for an office run day to day by those samurai, the tedai and the hired clerks, not for one with a resident intendant. It also speaks for an office whose district was large: 15,000 koku at the end of the Edo period and 50,000 at its peak, which the post calls a daimyo's scale.

Used for: who worked in a small office's writing room, on a visitor's account (Estates and other compounds)
