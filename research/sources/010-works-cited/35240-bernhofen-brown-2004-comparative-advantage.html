bernhofen-brown-2004-comparative-advantage

Daniel M. Bernhofen and John C. Brown, "A Direct Test of the Theory of Comparative Advantage: The Case of Japan", Journal of Political Economy 112.1 (February 2004), pp. 48-67 (https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.dartmouth.edu/dist/b/2093/files/2019/08/A-Direct-Test-of-the-Theory-of-Comparative-Advantage.pdf)

What it is: Two economists' test of trade theory on Japan's move from closed to open trade, comparing the prices of 1851-53, before the ports opened, with Japan's trade of 1868-1875 and its prices of 1869, and describing what the treaties allowed and what Japan bought and sold.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says which goods Japan exported and imported once its trade was open, which goods the treaties still kept from export, and which prices rose or fell with trade by 1869. Its trade figures begin only in 1868, after the Edo period, and its prices are taken from published series, Ono's Edo prices and the Mitsui books among them, deflated by an index of goods that were not traded, so it shows the direction of a change, not a year-by-year price. That change runs from 1851-53 to the single year 1869, for only about twenty goods: a "fall" means a fall against untraded goods, since all money prices rose after 1859, and the authors themselves put the rise in rice and legumes down to poor weather and the rise in cotton down to the American Civil War, not to the opening.

Used for: which goods' prices the opening of the ports changed in Japan (Money and prices)
