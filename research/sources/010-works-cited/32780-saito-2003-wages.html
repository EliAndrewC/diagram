saito-2003-wages

Osamu Saito, "Wages, inequality and pre-modern growth in Japan, 1727-1894", Hitotsubashi University Research Unit for Statistical Analysis in Social Sciences, discussion paper D03-02, December 2003 (https://hi-stat.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/research/discussion/2003/pdf/D03-02.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's paper on Japanese wages from the 1720s to the 1890s, drawing on the account books of a village near Osaka and on the wages paid to day laborers in Kyoto and carpenters in Osaka, to ask how far pay in the city and in the countryside moved together and how wide the gap between skilled and unskilled pay was.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the page that compares a Kyoto day laborer's pay with a farm worker's in a nearby village, year for year. Its comparison is of one village of the Osaka plain, about 40 km from Kyoto, at its spring rate, so it says how a well-connected village near the great cities compared with Kyoto, not how a remote one did; and its account of the city catching up in the 1750s and 1760s rests on the author's earlier study, which is not quoted here. Its skilled premium is two different figures, both for the base years 1802-4. In the village a carpenter earned 2.6 times a farm laborer's spring rate. The urban 4.7 compares Osaka carpenters with Kyoto day laborers, two cities, not one city's own pay ladder. The paper shows both ratios moving over time, the gap narrowing while real wages rose to about 1820 and widening as they fell after 1820, so neither is a rate for the whole Tokugawa period.

Used for: the Kyoto day wage against a nearby village's farm wage, and the skilled worker's premium (Money and prices)
