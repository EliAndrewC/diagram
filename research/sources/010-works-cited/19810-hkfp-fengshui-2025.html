Kit Lee and Michael Boyle, "Feng shui woodlands have protected Hong Kong; it's our turn to care for these long-neglected, forgotten forests", Hong Kong Free Press, 10 July 2025 (https://hongkongfp.com/2025/07/10/feng-shui-woodlands-have-protected-hong-kong-its-our-turn-to-care-for-these-long-neglected-forgotten-forests/)

What it is: An opinion piece in a Hong Kong news site by a doctoral student and a research fellow in two ecology labs of the University of Hong Kong, arguing for the protection of the territory's fengshui woods: it calls them the oldest remnant forest in a territory much of which is covered by young forest regrown in the 60-70 years since the Second World War; it estimates over 300 of them (116 in the government's inventory) and says 60 per cent of the known ones lie outside legally protected zones such as country parks and green belts.

Why it applies, and its limits: It dates the woods, not their size: many have stood for well over 300 years and some over 600, and much of Hong Kong's other forest is post-war regrowth, which tells a reader the surviving woods are not a modern planting. It gives no area for any wood, says nothing of whether a wood has grown or shrunk, and gives its ages without naming how they were found. Hong Kong is subtropical southern China, not Japan.

Used for: that the fengshui woods measured today are centuries old and not a product of modern protection, most of them lying outside any protected zone (Vegetation and terrain)
