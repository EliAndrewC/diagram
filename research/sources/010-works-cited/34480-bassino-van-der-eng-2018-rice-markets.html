bassino-van-der-eng-2018-rice-markets

Jean-Pascal Bassino and Pierre van der Eng, "Japan and the Asian Divergence: Market Integration, Climate Anomalies and Famines during the 18th and 19th Centuries", Hitotsubashi University Center for Economic Institutions working paper 2018-18 (version of March 2019), its abstract on RePEc/IDEAS (https://ideas.repec.org/p/hit/hitcei/2018-18.html)

What it is: The abstract of two economic historians' study of rice prices in 13 markets across Japan, 1720-1857, asking whether Japan's better-integrated rice markets, compared with China's and India's, explain the 'Little Divergence' in Asia and Japan's role in the 'Great Divergence'; it finds they did not prevent the great famines.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says which parts of Japan had well-integrated rice markets and what held the east back. Only the abstract was read, so nothing is taken from it but its stated findings; the abstract gives none of the paper's measures or its markets' names. The full paper is public and names both, but it has not been read for the record. The source-applicability check found that it takes the prices of ten of its thirteen markets, Osaka's among them, from Iwahashi (1981), the table in iwahashi-1981-gpih-rice-prices, and three from Yamasaki (1983). So its finding is not a check independent of that table.

Used for: how closely Japan's regional rice markets moved together (Money and prices)
