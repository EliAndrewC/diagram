Niiza City, 野火止用水・平林寺の文化的景観 (the cultural landscape of the Nobidome canal and Heirinji), chapter 2, section 1 "歴史の視点" ("The historical view", title translated), c. 2012 (in Japanese; https://www.city.niiza.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/7103.pdf)

What it is: The historical chapter of a city report on the Nobidome canal's landscape: why the canal was dug in 1655, how the four new-field villages of the Nobidome plateau were opened from 1653 and surveyed in 1661, and how their house lots, fields and waste were laid out in strips, with a table of the strips' frontage and depth.

Why it applies, and its limits: It reads the strips from Nobidome village's own survey register (the surviving registers are of 1661) and says only that the other three villages were laid out the same way; its table of average frontages and depths names no source and gives Kitano only a total. It says in one sentence, citing nothing, that no strips formed along the canal outside today's Niiza because the villages there had no water right or used the water to irrigate rather than to drink; the irrigated reaches it names are paddy new fields in the lowland across the Shingashi River. It speaks of four villages within today's Niiza only, a lord's planned project of dry new fields that it calls without precedent in its time, not a village that grew of itself; its strip is a whole household's holding of house lot, fields and waste, hundreds of ken deep, not a single dry plot, and it lays Nobidome village's strips at right angles to the highway, naming the canal only as one axis of the villages' layout.

Used for: the canal dug for the settlers' drinking water, and no strips where its water irrigated (Fields)
