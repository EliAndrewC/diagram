Wang Zhenghua 王正华, 「"Water" in the transaction of rural land and houses in north China in the Qing dynasty」 (translated; original: 「清代华北乡村田宅交易中的"水"」), 重庆大学学报(社会科学版) (Journal of Chongqing University, Social Science Edition), 27(1): 266-279, 2021, DOI 10.11835/j.issn.1008-5831.rw.2020.10.001 (http://qks.cqu.edu.cn/html/cqdxskcn/2021/1/20210123.htm)

What it is: A 2021 study by a doctoral student in history at Tsinghua University of how water - irrigated land, water shares, channels, drains and the eaves' drip - figured in Qing-period sales of rural land and houses in north China, from deeds of five villages in southern Shanxi held at Tsinghua, published deeds and deed steles, and local histories; its section on the drip gathers deeds bounded by it and a table of the drip customs of four provinces.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one page read that gives how wide a house's drip strip was by custom and how far a village house's eaves reached, and shows deeds bounding a plot by the drip or by a street. Its limits: the widths in its table of customs come from the Republican government's survey of civil custom (published in 1930), not from Qing records, and the study itself calls its reliability disputed; each width is reported for one county, Jiaxiang in Shandong (two or three chi, a custom the report says Jiaxiang does not have alone) and Pingyao in Shanxi (six or eight cun), both in north China and not the wet-rice south; its other figures are one 1884 deed for a house in the city of Baoding, a provincial capital rather than a village (the Baoding deed it quotes that runs to a street is a city house's as well), and one Shanxi writer's recollection of the eaves allowed in his Taihang village, Hongyadi, in the deeds made before collectivization; and its deeds quote their bounds in excerpts, so a deed that runs to a road may name more than the excerpt shows.

Used for: the drip strip a house kept at the foot of its wall, and how a deed met a street or road (Ways)
