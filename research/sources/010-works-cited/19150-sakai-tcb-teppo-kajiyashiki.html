Sakai Tourism and Convention Bureau, "祝・開館！鉄炮鍛冶屋敷 見学レポート" ("The Gunsmith's House opens: a visit report", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.sakai-tcb.or.jp/feature/detail/84)

What it is: A tourism bureau's report on the opening of the Inoue gunsmith's house in Sakai as a museum, walking through its rooms and the family's history from its documents.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the street-facing earth floor as the finishing room, where guns were assembled and adjusted, and says the forge now shown was newly built from a Meiji-period drawing, so it marks the forge as a reconstruction (it calls the counting desk in the shop room a re-creation too); it does not say which of the rest of the house is original and which was restored in the repair finished in 2024. Its limits: its heading calls the rebuilt forge an Edo one while its text rests it on a Meiji picture; it gives no size or place for the forge building; and it describes one exceptional house - by its own account much the largest in its quarter, 17.5 ken (about 35 m) across after growing from 6 ken in the late seventeenth century, home to the leading Sakai maker and still making guns in the Meiji era - so its finishing room, which the page itself says ordinary machiya lacked, is one leading gunsmith's arrangement rather than a typical town smith's, and the page does not say when the room took that use.

Used for: that a town gunsmith's street room was a finishing room and his forge a rebuilt separate room known from a Meiji drawing (Trades and services)
