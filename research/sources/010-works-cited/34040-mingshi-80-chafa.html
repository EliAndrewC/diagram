明史 (Mingshi, History of the Ming), 卷80, 食貨志四 (Treatise on Food and Money, part 4), its section on the tea laws (茶法), compiled under the Qing and presented in 1739 (Wikisource, in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明史/卷80)

What it is: The official history's account of the Ming state's salt and tea monopolies, including the bureaus that traded tea for horses on the Tibetan and western frontier.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the rates the state set: tea paid for a horse, the weight a merchant's license covered and its fee, and the silver a load of tea was commuted at, each tied to a reign. These are official rates and proposals, summarized by Qing compilers from Ming records, not prices paid in a market, and it gives no freight, days on the road or wage.

Used for: the Ming tea-for-horses rates and tea licenses (Money and prices)
