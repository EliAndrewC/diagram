yamamoto-torisu-1984

Yamamoto Shozo, Ishii Hideya, Maruyama Hiroaki and others, 鉾田町鳥栖地区における土地利用の構造 ("The structure of land use in the Torisu district of Hokota", title translated), 地域調査報告 6 (University of Tsukuba, 1984), pp. 25-36 (in Japanese; http://www.geoenv.tsukuba.ac.jp/~chicho/nenpo/6/01.pdf)

What it is: A geography department's field report on the land use of two farm settlements on a plateau edge in Ibaraki: Torisu Hongo, a clustered hamlet the authors date to before the Edo period, which farms paddy in the river lowland below it, and Torisu Shinden, a new-field row village on the plateau above. It traces their land use from the army survey maps of 1883-1885 to the authors' own survey of 1983, and draws each settlement's traditional arrangement as a model diagram.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the one study found that says where a clustered paddy hamlet's houses stood against its dry fields: paddy in front, dry fields behind, woodland beyond. Its limits: it describes one hamlet, its "traditional" arrangement is the authors' account of the land before the woods were cleared after the Second World War, the earliest records it reads are a village cadastral map of 1876 (for Torisu Shinden) and the army survey of 1883-1885, and the hamlet's pre-Edo founding dates the settlement, not the arrangement. The order follows the ground: the hamlet stands on the step between a wet river lowland and a dry plateau, so it says where the dry fields go where paddy meets upland at a slope, not on a level plain; and the 1880s survey shows dry fields all around the houses, where the authors' model draws them only behind.

Used for: where a clustered hamlet's houses stood between its paddy and its dry fields (Fields)
