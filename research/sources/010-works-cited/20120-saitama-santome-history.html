saitama-santome-history

Saitama Prefecture, 三富地域の歴史 ("The history of the Santome area", title translated), on the prefecture's page for the green Santome area (in Japanese; https://www.pref.saitama.lg.jp/a0108/908-20091216-188.html)

What it is: The prefectural government's short history of the new-field colonies of the Musashino plateau: the Nobidome villages of 1661 along the Kawagoe road, and Santome Shinden of 1694-1696 with its frontage, depth, road width and the arrangement of house lot, field and woodland on both sides of the road, posted in 2023 by the prefecture's land and water policy division. It cites the founding headman's account of 1699 only for the colony's completion and survey in 1696; for the rest it lists modern works (the Saitama prefectural history of 1988, the Miyoshi town history of 1986 and two articles), and it gives the Nobidome depth as "about 400 ken" with no source.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the strip row's figures for two colonies, and says the strips lay on both sides of the road, from a public body whose references are modern prefectural and town histories. It is an unsigned summary for the public rather than a study, and both colonies are planned dry-field settlements on one water-poor plateau, not paddy villages that grew on their own (Nobidome drank from a canal dug for it from the Tamagawa aqueduct, which the page says came to irrigate the land round it too; Santome drank from deep wells). The strip's size belongs to that ground: each Santome household got about 5 cho of house lot, field and coppice, and the page gives, as the accepted view, that the holding was made that large to offset the low yield of the new upland fields, so the row, the road down its middle and the order of house, field and wood carry to a paddy row village, while the 40 by 375 ken strip and the 400-ken depth do not - a paddy household's strip would be far smaller.

Used for: the strip row on both sides of a road at Santome (40 by 375 ken, a road of 6 ken) and at Nobidome (strips about 400 ken deep) (Homesteads)
