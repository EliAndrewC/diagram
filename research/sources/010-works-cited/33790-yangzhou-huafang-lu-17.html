李斗 (Li Dou), 《揚州畫舫錄》 (Records of the painted boats of Yangzhou, title translated), volume 17, 「工段營造錄」 ("Records of building works", title translated) (in Chinese; Wikisource transcription; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%8F%9A%E5%B7%9E%E7%95%AB%E8%88%AB%E9%8C%84/17; the contents, https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%8F%9A%E5%B7%9E%E7%95%AB%E8%88%AB%E9%8C%84; the author's preface, https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%8F%9A%E5%B7%9E%E7%95%AB%E8%88%AB%E9%8C%84/%E8%87%AA%E5%BA%8F)

What it is: An eighteen-volume account of the city of Yangzhou that its author collected over thirty years and dated, in his preface, to the 12th month of the Qianlong emperor's 60th year (1795-1796). Its 17th volume sets out how building work was reckoned: the materials, the craftsmen and the laborers each kind of work took, and how each was measured.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the laborers a length of fence or hedge, or of a stone bridge's stones lifted into place, was reckoned to take, a Qing norm for small work about a garden or a precinct. The volume says the Board of Works had fixed the rules for building and printed them, so these are rules of official estimating, not counts of any one job; it gives the number of laborers but not how long their work took, and no wage beside them.

Used for: the laborers reckoned per length of hedge, fence and lifted stone (Money and prices)
