zurndorfer-pune-cotton

Harriet T. Zurndorfer, "Cotton Textiles And Ming/Qing China In The Global Economy (1500-1840)", a paper filed on the London School of Economics' Global Economic History Network pages under its Pune conference, undated on its face but given at that conference, 18-20 December 2005 (https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/GEHNConferences/conf8/PUNEZurndorfer.pdf)

What it is: A Leiden historian's survey of the Ming and Qing cotton industry and its place in world trade, drawn from the secondary literature.

Why it applies, and its limits: It collects the modern estimates of state demand and output in one place. Its first page says "Not for citation without written permission of the author", so it is cited here with that said; it is a conference draft, not refereed. The output, state-demand and household figures taken from it are at second hand from Yan Zhongping, Li Bozhong or Xu Dixin and Wu Chengming, whose own tables were not read. Those figures are not like for like. It sets Li's 50 million bolts beside Yan's 20 million as estimates of late Ming production, but later gives Li's 50 million as Jiangnan's alone, and it does not say what area Yan's covers or whether the 8.4 bolts per mid-Qing household, also Li's, is Jiangnan's or the empire's. Its 15 million bolts of state demand is what it says demand "could have been as high as" before 1400, an upper estimate for the early Ming. By its own note a bolt ran from 5.72 to 11.45 meters, so a count of bolts is not a fixed amount of cloth.

Used for: Ming state demand, Songjiang's commerce, and the output and household figures (Money and prices)
