ja.wikipedia 地蔵菩薩 ("Jizō bosatsu", title translated), revision 111026026 (https://ja.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=%E5%9C%B0%E8%94%B5%E8%8F%A9%E8%96%A9&oldid=111026026)

What it is: The Japanese Wikipedia article on the bodhisattva Jizō, which describes the stone jizo of the roadside and the village in its overview and its sections on worship and on the tie to the road-gods (dōsojin), each passage citing modern Japanese encyclopedias or a Kyoto city pamphlet.

Why it applies, and its limits: It names the places a stone jizo stands - village boundaries, passes, crossroads, the foot of a bridge, graveyards - and says that in the Kinki region a jizo is kept for each town block. Its limits: the block-by-block jizo is a Kinki custom, described as it is kept today rather than dated; and it gives no figure for how many a village kept. It says each block keeps jizo statues, not exactly one, so one to a block is a floor for a town's count rather than the count itself; and its own source for the custom is a Kyoto city pamphlet, so it speaks for Kyoto first. The passages quoted are from revision 111026026, read in September 2026 and again, from an archived copy, in November 2026; the live article has since been rewritten without them.

Used for: where a stone jizo stands, and a jizo for each town block in Kinki (Religion and the dead)
