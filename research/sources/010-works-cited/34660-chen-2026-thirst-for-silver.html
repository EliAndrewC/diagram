Ting Chen, Jianan Li, Chong Pang and Yuan Zi, "A Thirst for Silver: Trade Shock, Taxation, and Local Unrest in Nineteenth-Century China" (working paper, June 2026), posted by the Vienna Institute for International Economic Studies (https://wiiw.ac.at/files/events/paper-yuan-zi-n-757.pdf)

What it is: An economic history working paper that uses county data and a trade model to measure how the rise of silver against copper in the early 1800s raised tax burdens and unrest in Qing China.

Why it applies, and its limits: It summarizes which metal served which purpose and that copper prices and wages held while the rate rose. It is an unrefereed paper, its background statements rest on the works it cites, and its welfare figure is a model's estimate, not a measurement. Its evidence that copper prices and wages held is a Beijing series, and its silver-copper rate is one chain store's ledger in Zhili, so both speak for the north first.

Used for: silver and copper's roles in the Qing and the cost of the silver shock (Money and prices)
