Tokugawa Institute for Forestry History (徳川林政史研究所), 『人・物・お金にみる山村の暮らし』 ("Life in a mountain village seen through its people, goods and money", title translated), booklet 9 (in Japanese; https://rinseishi.tokugawa.or.jp/pdf_file/booklet_09.pdf)

What it is: An outreach booklet of a research institute, written by two of its researchers, Nakaizumi Tsuyoshi and Hayashi Kotaro (March 2024), built on the ten surviving volumes of the diary kept by Naiki Hikoshichi, head of the house that was Kashimo's headman and the Owari domain's hereditary forest warden, in Kashimo, an upland village of Mino, between 1763 and 1775, quoting its entries and tabling what the household bought, sold and paid.

Why it applies, and its limits: It tables the price of salt at the purchases the household made from 1763 to 1774, a selection rather than every one, and the day wage the same household paid a hired farm hand, so a price and an unskilled wage of one place and decade stand side by side. It is one household of one inland village, and not a common one: the village headman's house and the domain's forest warden, paid a stipend in rice. Its salt is that house's yearly bulk purchase of 20 to 40 sho for miso-making, at the prices of the salt peddlers who came to the door, not a smallholder's small buy. Its wage is one man's 27 days of autumn farm work in 1774, a peak-season rate. It gives no craftsman's day wage of the period, and its conversions of Edo money to yen rest on a present-day rice price and carpenter's wage that the record must never take. It is an outreach booklet, not peer-reviewed; it gives one year wrongly in the Western calendar, and its table leaves out a second purchase of 1772 that its text quotes (an HTML note on the page that cites it says where).

Used for: the price of salt in an upland Mino village, 1763-1774, and a farm hand's day wage there in 1774 (Money and prices)
