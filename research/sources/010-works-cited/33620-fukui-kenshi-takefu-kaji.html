fukui-kenshi-takefu-kaji

Fukui Prefectural Archives, 『福井県史』通史編4 近世二 (History of Fukui Prefecture, general history vol. 4, early modern 2), 「鍛冶株仲間の成立」 ("The smiths' guild", title translated) (in Japanese; https://www.library-archives.pref.fukui.lg.jp/fukui/07/kenshi/T4/T4-3-01-02-03-02.htm)

What it is: The prefecture's official history, here on the smiths of Fuchū (renamed Takefu in 1869, now part of Echizen City, Fukui) who made sickles, vegetable knives and nails, and the sickle smiths' guild of bellows-shares that licensed them from the 1770s to the first years of Meiji (from 1868), written from the guild's own records.

Why it applies, and its limits: It counts the bellows-shares and the smithies of the town in 1797, 1850, 1853 and 1866, and prices a share. It gives no output per smith and no price of a knife, and does not say how many of the smiths made knives as well as sickles. Fuchū was a specialist town of edged tools with 72 to 129 smithies, so its count is a craft center's, never the number of smiths in an ordinary town or village. The count was also capped: the town magistrate set the number of bellows-shares, and the guild restricted how much was made.

Used for: the count of Takefu's smithies and their bellows-shares (Money and prices)
