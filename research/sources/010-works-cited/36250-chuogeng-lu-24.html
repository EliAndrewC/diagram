chuogeng-lu-24

Tao Zongyi (陶宗儀), Nancun chuogeng lu (南村輟耕錄, Notes Written While Resting from the Plow), chapter 24, the Sibu congkan text on Chinese Wikisource (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/南村輟耕錄_(四部叢刊本)/卷之二十四)

What it is: A chapter of miscellaneous notes by a writer of the late Yuan, with the earliest telling read of Huang Daopo's story.

Why it applies, and its limits: It says that Wunijing, a place of poor land fifty li east of the Songjiang seat, took up cotton with seed from Fujian and Guangdong, and that an old woman from Yazhou taught its tools. It dates her only as "early in the dynasty" and tells her story at least a generation later: by its own account her shrine was rebuilt after thirty years, had fallen again, and her name was fading. It gives no figures of output, acreage or price, and it speaks for that one village, not for the prefecture's cotton as a whole. The Wikisource transcription drops characters in this passage, none of which changes these points.
