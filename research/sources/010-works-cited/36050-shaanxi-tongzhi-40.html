shaanxi-tongzhi-40

「Shaanxi Provincial Gazetteer (Siku Quanshu edition), chapter 40, water works」 (translated; original: 「陜西通志 (四庫全書本)/卷040」), the gazetteer of the Yongzheng era (1735), on Wikisource (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/陜西通志_(四庫全書本)/卷040)

What it is: The second water-works chapter (水利二) of the Shaanxi provincial gazetteer compiled under the Yongzheng emperor and kept in the Qing imperial library, as transcribed on Wikisource. County by county it lists each canal, weir and pond, how far it lies from the county seat, what it draws from, and how many mu of fields it waters, most entries marked as taken from the county's own register, others from earlier gazetteers, the dynastic histories and stone inscriptions; each county ends with an appended list of old works, most marked abandoned or untraceable. It covers Fengxiang, Hanzhong, Xing'an, Shang, Tong, Hua and the northern prefectures, not Xi'an.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives, for named canals and ponds, the land each watered, so the area of one irrigation work's tract can be read from it - though not straight where it quotes several sources for one work: for the Wumen weir in Chenggu county a Yuan inscription gives 40,840 mu, a Ming record "nearly 50,000 qing" (plainly garbled), and the county register's parts sum to about 30,900 mu, so the record must say which figure it takes. Its "fields watered" are irrigated land of any crop, not paddy: Qianyang lies in the dry western Wei valley, where a canal's land need not be rice, while Chenggu is in the Hanzhong rice basin. Its register figures are acreages in the mu of the day, not measured, and many are rounded ("a hundred-odd mu"); the Wikisource text is a transcription with some rare characters lost, and the zh-hans view shows it in simplified characters.

Used for: the land one village canal watered in Qianyang county, and one pond in Xixiang county, and the much larger Wumen weir divided into sluices (Fields)
