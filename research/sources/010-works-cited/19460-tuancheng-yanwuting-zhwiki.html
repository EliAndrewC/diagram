zh.wikipedia 团城演武厅 ("Tuancheng drill hall", title translated) (in Chinese; https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-hans/%E5%9B%A2%E5%9F%8E%E6%BC%94%E6%AD%A6%E5%8E%85)

What it is: The Chinese Wikipedia article on the drill ground and drill hall built at Xiangshan outside Beijing in 1748 for the Jianrui camp, a picked banner force, and surviving today.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a drill hall built and dated before 1912, and gives its width in bays and its south-facing, hip-and-gable form. It is an imperial camp's hall at the capital, not a county's, so it shows what a large state drill hall could be rather than a county's; it gives bays, not meters, and describes the hall as it stands after its 1988-2003 repairs (from Beijing gazetteers of 2004-2006), not from a Qing record; a Beijing government page dates the hall to 1749, a year later than the article.

Used for: a drill hall of five bays dated to the Qing (Estates and other compounds)
