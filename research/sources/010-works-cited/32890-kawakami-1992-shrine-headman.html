kawakami-1992-shrine-headman

河上信行 (Kawakami Nobuyuki), 「江戸時代の村落における寺社造営と名主の役割 - 旧武蔵国多摩郡の中小寺社造営 -」 ("The building of temples and shrines in Edo-period villages and the role of the village headman: minor temples and shrines of the former Tama district of Musashi", title translated), 日本建築学会計画系論文報告集 (Journal of Architecture, Planning and Environmental Engineering, AIJ) 435 (May 1992), pp. 87-95 (in Japanese; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijax/435/0/435_KJ00004076856/_pdf)

What it is: An architectural historian's study of how the headmen of Tama-district villages west of Edo raised the money, chose the carpenters, bought the materials and paid the labor for building and repairing small temples and shrines from 1749 to 1842, from two headman families' papers and the diary of the headman of Shibasaki village.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the carpenters' bids, the separate contracts and the building funds of the 1841 rebuilding of Shibasaki's Hachiman shrine, whose sanctuary is a one-bay nagare-style hall with a 6-shaku bay, and the craftsmen's day rates and day counts of a sanctuary's re-roofing in 1749. Its figures are mostly in its notes, quoted from account books and the diary that it does not reprint in full. The 1841 build also raised a worship hall and a torii, the winning carpenter (bidding 30-odd ryo) is shown building the worship hall, and the paper does not say which buildings the bid or the 120-odd ryo of funds paid for, so neither is the cost of the sanctuary alone; the day counts of the new build are not given; and its carved panels, paid for outside the carpenter's contract, make it more ornate than a plain small one. The 1749 rates come in two tiers: local carpenters and sawyers at 9 to 15 days' work to the bu (about 1.0 to 1.67 monme of silver a day), and roofers brought from Edo at 3 monme a day. And the Shibasaki building was paid for by a village of about 219 households whose merchant houses lent to it, with gifts from the raftsmen of six villages on the Tama and Aki rivers.

Used for: what a small shrine's sanctuary, roof and stonework cost, and the day wages beside them (Money and prices)
