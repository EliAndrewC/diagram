li-bozhong-jiangnan

Li Bozhong, "The Practice of 'Ten Mu per Farmer' and the Scale of the Traditional Peasant Economy" (Zhongguo Nongshi, 1996) and Agricultural Development in Jiangnan, 1620-1850 - consulted at secondhand via LSE economic-history working papers and the EH.net review (https://eh.net/book_reviews/agricultural-development-in-jiangnan-1620-1850/)

What it is: Li Bozhong's studies of farming and handicrafts in Jiangnan, the Yangzi delta, from 1620 to 1850, read only through Kenneth Pomeranz's review of them on EH.Net (2003) and LSE working papers that cite them.

Why it applies, and its limits: Li's reconstructions of farm size and of men's and women's labor productivity in Ming and Qing Jiangnan are, in Pomeranz's words, generally the best we have. They are of the richest region of China, and Pomeranz names their limits: the documentary base is fairly narrow, and the evidence that country households bought rather than made their goods is sparser than for the cities; the record takes Li only as the review reports him.

Used for: mid-Qing Jiangnan farms averaged ~10 mu (~1.5 ac at the Ming-Qing mu of ~614 m2) per farm household; the narrow base of the estimates of women's work (Money and prices)
