li-bozhong-jiangnan

Li Bozhong, "The Practice of 'Ten Mu per Farmer' and the Scale of the Traditional Peasant Economy" (Zhongguo Nongshi, 1996) and Agricultural Development in Jiangnan, 1620-1850 - consulted at secondhand via LSE economic-history working papers and the EH.net review (https://eh.net/book_reviews/agricultural-development-in-jiangnan-1620-1850/)

What it is: Li Bozhong's studies of farming and handicrafts in Jiangnan, the Yangzi delta, from 1620 to 1850, read only at secondhand: Kenneth Pomeranz's review on EH.Net (2003) of Agricultural Development and of Li's Jiangnan de zaoqi gongyehua (Proto-Industrialization in the Yangzi Delta, 2000), and LSE working papers that cite the 1996 article.

Why it applies, and its limits: Li's reconstructions of agricultural productivity and factor inputs, among them men's and women's labor productivity, in Ming and Qing Jiangnan are, in Pomeranz's words, generally the best we have. They are of the richest region of China, and Pomeranz names their limits: the documentary base is fairly narrow, and the evidence that country households bought rather than made their goods is sparser than for the cities; the review gives no farm size, so the ~10 mu figure is taken from the LSE working papers' report of the 1996 article, not from Li or Pomeranz directly. It is also a late figure for China's most crowded region: the review reports family farms shrinking as Li's Jiangnan grew to perhaps 36 million people on about 43,000 km2 by 1850, so it is the delta's small late farm, not that of an ordinary county.

Used for: mid-Qing Jiangnan farms averaged ~10 mu (~1.5 ac at the Ming-Qing mu of ~614 m2) per farm household; the narrow base of the estimates of women's work (Money and prices)
