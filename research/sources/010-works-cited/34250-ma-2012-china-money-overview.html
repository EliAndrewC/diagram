ma-2012-china-money-overview

Debin Ma, "Money and Monetary System in China in the 19th-20th Century: An Overview", London School of Economics, Economic History working paper 159/12, January 2012 (https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/41940/1/WP159.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's survey of China's money from the 1800s to the 1930s: silver by weight, copper cash by count, foreign silver dollars and, later, banknotes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains how cash was strung and counted and why a string's count varied, with a nineteenth-century witness on cash counted two for one. It is an overview of the 1800s and after, not a series: it gives ranges and examples, not a rate for any one place and year, and takes its exchange-rate history from other works.

Used for: how many cash made a string, and the tael's weight in silver (Money and prices)
