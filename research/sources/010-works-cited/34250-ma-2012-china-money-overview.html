ma-2012-china-money-overview

Debin Ma, "Money and Monetary System in China in the 19th-20th Century: An Overview", London School of Economics, Economic History working paper 159/12, January 2012 (https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/41940/1/WP159.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's survey of China's money from about 1800 to 1950: silver by weight, copper cash by count, foreign silver dollars and, later, banknotes.

Why it applies, and its limits: It explains how cash was strung and counted and why a string's count varied, with a nineteenth-century witness on cash counted two for one. It is an overview of the 1800s and after, not a series: it gives ranges and examples, not a rate for any one place and year, and takes its exchange-rate history from other works. Its tael of "roughly 37 grams" of silver is a rounded footnote that cites nothing, and the paper itself says the tael varied by region and by trade, and the Kuping tael between Beijing and the provinces. So it gives the tael's size, not the weight of any one tael. The witness on cash counted two for one is a Western observer of 1889, quoted at second hand from Kann (1927).

Used for: how many cash made a string, and the tael's weight in silver (Money and prices)
