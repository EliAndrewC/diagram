E. K. Chambers, The Elizabethan Stage, vol. 2 (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1923), Project Gutenberg eBook #67423, plain text (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/67423.txt.utf-8)

What it is: The second volume of the standard history of the English stage to 1616, on the acting companies and the playhouses. For the companies' playing it relays the diary of Philip Henslowe, which records each day's play and takings at his theaters in 1592-1597, and it prints primary documents: Thomas Platter's account of 1599, an actor's articles of 1614, a draft royal license of King James's first years.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a comparative source: it says how often London's companies played, read only as a yardstick for players who know Shakespeare's stage, and never for any Rokugani figure or for a map. Its counts are Chambers' reading of Henslowe's diary, a record of one owner's theaters, not of every company; the Gutenberg text marks no page breaks in its body, so a citation can name a section (its contents list gives the printed page each section begins on) but not the page of a passage.

Used for: how often London's companies played, their seasons and closures (Comparative money: other societies)
