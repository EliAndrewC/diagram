miyashiro-kumehara-1698

宮代町 (Miyashiro town, Saitama), 文化財調査報告書『岡安家文書』関係史料集 ("Source collection for the cultural-property survey report on the Okayasu family papers", title translated), chapter 3, document 1: 元禄十一年 久米原村年貢割付状 (the tax assessment of Kumehara village for 1698), and documents 4, 7 and 12, the pledge deeds 元禄十三年 質地証文 (1700), 享保五年 質地証文 (1720) and 享保十一年 質地証文 (1726), document 31, 文化十五年 諸職人規定 (the craftsmen's wage rule of 1818), and document 44, 文政十三年 御高札建替入用見積 (the estimate of 1830 for rebuilding the notice board) (in Japanese; https://www.town.miyashiro.lg.jp/cmsfiles/contents/0000002/2922/okayasukebunsyo.pdf)

What it is: A town's printed transcription of a village headman's family papers; its first document is the lord's tax assessment of 1698 for Kumehara (signed 細六郎兵; a later note in the same collection names the village the fief of the bannerman Hosoi, not shogunal land under an intendant), a village of the Hyakken district of Saitama county, listing each grade of paddy, dry field and house land by area; three pledge deeds in the same collection record fields of the village pledged for a sum of gold for two or three years; a rule of 1818 agreed by thirty-nine villages of the district sets craftsmen's day wages in copper cash; and an estimate of 1830 prices the rebuilding of the village's notice board in silver, carpenters' days included.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the land of a small village of the Saitama plain grade by grade - about 8 cho of paddy against about 11 of dry field and 0.6 of house land - and the assessments printed with it deduct for flood loss, so its fields took water. Its limits: it is one village (the collection's assessments for 1699-1702 repeat the same areas, so they are the register's standing figures, not one year's measure); the 1698 assessment names no landform, but the collection's assessments of 1722 and 1723 list, outside the register, 2.7 cho of former Kasahara-marsh paddy (笠原沼), assessed apart as 見取場 since 1690 because its water stood too deep, so the village lay against a marsh; whether those 2.7 cho fall inside or beyond the register's 8 cho of paddy the assessments do not make plain, and if beyond, the village's paddy share was larger than the register's; calling the village lowland rests on that marsh and on our inference - the town's own page puts Miyashiro on the northeast edge of the Omiya terrace (8-11 m) with back marsh and a natural levee beside it, so its dry fields may be terrace fields rather than dry land within a lowland; it says nothing of a row or of where the dry fields lay; the Kanto plain carried unusually large shares of dry field, so this is one Kanto village's share, not a rice village's norm in western Japan or southern China; the areas are the tax register's; and the transcription prints vertical text that a reader must follow column by column. For money, the deeds give the sum lent on the land, not what it sold for, and three deeds are too few to give a going rate: 70 ryo in 1700 on a whole holding of 1.15 cho with a house plot, a bamboo grove and a flood paddy, pledged by a man of neighbouring Suga village with Kumehara's officials co-signing; 24 ryo in 1720 on 4.1 tan of low dry field, middle paddy and flood paddy; 3 ryo in 1726 on about 1 tan of low paddy. The ryo of 1700 is the coin debased in the Genroku recoinage of 1695, and those of 1720 and 1726 are the coin restored by the Shotoku-Kyoho reform, so the three sums are not in the same money. Its craftsmen's rule of 1818 gives the day wages thirty-nine villages, as employers, agreed among themselves to pay - a set rate, not wages seen paid, and silent on whether food came on top (the estimate of 1830 pays carpenters' food apart) - and names no unskilled laborer's wage, though three graded rates in it have their trade left blank in the transcription; its estimate of 1830 for a small timber structure prints a count of carpenter-days that its own sums do not match, and was drawn up in a year when a fire in Edo had raised prices.

Used for: the share of dry field in a lowland paddy village (Homesteads); what a village's fields were pledged for, and on what terms, and how paddy and dry field were taxed; craftsmen's day wages in the villages of Saitama county, north of Edo (Money and prices)
