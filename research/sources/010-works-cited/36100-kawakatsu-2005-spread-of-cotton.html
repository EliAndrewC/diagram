kawakatsu-2005-spread-of-cotton

Kawakatsu Heita, "Japanese Civilization (Part 23): The Spread of Cotton to the Far East", Japan Spotlight, November/December 2005, pp. 48-51 (https://web.archive.org/web/20231117042330/https://www.jef.or.jp/journal/pdf/special%20article_0511.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's four-page magazine survey of how cotton reached China, Korea and Japan, with a table of Korean cotton cloth sent to Japan drawn from two Japanese studies of 1941 and 1942.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the one run of dated counts of the Korean cloth read, and those counts cover only trade "the government" recorded, the page not saying which. Many of its rows are single consignments, dated to the month or tied to one named importer, and the table has nothing from 1425 to 1449 and stops at 1544, so it is not a yearly series of the trade. It has no footnotes, naming its sources in a list of main references, under its tables and figure and by author for a few remarks, so the "records" behind its eastern and western cotton regions go unnamed; its text calls 500,000 pieces an annual level where its table shows that figure for one year, and it misdates two farm manuals, printing 1597 and 1580-82 for works it says describe the late 1600s. On the seed it lists three rival dates (the 1490s to 1510s, 1588, 1592-95) and concludes only that it probably came more than once, so it supports a span and not one date.

Used for: the Korean cloth trade, the dates of the seed that took root, and the eastern and western cotton regions (Money and prices)
