saltstory-shio-no-keifu

増田幸右 (Masuda Kosuke), 「美味しい塩の系譜」 ("A lineage of good salt", title translated), the blog 塩物語 (Salt story), June 14, 2014 (in Japanese; https://saltstory.wordpress.com/2014/06/14/%E7%BE%8E%E5%91%B3%E3%81%97%E3%81%84%E5%A1%A9%E3%81%AE%E7%B3%BB%E8%AD%9C/)

What it is: A blog's history of salt making in Japan from the earliest times, with a passage on what a sho of salt cost in the Edo period as it was carried from the Inland Sea to the coasts and inland.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is the only account found of how the price of salt rose with distance inland. It names Hiroyama Gyodo's history of salt in Japan for the figures but gives no page, says the prices are "said to" have been so, and gives no year for any of them. Its top figure, 300 mon a sho at Ichinoseki, is the highest price it reports, not a usual inland one, and its rule that the price doubled two ri inland and tripled at three is given without an example.

Used for: how the price of salt rose inland in Edo-period Japan, as one secondary account tells it (Money and prices)
