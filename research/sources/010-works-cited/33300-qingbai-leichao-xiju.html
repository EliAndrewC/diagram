徐珂 (Xu Ke), 《清稗類鈔》 (Qing bai lei chao, "Classified anecdotes of the Qing", title translated), its section 戲劇類 (theater), Wikisource transcription (in Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/清稗類鈔/089)

What it is: A large collection of anecdotes and notes on Qing life, compiled by Xu Ke from earlier writings and published in 1917; its theater section covers the kinds of opera, the Beijing theaters and their customs, troupes, street performers and conjurors.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives how Beijing's theaters charged their seats and their fixed price before 1900, a rural Henan troupe's price for three days, and the 3 cash asked by a Hangzhou street showman's trained-toad act that Yuan Mei saw as a boy (about the 1720s), a novelty act some 150 years before the Beijing price. Its entries are mostly undated, drawn from writers of different reigns and gathered after the dynasty fell; the Henan price has no date at all; and it does not say which count of cash the Beijing price is in.

Used for: Beijing theater seats, a village troupe's hire and a street act's price in Qing China (Money and prices)
