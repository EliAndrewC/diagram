Our World in Data, "Share of population living in extreme poverty: Long-run estimates - Cost of basic needs", a chart page on the data of Michalis Moatsos (2021), archived August 17, 2026 (https://archive.ourworldindata.org/20260817-183715/grapher/share-of-population-living-in-extreme-poverty-lines-cbn.html)

What it is: The chart page of Our World in Data, a project of Global Change Data Lab, a nonprofit based in the UK, showing Moatsos's estimates of the share of the world living in extreme poverty from 1820 to 2018, with his own description of his method quoted and his source named.

Why it applies, and its limits: It is a short account of how Moatsos's long-run poverty lines are built: one line for every country and year, from each country's own prices rather than one global line. It does not say that only part of that span was priced: his own working paper of 2022 (MPRA paper 113035) prices the food in the line from the International Labour Organization's food prices of 1924 to 2008, and carries each country's line back to 1820 by its consumer price index, or holds it constant where there is none, so the nineteenth-century lines are extrapolated, not priced at the local prices of the time. The page names the basket only as minimal nutrition and adequately heated shelter, after Allen (2017). It quotes his method in three sentences and reports his figures after its own processing, so the detail of his work, in his chapter of 2021 that it names, is not on it.

Used for: how a long-run basic-needs poverty count is priced (Money and prices)
