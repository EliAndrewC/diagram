saito-1993-bakumatsu-wages

斎藤修 (Saito Osamu), 「幕末 - 明治の賃金変動再考」 ("Wage movements from the end of the shogunate into Meiji, reconsidered", title translated), 『経済研究』 (The Economic Review) 44:4 (October 1993), pp. 330-338 (in Japanese; https://econ-review.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/files/1995/keizaikenkyu4404330.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's re-examination of wage series for Edo-Tokyo from the 1830s to the 1870s, chiefly carpenters' wages, comparing the officially set rates in published price tables with the market rates paid, including their leap after the earthquake of 1855.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives an Edo carpenter's day wage of the 1850s in both ryo and silver, which also fixes the rate between them that the price tables used. Its flat 0.125 ryo is the officially set rate, which the author contrasts with the market rates beside it; it is Edo's, the shogun's city, not a village's; and its day laborers' series is given only as an index, with no figure.

Used for: an Edo carpenter's day wage, and the ryo in silver (Money and prices)
