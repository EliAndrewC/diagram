saito-1993-bakumatsu-wages

斎藤修 (Saito Osamu), 「幕末 - 明治の賃金変動再考」 ("Wage movements from the end of the shogunate into Meiji, reconsidered", title translated), 『経済研究』 (The Economic Review) 44:4 (October 1993), pp. 330-338 (in Japanese; https://econ-review.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/wp-content/uploads/files/1995/keizaikenkyu4404330.pdf)

What it is: An economic historian's re-examination of real-wage series for Edo-Tokyo from 1830 to 1894, chiefly building craftsmen's and carpenters' wages, with day laborers', servants' and Chōshi soy brewers' series beside them, comparing the officially set rates in published price tables with the market rates paid, including their leap after the earthquake of 1855.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives an Edo carpenter's day wage of the 1850s in both ryo and silver, which implies a rate of 60 monme of silver to the ryo. Its flat 0.125 ryo, held from 1853 to 1860, is the officially set rate with meal money included, which the author contrasts with the market rates beside it (4 monme and 1.2 for meals in 1854, 45 the year after the earthquake); the paper does not say whether the 60 monme to the ryo is the tables' conversion or the author's, and gives no market rate between gold and silver, which it notes moved sharply after the ports opened; it is Edo's, the shogun's city, not a village's; and its day laborers' series is given only as an index, with no figure.

Used for: an Edo carpenter's day wage, and the ryo in silver (Money and prices)
