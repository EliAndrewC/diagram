sbt-tudor-money-pot

Stephanie Appleton, "Shakespeare in 100 Objects: Money Pot", Shakespeare Birthplace Trust blog, 3 May 2013 (https://www.shakespeare.org.uk/explore-shakespeare/blogs/shakespeare-100-objects-money-pot/)

What it is: A museum's short article, by a doctoral researcher in history, on a sealed earthenware money box of 1550-1650 in its collection, the kind the gatherers of London's first theaters held at the doors; it quotes Ben Jonson's Bartholomew Fair of 1614 on paying at the door.

Why it applies, and its limits: Its account of where the gatherers stood is hedged ("would have been stationed") and the page reports pot fragments dug up at the Rose (1988) and the Theatre (2010); it is about London's playhouses, not touring companies. Its Jonson passage is a comic induction to a play of 1614, not a price list, and its sixpence at the door comes fifteen years after Platter's penny and is six times it.

Used for: the gatherers and their money boxes at a London theater's doors, and Jonson's sixpence at the door (Comparative money: other societies)
