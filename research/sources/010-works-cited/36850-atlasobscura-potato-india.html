atlasobscura-potato-india

Atlas Obscura, "In India, the British Hyped Potatoes to Justify Colonialism", 9 April 2019 (https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/potato-history-india)

What it is: A popular magazine article on the East India Company's promotion of the potato, drawing on Company ledgers, letters and food historians.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the Company's campaigns from 1797 and the 1814 letter on who ate it. It quotes the records only in short phrases, and its evidence is largely what the British in India wrote about their own program; the 1814 letter is one army wife's. Its statement that the promotion brought the potato into widespread cultivation gives no date, area or figure, and it shows who grew the crop only by whom the Company gave seed or money to. Rebecca Earle, whom it quotes, doubts the effect: citing David Arnold, she writes that the potato was already a commercial crop in Calcutta, the western Deccan and elsewhere before the Agricultural and Horticultural Society's scheme, spread in part by British troops' tastes, and that the Society did little to change that (History Workshop Journal, 2017, p. 183). So the article carries what the Company attempted, not how far the crop spread. It also calls Benjamin Heyne Scottish; he was German, born in Saxony.
