滕德永 (Teng Deyong), 「清代内务府房产经营状况探析」 ("An inquiry into the management of the Imperial Household Department's houses in the Qing", title translated), 故宫博物院院刊 (Palace Museum Journal) 2014(4) (in Chinese; https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2018/06/04/u5b1537cba43d1.pdf)

What it is: A historian's study, from the archives of the Imperial Household Department, of how the department let, sold and ran as shops the houses it held in Beijing, many of them confiscated from disgraced officials, with its schedules of rent and of sale prices by the size of a house's bays.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the department's rent for a bay of a house, which it says was collected monthly though the schedule itself names no period, and its sale price for a bay of the same size, so a rent can be set against a value. Both are administrative schedules, not market prices; the sale prices went by the number of purlins alone before 1731 and again from 1775, and were graded by a house's damage only from 1730 to 1775 and from 1802; its dwelling houses were let only to Han officials serving in the capital, though its shops were let to others; it does not date the rent schedule it quotes; and though the department collected its rents monthly, a Han official could instead have his deducted from his salary each quarter.

Used for: the Imperial Household Department's sale prices per bay in Beijing, and that its rents were collected monthly (Money and prices)
