eb-1911-cotton

"Cotton", Encyclopaedia Britannica, 11th edition (1911), as transcribed on Wikisource (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Cotton)

What it is: The long article on the plant and the trade in an English encyclopedia of 1911, credited on Wikisource to William George Freeman, Sydney John Chapman and Allan Noble Monkhouse.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives a contemporary summary of the trade of about 1900 and, in a few sentences written some sixty years after they ended, a retrospect of the East India Company's trials of foreign cotton between 1788 and 1850. Its names for the species predate the classification now used: it reports that most Indian cottons are often referred to G. herbaceum and that Watt dissents, and later work divides them between G. arboreum and G. herbaceum, so its name does not settle which species a given Indian cotton was. It dates the Company's trials only as a span, 1788 to 1850, with no year or place for any single one. Its figure for Japan, about 800,000 bales a year in the five years before the war with Russia, is a national total from late Meiji Japan, after factory spinning had grown up, and says nothing of what Japan imported before 1868; the passage does not say which bale it counts, and the article's own section on baling puts an Indian bale at about 400 lb against an American bale of 500 lb.

Used for: American cotton in India and Japan's imports of about 1900 (Money and prices)
