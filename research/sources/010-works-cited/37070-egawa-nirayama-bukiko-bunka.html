egawa-nirayama-bukiko-bunka

Cultural Heritage Online (Agency for Cultural Affairs), 江川家住宅（静岡県田方郡韮山町）武器庫 ("The Egawa house, Nirayama, Shizuoka: the weapons store", title translated) (https://bunka.nii.ac.jp/heritages/detail/122177)

What it is: The national cultural-properties database entry for the weapons store of the Egawa house at Nirayama in Izu, an Important Cultural Property: its date (late Edo), its size, roof form, entry and roofing, in one line.

Why it applies, and its limits: It measures a surviving weapons store that stands as a building of its own. The Egawa were the hereditary intendants who governed shogunal land around Nirayama for the shogun, and their office stood on their own house's grounds. The entry says none of this beyond naming the family intendants of Nirayama in a related item's title: it files the store as a building of the Egawa house, gives no description and no year for the office. So it shows what one intendant's house-office kept, not what a domain's seat kept, and stands for a county magistrate's seat only by analogy: the office lay in a hereditary family's own house over a district reaching into several provinces, where the setting's magistrate is an appointed official in a state-built yamen over one county. The entry gives no place in the compound, no contents and no doors beyond the gable-end entry.

Used for: where a seat kept its arms, and the size and build of a weapons store of its own (Estates and other compounds)
