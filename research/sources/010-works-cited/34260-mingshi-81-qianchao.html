mingshi-81-qianchao

明史 (History of the Ming, title translated), volume 81, 食貨五, 錢鈔 ("Food and goods, part 5: coin and paper money"), Wikisource text (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明史/卷81)

What it is: The treatise on the Ming dynasty's paper money and copper coin in the official dynastic history compiled under the Qing and finished in 1739, summarizing the court's coinage, its rulings on what cash was worth in silver, and the debates behind them, reign by reign.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the rates at which the court counted copper cash against silver from about 1500 to 1628. Most are rates the court fixed or used for its own payments and taxes, not market prices; where it reports what cash passed for in the market, it is debased private coin; and the history was compiled from the court's records a century after the dynasty fell.

Used for: the Ming court's rates of copper cash to the tael of silver (Money and prices)
