mingshi-81-qianchao

明史 (History of the Ming, title translated), volume 81, 食貨五, 錢鈔 ("Food and goods, part 5: coin and paper money"), Wikisource text (in classical Chinese; https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/明史/卷81)

What it is: The treatise on the Ming dynasty's paper money and copper coin in the official dynastic history compiled under the Qing and finished in 1739, summarizing the court's coinage, its rulings on what cash was worth in silver, and the debates behind them, reign by reign.

Why it applies, and its limits: It gives the rates at which the court counted copper cash against silver, from the first paper money of 1375 (a string of notes, a thousand cash and a tael of silver counted equal) to a standard of a thousand cash to the tael set after 1630, most of them from about 1500 on. Most are rates the court fixed or used for its own payments and taxes, not market prices; its one market rate for private coin is for debased cash; and the history was compiled from the court's records a century after the dynasty fell. Each rate is for one kind of coin, and kinds passed side by side at very different rates: one Jiajing ruling counted Jiajing coin at 7 to the fen of silver, earlier Ming coin at 10 and older dynasties' coin at 30, and under Wanli northern coin ran a third dearer than southern. So no single figure here is the empire's rate of cash to silver.

Used for: the Ming court's rates of copper cash to the tael of silver (Money and prices)
